### 1. Introduction to Perceptual Units of Loudness

In acoustics and noise control, understanding how humans perceive sound is crucial. While objective measures like sound pressure level (SPL) quantify the physical intensity of sound, they don't always directly correlate with our subjective experience of loudness. The concepts of **phones** and **sones** are perceptual units developed to quantify this subjective loudness.

*   **Phones:** Relate to loudness level, a subjective measure of how loud a sound is perceived relative to a reference tone.
*   **Sones:** Represent a more direct measure of perceived loudness, where a doubling of sones corresponds to a perceived doubling of loudness.

**(Relevant to CO3: To understand the mechanism of hearing, noise regulations and noise measuring devices - K2)**

### 2. The Phone (Loudness Level)

The **phone** is a unit used to express **loudness level**. It is defined in relation to a reference sound, which is a pure tone (a sine wave) of 1000 Hz at 40 dB SPL.

#### 2.1. Definition of the Phone:

*   A sound has a loudness level of **0 phons** if it is perceived to be as loud as a 1000 Hz tone presented at 0 dB SPL. (Note: 0 dB SPL is the threshold of hearing for a healthy young ear).
*   A sound has a loudness level of **N phons** if it is perceived to be as loud as a 1000 Hz tone presented at **N dB SPL**.

**(Reference: Ekinsler, Frey, Coppens, & Sanders, Fundamentals of Acoustics, Chapter 11: Psychoacoustics - Subjective Loudness, describes the development of these units.)**

#### 2.2. How Phones are Determined:

Phones are typically determined through **equal-loudness contours** (also known as Fletcher-Munson curves or Robinson-Dadson curves for more modern data). These contours represent combinations of different frequencies and sound pressure levels that are perceived to have the same loudness.

*   **Equal-Loudness Contours:** Graphs that plot SPL (dB) against frequency (Hz) for various loudness levels (phons).
*   **Subjective Testing:** These contours are generated by asking listeners to compare the loudness of different tones. For instance, a listener might adjust the SPL of a tone at a different frequency until it sounds as loud as a 1000 Hz tone at a specific SPL.

#### 2.3. Key Characteristics of Phones:

*   **Reference Point:** 0 phons is based on a 1000 Hz tone at 0 dB SPL.
*   **Linearity (Limited):** While phones provide a way to equate loudness across different frequencies, the relationship between phons and our perception of loudness is not perfectly linear. A 10-phon increase does not always correspond to a doubling of perceived loudness.
*   **Frequency Dependence:** The ear's sensitivity varies significantly with frequency. At low frequencies, a higher SPL is required to achieve the same perceived loudness as a 1000 Hz tone.

#### 2.4. Example:

If a person listens to a 100 Hz tone and finds it to be as loud as a 1000 Hz tone presented at 60 dB SPL, then the 100 Hz tone has a loudness level of **60 phons**.

**(Key Concept: Perceived loudness is not solely determined by Sound Pressure Level (SPL); it also depends on frequency.)**

---

### 3. The Sone (Perceived Loudness)

The **sone** is a unit designed to quantify **perceived loudness** in a more direct, ratio-scale manner. It is defined such that a sound of 1 sone is a reference loudness.

#### 3.1. Definition of the Sone:

*   A sound with a loudness level of **40 phons** is defined to have a loudness of **1 sone**.
*   If a sound is perceived to be twice as loud as a reference sound, it has twice the number of sones. For example, a sound perceived to be twice as loud as 1 sone has a loudness of 2 sones.

**(Reference: Ver & Beranek, Noise and Vibration Control Engineering, Chapter 4: Human Response to Noise - discusses psychometric scales and units like sones.)**

#### 3.2. Relationship between Sones and Phons:

The relationship between sones and phons is non-linear. A common approximation for this relationship, based on empirical data, is:

$L_s = 2^{(L_{phons} - 40) / 10}$

Where:
*   $L_s$ is the loudness in sones.
*   $L_{phons}$ is the loudness level in phons.

#### 3.3. Key Characteristics of Sones:

*   **Ratio Scale:** Sones are intended to be a ratio scale, meaning that the ratio of two sone values reflects the ratio of perceived loudness.
*   **Reference Point:** 1 sone is equivalent to 40 phons.
*   **Non-linear Relationship:** A 10-phon increase typically corresponds to a doubling of sones (i.e., a doubling of perceived loudness).

#### 3.4. Examples:

*   **Calculating Sones from Phons:**
    *   If a sound has a loudness level of 50 phons, its loudness in sones is:
        $L_s = 2^{(50 - 40) / 10} = 2^{10 / 10} = 2^1 = 2 \text{ sones}$
        This means a 50-phon sound is perceived to be twice as loud as a 40-phon sound.
    *   If a sound has a loudness level of 60 phons, its loudness in sones is:
        $L_s = 2^{(60 - 40) / 10} = 2^{20 / 10} = 2^2 = 4 \text{ sones}$
        This means a 60-phon sound is perceived to be four times as loud as a 40-phon sound, and twice as loud as a 50-phon sound.

*   **Calculating Phons from Sones:**
    *   If a sound has a loudness of 8 sones, its loudness level in phons is:
        $8 = 2^{(L_{phons} - 40) / 10}$
        Taking the logarithm base 2 of both sides:
        $\log_2(8) = (L_{phons} - 40) / 10$
        $3 = (L_{phons} - 40) / 10$
        $30 = L_{phons} - 40$
        $L_{phons} = 70 \text{ phons}$

**(Key Concept: The sone scale provides a measure where perceived loudness increases exponentially with sound pressure level, reflecting our subjective experience.)**

### 4. Application in Noise Control

Understanding phones and sones is vital for:

*   **Assessing Noise Impact:** It allows engineers to go beyond simple SPL measurements and assess how different noises will be perceived by humans.
*   **Setting Noise Criteria:** Noise regulations and design criteria often consider perceived loudness rather than just objective intensity.
*   **Comparing Noises:** Provides a way to compare the perceived loudness of different sounds, even if they have different frequency content. For example, a low-frequency rumble might require a higher SPL to sound as loud as a high-frequency hiss.

**(Reference: Munjal, Noise and Vibration Control, Chapter 7: Noise Criteria and Measurement - discusses subjective scales in noise control applications.)**

---

### 5. Important Points to Remember:

*   **Phones** are units of **loudness level**, defined relative to a 1000 Hz tone. They are a perceptually equal interval scale.
*   **Sones** are units of **perceived loudness**, representing a ratio scale. Doubling sones means perceived loudness doubles.
*   The relationship between sones and phons is **non-linear**: $L_s = 2^{(L_{phons} - 40) / 10}$.
*   **Equal-loudness contours** are fundamental to defining phons and understanding frequency-dependent hearing sensitivity.
*   These perceptual units help bridge the gap between objective acoustic measurements and subjective human experience of sound.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1 (K2 - Knowledge Level):**
What is the reference sound for the definition of the "phone" unit of loudness level?
A. A 100 Hz tone at 0 dB SPL
B. A 1000 Hz tone at 0 dB SPL
C. A 1000 Hz tone at 40 dB SPL
D. A pink noise signal at 40 dB SPL

**Answer:** C. A 1000 Hz tone at 40 dB SPL. (This is the definition of 40 phons. 0 phons is defined as being as loud as a 1000 Hz tone at 0 dB SPL).

**Question 2 (K2 - Knowledge Level):**
Which unit of loudness is intended to represent a ratio scale, where doubling the value means the perceived loudness doubles?
A. Decibel (dB)
B. Phon
C. Sone
D. Pascal (Pa)

**Answer:** C. Sone.

**Question 3 (K2 - Knowledge Level):**
If a sound has a loudness level of 50 phons, what is its approximate loudness in sones?
A. 1 sone
B. 1.5 sones
C. 2 sones
D. 4 sones

**Answer:** C. 2 sones.
*Calculation: $L_s = 2^{(50 - 40) / 10} = 2^{10 / 10} = 2^1 = 2$ sones.*

**Question 4 (K2 - Knowledge Level):**
If a sound is perceived to be twice as loud as a reference sound with a loudness of 3 sones, what is the loudness of the new sound in sones?
A. 3 sones
B. 4.5 sones
C. 6 sones
D. 9 sones

**Answer:** C. 6 sones.
*Explanation: Since sones represent a ratio scale, doubling the perceived loudness means doubling the sone value. 3 sones * 2 = 6 sones.*

**Question 5 (K2 - Knowledge Level):**
What is the relationship between the subjective perception of loudness and the Sound Pressure Level (SPL) for pure tones at different frequencies?
A. Perceived loudness is directly proportional to SPL at all frequencies.
B. Perceived loudness is independent of frequency.
C. At lower frequencies, higher SPLs are needed to achieve the same perceived loudness as at 1000 Hz.
D. At higher frequencies, lower SPLs are needed to achieve the same perceived loudness as at 1000 Hz.

**Answer:** C. At lower frequencies, higher SPLs are needed to achieve the same perceived loudness as at 1000 Hz.
*Explanation: This is a fundamental characteristic of human hearing, captured by equal-loudness contours.*
