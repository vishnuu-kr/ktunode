# ENGINEERING INSTRUMENTS AND MEASUREMENTS - Module 3: Force Measuring Devices: Torque and Shaft Power Measurement

## Topic: Shaft Power Measurement

---

### 1. Introduction to Shaft Power Measurement

Shaft power, often referred to as brake power (BP), is the useful power delivered by an engine or motor at its output shaft. It represents the mechanical work done per unit time. Measuring shaft power is crucial for evaluating the performance of prime movers like internal combustion engines, electric motors, and turbines.

**Key Concept:** Shaft power is fundamentally the product of torque and angular velocity.

**Formula:**
$$P = T \times \omega$$
Where:
*   $P$ = Shaft Power (Watts)
*   $T$ = Torque (Newton-meters, Nm)
*   $\omega$ = Angular velocity (radians per second, rad/s)

Alternatively, if angular velocity is in revolutions per minute (RPM):
$$P = \frac{2 \pi N T}{60000}$$
Where:
*   $N$ = Angular velocity (revolutions per minute, RPM)
*   $T$ = Torque (Newton-meters, Nm)

**Importance:**
*   Performance evaluation of engines and motors.
*   Efficiency determination.
*   Load testing and calibration.
*   Troubleshooting and diagnostics.

---

### 2. Fundamental Principles of Shaft Power Measurement

Shaft power measurement inherently relies on measuring two primary quantities:
1.  **Torque (T):** The twisting force applied to the shaft.
2.  **Angular Velocity (N or $\omega$):** The rotational speed of the shaft.

The measurement system must accurately capture these parameters simultaneously and then compute the power.

---

### 3. Torque Measurement Devices (Torque Sensors/Torquemeters)

Torque measurement is the critical component of shaft power measurement. Several devices are used for this purpose, primarily based on the principles of elasticity and force conversion.

#### 3.1. Dynamometers

Dynamometers are the most common devices used to measure torque and, consequently, shaft power. They work by applying a controlled load to the rotating shaft and measuring the reaction torque.

**Types of Dynamometers:**

##### 3.1.1. Absorption Dynamometers
These devices absorb the power produced by the engine/motor and dissipate it as heat.

*   **Prony Brake Dynamometer:**
    *   **Principle:** A simple and early form of dynamometer. It uses a band or blocks that are tightened around a rotating drum attached to the shaft. The friction generated opposes the rotation. The torque is measured by the force exerted on a lever arm connected to the brake band.
    *   **Setup:** A drum rotates with the shaft. A lever arm is attached to the brake mechanism. A spring balance or weights measure the force at the end of the lever arm.
    *   **Torque Calculation:** $T = F \times L$
        *   $F$ = Force measured by the spring balance/weights (N)
        *   $L$ = Length of the lever arm (m)
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, inexpensive.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Inefficient for high power, generates a lot of heat, manual adjustment is required.
    *   **Reference:** Dobelin, Chapter 14: Force and Torque Measurement.

*   **Hydraulic Dynamometer (Water Brake):**
    *   **Principle:** Uses water as the working fluid to absorb power. A rotor with vanes rotates within a stationary casing (stator) filled with water. The friction between the rotor and water, and the water's internal friction, creates a drag torque. The casing is mounted on bearings, and the reaction torque is measured by a lever arm.
    *   **Advantages:** Can handle very high power, efficient heat dissipation, relatively smooth load application.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires a water supply and cooling system, can be bulky.
    *   **Reference:** Sawhney & Sawhney, Chapter 21: Torque Measurement.

*   **Eddy Current Dynamometer:**
    *   **Principle:** Based on the principle of electromagnetic induction. A conductive rotor (usually made of aluminum or copper) rotates within a magnetic field generated by electromagnets. Eddy currents are induced in the rotor, which oppose the motion and dissipate energy as heat. The magnetic field strength is controlled to vary the load. The reaction torque on the electromagnets is measured.
    *   **Advantages:** Wide range of load control, responsive, no contact wear, good for steady-state and transient testing.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Generates heat, requires cooling.
    *   **Reference:** Nakra & Chaudhry, Chapter 6: Measurement of Torque.

*   **Electric Dynamometer (Dynamo-meter or Motor-Generator Set):**
    *   **Principle:** The device under test (DUT) is coupled to an electric motor that acts as a generator. The power generated by the DUT is absorbed by the electric generator. The generator is excited (field current adjusted) to control the load. The torque is measured by measuring the reaction force on the generator's frame, which is mounted on trunnion bearings.
    *   **Advantages:** Very precise control of load, can operate as a motor to drive the DUT for motoring tests, high power handling capabilities.
    *   **Disadvantages:** More complex and expensive than other types.
    *   **Reference:** Singh, S.K., Industrial Instrumentation and Control, Chapter 15: Torque Measurement.

##### 3.1.2. Torque-Measuring Couplings (Torquemeters)

These are installed directly between the prime mover and the driven load to measure torque without acting as a load absorber themselves. They are generally non-contact devices.

*   **Torsion Bar Type Torquemeter:**
    *   **Principle:** A shaft or torsion bar is placed between the driving and driven elements. When torque is applied, the torsion bar twists. This angular twist is proportional to the applied torque.
    *   **Measurement of Twist:**
        *   **Optical Methods:** Using mirrors attached to the input and output ends of the torsion bar. A light beam is reflected from these mirrors. The relative displacement of the reflected beams indicates the twist.
        *   **Strain Gauge Method:** Strain gauges are bonded to the surface of the torsion bar. As the bar twists, the gauges are subjected to tensile and compressive strains. By arranging them in a Wheatstone bridge configuration, a voltage output proportional to the torque is obtained. This is the most common and accurate method.
    *   **Advantages:** Non-intrusive, can measure torque directly from the shaft, high accuracy, can measure dynamic torques.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires careful calibration, sensitive to bending and axial loads if not properly designed.
    *   **Reference:** Dobelin, Chapter 14: Force and Torque Measurement.

*   **Magnetic Particle Torquemeter:**
    *   **Principle:** Operates on the principle of magnetostriction. The torque transmitted through a shaft causes a change in the magnetic properties of the material (e.g., permeability). This change is detected by magnetic sensors.
    *   **Advantages:** Non-contact, robust.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Less common than strain gauge types, accuracy can be affected by temperature and stray magnetic fields.

### 4. Angular Velocity Measurement Devices

The rotational speed of the shaft needs to be measured accurately.

*   **Tachometers:**
    *   **Mechanical Tachometers:** Use centrifugal force principles. A rotating shaft drives a flyweight mechanism, which moves a pointer on a calibrated scale.
    *   **Electrical Tachometers:**
        *   **DC Tachometers:** A small DC generator whose output voltage is directly proportional to its rotational speed.
        *   **AC Tachometers:** Employ AC generators (e.g., permanent magnet alternators) where the output frequency is proportional to speed.
        *   **Stroboscopic Tachometers:** Use a stroboscope to flash light at a specific frequency. The shaft is viewed under this flashing light. When the flashing frequency matches the shaft's rotational frequency, the shaft appears stationary, allowing speed measurement.
    *   **Electronic Tachometers (Non-contact):**
        *   **Photoelectric Tachometers:** Use a light source and a photodetector. A disc with slots or reflective markers is attached to the shaft. The light beam is interrupted or reflected as the disc rotates, generating pulses. The frequency of these pulses is measured.
        *   **Magnetic Tachometers:** Detect changes in a magnetic field caused by a rotating toothed wheel or a magnet attached to the shaft.

*   **Stroboscopes:**
    *   **Principle:** A flashing light source whose frequency can be adjusted. When the flashing frequency is an exact multiple or sub-multiple of the shaft's rotational speed, the shaft appears to be rotating slowly or be stationary.
    *   **Application:** Useful for observing rotating machinery and measuring speed without physical contact.
    *   **Reference:** Sawhney & Sawhney, Chapter 18: Speed Measurement.

### 5. Shaft Power Measurement Systems

A complete shaft power measurement system integrates torque and speed measurement.

*   **System Components:**
    1.  **Torque Sensor:** Measures the torque transmitted by the shaft.
    2.  **Speed Sensor:** Measures the rotational speed of the shaft.
    3.  **Signal Conditioning Unit:** Amplifies, filters, and converts sensor outputs into usable electrical signals.
    4.  **Data Acquisition System (DAQ):** Captures the torque and speed signals.
    5.  **Calculator/Controller:** Computes shaft power using the formula $P = T \times \omega$ or $P = \frac{2 \pi N T}{60000}$.
    6.  **Display/Recording Unit:** Shows the computed power value and records data.

*   **Integrated Torque and Speed Sensors:** Some modern torque sensors incorporate speed measurement capabilities. For example, a strain gauge torquemeter can also have a built-in rotary encoder or a magnetic pickup to measure speed.

*   **Data Acquisition and Processing:**
    *   The output signals from the torque and speed sensors are typically analog voltages.
    *   These signals are digitized by Analog-to-Digital Converters (ADCs) within the DAQ system.
    *   A microprocessor or computer then uses the digital values of torque and speed to calculate the shaft power in real-time.
    *   Averaging or filtering techniques may be applied to obtain stable power readings.

### 6. Application Examples and Considerations

*   **Automotive Engine Testing:** Dynamometers are used to test engines at various speeds and loads to determine brake horsepower, torque curves, and fuel efficiency.
*   **Electric Motor Characterization:** Measuring the output shaft power of electric motors to verify their performance specifications.
*   **Turbine Performance:** Assessing the power output of steam or gas turbines.
*   **Industrial Machinery:** Monitoring the power consumption and output of various rotating machinery.

**Important Considerations:**
*   **Accuracy and Calibration:** Torque and speed sensors must be regularly calibrated to ensure accurate power readings.
*   **Response Time:** For dynamic power measurement (e.g., during transient engine operation), the system must have a fast response time.
*   **Environmental Factors:** Temperature, vibration, and electrical noise can affect sensor performance. Proper shielding and compensation may be required.
*   **Power Losses:** In systems where torque is measured through a coupling, it's essential to consider any power losses between the coupling and the actual output shaft if the final output power is of interest.
*   **Units:** Ensure consistency in units (Nm for torque, rad/s or RPM for speed) when calculating power.

### 8. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Define shaft power and state its fundamental formula.
**Answer:** Shaft power is the mechanical power delivered at the output shaft of a rotating machine. It is calculated as the product of torque and angular velocity: $P = T \times \omega$.

**Question 2:** Briefly describe the working principle of an eddy current dynamometer.
**Answer:** An eddy current dynamometer uses the principle of electromagnetic induction. A rotor rotates within a magnetic field. Eddy currents are induced in the rotor, creating a drag force proportional to the speed and magnetic field strength. This drag produces a reaction torque that is measured.

**Question 3:** A shaft is rotating at 1500 RPM and transmitting a torque of 100 Nm. Calculate the shaft power in kilowatts (kW).
**Answer:**
Given:
$N = 1500$ RPM
$T = 100$ Nm

Using the formula $P = \frac{2 \pi N T}{60000}$:
$P = \frac{2 \times \pi \times 1500 \times 100}{60000}$
$P = \frac{300000 \pi}{60000}$
$P = 5 \pi$ Watts
$P \approx 5 \times 3.14159 = 15.708$ Watts

**Wait, there's a mistake in the calculation!** The formula for Watts from Nm and RPM is:
$P_{kW} = \frac{N \times T_{Nm}}{9548.8}$ or $P_{Watts} = \frac{2 \pi N_{RPM} \times T_{Nm}}{60}$

Let's recalculate:
$P = \frac{2 \times \pi \times 1500 \times 100}{60}$ Watts
$P = \frac{300000 \pi}{60}$ Watts
$P = 5000 \pi$ Watts
$P \approx 5000 \times 3.14159 = 15707.95$ Watts
$P \approx 15.71$ kW

**Correct Answer:** $P \approx 15.71$ kW

**Question 4:** What is the primary method used to measure torque in a torsion bar torquemeter?
**Answer:** The primary method is using strain gauges bonded to the surface of the torsion bar, arranged in a Wheatstone bridge configuration, to detect the angular twist. Optical methods can also be used.

**Question 5:** Differentiate between an absorption dynamometer and a torque-measuring coupling. Provide an example of each.
**Answer:**
*   **Absorption Dynamometer:** Absorbs the power produced by the prime mover and dissipates it as heat. It acts as a load.
    *   **Example:** Eddy Current Dynamometer, Hydraulic Dynamometer, Prony Brake.
*   **Torque-Measuring Coupling:** Measures torque transmitted through the shaft without absorbing power itself. It is installed in-line between the prime mover and the load.
    *   **Example:** Strain Gauge Torquemeter, Optical Torquemeter.

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   Shaft power is directly proportional to torque and angular velocity.
*   Dynamometers are devices that apply a load and measure torque to determine shaft power.
*   Common torque measurement methods include strain gauges on a torsion bar and reaction force measurement in dynamometers.
*   Accurate speed measurement is essential for calculating shaft power.
*   The choice of torque measurement device depends on the power range, required accuracy, and cost.
*   Always ensure units are consistent for accurate power calculations.
