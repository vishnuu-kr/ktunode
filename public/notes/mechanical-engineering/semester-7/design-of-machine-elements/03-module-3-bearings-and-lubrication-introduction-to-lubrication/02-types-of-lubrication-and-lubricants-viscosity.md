# DESIGN OF MACHINE ELEMENTS - MODULE 3: BEARINGS AND LUBRICATION

## Topic: Types of Lubrication and Lubricant Viscosity

---

### **Introduction to Lubrication (Brief Recap)**

Lubrication is the process of reducing friction between moving surfaces by introducing a lubricant between them. The primary goal is to minimize wear, heat generation, and power loss. Understanding different lubrication types and the properties of lubricants, particularly viscosity, is crucial for selecting the appropriate bearing and lubrication system for a given application.

### **1. Types of Lubrication Regimes**

The relative motion between surfaces and the presence of lubricant determine the lubrication regime. These regimes dictate the degree of metal-to-metal contact and the load-carrying capacity of the lubricant film.

#### **1.1. Boundary Lubrication**

*   **Definition:** In boundary lubrication, the lubricant film is very thin, and the surfaces are in close proximity, allowing for some direct metal-to-metal contact. The load is primarily supported by the lubricant's adsorbed layers and polar molecules that adhere to the metal surfaces.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   High friction.
    *   Significant wear.
    *   Surface roughness plays a critical role.
    *   Occurs at low speeds, high loads, and during start-up/shutdown.
    *   Additives (like extreme pressure additives) are crucial to prevent catastrophic wear.
*   **When it Occurs:**
    *   During starting and stopping of machinery.
    *   At very low speeds and high loads where hydrodynamic pressure cannot be fully established.
    *   In situations where lubricant supply is intermittent or insufficient.
*   **Example:** A heavily loaded gear tooth at slow speed, a cam and follower system with insufficient lubricant.

#### **1.2. Mixed Lubrication (or Hydrodynamic Boundary Lubrication)**

*   **Definition:** This is an intermediate regime between boundary and full film lubrication. There is partial metal-to-metal contact, but also some load is supported by a thin, continuous lubricant film.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Friction and wear are moderate.
    *   The lubricant film is thin but still provides some separation.
    *   Surface asperities intermittently contact each other.
*   **When it Occurs:**
    *   When operating conditions transition between boundary and hydrodynamic lubrication.
    *   In applications with fluctuating loads or speeds.
*   **Example:** A slowly rotating shaft under moderate load where a full oil film is not yet established.

#### **1.3. Hydrodynamic Lubrication**

*   **Definition:** In hydrodynamic lubrication, a continuous film of lubricant is generated between the moving surfaces due to their relative motion. This film completely separates the surfaces, preventing direct metal-to-metal contact. The load is carried entirely by the hydrodynamic pressure generated within the lubricant film.
*   **Key Principle (Reynolds Equation):** The pressure generated in the lubricant film is directly proportional to the viscosity of the lubricant, the relative speed of the surfaces, and inversely proportional to the film thickness.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Very low friction.
    *   Negligible wear.
    *   Requires sufficient relative speed and lubricant supply.
    *   The film thickness depends on viscosity, speed, load, and bearing geometry.
*   **When it Occurs:**
    *   At moderate to high speeds and moderate loads.
    *   When the bearing geometry allows for the formation of a converging wedge of lubricant.
*   **Example:** A smoothly rotating journal bearing at sufficient speed, an automotive engine crankshaft supported by oil film.

#### **1.4. Elastohydrodynamic Lubrication (EHL)**

*   **Definition:** EHL is a regime where elastic deformation of the moving surfaces plays a significant role in supporting the load. A thin but continuous lubricant film is formed, and the pressure within this film is so high that it causes elastic deformation of the surfaces, thus contributing to the film thickness and load support.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Very high pressures generated in the lubricant film.
    *   Surface deformation is elastic.
    *   Extremely thin film thickness (typically in the order of the wavelength of light).
    *   Essential for heavily loaded rolling element bearings and gear teeth.
*   **When it Occurs:**
    *   In concentrated contacts like gear teeth and rolling element bearings.
    *   Under very high loads and moderate to high speeds.
*   **Example:** Ball bearings, roller bearings, and gear meshes in transmissions.

#### **1.5. Solid Lubrication**

*   **Definition:** Solid lubricants are used when conventional liquid or grease lubricants are not suitable due to extreme temperatures, vacuum, or contamination. These lubricants adhere to the surfaces and provide a low-shear interface.
*   **Examples of Solid Lubricants:**
    *   **Graphite:** Hexagonal structure with weak inter-layer bonds, allowing layers to slide easily. Good at high temperatures.
    *   **Molybdenum Disulfide (MoS2):** Similar layered structure to graphite, providing excellent lubrication properties, especially in vacuum.
    *   **Tungsten Disulfide (WS2):** Similar properties to MoS2, often used in more extreme conditions.
    *   **PTFE (Polytetrafluoroethylene):** Known for its very low coefficient of friction.
*   **Applications:** High-temperature bearings, vacuum environments, aerospace applications, dry lubricant coatings.

### **2. Lubricant Viscosity**

Viscosity is the most critical property of a lubricant, defining its resistance to flow. It directly influences the formation and effectiveness of the lubricant film.

#### **2.1. Definition of Viscosity**

*   **Definition:** Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's internal resistance to flow. It represents the shear stress required to produce a unit rate of shear strain.
*   **Newton's Law of Viscosity:** For Newtonian fluids, the shear stress ($\tau$) is directly proportional to the rate of shear strain ($\frac{du}{dy}$):
    $\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$
    where:
    *   $\tau$ is the shear stress.
    *   $\mu$ is the dynamic viscosity (or absolute viscosity).
    *   $\frac{du}{dy}$ is the velocity gradient (rate of shear strain).

#### **2.2. Types of Viscosity**

*   **Dynamic Viscosity (Absolute Viscosity, $\mu$):**
    *   **Definition:** Resistance to shear within the fluid itself.
    *   **Units:**
        *   SI: Pascal-second (Pa·s) or N·s/m².
        *   CGS: Poise (P) or dyne·s/cm². 1 Poise = 0.1 Pa·s.
        *   Commonly used: centiPoise (cP). 1 cP = 0.001 Pa·s = 1 mPa·s.
        *   Water at 20°C has a viscosity of approximately 1 cP.
    *   **Significance:** Directly used in calculations for hydrodynamic lubrication (Reynolds equation).

*   **Kinematic Viscosity ($\nu$):**
    *   **Definition:** The ratio of dynamic viscosity to density ($\rho$). It describes how readily a fluid flows under gravity.
    *   **Formula:** $\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho}$
    *   **Units:**
        *   SI: meter squared per second (m²/s).
        *   CGS: Stokes (St) or cm²/s. 1 Stokes = 10⁻⁴ m²/s.
        *   Commonly used: centiStokes (cSt). 1 cSt = 10⁻⁶ m²/s.
    *   **Significance:** Widely used in lubricant specifications and for flow calculations where density is a factor. Lubricant viscosity grades (e.g., SAE grades) are typically based on kinematic viscosity.

#### **2.3. Factors Affecting Viscosity**

*   **Temperature:**
    *   **Effect:** Viscosity of liquids decreases significantly with increasing temperature. For gases, it increases with temperature.
    *   **Lubricants:** Most petroleum-based lubricants are Newtonian liquids. Their viscosity drops rapidly as temperature rises. This is a critical consideration in bearing design, as operating temperature can significantly alter lubrication performance.
    *   **Viscosity Index (VI):** A measure of how much a lubricant's viscosity changes with temperature.
        *   **High VI:** Less change in viscosity with temperature (desirable for a wide operating range).
        *   **Low VI:** Significant change in viscosity with temperature.
    *   **Reference:** Textbooks like Bhandari and Norton discuss the importance of temperature on viscosity and introduce the concept of VI.

*   **Pressure:**
    *   **Effect:** Viscosity of most liquids increases with increasing pressure. This effect is more pronounced at very high pressures.
    *   **Significance:** In EHL regimes, the extreme pressures can significantly increase the lubricant's effective viscosity, contributing to the formation of thick films.

*   **Shear Rate (for non-Newtonian fluids):**
    *   **Effect:** For Newtonian fluids, viscosity is independent of shear rate. However, some lubricants (e.g., greases, certain synthetic oils) exhibit non-Newtonian behavior.
        *   **Shear-thinning (Pseudoplastic):** Viscosity decreases with increasing shear rate.
        *   **Shear-thickening (Dilatant):** Viscosity increases with increasing shear rate.
    *   **Significance:** Understanding the rheological behavior is important for selecting lubricants for specific applications, especially those involving high shear rates.

#### **2.4. Viscosity Measurement and Standards**

*   **Saybolt Viscometer:** An older method that measures the time in seconds required for a fixed volume of liquid to flow through a calibrated orifice at a specific temperature. Results are reported in Saybolt Universal Seconds (SUS) or Saybolt Furol Seconds (SFS).
*   **Kinematic Viscometers (e.g., Ubbelohde Viscometer):** Measure the time for a fixed volume of liquid to flow through a capillary tube under gravity.
*   **Society of Automotive Engineers (SAE) Viscosity Grades:**
    *   SAE J300 classification is widely used for engine oils and gear oils.
    *   Grades are defined by a range of kinematic viscosities at 100°C (e.g., SAE 30, SAE 40).
    *   "W" grades (e.g., SAE 10W-30) indicate viscosity at low temperatures (cold cranking simulation). These oils are multi-grade oils.
*   **ISO Viscosity Grades (VG):**
    *   ISO 3448 defines 18 viscosity grades based on the mid-point kinematic viscosity at 40°C.
    *   These grades are labeled as ISO VG 2, ISO VG 3, ..., ISO VG 1500.
    *   Widely used for industrial lubricants.

#### **2.5. Importance of Viscosity in Lubrication**

*   **Hydrodynamic Film Thickness:** A higher viscosity lubricant can generate a thicker hydrodynamic film, providing better separation of surfaces and reducing wear.
*   **Load Carrying Capacity:** Increased viscosity generally leads to higher load-carrying capacity in hydrodynamic lubrication.
*   **Friction:** While higher viscosity can lead to increased viscous shear losses (and thus higher friction) within the lubricant itself, it generally reduces friction caused by boundary interactions when a full film is maintained. The overall effect depends on the lubrication regime.
*   **Sealing:** A sufficiently viscous lubricant can help seal the bearing against contaminants.
*   **Lubricant Flow:** Viscosity affects how easily the lubricant can flow into the bearing gap and maintain an adequate supply.
*   **Cold Start-up:** Oils with appropriate low-temperature viscosity are crucial to prevent excessive friction and wear during engine start-up.

### **3. Types of Lubricants**

Lubricants can be broadly classified based on their base material and additives.

#### **3.1. Mineral Oils**

*   **Source:** Derived from crude petroleum through refining processes.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Paraffinic:** Higher viscosity index, good low-temperature properties.
    *   **Napthenic:** Lower viscosity index, better solvency, good at low temperatures.
    *   **Aromatic:** Generally lower viscosity index and poor oxidation stability.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Cost-effective.
    *   Widely available.
    *   Good general-purpose lubricants.
    *   Can degrade at high temperatures or under severe oxidation.
*   **Applications:** Engine oils, gear oils, hydraulic fluids, general machinery lubrication.

#### **3.2. Synthetic Lubricants**

*   **Source:** Man-made through chemical synthesis.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Polyalphaolefins (PAOs):** Excellent viscosity-temperature characteristics, good thermal stability, low volatility.
    *   **Esters (Diesters, Polyol Esters):** Good solvency, high VI, excellent thermal and oxidative stability, good biodegradability.
    *   **Silicones:** Very wide temperature range, excellent thermal stability, but poor solvency and lubrication under high loads.
    *   **Polyglycols (PAGs):** Good lubricity, high VI, excellent thermal stability, some are water-soluble.
    *   **Phosphate Esters:** Excellent fire resistance, used in high-temperature hydraulic systems.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Superior performance under extreme conditions (high/low temperatures, high pressures).
    *   Higher cost than mineral oils.
    *   Better thermal and oxidative stability.
    *   Often tailored for specific applications.
*   **Applications:** Aerospace, high-performance automotive, high-temperature industrial machinery, refrigeration compressors.

#### **3.3. Greases**

*   **Definition:** A semi-solid lubricant consisting of a base oil, a thickening agent (soap or non-soap), and additives.
*   **Thickening Agents:**
    *   **Soap Thickeners:**
        *   Lithium soaps: Versatile, good water resistance.
        *   Calcium soaps: Good water resistance, limited temperature range.
        *   Sodium soaps: Good temperature stability but poor water resistance.
        *   Aluminum complex soaps: High temperature and water resistance.
    *   **Non-Soap Thickeners:**
        *   Polyurea, bentonite clay, silica gel, PTFE.
        *   Used for specialized high-temperature or chemical resistance applications.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Remains in place, providing lubrication where liquid oil would drip out.
    *   Good for sealing.
    *   Viscosity is more complex and depends on the thickener and base oil.
*   **Applications:** Bearings where a continuous oil supply is difficult, chassis lubrication, slow-speed heavily loaded applications.

#### **3.4. Lubricant Additives**

Additives are chemical compounds added to base oils to improve their properties or impart new ones.

*   **Anti-wear (AW) Additives:** Form a protective film on metal surfaces to prevent wear under moderate loads (e.g., zinc dialkyldithiophosphate - ZDDP).
*   **Extreme Pressure (EP) Additives:** Form a sacrificial chemical film that prevents welding and scuffing under very high loads (e.g., sulfurized oils, chlorinated hydrocarbons, phosphorus compounds).
*   **Antioxidants:** Inhibit oxidation of the base oil, extending its life (e.g., phenolic antioxidants, amine antioxidants).
*   **Rust and Corrosion Inhibitors:** Protect metal surfaces from attack by water and acids.
*   **Viscosity Index Improvers (VIIs):** Long-chain polymers that expand at higher temperatures, counteracting the natural drop in viscosity (e.g., polymethacrylates).
*   **Pour Point Depressants:** Improve low-temperature fluidity by preventing wax crystal formation.
*   **Detergents and Dispersants:** Keep contaminants suspended in the oil, preventing sludge formation.
*   **Anti-foam Agents:** Reduce the tendency of oil to form foam.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Lubrication Regime Selection:** The choice of lubrication regime depends heavily on speed, load, and the presence of lubricant. Hydrodynamic and EHL are ideal for minimizing wear.
*   **Viscosity is King:** Viscosity is the most crucial property for lubricant selection. It dictates the ability to form a protective film.
*   **Temperature Effects:** Always consider the operating temperature range. Viscosity changes significantly with temperature, and a high Viscosity Index (VI) is desirable for a stable lubricant performance.
*   **SAE and ISO Grades:** Familiarize yourself with common viscosity grading systems (SAE for automotive, ISO for industrial).
*   **Additives are Essential:** Modern lubricants rely heavily on additives to perform under demanding conditions.
*   **Boundary and EHL:** Boundary lubrication requires specialized additives. EHL is crucial for rolling contacts and heavily loaded gears.
*   **CO3 Connection:** Understanding these lubrication types and viscosity principles is fundamental to designing effective sliding contact bearings.

### **Practice Questions**

**Question 1:**
Differentiate between Hydrodynamic Lubrication and Elastohydrodynamic Lubrication (EHL). In which type of machine element would you primarily expect to find EHL?

**Answer 1:**
*   **Hydrodynamic Lubrication:** Relies solely on the relative motion of surfaces to generate pressure within a continuous lubricant film, completely separating the surfaces. Load is carried by hydrodynamic pressure.
*   **Elastohydrodynamic Lubrication (EHL):** Involves the elastic deformation of the mating surfaces due to extremely high lubricant film pressures. This deformation contributes to the lubricant film thickness.
*   **EHL is primarily found in:** Heavily loaded rolling element bearings (ball bearings, roller bearings) and gear teeth meshes.

**Question 2:**
A machine operates at low speeds and under a very high load. Which lubrication regime is most likely to be present, and what type of lubricant additive would be crucial for preventing severe wear?

**Answer 2:**
The most likely lubrication regime is **Boundary Lubrication** due to low speeds and high loads, which prevent the formation of a full hydrodynamic film.
To prevent severe wear, **Extreme Pressure (EP) additives** would be crucial. These additives form a sacrificial chemical film that prevents metal-to-metal contact and scuffing under high loads. Anti-wear (AW) additives might also be beneficial but EP additives are more critical in this severe condition.

**Question 3:**
Explain the significance of Viscosity Index (VI). If a lubricant has a high VI, what does it imply about its performance over a range of temperatures?

**Answer 3:**
The Viscosity Index (VI) is a measure of how much a lubricant's viscosity changes with temperature.
*   **High VI:** Implies that the lubricant's viscosity changes **less drastically** with temperature variations.
*   **Performance Implication:** A lubricant with a high VI is desirable for applications operating over a wide temperature range. It will maintain a more consistent viscosity, providing adequate lubrication at both low temperatures (during start-up) and high temperatures (under load and heat generation) compared to a lubricant with a low VI.

**Question 4:**
What are the primary components of a grease, and what are the advantages of using grease over oil in certain applications?

**Answer 4:**
The primary components of a grease are:
1.  **Base Oil:** Provides the lubricating properties.
2.  **Thickening Agent:** Typically a soap (like lithium, calcium, aluminum) or a non-soap material (like polyurea), which creates a matrix to hold the oil.
3.  **Additives:** Enhance performance (e.g., EP additives, antioxidants).

**Advantages of Grease over Oil:**
*   **Retention:** Grease stays in place in a bearing housing or on a surface, preventing leakage or dripping, which is ideal for slow-moving, heavily loaded applications or where a continuous oil supply is difficult.
*   **Sealing:** Grease acts as a better seal against contaminants (dust, moisture) than oil.
*   **Lubrication during Intermittent Operation:** It can provide lubrication even when machinery is not running continuously.

**Question 5:**
If a bearing is designed to operate under conditions where a full oil film cannot be sustained due to low speed or high load, what type of lubrication is assumed, and what specific lubricant property is of paramount importance to minimize wear?

**Answer 5:**
If a full oil film cannot be sustained, the lubrication regime is assumed to be **Boundary Lubrication**.
In boundary lubrication, the lubricant property of **viscosity** is still important, but the effectiveness of **boundary additives** (like anti-wear and extreme pressure additives) becomes paramount. These additives form protective films on the metal surfaces, and their chemical nature and ability to adhere to the metal under high stress are crucial to minimize wear.
