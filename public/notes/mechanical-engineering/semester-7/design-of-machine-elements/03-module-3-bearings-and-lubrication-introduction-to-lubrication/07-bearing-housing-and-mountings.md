# DESIGN OF MACHINE ELEMENTS - Module 3: Bearings and Lubrication - Introduction to Lubrication: Bearing Housing and Mountings

## Introduction to Bearing Housing and Mountings

This module focuses on the critical aspects of supporting and housing rolling contact bearings within a machine, ensuring their proper function, longevity, and the overall integrity of the machine element. We will explore the design considerations for bearing housings and various mounting techniques.

---

### 1. Functions of a Bearing Housing

A bearing housing, also known as a bearing pedestal or pillow block, plays a crucial role in the overall functionality and lifespan of a rolling contact bearing. Its primary functions are:

*   **Support and Location:** To rigidly support the bearing and accurately locate it in its working position, ensuring proper alignment of the shaft.
*   **Protection:** To protect the bearing from external contaminants such as dust, dirt, moisture, and corrosive agents.
*   **Lubrication Containment:** To retain the lubricant (oil or grease) within the bearing and prevent its leakage.
*   **Load Transmission:** To transmit the applied loads from the shaft to the supporting structure (e.g., machine frame).
*   **Heat Dissipation:** To help dissipate heat generated during bearing operation, especially in critical applications.
*   **Mounting Surface:** To provide a surface for mounting the bearing assembly to the machine structure.

---

### 2. Types of Bearing Housings

Bearing housings are generally classified based on their construction and mounting method. The selection depends on factors like load, speed, operating environment, and ease of maintenance.

#### 2.1. Split Type Housings (Two-Piece Housings)

*   **Description:** These housings are split into two halves, typically along the horizontal or vertical plane. They are commonly used for heavy-duty applications and where ease of assembly and disassembly is important, especially when the shaft cannot be easily passed through the bearing.
*   **Construction:** Usually made of cast iron or steel. They consist of a cap and a base, bolted together.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Easy to assemble and disassemble, especially on shafts that are already in place.
    *   Allows for inspection and replacement of the bearing without major dismantling of the machine.
    *   Can accommodate larger shaft diameters and loads.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires precise machining of the mating surfaces to ensure proper sealing.
    *   Can be more prone to leakage if not assembled correctly.
*   **Applications:** Heavy machinery, large shafts, railway axle boxes, shipbuilding.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Bhandari, V. B. (2020), *Design of Machine Elements***: Discusses split housings extensively, often with diagrams illustrating their construction and assembly for heavy shafts.
    *   **Norton, R. L. (2018), *Machine Design – An Integrated Approach***: Covers the practical aspects of split housings in industrial contexts.

#### 2.2. Solid Type Housings (One-Piece Housings)

*   **Description:** These are single-piece housings, typically designed as a block with a bore for the bearing. They are commonly used for lighter to medium-duty applications where the shaft can be assembled by passing it through the housing.
*   **Construction:** Can be made from cast iron, steel, or even aluminum alloys for lighter applications.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Simpler construction and generally more robust against leakage.
    *   Often more cost-effective for mass production.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Assembly and disassembly can be more challenging if the shaft cannot be easily maneuvered.
    *   Limited access for inspection or replacement without disassembling other components.
*   **Common Subtypes:**
    *   **Pillow Blocks (Plummer Blocks):** The most common type of solid housing, designed to be mounted on a flat surface. They have mounting holes in the base.
        *   **Referencing:**
            *   **PSG Design Data (2022)**: Provides dimensions and types of standard pillow blocks.
            *   **Bhandari, V. B. (2020), *Machine Design Data Book***: Offers selection guides and dimensional data for pillow blocks.
    *   **Flange Units:** Housings designed with a flange that allows them to be mounted on a vertical or horizontal surface, often on the side of a machine frame.
        *   **Referencing:**
            *   **Mahadevan & Reddy (2019), *Design Data Hand Book***: Includes data on flange units and their mounting configurations.
    *   **Cartridge Units:** Self-contained units where the bearing and housing are integrated. They are often spherical in shape and designed for easy installation into a machine cavity.
        *   **Referencing:**
            *   **Norton, R. L. (2018), *Machine Design – An Integrated Approach***: Discusses the convenience and applications of cartridge units.

### 3. Mounting of Bearing Housings

Proper mounting is crucial for ensuring the bearing operates correctly and to prevent premature failure. This involves securing the housing to the supporting structure and ensuring the correct axial and radial fit of the bearing within the housing.

#### 3.1. Housing Bore Design and Tolerances

*   **Purpose:** The bore of the housing must be accurately machined to provide the correct interference or clearance fit for the outer ring of the rolling element bearing. This fit is critical for preventing fretting corrosion and ensuring proper load transfer.
*   **Fit Selection:**
    *   **Shaft Fit (Inner Ring):** Generally a tight or interference fit (e.g., H7, K7) to prevent the inner ring from rotating on the shaft.
    *   **Housing Fit (Outer Ring):**
        *   **Fixed Bearing:** For the bearing that restricts axial movement, the outer ring is typically fitted with a slight interference (e.g., H7, JS7) in the housing bore.
        *   **Floating Bearing:** For the bearing that allows for axial expansion and contraction due to temperature changes, the outer ring is fitted with a clearance (e.g., H8, G7) in the housing bore. This is typically installed in the non-locating bearing position.
*   **Surface Finish:** A smooth surface finish within the housing bore is essential to prevent fretting and ensure good contact with the outer ring.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Bhandari, V. B. (2020), *Design of Machine Elements***: Chapter on fits and tolerances provides detailed information on housing bore specifications.
    *   **Shigley, J. E. (2003), *Mechanical Engineering Design***: Offers a comprehensive treatment of fits and tolerances in mechanical components.

#### 3.2. Sealing Arrangements

*   **Purpose:** To prevent ingress of contaminants and egress of lubricant. Effective sealing is paramount for bearing life.
*   **Types of Seals:**
    *   **Contact Seals:** Shaft seals that maintain physical contact with the rotating shaft or housing. Examples include felt seals, lip seals (O-rings, V-rings), and labyrinth seals with rubbing elements.
    *   **Non-Contact Seals:** Seals that do not maintain physical contact, relying on the geometry of their design to create a barrier. Examples include labyrinth seals (without rubbing), slinger discs, and shields.
    *   **External Seals:** Often added externally to the housing for extra protection in harsh environments.
*   **Materials:** Seal materials are chosen based on temperature, chemical resistance, and contact pressure (e.g., Nitrile rubber, Viton, PTFE).
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Norton, R. L. (2018), *Machine Design – An Integrated Approach***: Discusses various types of seals and their applications with examples.
    *   **Bhandari, V. B. (2020), *Machine Design Data Book***: Provides selection tables and design guidelines for seals.

#### 3.3. Lubrication Provisions

*   **Purpose:** To ensure the bearing receives and retains adequate lubrication.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Grease Lubrication:** Housings designed with grease fittings (zerk fittings) or ports for manual or automatic greasing.
    *   **Oil Lubrication:**
        *   **Splash Lubrication:** Bearing rotates in a sump of oil.
        *   **Circulating Oil Lubrication:** Oil is pumped to the bearing and then drained or filtered.
        *   **Oil Mist Lubrication:** A fine mist of oil is supplied to the bearing.
*   **Features:** Sight glasses for oil level indication, drain plugs, oil fill ports.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Bhandari, V. B. (2020), *Design of Machine Elements***: Dedicated sections on bearing lubrication, including methods and housing features.
    *   **Raghavendra K. (2019), *Design of Machine Elements II***: Covers the nuances of oil lubrication systems for bearings.

#### 3.4. Mounting Methods (Securing the Housing)

*   **Bolting:** The most common method. Housings are bolted to a rigid machine structure.
    *   **Base Mounting:** Pillow blocks mounted on a flat surface.
    *   **Flange Mounting:** Flange units mounted to vertical or horizontal faces.
    *   **Considerations:** Bolt size, grade, tightening torque, use of washers, and ensuring a rigid foundation.
*   **Welding:** In some specific applications, housings might be welded to a structural element, though this is less common for standard bearing housings.
*   **Alignment:** Crucial for proper operation. Misalignment can lead to increased vibration, noise, and premature bearing failure. Techniques like using precision machined mating surfaces and careful assembly are employed.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Sharma & Agarwal (2017), *Machine Design***: Discusses practical aspects of mounting and alignment.
    *   **Juvinall & Marshek (2011), *Fundamentals of Machine Component Design***: Explores the mechanical interface between housings and machine structures.

### 4. Design Considerations for Bearing Housings

The design of a bearing housing must account for various factors to ensure optimal performance and longevity.

#### 4.1. Material Selection

*   **Cast Iron (e.g., Grey Cast Iron):** Most common due to good machinability, damping capacity, and cost-effectiveness. Suitable for moderate loads and temperatures.
*   **Steel (e.g., Carbon Steel, Alloy Steel):** Used for higher loads, higher temperatures, or when greater strength and rigidity are required.
*   **Aluminum Alloys:** Used for lightweight applications, offering good heat dissipation but lower strength.
*   **Other Materials:** Composites or specialized alloys may be used in extreme environments.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Bhandari, V. B. (2020), *Design of Machine Elements***: Provides guidance on material selection based on application requirements.

#### 4.2. Stiffness and Rigidity

*   **Importance:** The housing must be sufficiently rigid to maintain the precise alignment of the bearing and shaft under load. Excessive deflection can lead to misalignment and reduced bearing life.
*   **Design Aspects:** Ribbing, thicker walls, and appropriate bracing are used to enhance stiffness.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Norton, R. L. (2018), *Machine Design – An Integrated Approach***: Emphasizes the structural integrity and stiffness requirements of machine components.

#### 4.3. Thermal Expansion

*   **Consideration:** Differential thermal expansion between the shaft, bearing, and housing can affect the fits and introduce stresses.
*   **Design:** In applications with significant temperature variations, provisions for axial expansion (e.g., using a floating bearing arrangement) are made.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Sharma & Agarwal (2017), *Machine Design***: Discusses thermal effects on fits and component design.

#### 4.4. Machining and Manufacturing Aspects

*   **Precision:** Housing bores require high precision machining to achieve the specified tolerances for bearing fits.
*   **Assembly:** Ease of assembly and disassembly is a key consideration, particularly for split housings.
*   **Cost:** Manufacturing costs, including material, machining, and assembly, influence the choice of housing type and material.

#### 4.5. Noise and Vibration Damping

*   **Damping:** Housings made of materials like cast iron offer inherent damping properties, which can help reduce operational noise and vibration.
*   **Design:** Ribbing and structural design can also influence vibration characteristics.

### 5. Mounting of Bearings in Housings

This section focuses on the actual process of installing the bearing within its housing.

#### 5.1. Interference Fits

*   **Inner Ring:** Typically an interference fit (e.g., H7, K7) onto the shaft. This is achieved by heating the inner ring or cooling the shaft before assembly.
*   **Outer Ring:**
    *   **Locating Bearing (Fixed):** Interference fit (e.g., H7, JS7) to prevent axial movement.
    *   **Non-Locating Bearing (Floating):** Clearance fit (e.g., H8, G7) to allow axial movement. This is often achieved by a slightly larger housing bore or by using an adapter sleeve or withdrawal sleeve that has a slight clearance.

#### 5.2. Adapter and Withdrawal Sleeves

*   **Purpose:** Used to mount bearings with tapered bores onto cylindrical shafts or to facilitate mounting and removal of bearings from shafts with cylindrical seats.
*   **Adapter Sleeves:** A tapered sleeve with a nut and lock washer. Tightening the nut pulls the sleeve into the bearing bore, creating the interference fit.
*   **Withdrawal Sleeves:** Similar to adapter sleeves but used for dismounting. A nut is screwed onto the sleeve, pushing the sleeve out of the bearing bore.
*   **Advantages:** Allows for mounting on simpler shafts, provides a controlled interference fit, and simplifies maintenance.
*   **Referencing:**
    *   **Bhandari, V. B. (2020), *Design of Machine Elements***: Detailed explanation and diagrams of adapter and withdrawal sleeves.
    *   **PSG Design Data (2022)**: Provides dimensions and selection criteria for sleeves.

#### 5.3. Direct Mounting on Cylindrical Seats

*   **Process:** Used when the shaft has a precisely machined cylindrical seat with the required taper. This is more common for specialized or very high-precision applications.

#### 5.4. Mounting Procedure

1.  **Cleaning:** Ensure both the bearing and housing bore are thoroughly cleaned.
2.  **Lubrication:** Apply a thin film of oil to the housing bore and the outer ring of the bearing (if a clearance fit is used) or to the shaft and inner ring (if an interference fit is used).
3.  **Assembly:**
    *   For interference fits, heat the housing or cool the bearing. For sleeve mounting, tighten the nut progressively.
    *   For clearance fits, carefully press the bearing into the housing.
4.  **Securing:** Install any locking devices (e.g., lock washers for sleeve nuts) and seals.
5.  **Axial Locating:** Ensure the fixed bearing is properly secured axially against its seat in the housing.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
What are the primary functions of a bearing housing in a machine? Explain the importance of each function.

**Answer:**
The primary functions of a bearing housing are:
1.  **Support and Location:** To rigidly support the bearing and accurately locate the shaft, ensuring proper alignment.
2.  **Protection:** To shield the bearing from contaminants like dust, dirt, and moisture.
3.  **Lubrication Containment:** To retain lubricant within the bearing assembly.
4.  **Load Transmission:** To transfer applied loads to the supporting structure.
5.  **Heat Dissipation:** To assist in removing heat generated during operation.

**Question 2:**
Differentiate between split type and solid type bearing housings. List two advantages and two disadvantages for each type.

**Answer:**
*   **Split Type Housings:**
    *   **Description:** Split into two halves for easier assembly on large shafts.
    *   **Advantages:** Easy assembly/disassembly on installed shafts, allows for inspection without major dismantling.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires precise machining for sealing, potentially prone to leakage.
*   **Solid Type Housings:**
    *   **Description:** One-piece construction, common for lighter applications.
    *   **Advantages:** Simpler construction, more robust against leakage, often cost-effective.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Assembly/disassembly can be difficult on fixed shafts, limited access.

**Question 3:**
Explain the concept of 'fixed' and 'floating' bearings in relation to housing fits. Why is this distinction important?

**Answer:**
*   **Fixed Bearing:** The bearing that restricts axial movement of the shaft. Its outer ring is fitted with an interference fit (e.g., H7) in the housing bore to prevent axial displacement.
*   **Floating Bearing:** The bearing that allows for axial expansion and contraction of the shaft due to thermal changes. Its outer ring is fitted with a clearance fit (e.g., H8) in the housing bore.
*   **Importance:** This distinction is crucial to accommodate thermal expansion and contraction of the shaft, preventing the build-up of axial stress that could damage the bearings or housing.

**Question 4:**
What are adapter sleeves, and in what situations are they commonly used?

**Answer:**
Adapter sleeves are tapered sleeves used to mount bearings with tapered bores onto cylindrical shafts. They are commonly used when:
*   The shaft does not have a precisely machined tapered seat.
*   A controlled interference fit is desired.
*   Ease of mounting and dismounting is required.
*   A lighter or less expensive shaft design is preferred.

**Question 5 (Design Problem):**
A rolling contact bearing is to be mounted on a shaft rotating at 1500 RPM. The ambient temperature is 25°C, and it is expected to reach up to 75°C during operation. The shaft is made of steel, and the housing is made of cast iron. What type of fit would you recommend for the outer ring of the bearing in the housing bore to accommodate thermal expansion? Justify your answer.

**Answer:**
Given the expected temperature variation (25°C to 75°C), the shaft will experience thermal expansion. To prevent excessive axial stress on the bearings and housing, it is advisable to use a **floating bearing arrangement** for at least one of the bearings. Therefore, the outer ring of the bearing in the housing bore should have a **clearance fit**. A typical fit for this scenario would be **H8, G7, or F8**. This allows the shaft to expand axially without inducing damaging forces on the bearing and housing. The other bearing (the locating bearing) would have an interference fit in its housing.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Housing Bore Precision:** The accuracy of the housing bore is critical for proper bearing seating and function.
*   **Fit Selection:** The choice of fit for both the inner and outer rings of the bearing directly impacts its performance and life. Always consider thermal expansion.
*   **Sealing:** Effective sealing is paramount to protect the bearing from contaminants and retain lubricant, significantly influencing bearing life.
*   **Rigidity:** The housing must be rigid enough to maintain precise alignment under load.
*   **Lubrication:** Provisions for adequate lubrication must be incorporated into the housing design.
*   **Ease of Maintenance:** Consider how the housing will facilitate bearing inspection, lubrication, and replacement.
