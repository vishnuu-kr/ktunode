# Module 4: Jet Propulsion – Cycles and Analysis – Gas Turbine Engines

## Topic: Thrust Equation

This topic delves into the fundamental principles governing the generation of thrust in jet propulsion systems, focusing on the mathematical relationships that define it. We will analyze how the momentum of the working fluid (air and combustion products) is manipulated to produce forward motion. This is a crucial step in understanding the performance of jet engines, aligning with **CO4: To understand the principles and characteristics of jet and rocket propulsion systems (Knowledge Level: K2)**, and also contributing to **CO1: To apply the principles of thermodynamics and fluid dynamics to understand the performance and efficiency of various gas turbine cycles (Knowledge Level: K3)** by linking thermodynamic processes to propulsive output.

### 1. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Thrust (F):** The net force produced by a jet engine that propels the aircraft forward. It is the reaction force to the expulsion of mass at high velocity.
*   **Momentum:** The product of mass and velocity ($p = mv$). A change in momentum of a fluid stream signifies the application of a force.
*   **Mass Flow Rate ($\dot{m}$):** The amount of mass passing through a control volume per unit time. For a jet engine, this includes the mass of incoming air and fuel.
*   **Velocity ($v$):** The speed and direction of the working fluid. In jet propulsion, the critical velocities are the inlet air velocity and the exhaust jet velocity.
*   **Static Thrust:** The thrust produced by a stationary engine (aircraft at rest on the ground).
*   **Dynamic Thrust (or Velocity Thrust):** The thrust generated due to the motion of the aircraft, which reduces the net momentum change.
*   **Gross Thrust:** The total momentum change of the exhaust jet.
*   **Net Thrust:** The actual propulsive force available to accelerate the aircraft, calculated by subtracting the ram drag from the gross thrust.
*   **Ram Drag:** The drag experienced by the aircraft due to the mass of air ingested by the engine and accelerated to the aircraft's speed. It's the momentum change of the incoming air.

### 2. Derivation of the Thrust Equation

The thrust equation is fundamentally derived from Newton's Second Law of Motion, applied to a control volume encompassing the jet engine.

**Fundamental Principle: Newton's Second Law**

Force = Rate of change of momentum

Considering a control volume around the jet engine, the net force acting on the engine is equal to the rate of change of momentum of the fluid passing through it.

**Control Volume Analysis:**

Let's consider a control volume that includes the engine from the inlet to the exhaust nozzle.

*   **Inlet:** Air enters the engine with a mass flow rate $\dot{m}_a$ and velocity $V_0$ (aircraft velocity).
*   **Combustion Chamber:** Fuel is injected and burned, increasing the mass flow rate to $\dot{m}_j = \dot{m}_a + \dot{m}_f$, where $\dot{m}_f$ is the fuel
