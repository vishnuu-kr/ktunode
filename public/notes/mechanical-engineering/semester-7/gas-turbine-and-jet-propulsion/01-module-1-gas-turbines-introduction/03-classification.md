# GAS TURBINE AND JET PROPULSION

## Module 1: Gas Turbines – Introduction

### Topic: Classification of Gas Turbines

---

### Learning Outcomes

*   Classify gas turbines based on various criteria.
*   Differentiate between different types of gas turbines.
*   Understand the fundamental working principle of a basic gas turbine.
*   Identify the main components of a gas turbine engine.
*   Recognize the primary applications of gas turbines.

### Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Gas Turbine:** A type of internal combustion engine that uses a combustion process to generate a high-temperature, high-pressure gas stream. This gas stream then expands through a turbine, producing mechanical work.
*   **Prime Mover:** An engine that converts some form of energy into mechanical energy. Gas turbines are a type of prime mover.
*   **Brayton Cycle:** The theoretical thermodynamic cycle upon which most gas turbines operate. It consists of isentropic compression, isobaric heat addition, isentropic expansion, and isobaric heat rejection. (Ref: Ganesan, Chapter 2; Yahya, Chapter 1; Mathur, Chapter 1)
*   **Combustion Chamber (Combustor):** The component where fuel is mixed with compressed air and ignited, leading to the generation of hot combustion gases.
*   **Turbine:** A rotating component with blades that extracts energy from the high-velocity gas stream.
*   **Compressor:** A rotating component that compresses the incoming air to a higher pressure.
*   **Shaft:** Connects the compressor and turbine, allowing rotational energy to be transferred.
*   **Thrust:** The force generated by a jet engine that propels an aircraft forward.

### Classification of Gas Turbines

Gas turbines can be classified based on several criteria, each highlighting different aspects of their design and application.

#### 1. Based on the Working Fluid

*   **Closed Cycle Gas Turbine:**
    *   The working fluid (e.g., air, nitrogen, helium) is heated indirectly by an external heat source (e.g., furnace, nuclear reactor) and then reused in a closed loop.
    *   Less sensitive to fuel quality.
    *   Potentially higher thermal efficiency, especially with specialized working fluids and high-temperature heat sources.
    *   **Example:** Early designs for power generation, potential for solar thermal applications.
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Section 1.3)

*   **Open Cycle Gas Turbine:**
    *   The working fluid (air) is drawn from the atmosphere, compressed, mixed with fuel, combusted, and then expanded through the turbine. The exhaust gases are discharged into the atmosphere.
    *   Simpler design, more common.
    *   **Example:** Jet engines, most industrial power generation plants.
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Section 1.3; Yahya, Chapter 1)

#### 2. Based on the Brayton Cycle Implementation

*   **Simple Gas Turbine:**
    *   Consists of only a compressor, combustor, and turbine.
    *   The simplest configuration, forming the basis of many gas turbine engines.
    *   **Example:** Basic aircraft turbojets.
    *   (Ref: Mathur, Chapter 2)

*   **Regenerative Gas Turbine:**
    *   Incorporates a **regenerator (or heat exchanger)** which preheats the compressed air entering the combustor using the heat from the exhaust gases.
    *   Increases thermal efficiency by reducing the fuel required for heating.
    *   **Example:** Some industrial power plants, especially where waste heat is available.
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Section 1.4; Yahya, Chapter 3)

*   **Intercooled Gas Turbine:**
    *   Incorporates **intercoolers** between stages of a multi-stage compressor. The air is cooled between compression stages, reducing the work required for compression.
    *   Improves overall efficiency and can allow for higher pressure ratios.
    *   **Example:** High-performance industrial gas turbines.
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Section 1.4)

*   **Reheat Gas Turbine:**
    *   Involves **reheating** the working fluid in a second combustor after partial expansion through the first turbine. This increases the work output of the turbine.
    *   Increases power output but can decrease thermal efficiency slightly due to increased fuel consumption.
    *   **Example:** Some large power generation units, particularly those with multiple turbine stages.
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Section 1.4)

*   **Combined Cycle Gas Turbine (CCGT):**
    *   This is a significant category and often considered a "type" of gas turbine system. It combines a gas turbine with a steam turbine.
    *   The hot exhaust gases from the gas turbine are used to generate steam in a Heat Recovery Steam Generator (HRSG). This steam then drives a steam turbine.
    *   Achieves very high overall thermal efficiencies (often exceeding 60%).
    *   **Example:** Modern large-scale power plants.
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Chapter 9; Yahya, Chapter 9; Mathur, Chapter 12)

#### 3. Based on Mechanical Configuration

*   **Single Shaft Gas Turbine:**
    *   The compressor and turbine are mounted on the same shaft.
    *   The turbine speed is directly dictated by the compressor speed and the load.
    *   **Example:** Many aircraft turbojets and turboprops, some industrial applications.
    *   (Ref: Mathur, Chapter 3)

*   **Two Shaft Gas Turbine:**
    *   Features a "gas generator" (compressor and high-pressure turbine) and a "free turbine" (low-pressure turbine).
    *   The free turbine is not mechanically connected to the gas generator's shaft and can rotate at a different speed. This is crucial for applications where variable output speed is needed.
    *   **Example:** Turboprop aircraft engines, turboshaft engines for helicopters and industrial drives, some industrial power generation.
    *   (Ref: Mathur, Chapter 3; Rolls Royce, Chapter 4)

*   **Three Shaft Gas Turbine:**
    *   Less common, typically has three concentric shafts for compressor stages, intermediate turbine stages, and final turbine stages.
    *   Allows for very specific speed matching between components and optimized performance across a wide range of operating conditions.
    *   **Example:** Some military aircraft engines where high performance is paramount.
    *   (Ref: Mathur, Chapter 3)

#### 4. Based on Application

*   **Aircraft Propulsion:**
    *   **Turbojet:** The simplest form, producing thrust solely from the high-velocity exhaust jet.
    *   **Turbofan:** Similar to a turbojet but incorporates a large fan at the front. A significant portion of the airflow bypasses the core engine, producing most of the thrust. This is more fuel-efficient and quieter than turbojets.
    *   **Turboprop:** The turbine drives a propeller through a gearbox. The propeller produces most of the thrust. Highly efficient at lower speeds and altitudes.
    *   **Turboshaft:** The turbine drives a shaft that can power other machinery, such as helicopter rotors, tanks, or electrical generators.
    *   (Ref: Mathur, Chapters 4, 5, 6; Rolls Royce, Chapters 2, 3, 4)

*   **Power Generation:**
    *   Used for generating electricity in power plants.
    *   Can operate as simple cycle, regenerative cycle, or as part of a combined cycle.
    *   **Example:** Peaking power plants (start up quickly), base load power plants (in combined cycle configurations).
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Chapter 9; Yahya, Chapter 9)

*   **Industrial Applications:**
    *   **Pumps and Compressors:** Driving large pumps and compressors in oil and gas industries.
    *   **Marine Propulsion:** Powering ships and naval vessels.
    *   **Locomotives:** Used in some high-speed train applications.
    *   (Ref: Ganesan, Chapter 10; Yahya, Chapter 10)

### Main Components of a Basic Gas Turbine Engine (Open Cycle)

Understanding the components is crucial for understanding the operation and classification.

1.  **Air Intake:** Designed to efficiently guide ambient air into the compressor with minimal pressure loss. (Ref: Rolls Royce, Chapter 2)
2.  **Compressor:** Increases the pressure of the incoming air. Can be axial-flow or centrifugal.
    *   **Axial Compressor:** Air flows parallel to the axis of rotation. Highly efficient for large airflows and high pressures. (Ref: Yahya, Chapter 4)
    *   **Centrifugal Compressor:** Air is drawn in axially and thrown outwards radially by a rotating impeller. Simpler, robust, and can achieve high pressure ratios in fewer stages but is less efficient for large volumes. (Ref: Yahya, Chapter 2)
3.  **Combustion Chamber (Combustor):** Fuel is injected and mixed with compressed air, and the mixture is ignited. The combustion process increases the temperature and volume of the gas. (Ref: Ganesan, Chapter 4; Mathur, Chapter 3)
4.  **Turbine:** Extracts energy from the high-temperature, high-pressure gas. The turbine drives the compressor and also produces useful work. Typically consists of multiple stages of rotating blades and stationary stator vanes. (Ref: Ganesan, Chapter 5; Yahya, Chapter 5)
5.  **Exhaust Nozzle:** Directs the expanded gas flow, and in jet engines, accelerates it to produce thrust. (Ref: Rolls Royce, Chapter 2)

### Examples and Applications

*   **Aircraft:**
    *   **Boeing 747:** Powered by turbofan engines.
    *   **F-16 Fighter Jet:** Powered by a turbojet or low-bypass turbofan engine.
    *   **ATR 72 Turboprop:** Propeller driven by a turboshaft engine.
    *   **Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk Helicopter:** Powered by turboshaft engines driving the rotors.

*   **Power Generation:**
    *   **GE LM2500:** A popular industrial gas turbine used for power generation and marine propulsion.
    *   **Siemens SGT-800:** Another common industrial gas turbine for power generation.

*   **Industrial:**
    *   **Pipelines:** Gas turbines driving compressors to move natural gas.
    *   **Ships:** Marine gas turbines for propulsion (e.g., U.S. Navy destroyers).

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Open vs. Closed Cycle:** The primary distinction is how the working fluid is handled. Open cycles are more common due to simplicity and higher mass flow rates.
*   **Brayton Cycle Variations:** Regenerative, intercooled, and reheat cycles are modifications to the basic Brayton cycle aimed at improving efficiency or power output.
*   **Combined Cycles:** Offer the highest thermal efficiencies by integrating gas and steam turbines.
*   **Shaft Configuration:** Single, two, and three-shaft configurations offer different control and performance characteristics, especially for varying load demands.
*   **Application Drives Design:** The specific application (aircraft propulsion, power generation, industrial drive) heavily influences the chosen gas turbine configuration and components.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Differentiate between open cycle and closed cycle gas turbines. (Knowledge Level: K2)

**Answer:**
In an **open cycle** gas turbine, the working fluid (air) is taken from the atmosphere, compressed, mixed with fuel, combusted, expanded through the turbine, and then exhausted back into the atmosphere. The working fluid is not reused.
In a **closed cycle** gas turbine, the working fluid is recirculated in a closed loop. An external heat source (not combustion within the cycle) heats the fluid, which is then compressed, expanded, and cooled before being reheated.

**Question 2:** What is the primary purpose of a regenerator in a gas turbine? (Knowledge Level: K2)

**Answer:**
The primary purpose of a regenerator is to preheat the compressed air entering the combustor by utilizing the heat from the hot exhaust gases. This reduces the amount of fuel required for combustion, thereby improving the overall thermal efficiency of the gas turbine.

**Question 3:** Explain why a two-shaft gas turbine is advantageous for helicopter propulsion compared to a single-shaft gas turbine. (Knowledge Level: K3)

**Answer:**
A two-shaft gas turbine has a "free turbine" which is not mechanically linked to the compressor and its driving turbine. This allows the free turbine (which drives the rotor in a helicopter) to operate at an optimal speed independent of the gas generator (compressor and high-pressure turbine) speed. This is crucial for helicopter operations, as rotor speed needs to be controlled to maintain lift and maneuverability, and it often needs to vary independently of the engine's core operating speed, especially during maneuvers or when starting. A single-shaft turbine would force the compressor and output shaft to rotate at the same speed, which is impractical for helicopter rotor control.

**Question 4:** Name at least three types of gas turbines used in aircraft propulsion. (Knowledge Level: K2)

**Answer:**
1.  Turbojet
2.  Turbofan
3.  Turboprop
4.  Turboshaft (while primarily for driving shafts, it's a direct derivative and often discussed alongside aircraft engines)

**Question 5:** A gas turbine used for power generation is most likely to be configured as a ____ cycle. (Fill in the blank) (Knowledge Level: K2)

**Answer:**
Combined Cycle (CCGT)
