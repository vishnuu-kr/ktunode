## Introduction to Variation in Quality

**Key Concept:** Variation is inherent in any process. In quality management, understanding and reducing variation is crucial for achieving consistent product or service quality and meeting customer expectations.

**Definition:** **Variation** refers to the differences or deviations observed in the outcomes of a process or from a desired standard. It represents the fact that no two products, services, or process outputs are exactly alike.

---

### 1. Understanding Variation (LO1)

*   **Why is Variation Important?**
    *   Excessive or uncontrolled variation leads to defects, inconsistencies, customer dissatisfaction, increased costs, and reduced efficiency.
    *   Reducing variation brings processes closer to their target values, leading to improved quality and predictability.
    *   It's a core principle in philosophies like Deming's System of Profound Knowledge, emphasizing the need to understand and manage variation (Mitra, 1998).

*   **Sources of Variation:** Variation can originate from various aspects of a process. These can be broadly categorized as:

    *   **Machine/Equipment:**
        *   Wear and tear on tools or machinery.
        *   Improper calibration or maintenance.
        *   Tooling limitations.
        *   Variations in machine settings.
        *   *Example:* A drilling machine whose drill bit is slightly off-center will produce holes that are not perfectly round, causing variation in hole diameter.

    *   **Man/People/Labor:**
        *   Lack of training or skills.
        *   Fatigue or inattention.
        *   Different work methods or habits among operators.
        *   Errors in measurement or data recording.
        *   *Example:* Two assemblers performing the same task might use slightly different techniques, leading to variations in the strength of the assembled product.

    *   **Material:**
        *   Variations in raw material properties (e.g., thickness, composition, purity).
        *   Inconsistent suppliers.
        *   Defects in incoming materials.
        *   *Example:* When manufacturing paper, variations in the wood pulp used can lead to differences in paper thickness and strength.

    *   **Method/Process:**
        *   Unclear or poorly defined procedures.
        *   Inconsistent application of methods.
        *   Outdated or inefficient processes.
        *   Variations in environmental conditions affecting the process.
        *   *Example:* A cooking recipe that doesn't specify precise ingredient quantities or cooking times can lead to highly variable taste and texture.

    *   **Measurement:**
        *   Inaccurate or imprecise measurement tools.
        *   Operator bias in reading measurements.
        *   Lack of standard operating procedures for measurements.
        *   Environmental factors affecting measurement accuracy.
        *   *Example:* Using a worn-out tape measure to measure lengths will introduce errors and variations in reported dimensions.

    *   **Environment:**
        *   Fluctuations in temperature, humidity, or pressure.
        *   Vibrations.
        *   Lighting conditions.
        *   Noise levels.
        *   *Example:* A manufacturing process sensitive to temperature might produce different results on a hot day versus a cold day.

### 2. Types of Variation (LO2)

**Key Concept:** Understanding the *type* of variation helps in determining the appropriate approach for its control and reduction.

*   **Common Cause Variation (or Chance Variation, Random Variation):**
    *   **Definition:** Variation that is inherent in the process and is due to the combined effect of many small, uncontrollable factors. It is the natural, expected variation of a stable process.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Distributed around the process average.
        *   Difficult to pinpoint a single cause.
        *   Requires process improvement (systemic changes) to reduce.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   Slight differences in the time it takes for different customers to complete a transaction at a checkout counter.
        *   Minor fluctuations in the diameter of a wire produced by a well-functioning machine.
    *   **Textbook Reference:** Montgomery (Introduction to Statistical Quality Control) extensively discusses common cause variation in the context of control charts. He emphasizes that these are the variations a stable process exhibits.

*   **Special Cause Variation (or Assignable Cause Variation, Non-Random Variation):**
    *   **Definition:** Variation that arises from specific, identifiable factors that are not part of the normal process. These are often sudden, unexpected shifts or patterns in the process.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Often appears as shifts, trends, or outliers on control charts.
        *   Can be investigated and eliminated by identifying and correcting the specific cause.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   A machine breakdown causing a sudden drop in production output.
        *   A new, untrained operator starting a shift, leading to increased errors.
        *   A power surge affecting a critical piece of equipment.
    *   **Textbook Reference:** Montgomery also highlights special cause variation, noting that their presence indicates an out-of-control process. Identifying and eliminating these causes is a primary goal of statistical process control.

**Relationship to Quality:**
*   A process exhibiting only common cause variation is considered **in statistical control**. While there's variation, it's predictable.
*   A process exhibiting special cause variation is considered **out of statistical control**, meaning its behavior is unpredictable and requires immediate attention.
*   **Goal of TQM:** To move towards a process that is only influenced by common causes and to reduce the magnitude of common cause variation (Besterfield et al., 2018).

### 3. Applying the Affinity Diagram to Categorize Causes of Variation (LO3)

**Key Concept:** The Affinity Diagram is a tool used to organize a large number of ideas, opinions, or data points into groups based on their natural relationships. It's particularly useful for brainstorming and structuring complex issues like the causes of variation.

**Description of Affinity Diagram:**
*   Also known as the KJ method (after its creator, Jiro Kawakita).
*   A visual tool for organizing ideas.
*   Helps to reveal underlying patterns and relationships among seemingly unrelated ideas.
*   Useful for complex problems with many potential causes.

**Steps to Create an Affinity Diagram for Causes of Variation:**

1.  **Brainstorm Causes:** Gather a team and brainstorm all possible causes of variation for a specific problem or process. Encourage everyone to contribute freely without judgment. Write each cause on a separate sticky note or card.
    *   *Example:* If the problem is "variation in the thickness of a manufactured sheet of plastic," brainstormed causes could include: "machine speed fluctuations," "material batch inconsistency," "operator adjustment frequency," "humidity levels," "tool wear," "inaccurate thickness gauge," "operator fatigue," "incorrect processing temperature," "cleaning schedule," etc.

2.  **Initial Grouping (Silent Sort):** Spread out all the sticky notes. Team members, working silently, begin to group related ideas together. Move the notes around until natural clusters emerge.
    *   **Important:** Avoid talking during this phase to prevent premature convergence and encourage individual thought.

3.  **Create Header Cards:** Once the notes are grouped, create a concise header card for each group. The header should capture the essence of the ideas within that group.
    *   *Example Header Cards:* "Machine Settings," "Material Properties," "Environmental Factors," "Operator Skill," "Measurement Issues."

4.  **Refine and Discuss:** Discuss the groupings and header cards as a team. Ensure everyone agrees on the placement of notes and the clarity of the headers. Reorganize if necessary.

5.  **Structure and Analysis:** The Affinity Diagram visually organizes the causes of variation. This structured view helps in:
    *   Identifying the most significant categories of causes.
    *   Prioritizing areas for further investigation and action.
    *   Facilitating communication and consensus among team members.

**Textbook Reference:** Besterfield et al. (2018) likely covers the Affinity Diagram as one of the "Seven Management and Planning Tools" or as a core TQM tool. They would emphasize its role in problem-solving and data organization. SubburajRamasamy (2017) might also detail its application in process improvement.

### 4. Relationship between Variation, Quality, and Customer Satisfaction (LO4)

**Key Concept:** Reducing variation is intrinsically linked to improving quality and, consequently, customer satisfaction.

*   **Variation and Quality:**
    *   **Definition of Quality:** Often defined as "fitness for use" or "conformance to requirements."
    *   **Impact of Variation:**
        *   **Increased Defects:** High variation means more products or services falling outside acceptable specifications, leading to defects.
        *   **Inconsistency:** Customers experience unpredictable performance, leading to dissatisfaction.
        *   **Rework and Scrap:** Defects often require rework or result in scrapped products, increasing costs.
        *   **Unpredictability:** Processes with high variation are difficult to manage and control, making it hard to meet targets reliably.
    *   **Textbook Reference:** Juran and Gryna (2004) in "Quality Planning and Analysis" discuss the cost of poor quality (COPQ), which is directly influenced by variation. Crosby (1979) in "Quality is Free" champions the idea that preventing defects (i.e., reducing variation) is more cost-effective than dealing with them.

*   **Variation and Customer Satisfaction:**
    *   **Customer Expectations:** Customers expect products and services to perform consistently and reliably as advertised.
    *   **Impact of Variation:**
        *   **Unmet Expectations:** Variation leads to products that don't perform as expected, causing disappointment.
        *   **Loss of Trust:** Inconsistent quality erodes customer trust and loyalty.
        *   **Negative Word-of-Mouth:** Dissatisfied customers are likely to share their negative experiences.
        *   **Competitive Disadvantage:** Competitors with more consistent quality can gain market share.
    *   **Example:** A restaurant that aims for consistent taste and service. If one day the soup is too salty, the next day the service is slow, and the day after the steak is overcooked, customers will not be satisfied and will likely go elsewhere. Reducing variation in recipes, service times, and cooking procedures is key to consistent customer satisfaction.
    *   **Textbook Reference:** Besterfield et al. (2018) and SubburajRamasamy (2017) will undoubtedly link TQM principles, including variation reduction, directly to achieving customer satisfaction.

**Important Point to Remember:** The ultimate goal of managing variation is not just to make things more predictable, but to deliver consistent, high-quality products and services that delight customers.

## Practice Questions and Exercises

**Instructions:** Attempt the following questions to test your understanding.

**Question 1 (LO1, LO2):**
Differentiate between common cause variation and special cause variation. Provide an example for each in the context of manufacturing a smartphone screen.

**Answer:**
*   **Common Cause Variation:** This is inherent, predictable variation present in a stable process. It's due to the cumulative effect of many small, often unidentifiable factors.
    *   *Example (Smartphone Screen):* Minor, random fluctuations in the etching process that result in microscopic variations in the surface smoothness of the glass, within an acceptable range.
*   **Special Cause Variation:** This is unpredictable variation due to specific, identifiable factors that are not part of the normal process. Its presence indicates an out-of-control process.
    *   *Example (Smartphone Screen):* A sudden surge in voltage during the deposition of a conductive layer, causing that specific batch of screens to have an abnormally high resistance.

**Question 2 (LO3):**
Imagine you are part of a team trying to understand the causes of variation in customer wait times at a bank. List five potential causes of variation and then group them using hypothetical Affinity Diagram header cards.

**Answer:**
**Potential Causes (Brainstormed):**
1.  Customer arriving at peak times
2.  Staff calling in sick
3.  Slow cashiers
4.  Complex transactions taking longer
5.  ATM machine down, forcing more customers inside
6.  Opening a new account process is lengthy
7.  Teller system freezing momentarily
8.  Customers not having necessary documents ready
9.  Bank holidays leading to longer queues
10. Staff training sessions causing reduced staffing

**Hypothetical Affinity Diagram Grouping:**

*   **Header: Customer Behavior**
    *   Customer arriving at peak times
    *   Customers not having necessary documents ready
*   **Header: Staffing & Performance**
    *   Staff calling in sick
    *   Slow cashiers
    *   Staff training sessions causing reduced staffing
*   **Header: Process Complexity**
    *   Complex transactions taking longer
    *   Opening a new account process is lengthy
*   **Header: System/Technology Issues**
    *   Teller system freezing momentarily
*   **Header: External Factors/Demand**
    *   ATM machine down, forcing more customers inside
    *   Bank holidays leading to longer queues

**Question 3 (LO4):**
Explain how reducing variation in a software development process can improve customer satisfaction.

**Answer:**
Reducing variation in software development can improve customer satisfaction by:
1.  **Increased Reliability:** Less variation means fewer bugs and errors in the final software. This leads to a more stable and reliable product that performs as expected, meeting customer needs consistently.
2.  **Predictable Performance:** Consistent development processes lead to predictable delivery times and consistent feature quality. Customers appreciate timely delivery and features that work as advertised.
3.  **Reduced Rework/Fixes:** When development variations cause defects, customers often have to wait for patches or fixes. Reducing these variations minimizes the need for post-release rework, leading to happier customers.
4.  **Better User Experience:** Consistent adherence to design standards and user interface guidelines, achieved by controlling development variation, results in a more intuitive and pleasant user experience.
5.  **Cost-Effectiveness (Indirectly for Customer):** While primarily a benefit to the organization, reduced rework and fewer defects mean the company can invest more in innovation or keep prices competitive, indirectly benefiting the customer.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Variation is inevitable**, but excessive or uncontrolled variation is the enemy of quality.
*   **Identify the source and type of variation** to apply the correct control and improvement strategies.
*   The **Affinity Diagram** is a powerful tool for structuring complex problems like identifying the myriad causes of variation.
*   **Reducing variation directly leads to higher quality and greater customer satisfaction.**
*   **Statistical Quality Control (SQC)** techniques (like control charts, discussed in later modules) are used to monitor and manage variation.
