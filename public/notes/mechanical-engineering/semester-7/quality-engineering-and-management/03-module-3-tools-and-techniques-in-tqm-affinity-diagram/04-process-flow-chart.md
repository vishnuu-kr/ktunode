### **1. Introduction to Process Flowcharts**

A process flowchart is a graphical representation of a sequence of operations, decisions, and activities within a process. It provides a visual map of how work is done, from start to finish. In Total Quality Management (TQM), process flowcharts are fundamental tools for understanding, analyzing, and improving processes.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Process:** A series of interrelated or interacting activities that transform inputs into outputs.
*   **Flowchart:** A diagram that uses standardized symbols to represent the steps, decisions, and flow of a process.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO3: Implement different tools and techniques in TQM (Knowledge Level: K3)** - Understanding and creating flowcharts is a core skill in implementing TQM techniques.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Besterfield et al., Fifth Edition, 2018:** Emphasizes flowcharts as a primary tool for process analysis and improvement in TQM. (Chapter 4: Tools and Techniques)
*   **SubburajRamasamy, First Edition, 2017:** Highlights flowcharts for visualizing process steps and identifying potential bottlenecks. (Chapter 5: Tools for Quality Improvement)

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Flowcharts are powerful communication tools.
*   They help in identifying areas for improvement and standardization.
*   They are essential for understanding the "as-is" state of a process before designing a "to-be" state.

### **2. Purpose and Benefits of Process Flowcharts in TQM**

Process flowcharts are utilized in TQM to achieve several critical objectives:

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Process Understanding:** Gain a clear, shared understanding of how a process actually works, not just how it's supposed to work.
*   **Process Analysis:** Identify inefficiencies, redundancies, bottlenecks, and areas of waste within a process.
*   **Process Improvement:** Serve as a basis for brainstorming and implementing solutions to enhance efficiency, effectiveness, and customer satisfaction.
*   **Standardization:** Establish a clear and consistent method for performing tasks.
*   **Training:** Provide a visual aid for training new employees on process procedures.
*   **Problem Solving:** Help in pinpointing the root causes of problems by tracing the process steps.

**Benefits:**

*   **Clarity and Simplicity:** Makes complex processes easier to understand.
*   **Identification of Bottlenecks:** Visually highlights steps that slow down the entire process.
*   **Reduction of Waste:** Helps in eliminating unnecessary steps or activities.
*   **Improved Communication:** Facilitates discussion and agreement among team members about a process.
*   **Enhanced Decision Making:** Provides data and context for making informed decisions about process changes.
*   **Foundation for Further Analysis:** Can be used in conjunction with other TQM tools like Pareto charts or cause-and-effect diagrams.

**Examples:**

*   **Manufacturing:** Mapping the steps involved in assembling a product to identify delays or quality control points.
*   **Service Industry:** Documenting the customer service interaction process to find areas for faster resolution or better customer experience.
*   **Administrative Processes:** Visualizing the steps in an invoice processing system to identify delays in payment.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Mitra, Second Edition, 1998:** Discusses flowcharts as a foundational tool for quality improvement by providing a visual roadmap. (Chapter 3: Basic Tools of Quality)
*   **Juran & Gryna, Third Edition, 2004:** Views flowcharts as essential for understanding the "process design" and its subsequent control. (Chapter 9: Process Control)

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO3: Implement different tools and techniques in TQM (Knowledge Level: K3)** - Demonstrating the practical application of flowcharts for quality improvement.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The primary goal is to understand and improve the process, not just to create a diagram.
*   Involve the people who actually perform the process when creating the flowchart.

### **3. Standard Flowchart Symbols**

Standardized symbols ensure that flowcharts are universally understood.

**Key Concepts & Symbols:**

*   **Terminator (Oval/Rounded Rectangle):** Indicates the start or end of a process.
    *   *Example:* "Start Order Processing", "End of Invoice".
*   **Process (Rectangle):** Represents a single step or action in the process.
    *   *Example:* "Receive Customer Order", "Inspect Component".
*   **Decision (Diamond):** Represents a point where a decision must be made, usually with "Yes" or "No" branches.
    *   *Example:* "Is Component within Tolerance?", "Is Order Complete?".
*   **Input/Output (Parallelogram):** Represents data or material entering or leaving the process.
    *   *Example:* "Customer Order Form", "Shipping Label".
*   **Connector (Circle):** Used to connect different parts of a flowchart, especially when it spans multiple pages or sections.
    *   *Example:* Connecting a decision branch to a later process step.
*   **Document (Rectangle with wavy bottom):** Represents a document or report generated or used in the process.
    *   *Example:* "Purchase Order", "Quality Report".
*   **Predefined Process (Rectangle with double vertical lines):** Represents a sub-process that is defined elsewhere.
    *   *Example:* "Perform Calibration" (if calibration itself has a detailed flowchart).
*   **Arrow/Flowline (Line with arrowhead):** Indicates the direction of flow through the process.

**Example Illustration:**

```
+-----------------+      +-----------------+      +-----------------+
|   Start (Oval)  |----->|   Process 1     |----->|  Decision? (D)  |
+-----------------+      |   (Rectangle)   |      +--------+--------+
                          +-----------------+               |
                                                              | Yes
                                                              v
                                                     +-----------------+
                                                     |   Process 2     |
                                                     |   (Rectangle)   |
                                                     +-----------------+
                                                              |
                                                              v
                                                     +-----------------+
                                                     |    End (Oval)   |
                                                     +-----------------+
```

*   **Besterfield et al., Fifth Edition, 2018:** Provides a comprehensive list and explanation of common flowchart symbols. (Chapter 4: Tools and Techniques)

### **4. Types of Process Flowcharts**

Different types of flowcharts are suited for various purposes.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Simple/Basic Flowchart:** The most common type, showing the sequence of major steps.
    *   *Purpose:* To understand the overall flow.
    *   *Example:* A high-level overview of a customer order fulfillment process.
*   **Detailed Flowchart (Cross-Functional or Swimlane Flowchart):** Shows steps, decisions, and also assigns responsibility for each step to different individuals or departments.
    *   *Purpose:* To understand interactions between different roles and identify handoff issues.
    *   *Example:* Mapping a customer complaint resolution process, showing steps handled by Customer Support, Technical Team, and Billing Department.
*   **Detailed Process Flowchart:** Provides a very granular view of a process, including every minor step and sub-step.
    *   *Purpose:* To identify very specific areas for improvement or to document a highly precise procedure.
    *   *Example:* Documenting the exact sequence of steps for operating a complex piece of machinery.
*   **Workflow Diagram:** Focuses on the flow of information or tasks, often used in service or administrative processes.

**Example Illustration (Swimlane Flowchart Concept):**

| Department A  | Department B  | Department C  |
| :------------ | :------------ | :------------ |
| Start Order   | Process Order | Ship Product  |
|               |               |               |
| Customer      | Warehouse     | Logistics     |
| Receives      | Fulfills      | Carrier       |
| Order         | Order         |               |

**(Note: This is a conceptual representation. Actual swimlane charts would use standard flowchart symbols within lanes.)**

**Textbook References:**

*   **Besterfield et al., Fifth Edition, 2018:** Discusses the utility of cross-functional flowcharts for analyzing interdepartmental processes. (Chapter 4: Tools and Techniques)
*   **SubburajRamasamy, First Edition, 2017:** Introduces workflow diagrams as a way to visualize information flow. (Chapter 5: Tools for Quality Improvement)

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO3: Implement different tools and techniques in TQM (Knowledge Level: K3)** - Selecting the appropriate flowchart type for a given analysis.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Choose the flowchart type that best suits the objective of the analysis.
*   Swimlane flowcharts are particularly useful for identifying process handoffs and departmental responsibilities.

### **5. Steps to Create a Process Flowchart**

Creating an effective flowchart involves a systematic approach.

**Key Steps:**

1.  **Identify the Process:** Clearly define the specific process you want to chart. What is its beginning and end?
    *   *Example:* "Order Entry Process", "New Employee Onboarding".
2.  **Gather Information:** Talk to people who actually perform the process. Observe the steps involved. Collect relevant documents.
    *   *Tip:* Use interviews, questionnaires, or direct observation.
3.  **Sequence the Steps:** List all the activities, decisions, inputs, and outputs in the order they occur.
4.  **Select Flowchart Symbols:** Choose the appropriate symbols for each step identified.
5.  **Draw the Flowchart:**
    *   Start with the "Start" terminator.
    *   Use arrows to connect the steps, showing the flow.
    *   Ensure a logical progression.
    *   Use decision diamonds for all points where choices are made.
    *   Clearly label each symbol with a concise description of the step.
    *   End with the "End" terminator.
6.  **Review and Validate:** Share the draft flowchart with the process participants and stakeholders. Ask for feedback to ensure accuracy and completeness.
7.  **Refine and Finalize:** Make necessary corrections based on the review. The final flowchart should be clear, accurate, and easy to understand.
8.  **Use the Flowchart:** Implement the analysis and improvement actions based on the finalized flowchart.

**Examples:**

*   **Creating a "Request for Proposal (RFP) Process" Flowchart:**
    1.  **Identify:** The process of creating and sending out an RFP.
    2.  **Gather Info:** Interview the purchasing department and legal team.
    3.  **Sequence:** Draft RFP -> Legal Review -> Budget Approval -> Issue RFP -> Receive Bids -> Evaluate Bids -> Select Vendor.
    4.  **Symbols:** Use rectangles for drafting/evaluating, diamonds for approvals, parallelograms for RFP document.
    5.  **Draw:** Connect steps with arrows.
    6.  **Review:** Show to procurement manager and legal counsel.
    7.  **Refine:** Add a step for "Internal Stakeholder Feedback" if missed.
    8.  **Use:** Identify a bottleneck in the legal review stage.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Mitra, Second Edition, 1998:** Outlines a step-by-step guide to constructing effective flowcharts for problem-solving. (Chapter 3: Basic Tools of Quality)
*   **Besterfield et al., Fifth Edition, 2018:** Provides a systematic approach to flowchart creation and its role in process improvement. (Chapter 4: Tools and Techniques)

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO3: Implement different tools and techniques in TQM (Knowledge Level: K3)** - Applying the practical steps to create and utilize a flowchart.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Accuracy is paramount. An inaccurate flowchart is worse than no flowchart.
*   Keep it simple enough to be understood, but detailed enough to be useful.
*   Involve the "doers" of the process; they have the most accurate knowledge.

### **6. Applications of Process Flowcharts in TQM**

Flowcharts are versatile and can be applied across various aspects of TQM.

**Key Applications:**

*   **Process Improvement Projects (e.g., Six Sigma, Lean):** Essential for defining the current state (SIPOC analysis often leads to a flowchart) and identifying opportunities for reduction in variation and waste.
*   **Quality Control:** Visualizing quality checks at different stages of a process.
*   **Risk Management:** Identifying potential failure points or risks within a process.
*   **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs):** Documenting how specific tasks should be performed consistently.
*   **Root Cause Analysis:** Tracing the process to pinpoint where problems originate.
*   **Customer Journey Mapping:** Understanding the customer's experience through various touchpoints.
*   **New Product Development:** Mapping the stages from concept to launch.

**Examples:**

*   **Lean Manufacturing:** A flowchart of a production line might reveal excessive inventory buildup (WIP) at a certain station, leading to a Lean initiative to reduce WIP.
*   **Healthcare:** A flowchart of patient admission might identify delays in physician consults, prompting changes to improve patient flow and reduce wait times.
*   **Software Development:** A flowchart of the bug fixing process could highlight long turnaround times between bug identification and resolution, leading to process optimization.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Montgomery, Third Edition, 1997:** Links process charting to statistical process control (SPC) by visualizing the steps where control measures are applied. (Chapter 2: Introduction to Statistical Process Control)
*   **Juran & Gryna, Third Edition, 2004:** Positions flowcharts as a tool for process management and control, crucial for achieving quality goals. (Chapter 9: Process Control)
*   **Crosby, 1979:** While not explicitly detailing flowcharts, Crosby's philosophy of "Quality is Free" implies that efficient, well-defined processes (visualized by flowcharts) lead to reduced costs of poor quality.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO1: Develop knowledge of quality management and contributions of quality gurus. (Knowledge Level: K2)** - Understanding how TQM principles are put into practice using tools like flowcharts.
*   **CO3: Implement different tools and techniques in TQM (Knowledge Level: K3)** - Demonstrating the broad applicability of flowcharts in TQM initiatives.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Flowcharts are not static; they should be updated as processes evolve.
*   The insights gained from a flowchart should drive action for improvement.

### **7. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**

What is the primary purpose of a process flowchart in TQM?
a) To document employee performance
b) To visualize the sequence of steps in a process for analysis and improvement
c) To create marketing materials
d) To manage financial accounts

**Answer:** b) To visualize the sequence of steps in a process for analysis and improvement

**Question 2:**

Which symbol is used to represent a decision point in a flowchart?
a) Rectangle
b) Oval
c) Diamond
d) Parallelogram

**Answer:** c) Diamond

**Question 3:**

You are asked to map the process of handling customer complaints within your organization. Different departments (Customer Service, Technical Support, Billing) are involved, and each handles specific steps. What type of flowchart would be most effective for this scenario? Explain why.

**Answer:** A **Cross-Functional Flowchart (Swimlane Flowchart)** would be most effective. This is because it clearly delineates the responsibilities of each department involved in the process, making it easy to identify where handoffs occur, potential delays between departments, and who is accountable for each step. This is crucial for understanding and improving interdepartmental processes.

**Question 4:**

List three benefits of using process flowcharts in a TQM initiative.

**Answer:**
1.  **Improved Process Understanding:** Provides a clear, shared view of how a process works.
2.  **Identification of Inefficiencies:** Helps in spotting bottlenecks, redundancies, and waste.
3.  **Facilitates Communication:** Serves as a common language for discussing process improvements among stakeholders.
    *(Other valid answers include: Standardization, basis for training, aids in problem-solving, etc.)*

**Question 5 (Scenario-Based Exercise):**

Imagine a simple process for submitting a leave request:

1.  Employee fills out a leave request form.
2.  Employee submits the form to their manager.
3.  Manager reviews the request.
4.  If approved, manager signs the form.
5.  If not approved, manager informs the employee.
6.  Approved form is sent to HR.
7.  HR records the leave.
8.  Employee is informed of the approved leave.

**Task:** Create a basic process flowchart for this scenario using standard symbols (you can describe the symbols and their connections).

**Answer (Description of Flowchart):**

*   **Start:** Oval labeled "Start Leave Request"
*   **Process:** Rectangle labeled "Employee Fills Leave Form"
*   **Process:** Rectangle labeled "Employee Submits Form to Manager"
*   **Process:** Rectangle labeled "Manager Reviews Request"
*   **Decision:** Diamond labeled "Is Leave Approved?"
    *   **No Branch:** Arrow to Process (Rectangle) labeled "Manager Informs Employee of Rejection" -> **End:** Oval labeled "End Process"
    *   **Yes Branch:** Arrow to Process (Rectangle) labeled "Manager Signs Form"
*   **Process:** Rectangle labeled "Approved Form Sent to HR"
*   **Process:** Rectangle labeled "HR Records Leave"
*   **Process:** Rectangle labeled "Employee Informed of Approval"
*   **End:** Oval labeled "End Process"

### **8. Conclusion and Key Takeaways**

Process flowcharts are indispensable tools in the TQM toolkit. They provide the foundational understanding necessary for any effective process analysis and improvement effort. By systematically mapping out how work is done, organizations can uncover opportunities to enhance efficiency, reduce errors, and ultimately deliver higher quality products and services.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Visualize Everything:** Flowcharts translate abstract processes into tangible visual representations.
*   **Teamwork is Key:** Involve process users for accuracy and buy-in.
*   **Focus on Improvement:** The flowchart is a means to an end – process improvement.
*   **Standardization Matters:** Use standard symbols for universal understanding.
