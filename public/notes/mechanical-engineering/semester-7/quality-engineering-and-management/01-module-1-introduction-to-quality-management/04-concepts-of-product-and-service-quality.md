# Quality Engineering and Management: Module 1 - Introduction to Quality Management
## Topic: Concepts of Product and Service Quality

This module introduces the fundamental concepts of quality, focusing on the distinction and interrelationships between product and service quality. Understanding these concepts is crucial for any organization aiming for excellence in its offerings and customer satisfaction.

---

### 1. Introduction to Quality

**Definition of Quality:**
*   The term "quality" is often subjective and can be defined in various ways depending on the context and perspective. However, a universally accepted approach is to view quality as the **degree to which a set of inherent characteristics of an object fulfills requirements** (ISO 9000:2015).
*   **Requirements** refer to needs or expectations that are stated, generally implied, or obligatory.
*   **Inherent characteristics** are properties that distinguish an object from other objects.

**Key Aspects of Quality:**
*   **Customer Focus:** Quality is ultimately defined by the customer's perception and satisfaction. What meets customer needs is high quality.
*   **Conformance to Requirements:** Products and services should meet specified standards, designs, and manufacturing processes.
*   **Fitness for Use:** Products and services should perform their intended function effectively and efficiently.
*   **Value for Money:** Customers often consider the price in relation to the perceived quality.
*   **Perfection/Excellence:** Striving for zero defects and continuous improvement.

---

### 2. Concepts of Product Quality

**Definition of Product Quality:**
Product quality refers to the characteristics of a tangible good or manufactured item that determine its ability to satisfy stated or implied needs.

**Key Dimensions of Product Quality (Based on Garvin's Dimensions - Besterfield et al.):**
Besterfield et al. (2018) often refer to David Garvin's seminal work on product quality, which outlines eight key dimensions:

*   **Performance:** The primary operating characteristics of a product.
    *   *Example:* A car's acceleration, fuel efficiency, or handling capabilities.
*   **Features:** The secondary characteristics that supplement the product's basic function.
    *   *Example:* For a smartphone, features include camera quality, storage capacity, or fingerprint scanner.
*   **Reliability:** The probability that a product will perform without failure for a specified period under given conditions.
    *   *Example:* The likelihood of a light bulb burning out within its advertised lifespan.
*   **Conformance:** The degree to which a product's design and operating characteristics meet established standards.
    *   *Example:* A manufactured bolt meeting precise dimensional specifications.
*   **Durability:** The amount of use one gets from a product before it deteriorates or breaks down.
    *   *Example:* The lifespan of a washing machine before requiring major repairs.
*   **Serviceability:** The ease, speed, competence, and courtesy with which a product can be repaired.
    *   *Example:* Availability of spare parts and skilled technicians for a refrigerator.
*   **Aesthetics:** The look, feel, sound, taste, and smell of a product.
    *   *Example:* The design, color, and texture of a piece of furniture.
*   **Perceived Quality:** The customer's subjective judgment about a product's overall excellence or superiority.
    *   *Example:* Brand reputation, customer reviews, and marketing influence perception of quality.

**Other Important Product Quality Concepts:**
*   **Design Quality:** The degree to which the design of a product meets customer needs and expectations.
*   **Manufacturing Quality:** The degree to which the product is manufactured according to its design specifications.

### 3. Concepts of Service Quality

**Definition of Service Quality:**
Service quality refers to the customer's judgment of the overall excellence or superiority of a service. It is often perceived as a gap between customer expectations and their perceptions of the service received.

**Key Dimensions of Service Quality (SERVQUAL Model - Parasuraman, Zeithaml, and Berry, as referenced in Besterfield et al. and Subburaj):**
The SERVQUAL model is a widely recognized framework for measuring service quality. It identifies five key dimensions:

*   **Tangibles:** The appearance of physical facilities, equipment, personnel, and communication materials.
    *   *Example:* The cleanliness of a restaurant, the professionalism of a bank teller's uniform, the website's appearance.
*   **Reliability:** The ability to perform the promised service dependably and accurately.
    *   *Example:* An airline delivering luggage to the correct destination on time, a utility company providing consistent service.
*   **Responsiveness:** The willingness to help customers and to provide prompt service.
    *   *Example:* A hotel quickly responding to a guest's room service request, a customer service representative answering calls promptly.
*   **Assurance:** The knowledge and courtesy of employees and their ability to convey trust and confidence.
    *   *Example:* A doctor explaining a medical procedure clearly, a financial advisor demonstrating expertise.
*   **Empathy:** The caring, individualized attention provided to customers.
    *   *Example:* A retail associate remembering a customer's name and preferences, a teacher providing personalized feedback.

**Challenges in Measuring Service Quality:**
*   **Intangibility:** Services cannot be seen, touched, or held before purchase.
*   **Inseparability:** Services are often produced and consumed simultaneously, involving the customer in the process.
*   **Variability:** The quality of service can vary significantly depending on who provides it, when, and how.
*   **Perishability:** Services cannot be stored for later sale or use.

**The Service Quality Gap (Gaps Model):**
Subburaj (2017) and Besterfield et al. (2018) often discuss the "Gaps Model" of service quality. This model highlights potential discrepancies between customer expectations and their perceptions. The key gaps include:

*   **The Listening Gap:** The gap between what customers want and what management perceives customers want. (Caused by inadequate market research, lack of upward communication.)
*   **The Product/Service Design Gap:** The gap between management's perception of customer wants and the actual design or specification of the service. (Caused by incorrect service design, inadequate standards.)
*   **The Service Performance Gap:** The gap between service quality specifications and the actual delivery of the service. (Caused by inability to perform, wrong service delivery, inconsistencies.)
*   **The Communication Gap:** The gap between the service delivered and what is communicated to customers about the service. (Caused by overpromising in advertising, poor internal communication.)
*   **The Expected Service vs. Perceived Service Gap (The Customer Gap):** This is the ultimate gap, representing the difference between what customers expect and what they perceive they have received. All other gaps contribute to this one.

### 4. Relationship Between Product and Service Quality

*   **Interdependence:** In many industries, products and services are closely intertwined. The quality of the service often influences the perception of product quality, and vice-versa.
    *   *Example:* Buying a high-tech gadget (product) might be overshadowed by poor technical support (service). Conversely, excellent after-sales service can enhance the perceived value of a product.
*   **Customer Experience:** Both product and service quality contribute to the overall customer experience. Organizations need to manage both aspects effectively.
*   **Total Quality Management (TQM):** TQM principles apply to both product and service quality. The focus is on continuous improvement across all aspects of an organization's operations.

---

### 5. Quality Gurus and their Contributions (Relates to CO1)

While this topic focuses on the concepts of product and service quality, it's important to acknowledge that early quality thinkers laid the groundwork for understanding and managing quality.

*   **W. Edwards Deming:** Emphasized a system approach to quality, the importance of statistical process control (SPC), and continuous improvement (PDCA cycle). His philosophy is central to TQM.
*   **Joseph M. Juran:** Defined quality as "fitness for use." He introduced the concept of the "trilogy" (quality planning, quality control, and quality improvement) and stressed the importance of quality costs. (Juran & Gryna, 2004)
*   **Philip B. Crosby:** Advocated for "Quality is Free" and emphasized preventing defects rather than inspecting them out. He introduced the concept of "zero defects." (Crosby, 1979)
*   **Kaoru Ishikawa:** Developed the Ishikawa diagram (cause-and-effect diagram) and promoted the use of quality circles. He also emphasized the customer as the ultimate judge of quality.

---

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   Quality is customer-centric.
*   Product quality is defined by inherent characteristics, while service quality is often a perception based on customer expectations and experiences.
*   Garvin's dimensions are key for understanding product quality.
*   The SERVQUAL model provides a framework for understanding service quality.
*   The Service Quality Gap model highlights challenges in service delivery.
*   Product and service quality are often interdependent and contribute to the overall customer experience.
*   Understanding historical quality thinking (gurus) provides context for modern quality management.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Multiple Choice Questions:**

1.  Which of the following is NOT one of David Garvin's dimensions of product quality?
    a) Performance
    b) Responsiveness
    c) Reliability
    d) Aesthetics
    **Answer:** b) Responsiveness (This is a dimension of service quality)

2.  According to the SERVQUAL model, the appearance of physical facilities and equipment falls under which dimension?
    a) Reliability
    b) Assurance
    c) Tangibles
    d) Empathy
    **Answer:** c) Tangibles

3.  The probability that a product will perform without failure for a specified period is known as:
    a) Durability
    b) Reliability
    c) Conformance
    d) Serviceability
    **Answer:** b) Reliability

**Short Answer Questions:**

1.  Define quality from a customer's perspective.
    **Answer:** Quality from a customer's perspective is the degree to which a product or service meets or exceeds their expectations and fulfills their needs. It is a subjective judgment based on their experience and perception.

2.  Explain the difference between product quality and service quality.
    **Answer:** Product quality refers to the characteristics of a tangible good that determine its ability to satisfy needs, often measurable by features, performance, and conformance. Service quality, on the other hand, refers to the customer's perception of a service's excellence, typically evaluated through dimensions like tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy, and is often characterized by gaps between expectations and perceptions.

3.  Briefly describe two of the SERVQUAL dimensions.
    **Answer:**
    *   **Responsiveness:** The willingness to help customers and to provide prompt service. This involves timeliness and a proactive approach to customer needs.
    *   **Empathy:** The caring, individualized attention provided to customers. This means understanding the customer's situation and showing genuine concern.

**Application/Scenario-Based Question:**

Consider a scenario where a customer buys a new smartphone.

a) Identify at least three dimensions of **product quality** relevant to this purchase.
b) Imagine the customer needs technical support after purchase. Identify at least three dimensions of **service quality** that would impact their satisfaction with the support experience.

**Answer (Example):**

a) **Product Quality Dimensions for a Smartphone:**
    *   **Performance:** How fast the processor is, battery life, screen responsiveness.
    *   **Features:** Camera megapixels, storage capacity, biometric security (fingerprint scanner, face ID).
    *   **Perceived Quality:** Brand reputation, design aesthetics, positive reviews.

b) **Service Quality Dimensions for Technical Support:**
    *   **Responsiveness:** How quickly the support representative answers the call or resolves the issue.
    *   **Assurance:** The technical knowledge and confidence of the support representative, making the customer feel their problem will be solved.
    *   **Empathy:** The support representative showing understanding of the customer's frustration and providing personalized assistance.
