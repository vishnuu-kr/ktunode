### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Compressor:** The heart of a vapor-compression refrigeration system. It is a device that receives low-pressure, low-temperature refrigerant vapor from the evaporator and compresses it into a high-pressure, high-temperature vapor, which is then discharged to the condenser.
*   **Work of Compression:** The energy required to increase the pressure of the refrigerant vapor. This is a significant factor in the overall energy consumption of the refrigeration system.
*   **Volumetric Efficiency:** The ratio of the actual volume of refrigerant vapor delivered by the compressor to its swept volume. It accounts for losses due to re-expansion of clearance volume, leakage, and throttling.
*   **Isentropic Efficiency:** The ratio of the work required for isentropic compression to the actual work required for compression. It indicates how close the compressor's performance is to an ideal isentropic process.
*   **Refrigeration Capacity:** The rate at which heat is removed from the refrigerated space.
*   **COP (Coefficient of Performance):** The ratio of the desired output (refrigeration capacity) to the required input (work of compression).

### Types of Compressors:

Compressors are broadly classified based on their working principle. The most common types found in refrigeration and air conditioning systems are:

**1. Reciprocating Compressors**

*   **Working Principle:** These compressors operate on the principle of positive displacement, similar to a piston in an engine cylinder. A piston moves back and forth within a cylinder, driven by a crankshaft.
    *   **Suction Stroke:** The piston moves away from the cylinder head, creating a low-pressure area. The suction valve opens, and refrigerant vapor from the evaporator enters the cylinder.
    *   **Compression Stroke:** The suction valve closes, and the piston moves towards the cylinder head, compressing the refrigerant vapor.
    *   **Discharge Stroke:** When the pressure in the cylinder exceeds the discharge pressure, the discharge valve opens, and the high-pressure, high-temperature vapor is pushed out into the condenser.
*   **Construction:**
    *   **Cylinder:** Houses the piston.
    *   **Piston:** Reciprocates within the cylinder.
    *   **Piston Rings:** Ensure a tight seal between the piston and cylinder.
    *   **Valves (Suction and Discharge):** Control the flow of refrigerant vapor into and out of the cylinder.
    *   **Connecting Rod:** Connects the piston to the crankshaft.
    *   **Crankshaft:** Converts rotary motion from the motor into reciprocating motion of the piston.
    *   **Crankcase:** Houses the crankshaft and lubrication system.
*   **Sub-types:**
    *   **Hermetically Sealed:** The motor and compressor are enclosed in a sealed casing. Commonly used in domestic refrigerators and small AC units.
    *   **Semi-Hermetic (or Accessible Hermetic):** The casing can be opened for servicing. Used in medium-sized commercial refrigeration.
    *   **Open-Type:** The motor is separate from the compressor and connected via a shaft and coupling. Used in larger industrial applications.
*   **Applications:** Domestic refrigerators, freezers, window air conditioners, split AC units, commercial refrigeration, some industrial applications.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   High volumetric efficiency.
    *   Good for a wide range of pressure ratios.
    *   Relatively simple design and robust construction.
    *   Can handle liquid slugging to some extent (depending on design).
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   More noise and vibration compared to other types.
    *   Higher maintenance requirements due to moving parts and valves.
    *   Limited to moderate capacities.
    *   Susceptible to wear and tear.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Arora C.P., Chapter on Compressors. Ramesh Chandra Arora, Chapter on Compressors.

---

**2. Rotary Compressors**

*   **Working Principle:** These compressors also work on the principle of positive displacement. They use rotating elements to trap and compress the refrigerant vapor.
*   **Sub-types:**
    *   **Rotary Vane Compressor:**
        *   **Construction:** Consists of a rotor with vanes that slide in and out of slots. The rotor is eccentrically mounted within a cylinder or casing. As the rotor turns, the vanes are pushed outwards by centrifugal force, forming expanding and contracting chambers between the rotor, vanes, and casing.
        *   **Working:** Refrigerant vapor enters the expanding chamber, is trapped, and then compressed as the chamber volume decreases due to the rotor's rotation and the movement of the vanes. The compressed vapor is then discharged.
        *   **Applications:** Window air conditioners, small split AC units, dehumidifiers.
        *   **Advantages:** Compact, low vibration, quiet operation, fewer moving parts than reciprocating.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Lower volumetric efficiency compared to reciprocating, sensitive to wear.
    *   **Scroll Compressor:**
        *   **Construction:** Consists of two interleaved spiral scrolls. One scroll is fixed (stationary), while the other is orbiting.
        *   **Working:** Refrigerant vapor enters the outer periphery of the fixed scroll. As the orbiting scroll moves, it forms pockets of refrigerant vapor between the two scrolls. These pockets move towards the center as they are compressed. The compressed vapor is discharged from a port at the center of the stationary scroll.
        *   **Applications:** Residential and commercial air conditioning systems, heat pumps.
        *   **Advantages:** High efficiency, quiet and smooth operation, low vibration, good reliability, fewer moving parts, can handle refrigerant/oil mixtures well.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Higher initial cost, sensitive to foreign particles, limited to certain pressure ratios.
    *   **Screw Compressor (or Rotary Screw Compressor):**
        *   **Construction:** Features two intermeshing helical rotors (male and female). The male rotor has external teeth, and the female rotor has internal teeth.
        *   **Working:** Refrigerant vapor enters the inlet at one end of the compressor. As the rotors turn, the meshing teeth create pockets that trap the vapor. The rotation of the rotors moves these pockets axially along the rotors, reducing their volume and thus compressing the vapor. The compressed vapor is discharged at the opposite end.
        *   **Applications:** Large commercial and industrial air conditioning, process cooling, chillers.
        *   **Advantages:** High capacity, continuous flow, good efficiency over a wide operating range, relatively quiet and low vibration, robust.
        *   **Disadvantages:** High initial cost, can be noisy if not properly designed, sensitive to liquid refrigerant entering the compressor.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Arora C.P., Chapter on Compressors. Ramesh Chandra Arora, Chapter on Compressors. Ahamadul Ameen, Chapter on Compressors.

---

**3. Centrifugal Compressors**

*   **Working Principle:** These are dynamic or radial compressors that utilize centrifugal force to increase the velocity and pressure of the refrigerant vapor.
    *   **Working:** Refrigerant vapor enters the compressor eye (center) of an impeller. The impeller rotates at high speed, imparting kinetic energy to the vapor, increasing its velocity. The high-velocity vapor then enters a diffuser, where its kinetic energy is converted into pressure energy.
*   **Construction:**
    *   **Impeller:** A rotating disc with vanes that impart velocity to the refrigerant.
    *   **Diffuser:** A stationary passage that converts velocity into pressure.
    *   **Casing:** Encloses the impeller and diffuser.
*   **Sub-types:**
    *   **Single-stage:** Uses one impeller.
    *   **Multi-stage:** Uses multiple impellers in series to achieve higher pressure ratios.
*   **Applications:** Large centrifugal chillers for air conditioning of large buildings, industrial process cooling.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   High capacity for a given size.
    *   Smooth and vibration-free operation.
    *   Low maintenance due to fewer moving parts.
    *   Can handle large volumes of vapor.
    *   Good for low-pressure ratios.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Low efficiency at part load conditions.
    *   Susceptible to surging (a condition where flow becomes unstable).
    *   Requires a starter motor to bring the impeller up to speed before refrigerant flow starts.
    *   Cannot handle liquid refrigerant.
    *   Not suitable for low evaporation temperatures.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Arora C.P., Chapter on Compressors. Ramesh Chandra Arora, Chapter on Compressors. W P Jones, Chapter on Compressors.

---

**4. Other Types (Less Common in Standard AC/Refrigeration)**

*   **Diaphragm Compressors:** Use a flexible diaphragm to compress the gas. Very good for handling corrosive or toxic gases as there are no seals. (Less common in typical RAC applications).
*   **Linear Compressors:** An advanced type often found in higher-efficiency domestic refrigerators. They use a linear motor to move a piston, offering better efficiency and quieter operation. (Mentioned in advanced texts like ASHRAE Handbook).

### Selection Criteria for Compressors:

The choice of compressor for a particular refrigeration or air conditioning system depends on several factors:

1.  **Capacity Requirement:** The cooling load determines the required refrigerant flow rate.
2.  **Pressure Ratio:** The difference between evaporator and condenser pressures.
3.  **Efficiency:** COP and energy consumption are crucial for operating costs.
4.  **Cost:** Initial purchase cost and long-term maintenance costs.
5.  **Reliability and Lifespan:** Expected durability and service life.
6.  **Noise and Vibration Levels:** Important for comfort in residential and commercial spaces.
7.  **Space and Weight Constraints:** Especially critical in compact appliances.
8.  **Type of Refrigerant:** Some compressors are better suited for certain refrigerants.
9.  **Operating Conditions:** Ambient temperature, humidity, and desired temperature of the cooled space.

---

### Important Points to Remember:

*   The compressor is the most energy-consuming component in a vapor-compression refrigeration system.
*   Volumetric and isentropic efficiencies are key performance indicators for compressors.
*   Hermetic compressors are common in smaller appliances due to their sealed nature, preventing refrigerant leakage.
*   Scroll compressors offer a good balance of efficiency, noise, and reliability for residential and commercial AC.
*   Centrifugal compressors are best suited for very large cooling loads where smooth operation is paramount.
*   Liquid slugging (liquid refrigerant entering the compressor) can cause severe damage to most compressor types, especially centrifugal and screw compressors. Reciprocating compressors can tolerate a small amount, but it can still lead to valve damage or rod bending.
*   Lubrication is crucial for the longevity of most compressors.

### Practice Questions and Exercises:

**Question 1:**
Which type of compressor is characterized by the use of intermeshing helical rotors?
(a) Reciprocating Compressor
(b) Scroll Compressor
(c) Screw Compressor
(d) Centrifugal Compressor

**Answer:** (c) Screw Compressor

**Question 2:**
Explain the working principle of a scroll compressor and list two of its advantages.
**Answer:** A scroll compressor uses two interleaved spiral scrolls, one orbiting relative to the other. This creates pockets that trap refrigerant vapor and move it towards the center, compressing it along the way.
Advantages: High efficiency, quiet operation, low vibration.

**Question 3:**
For a large office building requiring a cooling capacity of 500 tons of refrigeration, which type of compressor would likely be the most suitable and why?
**Answer:** A centrifugal compressor would likely be the most suitable. Centrifugal compressors are designed for very high cooling capacities, provide smooth and vibration-free operation which is desirable in large buildings, and have low maintenance requirements.

**Question 4:**
Compare and contrast the advantages and disadvantages of hermetically sealed reciprocating compressors and semi-hermetic reciprocating compressors.
**Answer:**
*   **Hermetically Sealed:**
    *   **Advantages:** Compact, no external shaft seal to leak, common in domestic appliances.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Difficult to repair or service as the unit is sealed, requires replacement if internal components fail.
*   **Semi-Hermetic:**
    *   **Advantages:** Can be opened for servicing and repair, allowing for easier replacement of internal parts like valves or rings.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Slightly larger and heavier than hermetic, still has a sealed casing but with bolted flanges for access.

**Question 5:**
What is the primary function of the diffuser in a centrifugal compressor?
**Answer:** The primary function of the diffuser in a centrifugal compressor is to convert the high kinetic energy (velocity) of the refrigerant vapor from the impeller into pressure energy.
