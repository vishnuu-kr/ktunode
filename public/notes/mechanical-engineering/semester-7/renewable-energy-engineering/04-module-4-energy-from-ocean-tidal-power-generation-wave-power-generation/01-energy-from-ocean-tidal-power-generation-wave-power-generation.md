# Renewable Energy Engineering - Module 4: Energy from Ocean

## Topic: Tidal Power Generation – Wave Power Generation

This module delves into two significant renewable energy sources derived from the ocean: tidal power and wave power. We will explore the principles, technologies, advantages, disadvantages, and current status of both. This topic directly contributes to **CO4: Gain knowledge about tidal and wave energy, energy from biomass and hydrogen (Knowledge Level: K2)** by focusing specifically on tidal and wave energy.

---

### 1. Introduction to Ocean Energy

The ocean represents a vast and largely untapped reservoir of renewable energy. The continuous motion of water bodies, driven by solar radiation and gravitational forces, creates potential for energy generation. Key forms of ocean energy include:

*   **Tidal Energy:** Harnessing the energy from the rise and fall of sea levels due to the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun.
*   **Wave Energy:** Capturing the kinetic and potential energy of ocean surface waves.
*   **Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion (OTEC):** Utilizing the temperature difference between warm surface water and cold deep ocean water.
*   **Ocean Current Energy:** Extracting energy from the flow of ocean currents.
*   **Salinity Gradient Energy (Osmotic Power):** Exploiting the energy released when freshwater mixes with saltwater.

*(Referenced from Twidell & Weir, 3rd Edn, Chapter 10)*

### 2. Tidal Power Generation

Tidal energy is one of the oldest and most established forms of ocean energy utilization. It relies on the predictable and regular movement of water associated with tides.

#### 2.1. Principles of Tidal Energy

*   **Tides:** Tides are the periodic rise and fall of sea levels caused by the combined gravitational forces of the Moon and the Sun, and the rotation of the Earth.
    *   **Lunar Tides:** The Moon's gravitational pull is the primary driver of tides.
    *   **Solar Tides:** The Sun's gravitational pull also contributes, though to a lesser extent.
    *   **Spring Tides:** Occur when the Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned, resulting in higher high tides and lower low tides (larger tidal range).
    *   **Neap Tides:** Occur when the Sun and Moon are at right angles to the Earth, resulting in lower high tides and higher low tides (smaller tidal range).
*   **Tidal Range:** The difference in height between high tide and low tide. A significant tidal range is crucial for efficient tidal power generation using barrages.
*   **Tidal Currents:** The horizontal movement of water associated with the rise and fall of sea levels. Tidal currents can also be harnessed.

#### 2.2. Tidal Energy Conversion Technologies

There are two main approaches to harnessing tidal energy:

**2.2.1. Tidal Barrages (Impoundment Schemes)**

*   **Concept:** A dam-like structure (barrage) is built across an estuary or bay with a high tidal range. This barrage creates a difference in water level between the upstream reservoir (basin) and the downstream sea.
*   **Operation:**
    *   **Ebb Generation:** Water is impounded in the basin during high tide. As the tide recedes, the water level difference increases. When this difference reaches a certain head, sluice gates in the barrage are opened, allowing water to flow from the basin through turbines, generating electricity.
    *   **Flood Generation:** Water is allowed to flow into the basin during flood tide. As the tide rises, the water level difference is utilized to drive turbines.
    *   **Two-Way Generation:** Combines both ebb and flood generation for maximum energy capture.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Barrage:** The main structure to impound water.
    *   **Sluice Gates:** To control water flow into and out of the basin.
    *   **Turbines:** Typically bulb turbines or Kaplan turbines, designed to operate efficiently at low heads and variable flow rates.
    *   **Generators:** Connected to the turbines to produce electricity.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   **Predictable and Reliable:** Tides are highly predictable, ensuring a steady power output.
    *   **High Energy Output:** Barrages can generate significant amounts of electricity, especially in locations with large tidal ranges.
    *   **Long Lifespan:** Barrages have a long operational life.
    *   **Potential Secondary Benefits:** Can provide flood defense, improve navigation, and create a freshwater reservoir.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **High Capital Cost:** Construction is very expensive.
    *   **Environmental Impact:**
        *   Disruption of estuarine ecosystems (salinity, sediment transport, intertidal habitats).
        *   Impact on fish migration.
        *   Changes in water quality.
    *   **Limited Suitable Sites:** Requires estuaries with large tidal ranges (typically > 5 meters) and suitable geology.
    *   **Long Construction Time:** Projects can take many years to complete.
    *   **Intermittent Power Output:** Generation is dependent on the tidal cycle.

*   **Examples:**
    *   **La Rance Tidal Power Station, France:** The first and one of the largest operational tidal barrages, built in 1966. It utilizes bulb turbines and has a capacity of 240 MW. (Referenced from Twidell & Weir, 3rd Edn, Chapter 10)
    *   **Sihwa Lake Tidal Power Station, South Korea:** The world's largest tidal power plant, commissioned in 2011, with a capacity of 254 MW. It's built across a man-made seawall.

**2.2.2. Tidal Stream Converters (In-stream Turbines)**

*   **Concept:** Devices placed directly in tidal currents to capture the kinetic energy of moving water. These are analogous to wind turbines but operate underwater.
*   **Operation:** The flow of tidal currents rotates the rotor blades of the turbine, which drives a generator to produce electricity.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Turbine Rotor:** With blades designed for water flow.
    *   **Generator:** Typically housed in a nacelle.
    *   **Support Structure:** Foundations, monopiles, or floating platforms.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   **Lower Environmental Impact:** Generally less disruptive to the marine environment and ecosystems compared to barrages.
    *   **No Impoundment:** Does not alter water levels or create reservoirs.
    *   **Less Disruption to Navigation:** Can be placed strategically without blocking major waterways.
    *   **Potentially Wider Site Availability:** Can be deployed in areas with strong tidal currents, even without a large tidal range.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **Lower Energy Density:** Kinetic energy of water is lower than the potential energy harnessed by barrages.
    *   **Challenges in Installation and Maintenance:** Working underwater presents significant engineering challenges.
    *   **Corrosion and Biofouling:** Marine environment leads to material degradation.
    *   **Navigational Hazards:** Potential risk to shipping and marine life.
    *   **Variable Power Output:** Depends on the strength of tidal currents, which vary cyclically.
    *   **Higher Cost per kWh (currently):** Technology is still maturing.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **MeyGen Project, Scotland:** One of the largest tidal stream projects globally, located in the Pentland Firth. It uses submerged turbines.
    *   **SeaGen, Northern Ireland:** An early prototype tidal stream generator that has been tested.

#### 2.3. Site Selection for Tidal Power

*   **Tidal Range:** High tidal range is essential for barrages (> 5 meters).
*   **Tidal Currents:** Strong tidal currents (> 2 m/s) are needed for stream converters.
*   **Topography:** Suitable estuaries or bays for barrage construction.
*   **Geology:** Stable seabed conditions for foundations.
*   **Environmental Sensitivities:** Minimizing impact on marine life, habitats, and human activities.
*   **Proximity to Grid Connection:** Economical connection to the electricity grid.

#### 2.4. Key Concepts in Tidal Power

*   **Tidal Prism:** The volume of water that flows into and out of an estuary during a tidal cycle.
*   **Head:** The difference in water level between the reservoir and the sea, driving the turbines.
*   **Cycle Time:** The duration of the tidal cycle (approximately 12 hours 25 minutes).
*   **Capacity Factor:** The ratio of actual energy produced over a period to the maximum possible energy that could have been produced if the plant ran at its rated capacity continuously. Tidal plants have capacity factors that reflect their intermittent nature.

#### **Important Points to Remember (Tidal Power):**

*   Tidal energy is predictable and renewable.
*   Barrages use potential energy (head), while stream converters use kinetic energy (current).
*   Environmental impact is a major consideration, especially for barrages.
*   La Rance is a pioneering example of tidal barrage technology.
*   MeyGen is a leading example of tidal stream technology.

### 3. Wave Power Generation

Ocean waves are generated by wind blowing over the surface of the sea. This kinetic and potential energy of waves can be converted into electricity.

#### 3.1. Principles of Wave Energy

*   **Wave Generation:** Wind transfers energy to the water surface, creating ripples that grow into waves.
*   **Wave Properties:**
    *   **Wave Height (H):** The vertical distance between the crest and the trough.
    *   **Wavelength (L):** The horizontal distance between two successive crests or troughs.
    *   **Wave Period (T):** The time taken for two successive crests or troughs to pass a fixed point.
    *   **Wave Speed (C):** The speed at which a wave crest travels.
*   **Energy Content of Waves:** Wave energy is proportional to the square of the wave height and the wave period. The power available in a unit width of wave front is given by:
    $P = \frac{\rho g^2}{32 \pi} T H^2$
    Where:
    *   $P$ = Power per unit width of wave front (W/m)
    *   $\rho$ = Density of seawater (approx. 1025 kg/m³)
    *   $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity (approx. 9.81 m/s²)
    *   $T$ = Wave period (s)
    *   $H$ = Wave height (m)
*   **Intermittency:** Wave energy is variable and depends on wind conditions and location.

*(Referenced from Twidell & Weir, 3rd Edn, Chapter 10)*

#### 3.2. Wave Energy Conversion Technologies

There are numerous concepts for converting wave energy, broadly categorized by their operating location and mechanism:

**3.2.1. Shoreline Devices**

*   **Concept:** Located on the shore or in very shallow water.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Oscillating Water Column (OWC):** A partially submerged chamber with an opening below the waterline. As waves enter the chamber, they cause the water level to rise and fall, forcing air through a turbine (e.g., Wells turbine) situated above the water level.
        *   *Key component: Wells turbine - a self-rectifying turbine that rotates in the same direction regardless of the airflow direction.*
    *   **Beach Absorbers:** Structures built on the beach to absorb wave energy.

**3.2.2. Nearshore Devices**

*   **Concept:** Deployed in shallow to moderate water depths, anchored to the seabed.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Attenuators:** Long, floating structures that are oriented parallel to the direction of wave propagation. They consist of segments linked by hinges. As waves pass along the structure, the relative motion between segments drives hydraulic pumps or generators.
        *   *Example: Pelamis - a snake-like device with multiple linked sections.*
    *   **Point Absorbers:** Buoyant structures that oscillate up and down with the waves. The relative motion between the floating body and a fixed reference (seabed or a spar buoy) drives a power take-off system (e.g., hydraulic rams, linear generators).
        *   *Example: Buoys that bob on the surface.*

**3.2.3. Offshore Devices**

*   **Concept:** Located in deeper water, further from the shore.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Overtopping Devices:** Floating or fixed structures with a ramp or reservoir that waves overtop. The captured water then flows back to the sea through conventional low-head hydro turbines.
        *   *Example: Wave Dragon - a floating offshore platform with a reservoir.*
    *   **Submerged Pressure Differential Devices:** Devices anchored to the seabed that utilize the pressure fluctuations beneath the waves.

#### 3.3. Key Components of Wave Energy Converters (WECs)

*   **Floating Structure/Body:** Captures wave energy by moving with the waves.
*   **Anchoring System:** Secures the device to the seabed.
*   **Power Take-Off (PTO) System:** Converts the mechanical motion into electrical energy. This can involve:
    *   **Hydraulic Rams:** Pumping hydraulic fluid.
    *   **Linear Generators:** Directly converting linear motion into electricity.
    *   **Pneumatic Systems:** Using air pressure to drive turbines.
*   **Transmission System:** Cables to transmit electricity to shore.

#### 3.4. Advantages of Wave Energy

*   **High Energy Density:** Waves can carry significant amounts of energy.
*   **Predictability (relative):** While variable, wave patterns can be forecast better than wind in some cases.
*   **Renewable and Sustainable:** Utilizes a natural resource.
*   **Potentially Wide Geographic Availability:** Waves are present in oceans worldwide.
*   **Lower Visual Impact (offshore):** Devices can be located far from shore.

#### 3.5. Disadvantages of Wave Energy

*   **Technological Immaturity:** Many WEC designs are still in the prototype or pre-commercial stages.
*   **High Capital Costs:** Development and deployment are expensive.
*   **Harsh Marine Environment:** Devices must withstand storms, corrosion, and biofouling.
*   **Intermittency and Variability:** Wave energy output fluctuates with sea state.
*   **Environmental Impact:** Potential impacts on marine ecosystems, navigation, and fishing.
*   **Grid Connection:** Difficult and costly to connect offshore devices to the grid.
*   **Maintenance Challenges:** Difficult and expensive to maintain offshore equipment.

#### **Important Points to Remember (Wave Power):**

*   Wave energy is derived from wind-driven surface motion.
*   Energy content depends on wave height and period.
*   OWC uses air turbines, while attenuators and point absorbers often use hydraulic or electrical PTO systems.
*   Wave energy technology is still in development, facing significant engineering and cost challenges.
*   The marine environment poses severe operational and maintenance challenges.

### 4. Comparison with Other Commercial Energy Systems (CO1)

*   **Tidal Power:**
    *   **Comparison to Fossil Fuels:** Tidal power is a clean, renewable source, unlike fossil fuels which emit greenhouse gases and pollutants. However, fossil fuels offer dispatchable power (can be turned on/off as needed), while tidal power's output is governed by the natural tidal cycle. Tidal barrages have high initial capital costs, similar to large fossil fuel or nuclear power plants, but with much lower operational fuel costs.
    *   **Comparison to Solar/Wind:** Tidal power is significantly more predictable than solar and wind power. Solar power is only available during the day and is affected by cloud cover. Wind power is intermittent and depends on wind speed. Tidal barrages offer a more consistent, albeit cyclical, power output.
    *   **Comparison to Hydroelectric:** Both tidal barrages and hydroelectric dams utilize water flow to drive turbines. However, hydroelectric dams typically rely on stored potential energy in reservoirs created by natural rainfall and topography, offering greater dispatchability. Tidal barrages rely on gravitational forces and the ebb/flow of tides.
*   **Wave Power:**
    *   **Comparison to Fossil Fuels:** Wave power is renewable and emission-free during operation. Fossil fuels are finite and polluting. Wave power's output is variable, unlike the potentially constant output of a fossil fuel plant.
    *   **Comparison to Solar/Wind:** Wave power can potentially offer a more consistent energy source than solar (daytime only) and wind (wind speed variability). However, wave energy technology is less mature and more expensive than established solar and wind technologies.
    *   **Comparison to Tidal Power:** Wave power is generally more variable than tidal power, especially tidal barrages. Tidal barrages have a predictable cycle, whereas wave power is dependent on weather systems affecting wave formation.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What are the primary forces responsible for generating tides?
**Answer:** The primary forces are the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun, and the Earth's rotation.

**Question 2:** Describe the main difference between tidal barrages and tidal stream converters in terms of energy extraction.
**Answer:** Tidal barrages extract potential energy from the difference in water level (head) created by a barrage across an estuary, while tidal stream converters extract kinetic energy directly from the flow of tidal currents.

**Question 3:** What type of turbine is commonly used in Oscillating Water Column (OWC) devices, and why?
**Answer:** The Wells turbine is commonly used. It is a self-rectifying turbine that rotates in the same direction regardless of the direction of airflow, simplifying the OWC system.

**Question 4:** List two major environmental concerns associated with building tidal barrages.
**Answer:**
1.  Disruption of estuarine ecosystems (salinity, sediment transport, habitat changes).
2.  Impact on fish migration.

**Question 5:** The energy content of ocean waves is most strongly dependent on which two wave parameters?
**Answer:** Wave height (H) and Wave period (T).

**Question 6:** True or False: Wave energy conversion devices typically have a higher capacity factor than tidal barrage power plants.
**Answer:** False. Tidal barrage power plants, due to the predictable nature of tides, generally have a more consistent output and can achieve higher capacity factors than most wave energy devices, which are subject to more variable weather conditions.

### 6. Key Takeaways and Important Points to Remember

*   **Ocean Energy Potential:** The oceans hold vast amounts of renewable energy, particularly from tides and waves.
*   **Tidal Power:**
    *   **Barrages:** Utilize potential energy due to tidal range. High cost, significant environmental impact, but predictable power. La Rance is a key example.
    *   **Stream Converters:** Utilize kinetic energy from tidal currents. Lower environmental impact, less disruptive, but technology is still maturing. MeyGen is a leading example.
    *   **Predictability:** Tidal power is highly predictable due to the astronomical forces driving it.
*   **Wave Power:**
    *   **Energy Source:** Wind-driven surface waves. Energy is proportional to $H^2 \times T$.
    *   **Technologies:** Diverse concepts (OWC, attenuators, point absorbers, overtopping devices) are under development.
    *   **Challenges:** Technological immaturity, high costs, harsh marine environment, and intermittency are major hurdles.
    *   **Environmental Aspect:** Generally considered to have a lower environmental footprint than tidal barrages but still requires careful assessment.
*   **CO1 Alignment:** Both tidal and wave energy represent alternatives to conventional energy systems. Their predictability (tidal) and renewability offer advantages over fossil fuels, but technological maturity and cost are factors for comparison.
