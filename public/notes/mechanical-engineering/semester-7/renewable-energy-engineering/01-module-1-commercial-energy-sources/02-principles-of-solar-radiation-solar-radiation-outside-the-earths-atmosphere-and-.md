# Renewable Energy Engineering: Module 1 - Commercial Energy Sources

## Topic: Principles of Solar Radiation: Solar Radiation Outside the Earth's Atmosphere and at the Earth's Surface, Solar Constant

### 1. Introduction to Solar Energy

*   **Definition:** Solar energy is the radiant energy emitted by the Sun, which is the primary source of almost all energy on Earth. It is a renewable and sustainable energy source.
*   **Importance in Commercial Energy:** While fossil fuels currently dominate commercial energy, solar energy is a crucial alternative and increasingly important component of the global energy mix.
    *   *(CO1: Differentiate between commercial energy systems and alternatives)*
    *   Solar energy offers a clean alternative to carbon-intensive commercial energy sources like coal and oil.

---

### 2. Solar Radiation Outside the Earth's Atmosphere

#### 2.1 The Sun as a Source of Radiation

*   The Sun is a giant nuclear fusion reactor where hydrogen atoms fuse to form helium, releasing vast amounts of energy in the form of electromagnetic radiation.
*   This radiation travels through space and reaches Earth.

#### 2.2 The Solar Constant ($I_{sc}$)

*   **Definition:** The solar constant is defined as the total solar irradiance (power per unit area) received at the top of the Earth's atmosphere, on a surface perpendicular to the incoming solar radiation, at the mean Earth-Sun distance.
*   **Value:** The accepted value for the solar constant is approximately **1361 W/m²** (or 1.361 kW/m²).
    *   *(Important Point to Remember: The solar constant is not truly constant, but it is a useful approximation.)*
*   **Measurement:** It is measured by satellites and space probes.
*   **Variations:**
    *   **Earth-Sun Distance:** The Earth's orbit is elliptical, not perfectly circular. This causes slight variations in the solar irradiance received throughout the year.
        *   Perihelion (closest to the Sun): ~1412 W/m² (early January)
        *   Aphelion (farthest from the Sun): ~1321 W/m² (early July)
    *   **Solar Activity:** Sunspot activity and solar flares can cause minor fluctuations in the solar constant.

#### 2.3 Spectral Distribution of Extraterrestrial Solar Radiation

*   The solar radiation outside the atmosphere is distributed across a wide spectrum of electromagnetic wavelengths.
*   The majority of this radiation falls within the ultraviolet (UV), visible, and infrared (IR) regions.
*   **Distribution (approximate):**
    *   Ultraviolet (wavelengths < 0.3 µm): ~5%
    *   Visible (0.3 µm to 0.7 µm): ~45%
    *   Infrared (wavelengths > 0.7 µm): ~50%
*   *(Reference: Twidell & Weir, 3rd Edn, Chapter 2, Figure 2.1 or similar)*

### 3. Solar Radiation at the Earth's Surface

*   When solar radiation enters the Earth's atmosphere, it interacts with atmospheric constituents, leading to a reduction and modification of its intensity and spectral distribution.

#### 3.1 Atmospheric Effects

*   **Absorption:**
    *   **Ozone:** Absorbs most of the UV radiation.
    *   **Water Vapor:** Absorbs infrared radiation in specific bands.
    *   **Carbon Dioxide:** Absorbs infrared radiation.
    *   **Oxygen and Nitrogen:** Absorb UV radiation.
*   **Scattering:**
    *   **Rayleigh Scattering:** Scattering by molecules in the atmosphere (e.g., nitrogen and oxygen). This is responsible for the blue color of the sky. It scatters shorter wavelengths (blue) more effectively than longer wavelengths (red).
    *   **Mie Scattering:** Scattering by larger particles such as dust, water droplets, and aerosols. This type of scattering is less dependent on wavelength and can contribute to the white appearance of clouds or haze.
*   **Reflection:** Clouds and atmospheric particles reflect a portion of the incoming solar radiation back into space.

#### 3.2 Components of Solar Radiation at the Surface

*   **Direct Radiation ($I_D$ or $I_{beam}$):** This is the solar radiation that has traveled directly from the Sun to the Earth's surface without being scattered or absorbed by the atmosphere. It is the radiation that falls on a surface perpendicular to the Sun's rays.
    *   *(CO2: Obtain a detailed knowledge in solar energy implementation for collection, conversion and storage)*
    *   Direct radiation is crucial for concentrating solar power (CSP) systems.
*   **Diffuse Radiation ($I_{diff}$):** This is the solar radiation that has been scattered by atmospheric molecules and particles, coming from all directions of the sky dome.
    *   *(CO2: Obtain a detailed knowledge in solar energy implementation for collection, conversion and storage)*
    *   Diffuse radiation is important for photovoltaic (PV) panels and for calculating total irradiance on non-tracking surfaces.
*   **Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI) ($I_G$ or $I_{GHI}$):** This is the total solar irradiance received on a horizontal surface at the Earth's surface. It is the sum of direct and diffuse radiation.
    *   $I_G = I_D + I_{diff}$
    *   *(CO2: Obtain a detailed knowledge in solar energy implementation for collection, conversion and storage)*
    *   *(Important Point to Remember: GHI is the most common measurement used for solar resource assessment globally.)*
*   **Beam Normal Irradiance (BNI) ($I_{BNI}$):** This is the direct solar irradiance received on a surface perpendicular to the Sun's rays. It is the direct component of extraterrestrial radiation after atmospheric attenuation.

#### 3.3 Factors Affecting Solar Radiation at the Surface

*   **Latitude:** Affects the angle of incidence of the sun's rays and the duration of daylight.
*   **Time of Day and Season:** Affects the Sun's position in the sky.
*   **Atmospheric Conditions:** Cloud cover, humidity, dust, and pollution levels.
*   **Altitude:** Higher altitudes generally have less atmospheric absorption and scattering.
*   **Albedo:** The reflectivity of the Earth's surface (e.g., snow, soil, vegetation).

#### 3.4 Spectral Distribution at the Surface

*   The spectrum of solar radiation reaching the surface is altered due to atmospheric absorption and scattering.
*   Infrared absorption by water vapor is significant.
*   UV radiation is reduced.
*   The relative proportion of diffuse radiation (which has a different spectral content than direct radiation) also affects the overall spectrum.

### 4. Key Concepts and Definitions Summary

*   **Solar Constant ($I_{sc}$):** Solar irradiance outside the Earth's atmosphere on a surface perpendicular to the Sun's rays at the mean Earth-Sun distance. (Approx. 1361 W/m²)
*   **Direct Radiation ($I_D$):** Solar radiation that travels directly from the Sun to the surface.
*   **Diffuse Radiation ($I_{diff}$):** Solar radiation scattered by the atmosphere.
*   **Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI):** Total solar radiation on a horizontal surface ($I_D + I_{diff}$).
*   **Beam Normal Irradiance (BNI):** Direct solar radiation on a surface perpendicular to the Sun's rays.

### 5. Examples and Applications

*   **Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Convert sunlight directly into electricity. They utilize both direct and diffuse radiation.
    *   *(CO2: Obtain a detailed knowledge in solar energy implementation for collection, conversion and storage)*
*   **Concentrating Solar Power (CSP) Systems:** Use mirrors to concentrate direct sunlight onto a receiver to generate heat, which then drives a turbine to produce electricity. These systems are highly dependent on direct beam radiation.
    *   *(CO2: Obtain a detailed knowledge in solar energy implementation for collection, conversion and storage)*
*   **Solar Thermal Collectors:** Used for water heating or space heating. They collect both direct and diffuse radiation.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Define the solar constant and state its approximate value.
**Answer 1:** The solar constant is the total solar irradiance received at the top of the Earth's atmosphere, on a surface perpendicular to the incoming solar radiation, at the mean Earth-Sun distance. Its approximate value is 1361 W/m².

**Question 2:** What are the main components of solar radiation received at the Earth's surface? Briefly explain each.
**Answer 2:**
*   **Direct Radiation:** Travels directly from the Sun without scattering.
*   **Diffuse Radiation:** Scattered by the atmosphere.
*   **Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI):** The sum of direct and diffuse radiation on a horizontal surface.

**Question 3:** Explain two key atmospheric effects that reduce solar radiation intensity as it passes through the atmosphere.
**Answer 3:**
*   **Absorption:** Atmospheric gases like ozone, water vapor, and carbon dioxide absorb specific wavelengths of solar radiation.
*   **Scattering:** Atmospheric molecules (Rayleigh scattering) and particles (Mie scattering) redirect solar radiation in different directions, reducing the direct component.

**Question 4:** Why are concentrating solar power (CSP) systems more dependent on direct beam radiation than photovoltaic (PV) systems?
**Answer 4:** CSP systems use mirrors to focus sunlight onto a receiver. This concentration requires the sunlight to arrive as a focused beam (direct radiation). Diffuse radiation cannot be effectively focused by mirrors. PV systems, however, can utilize both direct and diffuse radiation for electricity generation.

**Question 5:** If the solar constant is $I_{sc} = 1361$ W/m², what would be the theoretical maximum irradiance on a surface perfectly perpendicular to the Sun's rays at the top of the atmosphere if the Earth-Sun distance were at perihelion (assume irradiance is proportional to the inverse square of the distance, and mean distance = 1 AU)? (This requires external knowledge on Earth's orbit, but tests understanding of inverse square law).
**Answer 5:** (Requires additional information not strictly within the notes but tests concept application.)
At perihelion, the Earth-Sun distance is approximately 0.983 AU.
The irradiance is inversely proportional to the square of the distance.
$I_{perihelion} = I_{sc} \times (\frac{1 \text{ AU}}{0.983 \text{ AU}})^2$
$I_{perihelion} = 1361 \text{ W/m}^2 \times (\frac{1}{0.983})^2$
$I_{perihelion} \approx 1361 \text{ W/m}^2 \times (1.017)^2$
$I_{perihelion} \approx 1361 \text{ W/m}^2 \times 1.034 \approx 1409 \text{ W/m}^2$.
*(Note: The value of 1361 W/m² is an average; the actual value at perihelion can be quoted differently. This question aims to assess understanding of the inverse square relationship.)*

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   The solar constant is a key reference value for solar energy calculations, representing the radiation outside the atmosphere.
*   Atmospheric effects (absorption, scattering) significantly reduce and modify solar radiation reaching the Earth's surface.
*   Solar radiation at the surface consists of direct and diffuse components.
*   The type of solar technology (PV, CSP, solar thermal) determines its dependence on direct vs. diffuse radiation.
*   Understanding the principles of solar radiation is fundamental to designing and implementing effective solar energy systems.
