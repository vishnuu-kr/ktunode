## ENGINEERING MATERIALS: MODULE 2 - INTRODUCTION TO METALS

### Topic: Applications and Properties, Corrosion and Protection

This module delves into the fundamental aspects of metals, focusing on their diverse applications, inherent properties, and the critical phenomenon of corrosion, along with methods for its control and prevention. This knowledge is crucial for selecting appropriate metallic materials for various engineering applications, aligning with Course Outcomes CO1 and CO2.

---

### 1. Types of Metals: Ferrous and Non-Ferrous

Understanding the distinction between ferrous and non-ferrous metals is the foundation for appreciating their differing properties and applications.

#### 1.1. Ferrous Metals
*   **Definition:** Metals that contain iron as their primary constituent. They are generally strong, hard, and magnetic.
    *   **Key Concept:** Iron (Fe) is the defining element.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Iron:** The base metal, often impure.
    *   **Steel:** Iron alloyed primarily with carbon (typically less than 2.14 wt% carbon).
        *   **Carbon Steel:** Contains carbon as the main alloying element.
            *   **Low Carbon Steel (Mild Steel):** < 0.25% C. Ductile, malleable, weldable. Used for structural components, car bodies, pipes. (Budinski & Budinski, p. 245)
            *   **Medium Carbon Steel:** 0.25-0.60% C. Stronger than low carbon steel, but less ductile. Used for gears, shafts, connecting rods.
            *   **High Carbon Steel:** 0.60-2.14% C. Hard and strong, but brittle. Used for cutting tools, springs, high-strength wires.
        *   **Alloy Steel:** Contains other alloying elements besides carbon (e.g., chromium, nickel, molybdenum, vanadium) to enhance specific properties like strength, hardness, corrosion resistance, or toughness.
            *   **Stainless Steel:** Alloyed with at least 10.5% chromium, forming a passive chromium oxide layer for excellent corrosion resistance.
                *   **Austenitic Stainless Steels (e.g., 304, 316):** Non-magnetic, excellent corrosion resistance, good formability. Used for kitchenware, chemical processing equipment, medical implants. (Callister & Rethwisch, p. 587)
                *   **Martensitic Stainless Steels (e.g., 410, 420):** Magnetic, can be hardened by heat treatment, good strength and hardness. Used for knives, surgical instruments.
                *   **Ferritic Stainless Steels (e.g., 430):** Magnetic, good corrosion resistance, less expensive than austenitic. Used for automotive trim, appliances.
            *   **Tool Steels:** High-carbon alloy steels designed for hardness, wear resistance, and toughness at elevated temperatures. Used for cutting tools, dies, molds.
    *   **Cast Iron:** Iron alloyed with carbon (typically 2.14-4.5 wt% carbon) and silicon. It has a lower melting point than steel and is cast into complex shapes.
        *   **Grey Cast Iron:** Carbon exists as graphite flakes. Good machinability, damping capacity. Used for engine blocks, machine bases. (Shackelford, p. 362)
        *   **Ductile (Nodular) Cast Iron:** Graphite exists as spherical nodules. Stronger and more ductile than grey cast iron. Used for pipes, crankshafts.
        *   **White Cast Iron:** Carbon exists as iron carbide (cementite). Very hard and brittle, abrasion-resistant. Used for wear-resistant parts.
*   **Properties of Ferrous Metals:**
    *   High tensile strength and hardness.
    *   Magnetic properties (except some stainless steels).
    *   Can be heat-treated to modify properties.
    *   Prone to rust (corrosion) in the presence of moisture and oxygen, especially plain carbon steels.
    *   Relatively low cost for many common types.
*   **Applications:** Construction (structural beams, rebar), automotive industry (engine blocks, chassis), heavy machinery, pipelines, tools.

#### 1.2. Non-Ferrous Metals
*   **Definition:** Metals that do not contain iron as their primary constituent. They exhibit a wide range of properties and are often chosen for their lightness, corrosion resistance, electrical conductivity, or non-magnetic nature.
    *   **Key Concept:** Absence of significant iron content.
*   **Common Examples and Properties:**
    *   **Aluminum (Al):**
        *   **Properties:** Lightweight (low density), good corrosion resistance (forms a protective oxide layer), excellent electrical and thermal conductivity, ductile, non-magnetic.
        *   **Alloys:** Often alloyed with copper, magnesium, silicon, and zinc to improve strength.
        *   **Applications:** Aerospace (aircraft components), automotive (engine parts, body panels), electrical conductors, cookware, packaging (cans). (Budinski & Budinski, p. 412)
    *   **Copper (Cu):**
        *   **Properties:** Excellent electrical and thermal conductivity, ductile, malleable, good corrosion resistance, reddish color.
        *   **Alloys:**
            *   **Brass:** Copper-Zinc alloy. Good machinability, decorative. Used for plumbing fittings, musical instruments.
            *   **Bronze:** Copper-Tin alloy. Stronger and harder than brass. Used for bearings, propellers, sculptures.
        *   **Applications:** Electrical wiring, plumbing, heat exchangers, coins, decorative items. (Callister & Rethwisch, p. 633)
    *   **Nickel (Ni):**
        *   **Properties:** High strength, excellent corrosion and heat resistance, magnetic.
        *   **Applications:** Stainless steel production, superalloys, electroplating, batteries.
    *   **Titanium (Ti):**
        *   **Properties:** High strength-to-weight ratio, excellent corrosion resistance (especially in oxidizing environments), biocompatible, high melting point.
        *   **Applications:** Aerospace (aircraft frames, engine components), medical implants (hip and knee replacements), chemical processing equipment, sporting goods. (Shackelford, p. 401)
    *   **Magnesium (Mg):**
        *   **Properties:** Lightest structural metal, good machinability, can be susceptible to galvanic corrosion.
        *   **Applications:** Aerospace, automotive (engine components, wheels), portable electronics casings.
    *   **Lead (Pb), Tin (Sn), Zinc (Zn):**
        *   **Properties:** Low melting points, good corrosion resistance (especially zinc for galvanizing).
        *   **Applications:** Solders (tin/lead), galvanizing (zinc), batteries (lead).
*   **Properties of Non-Ferrous Metals (General):**
    *   Generally lower density than ferrous metals.
    *   Often more corrosion resistant.
    *   Non-magnetic (except nickel and some alloys).
    *   Good electrical and thermal conductivity (especially copper and aluminum).
    *   Heat-treatable to varying degrees.
    *   Can be more expensive than common steels.
*   **Applications:** Aerospace, electronics, automotive (lightweighting), medical devices, plumbing, construction.

### 2. Corrosion and Protection

Corrosion is a natural process that degrades metallic materials through chemical or electrochemical reactions with their environment. Understanding its mechanisms and prevention is vital for material selection and longevity of structures.

#### 2.1. Mechanisms of Corrosion

Corrosion is fundamentally an electrochemical process where a metal is oxidized (loses electrons) and a reduction reaction occurs elsewhere.

*   **Definition:** The electrochemical degradation of a metal.
    *   **Key Concept:** Oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions.
*   **Electrochemical Cell:** Corrosion requires an anode (where oxidation occurs), a cathode (where reduction occurs), an electrolyte (a conductive medium, usually water with dissolved ions), and an electrical connection between anode and cathode.
    *   **Anodic Reaction:** Metal loses electrons and becomes an ion (e.g., Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻). This is where metal loss occurs.
    *   **Cathodic Reaction:** Electrons are consumed in a reduction reaction. Common examples:
        *   In acidic environments with dissolved oxygen: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ (gas)
        *   In neutral or alkaline environments with dissolved oxygen: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (hydroxide ions)
*   **Types of Corrosion:**
    *   **Uniform Corrosion:** Corrosion occurs relatively evenly over the entire exposed surface.
        *   **Mechanism:** Large anodic and cathodic areas, often resulting in a thin, uniform oxide or corrosion product layer.
        *   **Example:** General rusting of steel in a humid environment. (Raghavan, p. 459)
    *   **Galvanic Corrosion:** Occurs when two dissimilar metals are in electrical contact in the presence of an electrolyte.
        *   **Mechanism:** The more active (less noble) metal becomes the anode and corrodes preferentially, while the more noble metal becomes the cathode and is protected. The further apart the metals are in the galvanic series, the more severe the corrosion.
        *   **Example:** Steel screw in a brass fitting, where the steel corrodes. (Callister & Rethwisch, p. 715)
    *   **Crevice Corrosion:** Localized corrosion that occurs within narrow openings or under deposits.
        *   **Mechanism:** Stagnant electrolyte in the crevice becomes depleted of oxygen, leading to the crevice acting as an anode. The area outside the crevice (with more oxygen) becomes the cathode.
        *   **Example:** Under gaskets, bolt heads, or in lap joints.
    *   **Pitting Corrosion:** Localized, deep attack that creates holes or pits in the metal surface.
        *   **Mechanism:** Often initiated by breakdown of protective passive films, especially in the presence of aggressive ions like chloride. Can be very dangerous as it reduces structural integrity with minimal visible damage.
        *   **Example:** Stainless steel in chloride environments.
    *   **Intergranular Corrosion:** Corrosion that preferentially attacks grain boundaries in a metal.
        *   **Mechanism:** Can occur if grain boundaries become sensitized due to depletion of alloying elements (e.g., chromium depletion in stainless steel near grain boundaries due to carbide precipitation at high temperatures).
        *   **Example:** "Welding decay" in certain stainless steels.
    *   **Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC):** Cracking that occurs in a metal under the combined action of tensile stress and a specific corrosive environment.
        *   **Mechanism:** Crack initiation at the surface, followed by propagation along a specific path (intergranular or transgranular) until catastrophic failure.
        *   **Example:** Chloride SCC in stainless steel, ammonia SCC in brass. (Budinski & Budinski, p. 514)
    *   **Erosion Corrosion:** Accelerated corrosion due to relative motion between a corrosive fluid and the metal surface.
        *   **Mechanism:** Mechanical removal of protective films, exposing fresh metal to the corrosive environment.
        *   **Example:** Flowing fluids in pipes, propellers.

#### 2.2. Methods of Corrosion Control and Prevention

Preventing or minimizing corrosion is crucial for extending the lifespan and maintaining the performance of metallic structures and components.

*   **Key Principle:** Disrupting the electrochemical cell or making the metal less susceptible to attack.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Material Selection:**
        *   Choosing inherently corrosion-resistant alloys (e.g., stainless steels, aluminum alloys, titanium alloys) for specific environments. (CO2, CO4)
        *   Using metals that are noble in the intended environment.
    *   **Environmental Modification:**
        *   **Deaeration:** Removing dissolved oxygen from water systems.
        *   **pH Control:** Adjusting the pH of the environment. For example, increasing pH can make many metals more passive.
        *   **Inhibitors:** Adding chemical substances that interfere with the anodic or cathodic reactions or form a protective film on the metal surface.
            *   **Examples:** Chromates, phosphates, organic inhibitors. (Askeland, p. 501)
    *   **Protective Coatings:** Applying a barrier layer to isolate the metal from the environment.
        *   **Metallic Coatings:**
            *   **Galvanizing:** Coating steel with zinc. Zinc acts as both a barrier and a sacrificial anode (corrodes preferentially to protect the steel). (Raghavan, p. 473)
            *   **Tin Plating:** Coating steel with tin. Tin is more noble than iron, so if the coating is breached, the steel will corrode preferentially (unless the tin layer is very thick or specific precautions are taken). Used for food cans.
            *   **Cadmium Plating:** Provides good corrosion resistance and sacrificial protection, but is toxic.
            *   **Chrome Plating:** Hard, decorative, and corrosion-resistant, but more expensive.
        *   **Non-Metallic Coatings:**
            *   **Paints and Organic Coatings:** Provide a barrier. Often contain pigments that can also offer corrosion inhibition (e.g., zinc chromate). Proper surface preparation is critical. (Budinski & Budinski, p. 533)
            *   **Ceramic Coatings (Enamels):** Vitreous coatings fused to metal surfaces, offering excellent chemical and wear resistance.
            *   **Polymer Coatings:** Various plastics applied as coatings.
    *   **Sacrificial Protection (Cathodic Protection):**
        *   **Concept:** Making the metal to be protected the cathode of an electrochemical cell.
        *   **Methods:**
            *   **Galvanic Anodes:** Connecting the metal to a more active (less noble) metal. The more active metal (anode) corrodes, supplying electrons to the protected metal (cathode).
                *   **Examples:** Zinc, magnesium, or aluminum anodes attached to steel structures (ships, pipelines, water heaters). (Callister & Rethwisch, p. 722)
            *   **Impressed Current Cathodic Protection (ICCP):** Using an external DC power source to supply electrons to the metal being protected. An inert anode is used in this system. Common for pipelines and large structures.
    *   **Design Modifications:**
        *   Avoiding crevices and sharp corners where stagnant conditions can develop.
        *   Ensuring good drainage to prevent water accumulation.
        *   Avoiding dissimilar metal contact where possible or insulating them if contact is necessary.
        *   Allowing for inspection and maintenance.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Ferrous metals (iron-based)** are generally strong and magnetic but prone to rust.
*   **Non-ferrous metals** offer diversity in properties like lightness, conductivity, and specific corrosion resistance.
*   **Corrosion is an electrochemical process** involving an anode, cathode, electrolyte, and electrical connection.
*   **Understanding the galvanic series** is crucial for predicting galvanic corrosion.
*   **Protective coatings** act as barriers or sacrificial layers.
*   **Cathodic protection** makes the metal the cathode, preventing oxidation.
*   **Material selection and design** are the first lines of defense against corrosion.

### Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
Which of the following is a non-ferrous metal?
a) Cast Iron
b) Stainless Steel
c) Aluminum
d) High Carbon Steel

**Answer:** c) Aluminum

**Explanation:** Aluminum is an element and its alloys do not contain iron as the primary constituent. Cast iron, stainless steel, and high carbon steel are all iron-based (ferrous) metals. (CO1, K2)

---

**Question 2:**
When two dissimilar metals are in electrical contact in the presence of an electrolyte, and one corrodes preferentially, this phenomenon is called:
a) Uniform Corrosion
b) Crevice Corrosion
c) Galvanic Corrosion
d) Pitting Corrosion

**Answer:** c) Galvanic Corrosion

**Explanation:** Galvanic corrosion occurs when dissimilar metals are coupled, leading to the more active metal corroding at an accelerated rate. (CO1, K2)

---

**Question 3:**
A common method of protecting steel from corrosion in a marine environment is to attach blocks of magnesium. What is the principle behind this method?

**Answer:** This method utilizes sacrificial protection, specifically galvanic anodes. Magnesium is more electrochemically active (less noble) than steel. When connected, magnesium acts as the anode and corrodes preferentially, supplying electrons to the steel (which becomes the cathode). This electron flow prevents the steel from oxidizing and corroding. (CO1, K2; CO2, K2)

---

**Question 4:**
Explain why stainless steel is resistant to corrosion and describe a situation where it might still corrode.

**Answer:** Stainless steel is resistant to corrosion primarily due to the presence of chromium. Chromium forms a thin, stable, and adherent passive oxide layer (chromium oxide, Cr₂O₃) on the surface of the steel when exposed to oxygen. This passive film acts as a barrier, preventing further chemical attack.

However, stainless steel can still corrode in certain situations:
1.  **Chloride Environments:** High concentrations of chloride ions (Cl⁻), found in saltwater or some industrial chemicals, can break down the passive film, leading to localized corrosion such as pitting and crevice corrosion.
2.  **Sensitization:** In certain stainless steels (especially older grades or improper heat treatments), heating to intermediate temperatures can cause chromium to precipitate as carbides at the grain boundaries. This depletes the adjacent regions of chromium, making the grain boundaries susceptible to intergranular corrosion.
3.  **Galvanic Corrosion:** If stainless steel is coupled with a much less noble metal in an electrolyte, it can become the cathode and suffer accelerated corrosion if the electrolyte conditions are aggressive. (CO1, K2; CO2, K2)

---

**Question 5:**
List three common types of protective coatings used for metals and briefly describe their function.

**Answer:**
1.  **Galvanizing (Zinc Coating):** Provides a barrier and sacrificial protection. Zinc is more active than iron, so it corrodes first, protecting the underlying steel.
2.  **Paints/Organic Coatings:** Act as a barrier to isolate the metal from the corrosive environment. Some pigments in the paint can also offer corrosion inhibition.
3.  **Tin Plating:** Primarily acts as a barrier coating. While tin is more noble than iron, if the coating is breached, the steel will corrode preferentially. It's chosen for its non-toxicity and ability to form a good seal (e.g., in food cans). (CO1, K2)
