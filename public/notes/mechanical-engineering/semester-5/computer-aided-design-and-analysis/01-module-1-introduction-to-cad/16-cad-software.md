### **Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **CAD (Computer-Aided Design):** The use of computer systems to assist in the creation, modification, analysis, or optimization of a design.
*   **CAD Software:** A specialized application program that provides tools and functionalities for performing CAD tasks.
*   **Geometric Modeling:** The process of creating and manipulating mathematical representations of geometric objects (points, lines, curves, surfaces, solids).
*   **Parametric Modeling:** A modeling approach where geometric entities are defined by parameters (dimensions, constraints, relationships). Changes to these parameters automatically update the model.
*   **Direct Modeling:** A modeling approach that allows for direct manipulation of geometry without relying on a feature history or parameters.
*   **2D Drafting:** Creating two-dimensional representations of designs, such as floor plans, schematics, and technical drawings.
*   **3D Modeling:** Creating three-dimensional representations of designs, allowing for visualization from multiple viewpoints and spatial understanding.
*   **Solid Modeling:** A type of 3D modeling that represents objects as solid volumes with properties like mass, density, and surface area. This is crucial for analysis and manufacturing.
*   **Surface Modeling:** A type of 3D modeling that represents objects using surfaces, often used for complex, freeform shapes like car bodies or aircraft wings.
*   **Parametric Feature-Based Modeling:** A common approach where models are built from a history of features (e.g., extrude, revolve, fillet) that are linked by parameters.
*   **Assembly Modeling:** Creating and managing multiple individual CAD models (parts) as a single, integrated unit (an assembly).
*   **Finite Element Analysis (FEA):** A numerical method used to predict how a product reacts to real-world forces, vibration, heat, fluid flow, and other physical effects. CAD software often integrates FEA capabilities.
*   **Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM):** The use of computer systems to plan, manage, and control manufacturing operations. CAD and CAM are often tightly integrated.

### **1. Functions and Capabilities of CAD Software (LO1)**

CAD software is a powerful tool that goes far beyond simple drawing. Its core functions include:

*   **Geometric Creation and Manipulation:**
    *   **2D Sketching:** Creating precise 2D entities like lines, arcs, circles, polygons, and splines, often with constraints (e.g., parallel, perpendicular, tangent).
    *   **3D Primitive Creation:** Generating basic 3D shapes like cubes, spheres, cylinders, cones, and tori.
    *   **Extrusion and Revolution:** Creating 3D solids by extending a 2D profile or revolving it around an axis.
    *   **Sweeping and Lofting:** Creating 3D solids by moving a 2D profile along a path (sweep) or blending between multiple profiles (loft).
    *   **Boolean Operations:** Combining or subtracting solid bodies (union, subtract, intersect).
    *   **Filleting and Chamfering:** Rounding or beveling edges to improve aesthetics or reduce stress concentrations.
    *   **Shelling:** Creating hollowed-out versions of solid objects.
    *   **Surface Generation and Manipulation:** Creating and modifying complex freeform surfaces for aesthetic or aerodynamic designs.

*   **Assembly Design:**
    *   **Component Placement:** Inserting and positioning individual part models within an assembly.
    *   **Mates and Constraints:** Defining relationships between components (e.g., coincident, concentric, distance) to control their relative positions and degrees of freedom.
    *   **Motion Studies:** Simulating the movement of assembled components.

*   **Documentation and Detailing:**
    *   **Automatic View Generation:** Creating standard orthographic views (top, front, side), isometric views, and detail views from 3D models.
    *   **Dimensioning and Tolerancing:** Adding precise measurements and manufacturing tolerances to drawings.
    *   **Bill of Materials (BOM):** Automatically generating lists of components in an assembly.
    *   **Annotation:** Adding notes, symbols, and other textual information to drawings.

*   **Analysis and Simulation Integration (FEA/CFD):**
    *   Many CAD systems offer integrated or add-on modules for Finite Element Analysis (FEA) and Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD).
    *   This allows engineers to simulate the behavior of a design under various conditions (stress, strain, heat transfer, fluid flow) directly within the CAD environment.
