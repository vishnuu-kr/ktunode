# MECHANICAL ENGINEERING LAB: MODULE 1 - ANGULAR MEASUREMENTS

## Topic: Angular Measurements using Sine Bar, Bevel Protractor, Combination Sets, Clinometers, Angle Dekkor, etc.

---

### **1. Introduction to Angular Measurement**

Angular measurement is crucial in mechanical engineering for ensuring the correct relative orientation of surfaces, components, and machine parts. Precise angular measurements are vital for the assembly, functioning, and performance of machinery.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Angle:** The figure formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (vertex).
*   **Units of Angular Measurement:** Degrees (°), Radians (rad), Minutes ('), Seconds (").
*   **Precision:** The degree of exactness of a measurement.
*   **Accuracy:** The closeness of a measurement to the true value.

---

### **2. Instruments for Angular Measurement**

This section details various instruments used for measuring angles, their principles of operation, and applications.

---

#### **2.1. Sine Bar**

**Description:**
A sine bar is a precision measuring instrument used for measuring or laying out angles. It utilizes the trigonometric relationship between an angle and the sine of that angle.

**Princ of Operation:**
Based on the sine rule: $\sin(\theta) = \frac{h}{L}$
Where:
*   $\theta$: The angle to be measured or set.
*   $h$: The vertical distance between the centers of the two rollers (or the difference in height of the two built-in standards).
*   $L$: The distance between the centers of the two rollers (standard length of the sine bar, usually 100 mm, 200 mm, or 300 mm).

**Construction:**
*   A hardened and ground steel bar with a high degree of flatness.
*   Two cylindrical steel rollers of equal diameter, accurately located at the ends of the bar.
*   The distance between the centers of these rollers is precisely known and is the standard length of the sine bar.
*   Some sine bars have built-in standards (pins) instead of rollers.

**Method of Use:**
1.  **Setting an Angle:**
    *   Place the sine bar on a surface plate.
    *   Determine the required angle $\theta$.
    *   Calculate the required height difference $h = L \sin(\theta)$.
    *   Using gauge blocks, stack them to achieve the calculated height $h$ under one of the rollers (or between the datum surface and the built-in standard).
    *   Ensure the surface of the workpiece is in contact with the top surface of the sine bar and the gauge blocks.
    *   The angle is now set between the workpiece surface and the surface plate.
2.  **Measuring an Angle:**
    *   Place the sine bar on a surface plate.
    *   Place the workpiece on the sine bar with one surface in contact with the bar.
    *   Place the other surface of the workpiece in contact with the roller.
    *   Use a dial indicator mounted on a stand to check for parallelism or perpendicularity with the surface plate by traversing the dial indicator along the sine bar.
    *   Adjust the height of the gauge blocks under the roller until the dial indicator shows zero deflection across the entire length of the sine bar.
    *   Measure the height $h$ of the gauge blocks.
    *   Calculate the angle using $\theta = \arcsin(\frac{h}{L})$.

**Applications:**
*   Machining of inclined surfaces on milling machines, lathes, and grinders.
*   Inspection of angles.
*   Setting up workpieces for angular operations.

**Important Points:**
*   Sine bars are most accurate when the angle is between 10° and 50°.
*   The accuracy depends heavily on the accuracy of the gauge blocks and the surface plate.
*   The distance between the rollers ($L$) must be known precisely.
*   The workpiece and sine bar must be clean and free from chips.
*   Contact points should be consistent and light.

**Textbook References:**
*   **Metrology for Engineers by Shotbolt C.R. and Gayler J.F.W:** Provides detailed descriptions of sine bar construction, principles, and usage for setting and measuring angles.
*   **Practical Engineering Metrology by Sharp K.W.B. and Hume Sir Isaac:** Offers practical guidance on using sine bars with gauge blocks for various machining and inspection tasks.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**
*   **CO1 (Choose appropriate instruments):** Understanding when a sine bar is the most suitable instrument for precise angular measurement.
*   **CO2 (Determine dimensional and form accuracies):** Using a sine bar to accurately set or measure angles on components, thereby verifying their dimensional accuracy.

#### **2.2. Bevel Protractor**

**Description:**
A bevel protractor is a precision instrument used for measuring and setting angles directly.

**Types:**
*   **Simple Bevel Protractor:** A graduated circular scale and a revolving arm with a magnifying glass.
*   **Universal Bevel Protractor:** More versatile, with interchangeable blades and attachments, allowing for more complex measurements.

**Princ of Operation:**
Direct reading of the angle from a graduated circular scale and a vernier scale for finer readings.

**Construction:**
*   **Base:** The part that rests on the surface.
*   **Blade:** A movable arm that can be set against the surface whose angle is to be measured.
*   **Stock:** A reference surface that is placed against the edge of the workpiece.
*   **Circular Scale:** A graduated disc (usually 360°) for reading the angle.
*   **Vernier Scale:** Used to obtain finer readings on the circular scale.

**Method of Use:**
1.  Place the stock of the protractor against one edge of the workpiece.
2.  Bring the blade in contact with the other edge of the workpiece.
3.  Read the angle directly from the main scale and the vernier scale.

**Applications:**
*   Measuring angles of machined surfaces.
*   Setting cutting tools at specific angles.
*   Checking angles of machine components.

**Important Points:**
*   Accuracy is dependent on the graduation of the scale and the vernier.
*   Ensure good contact between the stock, blade, and workpiece.
*   Cleanliness of the instrument and workpiece is essential.

**Textbook References:**
*   **Metrology for Engineers by Shotbolt C.R. and Gayler J.F.W:** Discusses different types of protractors and their use in measuring angles.
*   **Practical Engineering Metrology by Sharp K.W.B. and Hume Sir Isaac:** Provides practical examples of using bevel protractors in workshop environments.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**
*   **CO1 (Choose appropriate instruments):** Recognizing when a bevel protractor is suitable for direct angle measurement or setting.
*   **CO2 (Determine dimensional and form accuracies):** Using a bevel protractor to measure the angular deviation of a workpiece.

#### **2.3. Combination Sets**

**Description:**
A combination set is a versatile measuring tool that combines several measuring functions into one unit, including angle measurement.

**Components relevant to Angular Measurement:**
*   **Protractor Head:** Contains a revolving turret with a spirit level and a graduated scale, allowing for direct angle measurement up to 180°.
*   **Center Head:** Used for finding the center of a round bar or rectangular workpiece.
*   **Square Head:** Contains a spirit level and a 90° angle guide.

**Princ of Operation:**
The protractor head allows for direct reading of angles relative to a reference edge.

**Method of Use:**
1.  Attach the protractor head to the steel rule.
2.  Place the rule against one edge of the workpiece.
3.  Rotate the protractor head until the other edge of the workpiece is aligned with the index line on the protractor head.
4.  Read the angle directly from the scale.

**Applications:**
*   Measuring and setting 90° and 45° angles.
*   Checking squareness and parallelism.
*   Transferring angles.
*   Measuring depth and length.

**Important Points:**
*   Less precise than a sine bar or universal bevel protractor for fine angle measurements.
*   Useful for general-purpose angle checking and layout.
*   Ensure the rule is perpendicular to the protractor head for accurate 90° readings.

**Textbook References:**
*   **Practical Engineering Metrology by Sharp K.W.B. and Hume Sir Isaac:** Explains the various uses of a combination set, including its angular measuring capabilities.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**
*   **CO1 (Choose appropriate instruments):** Identifying the combination set as a convenient tool for general angular measurements and layout tasks.
*   **CO2 (Determine dimensional and form accuracies):** Using the protractor head to verify if angles on a workpiece conform to specifications.

#### **2.4. Clinometers**

**Description:**
A clinometer (also known as an inclinometer) is an instrument used for measuring angles of slope, elevation, or depression of an object with respect to gravity (vertical).

**Types:**
*   **Bubble Clinometer:** Uses a spirit level to indicate the angle.
*   **Pendulum Clinometer:** Uses a weighted pendulum that swings freely.
*   **Digital Clinometer:** Incorporates electronic sensors (like accelerometers or gyroscopes) for digital readout.

**Princ of Operation:**
*   **Bubble Clinometer:** The bubble in a spirit level tube indicates the deviation from the horizontal or vertical. The tube is often graduated to read angles directly.
*   **Pendulum Clinometer:** The pendulum hangs vertically. The angle of the workpiece relative to the pendulum indicates the slope.

**Method of Use (Bubble Clinometer):**
1.  Place the base of the clinometer on the surface whose angle is to be measured.
2.  The bubble in the spirit level tube will center when the surface is horizontal.
3.  Tilt the clinometer until the bubble is centered for an inclined surface.
4.  Read the angle from the graduated scale.

**Applications:**
*   Measuring slopes of ramps, bridges, and earthworks.
*   Setting angles on machine tools.
*   Measuring the inclination of shafts or pipes.
*   Used in surveying and construction.

**Important Points:**
*   Accuracy is dependent on the sensitivity of the spirit level or sensor.
*   Sensitive to vibrations.
*   Ensure the base is flat and makes good contact with the surface.

**Textbook References:**
*   **Metrology for Engineers by Shotbolt C.R. and Gayler J.F.W:** May touch upon clinometers in the context of angle measurement in broader metrology.
*   **Kinematics and Dynamics of Machinery by C.E.Wilson, P. Sadler:** While focusing on machinery, it might discuss the importance of understanding slopes and angles in machine analysis, where clinometers could be relevant for measurement.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**
*   **CO1 (Choose appropriate instruments):** Selecting a clinometer for measuring the slope of surfaces or the inclination of machine parts.
*   **CO2 (Determine dimensional and form accuracies):** Using a clinometer to assess the angular accuracy of inclined surfaces.

#### **2.5. Angle Dekkor (Auto Collimator)**

**Description:**
An Angle Dekkor (often a type of auto-collimator) is a highly accurate optical instrument used for measuring small angles very precisely. It projects a beam of light and measures the angular deviation by observing the reflected beam.

**Princ of Operation:**
*   An auto-collimator projects a collimated beam of light.
*   This beam is reflected off a mirror surface mounted on the workpiece.
*   The reflected beam returns to the auto-collimator and is viewed through a telescope equipped with a graticule (reticle).
*   Any angular tilt of the workpiece mirror causes a corresponding displacement of the reflected image on the graticule.
*   This displacement is calibrated to represent a very small angular change.

**Method of Use:**
1.  Set up the auto-collimator at a suitable distance from the workpiece.
2.  Mount a precisely made mirror flat on the surface whose angle is to be measured or set.
3.  Align the auto-collimator to target the mirror.
4.  Observe the graticule in the eyepiece.
5.  Adjust the workpiece or the auto-collimator to achieve the desired angular alignment (e.g., null reading on the graticule).
6.  For measuring, the angular change is read directly from the graticule or by precise movement of the auto-collimator.

**Applications:**
*   Measuring small angles with extreme precision (e.g., in metrology labs, optical instruments).
*   Checking squareness and flatness over long distances.
*   Alignment of machine tool components.
*   Measuring the wobble of rotating shafts.

**Important Points:**
*   Extremely high accuracy (can measure angles in seconds of arc).
*   Requires a stable environment and a good quality mirror.
*   Sensitive to vibrations.
*   Costly and complex to operate.

**Textbook References:**
*   **Metrology for Engineers by Shotbolt C.R. and Gayler J.F.W:** Likely covers auto-collimators as advanced metrology instruments for high-precision angular measurements.
*   **Practical Engineering Metrology by Sharp K.W.B. and Hume Sir Isaac:** May discuss optical methods for precise angle measurement, potentially including auto-collimators.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**
*   **CO1 (Choose appropriate instruments):** Identifying the auto-collimator as the instrument of choice for extremely precise small angle measurements.
*   **CO2 (Determine dimensional and form accuracies):** Using an auto-collimator to verify very fine angular accuracies on critical components.

### **3. Angular Measurements in Mechatronics and Machine Dynamics**

**Relationship to Mechatronics (Bolton, Ramachandran et al.):**
In mechatronic systems, precise control of angular position and velocity is paramount. Sensors like encoders and resolvers measure angular displacement, but the underlying calibration and verification of angular accuracy often rely on principles discussed here. For instance, controlling the angle of a robotic arm or the orientation of a platform requires accurate knowledge of angles, which might be initially set or verified using instruments like sine bars or protractors, or continuously monitored by integrated sensors.

**Relationship to Kinematics and Dynamics (Wilson & Sadler, Myskza):**
Understanding the kinematics and dynamics of machinery inherently involves analyzing angles. Mechanisms like linkages, gears, and cams operate based on specific angular relationships.
*   **Linkage Mechanisms:** The angles between links determine the position and orientation of other parts.
*   **Gear Trains:** The angular velocity ratio depends on the gear teeth profiles and arrangement.
*   **Cam Mechanisms:** The profile of a cam dictates the angular motion of the follower.

Measuring and setting these angles accurately using the instruments discussed is crucial for both designing and analyzing the performance of such machinery. For example, a sine bar could be used to set the angle of a connecting rod in a slider-crank mechanism during an experimental setup or for verifying its geometric accuracy.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**
*   **CO3 (Develop knowledge of designing and analyzing mechanisms):** The understanding of angular measurement instruments directly supports the ability to design mechanisms with specific angular requirements and analyze their motion.
*   **CO4 (Demonstrate the functions and control of various devices used for industrial automation):** While automation often uses digital sensors, the fundamental principles of angle measurement and the need for calibration remain, informing the understanding of how these systems are built and verified.

### **4. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
A sine bar of 200 mm length is used to machine an angle of 30°. What is the required height difference ($h$) to be set using gauge blocks?
*(Answer: $h = L \sin(\theta) = 200 \text{ mm} \times \sin(30^\circ) = 200 \text{ mm} \times 0.5 = 100 \text{ mm}$)*

**Question 2:**
While measuring an angle using a 100 mm sine bar, a height of 45.25 mm is achieved using gauge blocks. Calculate the angle being measured.
*(Answer: $\theta = \arcsin(\frac{h}{L}) = \arcsin(\frac{45.25 \text{ mm}}{100 \text{ mm}}) = \arcsin(0.4525) \approx 26.90^\circ$)*

**Question 3:**
Which instrument is most suitable for measuring a small angle of 5 minutes of arc with high precision?
*(Answer: Angle Dekkor / Auto-Collimator)*

**Question 4:**
A combination set is used to check if a machined surface is at 90° to a reference face. What component of the combination set is primarily used for this?
*(Answer: The Square Head or the Protractor Head)*

**Question 5:**
Explain the primary difference in the working principle between a sine bar and a bevel protractor.
*(Answer: A sine bar uses trigonometry with gauge blocks to set or measure angles indirectly, relying on a known length and calculated height. A bevel protractor directly measures angles using a graduated circular scale and vernier.)*

### **5. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Accuracy vs. Precision:** Understand the difference and how each instrument contributes to achieving them.
*   **Sine Bar Limitations:** Most accurate between 10°-50°; relies heavily on gauge block accuracy.
*   **Direct vs. Indirect Measurement:** Bevel protractors and clinometers offer direct readings, while sine bars provide indirect measurement via height calculation.
*   **Surface Finish:** Ensure workpieces and instrument contact surfaces are clean and free from burrs or foreign matter for accurate readings.
*   **Environmental Factors:** Temperature variations can affect the accuracy of precision instruments.
*   **Instrument Selection:** Choose the instrument based on the required precision, the nature of the measurement (setting vs. checking), and the available resources.
*   **Calibration:** All measuring instruments, including those for angular measurement, require periodic calibration to ensure their accuracy.
