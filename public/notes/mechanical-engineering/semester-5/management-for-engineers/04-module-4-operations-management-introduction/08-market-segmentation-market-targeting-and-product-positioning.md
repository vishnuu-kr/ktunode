## TOPIC: MARKET SEGMENTATION, MARKET TARGETING, AND PRODUCT POSITIONING

This topic delves into the fundamental strategic marketing concepts that guide businesses in understanding their customers and effectively delivering value. For engineers, these concepts are crucial for developing products and services that meet market needs and for contributing to the overall success of an organization.

### 1. INTRODUCTION TO MARKETING STRATEGY

Before diving into segmentation, targeting, and positioning (STP), it's essential to understand their place within the broader marketing strategy. Marketing, in essence, is the process of creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large (Kotler, Keller, Chernev, & Sheth, 2018). For engineers, understanding marketing helps them to not just build products, but to build the *right* products for the *right* people.

**Key Concept:** **Marketing Strategy** – A firm's overall plan for reaching potential consumers and transforming them into customers of their products or services. It involves understanding the market, identifying customer needs, and developing a plan to meet those needs profitably.

**(Koontz, Weihrich, & Cannice, 2020)** emphasize that effective management involves understanding the environment in which the organization operates. The market is a key component of this environment.

### 2. MARKET SEGMENTATION

Market segmentation is the process of dividing a broad consumer or business market, both existing and potential, into sub-groups of consumers (known as segments) based on some type of shared characteristics. The goal is to identify groups of customers with similar needs, wants, and buying behaviors, allowing businesses to tailor their marketing efforts more effectively.

**Importance of Market Segmentation:**

*   **Better Customer Understanding:** Helps in understanding the diverse needs and preferences within a market.
*   **Efficient Resource Allocation:** Allows companies to focus their marketing budgets and efforts on the most promising segments.
*   **Development of Targeted Products/Services:** Enables the creation of offerings that precisely meet the needs of specific customer groups.
*   **Enhanced Customer Satisfaction:** By catering to specific needs, companies can improve customer loyalty.
*   **Competitive Advantage:** Allows companies to identify underserved niches and gain a competitive edge.

**(Mahadevan, 2018)** highlights that operations management needs to be aligned with market demands. Market segmentation provides the foundational understanding of these demands.

**Bases for Segmenting Consumer Markets:**

*   **Geographic Segmentation:** Dividing the market based on geographical units such as nations, states, regions, counties, cities, or neighborhoods.
    *   *Example:* A clothing retailer might offer heavier, warmer clothing in colder regions and lighter clothing in warmer regions.
*   **Demographic Segmentation:** Dividing the market based on demographic variables such as age, gender, family size, family life cycle, income, occupation, education, religion, race, generation, and nationality.
    *   *Example:* Toy manufacturers segment their markets by age groups (e.g., toddlers, preschoolers, school-aged children). Luxury car brands often target high-income individuals.
*   **Psychographic Segmentation:** Dividing the market based on lifestyle, personality, values, attitudes, and interests. This is about understanding *why* people buy.
    *   *Example:* A company selling outdoor adventure gear might target individuals who are adventurous, enjoy nature, and have an active lifestyle.
*   **Behavioral Segmentation:** Dividing the market based on consumer knowledge, attitudes, uses, or responses to a product. This includes:
    *   **Occasion Segmentation:** Dividing the market based on specific times or occasions when buyers get the idea to buy, actually buy, or use the purchased item (e.g., holidays, birthdays).
    *   **Benefit Segmentation:** Dividing the market based on the benefits consumers seek from the product (e.g., convenience, durability, low price, status).
    *   **User Status:** Dividing the market into non-users, ex-users, potential users, first-time users, and regular users.
    *   **Usage Rate:** Dividing the market into light, medium, and heavy product users.
    *   **Loyalty Status:** Dividing the market based on customer loyalty to brands.
    *   *Example:* Airlines segment frequent flyers based on their loyalty status (e.g., Silver, Gold, Platinum), offering different perks.

**Bases for Segmenting Business Markets (Business-to-Business - B2B):**

*   **Demographic:** Industry, company size, location.
*   **Operating Variables:** Technology, user/non-user status, customer capabilities.
*   **Purchasing Approaches:** Purchasing function organization, power structure, nature of existing relationships, general purchasing policies, purchasing criteria.
*   **Situational Factors:** Urgency, specific application, size or order.
*   **Personal Characteristics:** Buyer-seller similarity, attitude toward risk, loyalty.

**(Tripathi & Reddy, 2017)** discuss the importance of understanding customer needs for effective management, and market segmentation is a primary tool for this.

### 3. MARKET TARGETING

Market targeting involves evaluating the attractiveness of each market segment and selecting one or more segments to enter. Once segments are identified, the company must decide which ones offer the greatest opportunity.

**Evaluating Market Segments:**

Companies evaluate segments based on several factors:

1.  **Segment Size and Growth:** Is the segment large enough to be profitable? Is it growing?
2.  **Segment Structural Attractiveness:** Consider factors like the intensity of existing competition, threat of new entrants, bargaining power of buyers, bargaining power of suppliers, and the availability of substitute products.
3.  **Company Objectives and Resources:** Does the segment fit with the company's long-term objectives? Does the company have the skills and resources to succeed in this segment?

**(Koontz, Weihrich, & Cannice, 2020)** stress that management must select strategies that align with the organization's capabilities and goals. Targeting a segment that cannot be effectively served is poor management.

**Strategies for Market Targeting:**

*   **Undifferentiated (Mass) Marketing:** A market coverage strategy in which a firm decides to ignore market differences and go after the whole market with one offer.
    *   *Focus:* Common needs rather than differences.
    *   *Pros:* Economies of scale in production and marketing.
    *   *Cons:* Difficult to satisfy everyone; vulnerable to competitors who target specific segments better.
    *   *Example:* Early Ford Model T ("any color as long as it's black").
*   **Differentiated (Segmented) Marketing:** A market-coverage strategy in which a firm decides to target several market segments and designs separate offers for each.
    *   *Focus:* Tailoring products and marketing mixes to specific segments.
    *   *Pros:* Higher sales and stronger position within each segment.
    *   *Cons:* Higher costs of production, marketing, and research.
    *   *Example:* Toyota offers different car models like the Camry (family sedan), Corolla (compact car), and Tundra (pickup truck) to cater to different consumer needs and preferences.
*   **Concentrated (Niche) Marketing:** A market-coverage strategy in which a firm goes after a large share of one or a few smaller segments or niches.
    *   *Focus:* Deep understanding and serving a specific, often smaller, group.
    *   *Pros:* Strong market position, high loyalty, efficient operations when well-executed.
    *   *Cons:* High risk if the segment declines or attracts many competitors.
    *   *Example:* A company specializing in custom-built orthotics for athletes.
*   **Micromarketing:** Tailoring products and marketing programs to the needs and wants of specific individuals or local customer groups. This includes:
    *   **Local Marketing:** Tailoring brands and promotions to the needs and wants of local customer groups (cities, neighborhoods, specific stores).
    *   **Individual Marketing:** Tailoring products and marketing programs to the needs and preferences of individual customers (mass customization).
    *   *Example:* Amazon's personalized recommendations based on browsing and purchase history. Local restaurants might offer specials tailored to local events.

**(Kotler, Keller, Chernev, & Sheth, 2018)** emphasize that choosing the right target market is a critical step in developing effective marketing strategies.

### 4. PRODUCT POSITIONING

Product positioning is the act of designing the company's offering and image to occupy a clear, distinctive, and desirable place relative to competing products in the minds of target consumers. It's about creating a perception in the customer's mind about what your product is and what it stands for.

**Importance of Product Positioning:**

*   **Clarifies Value Proposition:** Helps customers understand the unique benefits your product offers.
*   **Differentiates from Competition:** Sets your product apart from similar offerings.
*   **Guides Marketing Efforts:** Informs product development, pricing, promotion, and distribution decisions.
*   **Builds Brand Identity:** Creates a consistent and recognizable image for the product.

**(Griffin, 2017)** notes that effective management involves understanding how to communicate value to stakeholders, and product positioning is a key aspect of communicating value to customers.

**Bases for Positioning:**

Companies can position their products on the basis of:

*   **Specific Product Attributes:** Focusing on unique features or quality.
    *   *Example:* Volvo is often positioned on safety attributes.
*   **Benefits Offered:** Highlighting what the product does for the customer.
    *   *Example:* Crest toothpaste positioned on cavity prevention.
*   **Usage or Application:** Associating the product with a specific use or occasion.
    *   *Example:* Gatorade positioned for sports hydration.
*   **User:** Associating the product with a particular type of user.
    *   *Example:* Marlboro cigarettes were initially positioned for rugged cowboys.
*   **Against a Competitor:** Direct comparison to a rival.
    *   *Example:* Avis's "We Try Harder" campaign positioned against Hertz.
*   **Product Category:** Positioning the product as a leader or unique within its category.
    *   *Example:* A new protein bar positioned as the "healthiest option in the category."
*   **Price/Quality:** High price for high quality, or low price for value.
    *   *Example:* Rolex is positioned on high quality and luxury, while Walmart is positioned on low prices and value.

**Developing a Positioning Strategy (Positioning Statement):**

A good positioning statement is often structured as follows:

**"To [target segment], our [brand/product] is [frame of reference] that [point of difference]."**

*   **Target Segment:** Who are you trying to reach?
*   **Frame of Reference:** What category does your product belong to?
*   **Point of Difference:** What makes your product unique and superior within that category?

*Example:* **"To busy professionals, Starbucks is the premium coffeehouse that provides a high-quality, convenient, and comfortable experience, offering a welcome escape from the daily grind."**

**(Mahadevan, 2018)** discusses how operations must support the product's market promise, which is heavily influenced by its positioning.

### 5. THE MARKETING MIX (4 Ps) AND STP

The STP framework directly influences the Marketing Mix (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) – the set of tactical marketing tools that a firm blends to produce the response it wants in the target market.

*   **Product:** Based on segmentation and targeting, the product features, design, quality, branding, and packaging are developed to appeal to the chosen segments.
*   **Price:** Pricing strategies (e.g., premium, economy) are set considering the target market's willingness to pay and the perceived value.
*   **Place (Distribution):** Distribution channels (e.g., online, retail, wholesale) are selected to ensure the product is accessible to the target consumers.
*   **Promotion:** Communication strategies (advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, public relations) are designed to convey the product's positioning to the target market.

**(Koontz, Weihrich, & Cannice, 2020)** highlight the importance of an integrated approach in management, and how marketing mix elements must be coordinated to achieve organizational objectives.

### 6. EXAMPLE APPLICATION (Hypothetical Scenario)

Let's consider an engineering firm that designs and manufactures advanced prosthetic limbs.

1.  **Market Segmentation:**
    *   **Demographic:** Age (young athletes vs. elderly individuals), income levels (affordability of advanced prosthetics).
    *   **Psychographic:** Lifestyle (active vs. sedentary), desire for independence, interest in sports or hobbies.
    *   **Behavioral:** User status (first-time amputee vs. long-term user), benefit sought (durability, aesthetics, enhanced mobility, specialized sports functionality).
    *   **Geographic:** Access to specialized rehabilitation centers, local support networks.

    *Possible Segments Identified:*
    *   Segment A: Young, active amputee athletes seeking high-performance, lightweight prosthetics for competitive sports.
    *   Segment B: Older adults seeking comfortable, durable, and easy-to-use prosthetics for daily mobility and independence.
    *   Segment C: Individuals with moderate income seeking reliable, functional prosthetics at a competitive price point.

2.  **Market Targeting:**
    *   The firm analyzes these segments. Segment A shows high growth potential due to increasing interest in adaptive sports. Segment B represents a stable but significant market. Segment C might be too price-sensitive given the high cost of advanced technology.
    *   **Decision:** The firm decides to target Segment A (young athletes) and Segment B (older adults) as primary targets, leveraging their advanced technology and design capabilities.

3.  **Product Positioning:**
    *   **For Segment A (Young Athletes):** Position the prosthetic as the **"Ultimate performance enhancer for ambitious athletes,"** emphasizing its cutting-edge technology, lightweight design, responsiveness, and ability to help them achieve peak performance. *Point of Difference:* Superior biomechanics for athletic activity.
    *   **For Segment B (Older Adults):** Position the prosthetic as the **"Freedom to live independently,"** highlighting its comfort, durability, ease of use, and reliable support for everyday activities. *Point of Difference:* Unmatched comfort and longevity for daily life.

**Marketing Mix Implications:**

*   **Product:** Different models or customization options for each segment (e.g., specialized ankle/foot for athletes, ergonomic socket for older adults).
*   **Price:** Premium pricing for the athlete model (high R&D, specialized features) and a tiered pricing structure for the older adult segment (depending on customization and materials).
*   **Place:** Distribution through specialized prosthetics clinics, sports medicine centers, and direct-to-consumer online channels with robust virtual consultation options.
*   **Promotion:**
    *   *Segment A:* Sponsorship of adaptive sports events, partnerships with sports influencers, targeted digital advertising on sports platforms.
    *   *Segment B:* Partnerships with geriatric care providers, testimonials from satisfied users, educational content on maintaining independence, presence at health fairs.

This example shows how understanding different customer groups allows an engineering firm to tailor its product development and marketing strategies for maximum impact.

### 7. IMPORTANT POINTS TO REMEMBER

*   **STP is sequential:** Segmentation comes first, followed by targeting, and then positioning.
*   **Customer-centricity:** All these processes revolve around understanding and meeting customer needs.
*   **Data-driven decisions:** Effective segmentation and targeting rely on market research and data analysis.
*   **Dynamic nature:** Markets change, so segmentation and targeting strategies need periodic review and adjustment.
*   **Alignment is key:** Product positioning must be supported by the actual product and the entire marketing mix. For engineers, this means the product's design and manufacturing must deliver on the positioning promise.
*   **Competitive landscape:** Understanding competitors is crucial for identifying unique selling propositions and effective positioning.

### 8. PRACTICE QUESTIONS & EXERCISES

**Question 1:**
A company that manufactures electric scooters is considering entering a new city. What are at least three different bases they could use to segment the potential market for their scooters in this city, and provide a brief example for each?

**Answer:**
1.  **Geographic Segmentation:** They could segment by neighborhoods. For instance, offering different scooter models or rental plans in dense urban centers (requiring nimble, short-range scooters) versus suburban areas (potentially needing longer-range scooters or different pricing).
2.  **Demographic Segmentation:** They could segment by age. Younger adults (18-30) might be targeted with trendy, app-integrated scooters for commuting and leisure, while older adults (50+) might be targeted with more stable, user-friendly models for local errands.
3.  **Behavioral Segmentation:** They could segment by usage patterns. This could include "commuters" (seeking efficiency and reliability), "leisure riders" (seeking fun and recreational use), or "eco-conscious consumers" (prioritizing sustainability and low environmental impact).

**Question 2:**
Imagine you are an engineer working on a new type of smart home device that monitors air quality and purifies it. You've identified two potential target segments: health-conscious families with young children, and tech-savvy individuals living in urban apartments.
a) Choose one segment and develop a positioning statement for the device.
b) Briefly explain how the marketing mix (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) might differ for the *other* segment based on your chosen positioning.

**Answer:**
a) **Chosen Segment: Health-conscious families with young children.**
    **Positioning Statement:** "To health-conscious families with young children, our AuraPure Smart Air Monitor is the advanced home purification system that ensures a safe and healthy indoor environment, protecting your loved ones from airborne pollutants and allergens."

b) **Marketing Mix for the *other* segment (Tech-savvy individuals in urban apartments), assuming the positioning is: "To urban dwellers seeking smart home integration, AuraPure is the intelligent air quality system that seamlessly connects to your digital ecosystem, providing real-time data and automated purification for a cleaner, connected living space."**

*   **Product:** Focus on sleek design, minimal footprint, advanced app features (integration with other smart home devices like Alexa/Google Assistant), detailed data analytics and historical tracking, and perhaps customizable purification schedules via the app.
*   **Price:** Potentially a slightly higher price point due to advanced tech integration and smart features, perhaps with a subscription option for premium data analytics.
*   **Place:** Primarily online retail (e.g., Amazon, company website), electronics stores, and potentially partnerships with smart home installers. Less emphasis on traditional appliance stores.
*   **Promotion:** Digital marketing campaigns on tech blogs and social media, influencer marketing with tech reviewers, targeted ads on platforms frequented by tech enthusiasts, showcasing app functionality and smart home integration.

**Question 3:**
Why is it important for engineers to understand market segmentation, targeting, and product positioning, even if they are not directly in the marketing department? (Relate to CO1 and CO5)

**Answer:**
It is crucial for engineers to understand these concepts for several reasons, directly addressing CO1 and CO5:

*   **CO1 (Critical Analysis of Management Practices):** Understanding STP allows engineers to critically analyze how marketing decisions impact product development and operations. They can better understand *why* certain product features are prioritized, why specific quality standards are set, and how the target market influences design choices. This holistic view enables them to contribute more effectively to strategic decision-making and avoid developing products that don't align with market needs.
*   **CO5 (Understanding Functional Areas):** Marketing is a fundamental functional area of management. By understanding STP, engineers gain insight into how the marketing function operates and how it interacts with engineering and operations. This knowledge helps in fostering inter-departmental collaboration, ensuring that product design specifications are informed by market insights and that operational capabilities are aligned with marketing promises. It bridges the gap between "what can be built" and "what should be built for the market."

Essentially, an engineer who understands STP can design and produce products that are not only technically sound but also desirable, viable, and deliver value to the intended customers, ultimately contributing to the organization's success.
