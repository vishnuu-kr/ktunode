# DESIGN FOR MANUFACTURE AND ASSEMBLY

## Module 2: Introduction to Assembly: The Assembly Process

### Topic: Design Recommendations for Fasteners

---

### **1. Introduction to Fasteners in Assembly**

Fasteners are critical components in product assembly, used to join two or more parts together. Efficient and effective fastener selection and design significantly impact the overall assembly time, cost, and reliability. This topic focuses on design recommendations to optimize the use of fasteners during the assembly process, catering to both manual and automated assembly scenarios.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Fastener:** A mechanical device that holds two or more objects together or prevents motion between them.
*   **Assembly:** The process of joining individual components to create a finished product.
*   **Design for Assembly (DFA):** A design philosophy that aims to reduce the number of parts and simplify the assembly process to minimize cost and improve efficiency.

**Reference:**
*   Boothroyd, Dewhurst, & Knight (2010), Chapter 4: "Fasteners and Joining Methods," discusses various fastener types and their assembly considerations.
*   Chitale & Gupta (2011), Chapter 14: "Assembly," provides an overview of assembly operations and the role of fasteners.

### **2. Objectives of Design Recommendations for Fasteners**

The primary goal of these recommendations is to:

*   **Minimize Assembly Time:** Reduce the time taken to insert and tighten fasteners.
*   **Reduce Assembly Costs:** Lower the cost of fasteners and the labor associated with their use.
*   **Improve Assembly Reliability:** Ensure secure and consistent joint integrity.
*   **Facilitate Automation:** Design for easy handling and feeding of fasteners in automated assembly systems.
*   **Simplify Maintenance and Disassembly:** Allow for easy removal and reassembly of components when necessary.

### **3. General Design Principles for Fasteners in Assembly**

These principles aim to optimize the selection and application of fasteners to facilitate efficient assembly.

#### **3.1. Minimize the Number of Fasteners**

*   **Principle:** Use the fewest possible fasteners to achieve the required joint strength and rigidity.
*   **Rationale:** Each fastener adds time and cost to the assembly process (insertion, tightening, handling).
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   Consider alternative joining methods like welding, riveting, snap-fits, or integral features where appropriate.
    *   Evaluate if a single fastener can replace multiple fasteners.
    *   Design parts with self-locating features that reduce the need for precise alignment often achieved by multiple fasteners.
*   **Example:** Instead of using four screws to secure a cover, explore if two strategically placed screws, combined with snap-fit features on the cover, can suffice.

**Important Point to Remember:** Every fastener is an opportunity for error and time consumption. Always question the necessity of each fastener.

#### **3.2. Use Standardized Fasteners**

*   **Principle:** Whenever possible, utilize standard, off-the-shelf fasteners rather than custom-designed ones.
*   **Rationale:** Standard fasteners are readily available, cost-effective, and have well-established manufacturing processes, ensuring consistent quality.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   Adhere to industry standards (e.g., ISO, ANSI, DIN) for thread types, sizes, and head styles.
    *   Maintain a limited range of fastener types and sizes throughout a product to reduce inventory and tooling requirements.
*   **Example:** Using M4 screws with standard Phillips or Torx heads instead of a custom-threaded, uniquely headed bolt.

**Reference:**
*   Bralla (1998), Section 3.1: "Fasteners," provides information on common fastener types and their applications.

#### **3.3. Facilitate Easy Insertion and Alignment**

*   **Principle:** Design fastener holes and fastener heads to allow for quick and accurate insertion without requiring excessive manipulation.
*   **Rationale:** Misalignment and difficulty in starting a fastener are major sources of assembly time and frustration.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   **Chamfered Holes:** Provide a lead-in chamfer (typically 45 degrees) on the entrance of screw holes to guide the screw thread. This is crucial for both manual and automated assembly.
    *   **Clearance Holes:** Ensure sufficient clearance between the fastener shank and the hole to allow for easy insertion, especially in thicker materials. (Refer to fastener standards for recommended clearance sizes.)
    *   **Self-Tapping Screws:** Where appropriate, consider self-tapping screws that create their own threads, reducing the need for pre-tapping operations. Design holes with appropriate pilot diameters for self-tapping screws.
    *   **Captive Fasteners:** For components that require frequent disassembly and reassembly, use captive fasteners (e.g., screws attached to a specific part) to prevent them from being lost.
    *   **Locating Pins/Features:** Incorporate locating pins or other features that help align parts before fasteners are inserted, ensuring correct positioning.
*   **Example:** Designing a bolt hole with a 1mm chamfer on a plate to easily guide the bolt. Using a snap-fit mechanism to pre-align two parts before inserting a single securing screw.

**Reference:**
*   Boothroyd (2005), Chapter 5: "Fastener Insertion," details strategies for optimizing fastener insertion.
*   Molloy, Tilley, & Warman (1998), Chapter 3: "Design of Components for Ease of Assembly," discusses features that aid fastener insertion.

#### **3.4. Design for Easy Tightening and Torque Control**

*   **Principle:** Select fastener head types and access points that allow for easy and efficient tightening to the desired torque.
*   **Rationale:** Inefficient access for tightening leads to longer assembly cycles and potential for under- or over-tightening, affecting joint integrity.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   **Head Styles:** Choose head styles (e.g., Phillips, Torx, Allen) that are compatible with standard tooling and offer good engagement to prevent cam-out (slipping).
    *   **Access Clearance:** Ensure sufficient clearance around the fastener head for the chosen tooling (e.g., screwdriver, wrench, nut runner).
    *   **Thread Engagement:** Design for adequate thread engagement to achieve the required clamping force without over-stressing the threads. Generally, at least 1 to 1.5 times the fastener's major diameter is recommended.
    *   **Locking Mechanisms:** If vibration or thermal cycling is a concern, consider integrated locking features on fasteners (e.g., pre-applied thread-locking compounds, lock washers) or design for their inclusion.
*   **Example:** Using Torx (star) drive screws for better tool engagement and reduced cam-out compared to Phillips screws, especially in high-volume production. Ensuring enough space around a bolt head to use a powered torque wrench.

**Important Point to Remember:** Torque is critical for joint reliability. Design for consistent and controllable torque application.

#### **3.5. Design for High-Volume/Automated Assembly**

*   **Principle:** Adapt fastener designs and application processes for efficient handling by automated assembly equipment.
*   **Rationale:** Automation requires consistent part orientation, ease of feeding, and reliable insertion.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   **Bulk Feeding:** Design fasteners with features that allow them to be easily fed from bulk hoppers (e.g., consistent head shape, no sharp protrusions).
    *   **Oriented Fasteners:** Consider screws with features that inherently orient them (e.g., recessed heads) or design feeding mechanisms that ensure correct orientation.
    *   **Magnetic Handling:** For certain fastener types, consider materials that can be handled by magnetic feeders.
    *   **Self-Locking Threads:** Explore thread-forming screws that create their own threads in mating holes, eliminating the need for separate nuts and simplifying the feeding process.
    *   **Threadless Fasteners (e.g., Rivets, Snap-Fits):** These can often be more easily automated than threaded fasteners.
*   **Example:** Using screws with a hex socket head, which are easily picked and oriented by robotic grippers and bowl feeders. Designing a product so that all screws are oriented the same way for a single robotic screwdriver.

**Reference:**
*   Boothroyd (2005), Chapter 11: "Product Design for Assembly Automation," covers considerations for automated assembly of components, including fasteners.

#### **3.6. Design for Ergonomics and Safety (Manual Assembly)**

*   **Principle:** Ensure that fastener handling and tightening are comfortable and safe for human assemblers.
*   **Rationale:** Poor ergonomics can lead to repetitive strain injuries and reduced assembly speed. Safety is paramount.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   **Minimize Awkward Gripping:** Avoid requiring assemblers to hold small fasteners in awkward positions for extended periods.
    *   **Tool Compatibility:** Ensure fastener heads are compatible with standard, ergonomic assembly tools.
    *   **Prevent Sharp Edges:** Ensure no sharp edges on fasteners or mating parts that could cause injury.
    *   **Weight Consideration:** For large assemblies, consider the weight of tools required for fastening.
*   **Example:** Using screws with larger, easily graspable heads or providing a small feature to hold a screw in place while starting it.

**Reference:**
*   Dieter (2000), Chapter 11: "Human Factors in Design," touches upon ergonomic considerations in product design.

### **4. Specific Design Recommendations for Common Fastener Types**

#### **4.1. Screws**

*   **Head Styles:**
    *   **Phillips:** Common but prone to cam-out.
    *   **Torx (Star Drive):** Excellent torque transfer, less cam-out, good for automation.
    *   **Allen (Hex Socket):** Good for confined spaces, good torque transfer.
    *   **Slotted:** Simple but difficult to align and prone to slippage.
*   **Thread Types:**
    *   **Machine Screws:** Require pre-tapped holes or nuts.
    *   **Self-Tapping Screws:** Create their own threads. Choose the correct type for the mating material (e.g., thread-forming, thread-cutting).
    *   **Thread-Forming Screws:** Excellent for high-volume assembly as they eliminate the tapping step.
*   **Hole Design:**
    *   Chamfered entrance for easy starting.
    *   Clearance hole of appropriate size.
    *   Consider captive screw designs (e.g., threaded inserts with retained screws).

#### **4.2. Nuts**

*   **Standard Nuts:** Require bolt or screw to pass through.
*   **Captive Nuts:** Designed to be permanently attached to one part, eliminating the need to handle a separate nut during assembly. Can be press-fit, staked, or welded.
*   **Wing Nuts/Thumb Nuts:** For applications where frequent hand tightening/loosening is required, but not suitable for automated assembly.

#### **4.3. Rivets**

*   **Pop Rivets/Blind Rivets:** Easy to install from one side, good for joining dissimilar materials or when access to the backside is limited.
*   **Solid Rivets:** Require access to both sides for installation and forming.
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   Proper hole preparation (chamfer is beneficial).
    *   Consider rivet material and diameter for strength requirements.
    *   Automated riveting systems are highly efficient.

#### **4.4. Other Fastening Methods (Briefly Mentioned for Context)**

*   **Snap-Fits:** Integral features on parts that clip together. Highly efficient for assembly if designed correctly.
*   **Adhesives:** Bonding parts together, can eliminate fasteners entirely.
*   **Welding/Brazing:** Permanent joining methods.

**Reference:**
*   Chitale & Gupta (2011), Chapter 14: "Assembly," covers various joining methods including fasteners and rivets.
*   Boothroyd, Dewhurst, & Knight (2010), Chapter 4: "Fasteners and Joining Methods," provides in-depth coverage of different fastener types.

### **5. Design for Disassembly (DFD) using Fasteners**

*   **Principle:** Design assemblies so that they can be easily taken apart for maintenance, repair, or end-of-life recycling.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   Use threaded fasteners that can be removed and reinserted.
    *   Avoid permanent joining methods like strong adhesives or irreversible welding if disassembly is a requirement.
    *   Clearly label or color-code different fastener types if multiple types are used.
    *   Ensure tools for disassembly are readily available and easy to use.

### **6. Practice Questions**

**Question 1:**
Which of the following fastener head styles generally offers the best engagement and is less prone to cam-out, making it suitable for automated assembly?
(a) Slotted
(b) Phillips
(c) Torx (Star Drive)
(d) Flat Head

**Answer:** (c) Torx (Star Drive)

**Question 2:**
You are designing a plastic cover that needs to be attached to a metal chassis. What fastener design consideration would you prioritize to minimize assembly time in a high-volume production environment?
(a) Using custom-designed screws with a unique head shape for branding.
(b) Designing the plastic cover with integrated snap-fits and minimizing the number of threaded fasteners.
(c) Specifying large, decorative nuts for a hand-tightened assembly.
(d) Using very fine thread screws that require precise alignment.

**Answer:** (b) Designing the plastic cover with integrated snap-fits and minimizing the number of threaded fasteners.

**Question 3:**
Describe the importance of chamfering screw holes in the context of DFA.

**Answer:** Chamfering screw holes provides a lead-in ramp that guides the screw thread into the hole, making insertion faster and easier, especially for automated assembly. It reduces the risk of cross-threading and misalignment.

**Question 4:**
Explain why minimizing the number of fasteners is a core principle of Design for Assembly.

**Answer:** Each fastener adds to the assembly process through handling, insertion, and tightening. Reducing the number of fasteners directly reduces the total assembly time, labor costs, and the potential for assembly errors.

**Question 5:**
What are the advantages of using standard fasteners over custom-designed fasteners in product assembly?

**Answer:** Standard fasteners are readily available, lower in cost due to mass production, and have consistent quality and performance characteristics. This simplifies procurement, reduces inventory, and ensures reliable assembly.

---

### **7. Key Takeaways**

*   **Simplicity is Key:** Aim to reduce the number of fasteners and use standard types.
*   **Ease of Handling:** Design for easy insertion and alignment with chamfered holes and appropriate clearance.
*   **Tool Compatibility:** Ensure easy access for tightening with standard tooling and consider torque control.
*   **Automation Readiness:** Design fasteners and their application for efficient handling by robotic systems.
*   **Disassembly Matters:** Consider how fasteners will affect the product's life cycle for maintenance and recycling.
