# Module 4: Energy Storage - Control of Air and Water Pollution from Power Plants

## 1. Introduction to Environmental Concerns in Power Plants

Power plants, while essential for electricity generation, are significant sources of environmental pollution. This module focuses on mitigating some of the most critical pollutants: particulate matter, sulfur dioxide (SO2), and wastewater. Understanding these control mechanisms is crucial for sustainable power generation and achieving Course Outcomes like CO5 (Discuss the economics of power generation and pollution from power plants and their effect on the environment).

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Environmental Impact:** The adverse effects of power plant emissions and discharges on air, water, soil, and ecosystems.
*   **Pollution Control:** Technologies and strategies employed to reduce or eliminate harmful substances released from power plants.
*   **Regulatory Standards:** Government-mandated limits on pollutant concentrations in emissions and discharges, aimed at protecting public health and the environment.

## 2. Control of Particulate Matter (PM)

Particulate matter (PM) refers to a complex mixture of solid and liquid particles suspended in the atmosphere. In power plants, the primary source of PM is the combustion of fossil fuels, especially coal, where ash is a significant component.

**Sources of PM in Power Plants:**
*   **Fly Ash:** Fine particles of non-combustible mineral matter carried out of the furnace with the flue gases.
*   **Soot:** Unburned carbon particles resulting from incomplete combustion.
*   **Bottom Ash:** Larger particles that fall to the bottom of the furnace.

**Impact of PM:**
*   **Health Effects:** Respiratory and cardiovascular problems, reduced visibility.
*   **Environmental Effects:** Acid rain (when combined with other pollutants), soiling of surfaces, damage to vegetation.

**Control Technologies:**

### 2.1. Mechanical Collectors (Precipitators)

These devices use inertia and gravity to separate particles from flue gas. They are often used as a first stage of control.

*   **Cyclones:**
    *   **Working Principle:** Flue gas enters tangentially, creating a vortex. Heavier particles are thrown to the outer wall by centrifugal force and settle down, while cleaner gas exits from the center.
    *   **Efficiency:** Effective for larger particles (typically > 10-20 µm). Less effective for fine particles.
    *   **Types:** Single cyclone, multiple cyclones in parallel (multiclones).
    *   **Reference:** El Wakil (2017) discusses the principles of mechanical separation.

*   **Gravity Settlers (Settling Chambers):**
    *   **Working Principle:** Flue gas flows through a large chamber at low velocity, allowing heavier particles to settle due to gravity.
    *   **Efficiency:** Only effective for very coarse particles. Generally less efficient than cyclones.

### 2.2. Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs)

ESPs are highly efficient devices for removing fine particulate matter from flue gases. They are widely used in thermal power plants.

*   **Working Principle:**
    1.  **Ionization:** Flue gas passes through a region with high voltage electrodes (discharge electrodes), which creates a corona discharge, ionizing the gas molecules and charging the dust particles.
    2.  **Collection:** The charged particles are attracted to oppositely charged collecting electrodes (plates).
    3.  **Dislodging:** Collected particles are removed from the plates by mechanical rapping or vibratory mechanisms, causing them to fall into hoppers below.
    *   **Reference:** P. K. Nag (2017) provides a detailed explanation of ESPs, including electrical principles.
    *   **Key Parameters:** Voltage, gas flow rate, particle resistivity, plate spacing, rapping frequency.
    *   **Efficiency:** Can achieve efficiencies of 99% or higher, particularly for fine particles.
    *   **Types:** Dry ESPs (most common for fly ash), Wet ESPs (for sticky or corrosive particulates).

### 2.3. Fabric Filters (Baghouses)

Fabric filters use a fabric medium to physically trap particulate matter from the flue gas. They are highly efficient, especially for very fine particles.

*   **Working Principle:** Flue gas is passed through a series of fabric bags. Particles are collected on the surface of the fabric, forming a "dust cake" that further improves filtration efficiency.
*   **Reference:** Gupta (2012) details the construction and operation of fabric filters.
*   **Cleaning Mechanisms:**
    *   **Shaker Type:** Bags are mechanically vibrated to dislodge the dust cake.
    *   **Reverse Air:** A stream of clean air is blown in the reverse direction through the bags to dislodge dust.
    *   **Pulse Jet:** A short burst of compressed air is injected into the bag, creating a shockwave that dislodges dust.
*   **Efficiency:** Can achieve very high efficiencies (99.5% or more) even for sub-micron particles.
*   **Considerations:** Temperature limitations of fabric, pressure drop across the filter, maintenance of bags.

**Important Points to Remember for PM Control:**
*   The choice of PM control device depends on the particle size distribution, gas flow rate, temperature, and required efficiency.
*   ESPs and fabric filters are generally preferred for high-efficiency removal of fine PM.
*   Mechanical collectors are often used as a pre-cleaner to reduce the load on ESPs or fabric filters.

## 3. Control of Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)

SO2 is a major air pollutant produced from the combustion of sulfur-containing fuels, primarily coal and heavy oil. It is a key contributor to acid rain and respiratory problems.

**Sources of SO2:**
*   Sulfur content in the fuel (coal, oil).
*   Combustion process where sulfur reacts with oxygen.

**Impact of SO2:**
*   **Health Effects:** Respiratory irritation, aggravation of asthma and bronchitis.
*   **Environmental Effects:** Acid rain (damaging forests, lakes, buildings), formation of sulfate aerosols contributing to respiratory issues and reduced visibility.

**Control Technologies:**

### 3.1. Fuel Pre-treatment

*   **Coal Cleaning:** Physical processes (washing, gravity separation) can remove a significant portion of inorganic sulfur (pyrite) from coal before combustion.
    *   **Reference:** El Wakil (2017) discusses fuel preparation and its impact on emissions.

### 3.2. Combustion Modification

*   **Low-NOx Burners:** While primarily designed for NOx control, some modifications can slightly reduce SO2 emissions by influencing combustion conditions. This is generally not the primary method for SO2 control.

### 3.3. Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD)

FGD systems are the most common and effective method for removing SO2 from flue gases. They are typically installed after combustion and before particulate removal.

*   **Types of FGD:**

    *   **Wet Scrubbing:**
        *   **Working Principle:** Flue gas is brought into contact with a slurry or solution of an absorbent. The SO2 reacts with the absorbent and is removed.
        *   **Common Absorbents:**
            *   **Limestone (CaCO3):** The most widely used absorbent. Limestone is ground into a fine powder and mixed with water to form a slurry.
                *   **Reaction:**
                    $$ \text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{SO}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_3(s) + \text{CO}_2(g) $$
                    $$ \text{CaSO}_3(s) + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_4(s) $$
                *   **Byproduct:** Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) is often produced, which can be sold for use in wallboard.
            *   **Lime (CaO):** Can also be used, often derived from calcining limestone.
        *   **Scrubber Types:** Spray towers, venturi scrubbers, tray towers.
        *   **Reference:** P. K. Nag (2017) provides detailed diagrams and operational aspects of wet scrubbers.
        *   **Advantages:** High SO2 removal efficiency (90-98%), well-established technology, can produce marketable byproducts (gypsum).
        *   **Disadvantages:** Produces a large volume of wastewater, potential for scaling and corrosion, requires reheat of flue gas to prevent plume-related issues.

    *   **Dry Scrubbing:**
        *   **Working Principle:** A dry sorbent (e.g., hydrated lime - Ca(OH)2, sodium bicarbonate - NaHCO3) is injected into the flue gas, or a slurry of sorbent is sprayed and dried. SO2 reacts with the sorbent to form solid products, which are then removed by a particulate control device (e.g., fabric filter).
        *   **Reaction (with lime):**
            $$ \text{Ca(OH)}_2(s) + \text{SO}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(g) $$
            $$ \text{CaSO}_3(s) + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_4(s) $$
        *   **Advantages:** Produces less wastewater, no need for flue gas reheat, simpler process.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Lower SO2 removal efficiency compared to wet scrubbing, produces a dry solid waste that needs disposal.

    *   **Semi-Dry Scrubbing (Spray Dryer Absorption):**
        *   **Working Principle:** An alkaline slurry (e.g., lime slurry) is atomized into fine droplets, which are injected into the flue gas. The heat from the flue gas evaporates the water, and the dry sorbent reacts with SO2. The resulting dry product is collected.
        *   **Advantages:** Combines advantages of wet and dry systems, produces a dry byproduct.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Can be less efficient than wet scrubbing for very high SO2 concentrations.

### 3.4. Emerging Technologies

*   **Fluidized Bed Combustion (FBC):**
    *   **Working Principle:** Limestone is added directly to the combustion chamber, where it reacts with SO2 at high temperatures.
    *   **Reaction:**
        $$ \text{CaCO}_3(s) \rightarrow \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g) $$
        $$ \text{CaO}(s) + \text{SO}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_3(s) $$
        $$ \text{CaSO}_3(s) + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_4(s) $$
    *   **Advantages:** SO2 capture occurs during combustion, reducing the need for separate FGD systems, also effective for NOx control.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires specific fuel and sorbent characteristics, can produce a significant amount of solid waste.
    *   **Reference:** El Wakil (2017) covers advanced combustion techniques like FBC.

**Important Points to Remember for SO2 Control:**
*   FGD is the most common method for SO2 removal from flue gases.
*   Wet limestone scrubbing is widely used due to its high efficiency and potential for gypsum byproduct.
*   Fuel cleaning and FBC are alternative approaches to minimize SO2 emissions.

## 4. Control of Wastewater from Power Plants

Power plants generate various types of wastewater, each with specific pollutants requiring treatment before discharge. Proper wastewater management is crucial for protecting water bodies and aquatic life, aligning with CO5.

**Sources and Characteristics of Wastewater:**

### 4.1. Boiler Blowdown

*   **Source:** Removal of concentrated dissolved solids from the boiler water to maintain water quality and prevent scaling and corrosion.
*   **Pollutants:** High concentrations of dissolved solids (salts, silica), suspended solids, and sometimes chemical treatment agents.
*   **Treatment:**
    *   **Cooling:** Blowdown is typically very hot and needs to be cooled before further treatment or discharge.
    *   **Flash Evaporation:** Utilizes the high temperature to evaporate water, leaving concentrated solids behind.
    *   **Filtration:** To remove suspended solids.
    *   **Ion Exchange or Reverse Osmosis:** For demineralization if treated water is to be reused.

### 4.2. Cooling Tower Blowdown

*   **Source:** Water lost to evaporation in cooling towers is replenished with fresh makeup water. Blowdown is discharged to remove the concentrated dissolved solids and suspended solids that accumulate due to evaporation.
*   **Pollutants:** Increased concentration of dissolved solids (calcium, magnesium, silica), suspended solids, and chemicals used for scale and corrosion control (e.g., phosphates, chromates – though chromates are being phased out due to toxicity).
*   **Treatment:**
    *   **pH Adjustment:** To precipitate dissolved metals.
    *   **Chemical Precipitation:** Addition of chemicals to remove specific dissolved solids.
    *   **Sedimentation/Clarification:** To remove suspended solids.
    *   **Filtration:** To further polish the water.
    *   **Blowdown Minimization:** Optimizing cycles of concentration in cooling towers to reduce blowdown volume.

### 4.3. Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD) Wastewater

*   **Source:** Primarily from wet scrubbing systems.
*   **Pollutants:** Suspended solids (fly ash, unreacted sorbent), dissolved solids (sulfates, chlorides, calcium, magnesium, trace heavy metals like mercury, selenium), and sometimes residual acidity or alkalinity.
*   **Treatment:** This is a complex wastewater stream requiring multi-stage treatment.
    *   **Pre-treatment:** Removal of suspended solids through settling tanks or clarifiers.
    *   **pH Adjustment:** To precipitate dissolved metals and control the chemical reactions.
    *   **Oxidation:** To convert sulfite (SO3^2-) to sulfate (SO4^2-), which is more stable and less soluble. Often achieved by injecting air.
    *   **Precipitation:**
        *   **Lime Addition:** To precipitate dissolved metals and excess sulfite/sulfate.
        *   **Selenium Removal:** Selenium can be problematic and may require specific treatment steps like iron co-precipitation or biological treatment.
    *   **Clarification/Thickening:** To settle precipitated solids.
    *   **Filtration:** To remove remaining suspended solids.
    *   **Disposal of Sludge:** The concentrated sludge containing precipitated pollutants needs proper disposal in secure landfills.
    *   **Reference:** P. K. Nag (2017) and El Wakil (2017) detail the wastewater treatment processes associated with FGD.

### 4.4. Ash Handling Wastewater

*   **Source:** Water used for sluicing ash from boilers and precipitators, or from wet ash collectors.
*   **Pollutants:** High concentrations of suspended solids (fly ash, bottom ash), dissolved solids, and trace heavy metals leached from the ash (e.g., arsenic, mercury, lead).
*   **Treatment:**
    *   **Ash Settling Ponds (Ash Ponds):** Large ponds where ash particles settle out by gravity.
    *   **Clarifiers/Thickeners:** To concentrate the settled ash.
    *   **Filtration:** To remove finer suspended solids.
    *   **Disposal of Ash Slurry:** The thickened ash slurry is often dewatered and disposed of in landfills.
    *   **Effluent Treatment:** The water decanted from ash ponds may require further treatment to remove dissolved pollutants and heavy metals before discharge.

### 4.5. Chemical Cleaning Wastes

*   **Source:** Wastes generated from cleaning boiler tubes, condensers, and other equipment using chemical solutions.
*   **Pollutants:** Acids, alkalis, chelating agents, dissolved scale and corrosion products.
*   **Treatment:**
    *   **Neutralization:** To adjust pH.
    *   **Precipitation:** To remove dissolved metals.
    *   **Filtration:** To remove solids.
    *   **Specific chemical treatment:** Depending on the chemicals used for cleaning.

**Advanced Wastewater Treatment & Reuse:**

*   **Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD):** Aims to eliminate liquid wastewater discharge by treating and recycling all water within the plant. This is achieved through advanced technologies like membrane filtration (ultrafiltration, reverse osmosis) and evaporation/crystallization.
*   **Water Conservation:** Implementing practices to minimize water usage throughout the plant.

**Important Points to Remember for Wastewater Control:**
*   Wastewater treatment is a multi-stage process tailored to the specific pollutants present.
*   FGD wastewater and ash handling wastewater are often the most challenging to treat due to their pollutant load.
*   Regulatory discharge limits for various parameters (TSS, pH, heavy metals, etc.) must be met.
*   Water reuse and ZLD concepts are gaining importance for sustainable water management.

## 6. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Which of the following is the most common and effective method for removing sulfur dioxide (SO2) from flue gases?
a) Cyclone
b) Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP)
c) Fabric Filter
d) Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD)

**Answer:** d) Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD)

**Question 2:** In a wet limestone scrubber for SO2 control, what is the primary byproduct formed?
a) Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3)
b) Sulfur Trioxide (SO3)
c) Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O)
d) Calcium Sulfide (CaS)

**Answer:** c) Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O)

**Question 3:** Which type of particulate control device uses a fabric medium to trap particles and often employs mechanical shaking or pulse jets for cleaning?
a) Cyclone
b) Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP)
c) Fabric Filter (Baghouse)
d) Gravity Settler

**Answer:** c) Fabric Filter (Baghouse)

**Question 4:** Cooling tower blowdown wastewater primarily contains:
a) High levels of dissolved oxygen
b) Low concentrations of dissolved solids
c) Increased concentrations of dissolved and suspended solids due to evaporation
d) Only organic pollutants

**Answer:** c) Increased concentrations of dissolved and suspended solids due to evaporation

**Question 5:** Explain the basic working principle of an Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP) for particulate matter control.

**Answer:** An ESP uses high voltage to create a corona discharge that ionizes the flue gas. The dust particles passing through this ionized field become charged and are then attracted to oppositely charged collecting electrodes. These collected particles are periodically dislodged and fall into hoppers.

**Question 6:** List three main types of wastewater generated from power plants and briefly describe their primary pollutants.

**Answer:**
1.  **Boiler Blowdown:** High dissolved solids, suspended solids, heat.
2.  **Cooling Tower Blowdown:** Increased dissolved solids, suspended solids, treatment chemicals.
3.  **FGD Wastewater:** Suspended solids, dissolved sulfates, chlorides, trace heavy metals, residual sorbents.
4.  **Ash Handling Wastewater:** High suspended solids (ash particles), leached heavy metals.

**Question 7:** What is the advantage of dry scrubbing over wet scrubbing for SO2 control?

**Answer:** Dry scrubbing produces less wastewater and does not require flue gas reheat, simplifying the process and reducing some operational complexities.

## 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Particulate Matter Control:** ESPs and Fabric Filters are key for high-efficiency PM removal. Cyclones are often used for coarser particles.
*   **SO2 Control:** Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD) is the dominant technology, with wet limestone scrubbing being the most prevalent. Fuel pre-treatment and Fluidized Bed Combustion (FBC) are alternative strategies.
*   **Wastewater Management:** Each wastewater stream (boiler blowdown, cooling tower blowdown, FGD wastewater, ash handling wastewater) has unique pollutant characteristics requiring specific treatment processes.
*   **Environmental Compliance:** Power plants must adhere to strict regulatory limits for emissions and discharges to protect environmental quality and public health.
*   **Sustainability:** Focus is shifting towards water conservation, water reuse, and Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) to minimize environmental impact.
