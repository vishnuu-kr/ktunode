# Power Plant Engineering - Module 3: Thermal Power Plants

## Topic: General Layout, Site Selection, Fuel Handling, Storage, and Burning

---

### **Introduction**

Thermal power plants are the backbone of electricity generation in many parts of the world. They convert the chemical energy stored in fuels (primarily coal, oil, and natural gas) into electrical energy through a series of thermodynamic processes. This module delves into the foundational aspects of thermal power plants, focusing on their overall design, strategic placement, and the crucial processes of fuel management.

### **1. General Layout of a Thermal Power Plant (Steam Power Plant)**

The layout of a thermal power plant is designed to facilitate efficient operation, maintenance, and safety, while minimizing energy losses. It's a complex arrangement of various interconnected units.

**Key Components and their Arrangement:**

*   **Fuel Handling and Storage Area:**
    *   Located near the plant entrance or a dedicated supply route (railway siding, jetty).
    *   Includes unloading facilities, conveyors, crushers, and storage yards/tanks.
    *   Proximity to fuel supply is crucial.

*   **Boiler House (Fire Side):**
    *   The heart of the power plant where fuel is burned to produce high-pressure, high-temperature steam.
    *   Contains the boiler itself, furnace, superheaters, reheaters, economizer, and air preheaters.
    *   Requires substantial space for the boiler structure and associated equipment.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Emphasizes the importance of furnace design and combustion efficiency.
    *   **P. K. Nag (2017):** Discusses various boiler types and their layout considerations.

*   **Turbine House:**
    *   Houses the steam turbine(s) and the generator.
    *   Turbines are typically arranged in line with the generator.
    *   Requires a strong foundation to support heavy rotating machinery.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Details the mechanical arrangement and alignment of turbine and generator sets.

*   **Condenser and Cooling Water System:**
    *   Condensers are located beneath the turbine exhaust.
    *   The cooling water system includes pumps, intake structures, discharge structures, and cooling towers or natural water sources (rivers, lakes).
    *   **P. K. Nag (2017):** Explains different types of condensers and cooling water circuits.

*   **Boiler Feed Pump and Auxiliaries:**
    *   Feedwater pumps are located near the boiler to supply water at high pressure.
    *   Other auxiliaries include deaerators, chemical treatment plants, etc.

*   **Control Room:**
    *   The nerve center of the plant, equipped with instruments for monitoring and controlling all operations.
    *   Usually located with good visibility of critical equipment.

*   **Ash Handling Plant:**
    *   For coal-fired plants, a system to remove and dispose of ash generated during combustion.
    *   Includes ash crushers, ash slurry pumps, and ash disposal areas.

*   **Chimney (Stack):**
    *   Located strategically to disperse flue gases, considering wind direction and surrounding areas.
    *   Crucial for environmental compliance.

*   **Electrical Substation and Switchyard:**
    *   Transformers, switchgear, and transmission lines for stepping up the generated voltage and connecting to the grid.
    *   Typically located at the periphery of the plant.

**Factors Influencing Layout:**

*   **Safety:** Separation of high-temperature areas, hazardous materials, and personnel access.
*   **Efficiency:** Minimizing piping lengths for steam, water, and fuel; reducing pumping power.
*   **Maintenance:** Easy access to equipment for inspection and repair.
*   **Expansion:** Provision for future capacity upgrades.
*   **Environmental Regulations:** Chimney height, ash disposal, and noise control.
*   **Cost:** Optimization of construction and operational expenses.

**Example Layout:**

Imagine a linear arrangement where fuel enters at one end, moves to the boiler, then to the turbine, followed by the condenser. The chimney is positioned to allow for efficient dispersal of flue gases. Cooling water intake and discharge are designed to be environmentally sound.

### **2. Site Selection for Thermal Power Plants**

Choosing the right location is critical for the economic viability, operational efficiency, and environmental sustainability of a thermal power plant.

**Key Considerations:**

*   **Proximity to Fuel Sources:**
    *   Reduces transportation costs for coal, oil, or gas.
    *   **P. K. Nag (2017):** Highlights the significant impact of fuel transportation costs on overall power generation cost.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Discusses the economics of fuel sourcing and its impact on plant location.

*   **Availability of Cooling Water:**
    *   Thermal power plants require large quantities of cooling water for condensation.
    *   Sources include rivers, lakes, oceans, or the possibility of constructing cooling towers.
    *   Sufficient flow rate and acceptable water temperature are essential.
    *   **Nagpal & Sharma (2012):** Emphasize the critical need for reliable cooling water sources.

*   **Proximity to Load Centers:**
    *   Minimizes transmission losses and costs by locating the plant closer to areas of high electricity demand.
    *   **Manoj Kumar Gupta (2012):** Discusses transmission losses and their economic implications.

*   **Availability of Land:**
    *   Sufficient land is required for the plant buildings, fuel storage, ash disposal, cooling towers, and future expansion.
    *   Affordable land prices are also a factor.

*   **Transportation Facilities:**
    *   Access to railways, roadways, or waterways for transporting fuel, equipment, and personnel.
    *   **P. K. Nag (2017):** Notes that inadequate transportation can significantly hinder fuel supply.

*   **Geological and Topographical Conditions:**
    *   Stable ground for heavy foundations.
    *   Suitability for constructing cooling towers and other structures.
    *   Avoidance of flood-prone areas.

*   **Environmental Impact and Regulations:**
    *   Consideration of air pollution dispersion (wind patterns), water pollution from discharge, noise pollution, and ash disposal.
    *   Compliance with local and national environmental regulations.
    *   **CO5 Alignment:** This directly relates to discussing pollution and environmental effects.

*   **Labor Availability:**
    *   Availability of skilled and unskilled labor for construction and operation.

*   **Water Table and Drainage:**
    *   Impact on foundation design and potential for water contamination.

*   **Seismic Activity:**
    *   Need for earthquake-resistant design in seismically active zones.

**Example:**

A large coal-fired power plant might be located near a major river for cooling water and on a railway line for coal transport, even if it's not directly adjacent to the primary load center, due to the overwhelming cost savings in fuel transportation. Conversely, a natural gas-fired plant might be located closer to the load center, as gas transportation is less cumbersome.

### **3. Fuel Handling, Storage, and Burning**

This section covers the journey of fuel from its arrival at the plant to its combustion in the boiler.

#### **3.1 Fuel Handling**

The process of receiving, transporting, and preparing the fuel for combustion.

**Coal Handling:**

*   **Unloading:**
    *   **Railways:** Wagon tipplers, rotary car dumpers.
    *   **Ships/Barges:** Ship loaders/unloaders (grab cranes, bucket elevators).
    *   **Trucks:** Dump trucks unloading into hoppers.
*   **Transportation within the Plant:**
    *   **Belt Conveyors:** Most common for transporting coal over long distances.
    *   **Overland Conveyors:** For greater distances.
    *   **Trucks/Rail Cars:** For shorter hauls or specific areas.
*   **Crushing and Screening:**
    *   **Primary Crushers:** (e.g., Jaw crushers, impact crushers) reduce large lumps to manageable sizes.
    *   **Secondary Crushers:** (e.g., Roll crushers, hammer mills) further reduce the size.
    *   **Screening:** To separate coal based on size, often returning oversized coal for further crushing. This ensures uniform particle size for efficient combustion.
    *   **P. K. Nag (2017):** Details different types of crushers and their applications in coal preparation.
*   **Coal Pulverization:**
    *   For pulverized coal firing (most common in large thermal plants), coal is ground into a fine powder (like talcum powder) in pulverizers (e.g., ball mills, bowl mills, vertical roller mills).
    *   Fine particles ensure rapid and complete combustion.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Provides in-depth coverage of pulverizer types and their operational principles.

**Oil Handling:**

*   **Unloading:**
    *   **Ships/Barges:** Via pipelines at a jetty.
    *   **Tanker Trucks:** Via hoses.
*   **Storage:** In large storage tanks, often heated to maintain viscosity for easy pumping.
*   **Pumping and Heating:** Oil is pumped to the burners and may be heated to reduce viscosity.
*   **Atomization:** Oil is atomized (broken into fine droplets) at the burner to enhance combustion.

**Natural Gas Handling:**

*   **Transportation:** Via pipelines directly to the plant.
*   **Pressure Regulation:** Gas pressure is regulated before being supplied to the burners.
*   **Filtering:** Gas is filtered to remove impurities.

#### **3.2 Fuel Storage**

Safe and efficient storage of fuel to ensure continuous operation.

**Coal Storage:**

*   **Yard Storage:** Large open areas for stockpiling coal.
    *   **Live Storage:** Readily available coal for immediate use.
    *   **Dead Storage:** Coal stored for longer periods, less accessible.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Stacking:** Coal is piled in heaps using bulldozers and loaders.
    *   **Reclaiming:** Coal is reclaimed from stacks using bulldozers, front-end loaders, or stacker-reclaimers.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   **Spontaneous Combustion:** Coal piles can generate heat due to oxidation, leading to self-ignition. Proper ventilation, compaction, and fire-fighting measures are essential.
    *   **Weathering:** Coal can degrade in quality due to exposure to rain and sun.
    *   **Dust Control:** Measures like water spraying or chemical additives to suppress dust.
    *   **P. K. Nag (2017):** Discusses methods for preventing spontaneous combustion in coal storage yards.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Also covers safe coal storage practices.

**Oil Storage:**

*   **Storage Tanks:** Large cylindrical steel tanks, typically located in a bunded area (to contain spills).
*   **Heating:** Often equipped with heating coils to maintain flowable viscosity.
*   **Safety:** Fire protection systems (foam, water spray) are crucial due to the flammability of oil.

**Natural Gas Storage:**

*   Typically supplied directly via pipelines, minimizing on-site storage needs for large-scale plants.
*   Small on-site storage might be for peak shaving or as a backup.

#### **3.3 Fuel Burning (Combustion)**

The process of converting the chemical energy of the fuel into thermal energy.

**Principles of Combustion:**

*   Requires fuel, oxygen (from air), and ignition temperature.
*   **Complete Combustion:** Produces CO2 and H2O.
*   **Incomplete Combustion:** Produces CO, soot, and unburnt hydrocarbons, indicating inefficient burning and pollution.
*   **Excess Air:** A certain amount of excess air (above the stoichiometric requirement) is used to ensure complete combustion and account for non-ideal mixing and conditions. Too much excess air can lead to increased flue gas volume and heat loss.

**Methods of Firing in Boilers:**

*   **Pulverized Coal Firing:**
    *   Most common for large boilers.
    *   Finely ground coal is blown into the furnace with primary air.
    *   Secondary and sometimes tertiary air are supplied to ensure complete combustion.
    *   **Advantages:** High combustion efficiency, good control, can burn various coal types.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires pulverizers, potential for fly ash.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Provides extensive details on furnace design for pulverized coal.

*   **Stoker Firing:**
    *   Coal is fed onto a grate (mechanical stoker) and burns as a bed of fuel.
    *   Air is supplied from beneath the grate.
    *   **Types:** Spreader stokers, chain grate stokers, underfeed stokers.
    *   **Advantages:** Simpler than pulverized coal, can handle some moisture and caking coals.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Lower combustion efficiency, limited to smaller boilers, can produce more smoke.

*   **Oil Firing:**
    *   Oil is atomized and mixed with air in the burner.
    *   Atomization can be mechanical, steam, or air.
    *   **Advantages:** High efficiency, clean burning, easy control.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher fuel cost compared to coal, storage and handling require care.

*   **Natural Gas Firing:**
    *   Cleanest fossil fuel to burn.
    *   Requires less excess air than coal or oil.
    *   **Advantages:** High efficiency, very clean combustion, minimal ash, easy control.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Fuel cost can be higher, supply dependent on gas pipelines.

**Furnace Design Considerations:**

*   **Size and Shape:** To ensure adequate residence time for complete combustion.
*   **Refractory Lining:** To withstand high temperatures.
*   **Air Supply:** Proper distribution of primary, secondary, and tertiary air.
*   **Burner Placement:** Critical for efficient mixing and combustion.
*   **Heat Transfer:** Designing the furnace to transfer heat effectively to the water tubes.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Layout Optimization:** Efficiency, safety, and maintenance are key drivers for plant layout.
*   **Site Selection is Strategic:** Proximity to fuel, water, and load centers significantly impacts economics and operations.
*   **Fuel is the Lifeblood:** Reliable and cost-effective fuel handling and storage are paramount.
*   **Combustion Efficiency:** Maximizing the conversion of fuel's chemical energy to heat is crucial for performance and minimizing waste.
*   **Environmental Compliance:** Site selection and plant design must adhere to strict environmental regulations.
*   **Pulverized Coal:** The dominant technology for large thermal power plants due to its efficiency and flexibility.

**Numerical/Problem-Solving Questions:**

1.  A thermal power plant is to be located 300 km from the coal mine and 200 km from the load center. The cost of transporting coal is \$0.05 per tonne-km. The cost of transmitting electricity is \$0.01 per kWh-km. If the plant consumes 1000 tonnes of coal per day, and the average transmission distance is 200 km, should the plant be located closer to the coal mine or the load center to minimize transportation and transmission costs? (Assume uniform daily consumption and a simplified cost model). (CO5)
    *   **Answer:**
        *   Coal transport cost per day = 1000 tonnes * 300 km * \$0.05/tonne-km = \$15,000
        *   If located closer to load center (200 km from mine), coal transport cost = 1000 tonnes * 200 km * \$0.05/tonne-km = \$10,000. Difference = \$5,000 savings.
        *   If located closer to load center, transmission distance is 0 km. Assume original location requires 200 km transmission.
        *   Cost of transmission = (Daily energy generated * 200 km * \$0.01/kWh-km). Without knowing the plant capacity and load factor, we can't calculate the exact transmission cost. However, focusing on the fuel aspect: Moving closer to the mine saves \$5,000/day on coal transport. Any transmission cost savings from being closer to the load would need to be evaluated against this. Generally, fuel transport cost dominates for coal plants.

2.  A coal plant consumes 500 tonnes of coal per hour. The calorific value of coal is 25 MJ/kg. If 30% of the energy is lost in flue gases and incomplete combustion, and 35% is lost in the condenser, what is the efficiency of the thermal part of the plant? (CO2 - simplified concept of energy conversion)
    *   **Answer:**
        *   Energy input rate = 500 tonnes/hour * 1000 kg/tonne * 25 MJ/kg = 12,500,000 MJ/hour
        *   Energy converted to useful work and rejected heat = 100,000% - 30% = 70% of input energy.
        *   Energy lost in flue gases and incomplete combustion = 30% of input energy.
        *   Energy rejected in condenser = 35% of input energy.
        *   Total losses = 30% + 35% = 65%
        *   Efficiency = 100% - Total Losses = 100% - 65% = 35%.
        *   *(Note: This is a simplified efficiency calculation. The Rankine cycle efficiency would be calculated differently based on steam conditions.)*
