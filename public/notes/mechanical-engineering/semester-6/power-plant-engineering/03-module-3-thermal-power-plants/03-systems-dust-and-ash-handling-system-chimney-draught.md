# POWER PLANT ENGINEERING - MODULE 3: THERMAL POWER PLANTS

## Topic: Systems - Dust and Ash Handling System & Chimney Draught

### 1. Introduction

Thermal power plants, particularly those utilizing solid fuels like coal, generate significant amounts of fly ash and bottom ash. Efficient handling and disposal of this ash are crucial for plant operation, environmental compliance, and equipment longevity. Similarly, the combustion process produces flue gases containing various pollutants, and a chimney is essential for their safe dispersion into the atmosphere. This topic delves into the systems responsible for managing ash and the principles of chimney draught.

---

### 2. Dust and Ash Handling System

#### 2.1. Types of Ash Generated

*   **Fly Ash:** Fine particles carried along with the flue gases from the boiler. It's typically collected in air pollution control devices like Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs) or baghouses.
*   **Bottom Ash:** Heavier, coarser particles that fall to the bottom of the boiler furnace due to gravity. It's collected in the ash pit.

#### 2.2. Objectives of Ash Handling System

*   **Safe and Efficient Removal:** Transporting ash from the collection points to the disposal/utilization area without creating dust hazards or environmental pollution.
*   **Minimize Dust Emission:** Preventing the release of fine ash particles into the atmosphere during handling.
*   **Minimize Water Consumption:** Where applicable, especially in wet handling systems.
*   **Reduce Manual Labor:** Automating the process for efficiency and safety.
*   **Facilitate Ash Utilization:** Preparing ash for use in cement, concrete, brick manufacturing, etc.

#### 2.3. Ash Handling Systems

There are two primary methods for ash handling:

**a) Wet Ash Handling System**

*   **Working Principle:** Ash is mixed with water to form a slurry, which is then transported through pipelines to an ash pond or disposal area.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Ash Hoppers:** Collection points below the boiler (bottom ash) and at the base of dust collectors (fly ash).
    *   **Ash Sluicing System:** High-pressure water jets or pumps to create the slurry.
    *   **Ash Slurry Pumps:** Specially designed pumps to handle abrasive ash-water mixtures.
    *   **Pipelines:** Robust piping systems (usually steel) to transport the slurry.
    *   **Ash Sump/Pond:** Large areas for settling and dewatering of ash.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Effective in suppressing dust.
    *   Relatively simple operation.
    *   Good for transporting ash over long distances.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   High water consumption, leading to potential water scarcity issues and environmental concerns regarding ash pond effluent.
    *   Formation of ash slurry can be corrosive and abrasive, requiring specialized equipment.
    *   Requires large areas for ash ponds.
*   **Reference (El Wakil, 2017):** Discusses the challenges of handling particulate matter and the initial approaches involving wet scrubbing which is related to ash slurry formation.

**b) Dry Ash Handling System**

*   **Working Principle:** Ash is collected and transported in its dry state using mechanical or pneumatic conveying methods.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Ash Hoppers:** Similar to wet systems.
    *   **Ash Conveying Equipment:**
        *   **Mechanical Conveyors:** Screw conveyors, belt conveyors, bucket elevators for shorter distances and moderate capacities.
        *   **Pneumatic Conveyors:** Using air pressure or vacuum to move ash.
            *   **Pressure System:** Ash is injected into a pressurized air stream.
            *   **Vacuum System:** Air is drawn through the system, pulling ash along.
    *   **Dust Collectors/Filters:** Essential at transfer points and discharge points to prevent dust escape.
    *   **Ash Silos/Storage:** For temporary storage before disposal or utilization.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Conserves water.
    *   Reduces the risk of water pollution.
    *   Ash can be readily used for commercial purposes (e.g., cement, construction).
    *   Lower operational costs related to water and pumping.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires effective dust control measures at all stages, which can be complex.
    *   Higher initial cost for pneumatic conveying systems.
    *   Can be more prone to wear and tear due to abrasive dry ash.
*   **Reference (Nag, 2017):** Likely provides detailed descriptions of mechanical and pneumatic conveying systems used in various industrial applications, including power plants.

#### 2.4. Ash Utilization

*   **Cement Industry:** Fly ash is a pozzolanic material and is widely used as a partial replacement for cement in concrete.
*   **Brick Manufacturing:** Ash can be mixed with clay to produce bricks.
*   **Road Construction:** Used as a filler material or in the base layers of roads.
*   **Landfill Cover:** Used for covering waste in landfills.

#### 2.5. Ash Disposal

*   **Ash Ponds:** Large impoundments where ash slurry is discharged and allowed to settle.
*   **Dry Ash Disposal Sites:** Engineered landfills specifically designed for dry ash disposal, often with liners and leachate collection systems.

### 3. Chimney Draught

#### 3.1. Definition and Purpose

*   **Draught:** The difference in pressure between the base of the chimney and the atmosphere, which causes the flow of flue gases upwards and the ingress of combustion air into the furnace.
*   **Purpose of Chimney:**
    *   **Flue Gas Dispersion:** To safely discharge flue gases containing pollutants (CO2, SOx, NOx, particulate matter) into the atmosphere at a sufficient height to prevent them from reaching harmful concentrations at ground level.
    *   **Provide Combustion Air:** To draw in sufficient fresh air for efficient combustion in the boiler.
    *   **Enhance Combustion Efficiency:** By ensuring adequate airflow to the furnace.

#### 3.2. Types of Draught

Draught can be natural or artificial.

**a) Natural Draught**

*   **Princ:** Relies on the density difference between the hot flue gases inside the chimney and the cooler ambient air outside. The hot gases are less dense, creating an upward buoyant force.
*   **Formula for Natural Draught (Pressure Difference, $P_d$):**
    $P_d = H \times (ρ_{atm} - ρ_g) \times g$
    Where:
    *   $H$ = Height of the chimney (m)
    *   $ρ_{atm}$ = Density of ambient air (kg/m³)
    *   $ρ_g$ = Average density of flue gas in the chimney (kg/m³)
    *   $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity (m/s²)

*   **Chimney Effect:** The phenomenon where hot air or gas in a vertical column is lighter than the surrounding air or gas, creating an upward flow.
*   **Factors Affecting Natural Draught:**
    *   **Chimney Height ($H$):** Higher chimney leads to greater draught.
    *   **Temperature Difference:** Larger difference between flue gas temperature and ambient air temperature results in greater draught.
    *   **Flue Gas Density ($ρ_g$):** Lower flue gas density (achieved by higher temperature and lower gas composition of heavy elements like CO2, SO2) leads to greater draught.
    *   **Ambient Air Density ($ρ_{atm}$):** Higher ambient air density (at lower temperatures and altitudes) leads to greater draught.
    *   **Chimney Diameter/Area:** Affects the velocity of the gases but not the draught pressure itself, though it influences the resistance to flow.
    *   **Wind Velocity:** Can affect draught by creating suction at the top of the chimney.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   No running costs (no power consumption).
    *   Simple construction.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Draught is variable and dependent on atmospheric conditions, making it difficult to maintain consistent combustion.
    *   Requires very tall chimneys for significant draught, especially for large boilers.
    *   Not suitable for large modern power plants with high fuel firing rates.
*   **Reference (Nagpal & Sharma, 2012):** Likely details the fundamental principles of buoyancy and fluid dynamics that underpin natural draught.

**b) Artificial Draught**

Artificial draught systems use mechanical fans to either induce or force the airflow, providing a more controllable and reliable draught.

*   **Induced Draught (ID) Fan:**
    *   **Location:** Placed at the chimney base, *after* the dust collector (e.g., ESP or baghouse).
    *   **Function:** Sucks the flue gases from the boiler and pushes them up the chimney. This creates a negative pressure throughout the boiler, preventing leakage of hot gases into the boiler room.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Handles cooler, cleaner gases (after dust removal), prolonging fan life.
        *   Prevents leakage of hot gases and dust into the boiler room.
        *   Provides stable and controllable draught.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Requires a dust collector upstream.
        *   Fan handles flue gases, requiring corrosion-resistant materials.
    *   **Learning Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Explaining components of thermal power plants), CO5 (Pollution from power plants – how ID fan helps control emissions by directing gases to chimney).

*   **Forced Draught (FD) Fan:**
    *   **Location:** Placed at the boiler's air intake, *before* the furnace.
    *   **Function:** Blows fresh air into the furnace, creating a positive pressure. This positive pressure pushes the combustion gases through the boiler and out of the chimney.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Handles clean, cooler air, reducing wear and tear on the fan.
        *   Lower initial cost compared to ID systems.
        *   Can force air into the furnace for high combustion rates.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Any leakage in the boiler casing will result in hot gases escaping into the boiler room.
        *   The fan handles combustion air, which can be dusty if not filtered.
    *   **Learning Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Explaining components of thermal power plants).

*   **Balanced Draught:**
    *   **System:** Combines both FD and ID fans.
    *   **Function:** The FD fan supplies air to the furnace, and the ID fan removes flue gases from the boiler. This system maintains a slightly negative pressure in the furnace, preventing air leakage into the boiler room while ensuring controlled airflow for combustion.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Most stable and controllable draught.
        *   Prevents leakage of hot gases and dust.
        *   Allows for precise control of air-fuel ratio.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Higher initial cost and complexity due to two fans.
        *   Requires more space.
    *   **Learning Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Explaining components of thermal power plants), CO2 (Performance parameters can be influenced by efficient combustion via balanced draught).

#### 3.3. Draught Power

*   The power required by the fan to overcome the resistance of the air-fuel path and to discharge the flue gases up the chimney.
*   **Draught Power ($P_{draught}$):**
    $P_{draught} = \frac{(P_{induced} + P_{forced}) \times Q}{1000}$ (in kW, if pressure is in Pascal and flow rate in m³/s)
    Where:
    *   $P_{induced}$ = Induced draught pressure (Pa)
    *   $P_{forced}$ = Forced draught pressure (Pa)
    *   $Q$ = Volume flow rate of flue gas (m³/s)

#### 3.4. Chimney Design Considerations

*   **Height:** Determined by the need for effective dispersion of pollutants, considering local atmospheric conditions and regulations. Factors like surrounding terrain and buildings influence required height.
*   **Diameter:** Sized to carry the flue gases at a suitable velocity without excessive pressure drop. Velocity needs to be high enough to prevent downdraft but not so high as to cause excessive noise or erosion.
*   **Material:** Must be resistant to corrosion from acidic flue gases and high temperatures. Common materials include concrete, steel, and brick.
*   **Stack Height Regulations:** Environmental agencies set limits on ground-level concentrations of pollutants, which dictate the minimum chimney height required for effective dispersion.

#### 3.5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary difference between fly ash and bottom ash?
**Answer:** Fly ash consists of fine particles carried by flue gases, collected in air pollution control devices. Bottom ash is coarser and heavier, falling to the bottom of the boiler.

**Question 2:** Briefly explain the advantages of a dry ash handling system over a wet system.
**Answer:** Dry systems conserve water, reduce the risk of water pollution, and facilitate easier ash utilization in industries like cement and construction.

**Question 3:** If a chimney is 100 m high, and the average density of flue gas inside is 0.7 kg/m³ while the ambient air density is 1.2 kg/m³ at 20°C, calculate the natural draught pressure produced. Assume $g = 9.81 m/s²$.
**Answer:**
$P_d = H \times (ρ_{atm} - ρ_g) \times g$
$P_d = 100 \text{ m} \times (1.2 \text{ kg/m}³ - 0.7 \text{ kg/m}³) \times 9.81 \text{ m/s}²$
$P_d = 100 \times 0.5 \times 9.81$
$P_d = 490.5 \text{ Pa}$

**Question 4:** Where is an Induced Draught (ID) fan typically located in relation to the boiler and dust collector, and what is its primary function?
**Answer:** An ID fan is located at the base of the chimney, *after* the dust collector. Its primary function is to suck flue gases from the boiler and push them up the chimney, creating negative pressure in the boiler system.

**Question 5:** Which type of draught system is generally preferred for large modern thermal power plants and why?
**Answer:** Balanced draught is generally preferred for large modern thermal power plants. It combines the benefits of both forced and induced draught, offering stable and controllable airflow, preventing leakage of hot gases, and allowing for precise control of the air-fuel ratio, leading to efficient combustion and emission control.

### 4. Important Points to Remember

*   **Ash Handling:** Crucial for plant operation and environmental protection. Wet vs. Dry systems have distinct advantages and disadvantages. Dry systems are increasingly favored for water conservation and ash utilization.
*   **Chimney Draught:** Essential for both safe pollutant dispersion and efficient combustion air supply.
*   **Natural Draught:** Simple but less controllable, highly dependent on ambient conditions.
*   **Artificial Draught:** Induced, Forced, and Balanced systems offer better control. Balanced draught provides the most stable operation.
*   **Chimney Height:** A critical design parameter for pollutant dispersion, dictated by environmental regulations.
*   **Fan Selection:** For artificial draught systems, fan type, material, and capacity are critical for reliable operation.
