# Power Plant Engineering: Module 2 - Steam Generator Classifications

## Topic: Cochran Boiler, Lancashire Boiler, Cornish Boiler, Locomotive Boiler

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO1:** Explain the layout, components and working of steam, gas, hydro, and nuclear power plants. (Knowledge Level: K2) - *This module contributes by explaining the fundamental components (boilers) of a steam power plant.*

**Key Concepts and Definitions:**

*   **Steam Generator (Boiler):** A closed vessel in which water is heated and converted into steam by burning fuel. It is the heart of a thermal power plant, responsible for producing the high-pressure steam that drives the turbine.
*   **Fire-tube Boiler:** A boiler where hot combustion gases pass through tubes surrounded by water.
*   **Water-tube Boiler:** A boiler where water passes through tubes surrounded by hot combustion gases.
*   **Natural Circulation Boiler:** Boilers where the circulation of water within the boiler is achieved through the density difference between the water and steam bubbles.
*   **Forced Circulation Boiler:** Boilers where a pump is used to circulate water through the tubes.
*   **Stationary Boiler:** Boilers designed for fixed installations.
*   **Portable Boiler:** Boilers that are mounted on wheels for easy transportation.
*   **Mountings:** Safety and control devices fitted to a boiler, such as water level indicators, pressure gauges, safety valves, etc.
*   **Accessories:** Devices attached to a boiler to improve its efficiency or working, such as economizers, superheaters, feed water pumps, etc.

### 1. Cochran Boiler

**Introduction:**
The Cochran boiler is a vertical, externally fired, fire-tube boiler. It is a compact and relatively simple design, suitable for smaller industrial applications and for providing process steam. It is named after its inventor, Alexander Cochran.

**(Refer to El Wakil, M. M. - Power Plant Technology, Chapter on Steam Generators)**

**Classification:**

*   Vertical
*   Externally fired (furnace is external to the main shell)
*   Fire-tube boiler
*   Natural circulation

**Components:**

*   **Shell:** Cylindrical shell housing the water and steam.
*   **Furnace:** Usually corrugated or plain, located inside the shell, where fuel is burnt.
*   **Fire Tubes:** Tubes passing through the shell, carrying hot flue gases from the furnace to the smokebox.
*   **Combustion Chamber:** Located at the rear of the furnace, where complete combustion occurs.
*   **Water Space:** The volume within the shell occupied by water.
*   **Steam Space:** The volume above the water level where steam accumulates.
*   **Smoke Box:** Located at the front of the boiler, where flue gases collect before being discharged through the chimney.
*   **Grate:** Where fuel is burnt in the furnace.
*   **Chimney:** For the disposal of flue gases.
*   **Mountings:**
    *   **Water Level Indicator (Gauge Glass):** Shows the water level inside the boiler.
    *   **Pressure Gauge:** Indicates the steam pressure.
    *   **Safety Valve:** Prevents over-pressurization.
    *   **Blow-off Cock:** For draining the boiler.
    *   **Steam Stop Valve:** For controlling steam outlet.
    *   **Fusible Plug:** A safety device designed to melt and release steam if the water level drops too low, preventing furnace overheating.

**Working Principle:**

1.  Fuel is burnt on the grate inside the furnace.
2.  The hot flue gases from the furnace pass through the fire tubes.
3.  As the gases travel through the tubes, they transfer heat to the surrounding water in the shell.
4.  The flue gases then enter the smokebox at the front and are discharged through the chimney.
5.  The heat absorbed by the water causes it to convert into steam, which rises to the steam space.
6.  Steam is then drawn off through the steam stop valve.

**Advantages:**

*   Compact and occupies less floor space.
*   Simple construction and maintenance.
*   Relatively low initial cost.
*   Suitable for low to medium pressure applications.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Lower thermal efficiency compared to water-tube boilers.
*   Limited steam generation capacity.
*   Difficult to clean the fire tubes internally.
*   Not suitable for very high pressures due to the large diameter of the shell.

**Applications:**

*   Heating systems.
*   Small industrial plants.
*   Process steam generation.

**(Refer to Nag, P. K. - Power Plant Engineering, Chapter on Steam Boilers)**

### 2. Lancashire Boiler

**Introduction:**
The Lancashire boiler is a horizontal, internally fired, fire-tube boiler. It is characterized by two large diameter furnaces (flues) running the length of the shell, with a return path for hot gases through smaller fire tubes at the bottom. It was widely used in the past for industrial power generation.

**(Refer to Nagpal, G. R., Sharma, S. C. - Power Plant Engineering, Chapter on Boiler Classifications)**

**Classification:**

*   Horizontal
*   Internally fired (furnaces are inside the shell)
*   Fire-tube boiler
*   Natural circulation
*   Double-flue

**Components:**

*   **Shell:** Long cylindrical shell.
*   **Two Furnaces (Flues):** Two large diameter, corrugated or plain furnace tubes inside the shell.
*   **Fire Tubes:** Multiple smaller tubes at the bottom of the shell, carrying flue gases from the rear to the front.
*   **Grate:** Located at the front of each furnace for burning fuel.
*   **Combustion Chamber:** Rear combustion chamber where gases from furnaces meet and turn back into fire tubes.
*   **Water Space:** Occupied by water.
*   **Steam Space:** Occupied by steam.
*   **Bushes:** For mounting the furnaces to the front end plate.
*   **Door:** For access to the furnace.
*   **Bridge Wall:** A refractory wall at the end of the grate.
*   **Chimney:** Connected to the smokebox at the front.
*   **Smokebox:** At the front end.
*   **Mountings and Accessories:** Similar to other boilers, including economizer and superheater for improved efficiency.

**Working Principle:**

1.  Fuel is burnt on the grates inside the two furnaces.
2.  The hot flue gases from the furnaces pass through the rear combustion chamber and then divide to flow through the fire tubes at the bottom of the boiler.
3.  As the gases pass through the fire tubes, they give up heat to the surrounding water.
4.  The gases then reach the smokebox at the front and are discharged via the chimney.
5.  The heat transfer to the water generates steam.

**Advantages:**

*   Can generate a large amount of steam compared to some vertical boilers.
*   More efficient than simple vertical boilers due to the longer path of flue gases and return tubes.
*   Can be fitted with economizers and superheaters for further efficiency gains.
*   Relatively easy to clean the fire tubes from the front.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Requires a larger floor space and foundation.
*   Lower efficiency compared to water-tube boilers.
*   Difficult to clean the inside of the large furnaces.
*   Not suitable for very high pressures due to the large diameter of the shell and the internal furnaces.
*   Water circulation can be poor, leading to potential overheating of the furnace tops.

**Applications:**

*   Historically used in textile mills, paper mills, and for general industrial power generation.
*   Still found in some older industrial plants.

### 3. Cornish Boiler

**Introduction:**
The Cornish boiler is a horizontal, internally fired, fire-tube boiler that is similar in principle to the Lancashire boiler but features only **one** large internal furnace tube. It is known for its simplicity and was a common type for moderate steam requirements.

**(Refer to El Wakil, M. M. - Power Plant Technology, Chapter on Steam Generators)**

**Classification:**

*   Horizontal
*   Internally fired
*   Fire-tube boiler
*   Natural circulation
*   Single-flue

**Components:**

*   **Shell:** Long cylindrical shell.
*   **Single Furnace (Flue):** One large diameter furnace tube inside the shell.
*   **Fire Tubes:** Usually located below the furnace tube, carrying flue gases from the rear to the front.
*   **Grate:** Located at the front of the furnace.
*   **Combustion Chamber:** At the rear, where gases turn back into fire tubes.
*   **Water Space:** Occupied by water.
*   **Steam Space:** Occupied by steam.
*   **Mountings and Accessories:** Similar to other boilers, can be fitted with economizers and superheaters.

**Working Principle:**

1.  Fuel is burnt on the grate inside the single furnace tube.
2.  Hot flue gases pass through the rear combustion chamber.
3.  The gases then travel through the fire tubes located below the furnace tube.
4.  Heat is transferred from the gases to the surrounding water.
5.  The gases collect in the smokebox at the front and are discharged through the chimney.
6.  Steam is generated in the water space.

**Advantages:**

*   Simpler construction than the Lancashire boiler.
*   Requires less floor space than the Lancashire boiler.
*   More efficient than simple vertical boilers.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Lower steam generation capacity compared to Lancashire boilers.
*   Lower thermal efficiency compared to water-tube boilers.
*   Difficulty in cleaning the inside of the furnace tube.
*   Poor water circulation can occur, leading to potential overheating.
*   Not suitable for high pressures.

**Applications:**

*   Textile mills, sugar factories, and other industries requiring moderate steam.

### 4. Locomotive Boiler

**Introduction:**
The locomotive boiler is a specialized, high-capacity, horizontal, internally fired, fire-tube boiler designed for use in steam locomotives. It is characterized by its compact design and rapid steam generation capabilities, crucial for the demanding operational requirements of a train. It is a forced circulation boiler, often utilizing a blast pipe to enhance draft and combustion.

**(Refer to Nag, P. K. - Power Plant Engineering, Chapter on Steam Boilers)**
**(Refer to Gupta, Manoj Kumar - Power Plant Engineering, Chapter on Boiler Types)**

**Classification:**

*   Horizontal
*   Internally fired
*   Fire-tube boiler
*   Forced circulation (primarily due to the draft system)
*   Very high steam generation capacity
*   Compact and robust design

**Key Components and Special Features:**

*   **Firebox:** This is where the fuel is burnt and is much larger than the furnace in stationary boilers. It is often water-jacketed (surrounded by water) to absorb maximum heat and prevent overheating.
*   **Grate:** Located at the bottom of the firebox for fuel combustion.
*   **Tubes:**
    *   **Fire Tubes:** Carry hot gases from the firebox through the boiler shell to the smokebox.
    *   **Serve Tubes (or Smoke Tubes):** These are often integrated with the fire tubes and extend from the firebox to the smokebox.
    *   **Stay Tubes:** Tubes that also act as structural support, connecting the front and rear tube plates.
*   **Boiler Barrel:** The main cylindrical shell containing the fire tubes.
*   **Throat Plate:** Connects the firebox to the boiler barrel.
*   **Combustion Chamber:** Often integrated within the firebox for efficient burning.
*   **Smokebox:** At the front, where exhaust steam is used to create a draft.
*   **Chimney (Stack):** Discharges the flue gases.
*   **Blast Pipe:** A nozzle in the smokebox, connected to the exhaust steam from the engine. The high-velocity steam exiting the blast pipe creates a strong draft through the firebox and tubes, pulling in air for combustion and rapidly removing flue gases. This is a key feature for high performance.
*   **Superheater:** Usually placed in the smokebox, it reheats the steam after it leaves the boiler to increase its temperature and improve engine efficiency.
*   **Safety Valves:** Essential for pressure relief.
*   **Water Level Indicator, Pressure Gauge:** Standard mountings.

**Working Principle:**

1.  Fuel (coal or oil) is burnt on the grate in the spacious firebox.
2.  The firebox is surrounded by water, and the hot gases from combustion are directed through numerous small fire tubes that pass through the main boiler shell.
3.  The exhaust steam from the locomotive's cylinders is directed up the chimney through the blast pipe. This creates a powerful draft, pulling air into the firebox and flue gases through the fire tubes at high speed.
4.  The high velocity of gases and the large heating surface area of the numerous tubes allow for very rapid steam generation.
5.  Steam is collected in the steam space at the top.
6.  The steam then passes through the superheater (if fitted) before going to the cylinders.

**Advantages:**

*   Very high steam generation rate for its size.
*   Compact and robust design suitable for locomotive applications.
*   Quick steaming capability.
*   Efficient use of heating surface area due to numerous small tubes.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Lower thermal efficiency compared to modern stationary boilers due to high flue gas velocities and heat losses.
*   Requires constant attention and maintenance (e.g., cleaning tubes, ash removal).
*   Difficult to clean the fire tubes internally.
*   Not suitable for stationary use due to the blast pipe system being integral to the locomotive's operation.
*   High stresses on the boiler shell due to high pressure and fluctuating loads.

**Applications:**

*   Steam locomotives.
*   Some older ships and industrial equipment.

### Practice Questions

1.  **Question:** Differentiate between a fire-tube boiler and a water-tube boiler, providing one example of each. (Relates to CO1, K2)
    **Answer:**
    *   **Fire-tube boiler:** Hot combustion gases pass through tubes surrounded by water. Example: Lancashire boiler, Cochran boiler.
    *   **Water-tube boiler:** Water passes through tubes surrounded by hot combustion gases. Example: Babcock & Wilcox boiler.

2.  **Question:** Describe the role of the blast pipe in a locomotive boiler. (Relates to CO1, K2)
    **Answer:** The blast pipe is a nozzle in the smokebox through which exhaust steam from the locomotive's cylinders is expelled up the chimney. This high-velocity steam creates a strong draft, pulling air into the firebox for combustion and rapidly drawing flue gases through the boiler tubes, thus enabling very fast steam generation.

3.  **Question:** What are the primary advantages and disadvantages of a Cornish boiler compared to a Lancashire boiler? (Relates to CO1, K2)
    **Answer:**
    *   **Advantages of Cornish over Lancashire:** Requires less floor space and is simpler in construction (one furnace instead of two).
    *   **Disadvantages of Cornish over Lancashire:** Lower steam generation capacity.

4.  **Question:** Why are locomotive boilers designed with a large number of small tubes? (Relates to CO1, K2)
    **Answer:** The large number of small tubes significantly increases the heating surface area available for heat transfer from the flue gases to the water. This maximizes the rate of steam generation, which is crucial for the high power demands of a steam locomotive.

5.  **Question:** The Cochran boiler is classified as a vertical, externally fired, fire-tube boiler. Explain what each of these classifications implies for its design and operation. (Relates to CO1, K2)
    **Answer:**
    *   **Vertical:** The main shell is oriented vertically, leading to a compact footprint.
    *   **Externally fired:** The furnace where combustion occurs is outside the main shell, typically in a separate fire chamber.
    *   **Fire-tube:** The hot flue gases pass through tubes that are immersed in the water within the boiler shell.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Fire-tube vs. Water-tube:** This is a fundamental distinction. Fire-tube boilers are generally simpler and used for lower pressures and capacities, while water-tube boilers are more efficient and suitable for higher pressures and larger capacities.
*   **Capacity and Efficiency:** Larger boilers (like Lancashire) generally have higher steam capacities than smaller ones (like Cornish or Cochran). However, modern water-tube boilers significantly outperform all these fire-tube types in terms of thermal efficiency.
*   **Locomotive Boiler Specialty:** The locomotive boiler is optimized for rapid, high-volume steam production under dynamic conditions, with the blast pipe being a critical component for its operation.
*   **Circulation:** Natural circulation relies on density differences. Locomotive boilers achieve a pseudo-forced circulation due to the high draft created by the blast pipe.
*   **Historical Context:** Lancashire and Cornish boilers were workhorses of the industrial revolution but have largely been superseded by more efficient and higher-capacity water-tube boilers for stationary power generation.
