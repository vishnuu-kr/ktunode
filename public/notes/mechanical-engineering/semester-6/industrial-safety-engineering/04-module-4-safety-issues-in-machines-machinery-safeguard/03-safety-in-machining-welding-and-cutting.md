# Industrial Safety Engineering: Module 4 - Safety issues in Machines: Machinery Safeguarding
## Topic: Safety in Machining, Welding, and Cutting

This module delves into the critical safety aspects associated with common industrial processes: machining, welding, and cutting. Understanding and implementing proper safeguards are paramount to preventing accidents, protecting workers' health, and ensuring efficient operations.

### 1. Safety in Machining Operations

Machining involves the removal of material from a workpiece using cutting tools to shape it. Common machining operations include turning, milling, drilling, grinding, and shaping.

#### 1.1. Hazards in Machining Operations

*   **Mechanical Hazards:**
    *   **Moving Parts:** Rotating shafts, gears, belts, pulleys, chucks, tool holders, and cutting tools are major sources of entanglement and impact injuries.
    *   **Flying Chips (Swart):** Sharp metal chips ejected at high speeds can cause eye injuries, cuts, and abrasions.
    *   **Sharp Edges:** Workpieces and cutting tools themselves present sharp edges that can cause lacerations.
    *   **Workpiece Ejection:** Unsecured workpieces can be thrown from the machine during operation.
    *   **Crushing/Pinching Points:** Areas where moving and stationary parts come together, or where the workpiece is held, can cause crushing injuries.
*   **Electrical Hazards:**
    *   Faulty wiring, damaged cords, and improper grounding of machines can lead to electric shock.
*   **Thermal Hazards:**
    *   **Hot Chips and Workpieces:** Machining generates heat, making chips and workpieces dangerously hot.
    *   **Cutting Fluids/Lubricants:** Some cutting fluids can become hot during use, posing a burn risk.
*   **Noise Hazards:**
    *   High noise levels generated by machinery can lead to noise-induced hearing loss over time.
*   **Chemical Hazards:**
    *   Cutting fluids and lubricants can contain chemicals that cause skin irritation or respiratory problems if not handled properly.
*   **Ergonomic Hazards:**
    *   Repetitive motions, awkward postures, and heavy lifting of workpieces can lead to musculoskeletal disorders.

#### 1.2. Machinery Safeguarding in Machining

Safeguarding aims to prevent contact with hazardous moving parts.

*   **Types of Safeguards:**
    *   **Physical Barriers/Guards:**
        *   **Fixed Guards:** Permanently attached guards that enclose the hazard. Examples include machine enclosures, covers for belts and gears.
        *   **Interlocked Guards:** Guards that are connected to the machine's power control. The machine cannot operate if the guard is open, and the machine stops if the guard is opened during operation. (Jain, R.K., 2000).
        *   **Adjustable Guards:** Guards that can be adjusted to accommodate different workpiece sizes or operations, providing protection while allowing access. Example: drill press guards.
        *   **Self-Adjusting Guards:** Guards that automatically adjust themselves to the changing size of the workpiece or tool. Example: bandsaw wheel guards.
    *   **Devices:**
        *   **Presence Sensing Devices:** Light curtains, laser scanners, or safety mats that detect a person's presence and stop the machine.
        *   **Pull-back Devices:** Devices that retract the operator's hands from the danger zone during a stroke.
        *   **Two-Hand Controls:** Require the operator to use both hands to activate the machine, keeping hands away from the point of operation.
*   **Key Safeguarding Principles for Machining:**
    *   **Point of Operation Guarding:** Protecting the area where the workpiece is machined and chips are generated.
    *   **Power Transmission Guarding:** Enclosing all power transmission components like gears, belts, and pulleys.
    *   **General Machine Guarding:** Protecting against all other potential hazards.
    *   **Interlocking Mechanisms:** Essential for ensuring guards are in place before operation. (Grimaldi & Simonds, 2001).
    *   **Emergency Stop Buttons:** Clearly visible and accessible buttons to immediately shut down the machine.
    *   **Proper Maintenance:** Regularly inspecting and maintaining guards and safety devices.

#### 1.3. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for Machining

*   **Eye Protection:** Safety glasses with side shields or goggles are mandatory to protect against flying chips.
*   **Hand Protection:** Cut-resistant gloves, especially when handling sharp workpieces or chips. However, care must be taken with rotating machinery as loose gloves can be a snag hazard.
*   **Foot Protection:** Safety shoes with steel toe caps to protect against falling objects.
*   **Hearing Protection:** Earplugs or earmuffs if noise levels exceed permissible limits.
*   **Respiratory Protection:** Respirators might be needed if there's significant mist or vapor from cutting fluids.
*   **Clothing:** Close-fitting, durable clothing. Avoid loose sleeves, ties, or jewelry that can get caught.

#### 1.4. Best Practices and Safety Tips for Machining

*   **Training:** Ensure all operators are thoroughly trained on machine operation, hazards, and safety procedures.
*   **Machine Inspection:** Inspect machines and guards before each shift.
*   **Chip Removal:** Use brushes or specialized tools for chip removal; never use hands or compressed air.
*   **Workpiece Securing:** Ensure workpieces are properly clamped and secured.
*   **Lighting:** Adequate lighting in the work area is crucial.
*   **Housekeeping:** Maintain a clean and organized workspace to prevent slips, trips, and fire hazards.
*   **Cutting Fluid Management:** Use appropriate cutting fluids and ensure proper ventilation and handling to minimize health risks.

### 2. Safety in Welding Operations

Welding is a process of joining materials, usually metals, by melting and fusing them together, often with the addition of a filler material.

#### 2.1. Hazards in Welding Operations

*   **Thermal Hazards:**
    *   **Arc Rays (UV & IR Radiation):** Intense ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) radiation emitted by electric arcs can cause severe burns to skin (erythema) and eyes (arc eye/photokeratitis). (Anton, T.J., 1989).
    *   **Sparks and Molten Metal:** Flying sparks and molten metal droplets can cause burns and ignite flammable materials.
    *   **Hot Workpieces:** Welded joints and surrounding metal become very hot.
*   **Electrical Hazards:**
    *   **Electric Shock:** Welding equipment, especially arc welding machines, operates at high voltages and currents. Contact with live parts, faulty equipment, or working in wet conditions significantly increases the risk of severe electric shock.
*   **Fume and Gas Hazards:**
    *   **Welding Fumes:** Metal oxides, flux fumes, and base metal vapors are released during welding. The composition of fumes depends on the base metal, filler material, and coatings. These can cause respiratory irritation, metal fume fever, and long-term health effects like lung disease.
    *   **Shielding Gases:** Inert gases (e.g., Argon, Helium) and active gases (e.g., CO2) are used. In confined spaces, they can displace oxygen, leading to asphyxiation.
*   **Fire and Explosion Hazards:**
    *   Sparks and hot slag can ignite flammable materials, liquids, or gases.
    *   Welding in or near containers that previously held flammable substances without proper purging can lead to explosions.
*   **Noise Hazards:**
    *   Some welding processes (e.g., arc welding, grinding during welding) can generate high noise levels.
*   **Ergonomic Hazards:**
    *   Awkward postures, repetitive movements, and the weight of welding equipment can lead to musculoskeletal issues.

#### 2.2. Machinery Safeguarding and Control Measures in Welding

Safeguarding in welding focuses on protecting workers from radiation, fumes, electrical hazards, and fire.

*   **Radiation Protection:**
    *   **Welding Helmets and Shields:** Equipped with specialized filter lenses (shades) that absorb UV and IR radiation. The shade number depends on the welding process and current. (Blake, R., 1973).
    *   **Protective Clothing:** Flame-resistant clothing, leather aprons, spats, and gauntlets made of appropriate materials to cover exposed skin.
    *   **Screens and Curtains:** Fire-resistant screens or curtains to shield bystanders from welding arcs.
*   **Fume and Gas Control:**
    *   **Ventilation:**
        *   **Local Exhaust Ventilation (LEV):** Capturing fumes at the source using fume extractors (e.g., gooseneck extractors positioned close to the weld). This is the most effective method.
        *   **General Ventilation:** Diluting fumes by providing ample fresh air, especially in open areas.
    *   **Respiratory Protection:** Approved respirators (e.g., half-mask respirators with appropriate cartridges or supplied-air respirators) when ventilation is inadequate or for specific hazardous tasks.
    *   **Confined Space Entry Procedures:** Strict protocols for welding in confined spaces, including atmospheric monitoring, ventilation, and attendant personnel.
*   **Electrical Safety:**
    *   **Proper Insulation:** Ensure all welding cables and equipment are properly insulated and in good condition.
    *   **Grounding:** Properly ground the welding machine and workpiece.
    *   **Dry Conditions:** Avoid welding in wet or damp environments. If unavoidable, use a welding machine with automatic voltage control (AVC) or ensure adequate insulation.
    *   **Qualified Personnel:** Only trained and qualified personnel should perform electrical work on welding equipment.
*   **Fire and Explosion Prevention:**
    *   **Pre-work Fire Watch:** Inspect the work area for flammable materials and remove them or cover them with fire-resistant materials.
    *   **Fire Extinguishers:** Keep appropriate fire extinguishers readily available.
    *   **Hot Work Permits:** Obtain permits for welding in areas where fire hazards exist.
    *   **Purging Containers:** Thoroughly clean and purge any containers that have held flammable materials before welding.
*   **Noise Control:** Use hearing protection.

#### 2.3. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for Welding

*   **Eye and Face Protection:** Welding helmet with an appropriate shade lens (e.g., shade 9-13 for arc welding). Safety glasses worn under the welding helmet for additional protection.
*   **Head Protection:** Flame-resistant hood or cap under the welding helmet.
*   **Hand Protection:** Heavy-duty leather welding gloves to protect against heat, sparks, and metal.
*   **Foot Protection:** Leather safety boots with steel toe caps.
*   **Body Protection:** Flame-resistant clothing, long-sleeved shirts and trousers made of cotton, wool, or treated synthetics. Leather apron and spats for additional protection from sparks.
*   **Hearing Protection:** Earplugs or earmuffs, especially during grinding or noisy welding operations.

#### 2.4. Best Practices and Safety Tips for Welding

*   **Know Your Materials:** Understand the specific hazards associated with the metals and coatings being welded.
*   **Ventilation First:** Always prioritize ventilation to control fumes.
*   **Electrical Checks:** Regularly inspect welding leads and connections.
*   **Fire Prevention:** Treat every welding job as a potential fire hazard.
*   **Confined Space Awareness:** Exercise extreme caution and follow strict procedures when welding in confined spaces.
*   **Training:** Ensure welders are certified and trained on safety procedures for specific welding processes.

### 3. Safety in Cutting Operations

Cutting operations involve separating materials, often using thermal or mechanical means. Examples include oxy-fuel cutting, plasma cutting, laser cutting, and abrasive cutting.

#### 3.1. Hazards in Cutting Operations

*   **Thermal Hazards (especially for thermal cutting processes):**
    *   **Intense Heat and Flames:** Oxy-fuel and plasma cutting generate extreme heat and open flames.
    *   **Molten Metal and Slag:** Ejected molten material can cause severe burns and ignite surrounding materials.
    *   **Hot Workpieces:** Cut edges and the workpiece itself remain hot.
*   **Radiation Hazards (especially for thermal cutting processes):**
    *   **UV and IR Radiation:** Similar to welding, thermal cutting arcs and flames emit harmful radiation.
*   **Fume and Gas Hazards:**
    *   **Metal Fumes:** Vaporized base metals, filler materials (if used), and fluxes create hazardous fumes.
    *   **Cutting Gases:**
        *   **Oxy-fuel Cutting:** Oxygen and fuel gases (acetylene, propane) are used. Leaks can create fire and explosion hazards. Oxygen enrichment can make ordinary combustibles ignite more readily.
        *   **Plasma Cutting:** Ionized gas (plasma) and shielding gases are used. These can produce ozone and nitrogen oxides.
*   **Electrical Hazards (for plasma, laser, and powered abrasive cutting):**
    *   High voltages used in plasma and laser cutters pose a significant electrocution risk.
    *   Powered abrasive cutters involve electrical motors.
*   **Fire and Explosion Hazards:**
    *   Sparks, flames, and hot material can ignite flammable substances.
    *   Improper handling of compressed gas cylinders (oxy-fuel) can lead to explosions or uncontrolled releases.
*   **Noise Hazards:**
    *   Plasma cutters, grinders, and some mechanical cutters can produce high noise levels.
*   **Mechanical Hazards (for mechanical cutting):**
    *   Moving blades, cutting edges, pinch points, and ejected material are risks.
*   **Flying Debris:**
    *   Besides molten metal, sparks and fragments of the material being cut can be ejected.

#### 3.2. Machinery Safeguarding and Control Measures in Cutting

Safeguarding strategies mirror those for welding, with an emphasis on thermal, fume, electrical, and fire hazards.

*   **Radiation and Heat Protection:**
    *   **Welding/Cutting Helmets and Shields:** With appropriate shade lenses for thermal cutting.
    *   **Protective Clothing:** Flame-resistant and heat-resistant clothing, gloves, and footwear.
    *   **Screens and Curtains:** To shield others from sparks and radiation.
*   **Fume and Gas Control:**
    *   **Ventilation:** LEV is crucial for capturing fumes at the source. General ventilation for dilution.
    *   **Respiratory Protection:** Use of appropriate respirators based on the fume composition and concentration.
    *   **Gas Cylinder Safety:**
        *   Store cylinders upright, secured with straps.
        *   Keep cylinders away from heat sources and electrical circuits.
        *   Use correct regulators and flashback arrestors.
        *   Ensure proper valve handling and maintenance.
        *   Never use oil or grease on oxygen regulators or valves. (AIChE/CCPS, 1992).
*   **Electrical Safety:**
    *   Follow similar electrical safety precautions as in welding for plasma, laser, and powered abrasive cutters.
    *   Ensure proper grounding and insulation.
    *   Keep electrical components dry.
*   **Fire and Explosion Prevention:**
    *   Similar fire prevention measures as welding: remove flammables, fire watch, extinguishers, hot work permits.
*   **Mechanical Safeguarding (for mechanical cutters):**
    *   **Blade Guards:** Enclose rotating blades and cutting edges.
    *   **Interlocking Guards:** For powered cutters.
    *   **Ejection Guards:** To contain or direct ejected material.
*   **Noise Control:** Use hearing protection.

#### 3.3. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for Cutting

*   **Eye and Face Protection:** Cutting/welding helmet or goggles with appropriate shade lenses. Face shields over safety glasses for additional protection.
*   **Hand Protection:** Heat-resistant and cut-resistant gloves.
*   **Foot Protection:** Leather safety boots.
*   **Body Protection:** Flame-resistant clothing, leather apron, spats, gauntlets.
*   **Hearing Protection:** Earplugs or earmuffs.
*   **Respiratory Protection:** Respirator suitable for the specific fumes and gases generated.

#### 3.4. Best Practices and Safety Tips for Cutting

*   **Gas Cylinder Management:** Handle compressed gas cylinders with extreme care.
*   **Cutting Area Preparation:** Ensure the cutting area is free of flammable materials.
*   **Ventilation Assessment:** Always assess the need for ventilation before starting cutting operations.
*   **Equipment Checks:** Inspect cutting equipment, hoses, regulators, and electrical connections before use.
*   **Thermal Cutting Specifics:** Understand the specific properties of the gases and equipment used for thermal cutting.
*   **Mechanical Cutter Safety:** Adhere to guarding and operational procedures for mechanical cutting tools.

### Key Points to Remember:

*   **Proactive Safeguarding:** The most effective safety strategy is to design and implement safeguards *before* an accident occurs. (Blake, R., 1973).
*   **Hierarchy of Controls:** Whenever possible, use engineering controls (like guards and ventilation) over administrative controls (like procedures) and PPE.
*   **Training is Paramount:** No safeguard is effective if the operator is not properly trained on its use and the associated hazards.
*   **Regular Inspection and Maintenance:** Guards and safety devices must be regularly inspected and maintained to ensure they function correctly.
*   **Environmental Factors:** Consider the work environment (e.g., presence of flammables, dampness) when assessing and mitigating hazards.
*   **Understanding Material Properties:** The specific materials being machined, welded, or cut will dictate the unique hazards and required safeguards.

### Answers to Practice Questions:

**1. Multiple Choice Questions:**

a) iv) Radiation (While some machining processes might produce very minor radiation, it's not a primary hazard compared to the others listed. Radiation is a major hazard in welding and thermal cutting.)
b) ii) To filter out UV and IR radiation
c) iii) Oxygen enrichment can make ordinary combustibles ignite more easily
d) ii) A guard that stops the machine when opened

**2. Short Answer Questions:**

a) Three types of mechanical hazards in machining:
    *   Moving parts (e.g., rotating shafts, belts, gears)
    *   Flying chips (swart)
    *   Sharp edges
    *   Crushing/pinching points
    *   Workpiece ejection

b) The two main types of radiation hazards are:
    *   Ultraviolet (UV) radiation
    *   Infrared (IR) radiation

c) LEV in welding is important because it captures welding fumes at their source, preventing them from dispersing into the work environment and being inhaled by the welder or others. This is the most effective way to control fume exposure and protect respiratory health.

d) A critical safety precaution when handling compressed gas cylinders is to store them upright and secure them to prevent them from falling, and to use correct regulators and flashback arrestors. Additionally, never use oil or grease on oxygen regulators or valves.

**3. Scenario-Based Question:**

a) **Machinist Scenario:**
    *   **Hazards:** Flying chips (primary), mechanical hazards from rotating parts (lathe chuck, spindle), sharp edges on workpiece/tool.

b) **Recommendations for Machinist:**
    *   **Safeguards:** Ensure the lathe has a functional chip guard or a full enclosure if available.
    *   **PPE:** Mandatory safety glasses with side shields (or goggles). Consider cut-resistant gloves if handling sharp workpieces, but caution against loose gloves near rotating parts.

c) **Welder Scenario (Confined Space):**
    *   **Hazards:** Fumes and gases displacing oxygen (asphyxiation risk), accumulation of toxic welding fumes, potential for heat build-up.

d) **Recommendations for Welder:**
    *   **Safeguards:** Implement strict confined space entry procedures, including continuous forced ventilation (e.g., using blowers) to introduce fresh air and exhaust fumes. Use a supplied-air respirator or an air-purifying respirator with appropriate cartridges if ventilation is insufficient or for extended work. Employ a qualified attendant outside the confined space to monitor the welder and communicate.
    *   **PPE:** Welding helmet with appropriate shade, flame-resistant clothing, leather gloves, safety boots.

e) **Potential Consequences:**
    *   **Machinist:** Eye injury (corneal abrasion or laceration) from flying chips, cuts from sharp edges.
    *   **Welder:** Asphyxiation due to oxygen deficiency, acute or chronic respiratory illnesses from toxic fumes, burns from sparks or hot material.
