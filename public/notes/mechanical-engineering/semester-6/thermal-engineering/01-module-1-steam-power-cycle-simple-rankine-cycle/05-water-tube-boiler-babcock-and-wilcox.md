# Thermal Engineering: Module 1 - Steam Power Cycle (Simple Rankine Cycle)

## Topic: Water Tube Boiler - Babcock and Wilcox

---

### **1. Introduction to Boilers and their Role in Steam Power Cycles**

*   **Definition:** A boiler is a closed vessel in which water is heated, vaporized, and converted into steam under pressure.
*   **Function in Rankine Cycle:** Boilers are the heat input devices in the Rankine cycle. They supply high-pressure, high-temperature steam to the turbine, which is the prime mover of the power plant.
*   **Types of Boilers:**
    *   **Fire-tube boilers:** Hot gases pass through tubes surrounded by water. (e.g., Lancashire boiler, Cochran boiler)
    *   **Water-tube boilers:** Water passes through tubes surrounded by hot gases. These are generally preferred for higher pressures and capacities due to better heat transfer and safety.
*   **Importance of Boiler Efficiency:** Efficient heat transfer in the boiler directly impacts the overall thermal efficiency of the power plant.

---

### **2. Water Tube Boilers: Advantages and Principles**

*   **Princ of Operation:** In water-tube boilers, the combustion gases flow on the outside of the tubes, while the water circulates inside. This arrangement allows for higher steam pressures and capacities compared to fire-tube boilers.
*   **Advantages of Water-Tube Boilers:**
    *   Higher steam pressures and temperatures can be achieved.
    *   Larger heating surfaces are possible, leading to better heat transfer.
    *   More flexible in design and can be adapted for various fuel types.
    *   Safer due to the smaller volume of water contained within the tubes. If a tube ruptures, the explosion is less severe.
    *   Quicker steaming due to rapid circulation of water.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Furnace:** Where fuel is burned.
    *   **Boiler Tubes:** Carry water and steam.
    *   **Superheater:** Heats the steam further after it leaves the boiler drum.
    *   **Economizer:** Preheats the feedwater using flue gases before it enters the boiler drum.
    *   **Air Preheater:** Heats the combustion air using flue gases.
    *   **Boiler Drum (or Steam Drum):** A large vessel where steam is separated from water.
    *   **Downcomers:** Tubes that carry water from the drum to the lower headers.
    *   **Risers:** Tubes where water is heated and turns into steam, rising back to the drum.

---

### **3. The Babcock and Wilcox Boiler**

The Babcock and Wilcox boiler is a prominent example of a modern, high-capacity water-tube boiler. It is known for its reliability, efficiency, and flexibility.

#### **3.1. Construction and Working Principle**

*   **Key Features:**
    *   **Inclined Water Tubes:** The boiler features a large number of inclined tubes (usually at an angle of about 45 degrees to the horizontal) connecting a lower water/steam drum to an upper steam drum.
    *   **Brick Furnace:** The furnace is typically enclosed in firebrick masonry to withstand high temperatures and to direct hot gases effectively over the tubes.
    *   **Baffles:** Metal plates (baffles) are strategically placed to guide the flow of flue gases across the water tubes, increasing the time of contact and thus improving heat transfer.
    *   **Downcomers and Risers:** Larger diameter tubes (downcomers) carry relatively cooler water from the steam drum to the mud drum (or lower headers). The inclined water tubes act as risers, where water is heated, steam is generated, and the mixture rises back to the steam drum.
    *   **Steam Drum:** The upper drum serves as a separator for steam and water. Steam collects at the top, and water collects at the bottom.
    *   **Mud Drum (or Lower Drum):** Located at the bottom of the bank of inclined tubes, it collects any sediment.
    *   **Superheater:** Typically located in the path of the hottest flue gases, between the boiler tubes and the chimney. It further heats the saturated steam produced in the boiler drum.
    *   **Economizer:** Placed in the path of flue gases before they exit to the chimney. It preheats the incoming feedwater, recovering waste heat.
    *   **Air Preheater:** Often placed after the economizer or in parallel, it heats the combustion air using remaining heat in the flue gases, improving combustion efficiency.

*   **Working Cycle:**
    1.  **Combustion:** Fuel (coal, oil, gas) is burned in the furnace, producing hot flue gases.
    2.  **Heat Transfer:** The hot flue gases flow over the inclined water tubes and through baffles. Heat is transferred from the gases to the water inside the tubes.
    3.  **Circulation:** Water from the steam drum flows down through the downcomers to the mud drum. This water then enters the inclined tubes, gets heated, and a mixture of water and steam rises back into the steam drum. This natural circulation is driven by the density difference between the water in the downcomers and the steam-water mixture in the risers.
    4.  **Steam Generation:** Inside the tubes, water absorbs heat and boils, forming steam.
    5.  **Steam Separation:** In the steam drum, the steam separates from the water.
    6.  **Superheating (Optional but common):** The saturated steam from the drum passes through the superheater, where it absorbs further heat from the flue gases, becoming superheated steam.
    7.  **Feedwater Preheating:** Incoming feedwater is preheated in the economizer using the residual heat from the flue gases.
    8.  **Air Preheating (Optional):** Combustion air is preheated in the air preheater to improve combustion efficiency.
    9.  **Flue Gas Discharge:** The cooled flue gases are then discharged to the atmosphere through the chimney.

#### **3.2. Advantages of Babcock and Wilcox Boiler**

*   **High Efficiency:** Effective use of baffles and placement of superheater and economizer ensure high heat transfer efficiency.
*   **High Steam Generation Rate:** The large heating surface area and efficient circulation allow for a high rate of steam generation.
*   **Reliability and Durability:** Robust construction and good material selection ensure a long operational life.
*   **Flexibility:** Can be designed to operate with various fuels and at different pressure/temperature conditions.
*   **Safety:** Designed with safety features like safety valves and fusible plugs.

#### **3.3. Applications**

*   Thermal power plants (for electricity generation).
*   Industrial processes requiring large quantities of steam.
*   Marine propulsion.

### **4. Heat Transfer Principles in Water Tube Boilers**

*   **Mechanisms of Heat Transfer:**
    *   **Convection:** Heat transfer from flue gases to the outer surfaces of the tubes, and from the inner tube surfaces to the water/steam mixture.
    *   **Radiation:** Significant heat transfer from the furnace walls (incandescent fuel bed and gases) to the boiler tubes.
    *   **Conduction:** Heat transfer through the tube walls.
*   **Factors Affecting Heat Transfer:**
    *   **Temperature difference:** Driving force for heat transfer.
    *   **Surface area:** Larger area leads to more heat transfer.
    *   **Heat transfer coefficients:** Influenced by fluid properties, flow rates, and presence of scale/deposits.
    *   **Flue gas velocity and turbulence:** Increased turbulence enhances convection.
    *   **Baffling:** Directs gas flow to maximize contact time and turbulence.
    *   **Cleanliness of surfaces:** Fouling and scaling reduce heat transfer.

### **5. Performance Parameters and Calculations**

*   **Boiler Efficiency:** The ratio of the useful heat absorbed by the water and steam to the heat supplied by the fuel.
    *   $\eta_{boiler} = \frac{\text{Heat absorbed by water and steam}}{\text{Heat supplied by fuel}}$
    *   **Method 1 (Direct Method):**
        $\eta_{boiler} = \frac{m_s (h_g - h_f)}{m_f \times CV_f}$
        Where:
        *   $m_s$ = mass of steam generated per hour (kg/hr)
        *   $h_g$ = enthalpy of superheated steam (kJ/kg)
        *   $h_f$ = enthalpy of feedwater (kJ/kg)
        *   $m_f$ = mass of fuel burnt per hour (kg/hr)
        *   $CV_f$ = Calorific Value of fuel (kJ/kg)
    *   **Method 2 (Indirect Method - Heat Loss Method):**
        $\eta_{boiler} = 1 - \frac{\text{Sum of heat losses}}{\text{Heat supplied by fuel}}$
        Common heat losses include:
        *   Loss due to dry flue gases ($Q_1$)
        *   Loss due to moisture in flue gases ($Q_2$)
        *   Loss due to incomplete combustion of carbon ($Q_3$)
        *   Loss due to radiation and other unaccounted losses ($Q_4$)

*   **Equivalent Evaporation:** The amount of steam that would be produced if water at boiling point (100°C) were evaporated into dry saturated steam at atmospheric pressure (101.325 kPa).
    *   **Equivalent Evaporation from and at 100°C** $= m_s \frac{(h_g - h_f)}{2257}$ (in kg/hr), where 2257 kJ/kg is the latent heat of vaporization at atmospheric pressure.
    *   This is a standard measure for comparing boiler performance independent of operating pressures and feedwater temperatures.

*   **Boiler Horsepower (BHP):** A unit of power used for boilers. One BHP is equivalent to the evaporation of 33,475 kJ/hr or 15.65 kg/hr of water from and at 100°C.
    *   $BHP = \frac{\text{Equivalent Evaporation (kg/hr)}}{15.65}$

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   Water-tube boilers are preferred for high-pressure and high-capacity applications due to safety and efficiency.
*   The Babcock and Wilcox boiler uses inclined tubes for natural circulation and efficient heat transfer.
*   Baffles are crucial for increasing flue gas turbulence and contact time with boiler tubes.
*   Superheaters increase steam temperature, improving cycle efficiency and reducing moisture in turbine blades.
*   Economizers and air preheaters recover waste heat from flue gases, boosting overall plant efficiency.
*   Boiler efficiency is a key performance indicator and can be calculated using direct or indirect methods.
*   Equivalent evaporation provides a standardized way to compare boiler performance.

### **8. Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:**
What is the primary advantage of a water-tube boiler over a fire-tube boiler for large-scale power generation?

**Answer:**
The primary advantage is their ability to safely operate at much higher pressures and generate steam at higher rates due to the larger heating surface area and better heat transfer characteristics. Also, in case of tube rupture, the explosion is less severe in water-tube boilers due to the smaller volume of water within the tubes.

**Question 2:**
Explain the role of baffles in a Babcock and Wilcox boiler.

**Answer:**
Baffles are strategically placed metal plates within the boiler casing that guide the flow of hot flue gases across the water tubes. Their purpose is to increase the turbulence of the gases and the time of contact between the gases and the tubes, thereby maximizing the rate of heat transfer from the gases to the water.

**Question 3:**
A Babcock and Wilcox boiler generates 50,000 kg of steam per hour at a pressure of 40 bar. The feedwater is supplied at 120°C. If the enthalpy of steam at 40 bar (saturated) is 2800 kJ/kg and the enthalpy of feedwater at 120°C is 500 kJ/kg, calculate the heat absorbed by the water and steam per hour.

**Given:**
*   $m_s = 50,000$ kg/hr
*   $h_g = 2800$ kJ/kg
*   $h_f = 500$ kJ/kg

**Calculation:**
Heat absorbed by water and steam = $m_s (h_g - h_f)$
= $50,000 \text{ kg/hr} \times (2800 \text{ kJ/kg} - 500 \text{ kJ/kg})$
= $50,000 \times 2300$ kJ/hr
= $115,000,000$ kJ/hr
= $115,000$ MJ/hr

**Question 4:**
A boiler produces 10,000 kg of steam per hour at 10 bar pressure, with 50°C superheat. The feedwater enters at 90°C. If the boiler efficiency is 85% and the calorific value of the fuel is 30,000 kJ/kg, calculate the fuel consumption per hour.
*(Assume properties of steam from steam tables or approximate values if not provided)*
*Let's assume enthalpy of saturated steam at 10 bar = 2779 kJ/kg. Enthalpy of superheated steam at 10 bar, 50°C superheat = 2779 + (specific heat of steam * temp rise) ≈ 2779 + (2.0 * 50) = 2879 kJ/kg. Enthalpy of feedwater at 90°C ≈ 377 kJ/kg.*

**Given:**
*   $m_s = 10,000$ kg/hr
*   $h_g(\text{superheated}) = 2879$ kJ/kg
*   $h_f = 377$ kJ/kg
*   $\eta_{boiler} = 85\% = 0.85$
*   $CV_f = 30,000$ kJ/kg

**Calculation:**
Heat absorbed by water and steam = $m_s (h_g - h_f)$
= $10,000 \text{ kg/hr} \times (2879 \text{ kJ/kg} - 377 \text{ kJ/kg})$
= $10,000 \times 2502$ kJ/hr
= $25,020,000$ kJ/hr

Boiler Efficiency = $\frac{\text{Heat absorbed by water and steam}}{\text{Heat supplied by fuel}}$
$0.85 = \frac{25,020,000 \text{ kJ/hr}}{m_f \times 30,000 \text{ kJ/kg}}$

$m_f = \frac{25,020,000}{0.85 \times 30,000}$
$m_f = \frac{25,020,000}{25,500}$ kg/hr
$m_f \approx 981.18$ kg/hr

**Answer:** The fuel consumption is approximately 981.18 kg/hr.
