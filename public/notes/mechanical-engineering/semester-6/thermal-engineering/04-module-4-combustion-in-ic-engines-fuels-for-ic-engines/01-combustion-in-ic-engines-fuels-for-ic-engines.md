## 1. Introduction to Combustion in IC Engines

Combustion is the rapid chemical reaction between a substance with an oxidant, usually oxygen, to produce heat and light. In Internal Combustion (IC) engines, combustion occurs within a confined space (the cylinder) and the energy released drives the piston.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Combustion:** A process of rapid chemical reaction that releases energy.
*   **Oxidant:** Typically oxygen in the air.
*   **Fuel:** The substance that reacts with the oxidant.
*   **Cylinder:** The confined space where combustion takes place.
*   **Stoichiometric Mixture:** The ideal ratio of fuel and air that results in complete combustion, producing only carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
*   **Rich Mixture:** More fuel than air is present; incomplete combustion occurs.
*   **Lean Mixture:** More air than fuel is present; combustion is less efficient.

**Rudra Moorthy (2003) highlights:** The efficiency and performance of an IC engine are heavily dependent on the complete and controlled combustion of the fuel-air mixture.

**V. Ganesan (2002) emphasizes:** The fundamental goal of combustion in an IC engine is to convert chemical energy stored in the fuel into thermal energy, which is then converted into mechanical work.

## 2. Fuels for IC Engines

Fuels are the primary energy source for IC engines. They are broadly classified based on their origin and processing.

### 2.1 Classification of Fuels

**A. Based on Origin:**

*   **Petroleum Fuels (Mineral Fuels):** Derived from crude oil. These are the most common fuels used in modern IC engines.
    *   **Gasoline (Petrol):** Primarily used in Spark Ignition (SI) engines.
    *   **Diesel Fuel:** Primarily used in Compression Ignition (CI) engines.
    *   **Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG):** Propane and butane, used in SI engines.
    *   **Compressed Natural Gas (CNG):** Primarily methane, used in SI engines.

*   **Biofuels:** Derived from renewable organic matter.
    *   **Ethanol:** Produced from fermentation of sugars and starches (e.g., corn, sugarcane).
    *   **Biodiesel:** Produced from vegetable oils or animal fats.
    *   **Biogas:** Produced from the anaerobic digestion of organic matter.

*   **Synthetic Fuels (Synfuels):** Produced from non-petroleum sources through chemical processes.
    *   **Gasoline/Diesel from Coal (Fischer-Tropsch process):**
    *   **Hydrogen:** Can be produced from electrolysis of water or steam reforming of natural gas.

**B. Based on Engine Type:**

*   **Fuels for Spark Ignition (SI) Engines:** Require a spark plug to initiate combustion. Generally more volatile fuels.
    *   Gasoline
    *   LPG
    *   CNG
    *   Ethanol (often blended with gasoline)

*   **Fuels for Compression Ignition (CI) Engines:** Ignition occurs due to high compression temperature. Require fuels with good self-ignition properties.
    *   Diesel Fuel
    *   Biodiesel

**R.K Rajput (2010) notes:** The choice of fuel is dictated by the engine design (SI vs. CI) and its combustion characteristics.

## 3. Properties of Fuels and Their Impact

The properties of fuels significantly influence engine performance, efficiency, and emissions.

### 3.1 Petroleum Fuels

**A. Gasoline (Petrol):**

*   **Composition:** A complex mixture of hydrocarbons, primarily paraffins (alkanes), naphthenes (cycloalkanes), and aromatics.
*   **Properties:**
    *   **Volatility:** The tendency to vaporize. Important for cold starting and fuel-air mixture formation. Measured by Reid Vapor Pressure (RVP) and distillation range.
    *   **Octane Number (ON):** A measure of a gasoline's resistance to knocking or auto-ignition.
        *   **Knocking (Detonation):** Undesirable auto-ignition of the fuel-air mixture in the combustion chamber, leading to a sharp knocking sound and potential engine damage.
        *   **Octane Rating Scale:**
            *   **n-Heptane:** Assigned an octane number of 0 (highly prone to knocking).
            *   **Iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane):** Assigned an octane number of 100 (highly resistant to knocking).
            *   **Octane Number = (% Iso-octane in mixture) + 0.5 * (% n-Heptane in mixture)**
        *   **Anti-knock Agents:** Additives like tetraethyl lead (TEL - now banned in many countries) and methylcyclopentadienyl manganese tricarbonyl (MMT) were used to increase octane number. Today, oxygenates like MTBE and ethanol are used.
    *   **Heat of Vaporization:** The energy required to vaporize the fuel. Lower heat of vaporization leads to better volumetric efficiency due to charge cooling.
    *   **Gum Formation:** Tendency to form sticky deposits in fuel systems due to oxidation of unsaturated hydrocarbons.
    *   **Additives:** Detergents, anti-oxidants, anti-corrosion agents, etc., are added to improve performance and reduce emissions.

**V. Ganesan (2002) explains:** Higher compression ratios in SI engines require fuels with higher octane numbers to prevent knocking and achieve better thermal efficiency.

**Example:** A gasoline with an octane number of 91 means it has the same anti-knock quality as a mixture of 91% iso-octane and 9% n-heptane.

**B. Diesel Fuel:**

*   **Composition:** Primarily straight-chain hydrocarbons (paraffins) and some naphthenes and aromatics. Less volatile than gasoline.
*   **Properties:**
    *   **Cetane Number (CN):** A measure of the ignition quality of diesel fuel – its readiness to auto-ignite under compression.
        *   **Ignition Delay:** The time lag between the injection of fuel into the hot compressed air and the start of combustion. A shorter ignition delay is desirable.
        *   **Cetane Rating Scale:**
            *   **n-Cetane (Hexadecane):** Assigned a cetane number of 100 (excellent ignitability).
            *   **Alpha-methylnaphthalene:** Assigned a cetane number of 0 (poor ignitability).
            *   **Cetane Number = (% n-Cetane in mixture) + 0.15 * (% Alpha-methylnaphthalene in mixture)**
        *   **Cetane Improvers:** Additives like alkyl nitrates are used to increase cetane number.
    *   **Flash Point:** The lowest temperature at which the fuel gives off sufficient vapor to form an ignitable mixture with air. Higher flash point means safer handling.
    *   **Fire Point:** The temperature at which the fuel ignites and burns continuously.
    *   **Viscosity:** Affects fuel atomization and pump operation.
    *   **Cloud Point & Pour Point:** Indicate the temperature at which wax crystals start to form, hindering fuel flow.
    *   **Sulfur Content:** Leads to sulfur oxide (SOx) emissions, which are harmful. Refineries aim to reduce sulfur content (ultra-low sulfur diesel - ULSD).

**J.B. Heywood (2011) points out:** A higher cetane number in diesel fuel results in shorter ignition delay, smoother combustion, reduced exhaust smoke, and lower engine noise.

**Example:** A diesel fuel with a cetane number of 45 has the same ignition delay characteristics as a blend of 45% n-cetane and 55% alpha-methylnaphthalene under standard test conditions.

**C. LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas):**

*   **Composition:** Primarily propane (C3H8) and butane (C4H10).
*   **Properties:**
    *   Stored as a liquid under pressure.
    *   High octane number (around 100-110).
    *   Burns cleaner than gasoline, with lower CO and hydrocarbon emissions.
    *   Requires a pressurized fuel system.

**D. CNG (Compressed Natural Gas):**

*   **Composition:** Primarily methane (CH4).
*   **Properties:**
    *   Stored as a gas under very high pressure.
    *   High octane number (around 120-130).
    *   Very clean burning, significantly lower emissions than gasoline or diesel.
    *   Requires robust high-pressure fuel tanks.

### 3.2 Biofuels

**A. Ethanol (C2H5OH):**

*   **Production:** Fermentation of biomass (corn, sugarcane, cellulose).
*   **Properties:**
    *   High octane number (around 100-113).
    *   Can be blended with gasoline (e.g., E10 - 10% ethanol, E85 - 85% ethanol).
    *   Good oxygenate, promoting more complete combustion.
    *   Higher heat of vaporization than gasoline, leading to charge cooling and potential volumetric efficiency improvement.
    *   Can absorb water, requiring careful fuel system design.
    *   Lower energy density than gasoline, meaning lower mileage per gallon.

**B. Biodiesel:**

*   **Production:** Transesterification of vegetable oils or animal fats.
*   **Properties:**
    *   Good cetane number (typically 45-55).
    *   Lower flash point and higher viscosity than diesel fuel.
    *   Lower sulfur content than conventional diesel.
    *   Burns cleaner, with reduced particulate matter and CO emissions.
    *   Can be blended with diesel (e.g., B5 - 5% biodiesel, B20 - 20% biodiesel).
    *   Can cause issues with rubber seals and hoses in older engines.
    *   Lower energy density than diesel.

**V Sajith and Shijo Thomas (2017) discuss:** Biofuels offer a promising avenue for reducing reliance on fossil fuels and mitigating greenhouse gas emissions from IC engines.

### 3.3 Synthetic Fuels

*   **Hydrogen (H2):**
    *   **Properties:**
        *   Very high energy content per unit mass.
        *   Burns very cleanly, producing only water as a byproduct.
        *   Very low auto-ignition temperature and wide flammability range.
        *   Requires high-pressure storage or advanced fuel cell technology.
        *   Can cause issues with NOx formation at high combustion temperatures.

*   **Fischer-Tropsch Fuels:**
    *   Synthetic hydrocarbons produced from syngas (CO + H2) derived from coal, natural gas, or biomass.
    *   Can be tailored to have properties similar to gasoline or diesel.
    *   Production is energy-intensive.

## 4. Combustion Phenomena and Fuel Properties

The interaction between fuel properties and the combustion process is crucial for engine operation.

### 4.1 SI Engine Combustion

*   **Process:** Fuel-air mixture is compressed, and ignition is initiated by a spark plug. A flame front propagates across the combustion chamber.
*   **Knocking (Detonation):** Occurs when the unburnt fuel-air mixture ahead of the flame front auto-ignites due to high temperature and pressure.
    *   **Octane number** is the primary property to combat knocking. Fuels with higher octane numbers are less prone to auto-ignition.
    *   **Factors influencing knocking:** Compression ratio, engine speed, load, spark timing, fuel-air ratio, and combustion chamber design.

### 4.2 CI Engine Combustion

*   **Process:** Air is compressed to a high temperature, and fuel is injected directly into the hot air. Combustion begins upon injection.
*   **Ignition Delay:** The time between fuel injection and the start of combustion.
    *   **Cetane number** is the key property. Higher cetane number leads to shorter ignition delay.
    *   **Impact of Ignition Delay:**
        *   **Short Ignition Delay:** Leads to rapid pressure rise, rough running, high noise levels, and increased NOx emissions.
        *   **Long Ignition Delay:** Leads to excessive fuel accumulation before ignition, resulting in smoky exhaust and potential engine damage.
*   **Factors influencing ignition delay:** Cetane number, injection timing, injection pressure, air temperature and pressure, and fuel-air ratio.

**H N Gupta (2018) emphasizes:** The ability of a fuel to withstand compression without auto-igniting (octane number for SI) and its ability to ignite readily under compression (cetane number for CI) are critical performance indicators.

## 5. Storage, Handling, and Future Trends

**A. Storage and Handling:**

*   **Gasoline:** Stored in tanks, requires measures to prevent evaporation and degradation.
*   **Diesel:** Stored in tanks, requires protection from water contamination and extreme cold (for pour point issues).
*   **LPG:** Stored in pressurized cylinders or tanks. Requires specialized equipment for refueling.
*   **CNG:** Stored in high-pressure cylinders. Requires specialized high-pressure refueling stations.
*   **Biofuels:** Ethanol can absorb water, requiring proper storage. Biodiesel can solidify at low temperatures.

**B. Future Trends:**

*   **Increased use of biofuels:** To reduce reliance on fossil fuels and lower carbon footprint.
*   **Development of advanced synthetic fuels:** To meet stricter emission standards.
*   **Alternative fuels:** Hydrogen, ammonia, and electric vehicles (EVs) are gaining traction, though the focus of this module is on fuels for IC engines.
*   **Fuel efficiency improvements:** Through engine design and advanced combustion strategies.
*   **Stricter emission regulations:** Driving the need for cleaner fuels and more efficient combustion.

## 6. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Define knocking in SI engines and explain the significance of octane number in relation to knocking. (CO4, K2)

**Answer:** Knocking, also known as detonation, is an uncontrolled, spontaneous auto-ignition of the fuel-air mixture in the combustion chamber ahead of the normal flame front. This results in pressure waves that cause a characteristic knocking sound and can lead to engine damage. The octane number is a measure of a fuel's resistance to knocking. A higher octane number indicates that the fuel can withstand higher compression ratios and temperatures without auto-igniting, thus preventing knocking.

**Question 2:** What is the difference between octane number and cetane number? Which type of engine uses each as a primary performance indicator? (CO3, K3)

**Answer:**
*   **Octane Number:** Measures the resistance of a fuel to **auto-ignition (knocking)** in **Spark Ignition (SI) engines**. Higher octane number means better resistance to knocking.
*   **Cetane Number:** Measures the **ignition delay** of a fuel in **Compression Ignition (CI) engines**. Higher cetane number means shorter ignition delay and quicker ignition.

**Question 3:** List three properties of diesel fuel and briefly explain their importance. (CO3, K3)

**Answer:**
1.  **Cetane Number:** Indicates ignition quality. A higher cetane number leads to a shorter ignition delay, smoother combustion, and reduced exhaust smoke.
2.  **Flash Point:** The lowest temperature at which fuel vapor can ignite with an ignition source. A higher flash point ensures safer storage and handling.
3.  **Viscosity:** Affects fuel atomization and pump performance. Proper viscosity ensures good spray formation for efficient combustion.

**Question 4:** Why is hydrogen considered a potentially clean fuel for IC engines, and what are some of its challenges? (CO4, K2)

**Answer:** Hydrogen is considered a clean fuel because its combustion product is primarily water (H2O), with no carbon emissions. However, challenges include its low energy density by volume, requiring high-pressure storage or large tank volumes, and the potential for NOx formation at high combustion temperatures. It also has a very wide flammability range and low auto-ignition temperature, which requires careful engine control.

**Question 5:** Explain the concept of a stoichiometric mixture. What are the implications of operating with a rich or lean mixture in an SI engine? (CO4, K2)

**Answer:** A stoichiometric mixture is the ideal ratio of fuel and air that results in complete combustion, meaning all the fuel and oxygen are consumed, producing only carbon dioxide and water.
*   **Rich Mixture (excess fuel):** Leads to incomplete combustion, producing more carbon monoxide (CO) and unburnt hydrocarbons (HC) in the exhaust. It can also lead to higher engine temperatures if not managed.
*   **Lean Mixture (excess air):** Results in more complete combustion of the fuel, leading to lower CO and HC emissions. However, excessively lean mixtures can lead to misfires, reduced power output, and increased NOx emissions due to higher combustion temperatures.

## 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **SI Engines:** Use gasoline, LPG, CNG. Octane number is critical. Ignition by spark plug.
*   **CI Engines:** Use diesel, biodiesel. Cetane number is critical. Ignition by compression.
*   **Octane Number:** Resistance to knocking. Higher is better for SI engines.
*   **Cetane Number:** Ignition quality (ignition delay). Higher is better for CI engines.
*   **Volatility:** Important for gasoline (cold starting, mixture formation).
*   **Heat of Vaporization:** Affects charge cooling and volumetric efficiency.
*   **Biofuels:** Renewable alternatives with environmental benefits but also unique handling and property considerations.
*   **Emissions:** Fuel properties and combustion quality directly impact emissions like CO, HC, NOx, and particulate matter.
*   **Modern Fuels:** Contain additives to improve performance, stability, and reduce emissions.
