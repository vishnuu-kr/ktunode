# Thermal Engineering: Module 4 - Combustion in IC Engines: Fuels for IC Engines

## Topic: C.I. Engines: Stages of Combustion in C.I. Engines

---

### **Introduction**

This topic delves into the intricate process of combustion that occurs within Compression Ignition (CI) engines, commonly known as diesel engines. Unlike Spark Ignition (SI) engines, CI engines rely on the auto-ignition of fuel due to the high temperature of compressed air. Understanding the stages of combustion is crucial for analyzing engine performance, efficiency, and emissions.

### **Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Compression Ignition (CI) Engine:** An internal combustion engine where ignition of the fuel is initiated by the high temperature of the air compressed in the combustion chamber, rather than by a spark.
*   **Auto-ignition:** The spontaneous ignition of a combustible mixture when it reaches its auto-ignition temperature.
*   **Ignition Delay Period:** The time interval between the start of fuel injection and the onset of auto-ignition. This is a critical parameter in CI engines.
*   **Fuel-Air Mixture:** In CI engines, the fuel is injected into highly compressed, hot air. The mixing process and the ensuing combustion are complex and occur over a range of fuel-air ratios.
*   **Pressure-Crank Angle Diagram:** A graphical representation of the cylinder pressure as a function of the engine's crank angle during a combustion cycle. It is a primary tool for analyzing the combustion process.

### **Stages of Combustion in CI Engines**

The combustion process in a CI engine can be broadly divided into four distinct stages, occurring sequentially as the fuel is injected and mixes with the hot compressed air. These stages are commonly visualized and analyzed using the pressure-crank angle diagram.

**Diagrammatic Representation (Conceptual - refer to textbooks for actual diagrams):**

*   **X-axis:** Crank Angle (degrees)
*   **Y-axis:** Cylinder Pressure (bar or MPa)

The diagram typically shows an initial rise in pressure due to compression, followed by a rapid rise due to combustion, and then a gradual decrease as the piston moves down during the power stroke. The "humps" or distinct regions of pressure rise correspond to the combustion stages.

---

#### **Stage 1: Ignition Delay Period**

*   **Description:** This is the period between the commencement of fuel injection and the initial auto-ignition of the fuel-air mixture. It is a crucial phase that significantly influences the subsequent combustion process and engine performance.
*   **Processes Involved:**
    *   **Physical Processes:**
        *   **Fuel Injection:** The fuel is injected into the combustion chamber at high pressure.
        *   **Atomization:** The injected fuel stream breaks down into fine droplets.
        *   **Vaporization:** The liquid fuel droplets absorb heat from the hot compressed air and vaporize into a gaseous state.
        *   **Mixing:** The fuel vapor mixes with the hot compressed air.
    *   **Chemical Processes:**
        *   **Pre-ignition Reactions:** Complex chemical reactions begin to occur within the fuel vapor and air mixture at high temperatures. These are typically free-radical chain reactions.
        *   **Formation of Intermediate Products:** Molecules like aldehydes, peroxides, and organic acids are formed.
        *   **Accumulation of Reactants:** These intermediate products accumulate as the temperature and pressure increase.
*   **Factors Influencing Ignition Delay:**
    *   **Fuel Properties:**
        *   **Cetane Number:** A higher cetane number indicates a fuel that ignites more readily, resulting in a shorter ignition delay. (Rudra Moorthy, 2003; R.K Rajput, 2010)
        *   **Latent Heat of Vaporization:** Fuels with a lower latent heat of vaporization vaporize faster, potentially reducing ignition delay.
    *   **Engine Operating Conditions:**
        *   **Air Temperature/Compression Ratio:** Higher air temperature (due to higher compression ratio or pre-heating) shortens the delay. (V. Ganesan, 2002)
        *   **Air Pressure:** Higher air pressure also promotes faster chemical reactions.
        *   **Injection Pressure:** Higher injection pressure leads to better atomization and atomization, potentially reducing delay.
        *   **Engine Speed:** Higher speeds can lead to shorter effective delay due to less time for heat loss.
    *   **Injection Timing:** The timing of fuel injection relative to the piston position.
*   **Duration:** Typically ranges from 1 to 2 milliseconds (or 5 to 15 degrees of crank angle at typical engine speeds).
*   **Importance:** A shorter ignition delay leads to a more gradual pressure rise, while a longer delay can result in a sudden, rapid pressure rise, causing knocking.

---

#### **Stage 2: Premixed Combustion (or Rapid Combustion)**

*   **Description:** Once the ignition delay period is over, a significant portion of the injected fuel that has mixed with air during the delay period and auto-ignites almost simultaneously. This leads to a very rapid burning of this fuel-air mixture.
*   **Processes Involved:**
    *   **Rapid Burning:** The pre-mixed fuel-air charge burns at a very high rate, producing a significant amount of heat energy.
    *   **Fast Pressure Rise:** This rapid heat release causes a steep increase in cylinder pressure and temperature.
    *   **Homogeneous Combustion:** This stage is characterized by the combustion of a relatively homogeneous mixture.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   This stage is responsible for the steepest slope on the pressure-crank angle diagram.
    *   The rate of pressure rise is very high.
*   **Factors Influencing:** The amount of fuel mixed with air during the ignition delay period is the primary factor. Good atomization and mixing during Stage 1 lead to more fuel being available for premixed combustion.
*   **Duration:** Relatively short, often only a few degrees of crank angle.

---

#### **Stage 3: Diffusion Combustion (or Mixing-Controlled Combustion)**

*   **Description:** After the premixed fuel has been consumed, the remaining fuel is injected into the cylinder. This fuel does not auto-ignite immediately upon injection. Instead, it vaporizes, mixes with the hot air, and then burns. The rate of this combustion is controlled by the rate at which the fuel can mix with the oxygen in the air.
*   **Processes Involved:**
    *   **Intermittent Injection and Mixing:** Fuel continues to be injected intermittently.
    *   **Diffusion of Reactants:** Fuel vapor diffuses into the oxygen-rich regions of the air, and oxygen diffuses into the fuel-rich regions.
    *   **Laminar and Turbulent Diffusion:** Combustion occurs in a diffusion flame, where reactants diffuse across the flame front. Both laminar and turbulent diffusion contribute to the burning rate.
    *   **Controlled Burning:** The rate of heat release is limited by the rate of mixing of fuel and air.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   This stage is characterized by a less steep but sustained rise in cylinder pressure, or a plateau in pressure, as the piston starts to move downwards.
    *   The rate of pressure rise is significantly slower than in Stage 2.
*   **Factors Influencing:**
    *   **Fuel Injection Rate:** Higher injection rates can lead to a faster burning rate, provided there is sufficient air.
    *   **Spray Characteristics:** Fine atomization and good penetration of the fuel spray are crucial for efficient mixing. (V Sajith and Shijo Thomas, 2017)
    *   **Air Motion (Swirl and Squish):** Turbulence induced by air motion enhances mixing and thus the rate of diffusion combustion. (J.B. Heywood, 2011)
    *   **Fuel-Air Ratio:** The availability of oxygen for mixing with the injected fuel.
*   **Duration:** This is typically the longest stage of combustion.

---

#### **Stage 4: The End of Combustion (or Late Combustion)**

*   **Description:** By this stage, most of the injected fuel has been burned. The combustion process continues at a decreasing rate as the remaining fuel mixes and burns. The heat release rate gradually diminishes as the availability of unburnt fuel and oxygen decreases.
*   **Processes Involved:**
    *   **Completion of Burning:** The last of the injected fuel burns.
    *   **Cooling and Expansion:** The combustion products begin to cool due to heat transfer to the cylinder walls and expansion as the piston moves downwards.
    *   **Dissociation of Products:** At very high temperatures, combustion products like CO2 and H2O can dissociate into CO, O2, H2, and OH, absorbing heat. This effect is more pronounced in SI engines but can occur to a lesser extent in CI engines.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   The pressure in the cylinder starts to fall as the piston moves downwards and the rate of heat release decreases.
    *   The pressure-crank angle curve shows a gentle slope downwards.
*   **Factors Influencing:** The completeness of combustion and the efficiency of mixing in the previous stages. Incomplete combustion can lead to increased exhaust emissions.
*   **Duration:** Continues until all the combustible material is consumed.

---

### **Summary of Stages and Pressure-Crank Angle Diagram**

| Stage                        | Duration                                  | Key Processes                                     | Pressure-Crank Angle Diagram Characteristic                       |
| :--------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Ignition Delay**        | Start of injection to ignition            | Atomization, Vaporization, Mixing, Pre-ignition reactions | Little or no pressure rise, following compression line.             |
| **2. Premixed Combustion**   | Ignition to end of premixed burning       | Rapid burning of premixed fuel-air mixture        | Steepest pressure rise.                                           |
| **3. Diffusion Combustion**  | End of premixed to end of injection       | Diffusion-controlled mixing and burning           | Less steep pressure rise or plateau.                              |
| **4. End of Combustion**     | End of injection to exhaust valve opening | Decreasing heat release, cooling, expansion       | Pressure falls as piston moves down; slower than compression. |

**Important Note:** The exact boundaries between these stages can be fluid, and there can be overlap, especially between stages 2 and 3. The nature of the transition depends heavily on fuel properties and operating conditions. (Rudra Moorthy, 2003)

### **Factors Affecting Combustion Stages**

*   **Fuel Properties (Cetane Number):**
    *   **High Cetane Number:** Shorter ignition delay, more fuel for premixed combustion, steeper initial pressure rise, potentially higher peak pressures, reduced knocking tendency.
    *   **Low Cetane Number:** Longer ignition delay, less fuel for premixed combustion, more fuel in diffusion stage, smoother pressure rise but potentially lower peak pressures, higher knocking tendency.
*   **Injection Timing:**
    *   **Early Injection:** Longer ignition delay period before TDC, leading to more premixed combustion near TDC. Can increase peak pressure and knock.
    *   **Late Injection:** Shorter ignition delay, less time for premixed combustion. Can lead to incomplete combustion and reduced power output if too late.
*   **Fuel Injection Pressure & Nozzle Design:**
    *   **Higher Injection Pressure:** Better atomization and spray penetration, leading to improved mixing and potentially shorter ignition delay and faster diffusion burning.
    *   **Nozzle Hole Size & Number:** Affects spray characteristics and fuel distribution.
*   **Engine Load:** Higher load generally leads to higher cylinder temperatures and pressures, which can shorten ignition delay and affect the rate of diffusion combustion.
*   **Engine Speed:** While higher speeds reduce the time available for processes, the increased turbulence can sometimes compensate or alter the relative durations of stages.
*   **Swirl and Turbulence:** Enhanced air motion improves mixing, accelerating both premixed and diffusion combustion.

---

### **Implications on Engine Performance**

*   **Peak Pressure:** Directly related to the rate of heat release. High peak pressures can stress engine components.
*   **Rate of Pressure Rise:** A very high rate of pressure rise (often due to a long ignition delay followed by rapid premixed combustion) causes engine knock, leading to noise, vibration, and potential engine damage.
*   **Ignition Delay:** A critical parameter. Excessive delay leads to knocking. Insufficient delay can lead to a lack of premixed combustion, making the combustion process smoother but potentially less efficient at very high speeds.
*   **Heat Release Rate:** The integral of the heat release rate over the combustion period determines the total work done. The shape of the heat release curve, dictated by the combustion stages, influences torque and power output.
*   **Combustion Efficiency:** The completeness of combustion in all stages affects the amount of fuel converted to useful work. Incomplete combustion leads to unburnt hydrocarbons and smoke.

### **Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** Define the ignition delay period in a CI engine and list three factors that influence it.

**Answer:** The ignition delay period is the time interval between the start of fuel injection and the onset of auto-ignition of the fuel in the combustion chamber of a CI engine.
Factors influencing ignition delay:
1.  **Fuel Cetane Number:** Higher cetane number leads to a shorter delay.
2.  **Air Temperature:** Higher air temperature (due to higher compression ratio) shortens the delay.
3.  **Fuel Injection Pressure:** Higher injection pressure improves atomization and mixing, potentially shortening the delay.

**Question 2:** Describe the premixed combustion stage in a CI engine. What is its main characteristic on a pressure-crank angle diagram?

**Answer:** The premixed combustion stage occurs after the ignition delay period, where a significant portion of the fuel that has mixed with air during the delay period auto-ignites almost simultaneously. This leads to a very rapid burning of this premixed charge. Its main characteristic on a pressure-crank angle diagram is a steep and rapid rise in cylinder pressure.

**Question 3:** How does diffusion combustion differ from premixed combustion in a CI engine?

**Answer:**
*   **Premixed Combustion:** Occurs when a significant amount of fuel has already mixed with air during the ignition delay and auto-ignites rapidly. The burning rate is limited by chemical kinetics.
*   **Diffusion Combustion:** Occurs after the premixed charge is consumed, where subsequent fuel is injected and burns as it diffuses into the hot air. The burning rate is controlled by the rate of mixing of fuel and air.

**Question 4:** Explain the phenomenon of engine knock in CI engines and relate it to the stages of combustion.

**Answer:** Engine knock in CI engines is typically caused by a prolonged ignition delay period. This delay allows a larger quantity of fuel to accumulate in the combustion chamber. When auto-ignition finally occurs, a significant portion of the fuel burns almost simultaneously, leading to a very rapid and uncontrolled pressure rise. This rapid pressure rise creates shock waves within the cylinder, resulting in the characteristic knocking sound and potentially damaging the engine. The knock is primarily associated with the rapid pressure rise during the **premixed combustion stage (Stage 2)**, which is a consequence of a long **ignition delay period (Stage 1)**.

**Question 5:** A CI engine is operating with a fuel that has a low cetane number. How would this typically affect the stages of combustion compared to a fuel with a high cetane number?

**Answer:** A fuel with a low cetane number will have a longer ignition delay period (Stage 1). This means more fuel will be injected before ignition occurs. Consequently, when ignition does happen, a larger amount of fuel will be available for premixed combustion (Stage 2), leading to a more rapid pressure rise and a higher likelihood of knocking. The diffusion combustion stage (Stage 3) will likely be more dominant as a larger proportion of the fuel burns in this manner.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Ignition Delay is Critical:** It dictates the start of combustion and influences the rate of pressure rise.
*   **Cetane Number is Key:** Directly correlates with ignition delay and knock tendency.
*   **Mixing is Paramount:** Effective atomization and air-fuel mixing are essential for efficient combustion across all stages.
*   **Pressure-Crank Angle Diagram:** A vital tool for visualizing and analyzing the combustion stages.
*   **Knocking:** A dangerous phenomenon caused by excessive ignition delay and rapid premixed combustion.
