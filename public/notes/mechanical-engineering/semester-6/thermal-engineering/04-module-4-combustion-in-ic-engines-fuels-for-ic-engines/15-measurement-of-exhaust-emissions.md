# Thermal Engineering: Module 4 - Combustion in IC Engines: Fuels for IC Engines

## Topic: Measurement of Exhaust Emissions

This module focuses on understanding and quantifying the pollutants generated by Internal Combustion (IC) engines. Accurately measuring these emissions is crucial for assessing engine performance, environmental impact, and for complying with stringent regulations.

### 1. Necessity of Measuring Exhaust Emissions

**Key Concept:** Environmental regulations, health concerns, and engine performance optimization drive the need for accurate emission measurement.

*   **Environmental Protection:**
    *   **Global Warming:** Greenhouse gases like CO2 and N2O contribute to climate change.
    *   **Acid Rain:** SOx and NOx form acidic compounds that damage ecosystems.
    *   **Ozone Depletion:** Certain compounds can deplete the stratospheric ozone layer.
    *   **Smog Formation:** Hydrocarbons (HC) and NOx react in the presence of sunlight to form ground-level ozone, a major component of smog.
*   **Public Health:**
    *   **Respiratory Issues:** Particulate Matter (PM), CO, SOx, and ozone can cause or exacerbate respiratory diseases.
    *   **Carcinogenic Effects:** Some hydrocarbons and heavy metals in PM are carcinogenic.
    *   **Neurological Effects:** Lead (historically used in gasoline) is a neurotoxin.
*   **Engine Performance and Efficiency:**
    *   Uncontrolled combustion leads to higher emissions.
    *   Monitoring emissions can help diagnose combustion issues and optimize fuel-air ratios for better efficiency and reduced pollution.
*   **Regulatory Compliance:**
    *   Governments worldwide set emission standards (e.g., Euro standards, EPA standards) for vehicles and industrial equipment.
    *   Accurate measurement is required to ensure compliance and avoid penalties.

**Important Point to Remember:** The drive for cleaner air and healthier environments is the primary motivator for measuring exhaust emissions.

### 2. Gaseous and Particulate Emissions from IC Engines

**Key Concept:** Various chemical compounds and solid particles are released as byproducts of fuel combustion in IC engines. The composition and quantity depend on fuel type, engine design, operating conditions, and the presence of emission control devices.

#### 2.1. Gaseous Emissions

*   **Carbon Dioxide (CO2):**
    *   **Formation:** Complete combustion of hydrocarbons.
    *   **Significance:** Major greenhouse gas contributing to global warming.
    *   **Measurement:** Non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) analyzers.
    *   *Example:* For every gallon of gasoline burned, approximately 8.8 kg of CO2 is produced.
*   **Carbon Monoxide (CO):**
    *   **Formation:** Incomplete combustion of fuel, typically due to rich fuel-air mixtures or insufficient oxygen.
    *   **Significance:** Toxic gas, reduces oxygen-carrying capacity of blood, irritant to respiratory system.
    *   **Measurement:** NDIR analyzers.
    *   *Example:* Can be significantly high in cold starts or under heavy acceleration with rich mixtures.
*   **Unburnt Hydrocarbons (HC) / Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs):**
    *   **Formation:** Incomplete combustion, flame quenching near cylinder walls, oil getting past piston rings, incomplete vaporization of fuel.
    *   **Significance:** Contribute to smog formation, some are carcinogenic, odor producers.
    *   **Measurement:** Flame ionization detectors (FID).
    *   *Example:* Methane, ethane, propane, benzene, toluene.
*   **Oxides of Nitrogen (NOx):**
    *   **Formation:** High-temperature combustion. Nitrogen and oxygen in the air react at high temperatures and pressures.
    *   **Significance:** Contribute to smog, acid rain, respiratory problems.
    *   **Measurement:** Chemiluminescence analyzers (NCD).
    *   *Example:* Primarily NO and NO2.
*   **Sulfur Oxides (SOx):**
    *   **Formation:** Combustion of sulfur present as an impurity in fuels (especially diesel and heavy oils).
    *   **Significance:** Major contributor to acid rain, respiratory irritant.
    *   **Measurement:** UV fluorescence analyzers.
    *   *Example:* SO2 is the primary SOx emission.
*   **Aldehydes:**
    *   **Formation:** Partial oxidation of hydrocarbons.
    *   **Significance:** Irritants, contribute to smog, some are carcinogenic.
    *   **Measurement:** Specific chemical analyzers or sampling with impingers followed by chemical analysis.
    *   *Example:* Formaldehyde, acetaldehyde.

#### 2.2. Particulate Matter (PM) Emissions

**Key Concept:** Solid or liquid particles suspended in the exhaust gas, primarily from incomplete combustion and fuel/lubricant additives.

*   **Soot:**
    *   **Formation:** Incomplete combustion of fuel, especially in diesel engines, under rich conditions. Consists mainly of carbon.
    *   **Significance:** Major component of diesel exhaust PM, contributes to respiratory problems and visibility reduction.
    *   **Measurement:** Gravimetric analysis (filter paper collection), optical/light scattering methods, mass sensing.
*   **Sulfates:**
    *   **Formation:** Oxidation of SO2 in the exhaust gases, especially when sulfur content in fuel is high.
    *   **Significance:** Acidic particles, contribute to respiratory issues.
    *   **Measurement:** Particulate filters followed by chemical analysis.
*   **Other Particles:**
    *   **Formation:** Ash from fuel additives, wear particles, condensed organic compounds.
    *   **Significance:** Can include heavy metals (e.g., from lubricating oil additives), organic aerosols.
    *   **Measurement:** Particulate filters followed by elemental and chemical analysis (e.g., XRF, ICP-MS).

**Reference:** Rajput, R.K. (2010). *Thermal Engineering*. Laxmi Publications. Chapter 22 covers emissions and their control, detailing the formation and significance of these pollutants.

**Important Point to Remember:** Diesel engines are generally known for higher PM emissions, while gasoline engines tend to have higher CO and HC emissions without proper emission controls.

### 3. Working Principles of Common Exhaust Gas Analyzers

**Key Concept:** Specialized instruments are used to detect and quantify the concentration of specific pollutants in the exhaust gas.

#### 3.1. Non-Dispersive Infrared (NDIR) Analyzer

*   **Target Gases:** CO, CO2, HC (hydrocarbons).
*   **Working Principle:**
    1.  The exhaust gas sample flows into a measurement cell.
    2.  A broad-spectrum infrared source emits IR radiation.
    3.  This radiation passes through the measurement cell and then through a reference cell.
    4.  The measurement cell contains the sample gas, which absorbs specific wavelengths of IR radiation corresponding to the molecules present (CO, CO2, HC).
    5.  A detector (often a dual-chamber detector separated by a diaphragm) measures the difference in IR intensity between the reference and measurement beams.
    6.  The reference cell is usually filled with a non-absorbing gas or a reference concentration of the target gas.
    7.  The amount of IR absorbed is directly proportional to the concentration of the gas in the sample.
    *   **Goldschmidt Cell:** A common design where the diaphragm deflects due to pressure changes caused by IR absorption.
    *   **Broad-band detector:** Measures the overall change in IR intensity.
    *   **Narrow-band detector:** Uses interference filters to isolate specific absorption wavelengths for higher selectivity.
*   **Advantages:** Robust, reliable, widely used, relatively inexpensive.
*   **Limitations:** Can suffer from cross-interference (e.g., H2O and CO2 can interfere with HC measurements).

#### 3.2. Flame Ionization Detector (FID)

*   **Target Gas:** Unburnt Hydrocarbons (HC).
*   **Working Principle:**
    1.  The exhaust gas sample is mixed with hydrogen gas and air.
    2.  This mixture is passed through a burner jet where it is ignited, creating a flame.
    3.  Organic molecules (hydrocarbons) in the sample, when passing through the flame, undergo ionization, producing positively charged ions and electrons.
    4.  A voltage is applied between the burner jet (collector electrode) and a surrounding electrode.
    5.  The ions and electrons are attracted to their respective electrodes, creating a small electrical current.
    6.  This current is amplified and measured.
    7.  The magnitude of the current is directly proportional to the number of carbon atoms in the hydrocarbon molecules that pass through the flame.
*   **Advantages:** Highly sensitive to hydrocarbons, virtually insensitive to most other common exhaust gases (H2O, CO2, N2, O2, CO, NOx).
*   **Limitations:** Requires a supply of hydrogen and air, cannot measure methane effectively (as it contains only one carbon atom).

#### 3.3. Chemiluminescence Analyzer (for NOx)

*   **Target Gas:** Oxides of Nitrogen (NOx).
*   **Working Principle:**
    1.  **NO Measurement:** The exhaust gas sample is directly introduced into a reaction chamber. NO reacts with ozone (O3) to produce excited states of NO2 molecules. As these excited molecules return to their ground state, they emit light in the infrared region. This light is detected by a photomultiplier tube (PMT).
    2.  **NOx Measurement (Total):** Before entering the reaction chamber, the exhaust gas sample is passed through a **catalytic converter** (typically a heated platinum catalyst) which reduces NO2 to NO. This converts all NOx (NO + NO2) into NO. Then, this mixture reacts with ozone as described above, and the total NOx concentration is measured.
    3.  The difference between the total NOx reading and the direct NO reading gives the concentration of NO2.
*   **Advantages:** Highly sensitive and selective for NOx.
*   **Limitations:** Requires a source of ozone (generated internally or externally), can be sensitive to variations in oxygen concentration.

#### 3.4. Ultraviolet (UV) Fluorescence Analyzer (for SOx)

*   **Target Gas:** Sulfur Oxides (SOx), primarily SO2.
*   **Working Principle:**
    1.  The exhaust gas sample is exposed to UV radiation.
    2.  SO2 molecules absorb UV radiation and get excited to higher energy states.
    3.  As they de-excite, they re-emit energy in the form of fluorescence (UV light at a longer wavelength).
    4.  A detector (like a PMT) measures the intensity of this fluorescence.
    5.  The intensity of the emitted fluorescence is proportional to the concentration of SO2 in the sample.
*   **Advantages:** Highly selective for SO2.
*   **Limitations:** Requires a UV source and can be affected by other substances that fluoresce under UV light.

#### 3.5. Oxygen (O2) Analyzer

*   **Target Gas:** Oxygen (O2).
*   **Working Principle:** Several types exist:
    *   **Electrochemical Sensor (Zirconia Sensor):** Works on the principle of ionic conductivity of zirconia at high temperatures. The difference in oxygen partial pressure between the sample gas and ambient air creates a voltage across the zirconia element. This voltage is proportional to the oxygen concentration.
    *   **Paramagnetic Sensor:** Oxygen is paramagnetic (attracted to a magnetic field). This property is exploited in some analyzers.
*   **Advantages:** Essential for determining air-fuel ratio (AFR) and for emission control systems (e.g., catalytic converters).
*   **Limitations:** Zirconia sensors require high operating temperatures.

**Reference:** Ganesan, V. (2002). *Fundamentals of IC Engines*. Tata McGraw-Hill. Chapter 13 discusses exhaust gas analysis and the instruments used, including descriptions of NDIR and FID. Heywood, J.B. (2011). *I.C Engine Fundamentals*. McGraw-Hill. Provides detailed explanations of measurement techniques and analyzers in Chapters 13 and 14.

**Important Point to Remember:** Each analyzer is designed to detect a specific pollutant based on its unique physical or chemical properties.

### 4. Methodologies for Sampling and Analyzing Exhaust Emissions

**Key Concept:** Obtaining a representative sample of exhaust gas and performing the analysis correctly are critical for accurate results.

#### 4.1. Sampling Techniques

*   **Direct Sampling:** The analyzer is directly connected to the exhaust pipe. This is common for on-board diagnostics or when testing stationary engines.
    *   **Requirements:** Heated sample lines are often necessary, especially for diesel exhaust, to prevent condensation of water and heavy hydrocarbons.
*   **Proportional Sampling:** The sample is drawn from the exhaust stream at a rate proportional to the engine's exhaust flow rate. This is important for determining average emission concentrations over a duty cycle.
    *   **Total Flow Sampling:** The entire exhaust flow is passed through a sample collection device (e.g., a bag or a dilution tunnel).
    *   **Partial Flow Sampling:** A small, metered fraction of the exhaust flow is extracted. This is more common in modern testing.
*   **Dilution Tunnel Sampling:**
    *   **Purpose:** To cool the exhaust, prevent condensation, and dilute the sample to a consistent level before analysis. Crucial for diesel emissions due to high PM concentrations and water vapor.
    *   **Mechanism:** A controlled amount of dilution air is mixed with the exhaust gas in a tunnel. The dilution ratio is monitored.
    *   **Types of Dilution:**
        *   **Constant Volume Sampling (CVS):** The total diluted exhaust flow is maintained at a constant volume per unit time, regardless of engine operating conditions. This ensures that any sample collected is representative of the average emission over the entire test cycle.
        *   **Variable Volume Sampling (VVS):** The flow rate is varied, but a sample is taken proportionally to this flow.

#### 4.2. Analysis Procedures

*   **Bag Sampling:**
    *   **Method:** Exhaust gas is collected in special Tedlar bags over a specific test cycle (e.g., a driving cycle for vehicles).
    *   **Analysis:** The bags are then analyzed using the appropriate gas analyzers. Separate bags are used for "raw" exhaust and "diluted" exhaust (from CVS).
    *   **Calculation:** Emissions are calculated based on the concentration in the bag, the volume of gas in the bag, and the engine's fuel consumption or power output.
*   **Online Analysis:**
    *   **Method:** The gas sample is continuously fed to the analyzers in real-time as the engine operates. This is the most common method for modern emission testing.
    *   **Advantages:** Provides instantaneous emission data, allows for real-time feedback and control.
*   **Particulate Matter Analysis:**
    *   **Method:** Exhaust gas is passed through a pre-weighed filter medium (e.g., a glass fiber filter).
    *   **Procedure:** The filter is placed in the exhaust stream for a specific duration or until a certain pressure drop is reached.
    *   **Analysis:** After sampling, the filter is dried and weighed again. The difference in weight gives the mass of PM collected. This is then divided by the volume of exhaust gas sampled to determine the PM concentration (e.g., mg/m³).
    *   **Further Analysis:** Collected PM can be subjected to chemical analysis to determine its composition (e.g., carbon content, sulfates, metals).

**Reference:** Sajith, V., & Thomas, S. (2017). *Internal Combustion Engines*. Oxford University Press. Discusses sampling methods and their importance for accurate measurement in Chapter 11.

**Important Point to Remember:** The choice of sampling technique and analysis method depends on the type of engine, the specific pollutants to be measured, and the required accuracy for regulatory compliance. CVS is a standard for vehicle emissions testing.

### 5. Calibration and Maintenance of Emission Measurement Equipment

**Key Concept:** Regular calibration and proper maintenance are essential to ensure the accuracy and reliability of emission analyzers.

#### 5.1. Calibration

*   **Purpose:** To ensure that the instrument's output signal correctly corresponds to the actual concentration of the pollutant.
*   **Types of Calibration:**
    *   **Zero Calibration:** The analyzer is exposed to a gas stream that contains none of the target pollutant (e.g., purified nitrogen). This establishes the baseline reading.
    *   **Span Calibration:** The analyzer is exposed to a gas mixture of known concentration (calibration gas) for each pollutant. The analyzer's response is adjusted to match the known concentration.
    *   **Pre-calibration Checks:** Performed before each use to ensure basic functionality.
    *   **Periodic Calibration:** Performed regularly (e.g., daily, weekly, or monthly) as recommended by the manufacturer.
*   **Calibration Gases:**
    *   High-purity calibration gases with certified concentrations of the target pollutants (e.g., CO, HC, NOx, SO2) are used.
    *   These gases are traceable to national standards (e.g., NIST in the US).
*   **In-line Interference Check:** Many analyzers have internal mechanisms or procedures to compensate for known interferences from other gases.

#### 5.2. Maintenance

*   **Filter Replacement:** Air filters, particulate filters, and sample line filters need regular replacement to prevent blockages and contamination.
*   **Sample Line Integrity:** Leaks in sample lines can lead to inaccurate readings. Regular inspection and repair are necessary.
*   **Burner Maintenance (FID):** The burner jet needs to be kept clean to ensure stable operation.
*   **Detector Maintenance:** Depending on the detector type, specific maintenance procedures might be required (e.g., cleaning optical surfaces, checking sensor health).
*   **Software Updates:** Keeping the analyzer's firmware and software updated ensures optimal performance and access to the latest features.
*   **Troubleshooting:** Familiarity with common error codes and troubleshooting procedures is crucial.
*   **Record Keeping:** Maintaining detailed records of calibration, maintenance, and repairs is important for quality assurance and regulatory audits.

**Reference:** Heywood, J.B. (2011). *I.C Engine Fundamentals*. McGraw-Hill. Chapter 14 offers insights into the practical aspects of instrument calibration and maintenance. Rathore, M. (2010). *Thermal Engineering*. McGraw Hill Education India. May touch upon practical aspects of instrumentation in relevant chapters.

**Important Point to Remember:** Calibration is not a one-time event but an ongoing process that is as important as the measurement itself. Neglecting calibration will render even the most sophisticated analyzer useless.

### Practice Questions and Answers:

**Question 1:** Which of the following is primarily responsible for contributing to acid rain?
    a) CO
    b) HC
    c) NOx and SOx
    d) CO2

**Answer:** c) NOx and SOx
**Explanation:** NOx reacts with water and oxygen to form nitric acid, and SOx reacts to form sulfuric acid, both of which contribute to acid rain.

**Question 2:** What type of detector is most commonly used to measure unburnt hydrocarbons (HC) in exhaust emissions?
    a) NDIR
    b) FID
    c) Chemiluminescence
    d) UV Fluorescence

**Answer:** b) FID
**Explanation:** Flame Ionization Detectors (FID) are highly sensitive to hydrocarbons due to the ionization of carbon atoms in a flame.

**Question 3:** The CVS (Constant Volume Sampling) system is primarily used for:
    a) Direct measurement of NOx.
    b) Real-time monitoring of CO.
    c) Collecting representative samples of exhaust emissions over a test cycle, particularly for vehicles.
    d) Measuring particulate matter using gravimetric methods.

**Answer:** c) Collecting representative samples of exhaust emissions over a test cycle, particularly for vehicles.
**Explanation:** CVS ensures that the sampled exhaust is diluted to a constant volume, making the collected sample representative of the overall emissions during a transient test cycle.

**Question 4:** Why is it important to calibrate exhaust gas analyzers regularly?
    a) To ensure the instrument looks good.
    b) To compensate for wear and tear and maintain accuracy.
    c) To increase the sensitivity of the detector.
    d) To change the operating temperature of the analyzer.

**Answer:** b) To compensate for wear and tear and maintain accuracy.
**Explanation:** Calibration adjusts the instrument's response to match known standards, correcting for any drift or changes that may occur over time.

**Question 5:** A Zirconia sensor is commonly used to measure which component in exhaust gas?
    a) CO
    b) HC
    c) NOx
    d) O2

**Answer:** d) O2
**Explanation:** Zirconia sensors operate based on the difference in oxygen partial pressure and are widely used for measuring oxygen concentration in exhaust gases.

### Summary of Key Points to Remember:

*   **Necessity:** Emission measurement is vital for environmental protection, public health, and regulatory compliance.
*   **Key Pollutants:** Be familiar with the common gaseous (CO, HC, NOx, CO2, SOx) and particulate (PM, soot) emissions.
*   **Analyzer Principles:** Understand the basic working principles of NDIR (CO, CO2, HC), FID (HC), Chemiluminescence (NOx), and UV Fluorescence (SOx) analyzers.
