# SOLAR ENERGY CONSERVATION SYSTEMS

## Module 2: Solar Collectors: Flat Plate Collector

### Topic: Parabolic Dish Collector

This section delves into parabolic dish collectors, a significant type of concentrating solar collector. Understanding these collectors is crucial for appreciating the diverse range of solar energy technologies and their applications in conserving energy.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Concentrating Solar Collector (CSC):** A solar collector that uses a system of mirrors or lenses to concentrate solar radiation onto a small area receiver. This significantly increases the incident solar flux compared to non-concentrating collectors like flat plate collectors.
*   **Parabolic Dish Collector:** A type of concentrating solar collector that uses a parabolic reflector (dish-shaped) to focus incoming solar rays onto a receiver located at the focal point of the parabola.
*   **Parabolic Reflector:** A mirror or reflective surface shaped like a paraboloid. When illuminated by parallel rays (like sunlight), it reflects all rays to converge at a single point – the focal point.
*   **Receiver:** The component of the solar collector that absorbs the concentrated solar radiation and converts it into thermal energy. For parabolic dish collectors, receivers are typically designed to handle high temperatures.
*   **Focal Point/Focal Length:** The point where the parabolic reflector converges parallel rays of light. The focal length is the distance from the vertex of the parabola to the focal point.
*   **Concentration Ratio:** The ratio of the aperture area (the area receiving direct sunlight) to the receiver area. A higher concentration ratio means more solar energy is focused onto a smaller area, leading to higher temperatures.
*   **Tracking System:** A mechanism that continuously moves the collector to keep it oriented towards the sun, maximizing the amount of solar radiation intercepted. Parabolic dish collectors require accurate two-axis tracking.
*   **Heat Transfer Fluid (HTF):** The fluid that circulates through the receiver, absorbs the heat, and transports it to where it can be used (e.g., for power generation or industrial processes). For parabolic dishes, HTFs capable of withstanding high temperatures are used, such as molten salts, synthetic oils, or even steam.

---

### 3. Principles of Operation:

1.  **Reflection and Concentration:** The parabolic dish, made of highly reflective material, precisely reflects incoming solar radiation. Due to the parabolic geometry, all parallel solar rays incident on the dish are reflected towards a single focal point.
2.  **Receiver Absorption:** The receiver is strategically placed at the focal point of the parabolic dish. It is designed to efficiently absorb the concentrated solar energy.
3.  **Heat Transfer:** A heat transfer fluid flows through the receiver. This fluid absorbs the thermal energy from the concentrated sunlight, raising its temperature significantly.
4.  **Tracking:** A sophisticated two-axis tracking system ensures that the dish is always oriented directly towards the sun. This maintains the maximum solar flux at the focal point throughout the day. The tracking is typically solar-based (using light sensors) or time-based (using astronomical data).
5.  **Energy Utilization:** The hot heat transfer fluid is then transported to a power block (e.g., a heat engine like a Stirling engine or a steam turbine) or used directly for high-temperature industrial process heat.

---

### 4. Design Features and Components:

*   **Parabolic Reflector:**
    *   **Material:** Typically made of polished aluminum, silvered glass, or specialized reflective films. The reflectivity is a critical parameter for performance.
    *   **Shape:** Precisely formed parabolic shape to ensure accurate focusing.
    *   **Aperture Area:** The diameter of the dish determines the aperture area and thus the amount of solar radiation collected.
*   **Receiver:**
    *   **Type:** Can be a cavity receiver, a flat plate receiver, or a finned tube receiver, depending on the application and HTF.
    *   **Design:** Optimized for maximum absorption of incident radiation and minimum heat loss to the surroundings. Often has selective coatings to enhance absorption and reduce emission.
    *   **Location:** Placed at the focal point, often supported by a pedestal or frame.
*   **Support Structure and Pedestal:**
    *   Provides structural integrity and supports the receiver at the focal point.
    *   Integrates the tracking mechanism.
*   **Tracking System:**
    *   **Actuators:** Motors and gears that move the dish.
    *   **Control System:** Receives input from sensors or time-based algorithms to control the actuators and maintain solar alignment.
*   **Heat Transfer Fluid (HTF) Loop:**
    *   Piping, pumps, and heat exchangers to circulate the HTF and transfer thermal energy.

---

### 5. Advantages of Parabolic Dish Collectors:

*   **High Concentration Ratios:** Can achieve very high concentration ratios (e.g., >1000 suns), leading to very high temperatures (up to 750°C or more).
*   **High Efficiency:** Due to high concentration and direct conversion of solar energy to thermal energy, they can be very efficient in converting incident solar radiation to usable heat.
*   **Versatility in Applications:** Suitable for a wide range of applications requiring high temperatures, including:
    *   Electricity generation (often coupled with Stirling engines or microturbines).
    *   Industrial process heat (e.g., chemical processing, manufacturing).
    *   Desalination.
    *   Hydrogen production.
*   **Modular and Scalable:** Can be deployed as individual units or in large solar farms.

---

### 6. Disadvantages of Parabolic Dish Collectors:

*   **Requires Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI):** Only effective when the sun is directly overhead. Cloud cover or diffuse radiation significantly reduces performance.
*   **Two-Axis Tracking is Essential:** Requires complex and accurate tracking systems, which adds to cost and maintenance.
*   **Higher Cost per Unit Area:** Compared to flat plate collectors, they are generally more expensive due to the precision of the parabolic shape, reflective materials, and tracking systems.
*   **Focal Point Variability:** Maintaining the receiver precisely at the focal point can be challenging, especially with wind or structural deformation.
*   **Potential for Glare and Safety Hazards:** Concentrated sunlight can be dangerous, requiring careful site selection and operation protocols.

---

### 7. Comparison with Flat Plate Collectors:

| Feature             | Flat Plate Collector                                | Parabolic Dish Collector                                       |
| :------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Concentration**   | None (or very low)                                  | High (e.g., >1000 suns)                                        |
| **Operating Temp.** | Low to medium (30-100°C)                            | High (up to 750°C+)                                            |
| **Tracking**        | Fixed or single-axis (optional)                     | Two-axis required                                              |
| **Irradiance Req.** | Direct and diffuse solar radiation                  | Primarily Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI)                       |
| **Complexity**      | Simple                                              | Complex (reflector shape, tracking system)                     |
| **Cost**            | Lower                                               | Higher                                                         |
| **Applications**    | Water heating, space heating, low-temp processes    | Electricity generation, industrial process heat, high-temp applications |
| **Efficiency (at temp.)** | Decreases with increasing temperature due to losses | Increases with increasing temperature due to high concentration |

### 8. Applications:

*   **Dish-Stirling Systems:** A common configuration where a parabolic dish focuses sunlight onto a Stirling engine located at the focal point. The heat from the concentrated sunlight drives the Stirling engine, which then powers a generator to produce electricity. These systems are known for their high efficiency and modularity.
    *   **Example:** The Solar Systems America's 'Sun-Dish' or the formerly prominent Stirling Energy Systems (SES) SunCatcher.
*   **Parabolic Dish for Industrial Process Heat:** Providing high-temperature heat for manufacturing, food processing, or other industrial applications.
*   **Solar Thermal Power Plants:** Large-scale arrays of parabolic dish collectors can be used to generate steam for traditional steam turbines to produce electricity.

### 10. Answers to Practice Questions:

**Answer 1:** The primary advantage is the ability to achieve much higher operating temperatures due to solar concentration. This allows for more efficient thermodynamic cycles (like those in Stirling engines or steam turbines) needed for electricity generation, which flat plate collectors cannot achieve.
**(CO2, K1)**

**Answer 2:** A parabolic dish collector concentrates sunlight onto a very small focal point. For maximum energy absorption, this focal point must remain precisely aligned with the sun. The sun's apparent position in the sky changes in both altitude (up/down) and azimuth (east/west) throughout the day and year. Therefore, two-axis tracking is required to continuously orient the dish towards the sun. Flat plate collectors, collecting diffuse and direct radiation over a larger area without concentration, are less sensitive to precise alignment and can function reasonably well with fixed or single-axis tracking.
**(CO2, K2)**

**Answer 3:**
*   Aperture Area ($A_{ap}$) = $\pi \times (\text{diameter}/2)^2 = \pi \times (5 \text{ m}/2)^2 = \pi \times (2.5 \text{ m})^2 = \pi \times 6.25 \text{ m}^2 \approx 19.63 \text{ m}^2$
*   Receiver Area ($A_{rec}$) = $0.01 \text{ m}^2$
*   Concentration Ratio (CR) = $A_{ap} / A_{rec} = 19.63 \text{ m}^2 / 0.01 \text{ m}^2 = 1963$
The approximate concentration ratio is **1963**.
**(CO2, K3)**

**Answer 4:**
1.  **Electricity generation:** Particularly with high-efficiency engines like Stirling engines, where high temperatures are crucial for thermodynamic efficiency.
2.  **High-temperature industrial process heat:** For applications like chemical reactions, metal treatment, or steam generation for industrial processes.
**(CO2, K1)**

**Answer 5:** A significant disadvantage is their reliance on **Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI)**. This means they perform poorly on cloudy days or when there is significant atmospheric haze, as they cannot effectively utilize diffuse solar radiation. This limits their geographic applicability and consistent energy output compared to flat plate collectors which can utilize both direct and diffuse radiation.
**(CO2, K2)**

---

### 11. Important Points to Remember:

*   Parabolic dish collectors are a type of **concentrating solar collector (CSC)**.
*   They use a **parabolic reflector** to focus sunlight onto a **receiver** at the **focal point**.
*   They achieve **high concentration ratios** and consequently **high operating temperatures**.
*   **Two-axis tracking** is essential for their optimal performance.
*   Their primary applications are in **electricity generation (Dish-Stirling)** and **high-temperature industrial process heat**.
*   They are most effective when there is high **Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI)**.
*   They are more **complex and expensive** than flat plate collectors but offer higher temperature capabilities.
