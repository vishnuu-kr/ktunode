# Non-Destructive Testing: Magnetic Particle Testing - Methods of Magnetization

## Module 2: Magnetic Particle Testing

---

### Introduction to Magnetic Particle Testing (MPT)

Magnetic Particle Testing (MPT) is a widely used non-destructive testing method for detecting surface and near-surface discontinuities in ferromagnetic materials. It relies on the principle that discontinuities disrupt the magnetic flux flow in a magnetized part, causing some flux to leak into the surrounding air. Magnetic particles, when applied to the surface, are attracted to these flux leakage fields, forming a visible indication of the defect.

**Key Concept:** MPT is applicable only to ferromagnetic materials (e.g., iron, nickel, cobalt, and their alloys) which can be readily magnetized.

**References:**
*   *Practical Non-destructive testing* by Baldev Raj (Chapter on Magnetic Particle Testing)
*   *Non -destructive testing* by Hull V and V John (Chapter on Magnetic Particle Testing)

---

### Methods of Magnetization

Effective MPT relies on establishing a sufficiently strong magnetic field within the component being tested. The method of magnetization chosen depends on the shape and size of the workpiece, as well as the orientation of the expected defects. The goal is to create a magnetic field that is perpendicular to the potential discontinuity.

**Key Concept:** The direction of magnetization is crucial. To detect a surface or near-surface defect, the magnetic field lines must cross the defect at approximately a 90-degree angle.

### Magnetization Techniques

Several techniques are employed to magnetize ferromagnetic materials for MPT. These techniques aim to establish a strong magnetic field with the correct orientation.

#### 1. Head Shot Technique (Circular Magnetization)

**Description:**
The head shot technique involves passing an electric current directly through the component itself, typically by placing it between two electrical contacts (probes or heads). This current flow creates a magnetic field that circles around the conductor (the component). The magnetic field lines are concentric circles around the current path.

**How it Works:**
*   The component is placed between two electrodes.
*   A high-amperage, low-voltage current is passed through the component from one electrode to the other.
*   This current creates a circular magnetic field within the component.

**Applications:**
*   Ideal for testing long, cylindrical parts like shafts, pipes, and bars.
*   Effective for detecting circumferential cracks or discontinuities running parallel to the length of the component.

**Advantages:**
*   Simple setup for cylindrical parts.
*   Can achieve high magnetic field strengths.

**Limitations:**
*   May not be suitable for irregularly shaped parts.
*   The current path can create localized heating, especially in thin-walled or long components.

**Important Point to Remember:** The head shot technique is primarily used to detect discontinuities oriented longitudinally (along the length of the part) because the magnetic field lines are circular.

**References:**
*   *Practical Non-destructive testing* by Baldev Raj (Section on Circular Magnetization)
*   *Non Destructive testing Techniques* by Ravi Prakash (Section on Head Shot Testing)

### 2. Coil Shot Technique (Longitudinal Magnetization)

**Description:**
The coil shot technique involves placing the component inside a coil and passing a current through the coil. This creates a magnetic field that runs parallel to the axis of the coil, effectively magnetizing the component longitudinally.

**How it Works:**
*   The component is placed within a solenoid (coil).
*   An electric current is passed through the coil.
*   This generates a magnetic field parallel to the axis of the coil and the component.

**Applications:**
*   Effective for detecting discontinuities oriented transversely (perpendicular to the length of the component), such as surface cracks.
*   Can be used for testing various shapes, including bars, tubes, and complex geometries, by appropriately positioning them within the coil.

**Advantages:**
*   Versatile for different part shapes and sizes.
*   Can achieve uniform magnetic field distribution.

**Limitations:**
*   Requires a coil of appropriate size for the component.
*   For very long parts, achieving uniform magnetization throughout can be challenging.

**Important Point to Remember:** The coil shot technique is used to detect discontinuities oriented transversely because the magnetic field lines are parallel to the length of the part.

**References:**
*   *Practical Non-destructive testing* by Baldev Raj (Section on Longitudinal Magnetization)
*   *New Technologies in electromagnetic non-destructive Testing* by Songling Huang & Shen Wang (Discusses coil configurations for electromagnetic testing)

### 3. Central Conductor Testing

**Description:**
Central conductor testing is a variation of circular magnetization where a conductive rod or cable is passed through the hollow center of a tubular or ring-shaped component. Current is passed through this central conductor, creating a magnetic field that circles around the conductor and passes through the walls of the component.

**How it Works:**
*   A conductive rod or cable is inserted through the hollow of the workpiece.
*   Current is passed through the central conductor.
*   This generates a circular magnetic field that penetrates the walls of the component.

**Applications:**
*   Specifically designed for testing hollow components like pipes, tubes, bushings, and rings.
*   Effective for detecting longitudinal defects on the inner and outer surfaces of these components.

**Advantages:**
*   Efficiently magnetizes hollow sections.
*   Provides a means to magnetize internal surfaces.

**Limitations:**
*   Requires a central conductor of appropriate size and material.
*   The magnetic field strength can decrease towards the outer diameter of thicker-walled components.

**Important Point to Remember:** This technique is a specialized form of circular magnetization for hollow items, used to detect longitudinal flaws.

**References:**
*   *Practical Non-destructive testing* by Baldev Raj (Section on Central Conductor Testing)
*   *Non -destructive testing* by Hull V and V John (Mention of techniques for hollow parts)

### 4. Magnetization Using Yokes (AC Yoke and DC Yoke)

**Description:**
Yokes are portable electromagnetic devices used to establish a magnetic field across a specific area of the component. They consist of an electromagnet with two poles that are placed on the surface of the component.

**Types of Yokes:**
*   **AC Yoke:** Operates on alternating current. The alternating current produces a continuously changing magnetic field.
    *   **Advantages:** Excellent for detecting shallow surface cracks due to the "skin effect" which concentrates the magnetic field on the surface. Portable and versatile.
    *   **Limitations:** Penetration into the material is limited, so it's less effective for near-surface defects.
*   **DC Yoke (or Permanent Magnet Yoke):** Operates on direct current or uses permanent magnets. The direct current produces a steady, strong magnetic field.
    *   **Advantages:** Provides deeper magnetic field penetration, making it more effective for detecting slightly deeper, near-surface defects.
    *   **Limitations:** Less sensitive to very fine surface cracks compared to AC yokes. Permanent magnet yokes are limited by the strength of the magnet.

**How it Works:**
*   The poles of the yoke are placed on the surface of the ferromagnetic material.
*   When energized (either AC or DC), a magnetic field is created between the poles, permeating the material in that localized area.
*   The orientation of the magnetic field depends on the placement of the yoke. For longitudinal defects, the yoke is placed transversely to the suspected flaw, and vice versa.

**Applications:**
*   Ideal for inspecting large, complex shapes, welds, and localized areas where other methods are impractical.
*   Used for both general surface inspection and inspecting specific zones.

**Important Point to Remember:** Yokes are versatile for portable inspections. AC yokes are better for surface cracks, while DC yokes are better for near-surface defects due to their better penetration. The distance between the yoke poles and the surface is critical for effective magnetization.

**References:**
*   *Practical Non-destructive testing* by Baldev Raj (Section on Yoke Testing)
*   *Non Destructive testing Techniques* by Ravi Prakash (Section on Yoke Magnetization)
*   *Recent Advances in Non -Destructive Inspection* by Carosena Meola (May discuss advancements in yoke technology)

### Induced Magnetization Methods (Brief Overview)

While the above are common techniques, other methods exist for specific applications.

*   **Permanent Magnets:** Can be used for localized magnetization, particularly for smaller components or specific areas. Less common in industrial MPT due to limited field strength control and portability issues.
*   **Flux-Sensitive Materials:** Certain materials exhibit changes in properties when exposed to magnetic fields, allowing for indirect magnetization or field strength monitoring.

---

### Key Considerations for Magnetization

1.  **Magnetic Field Strength:** The strength of the magnetic field must be sufficient to reveal the expected defects. Standards (e.g., ASTM E709, ISO 9934) provide guidelines for required field strengths or amperages.
2.  **Magnetization Direction:** As mentioned, the field must be oriented to cross potential discontinuities at approximately 90 degrees. This often requires multiple magnetizations in different directions.
3.  **Demagnetization:** After testing, components are typically demagnetized to prevent residual magnetism, which can attract dust and debris and interfere with subsequent operations.
4.  **Type of Current:** AC is generally more sensitive to surface defects, while DC (or half-wave DC) is better for near-surface defects due to deeper field penetration.
5.  **Particle Type:** The type of magnetic particle (dry or wet, fluorescent or non-fluorescent) should be chosen based on the inspection conditions and defect sensitivity required.

### Practice Questions

**Question 1:**
Which magnetization technique is most suitable for detecting longitudinal cracks in a solid cylindrical shaft?
a) Coil Shot Technique
b) Head Shot Technique
c) Central Conductor Testing
d) AC Yoke Testing

**Answer:**
b) Head Shot Technique. The head shot technique creates a circular magnetic field, which is perpendicular to longitudinal cracks, making them detectable.

**Question 2:**
What is the primary advantage of using an AC yoke over a DC yoke for detecting surface cracks?
a) Deeper magnetic field penetration
b) Higher magnetic field strength
c) Increased sensitivity to shallow surface defects
d) Better for detecting subsurface defects

**Answer:**
c) Increased sensitivity to shallow surface defects. The skin effect of AC concentrates the magnetic field on the surface, making it ideal for shallow cracks.

**Question 3:**
For testing a hollow pipe, which magnetization method would be most efficient for detecting discontinuities along the pipe's length?
a) Coil Shot Technique
b) Head Shot Technique
c) Central Conductor Testing
d) DC Yoke Testing

**Answer:**
c) Central Conductor Testing. This method is specifically designed for hollow components to create a magnetic field that penetrates the pipe walls, effectively detecting longitudinal flaws.

**Question 4:**
Explain why a component might need to be magnetized in two different directions during an MPT inspection.
**Answer:**
A component might need to be magnetized in two different directions to ensure that discontinuities oriented in different directions are detected. For example, if a part has both longitudinal and transverse flaws, magnetizing it longitudinally will reveal transverse flaws, and magnetizing it transversely will reveal longitudinal flaws. This is because the magnetic field must be perpendicular to the discontinuity to create a leakage field.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Ferromagnetic Materials Only:** MPT can only be applied to materials that can be magnetized.
*   **Perpendicularity is Key:** The magnetic field lines must intersect potential defects at approximately 90 degrees.
*   **AC vs. DC:** AC is best for surface defects, DC for near-surface defects.
*   **Portability:** Yokes are excellent for portable inspections of complex shapes.
*   **Demagnetization:** A necessary post-inspection step.
*   **Standards Compliance:** Always follow relevant industry standards for field strength and inspection procedures.
