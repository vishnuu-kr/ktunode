## Quality Engineering and Management: Module 4 - Strategic Quality Management

### Topic: Total Productive Maintenance (TPM)

**Module 4 Focus:** Integrating quality into strategic management. This module explores how quality principles are embedded within the overarching business strategy to achieve sustainable competitive advantage. Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) is a crucial element in this integration, focusing on maximizing the effectiveness of equipment and thereby impacting product quality, cost, and delivery.

**Course Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **CO1:** Develop knowledge of quality management and contributions of quality gurus. (TPM's philosophy aligns with the proactive, waste-reducing principles championed by many quality gurus.)
*   **CO2:** Identify various human dimensions of TQM. (TPM heavily emphasizes employee involvement and empowerment, a key human dimension.)
*   **CO3:** Implement different tools and techniques in TQM. (TPM utilizes a structured set of tools and techniques for equipment improvement.)

### 1. Introduction to Total Productive Maintenance (TPM)

**Key Concept:** TPM is a proactive and preventative approach to equipment management that aims to improve overall equipment effectiveness (OEE) through the active involvement of all employees, from top management to shop-floor operators. It seeks to eliminate the "six big losses" that hinder equipment performance.

**Definition:**

*   **TPM (Total Productive Maintenance):** A philosophy that emphasizes the holistic management of equipment, integrating maintenance with production and involving all employees to achieve zero breakdowns, zero defects, and zero accidents.

**Origin and Evolution:**

*   TPM originated in Japan in the 1960s, building upon the principles of **Preventive Maintenance**.
*   It was first introduced by **Seiichi Nakajima**, who developed the basic pillars of TPM.
*   The core idea is to shift the responsibility for basic equipment care from the maintenance department to the operators themselves, fostering a sense of ownership and proactive problem-solving.

**Strategic Importance of TPM:**

*   **Competitive Advantage:** Reliable equipment directly impacts product quality, production efficiency, lead times, and ultimately, customer satisfaction. TPM contributes to all these aspects.
*   **Cost Reduction:** By minimizing breakdowns, reducing rework due to equipment failure, and optimizing maintenance resources, TPM significantly lowers operational costs.
*   **Increased Uptime & Throughput:** Maximizing equipment availability and performance directly translates to higher production output.
*   **Improved Product Quality:** Consistent equipment performance leads to fewer defects and variations in product quality.
*   **Enhanced Safety:** A well-maintained and proactively managed equipment environment reduces the risk of accidents.
*   **Employee Morale & Engagement:** Empowering operators with maintenance responsibilities boosts job satisfaction and team spirit.

### 2. The Goal of TPM: Zero Losses

TPM aims to achieve a state of "Zero Losses" which are categorized into the "Six Big Losses":

*   **Loss 1: Breakdowns (Equipment Failure):** Unplanned stoppages due to equipment malfunction.
    *   **Impact:** Production halts, missed deadlines, potential quality issues.
    *   **TPM Approach:** Focus on preventive maintenance, predictive maintenance, and operator basic care to prevent failures.
*   **Loss 2: Setup and Adjustment Losses (Changeover Time):** Time lost during product changeovers or adjustments to equipment.
    *   **Impact:** Reduced production time, increased lead times, potential for errors during setup.
    *   **TPM Approach:** Implementing **SMED (Single-Minute Exchange of Die)** techniques to drastically reduce changeover times.
*   **Loss 3: Idling and Minor Stoppages:** Short, frequent interruptions that disrupt the continuous flow of production.
    *   **Impact:** Reduced output, operator frustration, difficulty in identifying root causes.
    *   **TPM Approach:** Analyzing causes of minor stoppages, improving operator responsiveness, and ensuring smooth material flow.
*   **Loss 4: Speed Losses (Reduced Speed):** Equipment running at a slower speed than its designed optimal speed.
    *   **Impact:** Lower production output, inefficient resource utilization.
    *   **TPM Approach:** Identifying and rectifying factors causing speed reduction, such as wear and tear, poor lubrication, or incorrect settings.
*   **Loss 5: Quality Defects and Rework:** Production of non-conforming products that require scrap or rework.
    *   **Impact:** Increased costs, wasted materials and labor, customer dissatisfaction.
    *   **TPM Approach:** Identifying and eliminating the root causes of quality defects originating from equipment issues.
*   **Loss 6: Startup and Yield Losses:** Initial production runs after startup or maintenance that produce a higher proportion of defective products.
    *   **Impact:** Waste of materials and resources, delayed delivery.
    *   **TPM Approach:** Standardizing startup procedures, ensuring proper machine warm-up, and using statistical process control (SPC) during startup.

**Textbook Reference:**

*   **Besterfield et al. (2018):** Likely discusses the Six Big Losses as a core component of understanding equipment effectiveness and the challenges TPM addresses. Chapters on TQM tools and techniques may detail methods to combat these losses.
*   **Subburaj Ramasamy (2017):** May elaborate on the systematic approach to identifying and quantifying these losses within a TQM framework.

### 3. The Pillars of TPM

TPM is typically structured around a set of eight supporting pillars, each representing a key area of focus:

**I. Autonomous Maintenance (Jishu Hozen):**

*   **Concept:** Empowering operators to take responsibility for the basic maintenance of their own equipment.
*   **Activities:** Cleaning, inspection, lubrication, tightening of bolts, minor adjustments, and initial defect detection.
*   **Benefits:** Increased operator ownership, early detection of problems, reduced burden on the maintenance department, improved equipment cleanliness.
*   **Stages of Autonomous Maintenance:**
    1.  **Initial Cleaning & Inspection:** Thoroughly clean and inspect the equipment to identify initial problems.
    2.  **Elimination of Sources of Dirt & Contamination:** Implement measures to prevent dirt and contamination.
    3.  **Establishment of Cleaning & Lubrication Standards:** Develop standardized procedures for cleaning and lubrication.
    4.  **General Inspection Standards:** Create general inspection checklists for routine checks.
    5.  **Equipment Inspection & Diagnosis:** Operators learn to identify potential issues through various inspection techniques.
    6.  **Workplace Organization & Standardization:** Maintain order and cleanliness in the work area.
    7.  **Full Implementation of Autonomous Maintenance:** Continuous improvement and self-management.

**II. Planned Maintenance (Keikaku Hozen):**

*   **Concept:** Proactive maintenance activities scheduled based on time, usage, or condition to prevent failures.
*   **Activities:** Preventive maintenance (scheduled inspections, servicing, parts replacement), predictive maintenance (monitoring equipment condition through vibration analysis, thermography, oil analysis).
*   **Benefits:** Reduced breakdowns, extended equipment life, optimized maintenance schedules.

**III. Quality Maintenance (Hinshitsu Hozen):**

*   **Concept:** Ensuring that equipment operates in a way that prevents quality defects.
*   **Activities:** Identifying and eliminating the root causes of quality defects related to equipment, establishing equipment standards for quality, implementing in-process quality checks.
*   **Benefits:** Reduced scrap and rework, improved product consistency.

**IV. Focused Improvement (Kobetsu Kaizen):**

*   **Concept:** Organizing cross-functional teams to systematically address specific equipment-related problems and achieve significant improvements.
*   **Activities:** Root cause analysis, problem-solving techniques, implementation of counter-measures.
*   **Benefits:** Targeted problem resolution, continuous improvement culture.

**V. Early Equipment Management (Seibi Koyu):**

*   **Concept:** Designing and procuring new equipment that is reliable, maintainable, and user-friendly from the outset.
*   **Activities:** Incorporating maintenance and operational considerations into the design and purchase of new equipment, using life cycle cost analysis.
*   **Benefits:** Reduced long-term maintenance costs, improved operational efficiency.

**VI. Training and Education:**

*   **Concept:** Providing employees with the necessary skills and knowledge to perform their TPM roles effectively.
*   **Activities:** Operator training on basic maintenance, maintenance personnel training on advanced techniques, cross-training.
*   **Benefits:** Enhanced employee competency, improved problem-solving capabilities.

**VII. Safety, Health, and Environment (SHE):**

*   **Concept:** Integrating safety, health, and environmental considerations into all TPM activities.
*   **Activities:** Hazard identification and risk assessment related to equipment, ensuring compliance with environmental regulations, promoting a safe working environment.
*   **Benefits:** Accident prevention, regulatory compliance, positive environmental impact.

**VIII. TPM in Supporting Departments:**

*   **Concept:** Extending TPM principles to administrative and supporting departments (e.g., purchasing, HR, engineering) to support the overall maintenance and operational excellence.
*   **Activities:** Streamlining processes, reducing waste in supporting functions, fostering a quality-oriented mindset throughout the organization.
*   **Benefits:** Improved overall organizational efficiency and effectiveness.

**Textbook Reference:**

*   **Besterfield et al. (2018) & Subburaj Ramasamy (2017):** These textbooks are likely to dedicate significant sections to explaining these pillars in detail, providing operational guidelines and case studies.
*   **Juran & Gryna (2004):** May offer insights into the maintenance aspects of quality management and how TPM contributes to a more robust quality system.

### 4. Implementing TPM: The Step-by-Step Approach

TPM implementation is a journey that requires a systematic and phased approach:

1.  **Announcement and Education:** Top management announces the TPM initiative, and all employees are educated on its principles, objectives, and benefits.
2.  **TPM Promotion Campaign:** Launch a campaign to build awareness and enthusiasm for TPM.
3.  **Establishment of a TPM Committee:** Form a cross-functional committee to oversee TPM implementation.
4.  **Identify Target Equipment and Set Goals:** Select specific equipment for initial TPM implementation and set clear, measurable goals (e.g., reduce breakdowns by X%, improve OEE by Y%).
5.  **Develop a Plan for Improving Equipment:** Create a detailed plan for each of the eight pillars, tailored to the specific equipment and organizational context.
6.  **Implementation Phase:** Execute the plans for each pillar, focusing on autonomous maintenance, planned maintenance, and focused improvement initially.
7.  **Evaluation and Expansion:** Regularly review progress, measure results against goals, and expand TPM implementation to other equipment and departments.

**Key to Success:**

*   **Top Management Commitment:** Unwavering support and involvement from leadership are paramount.
*   **Employee Involvement and Empowerment:** TPM thrives on the active participation of all employees.
*   **Cross-functional Collaboration:** Effective communication and teamwork between production, maintenance, and other departments are essential.
*   **Continuous Improvement Culture:** TPM is not a one-time project but an ongoing process of learning and improvement.
*   **Training and Skill Development:** Investing in employee training is critical for successful implementation.
*   **Data Collection and Analysis:** Tracking key performance indicators (KPIs) like OEE and breakdown frequency is vital for monitoring progress and identifying areas for improvement.

### 5. Measuring TPM Success: Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE)

**Key Concept:** OEE is a vital metric used to measure the performance of equipment and is a cornerstone for evaluating TPM effectiveness. It quantifies how well manufacturing equipment is utilized.

**OEE Formula:**

**OEE = Availability x Performance x Quality**

*   **Availability:** The percentage of planned production time that the equipment is actually running.
    *   *Formula:* `Availability = Actual Run Time / Planned Production Time`
    *   *Excludes:* Planned downtime (scheduled maintenance, breaks, shift changes) and unplanned downtime (breakdowns, setups).
*   **Performance:** The speed at which the equipment is running compared to its ideal or design speed.
    *   *Formula:* `Performance = (Total Pieces Produced x Ideal Cycle Time) / Actual Run Time`
    *   *Considers:* Minor stoppages, speed losses.
*   **Quality:** The percentage of good parts produced out of the total parts produced.
    *   *Formula:* `Quality = Good Parts Produced / Total Parts Produced`
    *   *Considers:* Defects, rework, scrap.

**Interpreting OEE:**

*   **World Class OEE:** Generally considered to be 85% or higher.
*   **Good OEE:** 60-70% is often considered good for many industries.
*   **Low OEE:** Below 50% indicates significant room for improvement.

**Textbook Reference:**

*   **Mitra (1998):** Likely provides a detailed explanation of OEE as a performance metric and its calculation, possibly linking it to statistical quality control concepts.
*   **Montgomery (Third Edition):** While primarily focused on statistical quality control, may touch upon performance metrics like OEE in the context of process capability and monitoring.

**Example Calculation:**

Consider a machine with a planned production time of 8 hours (480 minutes) per shift.

*   **Planned Production Time:** 480 minutes
*   **Breakdowns:** The machine stops for 30 minutes due to a breakdown.
*   **Setup Time:** A setup takes 15 minutes.
*   **Actual Run Time:** 480 minutes - 30 minutes (breakdown) - 15 minutes (setup) = 435 minutes.
*   **Total Pieces Produced:** 1000 pieces.
*   **Ideal Cycle Time:** 0.5 minutes per piece.
*   **Defective Parts:** 20 pieces.
*   **Good Parts Produced:** 1000 - 20 = 980 pieces.

**Calculations:**

1.  **Availability:**
    *   `Availability = 435 minutes / 480 minutes = 0.90625` (or 90.625%)

2.  **Performance:**
    *   `Ideal Run Time = 1000 pieces * 0.5 minutes/piece = 500 minutes`
    *   `Performance = 500 minutes / 435 minutes = 1.1494` (This is greater than 1, indicating the machine ran faster than ideal in some periods. A more realistic calculation would consider actual production capacity.)
    *   *Let's assume the machine's ideal rate is 120 pieces per hour.*
    *   *In 435 minutes (7.25 hours), the machine should produce:* `7.25 hours * 120 pieces/hour = 870 pieces`
    *   *Actual pieces produced: 1000 pieces*
    *   *Revised Performance Calculation:* `Performance = Actual Production / Potential Production = 1000 pieces / 870 pieces = 1.1494` (Still greater than 1, suggesting the machine exceeded its ideal output rate. A more robust OEE calculation would use the machine's rated capacity.)
    *   *Let's use a different example where actual production is less than ideal:*
        *   *Ideal output for 435 minutes (7.25 hours) @ 100 pieces/hour = 725 pieces*
        *   *Actual pieces produced = 650 pieces*
        *   `Performance = 650 pieces / 725 pieces = 0.8965` (or 89.65%)

3.  **Quality:**
    *   `Quality = 980 good parts / 1000 total parts = 0.98` (or 98%)

4.  **OEE:**
    *   `OEE = 0.90625 (Availability) * 0.8965 (Performance) * 0.98 (Quality) = 0.796` (or 79.6%)

This OEE of 79.6% indicates that the machine is operating effectively, but there is still room for improvement in availability, performance, and quality.

### 6. TPM and Strategic Quality Management

**Connecting TPM to Strategic Goals:**

*   **Customer Focus:** By ensuring reliable equipment and high-quality output, TPM directly contributes to meeting customer expectations for timely delivery and defect-free products.
*   **Continuous Improvement (Kaizen):** The "Focused Improvement" pillar and the overall TPM philosophy embody the principle of continuous improvement, a cornerstone of strategic quality management.
*   **Employee Involvement:** TPM aligns with the strategic imperative of empowering employees and fostering a culture of quality ownership, as advocated by quality gurus like Deming and Crosby.
*   **Process Management:** TPM focuses on optimizing equipment processes, which are integral to the overall strategic management of an organization's operations.
*   **Cost Management:** By reducing waste, downtime, and rework, TPM contributes to achieving strategic cost management objectives.

**TPM as a Tool for Strategic Advantage:**

*   **Reduced Operating Costs:** Lower maintenance expenses, less scrap, and reduced energy consumption.
*   **Improved Market Responsiveness:** Faster changeovers and higher throughput enable quicker adaptation to market demands.
*   **Enhanced Reputation:** Consistent quality and reliable delivery build a strong brand image.
*   **Sustainable Operations:** Efficient equipment utilization and a focus on safety and environment contribute to long-term sustainability.

**Textbook Reference:**

*   **Besterfield et al. (2018) & Subburaj Ramasamy (2017):** These texts will likely situate TPM within the broader context of TQM and strategic quality management, explaining how it supports organizational objectives.
*   **Crosby (1979):** Crosby's emphasis on "Quality is Free" aligns with the cost-saving benefits of TPM. His focus on prevention and quality culture resonates with TPM's proactive approach.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **TPM is a holistic approach:** It involves people, processes, and equipment.
*   **Empowerment is key:** Operators are central to TPM success.
*   **Zero Losses is the ultimate goal:** Breakdowns, defects, and accidents must be eliminated.
*   **OEE is the primary metric:** Track and improve OEE to measure progress.
*   **TPM requires sustained effort:** It's a cultural shift, not a quick fix.
*   **TPM is a strategic tool:** It directly contributes to competitive advantage.
*   **The eight pillars provide a structured framework:** Follow the pillars for systematic implementation.

### 8. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1 (CO5):** Explain how Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) aligns with the principles of strategic quality management. Provide at least two examples.

**Answer:** TPM aligns with strategic quality management by:
1.  **Enhancing Customer Satisfaction:** By ensuring reliable equipment and minimizing defects, TPM directly contributes to delivering high-quality products on time, meeting customer expectations.
2.  **Driving Cost Reduction:** TPM's focus on eliminating waste (breakdowns, rework, downtime) leads to lower operating costs, a key strategic objective for any organization.
    *   **Example:** A manufacturing company implementing TPM reduces unplanned machine downtime by 50%, leading to a 15% increase in production output and significant cost savings in overtime labor.
    *   **Example:** Through Autonomous Maintenance, operators identify and fix minor lubrication issues, preventing a major bearing failure that would have cost $10,000 in repairs and lost production.

**Question 2 (CO3):** Describe the concept of Autonomous Maintenance and list three specific activities an operator might perform as part of this pillar.

**Answer:** Autonomous Maintenance (Jishu Hozen) is the pillar of TPM that empowers operators to take responsibility for the basic maintenance of their own equipment. This fosters ownership and proactive problem-solving. Three specific activities an operator might perform are:
1.  **Cleaning:** Thoroughly cleaning the equipment to remove dirt, dust, and debris, which can prevent contamination and allow for better inspection.
2.  **Lubrication:** Ensuring all lubrication points are properly lubricated according to schedule and using the correct type of lubricant.
3.  **Inspection:** Visually inspecting the equipment for signs of wear, leaks, loose parts, or any abnormalities, and reporting them promptly.

**Question 3 (CO3):** Calculate the Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) for a machine with the following data:
*   Planned Production Time: 8 hours (480 minutes)
*   Downtime due to minor stoppages: 10 minutes
*   Downtime due to planned maintenance: 20 minutes
*   Downtime due to breakdown: 25 minutes
*   Total Pieces Produced: 500
*   Ideal Cycle Time per Piece: 1 minute
*   Defective Pieces: 15

**Answer:**

1.  **Actual Run Time:**
    *   Planned Production Time = 480 minutes
    *   Total Downtime = Minor Stoppages + Planned Maintenance + Breakdown = 10 + 20 + 25 = 55 minutes
    *   Actual Run Time = 480 minutes - 55 minutes = 425 minutes

2.  **Availability:**
    *   Planned Production Time (excluding planned maintenance) = 480 - 20 = 460 minutes
    *   Availability = Actual Run Time / (Planned Production Time - Planned Maintenance)
    *   Availability = 425 minutes / 460 minutes = 0.9239 (92.39%)

3.  **Performance:**
    *   Ideal Run Time = Total Pieces Produced * Ideal Cycle Time = 500 pieces * 1 minute/piece = 500 minutes
    *   Performance = Ideal Run Time / Actual Run Time
    *   Performance = 500 minutes / 425 minutes = 1.1765 (117.65%) - *Note: This indicates the machine is running faster than its ideal speed during the run time.*

4.  **Quality:**
    *   Good Parts Produced = Total Pieces Produced - Defective Pieces = 500 - 15 = 485 pieces
    *   Quality = Good Parts Produced / Total Pieces Produced
    *   Quality = 485 pieces / 500 pieces = 0.97 (97%)

5.  **OEE:**
    *   OEE = Availability * Performance * Quality
    *   OEE = 0.9239 * 1.1765 * 0.97 = 1.051 (or 105.1%)

    *Self-Correction/Note:* An OEE greater than 100% is unusual and suggests that the "Ideal Cycle Time" might be too conservative or that the machine is consistently outperforming its rated capacity. In practice, it's important to review the basis for these figures. For the purpose of this exercise, the calculation is as performed. A more practical scenario would be if the machine's maximum output in 425 minutes was, say, 450 pieces. Then Performance would be 450/425 = 1.0588, leading to an OEE of 0.9239 * 1.0588 * 0.97 = 0.949 or 94.9%.

**Question 4 (CO1):** How does TPM contribute to the broader goals of Total Quality Management (TQM)?

**Answer:** TPM contributes to TQM by:
*   **Improving Process Reliability:** Ensuring equipment consistently performs as intended, which is fundamental to achieving stable and predictable processes, a TQM tenet.
*   **Reducing Waste:** TPM targets the "six big losses," which are all forms of waste, a key focus of TQM.
*   **Empowering Employees:** Fostering a culture of participation and ownership among operators aligns with the human-centric approach of TQM.
*   **Enhancing Product Quality:** By proactively addressing equipment-related causes of defects, TPM directly supports the TQM goal of producing high-quality products.
