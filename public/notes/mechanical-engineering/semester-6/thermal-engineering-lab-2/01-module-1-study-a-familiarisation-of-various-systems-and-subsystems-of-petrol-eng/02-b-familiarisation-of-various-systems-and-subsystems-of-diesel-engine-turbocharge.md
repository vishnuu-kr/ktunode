# Thermal Engineering Lab-2: Module 1 - Study of Diesel & Turbocharged Engines

## Topic: b) Familiarisation of various systems and subsystems of Diesel engine / Turbocharged engine

This module focuses on understanding the fundamental components and operational principles of Diesel engines and their turbocharged variants. This knowledge is crucial for identifying engine parts (CO1) and will lay the groundwork for analyzing their performance (CO2) and emissions (CO3) in subsequent lab sessions.

### Learning Outcomes Covered:

*   **CO1: Identify various systems and subsystems of Diesel and petrol engines (Knowledge Level: K1)** - This section directly addresses this outcome by detailing the components of a Diesel engine.

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Diesel Engine:** An internal combustion engine that ignites fuel by the heat of compression rather than by a spark. The air is compressed to a much higher temperature, and then diesel fuel is injected directly into the cylinder, where it ignites spontaneously.
    *   *Reference:* V. Ganesan, "Fundamentals of IC Engines," Chapter on Diesel Engines.
*   **Turbocharged Engine:** An engine that uses a turbocharger, a type of turbine-driven forced induction device, to increase power output and efficiency. It works by using the engine's exhaust gases to spin a turbine, which in turn drives a compressor that forces more air into the engine cylinders.
    *   *Reference:* J.B. Heywood, "I.C Engine Fundamentals," Chapter on Forced Induction.
*   **Compression Ratio:** The ratio of the volume of the cylinder when the piston is at the bottom dead center (BDC) to the volume when the piston is at the top dead center (TDC). Diesel engines typically have higher compression ratios than petrol engines.
*   **Fuel Injection:** The process of delivering fuel into the combustion chamber or intake manifold. In Diesel engines, this is typically direct injection under very high pressure.
*   **Combustion:** The rapid chemical reaction between a substance and an oxidant, usually oxygen, that produces heat and light. In Diesel engines, it's characterized by diffusion combustion.
    *   *Reference:* Stephen R. Turns, "An Introduction to Combustion: Concepts and Applications," Chapter on Combustion in Internal Combustion Engines.

### Core Systems and Subsystems of a Diesel Engine:

Here's a breakdown of the essential systems that make up a typical Diesel engine.

#### 1. Fuel System:

The fuel system is responsible for storing, filtering, and delivering diesel fuel to the combustion chamber at the correct pressure and time.

*   **Fuel Tank:** Stores the diesel fuel.
*   **Fuel Filter(s):** Removes impurities from the fuel to prevent damage to the injection system. This is crucial for Diesel engines due to the precision of the fuel injection equipment.
    *   *Example:* Multi-stage filtration, including primary and secondary filters.
*   **Fuel Pump (Low Pressure):** Draws fuel from the tank and supplies it to the high-pressure pump.
    *   *Types:* Gear pump, diaphragm pump.
*   **Fuel Injection Pump (High Pressure):** This is the heart of the Diesel fuel system. It delivers fuel to the injectors at very high pressures (often exceeding 1000 bar).
    *   *Types:*
        *   **Inline Injection Pump:** Each cylinder has its own pumping element.
        *   **Distributor Injection Pump:** A single pumping element supplies fuel to all cylinders via a distributor.
    *   *Reference:* V. Ganesan, "Fundamentals of IC Engines," Chapter on Fuel Injection Systems.
*   **Fuel Injectors:** Atomize and spray the high-pressure fuel into the combustion chamber at the precise moment.
    *   *Types:*
        *   **Holes-type Injector:** Fuel sprays through multiple small holes.
        *   **Pintle-type Injector:** A movable pintle controls the fuel flow and spray pattern.
    *   *Importance:* Proper atomization is vital for efficient combustion.
*   **High-Pressure Fuel Lines:** Carry the high-pressure fuel from the injection pump to the injectors.

#### 2. Air Intake and Turbocharging System:

This system provides the necessary air for combustion. Turbocharging significantly enhances this process.

*   **Air Filter:** Cleans incoming air before it enters the engine.
*   **Intake Manifold:** Distributes air to each cylinder.
*   **Turbocharger (for Turbocharged Diesel Engines):**
    *   **Turbine:** Driven by the engine's exhaust gases.
    *   **Compressor:** Driven by the turbine via a shaft, compresses intake air.
    *   **Intercooler (Optional but Common):** Cools the compressed air from the turbocharger, increasing its density and improving volumetric efficiency.
        *   *Benefit:* Denser air means more oxygen available for combustion, leading to higher power output and improved efficiency.
    *   *Reference:* J.B. Heywood, "I.C Engine Fundamentals," Chapter on Turbochargers and Superchargers.
*   **Wastegate (on some Turbochargers):** Controls the boost pressure by bypassing some exhaust gas from the turbine.

#### 3. Engine Mechanical Components:

These are the core moving parts of the engine.

*   **Cylinder Block:** Houses the cylinders, crankshaft, and camshaft.
*   **Cylinders:** Where combustion takes place.
*   **Pistons:** Move up and down within the cylinders, driven by combustion pressure and connected to the crankshaft.
*   **Piston Rings:** Seal the gap between the piston and cylinder wall to prevent combustion gas leakage and control oil.
*   **Connecting Rods:** Connect the pistons to the crankshaft.
*   **Crankshaft:** Converts the linear motion of the pistons into rotational motion.
*   **Cylinder Head:** Seals the top of the cylinders and houses the valves and injector.
*   **Valves (Intake & Exhaust):** Control the flow of air into and exhaust gases out of the cylinders.
*   **Camshaft:** Operates the valves by means of lobes and followers.
*   **Timing Gears/Belt/Chain:** Ensures proper synchronization between the crankshaft and camshaft.

#### 4. Lubrication System:

Ensures all moving parts are adequately lubricated to reduce friction and wear.

*   **Oil Sump/Pan:** Stores the engine oil.
*   **Oil Pump:** Circulates oil throughout the engine.
*   **Oil Filter:** Removes impurities from the lubricating oil.
*   **Oil Passages:** Ducts within the engine block and head for oil circulation.
*   **Oil Cooler (Optional):** Maintains optimal oil temperature.

#### 5. Cooling System:

Dissipates excess heat generated during combustion to prevent engine overheating.

*   **Coolant:** Typically a mixture of water and antifreeze.
*   **Water Pump:** Circulates coolant through the engine.
*   **Radiator:** Dissipates heat from the coolant to the atmosphere.
*   **Thermostat:** Regulates coolant flow to the radiator to maintain optimal operating temperature.
*   **Cooling Fan:** Draws air through the radiator.
*   **Coolant Passages:** Ducts within the engine block and head for coolant circulation.

#### 6. Ignition System (Not applicable to Diesel engines):

It's important to note that Diesel engines do *not* have a spark ignition system. Combustion is initiated by compression heat. This is a key difference from petrol engines.

#### 7. Exhaust System:

Removes combustion products from the engine.

*   **Exhaust Manifold:** Collects exhaust gases from each cylinder.
*   **Exhaust Pipe:** Carries exhaust gases away.
*   **Muffler/Silencer:** Reduces exhaust noise.
*   **Catalytic Converter (often present for emission control):** Converts harmful pollutants into less harmful substances.

### Systems and Subsystems of a Turbocharged Engine (Specifics):

Turbocharging is an addition to the standard engine systems, primarily enhancing the air intake.

*   **Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) System (Often integrated with Turbocharging):** Some exhaust gas is recirculated back into the intake manifold to reduce NOx emissions by lowering combustion temperatures. This system needs careful control to avoid performance degradation.
    *   *Reference:* J.B. Heywood, "I.C Engine Fundamentals," Chapter on Emission Control.
*   **Boost Pressure Control:** Essential for managing the turbocharger's output.
    *   **Boost Pressure Sensor:** Monitors the pressure of the air from the turbocharger.
    *   **Engine Control Unit (ECU):** Uses sensor data to control actuators that manage boost pressure (e.g., wastegate).

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **High Compression Ratio:** Diesel engines operate at significantly higher compression ratios than petrol engines, which is fundamental to their auto-ignition process.
*   **Compression Ignition:** Diesel fuel ignites due to the heat of compressed air, not a spark plug.
*   **Direct Fuel Injection:** Fuel is injected directly into the combustion chamber at high pressure, leading to precise fuel-air mixing and efficient combustion.
*   **Turbocharging Benefits:** Increases power, torque, and fuel efficiency by forcing more air into the cylinders.
*   **Precision of Fuel System:** The fuel injection system in Diesel engines is highly precise and sensitive to contamination.
*   **No Spark Plugs:** A critical distinction from petrol engines.
*   **Different Combustion Process:** Diesel combustion is a diffusion flame, whereas petrol engines typically exhibit premixed flame combustion.
    *   *Reference:* Stephen R. Turns, "An Introduction to Combustion: Concepts and Applications," Chapters on Diffusion Flames and Premixed Flames.

### Practice Questions:

1.  **Identify the key difference in the ignition process between a Diesel engine and a petrol engine.**
    *   **Answer:** Diesel engines use compression ignition, while petrol engines use spark ignition.
2.  **What is the primary function of a turbocharger in a Diesel engine?**
    *   **Answer:** To force more air into the cylinders, increasing power output and efficiency.
3.  **List at least three essential components of a Diesel engine's fuel system.**
    *   **Answer:** Fuel tank, fuel filters, fuel injection pump, fuel injectors.
4.  **Why is the lubrication system particularly important for engines with turbochargers?**
    *   **Answer:** Turbochargers operate at very high speeds and temperatures, requiring excellent lubrication for their bearings to prevent premature wear and failure.
5.  **What is the role of the intercooler in a turbocharged engine?**
    *   **Answer:** To cool the compressed intake air, increasing its density and allowing more oxygen into the cylinders.
