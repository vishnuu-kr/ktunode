# Introduction to Business Analytics: Module 4 - Prescriptive Analytics: Introduction

## Topic: Business Performance Management: Business Performance Management Cycle

### **1. Understanding Business Performance Management (BPM)**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Business Performance Management (BPM):** A continuous, structured process that helps organizations manage and improve their performance by aligning strategic goals with operational activities. It involves setting objectives, measuring progress, analyzing results, and taking corrective actions.
*   **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):** Quantifiable measures used to track and evaluate the success of an organization, employee, or specific activity in meeting objectives.
*   **Balanced Scorecard:** A strategic performance management tool that provides a comprehensive view of organizational performance by looking beyond traditional financial metrics to include customer, internal processes, and learning & growth perspectives. (Referenced in Sharda, Delen & Turban, 2018).
*   **Objectives:** Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) goals that an organization aims to accomplish.
*   **Strategy:** A high-level plan to achieve one or more long-term goals under conditions of uncertainty.

**Definition:**

BPM is a systematic approach that involves defining strategic objectives, establishing performance measures, monitoring performance against these measures, and taking actions to improve performance. It's a closed-loop system designed to ensure that organizational efforts are focused on achieving strategic goals.

**From Textbooks:**

*   **Sharda, Delen & Turban (2018)** discuss BPM as a framework for managing organizational performance. They often link it to business intelligence and the use of dashboards and reports to monitor progress.

### **2. The Business Performance Management Cycle**

BPM is not a one-time activity but a recurring cycle that drives continuous improvement. While specific models might have slight variations, the core stages are generally consistent.

**Key Stages of the BPM Cycle:**

1.  **Define Strategy and Objectives:**
    *   **Description:** This is the foundational stage where the organization's vision, mission, and strategic goals are clearly articulated. Objectives are then broken down into specific, measurable targets.
    *   **Key Activities:**
        *   Developing a clear vision and mission statement.
        *   Identifying key strategic priorities.
        *   Setting SMART objectives at different levels of the organization (corporate, departmental, individual).
        *   Defining the Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) that will be used to measure progress towards these objectives.
    *   **Example:** A retail company's objective might be to "Increase online sales by 15% in the next fiscal year." The associated KPI could be "Monthly Online Sales Revenue."

2.  **Measure Performance:**
    *   **Description:** This stage involves collecting data related to the defined KPIs. The focus is on ensuring data accuracy, completeness, and relevance.
    *   **Key Activities:**
        *   Identifying data sources.
        *   Implementing data collection mechanisms (e.g., surveys, transaction logs, operational systems).
        *   Ensuring data quality through validation and cleaning processes.
        *   Establishing reporting frequencies and formats.
    *   **Example:** The retail company would collect data on its monthly online sales revenue from its e-commerce platform.

3.  **Analyze Performance:**
    *   **Description:** In this stage, the collected data is analyzed to understand performance trends, identify variances from targets, and diagnose the root causes of performance issues or successes. This is where business analytics plays a critical role.
    *   **Key Activities:**
        *   Comparing actual performance against targets.
        *   Identifying deviations and their significance.
        *   Using various analytical techniques (e.g., descriptive analytics, diagnostic analytics) to uncover patterns and insights.
        *   Root cause analysis to understand *why* performance is deviating.
    *   **Example:** Analyzing the online sales data might reveal that sales are lower than expected on weekdays but peak during weekends, suggesting a need to improve weekday marketing or customer engagement.

4.  **Monitor and Report Performance:**
    *   **Description:** The analyzed performance data is then communicated to relevant stakeholders through reports, dashboards, and presentations. This stage ensures transparency and provides actionable intelligence.
    *   **Key Activities:**
        *   Creating performance dashboards and reports.
        *   Visualizing data effectively (using charts and graphs, aligning with CO4).
        *   Communicating findings to management and relevant teams.
        *   Providing context and interpretation of the data.
    *   **Example:** A dashboard is created showing monthly online sales revenue, comparing it to the target, and highlighting the performance trend.

5.  **Take Action and Adjust Strategy:**
    *   **Description:** Based on the analysis and insights gained, the organization takes corrective actions or adjusts its strategy and objectives. This stage closes the loop and drives improvement. This is where prescriptive analytics begins to inform decisions.
    *   **Key Activities:**
        *   Developing action plans to address performance gaps.
        *   Implementing new initiatives or modifying existing ones.
        *   Allocating resources to support the action plans.
        *   Revising objectives or strategies if they are no longer relevant or achievable.
    *   **Example:** The retail company might decide to launch targeted weekday promotions or improve its website's user experience to boost sales during slower periods. This decision is informed by the analysis of performance data.

### **3. The Role of Analytics in BPM**

Analytics is not just a part of BPM; it's the engine that drives its effectiveness.

*   **Descriptive Analytics:** Used to understand past performance (e.g., "What were our sales last quarter?"). This is crucial for the "Measure" and "Analyze" stages.
*   **Diagnostic Analytics:** Used to understand the *reasons* behind past performance (e.g., "Why did sales decline in that region?"). Essential for the "Analyze" stage.
*   **Predictive Analytics:** Used to forecast future outcomes (e.g., "What will our sales be next quarter if trends continue?"). Informs the "Take Action" stage by anticipating future scenarios.
*   **Prescriptive Analytics:** Used to recommend specific actions to achieve desired outcomes (e.g., "What marketing campaign will maximize sales growth?"). Directly supports the "Take Action" stage by guiding decision-making.

**Example:**

A company struggling with customer churn (problem):

1.  **Define Strategy:** Objective: Reduce customer churn by 5% in 6 months. KPI: Monthly Churn Rate.
2.  **Measure Performance:** Track monthly churn rate and customer demographics.
3.  **Analyze Performance:** Use descriptive analytics to see churn trends. Use diagnostic analytics to identify that customers who haven't used a specific feature in 30 days are more likely to churn.
4.  **Monitor & Report:** Report the churn rate and findings to the customer success team.
5.  **Take Action & Adjust Strategy:** Prescriptive analytics can suggest: "Offer a personalized tutorial for feature X to customers who haven't used it in 20 days." This action is taken to reduce churn. The effectiveness of this action is then fed back into the cycle.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   BPM is a **continuous, iterative cycle**.
*   **Alignment** between strategy, objectives, KPIs, and actions is paramount.
*   **Data quality** is critical for the reliability of the entire cycle.
*   **Analytics** transforms BPM from a reporting exercise into a strategic management tool.
*   The cycle provides a framework for **driving continuous improvement**.

### **4. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Which of the following is NOT a primary stage of the Business Performance Management (BPM) cycle?
a) Define Strategy and Objectives
b) Measure Performance
c) Monitor and Report Performance
d) Innovate and Disrupt

**Answer:** d) Innovate and Disrupt. While innovation can be an outcome of effective BPM, it's not a core stage *within* the cyclical process itself. The cycle is more focused on managing existing performance against defined goals.

**Question 2:**
A marketing manager is reviewing sales data from the past quarter to understand why a particular product's sales were lower than expected. Which type of analytics is primarily being used in this scenario?
a) Predictive Analytics
b) Prescriptive Analytics
c) Diagnostic Analytics
d) Descriptive Analytics

**Answer:** c) Diagnostic Analytics. The manager is trying to understand the *reason* behind the performance, which is the core function of diagnostic analytics. Descriptive analytics would simply present the sales figures, while predictive would forecast future sales, and prescriptive would recommend actions.

**Question 3:**
Imagine you are part of a project team aiming to improve customer satisfaction for an online streaming service. List the steps of the BPM cycle and provide a brief example for each step relevant to this scenario.

**Answer:**

1.  **Define Strategy and Objectives:**
    *   **Objective:** Increase average customer satisfaction score by 10% within the next quarter.
    *   **KPI:** Average Customer Satisfaction Score (measured via post-interaction surveys).
2.  **Measure Performance:**
    *   **Example:** Collect customer satisfaction scores from surveys conducted after a user contacts support or completes a key interaction (e.g., finishing a show).
3.  **Analyze Performance:**
    *   **Example:** Analyze survey data to find that customers are dissatisfied with response times when contacting customer support. Use sentiment analysis on open-ended feedback to identify common complaints about the support process.
4.  **Monitor and Report Performance:**
    *   **Example:** Create a dashboard showing the average customer satisfaction score over time, broken down by interaction type. Highlight that customer support interactions have the lowest scores.
5.  **Take Action and Adjust Strategy:**
    *   **Example:** Implement a new system to reduce customer support response times. Prescriptive analytics could suggest the optimal staffing levels for the support team or the most effective communication channels to offer. Monitor the impact of these changes on the satisfaction score.

**Question 4 (Fill in the Blanks):**
The BPM cycle aims to achieve _________ improvement by _________ strategic goals with operational activities.

**Answer:** Continuous, aligning.

**Question 5 (Conceptual):**
How does the BPM cycle ensure accountability within an organization?

**Answer:** The BPM cycle ensures accountability by clearly defining objectives and the KPIs used to measure them. This makes performance transparent. When performance deviates, the analysis stage helps pinpoint reasons, and the action stage assigns responsibility for implementing solutions. Regular monitoring and reporting keep all stakeholders informed and aware of progress (or lack thereof), fostering a culture of accountability.
