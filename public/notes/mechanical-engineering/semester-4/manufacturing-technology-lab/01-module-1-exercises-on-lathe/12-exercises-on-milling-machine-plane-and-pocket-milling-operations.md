# Manufacturing Technology Lab - Module 1: Exercises on Milling Machine

## Topic: Plane and Pocket Milling Operations

---

### 1. Introduction to Milling Machines

Milling is a machining process that produces features on a workpiece by removing material with a rotating cutter. It's a versatile process capable of creating flat surfaces, slots, holes, and complex shapes.

*   **Types of Milling:**
    *   **Peripheral Milling (Slab Milling):** The cutting takes place on the periphery of the milling cutter. Produces flat surfaces.
    *   **Face Milling:** The cutting takes place on the face of the milling cutter. Typically used for producing flat surfaces, often perpendicular to the workpiece axis.
*   **Types of Milling Machines:**
    *   **Horizontal Milling Machine:** Spindle axis is horizontal. Often used for slab milling and cutting slots.
    *   **Vertical Milling Machine:** Spindle axis is vertical. Versatile for face milling, end milling, and operations requiring precise vertical control.
    *   **Universal Milling Machine:** Can be set up for both horizontal and vertical milling.

**Reference:** *Production Technology by HMT (Tata McGraw Hill, 2017)* - Chapter on Milling Machines provides detailed information on machine types, operations, and components.

### 2. Plane Milling Operations

**Definition:** Plane milling is a machining process used to produce a flat surface on a workpiece. This surface is parallel to the axis of rotation of the milling cutter.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Workpiece:** The material being machined.
*   **Milling Cutter:** The rotating tool with cutting edges used to remove material.
    *   **Types of Plane Milling Cutters:**
        *   **Slab Mills:** Wide, disc-shaped cutters with teeth on the periphery. Used for peripheral milling.
        *   **Face Mills:** Disc-shaped cutters with teeth on the face and periphery. Used for face milling.
*   **Spindle:** Rotates the milling cutter.
*   **Table:** Holds and moves the workpiece relative to the cutter.
*   **Feed:** The rate at which the workpiece is moved against the rotating cutter.
*   **Depth of Cut:** The distance the cutter penetrates into the workpiece.
*   **Cutting Speed (V):** The peripheral speed of the cutting tool.
*   **Spindle Speed (N):** The rotational speed of the milling cutter in revolutions per minute (RPM).
    *   **Relationship:** $V = \frac{\pi D N}{1000}$, where $D$ is the cutter diameter in mm and $V$ is in m/min.
*   **Feed Rate (f):** The distance the workpiece is advanced per revolution of the cutter (mm/rev) or per minute (mm/min).
*   **Chip Thickness (t):** The thickness of the material removed by each cutting tooth.
*   **Material Removal Rate (MRR):** The volume of material removed per unit time.
    *   **For Slab Milling:** $MRR = Depth \ of \ Cut \times Width \ of \ Cut \times Feed \ Rate$
    *   **For Face Milling:** $MRR = Depth \ of \ Cut \times Diameter \ of \ Cutter \times Width \ of \ Cut \times Feed \ Rate$ (More accurately involves the overlap and engagement of the cutter)

**Procedure for Plane Milling:**

1.  **Workpiece Setup:** Securely clamp the workpiece to the milling machine table using vises, clamps, or fixtures. Ensure it is level and at the correct height for the cutter.
2.  **Cutter Selection and Mounting:** Select an appropriate milling cutter (e.g., slab mill or face mill) based on the surface to be machined and the material. Mount it securely on the spindle.
3.  **Setting Cutting Parameters:**
    *   **Cutting Speed (V):** Determine based on cutter material, workpiece material, and desired surface finish.
    *   **Spindle Speed (N):** Calculate using the cutting speed formula ($N = \frac{1000 V}{\pi D}$).
    *   **Feed Rate (f):** Select based on cutter type, diameter, number of teeth, and desired surface finish.
    *   **Depth of Cut (d):** Determine based on the amount of material to be removed and the cutter's capacity.
4.  **Zero Setting:** Bring the cutter into contact with the workpiece surface. Use a dial indicator or feeler gauge to accurately set the zero point for the depth of cut.
5.  **Machining:**
    *   Start the spindle rotation.
    *   Engage the feed to move the workpiece towards the cutter.
    *   **Up-Milling (Conventional Milling):** The cutter rotates against the direction of feed. Produces a thinner chip at the beginning of the cut and a thicker chip at the end. Generates an upward force on the workpiece. Preferred for roughing and when the workpiece has a soft base.
    *   **Down-Milling (Climb Milling):** The cutter rotates in the same direction as the feed. Produces a thicker chip at the beginning of the cut and a thinner chip at the end. Generates a downward force on the workpiece. Results in better surface finish and longer tool life, but requires a rigid machine and proper workholding.
6.  **Completion:** Once the required depth of cut is achieved and the full length is traversed, disengage the feed and retract the cutter.

**Important Points to Remember for Plane Milling:**

*   **Rigidity:** Ensure the workpiece and cutter setup is rigid to prevent vibration and chatter, which degrade surface finish and tool life.
*   **Coolant:** Use coolant to reduce heat, lubricate the cutting zone, and flush away chips, especially for higher cutting speeds or tougher materials.
*   **Chip Load:** Maintain an appropriate chip load per tooth to ensure efficient material removal without overloading the cutter or machine.
*   **Surface Finish:** Down-milling generally provides a better surface finish.

### 3. Pocket Milling Operations

**Definition:** Pocket milling is a machining process used to create a cavity or recess into a workpiece. This cavity can have various shapes (rectangular, circular, irregular) and depths.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Pocket:** A recessed area machined into the workpiece.
*   **End Mill:** A type of milling cutter with cutting teeth on the periphery and the end face. Essential for pocket milling as it can machine both horizontally and vertically.
    *   **Types of End Mills:**
        *   **Two-Flute:** Good for slotting and plunging, less efficient for peripheral milling.
        *   **Multi-Flute:** Suitable for peripheral milling and producing good surface finish.
*   **Plunge Cutting:** The ability of an end mill to cut vertically into the workpiece. Only end mills with end-cutting teeth can do this.
*   **Ramping:** A technique where the end mill enters the workpiece at an angle rather than plunging vertically, reducing stress on the tool and machine.
*   **Stepover:** The distance the cutter moves sideways (perpendicular to the cutting path) between passes. Affects the surface finish of the pocket walls and bottom.
*   **Zig-zag/Bi-directional Milling:** The cutter moves back and forth across the pocket.
*   **Contour Milling:** Machining along the boundary of the pocket.
*   **Roughing vs. Finishing Passes:**
    *   **Roughing:** Removes the bulk of the material quickly, often with larger stepovers and depths of cut.
    *   **Finishing:** Uses smaller stepovers and depths of cut to achieve the final desired dimensions and surface finish.

**Procedure for Pocket Milling:**

1.  **Workpiece Setup:** Securely clamp the workpiece to the milling machine table.
2.  **Cutter Selection and Mounting:** Select an appropriate end mill. The diameter of the end mill will influence the minimum corner radius that can be achieved in the pocket. Use a larger diameter cutter for roughing to clear material faster.
3.  **Setting Cutting Parameters:**
    *   **Cutting Speed (V) and Spindle Speed (N):** Similar principles to plane milling, considering the end mill material and workpiece.
    *   **Feed Rate (f):** Typically chosen as a feed per tooth ($f_z$) for end mills. $F = N \times f_z \times Z$, where $F$ is the feed rate in mm/min, $N$ is spindle speed, and $Z$ is the number of teeth.
    *   **Depth of Cut (d):** For roughing, can be a significant portion of the cutter diameter. For finishing, it's usually a small amount (e.g., 0.1-0.5 mm).
    *   **Stepover (Radial):** For roughing, a larger stepover (e.g., 50-70% of cutter diameter) is used. For finishing, a smaller stepover (e.g., 10-30% of cutter diameter) is crucial for surface finish.
4.  **Zero Setting:** Accurately locate the starting point for the pocket. Often, the center of the pocket or a specific corner is used.
5.  **Machining:**
    *   **Plunge (if necessary):** If plunging, ensure the end mill is designed for it. Plunge at a slower feed rate than peripheral cutting.
    *   **Ramp (preferred):** Machine at an angle into the workpiece. This is generally safer and more efficient.
    *   **Pocket Clearing Strategy:**
        *   **Island/Perimeter Strategy:** Machine around any internal features (islands) first, then clear the remaining material.
        *   **Zig-zag/Onion Skinning:** The cutter moves back and forth, clearing material efficiently. Often used for roughing.
        *   **Spiral Machining:** The cutter moves in a spiral path, clearing material. Can be effective for starting pockets.
    *   **Roughing Passes:** Complete the bulk of the material removal using larger depths of cut and stepovers.
    *   **Finishing Passes:** After roughing, make one or more finishing passes with a smaller depth of cut and a finer stepover to achieve the desired surface finish and dimensional accuracy.
6.  **Completion:** Once the pocket is machined to the required depth and specifications, retract the cutter.

**Important Points to Remember for Pocket Milling:**

*   **Corner Radii:** The internal corners of a pocket will have a radius equal to the radius of the end mill used. To achieve sharp internal corners, you would need to use a smaller diameter end mill for a finishing pass or a wire EDM process.
*   **Tool Selection:** The choice of end mill diameter is critical. It must be small enough to fit within the pocket dimensions, but a larger cutter will clear material faster.
*   **Chip Evacuation:** Ensure good chip evacuation, especially in deep pockets, to prevent tool breakage and poor surface finish. Use coolant and potentially air blasts.
*   **CNC Machining:** Pocket milling is highly suited for CNC machining due to the need for precise path control and repeatability. (Relates to CO3).
*   **Cutting Force Analysis:** Understanding cutting forces is vital to select appropriate parameters, preventing tool breakage and ensuring a good surface finish. (Relates to CO2).

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
A slab milling operation is performed on a mild steel block using a 50 mm diameter slab mill. The cutting speed is 30 m/min, the depth of cut is 3 mm, the width of cut is 40 mm, and the feed per tooth is 0.15 mm/tooth. The cutter has 6 teeth.
a) Calculate the required spindle speed (RPM).
b) Calculate the feed rate in mm/min.
c) Calculate the material removal rate (MRR).

**Answer 1:**
a) **Spindle Speed (N):**
   $V = \frac{\pi D N}{1000}$
   $N = \frac{1000 V}{\pi D} = \frac{1000 \times 30}{\pi \times 50} \approx 191 \text{ RPM}$

b) **Feed Rate (F):**
   First, calculate the feed per revolution: $f_{rev} = f_z \times Z = 0.15 \text{ mm/tooth} \times 6 \text{ teeth} = 0.9 \text{ mm/rev}$
   $F = N \times f_{rev} = 191 \text{ RPM} \times 0.9 \text{ mm/rev} \approx 171.9 \text{ mm/min}$

c) **Material Removal Rate (MRR):**
   $MRR = \text{Depth of Cut} \times \text{Width of Cut} \times \text{Feed Rate}$
   $MRR = 3 \text{ mm} \times 40 \text{ mm} \times 171.9 \text{ mm/min} \approx 20628 \text{ mm}^3/\text{min}$

**Question 2:**
When performing pocket milling with an end mill, what is the significance of the "stepover" parameter on the final surface finish of the pocket walls?

**Answer 2:**
The stepover (also known as radial stepover) is the distance the milling cutter moves sideways between parallel cutting paths. A **smaller stepover** results in a finer surface finish because the individual cutter marks left on the surface are closer together, and there is more overlap between passes. Conversely, a **larger stepover** will leave more pronounced tool marks, leading to a rougher surface finish. For finishing passes in pocket milling, a small stepover is crucial to achieve a smooth surface.

---

**Question 3:**
Explain the difference between up-milling (conventional milling) and down-milling (climb milling) in plane milling operations and describe a scenario where one might be preferred over the other.

**Answer 3:**
*   **Up-Milling (Conventional Milling):**
    *   **Mechanism:** The cutter rotates against the direction of feed. The chip starts thin and becomes thicker as the tooth progresses through the workpiece.
    *   **Forces:** Generates an upward force on the workpiece, tending to lift it away from the table.
    *   **Preference:** Generally preferred for roughing, when the workpiece is not rigidly held, or when there is a soft skin or scale on the workpiece that could deflect the cutter in down-milling. It provides better chip formation in some materials.

*   **Down-Milling (Climb Milling):**
    *   **Mechanism:** The cutter rotates in the same direction as the feed. The chip starts thick and becomes thinner as the tooth progresses.
    *   **Forces:** Generates a downward force on the workpiece, helping to hold it against the table.
    *   **Preference:** Generally preferred for finishing operations due to its ability to produce a better surface finish and longer tool life. It is also more efficient when the machine has anti-backlash mechanisms to handle the cutting forces, ensuring consistent feed. It is crucial for the milling machine to be rigid and have minimal backlash in the table feed mechanism, otherwise, the cutter can chatter or "dig in."

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Safety First:** Always wear safety glasses, keep hands clear of moving parts, and ensure the workpiece is securely clamped.
*   **Material Properties:** The cutting parameters are highly dependent on the workpiece material. Consult machining handbooks or charts.
*   **Tool Condition:** Use sharp cutters for better surface finish, reduced cutting forces, and longer tool life.
*   **Workholding:** Proper workholding is paramount for accuracy and safety.
*   **Coolant:** Use appropriate coolant for the material and operation to manage heat and lubrication.
*   **First Pass:** Always take a light "dry run" or a very shallow first pass to verify setup and alignment before committing to the full depth of cut.
*   **Pocket Corner Radii:** Remember that internal corners of pockets will inherit the radius of the end mill used.
