# MANUFACTURING TECHNOLOGY LAB: Module 1 - Exercises on Surface Grinding Machine

## Topic: Grinding of a Flat Surface

---

### 1. Introduction to Surface Grinding

**Definition:** Surface grinding is an abrasive machining process used to produce a smooth, flat or contoured surface. It is a finishing operation that removes a small amount of material to achieve high accuracy and excellent surface finish.

**Purpose:**
*   To achieve a high degree of flatness and parallelism.
*   To obtain a fine surface finish (low surface roughness).
*   To remove small amounts of material, often for final dimensioning.
*   To grind hardened materials that are difficult to machine by other methods.

**Relationship to Course Outcomes:**
*   **CO1 (Machine Operation & Quality):** This exercise directly addresses the operation of a machine tool (surface grinder) to produce specific part features (flat surface) to desired quality (accuracy, surface finish). (Knowledge Level: K3 - Application)

### 2. The Surface Grinding Machine

**2.1 Components of a Surface Grinding Machine:**

*   **Grinding Wheel:** The cutting tool, made of abrasive grains bonded together.
    *   **Abrasive Materials:** Alumina (Aluminum Oxide - Al₂O₃) for ferrous metals, Silicon Carbide (SiC) for non-ferrous and hard materials. (Refer to HMT Production Technology, Chapter on Grinding)
    *   **Grain Size:** Coarse (faster cutting, rougher finish), Fine (slower cutting, finer finish).
    *   **Hardness:** Soft, Medium, Hard (relates to the strength of the bond).
    *   **Bond:** Vitrified (most common, strong, porous), Resinoid (flexible, good for high speeds), Rubber (for very fine finishes and high speeds).
    *   **Structure:** Open (less prone to loading), Dense (finer finish).
*   **Wheel Spindle:** Supports and rotates the grinding wheel at high speed.
*   **Worktable:** Holds the workpiece. Can be:
    *   **Reciprocating:** Moves back and forth.
    *   **Rotary:** Rotates the workpiece.
*   **Workholding Devices:**
    *   **Magnetic Chuck:** Most common for ferrous workpieces. Utilizes electromagnetism or permanent magnets to securely hold the workpiece. (Refer to HMT Production Technology, Chapter on Grinding).
    *   **Vise:** For non-magnetic or irregularly shaped workpieces.
    *   **Clamps:** Used for specific workpiece geometries.
*   **Grinding Wheel Dresser:** Used to clean, sharpen, and shape the grinding wheel.
    *   **Diamond Dresser:** For shaping and sharpening.
    *   **Star Dresser:** For cleaning and opening up a loaded wheel.
*   **Coolant System:** Supplies a fluid (coolant or cutting fluid) to lubricate the grinding zone, cool the workpiece and wheel, and flush away chips.
*   **Traverse Mechanism:** Controls the cross-feed movement of the wheelhead or table.
*   **Downfeed Mechanism:** Controls the vertical movement of the grinding wheel.

**2.2 Types of Surface Grinding Machines:**

*   **Horizontal Spindle, Reciprocating Table:** Most common type. Grinds flat surfaces using the side or periphery of the wheel.
*   **Vertical Spindle, Rotary Table:** Grinds flat surfaces using the end face of the grinding wheel. Suitable for larger diameter workpieces and higher production rates.
*   **Vertical Spindle, Reciprocating Table:** Less common.

### 3. Principles of Surface Grinding (Flat Surface)

**3.1 Grinding Action:**

*   The grinding wheel rotates at high speed.
*   The workpiece is traversed or rotated under the wheel.
*   The abrasive grains on the wheel act as cutting tools, shearing off small chips of material.
*   The depth of cut (downfeed) determines the amount of material removed per pass.

**3.2 Parameters and their Impact:**

*   **Wheel Speed:** Higher speeds generally lead to faster material removal but can cause increased wheel wear and heat generation. (Refer to HMT Production Technology, Chapter on Grinding for recommended speeds).
*   **Worktable Traverse Speed:** Affects the surface finish and chip formation. Slower speeds can lead to a better finish but may increase heat.
*   **Cross-feed:** The amount the wheel is moved sideways in each pass. A wider cross-feed removes material faster but may result in a less uniform finish.
*   **Depth of Cut (Downfeed):** A critical parameter.
    *   **Heavy Downfeed:** High material removal rate, significant heat generation, increased wheel wear, risk of wheel loading, potential for workpiece distortion or burning.
    *   **Light Downfeed:** Lower material removal rate, less heat, finer finish, less wheel wear, reduced risk of burning.
    *   **General Rule:** It is often better to take multiple light passes rather than one heavy pass to achieve a good surface finish and avoid thermal damage. (Refer to Chapman, Workshop Technology Part I, for principles of metal removal).
*   **Wheel Selection:** Crucial for efficient grinding. The correct abrasive, grit size, hardness, and bond type must be chosen based on the workpiece material, desired finish, and machine capabilities. (Refer to HMT Production Technology, Chapter on Grinding for wheel selection charts).

### 4. Procedure for Grinding a Flat Surface

**Objective:** To grind a flat surface on a given workpiece to specified dimensions and surface finish.

**Steps:**

1.  **Machine Setup and Safety:**
    *   Ensure the machine is clean and in good working order.
    *   Wear appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): safety glasses, safety shoes, lab coat/apron.
    *   Ensure the grinding wheel guard is in place.
    *   Check for any loose parts or obstructions.

2.  **Wheel Selection and Mounting:**
    *   Select an appropriate grinding wheel based on the workpiece material (e.g., for steel, use Aluminum Oxide wheel; for carbide, use Silicon Carbide wheel).
    *   Inspect the wheel for any cracks or damage.
    *   Mount the wheel securely on the spindle using flanges and a proper nut. Ensure the wheel is clean and the blotter paper is in good condition.
    *   **IMPORTANT:** Perform a "ring test" on the wheel (gently tap it with a non-metallic object to hear a clear ring, indicating no cracks).

3.  **Wheel Dressing and Trueing:**
    *   **Trueing:** To restore the concentricity and roundness of the wheel. This is done when the wheel is out-of-balance or has developed a taper.
    *   **Dressing:** To remove loaded abrasive grains, expose new sharp grains, and create a desired wheel profile.
    *   **Procedure:**
        *   Mount a diamond dresser in the magnetic chuck or workholding device.
        *   Bring the wheel into contact with the dresser.
        *   Apply a light downfeed.
        *   Traverse the table or rotate the workpiece to dress the entire face of the wheel.
        *   Use coolant during dressing to prevent overheating.
        *   After dressing, take a light pass over the workpiece to remove any dressing residue.

4.  **Workpiece Preparation and Mounting:**
    *   Clean the workpiece and the magnetic chuck thoroughly. Remove any dirt, oil, or burrs.
    *   If using a magnetic chuck, demagnetize the workpiece if necessary before mounting to prevent residual magnetism.
    *   Position the workpiece on the magnetic chuck or vise. Ensure it is securely held and stable.
    *   For flatness and parallelism, use parallel strips or suitable support if required.

5.  **Setting Grinding Parameters:**
    *   **Downfeed:** Set an appropriate initial downfeed (e.g., 0.01 mm to 0.02 mm for the first few passes, then potentially increase for roughing and decrease for finishing).
    *   **Traverse Speed:** Set a moderate traverse speed (e.g., 10-15 m/min).
    *   **Cross-feed:** Set a suitable cross-feed (e.g., 1/2 to 3/4 of the wheel width for efficient material removal, or a finer cross-feed for better finish).

6.  **Grinding Operation:**
    *   Start the spindle and coolant flow.
    *   Bring the grinding wheel down to contact the workpiece.
    *   Engage the table traverse.
    *   Apply the downfeed in increments at the end of each table pass or at intervals.
    *   **IMPORTANT:** Listen to the grinding sound. A smooth, consistent sound indicates proper grinding. A harsh, screeching sound may indicate the wheel is loaded, the downfeed is too heavy, or the workpiece is not properly supported.
    *   Periodically check the workpiece dimensions and surface finish with measuring instruments (e.g., surface plate, indicator, micrometer).
    *   **Finishing Passes:** For the final few passes, significantly reduce the downfeed (e.g., 0.005 mm or less) and potentially reduce the cross-feed and traverse speed to achieve the desired surface finish.
    *   **Wheel Dressing (if needed):** If the surface finish deteriorates or burning occurs, re-dress the wheel.

7.  **Post-Grinding:**
    *   Turn off the grinding wheel and table traverse.
    *   Remove the workpiece from the chuck.
    *   Clean the workpiece and the machine.
    *   Demagnetize the workpiece if it was held on a magnetic chuck.
    *   Inspect the ground surface for flatness, surface finish, and absence of burns or grinding marks.

### 5. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Abrasive:** The hard, sharp particles that perform the cutting action.
*   **Bond:** The material that holds the abrasive grains together.
*   **Wheel Face:** The surface of the grinding wheel that does the cutting.
*   **Loading:** When chips of workpiece material become embedded in the grinding wheel's pores, reducing its cutting ability and generating excessive heat.
*   **Glazing:** When the abrasive grains become dull and the bond wears away, leaving a smooth, shiny surface on the wheel. This reduces cutting efficiency and can cause burning.
*   **Burning:** Thermal damage to the workpiece surface caused by excessive heat generated during grinding. It can result in micro-cracks and reduced material properties.
*   **Wheel Truing:** Restoring the concentricity and geometrical accuracy of the grinding wheel.
*   **Wheel Dressing:** Sharpening and cleaning the grinding wheel to expose fresh cutting edges and prevent loading.
*   **Downfeed:** The vertical depth of cut per pass.
*   **Cross-feed:** The sideways movement of the grinding wheel relative to the workpiece per pass.
*   **Traverse Speed:** The speed at which the workpiece or wheel moves along the grinding axis.
*   **Surface Roughness:** A measure of the fine-scale irregularities on the surface of the workpiece.

### 6. Factors Affecting Surface Finish and Accuracy

*   **Wheel Properties:** Grit size, grade, structure, and bond.
*   **Grinding Parameters:** Depth of cut, table traverse speed, cross-feed.
*   **Wheel Condition:** Sharpness, cleanliness, and true running.
*   **Coolant:** Type and flow rate.
*   **Workholding:** Rigidity and stability of the workpiece mounting.
*   **Machine Rigidity:** Vibration and deflection of the machine components.
*   **Cleanliness:** Absence of dirt and debris in the grinding zone.
*   **Dressing Technique:** Proper dressing is crucial for optimal performance.

### 7. Troubleshooting Common Problems

| Problem          | Possible Cause                                                                 | Solution                                                                                             |
| :--------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Burning**      | Downfeed too heavy, traverse speed too slow, dull wheel, poor coolant flow.    | Reduce downfeed, increase traverse speed, dress wheel, improve coolant delivery, use finer grit wheel. |
| **Loading**      | Workpiece material too soft/gummy, dull wheel, insufficient downfeed/crossfeed. | Dress wheel more aggressively, use coarser grit wheel, increase downfeed/crossfeed, use appropriate coolant. |
| **Poor Surface Finish** | Dull wheel, wrong wheel selection, heavy downfeed, heavy crossfeed, dirt. | Dress wheel, use finer grit wheel, reduce downfeed/crossfeed, ensure cleanliness, increase table traverse speed. |
| **Chatter Marks** | Wheel imbalance, worn spindle, loose workholding, vibration.                    | Dress and balance wheel, check spindle, ensure rigid workholding, check machine foundation.        |
| **Loss of Flatness** | Wheel not dressed true, workpiece not properly supported, uneven pressure.   | True and dress wheel properly, use parallels for support, ensure consistent downfeed.              |

### 9. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** You are tasked with grinding a flat surface on a hardened tool steel workpiece to achieve a fine surface finish. What type of grinding wheel abrasive and bond would you likely choose and why?
**Answer:** For hardened tool steel, an **Aluminum Oxide (Al₂O₃) abrasive** is generally preferred due to its toughness and ability to grind ferrous materials effectively. For a fine surface finish, a **finer grit size** (e.g., grit number 60 or finer) would be selected. A **vitrified bond** is common for its strength and porosity, or a **resinoid bond** might be considered for its flexibility and ability to handle higher speeds if necessary.

**Question 2:** What is the difference between wheel truing and wheel dressing?
**Answer:**
*   **Truing** is the process of restoring the concentricity and geometrical accuracy of the grinding wheel, especially when it's out-of-balance or has worn unevenly.
*   **Dressing** is the process of removing dull abrasive grains and embedded chips (loading) from the wheel surface to expose fresh, sharp cutting edges and improve its cutting action.

**Question 3:** Explain the potential consequences of taking an excessively heavy downfeed during surface grinding.
**Answer:** Taking an excessively heavy downfeed can lead to:
*   **Burning of the workpiece:** Due to excessive heat generation.
*   **Wheel loading and glazing:** The wheel can't effectively clear chips.
*   **Reduced surface finish:** The surface may exhibit grinding marks or a poor finish.
*   **Increased risk of wheel fracture:** Due to excessive force.
*   **Workpiece distortion:** Due to thermal stresses.
*   **Increased power consumption.**

**Question 4:** You observe a harsh, screeching sound during surface grinding. What are the likely causes and what steps would you take to rectify the situation?
**Answer:**
*   **Likely Causes:** Wheel loading, excessive downfeed, dull wheel, workpiece not properly supported, or improper traverse speed.
*   **Rectification Steps:**
    1.  **Stop the machine immediately.**
    2.  **Dress the wheel:** This is the first and most common solution for loading or a dull wheel.
    3.  **Reduce the downfeed:** Take lighter passes.
    4.  **Check workpiece support:** Ensure it is securely held and not vibrating.
    5.  **Adjust traverse speed:** Experiment with slightly faster traverse.
    6.  **Consider a different wheel:** If the problem persists, the wheel might be unsuitable for the material.

**Practical Exercise:**
Perform the surface grinding of a flat surface on a mild steel block.
1.  Measure the initial dimensions of the block.
2.  Select an appropriate grinding wheel.
3.  True and dress the grinding wheel.
4.  Mount the workpiece securely on the magnetic chuck.
5.  Set appropriate grinding parameters (downfeed, cross-feed, traverse speed).
6.  Grind the surface, taking light finishing passes.
7.  Periodically measure the dimensions and assess the surface finish.
8.  Document your parameters, observations, and the final results.

### 10. Important Points to Remember

*   **Safety First:** Always wear PPE and ensure machine guards are in place.
*   **Wheel Selection is Crucial:** Match the wheel to the workpiece material and desired finish.
*   **Dressing and Truing are Essential:** A sharp, true wheel is key to quality grinding.
*   **Light Downfeeds for Finishing:** Avoid heavy cuts when a good surface finish is required.
*   **Coolant is Your Friend:** Use coolant to reduce heat, lubricate, and flush chips.
*   **Listen to the Machine:** The sound of grinding provides valuable feedback on the process.
*   **Cleanliness:** Keep the machine and workpiece clean to prevent contamination and improve results.
*   **Measure and Inspect:** Regularly check dimensions and surface finish.
