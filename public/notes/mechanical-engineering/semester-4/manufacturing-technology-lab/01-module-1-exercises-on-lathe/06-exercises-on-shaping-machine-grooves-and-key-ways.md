# Manufacturing Technology Lab: Module 1 - Exercises on Shaping Machine

## Topic: Grooves and Keyways

This module focuses on the practical application of the shaping machine for producing specific features on workpieces, namely grooves and keyways. These operations are fundamental in manufacturing for creating seating for components like keys in shafts.

---

### **1. Introduction to the Shaping Machine**

The shaping machine is a reciprocating machine tool that utilizes a single-point cutting tool to remove material from a workpiece. It is primarily used for producing flat surfaces, but with appropriate tooling, it can also generate grooves and other forms.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Reciprocating Motion:** The cutting tool moves back and forth in a straight line.
*   **Up-cut vs. Down-cut Shaping:**
    *   **Up-cut (Conventional):** Tool cuts on the return stroke. Workpiece held firmly, cleaner finish.
    *   **Down-cut (Climb):** Tool cuts on the forward stroke. Faster material removal, can be rougher. Shaping machines primarily operate with up-cut.
*   **Work Holding:** Workpieces are typically held on a table that moves horizontally (table feed) and vertically (ram adjustment).
*   **Tool Movement:**
    *   **Cutting Stroke:** Tool moves across the workpiece.
    *   **Return Stroke:** Tool moves back to its starting position without cutting.
    *   **Feed Stroke:** The table moves the workpiece laterally or the ram moves vertically between cutting strokes.

**Reference:**

*   **Production Technology by HMT (Tata McGraw Hill, 2017):** Provides a comprehensive overview of the shaping machine's construction, operation, and applications.
*   **Workshop Technology Part I by W. A. J. Chapman (ELBS & Edward Arnold Publishers, 1972):** Offers fundamental principles of machine tools, including the shaping machine.

### **2. Shaping Machine Operations for Grooves**

Grooves are channels or recesses machined into a workpiece. They are often used for retaining rings, sealing elements, or to facilitate the assembly of components.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Types of Grooves:**
    *   **Straight Grooves:** Parallel to the axis of the workpiece.
    *   **Circular Grooves:** Formed around the circumference of a workpiece.
    *   **T-Slots:** Dovetail-shaped grooves used for mounting workpieces.
    *   **Rectangular Grooves:** Simple U-shaped channels.
*   **Cutting Tool:** A single-point cutting tool with specific geometry is used, often with a nose radius to create a rounded bottom for some grooves.
*   **Machining Process:**
    1.  **Setup:** Securely mount the workpiece on the shaping machine table using vises, clamps, or chucks. Ensure proper alignment.
    2.  **Tool Setting:** Position the cutting tool at the starting point of the groove.
    3.  **Depth of Cut:** Set the vertical feed for the desired groove depth.
    4.  **Feed Rate:** Determine the appropriate table feed rate for efficient material removal and surface finish.
    5.  **Cutting Stroke:** Engage the cutting stroke. The tool moves across the workpiece.
    6.  **Return Stroke:** The tool returns.
    7.  **Repeated Cycles:** Repeat the cutting and return strokes, with the table advancing or the ram lowering (depending on the groove type) after each cut until the desired groove is formed.

**Example:** Machining a rectangular groove on the face of a rectangular block.

*   **Setup:** Clamp the block securely in a vise.
*   **Tool:** Use a square-nosed tool.
*   **Process:**
    *   Set the ram to the correct height.
    *   Position the tool at one end of the groove.
    *   Set the depth of cut for the first pass.
    *   Engage the cutting stroke.
    *   After the return stroke, engage the cross-feed to move the workpiece laterally to the required width of the groove.
    *   Repeat until the groove is cut to the desired depth and width. For a rectangular groove, you would typically make multiple passes, incrementally lowering the tool.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Tool Grinding:** The cutting tool must be ground with appropriate clearance angles to prevent rubbing and ensure effective cutting. For grooves, tools with specific profiles might be required (e.g., form tools for T-slots).
*   **Workpiece Holding:** Robust clamping is crucial to prevent vibration and ensure accuracy.
*   **Lubrication/Cooling:** Use cutting fluid to reduce heat and improve surface finish.

### **3. Shaping Machine Operations for Keyways**

Keyways are slots machined into shafts or hubs to accommodate keys, which transmit torque between rotating and stationary components.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Keyway Geometry:** Typically rectangular slots, but can also be semicircular (Woodruff keyways).
*   **Cutting Tool:**
    *   **Straight Keyways:** A square-nosed cutting tool or a side-cutting tool is used. Multiple passes are usually required.
    *   **Woodruff Keyways:** A special form tool or an end-milling cutter on a shaping machine (with indexing) can be used.
*   **Machining Process for Straight Keyways:**
    1.  **Setup:** Mount the shaft horizontally or vertically in a chuck or between centers. Ensure it is properly aligned.
    2.  **Tool Setting:** Position the cutting tool at the starting point of the keyway, usually on the periphery of the shaft.
    3.  **Depth of Cut:** Set the vertical feed to remove material for the keyway depth.
    4.  **Feed Rate:** Control the table feed for the length of the keyway.
    5.  **Indexing:** After each cutting stroke and return stroke, the workpiece needs to be indexed (rotated) by a small amount to position the next cutting path. For straight keyways, indexing is usually done by rotating the shaft by a small increment after each pass.
    6.  **Multiple Passes:** The keyway is formed by taking multiple passes, incrementally lowering the tool with each pass, and advancing the feed along the length of the shaft.

**Example:** Machining a straight rectangular keyway in a shaft.

*   **Setup:** Mount the shaft in a 4-jaw chuck on the shaping machine. Ensure the shaft is centered.
*   **Tool:** Use a square-nosed tool.
*   **Process:**
    *   Position the tool on the surface of the shaft at the starting end of the keyway.
    *   Set the depth of cut for the first pass (e.g., 1-2 mm).
    *   Set the table feed rate for the length of the keyway.
    *   Engage the cutting stroke.
    *   After the return stroke, engage the cross-feed to lower the tool by the depth of cut.
    *   **Crucially:** The shaft needs to be rotated by a very small amount to position for the next cut. This rotation is done by hand on the chuck. For a keyway of a certain width, you would make several passes, advancing the tool sideways with each set of cuts. A common approach is to make two side cuts to form the width of the keyway, then advance the tool vertically for the next depth.
    *   Repeat this process until the desired depth and width of the keyway are achieved.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Accuracy of Indexing:** Precise rotation of the workpiece is critical for achieving the correct keyway dimensions.
*   **Tool Life:** Keyway cutting can be demanding on the tool due to the cumulative material removal. Regular tool inspection and grinding are essential.
*   **Reference:** **Production Technology by HMT (Tata McGraw Hill, 2017)** discusses specialized machining techniques for shafts, including keyway cutting.

### **4. Work Holding and Setup Considerations**

Proper work holding and setup are paramount for successful shaping machine operations, especially for grooves and keyways, where accuracy is important.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Vise:** Used for holding rectangular or irregularly shaped workpieces.
*   **Clamps and Bolts:** Used to secure workpieces directly to the shaping machine table.
*   **Chuck:** For holding cylindrical workpieces, like shafts, for operations like machining keyways.
*   **Centers:** For supporting long shafts between the headstock and tailstock.
*   **Indexing Head (Dividing Head):** Can be used for more precise angular divisions, which can be useful for cutting multiple grooves or specific patterns.
*   **Workpiece Support:** For longer workpieces, additional support might be needed to prevent sagging.

**Reference:**

*   **Workshop Technology Part I by W. A. J. Chapman (ELBS & Edward Arnold Publishers, 1972):** Covers various work holding devices and their applications.

### **5. Cutting Parameters and Tool Geometry**

Selecting appropriate cutting parameters and ensuring correct tool geometry significantly impacts the efficiency, surface finish, and accuracy of the operation.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Cutting Speed:** The speed at which the cutting tool moves across the workpiece. For shaping machines, this is related to the ram stroke speed.
*   **Feed Rate:** The amount the workpiece is moved laterally or the tool is lowered after each cutting stroke.
*   **Depth of Cut:** The thickness of the material removed in a single pass.
*   **Tool Geometry:**
    *   **Rake Angles (Back Rake, Side Rake):** Affect chip formation and cutting forces.
    *   **Clearance Angles (End Clearance, Side Clearance):** Prevent the tool flank from rubbing against the workpiece.
    *   **Cutting Edge Angle/Nose Radius:** Influences surface finish and tool strength. For grooves, a specific nose radius might be needed at the bottom to create a radiused corner.
*   **Cutting Forces and Power Consumption (CO2):**
    *   The force exerted by the tool on the workpiece during cutting.
    *   The power required to drive the machine.
    *   These are influenced by cutting speed, feed rate, depth of cut, material being cut, and tool geometry. HMT's "Production Technology" would delve into the calculations for cutting forces and power.

**Reference:**

*   **Production Technology by HMT (Tata McGraw Hill, 2017):** Provides detailed information on cutting parameters, tool geometry for various operations, and methods for calculating cutting forces and power.
*   **Numerical Control of Machine Tools by Yoram Koren (McGraw-Hill, 2014):** While focused on CNC, the fundamental principles of cutting mechanics discussed are relevant.

### **6. Safety Precautions**

Working with shaping machines requires strict adherence to safety protocols.

**Key Precautions:**

*   **Wear appropriate PPE:** Safety glasses, sturdy footwear, and no loose clothing.
*   **Ensure workpiece is securely held:** Loose workpieces can be ejected with force.
*   **Keep hands away from moving parts:** Ram, tool, and table.
*   **Do not attempt to adjust during operation:** Stop the machine before making any adjustments.
*   **Proper chip removal:** Use a brush or tool, never hands.
*   **Know the emergency stop location:** Be prepared to use it.
*   **Clear the work area:** Keep it free of obstructions.

### **7. Practical Exercises and Questions**

**Exercise 1: Machining a Rectangular Groove**

*   **Task:** Machine a rectangular groove of 10mm width, 5mm depth, and 50mm length on the face of a mild steel block (50mm x 50mm x 20mm).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Set up the mild steel block in a vise on the shaping machine table. Ensure the surface to be machined is parallel to the table.
    2.  Select a suitable square-nosed cutting tool.
    3.  Set the ram stroke length to approximately 60mm.
    4.  Position the tool at one end of the desired groove location.
    5.  Set the vertical feed for the first pass (e.g., 1mm).
    6.  Engage the cutting stroke.
    7.  After the return stroke, engage the cross-feed to move the tool laterally by half the desired groove width plus a small allowance (e.g., 5mm + 0.5mm) to clear the tool nose.
    8.  Repeat the cutting stroke.
    9.  After the return stroke, lower the tool by the depth of cut (e.g., 1mm).
    10. Engage the cross-feed by the remaining half of the groove width (e.g., 5mm).
    11. Repeat the cutting stroke.
    12. Continue lowering the tool and making passes until the groove is 5mm deep.
    13. For achieving the full width, you might need to make two passes with the tool shifted laterally.

**Exercise 2: Machining a Straight Keyway**

*   **Task:** Machine a straight rectangular keyway of 6mm width and 3mm depth on a mild steel shaft of 25mm diameter and 100mm length.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Mount the mild steel shaft in a 4-jaw chuck, ensuring it is centered and securely held.
    2.  Select a square-nosed cutting tool.
    3.  Set the ram stroke length to be slightly longer than the keyway length (e.g., 110mm).
    4.  Position the tool at the starting end of the keyway on the shaft's periphery.
    5.  Set the vertical feed for the first pass (e.g., 1mm).
    6.  Engage the cutting stroke.
    7.  After the return stroke, lower the tool by the depth of cut (e.g., 1mm).
    8.  **Crucially:** Manually rotate the chuck by a small amount to bring the next cutting edge into position. For a 6mm wide keyway, you'll need to make multiple passes. A common approach is to make two side cuts for the width and then advance the tool vertically.
    9.  Repeat steps 6-8, incrementally lowering the tool and rotating the shaft, until the keyway reaches the desired depth (3mm).

**Practice Questions:**

1.  What is the primary difference between up-cut and down-cut shaping? (Answer: Direction of cutting stroke relative to workpiece movement).
2.  What type of tool is typically used for machining a straight keyway? (Answer: Square-nosed tool or side-cutting tool).
3.  Why is precise indexing important when machining keyways? (Answer: To ensure the correct width and concentricity of the keyway).
4.  List three common work-holding devices used on a shaping machine. (Answer: Vise, clamps, chuck).
5.  How can you improve the surface finish when machining a groove on a shaping machine? (Answer: Use a finer feed rate, appropriate cutting speed, sharp tool with a nose radius, and cutting fluid).
6.  What safety precautions are essential when operating a shaping machine? (Answer: PPE, secure workpiece, keep hands clear of moving parts).
