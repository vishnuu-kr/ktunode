# Machine Tools and Metrology - Module 3: Metrology

## Topic: Limit Gauging

### 1. Need for Inspection, Accuracy and Precision, Calibration, Errors in Measurement, Standards of Measurement

While this topic is foundational for understanding limit gauging, the core of this section focuses on the practical application of gauges. For a comprehensive understanding of these introductory concepts, refer to your textbooks in the introductory sections of Metrology.

**Key Concepts to Remember:**

*   **Inspection:** The process of examining a product to determine whether it conforms to specified requirements.
*   **Accuracy:** The degree of closeness of a measurement to the true value of the quantity being measured.
*   **Precision:** The degree of reproducibility of a measurement under unchanged conditions.
*   **Calibration:** The comparison of a measuring instrument to a known standard of measurement to detect and record any deviation from the true value.
*   **Errors in Measurement:** The difference between the measured value and the true value of the quantity. These can be systematic (predictable) or random (unpredictable).
*   **Standards of Measurement:** Physical quantities having defined magnitudes and used as references for measurements (e.g., meter, kilogram, second).

---

### 2. Limit Gauging

Limit gauging is a method of inspection used to quickly check whether a manufactured part falls within the specified tolerance limits for its dimensions. It utilizes "go" and "no-go" gauges, which represent the maximum and minimum acceptable sizes of a feature, respectively.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Purpose:** To ensure interchangeability of parts by quickly and economically checking if they conform to specified tolerances. This is crucial for mass production where components need to fit together without selective assembly.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Fast and economical for high-volume production.
    *   Requires less skill from the operator compared to precision measuring instruments.
    *   Reduces the possibility of human error in interpretation.
    *   Ideal for shop-floor inspection.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Does not provide a direct measurement of the actual size, only whether it is within limits.
    *   Gauges wear out over time and require recalibration or replacement.
    *   Can be expensive to manufacture for very tight tolerances.

### 3. Classification of Gauges

Gauges can be classified based on various criteria. For limit gauging, the primary focus is on their application and construction.

**A. Based on Application/Design:**

*   **Plug Gauges:** Used for inspecting the size of holes or internal features.
    *   **Simple Plug Gauges:** A solid cylindrical rod with a "go" end and a "no-go" end.
    *   **Built-up Plug Gauges:** Consist of a handle and renewable gauge members, used for larger holes to reduce cost and facilitate replacement of worn ends.
    *   **Reversible Plug Gauges:** Have a single gauge member that can be reversed when one end wears out, effectively doubling its life.
    *   **Progressive Plug Gauges:** Combines the "go" and "no-go" sections on a single end, with the "go" end leading. This allows for a quick check of both limits in a single insertion.

    *   **Example:** A hole for a shaft needs to be between 10.00 mm and 10.02 mm.
        *   The "go" plug gauge would have a diameter of 10.00 mm.
        *   The "no-go" plug gauge would have a diameter of 10.02 mm.
        *   **Acceptable part:** The "go" end enters the hole, but the "no-go" end does not.

*   **Ring Gauges:** Used for inspecting the outside diameter of shafts or external features.
    *   **Plain Ring Gauges:** A ring with a precisely ground and lapped internal diameter, representing either the maximum material limit or the minimum material limit.
    *   **Gap Gauges (Snap Gauges):** These are often C-shaped and have two measuring jaws.
        *   **Fixed Gap Gauges:** Have fixed "go" and "no-go" anvils at a set distance.
        *   **Adjustable Gap Gauges:** Allow for adjustment of the gap between the anvils to accommodate wear or slight variations in tolerances.

    *   **Example:** A shaft needs to be between 20.00 mm and 19.98 mm.
        *   The "go" ring gauge would have an internal diameter of 20.00 mm.
        *   The "no-go" ring gauge would have an internal diameter of 19.98 mm.
        *   **Acceptable part:** The "go" ring gauge passes over the shaft, but the "no-go" ring gauge does not.
