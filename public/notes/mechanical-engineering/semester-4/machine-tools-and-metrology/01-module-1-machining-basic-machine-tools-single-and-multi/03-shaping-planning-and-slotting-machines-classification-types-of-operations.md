# Machine Tools and Metrology: Module 1 - Machining: Basic Machine Tools: Shaping, Planning, and Slotting Machines

## 1. Introduction to Machining Operations

Machining is a subtractive manufacturing process where material is removed from a workpiece to achieve the desired shape and dimensions. This is typically achieved by using a cutting tool that moves relative to the workpiece. Basic machine tools are fundamental in achieving these operations, and among them, **Shaping, Planning, and Slotting machines** play a crucial role in producing flat surfaces, grooves, and keyways.

**Course Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Describe various machine tool operations) - This section introduces the foundational concept of machining and sets the stage for understanding the specific operations performed by shaping, planning, and slotting machines.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Workpiece:** The material being machined.
*   **Cutting Tool:** The tool used to remove material.
*   **Relative Motion:** The movement between the cutting tool and the workpiece.
*   **Machining Operation:** The specific process of material removal.

**Referenced Textbooks:**

*   *Elements of Workshop Technology Vol-II Machine Tools* by S K Hajra Choudhury Nirjhar Roy

## 2. Shaping Machines

Shaping machines are used to produce flat surfaces, but they can also be used for some angular and curved surfaces. They are characterized by the fact that the **workpiece reciprocates** while the **cutting tool makes a single cutting stroke** and is then lifted during the return stroke to avoid cutting.

**Course Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Describe various machine tool operations) - This section details the operation of a shaping machine, a fundamental machine tool operation.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Reciprocating Motion:** Back-and-forth motion.
*   **Cutting Stroke:** The stroke during which the tool removes material.
*   **Return Stroke:** The stroke during which the tool is lifted and does not cut.
*   **Workpiece Motion:** The workpiece is held on a table and moved horizontally to achieve the desired width of cut.
*   **Tool Motion:** The tool is mounted on an adjustable head and makes a reciprocating motion.

### 2.1 Classification of Shaping Machines

Shaping machines can be classified based on their design and mechanism:

*   **According to the type of drive:**
    *   **Crank and Slotted Link Mechanism:** The most common type, using a crank and slotted lever mechanism to convert rotary motion into reciprocating motion. (Refer to *Elements of Workshop Technology Vol-II* for detailed mechanism diagrams).
    *   **Hydraulic Drive:** Offers smoother operation, better speed control, and constant cutting force.

*   **According to the position of the cutting tool:**
    *   **Horizontal Shaping Machine:** The tool head moves horizontally. This is the most common type.
    *   **Vertical Shaping Machine (Often referred to as a Slotting Machine):** The tool head moves vertically. While often categorized as a slotting machine, its operational principle shares similarities.

*   **According to the arrangement of the main drive:**
    *   **General Purpose Shaping Machine:** Used for a wide range of operations.
    *   **Standard Shaping Machine:** Designed for specific types of work.

### 2.2 Types of Operations Performed by Shaping Machines

Shaping machines are versatile and can perform various operations:

*   **Plain or Flat Surfacing:** Machining of flat surfaces. The workpiece is fed against the reciprocating tool.
    *   **Example:** Creating a flat top surface on a block of metal.

*   **Angular Surfacing:** Machining of surfaces inclined at an angle to the base. This is achieved by swiveling the tool head or the table.
    *   **Example:** Shaping a beveled edge on a workpiece.

*   **Grooving:** Cutting grooves or slots in the workpiece.
    *   **Example:** Creating a T-slot for a machine vise jaw.

*   **Forming:** Machining of irregular or curved surfaces using specially shaped form tools.
    *   **Example:** Machining a concave or convex surface.

*   **Cutting Keyways:** Machining narrow slots to fit keys for securing shafts to pulleys or gears. This is a common operation.
    *   **Example:** Cutting a keyway in a hub of a pulley.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Shapers are best suited for short stroke operations and for machining smaller workpieces.
*   The return stroke is idle, leading to lower metal removal rates compared to planers for long workpieces.
*   The cutting tool is usually held in a tool post on the ram, which reciprocates.

## 3. Planning Machines

Planning machines are also used for machining flat surfaces, but they are designed for much larger and heavier workpieces than shapers. The key difference is that in a planer, the **workpiece reciprocates** while the **cutting tool remains stationary** (except for cross-feed and vertical feed adjustments).

**Course Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Describe various machine tool operations) - This section explains the operation of a planer, another fundamental machine tool.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Workpiece Reciprocation:** The workpiece, mounted on a table, moves back and forth.
*   **Tool Stationarity:** The cutting tool is held in a tool head mounted on a cross-rail and makes no reciprocating motion.
*   **Longitudinal Feed:** The movement of the workpiece in the direction of cutting.
*   **Cross Feed:** The horizontal movement of the tool head across the workpiece.
*   **Vertical Feed:** The vertical movement of the tool head.

### 3.1 Classification of Planning Machines

Planers can be classified based on their drive mechanism and design:

*   **According to the type of drive:**
    *   **Reciprocating Drive (Conventional Planer):** Typically driven by a DC motor or AC motor with a reversing switch. The cutting and return strokes are generally at different speeds for efficiency.
    *   **Rotary Drive (Rotary Planer):** The workpiece is mounted on a rotating table, and the cutting tool reciprocates across it. Less common.
    *   **Hydraulic Drive:** Offers smooth and precise control over speed and feed.

*   **According to the design of the machine:**
    *   **Standard Planer:** The most common type, with a fixed bed and a moving table.
    *   **Open-Side Planer:** One side of the housing is removed, allowing for wider workpieces or workpieces with projections.
    *   **Gantry Planer (Bridge Planer):** A large machine where the cutting tool is mounted on a bridge or gantry that moves over a stationary workpiece. Used for very large structures.
    *   **Edge Planer:** Used for machining edges of large plates, such as shipbuilding plates.

### 3.2 Types of Operations Performed by Planning Machines

Planers are primarily used for heavy-duty surfacing operations:

*   **Flat Surfacing:** Machining large flat surfaces on castings, forgings, and structural components.
    *   **Example:** Planing the ways of a lathe bed or the base of a large machine tool.

*   **Grooving and Slotting:** Cutting grooves, slots, and pockets, especially on large workpieces.
    *   **Example:** Machining a large T-slot in a machine base.

*   **Angular Machining:** Machining inclined surfaces by adjusting the angle of the tool head.
    *   **Example:** Planing an angular mounting surface on a heavy machine component.

*   **Forming:** Less common than on shapers due to the nature of the machine, but possible with specially designed form tools for simpler forms on large workpieces.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Planers are designed for heavy-duty work and are suitable for long strokes and large workpieces.
*   The workpiece reciprocates, allowing the tool to remain relatively stationary.
*   The cutting action occurs during one direction of the table's travel (cutting stroke), and the return stroke is at a faster speed.
*   Refer to *Manufacturing Science* by Ghosh and Mallik for detailed explanations of the kinematics and mechanics of planing.

## 4. Slotting Machines

Slotting machines, also known as **vertical shapers**, are specifically designed to produce slots, keyways, and internal shapes. In this machine, the **cutting tool reciprocates vertically**, while the **workpiece is indexed or fed horizontally** to achieve the desired shape.

**Course Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Describe various machine tool operations) - This section details the operations of a slotting machine, a specialized machine tool.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Vertical Reciprocation:** The cutting tool moves up and down.
*   **Workpiece Indexing/Feeding:** The workpiece is moved horizontally (longitudinally and transversely) to create the slot or internal shape.
*   **Internal Machining:** Slotting machines are particularly effective for machining internal features.

### 4.1 Classification of Slotting Machines

Slotting machines are primarily classified based on their drive mechanism:

*   **According to the type of drive:**
    *   **Crank and Slotted Link Mechanism:** Similar to shapers, a crank mechanism converts rotary motion to vertical reciprocating motion.
    *   **Hydraulic Drive:** Provides smooth, stepless speed control and high torque for heavy cuts.

*   **According to the table arrangement:**
    *   **Standard Slotting Machine:** Features a rotary table with longitudinal and transverse feeds.
    *   **Other specialized designs:** May exist for specific applications.

### 4.2 Types of Operations Performed by Slotting Machines

Slotting machines excel at producing internal features:

*   **Keyway Cutting:** Machining of keyways in gears, pulleys, and hubs. This is a primary application.
    *   **Example:** Cutting a straight keyway in the bore of a gear.

*   **Grooving:** Machining internal grooves, oil grooves, and internal splines.
    *   **Example:** Cutting an oil groove inside a cylindrical bore.

*   **Internal Machining of Irregular Shapes:** Creating internal shapes that are not circular, such as cams or complex internal profiles.
    *   **Example:** Machining a square or hexagonal hole in a workpiece.

*   **Die Sinking:** Creating recessed shapes in dies, often using form tools.
    *   **Example:** Machining the cavity of a stamping die.

*   **External Surfacing (Less Common):** While primarily for internal work, they can also be used for external shaping of smaller components or for facing operations.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Slotting machines are characterized by their vertical cutting action.
*   They are ideal for producing internal slots, keyways, and irregular internal profiles.
*   The cutting tool is mounted on a ram that reciprocates vertically.
*   The depth of cut is controlled by the vertical feed of the tool, and the width of the slot by the transverse feed of the table.

## 5. Comparison of Shaping, Planning, and Slotting Machines

| Feature           | Shaping Machine                                     | Planning Machine                                     | Slotting Machine                                     |
| :---------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary Motion** | Tool reciprocates (horizontal)                      | Workpiece reciprocates (horizontal)                  | Tool reciprocates (vertical)                         |
| **Workpiece Size** | Small to medium                                     | Large to very large                                  | Small to medium                                      |
| **Stroke Length** | Short to medium                                     | Long                                                 | Short to medium                                      |
| **Cutting Action**| Tool cuts on forward stroke; idle on return         | Workpiece cuts on forward stroke; idle on return     | Tool cuts on downward stroke; idle on upward         |
| **Typical Work**  | Flat surfaces, keyways, angular surfaces on small parts | Large flat surfaces, heavy sections, long workpieces | Internal keyways, grooves, slots, internal profiles |
| **Chip Disposal** | Good                                                | Good                                                 | Good                                                 |
| **Accuracy**      | Good for its class                                  | Good for its class                                   | Good for its class                                   |
| **Metal Removal** | Lower than planer for long workpieces               | Higher than shaper for long workpieces               | Moderate                                             |

While this module focuses on the types of operations, it's important to note that the efficiency of these machines is often evaluated by machining time and power consumption.

**Course Outcome Alignment:** CO2 (Determine machining time and power consumption in various machining processes) - This section introduces the concept that will be further explored in subsequent modules.

## 8. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
In a **shaping machine**, the cutting tool reciprocates horizontally while the workpiece is fed. In a **planning machine**, the workpiece reciprocates horizontally, and the cutting tool remains stationary (except for feed adjustments).

**Answer 2:**
Three types of operations performed on a slotting machine are:
*   Keyway cutting
*   Internal grooving
*   Machining of internal irregular shapes (e.g., square or hexagonal holes)

**Answer 3:**
For machining a large, flat surface on a heavy machine base, a **planning machine** would be the preferred choice. This is because planers are designed for large and heavy workpieces, and the workpiece reciprocates, allowing for efficient machining of long surfaces.

**Answer 4:**
The advantage of a hydraulic drive over a crank and slotted link mechanism for a shaping machine includes:
*   **Smoother operation:** Hydraulic drives provide a more uniform and less jerky motion.
*   **Better speed control:** Stepless speed variation is possible, allowing for optimal cutting speeds for different materials and depths of cut.
*   **Constant cutting force:** This can lead to improved surface finish and tool life, especially under varying cutting loads.

**Answer 5:**
The return stroke in a shaping machine is generally faster than the cutting stroke to **reduce the overall machining time**. Since no material is being removed during the return stroke, a quicker movement of the tool away from the workpiece minimizes the idle time and increases productivity.

## 9. Important Points to Remember (Summary)

*   **Shaping Machines:** Tool reciprocates, workpiece feeds. Best for short strokes, smaller workpieces, flat, angular, and grooved surfaces.
*   **Planning Machines:** Workpiece reciprocates, tool is stationary. Best for large workpieces and long surfaces.
*   **Slotting Machines:** Tool reciprocates vertically, workpiece feeds. Primarily used for internal slots, keyways, and complex internal shapes.
*   The choice between these machines depends on the size of the workpiece, the nature of the surface to be machined, and the required precision.
*   Understanding the relative motion and the specific operations each machine is designed for is crucial for selecting the appropriate tool for a given manufacturing task.
