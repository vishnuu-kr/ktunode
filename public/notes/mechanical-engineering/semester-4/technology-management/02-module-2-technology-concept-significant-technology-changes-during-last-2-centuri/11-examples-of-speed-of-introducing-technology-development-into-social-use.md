# Technology Management: Module 2 - Technology Concept & Significant Technological Changes (Last 2 Centuries)

## Topic: Examples of Speed of Introducing Technology Development into Social Use

This module explores the profound technological shifts of the last two centuries and their impact on society. We will specifically examine the **speed at which new technologies are developed, adopted, and integrated into social use**, understanding that this pace has dramatically accelerated.

---

*   **Identifying key drivers and inhibitors of adoption:** What factors influence the speed of technological diffusion?
*   **Analyzing the impact of different technologies:** How have varying adoption speeds affected different aspects of society and business?

### 1. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Technological Change:** The innovation, modification, and diffusion of new technologies. This encompasses the entire lifecycle from invention to widespread societal adoption.
*   **Technology Development:** The process of creating new technologies or improving existing ones. This often involves R&D, prototyping, and testing.
*   **Social Use/Adoption:** The widespread integration and utilization of a technology by individuals, communities, and institutions within a society.
*   **Speed of Introduction/Diffusion:** The rate at which a new technology moves from its initial development or invention to broad societal adoption. This can be measured by the time it takes to reach a certain percentage of the population or market penetration.
*   **Disruptive Innovation (Tushman & Anderson, 2004; Christensen, Burgelman, et al., 2009):** A process whereby a smaller company with fewer resources is able to successfully challenge established incumbent businesses. Often, the innovation is simpler, more convenient, or cheaper, and initially appeals to a niche market. Over time, it improves and displaces established market-leading firms, products, and alliances.
*   **Incremental Innovation (Rastogi, 2009):** Small, ongoing improvements to existing products, services, or processes.
*   **Radical Innovation (Khurana, 2012):** Innovations that create entirely new markets or fundamentally alter existing ones.
*   **Technological Trajectory (Tushman & Anderson, 2004):** The path of technological improvement and innovation along a particular "S-curve" of development.

### 2. Historical Perspective: The Accelerating Pace of Diffusion

The last two centuries have witnessed a dramatic acceleration in the speed at which new technologies are introduced and adopted by society.

#### 2.1 Pre-Industrial Revolution (Pre-18th Century)

*   **Pace:** Very slow. Innovations were often incremental and took generations, if not centuries, to spread beyond their initial location.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **The Plow:** Developed in antiquity, its widespread adoption and improvement took millennia.
    *   **Waterwheel:** Invented in the Roman Empire, its diffusion across Europe was gradual.
*   **Drivers:** Limited communication, transportation, and literacy. Knowledge transfer was primarily oral or through apprenticeships.

#### 2.2 The First Industrial Revolution (Late 18th - Mid-19th Century)

*   **Pace:** Slow but accelerating compared to previous eras.
*   **Key Technologies:** Steam engine, power loom, cotton gin.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Steam Engine (James Watt's improvements, 1770s):** While the concept existed earlier, Watt's efficient design significantly boosted its adoption in factories and transportation. It took several decades for steam power to become dominant in manufacturing.
    *   **Telegraph (Morse, 1830s-1840s):** This was one of the first technologies to show a faster diffusion, with telegraph lines rapidly connecting major cities in the latter half of the 19th century.
*   **Drivers:** Early industrialization, growth of factories, some improvements in transportation (canals, early railways), and growing scientific understanding.
*   **Reference:** Rastogi (2009) discusses the foundational role of these technologies in transforming production.

#### 2.3 The Second Industrial Revolution (Late 19th - Early 20th Century)

*   **Pace:** Significantly faster diffusion due to improved infrastructure and communication.
*   **Key Technologies:** Electricity, telephone, internal combustion engine, mass production (assembly line).
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Electricity:** From its commercialization in the 1880s (Edison's power station), electricity rapidly powered cities and industries. Within 20-30 years, it was a common utility in urban areas.
    *   **Telephone (Bell, 1876):** By the early 20th century, telephones were becoming increasingly common in businesses and affluent homes, connecting people over longer distances than the telegraph.
    *   **Automobile (Ford Model T, 1908):** While initially a luxury item, Henry Ford's assembly line drastically reduced the cost and increased production. The automobile went from a novelty to a common mode of transport within a few decades.
*   **Drivers:** Mass communication (newspapers, magazines), expansion of railways and telegraph networks, rising literacy rates, and increasing scientific and engineering capabilities.
*   **Reference:** Khurana (2012) highlights the impact of mass production on product availability and adoption rates.

#### 2.4 The Information Age / Third Industrial Revolution (Mid-20th Century - Present)

*   **Pace:** Exponential acceleration in diffusion.
*   **Key Technologies:** Transistor, integrated circuit, personal computer, internet, mobile phones, social media.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Transistor (1947):** Initially used in niche applications, its widespread adoption in radios, then computers, took about 15-20 years.
    *   **Personal Computer (Late 1970s/Early 1980s):** From hobbyist machines to household staples, PCs saw rapid adoption throughout the 1980s and 1990s, driven by falling prices and increasing usability.
    *   **Internet (Publicly available 1990s):** The internet's growth was staggering. From a few million users in the early 90s, it reached over a billion by the mid-2000s.
    *   **Mobile Phones/Smartphones:** Mobile phones took off in the 1990s. Smartphones, introduced in the late 2000s (iPhone 2007, Android 2008), achieved near-ubiquitous penetration in many developed nations within a decade.
    *   **Social Media:** Platforms like Facebook and Twitter grew from a few million users to over a billion within 5-10 years of their public launch.
*   **Drivers:** Globalization, digital networks, miniaturization of technology, falling costs, ease of use, network effects, and rapid innovation cycles.
*   **Reference:** Narayanan (2002) and Trott (2009) delve into the management of innovation and new product development in this era, emphasizing rapid cycles. Burgelman, Christensen, et al. (2009) discuss how these technologies often represented disruptive innovations that reshaped industries.

### 3. Factors Influencing the Speed of Technology Introduction into Social Use

The speed of diffusion is not uniform. Several factors influence how quickly a technology is adopted:

#### 3.1 Technology Characteristics:

*   **Relative Advantage (Rogers' Diffusion of Innovations):** How much better is the innovation than the idea it supersedes? (e.g., Smartphones vs. feature phones).
*   **Compatibility:** How consistent is the innovation with existing values, past experiences, and needs of potential adopters? (e.g., Early personal computers needing specialized knowledge vs. user-friendly interfaces).
*   **Simplicity/Complexity:** The easier a technology is to understand and use, the faster it will be adopted.
*   **Trialability:** The degree to which an innovation can be experimented with on a limited basis. (e.g., Free trials of software, test drives of cars).
*   **Observability:** The degree to which the results of an innovation are visible to others. (e.g., Seeing neighbors with new gadgets).
*   **Perceived Risk:** Technologies with lower perceived risks (financial, technical, social) are adopted faster.

#### 3.2 Communication Channels:

*   **Mass Media:** Effective for creating awareness of innovations.
*   **Interpersonal Channels:** Crucial for persuasion and influencing adoption decisions, especially from opinion leaders.
*   **Digital Networks:** Dramatically accelerate information flow and peer-to-peer recommendations.

#### 3.3 Time:

*   **Rate of Adoption:** The speed at which individuals adopt an innovation.
*   **Innovation-Decision Process:** The mental stages through which an individual passes from first hearing about an innovation to forming an attitude towards it, deciding to adopt or reject it, and confirming this decision.

#### 3.4 Social System:

*   **Opinion Leaders:** Individuals who influence the attitudes or behaviour of others.
*   **Change Agents:** Individuals or agencies who attempt to influence innovation-decisions in a desired direction.
*   **Norms and Culture:** Societal acceptance and receptiveness to new ideas.
*   **Economic Factors:** Affordability, government incentives, and market demand.

### 5. Implications of Accelerated Diffusion

*   **Shorter Product Lifecycles:** Companies must innovate continuously to stay competitive.
*   **Increased Competition:** Rapid diffusion means competitors can adopt successful technologies quickly.
*   **Rapid Market Saturation:** Markets can reach saturation faster, requiring companies to find new avenues for growth.
*   **Societal Adaptation Challenges:** Societies need to adapt quickly to new technologies, raising issues of digital divide, privacy, misinformation, and reskilling.
*   **Disruption of Established Industries:** Technologies with rapid adoption can quickly displace incumbent businesses that fail to adapt (Tushman & Anderson, 2004).
*   **New Business Models:** The speed of adoption often necessitates new ways of delivering value and capturing revenue (e.g., subscription services for software).

### 7. Practice Questions with Answers

1.  **Compare and contrast** the speed of diffusion of the telephone in the late 19th century with the speed of diffusion of smartphones in the early 21st century. What factors account for the difference?
    *   **Answer:** The telephone took several decades to achieve significant penetration, while smartphones achieved near-ubiquitous use in many regions within a decade.
        *   **Factors for Telephone's slower diffusion:** Higher cost, required dedicated infrastructure installation for each user, less perceived immediate necessity for average households, limited complementary technologies.
        *   **Factors for Smartphone's faster diffusion:** Falling costs (relative to capability), utilized existing cellular and Wi-Fi infrastructure, offered a convergence of multiple devices (phone, camera, internet browser, GPS), intuitive user interfaces, vast app ecosystems, strong network effects, and rapid marketing.

2.  **Identify a recent technology** (e.g., AI-powered chatbots, electric vehicles) and analyze its current stage of social use. Based on the factors discussed, predict the likely speed of its future adoption and the key challenges or enablers for its widespread use.
    *   **Answer (Example: Electric Vehicles):**
        *   **Current Stage:** Increasing visibility, growing market share in new car sales, but still a relatively small percentage of the total vehicle fleet. Adoption is uneven, concentrated in regions with charging infrastructure and incentives.
        *   **Likely Speed of Future Adoption:** Moderate to fast, potentially accelerating.
        *   **Key Enablers:** Improving battery technology (range, cost), expansion of charging infrastructure, government incentives/regulations, declining battery costs, environmental awareness, increased model availability.
        *   **Key Challenges:** High upfront cost, range anxiety, charging time/availability, grid capacity, consumer education, manufacturing scale-up.

3.  **Explain the concept of "network effects"** and how it contributes to the rapid diffusion of certain technologies, using an example.
    *   **Answer:** Network effects occur when the value of a product or service increases for each new user. As more people join, the system becomes more useful and attractive.
        *   **Example: The Internet/Social Media:** The internet's value increased as more websites and users connected. Social media platforms like Facebook or Instagram become indispensable as more friends and family join. This creates a positive feedback loop, driving rapid adoption.

4.  **According to Rogers' diffusion of innovations framework, what are the five characteristics of an innovation that influence its adoption rate? How do these apply to the internet?**
    *   **Answer:** The five characteristics are:
        1.  **Relative Advantage:** The internet offered superior access to information, communication, and services compared to traditional methods, a significant advantage.
        2.  **Compatibility:** Initially, compatibility was an issue (technical knowledge required). However, as user-friendly interfaces (browsers) emerged, it became more compatible with existing communication needs.
        3.  **Simplicity/Complexity:** Early internet use was complex. However, the development of graphical browsers like Mosaic and Netscape made it significantly simpler and more accessible.
        4.  **Trialability:** Early adopters could experiment with dial-up connections, and later, free email accounts and basic websites allowed for limited trials.
        5.  **Observability:** Seeing others benefit from email, online shopping, or information access encouraged adoption. The visibility of the internet's impact on businesses and communication was immense.

---

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   The pace of technological diffusion has **accelerated dramatically** over the last two centuries.
*   Early technologies like the steam engine diffused over decades or centuries, while modern digital technologies (internet, smartphones) can achieve widespread use within a single decade.
*   **Multiple factors** influence adoption speed, including the technology's own characteristics, communication methods, time, and the social system.
*   **Disruptive innovations** often start by appealing to niche markets and then rapidly expand, changing the competitive landscape.
*   Understanding the speed of adoption is crucial for **technology management, strategy, and policy-making.**
