# Product Design and Innovation: Module 2 - Creativity Techniques: Creative Thinking

## Topic: Conceptual Design: Generation, Selection & Embodiment of Concept

---

### 1. Introduction to Conceptual Design

**Definition:** Conceptual design is the phase of product development where a set of product concepts is generated, evaluated, and refined. It's about exploring a broad range of potential solutions to the identified customer needs and product requirements. This phase is crucial for setting the direction of the entire product development process.

**Importance:**
*   **Explores a wide solution space:** Prevents premature convergence on a single idea.
*   **Reduces risk:** Identifies potential flaws and challenges early on.
*   **Foundation for later stages:** A well-defined concept guides detailed design and implementation.
*   **Aligns with CO2:** Directly contributes to developing knowledge of robust and conceptual design.

**Textbook Reference:** Ulrich & Eppinger (2003) emphasize conceptual design as a critical early phase where the "greatest impact on the product's success can be made with the least investment."

---

### 2. Concept Generation: Creating the Ideas

This phase focuses on generating a large quantity and variety of potential product concepts. The goal is quantity over quality at this stage.

#### 2.1. Understanding the Need for Diverse Concepts

*   **Avoids "first good idea syndrome":** Prevents designers from settling on the initial, possibly suboptimal, solution.
*   **Maximizes chances of innovation:** Broader exploration increases the likelihood of discovering breakthrough ideas.
*   **Addresses different user needs and contexts:** Solutions might appeal to different market segments or usage scenarios.

#### 2.2. Techniques for Concept Generation

**a) Brainstorming:**
*   **Definition:** A group creativity technique involving spontaneous generation of ideas in a free-wheeling manner.
*   **Principles:**
    *   **Defer judgment:** No criticism allowed during idea generation.
    *   **Encourage wild ideas:** The more unusual, the better.
    *   **Build on others' ideas:** Combine and improve upon existing suggestions.
    *   **Go for quantity:** Aim for a large number of ideas.
    *   **Stay focused:** Keep the discussion on the problem at hand.
*   **Example:** A team designing a new backpack might brainstorm ideas like: self-heating fabric, integrated charging, modular compartments, anti-gravity suspension, biometric security.

**b) Brainwriting (e.g., 6-3-5 Method):**
*   **Definition:** A more structured brainstorming method where participants write down ideas and pass them around for others to build upon.
*   **Process (6-3-5):** 6 participants, each writes 3 ideas in 5 minutes. Ideas are passed to the next person, who adds 3 more ideas. This continues until everyone has contributed to everyone else's ideas.
*   **Benefits:** Encourages quieter members to contribute, reduces dominance by a few individuals.

**c) SCAMPER Method:**
*   **Definition:** A mnemonic checklist of action verbs that prompt creative thinking about existing products or problems.
*   **Categories:**
    *   **Substitute:** What can be replaced?
    *   **Combine:** What can be combined?
    *   **Adapt:** What can be adapted?
    *   **Modify/Magnify/Minify:** What can be changed (size, shape, color, etc.)?
    *   **Put to another use:** What else can it be used for?
    *   **Eliminate:** What can be removed or simplified?
    *   **Reverse/Rearrange:** What can be inverted or reordered?
*   **Example:** For a coffee mug:
    *   **Substitute:** Replace ceramic with recycled plastic.
    *   **Combine:** Combine with a built-in coaster.
    *   **Adapt:** Adapt the shape for better grip in extreme temperatures.
    *   **Modify:** Magnify the handle for easier holding.
    *   **Put to another use:** Use as a planter.
    *   **Eliminate:** Eliminate the handle for a minimalist design.
    *   **Reverse:** Make the mug stackable from the bottom up.

**d) Analogous Systems:**
*   **Definition:** Looking at solutions in unrelated fields or natural systems that address similar problems.
*   **Example:** Studying how birds' wings generate lift to design better airplane wings, or how natural materials self-repair to design self-healing materials.
*   **Reference Book:** Otto & Wood (2000) discuss drawing analogies from natural systems and other industries as a powerful source of inspiration.

**e) Mind Mapping:**
*   **Definition:** A visual tool for organizing thoughts and ideas around a central topic.
*   **Process:** Start with a central idea, then branch out with related sub-ideas.
*   **Benefits:** Helps in exploring connections and generating a wide range of related concepts.

**f) Morphological Analysis:**
*   **Definition:** A systematic method for generating concepts by identifying key attributes of a product and then considering variations for each attribute.
*   **Process:** Create a grid where rows represent product attributes and columns represent variations for each attribute. Each cell represents a potential concept.
*   **Example:** For a desk lamp:
    | Attribute       | Option 1          | Option 2       | Option 3        |
    | :-------------- | :------------------ | :--------------- | :---------------- |
    | Light Source    | LED               | Incandescent     | Halogen           |
    | Arm Type        | Flexible Gooseneck | Articulated      | Spring-loaded     |
    | Base            | Weighted          | Clamp-on         | Wall-mounted      |
    | Power Source    | AC Adapter        | Battery          | USB               |
*   Combining options (e.g., LED + Flexible Gooseneck + Clamp-on + USB) creates a specific concept.

#### 2.3. Documenting Concepts

*   **Concept Sketching:** Simple drawings to convey the basic form and function.
*   **Concept Description:** Written explanations of the idea, its features, and benefits.
*   **Feature Lists:** Detailed lists of what the concept offers.

**Important Point to Remember:** The goal of concept generation is breadth. Don't filter or judge ideas at this stage.

### 3. Concept Selection: Choosing the Best Ideas

Once a wide range of concepts has been generated, the next step is to select the most promising ones for further development. This involves evaluation and decision-making.

#### 3.1. Criteria for Concept Evaluation

*   **Technical Feasibility:** Can the concept be manufactured with current technology?
*   **Market Appeal:** Will customers want this product?
*   **Economic Feasibility:** Is the concept profitable?
*   **Fit with Company Strategy:** Does it align with the company's goals and capabilities?
*   **External Factors:** Regulatory compliance, environmental impact, etc.

#### 3.2. Methods for Concept Selection

**a) Concept Screening Matrix:**
*   **Definition:** A simple matrix that uses a set of evaluation criteria to score and rank concepts.
*   **Process:**
    1.  List key evaluation criteria.
    2.  Assign a weight to each criterion based on its importance.
    3.  Score each concept against each criterion (e.g., on a scale of 1-5).
    4.  Calculate a weighted score for each concept (Score * Weight).
    5.  Rank concepts based on their total weighted scores.
*   **Example:**

    | Criteria        | Weight | Concept A Score (1-5) | Concept A Weighted | Concept B Score (1-5) | Concept B Weighted |
    | :-------------- | :----- | :-------------------- | :----------------- | :-------------------- | :----------------- |
    | Market Appeal   | 4      | 4                     | 16                 | 3                     | 12                 |
    | Technical Feas. | 3      | 5                     | 15                 | 4                     | 12                 |
    | Profitability   | 3      | 3                     | 9                  | 5                     | 15                 |
    | **Total**       | **10** |                       | **40**             |                       | **39**             |

    In this example, Concept A is slightly preferred over Concept B based on these criteria.

**b) Pugh Concept Selection Method (Controlled Convergence):**
*   **Definition:** A structured decision-making process that compares concepts against a baseline or "datum" concept.
*   **Process:**
    1.  Select a datum concept (either the current product, a competitor's product, or an internal benchmark).
    2.  Identify key engineering and customer-focused criteria.
    3.  For each criterion, compare each new concept to the datum:
        *   '+' if the concept is better than the datum.
        *   '0' if the concept is about the same as the datum.
        *   '-' if the concept is worse than the datum.
    4.  Sum the '+' and '-' scores for each concept. A concept with more '+' than '-' is generally preferred.
*   **Benefits:** Provides a systematic way to compare multiple concepts against a benchmark, leading to focused refinement.

**c) Decision Trees:**
*   **Definition:** A graphical representation of decisions and their potential outcomes, used to analyze choices under uncertainty.
*   **Use in Concept Selection:** Can help evaluate concepts based on their potential success, risk, and required investment at different stages.

**d) Voice of the Customer (VOC):**
*   **Definition:** Gathering and analyzing customer feedback to understand their needs, preferences, and pain points.
*   **Application:** Used to inform evaluation criteria and assess how well concepts meet customer expectations.
*   **Aligns with CO1:** Understanding customer needs is fundamental to defining product lifecycle and development.

**Important Point to Remember:** Concept selection is a critical stage. It's important to use objective criteria and involve multiple stakeholders to avoid bias.

### 4. Concept Embodiment: Developing the Chosen Concepts

Once a few promising concepts are selected, they need to be fleshed out into more detailed descriptions and preliminary designs. This is concept embodiment.

#### 4.1. Definition and Purpose

*   **Definition:** The process of translating selected concepts into more concrete and detailed descriptions of the product. This involves refining the concept and adding detail about its form, function, and key features.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   To make the concept tangible and understandable.
    *   To identify key technical challenges and opportunities.
    *   To provide a basis for more detailed design and prototyping.

#### 4.2. Key Activities in Concept Embodiment

**a) Detailed Sketching and Modeling:**
*   **3D CAD Models:** Creating digital representations of the product.
*   **Physical Models (Mock-ups):** Building rough physical prototypes to test ergonomics, aesthetics, and basic functionality.
*   **Reference Book:** Otto & Wood (2000) highlight the role of physical and digital modeling in visualizing and refining concepts.

**b) Feature Definition:**
*   **Functionality Breakdown:** Detailing the specific functions the product will perform.
*   **User Interface (UI) / User Experience (UX) Design:** Defining how users will interact with the product.

**c) Technical Specifications:**
*   **Material Selection:** Identifying potential materials based on performance, cost, and availability.
*   **Performance Targets:** Defining measurable goals for aspects like speed, power, durability, etc.

**d) Feasibility Studies:**
*   **Prototyping:** Building functional prototypes to test key aspects of the design.
*   **Simulation:** Using computer-aided engineering (CAE) tools to test performance under various conditions.
*   **Aligns with CO2:** This stage directly leads to understanding robust design principles as concepts are tested for performance and reliability.

**e) Refinement based on Feedback:**
*   **Customer Reviews:** Presenting embodied concepts to potential customers for feedback.
*   **Internal Reviews:** Seeking input from engineering, marketing, and manufacturing teams.

**f) Identifying Key Technologies:**
*   Determining if any new or unproven technologies are required for the concept to succeed.

#### 4.3. Documentation of Embodied Concepts

*   **Concept Development Documents (CDDs):** Comprehensive documents detailing the chosen concept, including specifications, drawings, marketing rationale, and business case.
*   **Product Design Specifications (PDS):** A detailed document outlining all the requirements the product must meet.

**Important Point to Remember:** Concept embodiment transforms abstract ideas into tangible possibilities. It's an iterative process of refinement and validation.

### 6. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
A team is brainstorming ideas for a new smart home device. They are adhering to the principle of "defer judgment." What does this principle mean in the context of their brainstorming session?

**Answer 1:**
"Defer judgment" means that during the brainstorming session, the team should not criticize or evaluate any ideas presented, no matter how wild or impractical they may seem initially. The focus is purely on generating a large quantity of ideas without interruption or negative feedback. Evaluation and filtering come later.

**Question 2:**
You are using the Pugh Concept Selection method to compare three new concepts (A, B, C) against a baseline product (Datum). For the criterion "Battery Life," Concept A is better than the Datum, Concept B is about the same, and Concept C is worse. How would you represent this using the Pugh method notation?

**Answer 2:**
*   Concept A: +
*   Concept B: 0
*   Concept C: -

**Question 3:**
What is the primary goal of the concept generation phase in product development?
(a) To select the best concept immediately.
(b) To generate a wide variety of potential solutions.
(c) To detail the final product specifications.
(d) To conduct market research.

**Answer 3:**
(b) To generate a wide variety of potential solutions.

**Question 4:**
Explain the SCAMPER technique and provide one example of how it could be applied to improve a bicycle.

**Answer 4:**
SCAMPER is a mnemonic checklist of action verbs (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse/Rearrange) used to prompt creative thinking about existing products or problems.

Example Application (Bicycle):
*   **Modify (Magnify):** Magnify the tire width for better traction on rough terrain.
*   **Combine:** Combine the bicycle with an electric motor for assisted pedaling.
*   **Substitute:** Substitute traditional spokes with carbon fiber composite structures for lighter weight.

**Question 5:**
Why is it important to consider technical feasibility and market appeal during concept selection, even if the goal of generation was quantity?

**Answer 5:**
While concept generation prioritizes quantity and diversity, concept selection must be guided by practical considerations. Technical feasibility ensures the concept can actually be built, while market appeal ensures there is a customer demand for it. Ignoring these factors early on would lead to wasted resources developing unviable ideas. A concept may be innovative but if it's impossible to manufacture or no one wants to buy it, it will not be successful.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **The "Front-End" is Crucial:** The conceptual design phase has the greatest impact on a product's success. Spend time and resources here.
*   **Quantity Breeds Quality:** Don't shy away from generating many ideas. The best ideas often emerge from a broad exploration.
*   **Structure is Key:** While creative thinking can be free-wheeling, structured methods for generation, selection, and embodiment are essential for progress.
*   **Customer Focus:** Always keep the customer's needs and desires at the forefront during all stages.
*   **Cross-Functional Teams:** Involve diverse perspectives (engineering, marketing, design, manufacturing) in all phases of conceptual design.
*   **Iteration:** Conceptual design is rarely linear. Be prepared to revisit earlier stages as new information is gained.
*   **Documentation Matters:** Clearly document your concepts, decisions, and rationale for future reference and development.
