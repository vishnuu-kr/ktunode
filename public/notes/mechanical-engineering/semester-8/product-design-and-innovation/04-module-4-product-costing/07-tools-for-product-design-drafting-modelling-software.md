# Product Design and Innovation: Module 4 - Product Costing

## Topic: Tools for Product Design – Drafting / Modelling Software

**Knowledge Level (K2):** This module focuses on understanding and recalling concepts related to product costing and the tools used in product design.

---

### Introduction to Drafting and Modeling Software in Product Design

In the realm of Product Design and Innovation, the transition from a concept to a tangible product involves a series of critical steps. Product costing, as explored in this module, is intrinsically linked to the design process. The tools we use during design directly influence the cost of a product by impacting material selection, manufacturing processes, and assembly. Drafting and modeling software are fundamental tools that facilitate this process, enabling designers to visualize, refine, and document their ideas.

**Key Concept:** **Product Costing** is the process of identifying and quantifying all costs associated with bringing a product to market, including design, development, manufacturing, marketing, and distribution.

**Key Concept:** **Drafting** refers to the creation of technical drawings and specifications that communicate design intent.

**Key Concept:** **Modeling Software** (often referred to as CAD - Computer-Aided Design) allows for the creation of 2D and 3D digital representations of products.

---

### Learning Outcome 1: Understand the role of drafting and modeling software in product development.

Drafting and modeling software play a crucial role throughout the product development lifecycle, impacting various stages and contributing to effective product costing.

*   **Conceptualization & Idea Generation:**
    *   **Sketching and Basic 2D Drafting:** Quickly capture initial ideas and explore different forms.
    *   **3D Modeling:** Visualize concepts in three dimensions, allowing for better understanding of spatial relationships and aesthetics.
    *   **Impact on Costing:** Early identification of potential design flaws or expensive material choices can prevent costly revisions later.
*   **Product Design & Refinement:**
    *   **Detailed 2D Drawings:** Create precise dimensions, tolerances, and manufacturing notes for components.
    *   **Advanced 3D Modeling:** Develop complex geometries, perform virtual assembly, and simulate functionality.
    *   **Parametric Modeling:** Design changes can be easily propagated through the model, saving significant time and effort.
    *   **Impact on Costing:** Precise design ensures that manufacturing processes are efficient, minimizing material waste and labor costs.
*   **Prototyping & Testing:**
    *   **Generating Files for Rapid Prototyping (3D Printing):** CAD models are directly used to create physical prototypes.
    *   **Virtual Simulation:** Analyze product performance under various conditions (stress, thermal, fluid dynamics) before physical testing.
    *   **Impact on Costing:** Reduces the need for multiple expensive physical prototypes and allows for early identification of performance issues that could lead to costly redesigns.
*   **Manufacturing & Production:**
    *   **Generating Manufacturing Instructions (CAM - Computer-Aided Manufacturing):** CAD data is used to program CNC machines.
    *   **Tooling Design:** Design molds, dies, and fixtures based on CAD models.
    *   **Impact on Costing:** Accurate digital data ensures smooth production, reducing errors, scrap, and downtime.
*   **Documentation & Communication:**
    *   **Bills of Materials (BOM):** Often generated directly from CAD models, listing all components and their quantities.
    *   **Technical Manuals and User Guides:** 3D models can be used to create clear and informative visuals.
    *   **Impact on Costing:** Clear documentation minimizes misinterpretations, leading to fewer errors in sourcing, assembly, and maintenance, thus reducing overall cost.

**Textbook Reference (Ulrich & Eppinger, 2003):** Chapter 6, "Product Specifications," highlights the importance of detailed design documents created using CAD for defining product requirements and guiding the development process, which directly impacts cost estimation.

**Example:** A designer uses SolidWorks to create a 3D model of a new smartphone casing. They can then use this model to:
    *   Visualize how the screen, battery, and other components will fit.
    *   Generate precise 2D drawings for manufacturing the injection mold.
    *   Export a file for 3D printing a prototype to test ergonomics.
    *   Create a Bill of Materials for cost estimation.

### Learning Outcome 2: Explore different types of drafting and modeling software.

The landscape of design software is diverse, with tools catering to different needs and levels of complexity.

#### 2.1 Drafting Software (Primarily 2D CAD)

Historically, drafting software was focused on creating 2D technical drawings. While 3D modeling has largely taken over for complex product design, 2D drafting remains valuable for specific applications.

*   **Key Functions:**
    *   Creating technical drawings with precise dimensions, lines, shapes, and annotations.
    *   Generating manufacturing blueprints.
    *   Detailing assemblies and sub-assemblies.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **AutoCAD:** A long-standing industry standard for 2D drafting and design.
    *   **DraftSight:** A more affordable alternative for 2D CAD.
    *   **CorelDRAW (for technical illustration):** While not strictly CAD, it's used for creating visually appealing technical illustrations.

#### 2.2 Modeling Software (3D CAD)

3D CAD software is the cornerstone of modern product design, enabling the creation of virtual prototypes with high fidelity.

*   **Key Functions:**
    *   **Parametric Modeling:** Designs are driven by parameters and constraints, allowing for easy modification and automation of design changes. (e.g., changing a diameter automatically updates related features).
    *   **Direct Modeling:** Manipulates geometry directly without relying on a history of operations. Useful for editing imported geometry or making quick, non-parametric changes.
    *   **Surface Modeling:** Creating complex, freeform shapes often used in aesthetic design or for complex aerodynamic surfaces.
    *   **Solid Modeling:** Creating objects with volume and mass, defined by their boundaries. This is the most common type for mechanical design.
    *   **Assembly Modeling:** Combining multiple individual parts into a complete product, defining relationships and constraints between them.
    *   **Simulation & Analysis (often integrated or add-ons):**
        *   **Finite Element Analysis (FEA):** Simulating stress, strain, and deformation.
        *   **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD):** Simulating fluid flow and heat transfer.
        *   **Motion Analysis:** Simulating the movement and interaction of assembly components.
    *   **Rendering:** Creating photorealistic images of the product for visualization and marketing.
    *   **Generating Manufacturing Data:** Exporting files for CAM, 3D printing (STL, STEP), and other manufacturing processes.

*   **Types of 3D CAD Software:**
    *   **Full-Featured Professional Suites:**
        *   **SolidWorks:** Widely used in mechanical engineering and product design, known for its user-friendliness and robust capabilities.
        *   **Autodesk Inventor:** Similar to SolidWorks, offering a comprehensive suite for mechanical design and simulation.
        *   **PTC Creo (formerly Pro/ENGINEER):** A powerful, parametric modeling solution with strong capabilities in large assemblies and complex surfacing.
        *   **CATIA:** High-end software used extensively in automotive and aerospace industries for its advanced surfacing and integrated PLM (Product Lifecycle Management) capabilities.
        *   **Siemens NX:** Another high-end solution offering integrated CAD, CAM, and CAE capabilities.
    *   **More Specialized or Entry-Level Options:**
        *   **Fusion 360:** Cloud-based, integrated CAD/CAM/CAE software popular among startups and hobbyists, offering a subscription model.
        *   **Onshape:** Fully cloud-based CAD system, facilitating collaboration.
        *   **Rhino:** Popular for its advanced surface modeling capabilities, often used in industrial design and architecture.
        *   **SketchUp:** Known for its ease of use for conceptual design and architectural visualization.

**Reference Book Reference (Otto & Wood, 2000):** Chapter 5, "Product Representation and Modeling," discusses various methods of representing product designs, including 2D drawings and 3D models, and their implications for communication and analysis.

**Important Point to Remember:** The choice of software often depends on the complexity of the product, the industry, budget, and the need for integration with other systems (like CAM or PLM).

### Learning Outcome 3: Understand how these tools facilitate design for manufacturing and assembly (DFMA).

DFMA principles are crucial for designing cost-effective and efficient products. Drafting and modeling software are powerful enablers of DFMA.

*   **Design for Manufacturing (DFM):** Focuses on designing products to be easily and economically manufactured.
    *   **How Software Helps:**
        *   **Material Selection:** 3D models can be associated with material properties, allowing for early cost estimation and performance analysis. Designers can quickly swap materials to see cost impacts.
        *   **Process Selection:** CAD software can incorporate manufacturing process constraints. For example, the software might flag features that are difficult or expensive to machine (e.g., deep, narrow pockets).
        *   **Tolerance Analysis:** Detailed drawings with GD&T (Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing) specify manufacturing tolerances. Software can help analyze the impact of these tolerances on assembly and function.
        *   **Draft Angle Analysis (for molded parts):** Software can automatically check if sufficient draft angles are present on parts intended for injection molding or die casting, preventing ejection problems.
        *   **Wall Thickness Analysis:** Helps ensure uniform wall thickness in molded parts to avoid warping or sink marks, which can increase scrap and costs.
        *   **Machining Simulation (CAM integration):** Simulating machining toolpaths before production can identify collisions or inefficient cuts, optimizing machining time and reducing tooling wear.

*   **Design for Assembly (DFA):** Focuses on designing products to be easily and economically assembled.
    *   **How Software Helps:**
        *   **Virtual Assembly:** Assembling components digitally allows designers to identify potential interference issues, access problems for tools, and ergonomic challenges during assembly.
        *   **Minimizing Part Count:** 3D modeling allows for the integration of multiple functions into a single part, reducing the total number of components, fasteners, and assembly steps.
        *   **Standardization:** CAD libraries can store standard components (screws, bolts, connectors), promoting their use and simplifying inventory and assembly.
        *   **Ease of Handling:** Designers can visualize how parts will be grasped and manipulated by assemblers (or robots), identifying awkward shapes or sizes.
        *   **Fixture Design:** CAD models can be used to design assembly fixtures that simplify the placement and holding of parts.

**Textbook Reference (Ulrich & Eppinger, 2003):** Chapter 11, "Design for Manufacturing," extensively discusses how design choices impact manufacturing costs. CAD tools are implicitly used to implement these principles by enabling detailed analysis of geometries and tolerances.

**Example:** A product designer is creating a new electronic device enclosure.
    *   **DFM:** Using SolidWorks, they perform a draft angle analysis on the plastic housing. They also use wall thickness analysis to ensure the plastic can be molded consistently. They check for features that might require expensive machining operations.
    *   **DFA:** They assemble all the internal components (PCB, battery, screen) virtually. They discover that a specific screw location is difficult to access with a screwdriver. They reposition the screw and update the design, preventing assembly line issues and labor cost increases. They also realize two separate brackets can be combined into one, reducing part count by one.

### Learning Outcome 4: Understand the use of value engineering in product design through these tools.

Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic method to improve the "value" of goods or products and services by examining function. Value is defined as the ratio of function to cost. If the ratio can be improved by either improving function, reducing cost, or both, value is increased.

*   **How Software Supports Value Engineering:**
    *   **Function Analysis:** While software doesn't directly perform function analysis (which is a conceptual process), the clear visualization provided by 3D models aids in understanding how each component contributes to the product's overall function.
    *   **Cost Reduction through Design Optimization:**
        *   **Material Substitution:** Easily create alternative designs with different materials and compare their estimated costs and performance (e.g., using a lighter but stronger plastic instead of metal).
        *   **Simplification:** Identifying opportunities to combine parts, eliminate unnecessary features, or use standard components more effectively through virtual assembly and design exploration.
        *   **Process Improvement:** Analyzing the manufacturability of different design iterations can lead to selecting more cost-effective manufacturing processes.
    *   **Virtual Prototyping and Testing:** Reduces the cost and time associated with physical prototyping for VE studies. Designers can quickly test functional improvements or cost-saving design changes virtually.
    *   **Cost Estimation Integration:** Many CAD systems can integrate with cost estimation tools or have built-in cost estimators based on material, volume, and manufacturing processes. This allows designers to quantify the cost impact of VE-driven design changes.
    *   **Collaboration:** Cloud-based CAD platforms facilitate collaboration among cross-functional teams (design, manufacturing, procurement) to brainstorm and evaluate VE opportunities.

**Reference Book Reference (Otto & Wood, 2000):** Chapter 10, "Value Engineering," discusses the systematic approach to improving value. While the book focuses on the principles, the use of CAD tools is implied as essential for implementing many VE suggestions, such as redesigning components for cost reduction or functional improvement.

**Example:** A team is tasked with reducing the cost of an existing product.
    *   Using the 3D CAD model of the product, they identify a complex machined metal bracket that is expensive to produce.
    *   Through brainstorming (supported by the visualization of the part's function), they consider replacing it with a plastic injection-molded part.
    *   They create a new design for the bracket in CAD, ensuring it meets the strength requirements through FEA simulation.
    *   They then compare the estimated manufacturing cost of the plastic part with the metal part directly within their CAD environment or integrated costing tools. This analysis shows a significant cost saving, confirming the VE initiative's success.

### Learning Outcome 5: Understand how ergonomics and rapid prototyping are incorporated using these tools.

These advanced aspects of product design are heavily reliant on modern modeling software and associated technologies.

#### 5.1 Incorporating Ergonomics

Ergonomics is the study of how people interact with products, aiming to optimize user comfort, safety, and efficiency.

*   **How Software Helps:**
    *   **3D Human Body Models:** Many CAD systems allow for the import or creation of digital human models (manikins).
    *   **Reach and Clearance Analysis:** These manikins can be used to simulate human reach envelopes, check for clearance issues for hands or tools during operation or assembly, and assess visibility.
    *   **Posture and Comfort Analysis:** Designers can evaluate how a user's posture might be affected by the product's design, ensuring comfort for prolonged use.
    *   **Hand Grip and Interface Design:** Detailed 3D modeling allows for the precise shaping of handles, buttons, and controls for optimal grip and ease of use.
    *   **Virtual Mock-ups:** Creating virtual mock-ups allows for early ergonomic evaluations by a wider team, including potential users, before any physical prototypes are made.
    *   **Motion Analysis:** Simulate the motions a user would perform while interacting with the product to identify awkward or strenuous movements.

**Textbook Reference (Ulrich & Eppinger, 2003):** While not a dedicated chapter on ergonomics in relation to specific software, the principles discussed in "Product Specifications" (Chapter 6) would involve defining ergonomic requirements, which are then realized and validated through modeling software.

#### 5.2 Incorporating Rapid Prototyping

Rapid prototyping (RP), also known as Additive Manufacturing, is a process of creating physical models directly from digital designs.

*   **How Software Helps:**
    *   **STL File Generation:** The most common output format for 3D printing is the STL (stereolithography) file, which represents the 3D model as a mesh of triangles. CAD software has built-in functionality to export models in this format.
    *   **Design for Manufacturability (for RP):** Designers can modify their CAD models to optimize them for specific RP processes (e.g., ensuring sufficient wall thickness for FDM, adding support structures, or orienting parts for better surface finish).
    *   **Iterative Design:** The ability to quickly generate physical prototypes allows for rapid iteration. A designer can print a part, test it, identify flaws, modify the CAD model, and print a new version within hours or days.
    *   **Functional Prototyping:** High-fidelity prototypes can be printed using advanced materials and technologies, allowing for functional testing of critical aspects of the design.
    *   **Fit and Form Testing:** Prototypes are invaluable for checking how different components fit together and how the overall product feels and looks in the real world.

**Reference Book Reference (Otto & Wood, 2000):** Chapter 6, "Rapid Prototyping and Tooling," directly addresses the role of CAD models in generating data for RP technologies. It highlights how these models are translated into printable files.

**Example:** A team is developing a new power tool.
    *   **Ergonomics:** Using SolidWorks, they create a digital manikin and position it to simulate holding the power tool. They adjust the grip shape and button placement in the CAD model to ensure comfortable and safe operation, considering different hand sizes.
    *   **Rapid Prototyping:** They export the CAD model of the new grip design as an STL file. This file is sent to a 3D printer, which produces a physical prototype of the grip in a few hours. The team can then hold this prototype, test the grip, and refine the design based on this tangible feedback, all within a single day.

### Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
Which of the following software types is primarily used for creating technical drawings with precise dimensions and annotations?
a) 3D Modeling Software
b) CAM Software
c) 2D Drafting Software
d) Simulation Software

**Answer:** c) 2D Drafting Software

**Question 2:**
Explain how parametric modeling in 3D CAD software can contribute to cost reduction during the product development process.

**Answer:** Parametric modeling allows designers to define relationships and constraints between design features. If a key parameter (e.g., a diameter, a length) needs to be changed, the software automatically updates all related features and dimensions. This significantly reduces the time and effort required for design modifications, preventing costly redesign cycles and allowing for faster response to market feedback or manufacturing constraints. It also aids in exploring design variations quickly, which can lead to identifying more cost-effective solutions.

**Question 3:**
A product designer is using CAD software to design a part that will be injection molded. What specific analysis might they perform within the software to improve manufacturability and reduce potential defects?

**Answer:** The designer might perform a **draft angle analysis** to ensure sufficient angles are present for easy removal from the mold, preventing sticking and damage. They might also conduct a **wall thickness analysis** to identify areas with too thin or too thick walls, which can lead to warping, sink marks, or incomplete filling of the mold, all of which increase scrap rates and costs.

**Question 4:**
How does the virtual assembly capability of 3D CAD software support Design for Assembly (DFA)?

**Answer:** Virtual assembly allows designers to digitally piece together all the components of a product. This helps identify potential issues such as:
    *   **Interference:** Parts occupying the same space.
    *   **Accessibility:** Difficulty in reaching fasteners or components for assembly or maintenance.
    *   **Tool Clearance:** Ensuring assembly tools can access the required areas.
    *   **Ergonomics:** Evaluating how easy it is for a human assembler to handle and place parts.
By resolving these issues digitally, designers can create products that are easier, faster, and cheaper to assemble, reducing labor costs and the likelihood of assembly errors.

**Question 5:**
You are designing a new handle for an appliance. How can you use 3D modeling software to incorporate ergonomic principles?

**Answer:** You can use the software to:
    *   Create a detailed 3D model of the handle, focusing on its shape, texture, and grip.
    *   Import or create digital human hand models (manikins) of various sizes.
    *   Use these manikins to simulate how different users would grip the handle, checking for comfortable and secure contact points.
    *   Analyze the overall reach and posture required to operate controls associated with the handle.
    *   Perform virtual fit tests to ensure the handle feels natural and comfortable in hand.

### Key Points to Remember

*   **Integral to Product Development:** Drafting and modeling software are not just visualization tools; they are critical for communication, analysis, and driving manufacturing processes throughout the product lifecycle.
*   **Impact on Cost:** Early design decisions made using these tools have a significant impact on product cost by influencing material selection, manufacturing processes, assembly efficiency, and prototype expenditure.
*   **DFMA Enablers:** Software features like draft analysis, wall thickness analysis, and virtual assembly are essential for implementing Design for Manufacturing and Assembly principles.
*   **Value Engineering Support:** These tools facilitate VE by enabling rapid design iteration, material substitution analysis, and cost estimation of design changes.
*   **Ergonomics and RP Foundation:** Advanced product development aspects like ergonomics and rapid prototyping rely heavily on the capabilities of 3D modeling software for visualization, simulation, and data output.
*   **Evolving Technology:** The capabilities of CAD software are continuously advancing, with integrated simulation, AI-driven design, and cloud-based collaboration becoming increasingly important.
