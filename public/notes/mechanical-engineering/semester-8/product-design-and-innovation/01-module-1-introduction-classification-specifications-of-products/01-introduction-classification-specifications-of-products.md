# Product Design and Innovation: Module 1 - Introduction: Classification/Specifications of Products

## 1. Introduction to Product Design and Innovation

This module provides a foundational understanding of what constitutes a product and how products are classified and specified. This knowledge is crucial for all subsequent stages of product development, from conceptualization to implementation.

### 1.1 What is a Product?

A product is anything that can be offered to a market to satisfy a want or need. It can be a physical good, a service, an idea, an experience, or a combination of these.

*   **Key Concept:** Products are created to solve problems or fulfill desires for target customers.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Physical Goods:** Smartphone, car, chair, book
    *   **Services:** Haircut, banking, software subscription, consulting
    *   **Ideas/Concepts:** A new business model, a social campaign
    *   **Experiences:** A theme park visit, a concert
*   **Ulrich & Eppinger (2003):** Emphasize that a product is the output of a development process, designed to meet customer needs and market opportunities. They differentiate between a "product" and a "service" but acknowledge the growing trend of "product-service systems" where tangible products are bundled with intangible services.

### 1.2 The Importance of Product Classification and Specification

Understanding how to classify and specify products is fundamental for:

*   **Effective Communication:** Provides a common language for design teams, engineers, marketing, and manufacturing.
*   **Organized Development:** Helps in structuring the product development process.
*   **Competitive Analysis:** Allows for benchmarking against existing products.
*   **Targeting Markets:** Enables the identification of specific customer segments.
*   **Setting Design Goals:** Defines the performance and quality targets for the product.

### 1.3 Classification of Products

Products can be classified in various ways, each offering a different perspective on their nature and market.

#### 1.3.1 Classification by Tangibility

*   **Tangible Products (Goods):** Physical objects that can be touched, seen, and stored.
    *   **Examples:** Electronics, appliances, furniture.
    *   **Ulrich & Eppinger (2003):** Focus heavily on the development of tangible products, outlining the stages and processes involved.
*   **Intangible Products (Services):** Activities or benefits that are primarily non-physical and are consumed at the time they are produced.
    *   **Examples:** Consulting, legal advice, healthcare.
    *   **Note:** While this module focuses on product design, understanding the service aspect is crucial as many products are now integrated with services.

#### 1.3.2 Classification by Consumer Behavior/Purchasing Habits

*   **Consumer Products:** Products purchased by individuals or households for their own personal use.
    *   **Convenience Products:** Purchased frequently with minimal comparison and effort.
        *   **Examples:** Toothpaste, newspapers, fast food.
    *   **Shopping Products:** Purchased less frequently, where consumers compare carefully on suitability, quality, price, and style.
        *   **Examples:** Furniture, clothing, major appliances.
    *   **Specialty Products:** Products with unique characteristics or brand identification for which a significant group of buyers is willing to make a special purchase effort.
        *   **Examples:** Specific brands of luxury cars, designer clothing, high-end photography equipment.
    *   **Unsought Products:** Products that consumers either do not normally think of buying or do not know about.
        *   **Examples:** Life insurance, funeral services, new technologies before widespread awareness.
*   **Industrial Products (Business-to-Business - B2B):** Products purchased by organizations for use in their operations, to produce other goods or services, or for resale.
    *   **Raw Materials:** Products used in the manufacturing process without further processing.
        *   **Examples:** Coal, timber, agricultural products.
    *   **Component Parts:** Finished goods that become part of the buyer's product.
        *   **Examples:** Engines for cars, processors for computers.
    *   **Capital Items:** Major equipment and accessory equipment used in the production process.
        *   **Examples:** Factory machinery, computers, office furniture.
    *   **Supplies and Services:** Operational supplies (e.g., lubricants, stationery) and business services (e.g., legal services, maintenance).

#### 1.3.3 Classification by Durability and Tangibility

*   **Nondurable Goods (Soft Goods):** Tangible products consumed quickly, typically over one or a few uses.
    *   **Examples:** Food, beverages, soap.
*   **Durable Goods (Hard Goods):** Tangible products that typically last for an extended period, often requiring more effort in the purchase decision.
    *   **Examples:** Refrigerators, cars, furniture.

#### 1.3.4 Classification by Technology and Complexity

*   **Commodity Products:** Standardized products with little differentiation between brands.
    *   **Examples:** Sugar, salt, basic chemicals.
*   **Differentiated Products:** Products with unique features, branding, or quality that distinguish them from competitors.
    *   **Examples:** Smartphones, cars, software.
*   **High-Technology Products:** Products characterized by rapid innovation and frequent updates.
    *   **Examples:** Consumer electronics, software, biotechnology.
*   **Low-Technology Products:** Products with stable technologies and slower innovation cycles.
    *   **Examples:** Basic tools, simple furniture, agricultural implements.

#### 1.3.5 Classification by Purpose or Function

*   **Functional Products:** Designed primarily for performance and utility.
    *   **Examples:** Tools, machinery, laboratory equipment.
*   **Innovative Products:** Designed to offer novel features, user experiences, or solutions to unmet needs.
    *   **Examples:** Smart home devices, wearable technology, virtual reality headsets.

*   **Important Point to Remember:** These classifications are not mutually exclusive. A product can fit into multiple categories simultaneously (e.g., a smartphone is a tangible, durable, differentiated, high-technology consumer product).

### 1.4 Product Specifications

Product specifications are precise, measurable descriptions of the required characteristics, performance, and quality of a product. They serve as the detailed blueprint for design and manufacturing.

#### 1.4.1 Types of Specifications

*   **Functional Specifications:** Describe *what* the product must do.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   A smartphone must be able to make calls, send messages, and browse the internet.
        *   A car must accelerate from 0 to 60 mph in under 8 seconds.
        *   A blender must be able to blend frozen fruit within 30 seconds.
*   **Performance Specifications:** Quantify the functional requirements, specifying measurable targets.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   Battery life: 24 hours of continuous talk time.
        *   Processing speed: 2.5 GHz quad-core processor.
        *   Weight capacity: Supports up to 200 kg.
        *   Water resistance: IP68 rating.
*   **Physical Specifications:** Describe the physical attributes of the product.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   Dimensions: 150mm x 75mm x 8mm.
        *   Weight: 180 grams.
        *   Material: Brushed aluminum casing, Gorilla Glass screen.
        *   Color: Midnight black.
*   **Manufacturing Specifications:** Detail how the product should be produced.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   Tolerances: ±0.1 mm for critical dimensions.
        *   Surface finish: Ra 1.6 µm.
        *   Assembly methods: Screw assembly, adhesive bonding.
        *   Materials: Specific grades of steel, ABS plastic.
*   **Reliability Specifications:** Define the expected lifespan and failure rate.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF): 50,000 hours.
        *   Warranty period: 2 years.
*   **Aesthetic Specifications:** Describe the visual and tactile qualities.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   Color palette: Pantone 286 C (Blue).
        *   Shape: Ergonomic, rounded edges.
        *   Texture: Matte finish.

#### 1.4.2 The Process of Developing Specifications

*   **Identify Customer Needs (Voice of the Customer - VOC):** Understanding what the customer truly wants, often expressed in qualitative terms.
*   **Establish Target Specifications:** Translate customer needs into measurable technical terms. These are initial, aspirational goals.
*   **Trade-offs:** Balancing competing needs and constraints (e.g., cost vs. performance).
*   **Refinement:** Iteratively refining specifications as the design progresses and new information becomes available.
*   **Ulrich & Eppinger (2003):** Dedicate a significant portion of their book to the "Planning" phase, which includes identifying customer needs and establishing target specifications. They emphasize the importance of a structured process to avoid costly design changes later. They introduce the concept of **metrics** (how we measure a need) and **units** (the unit of measure).

#### 1.4.3 Key Concepts in Specifications

*   **Metrics:** A specific measure of a performance characteristic. (e.g., battery life in hours).
*   **Units:** The standard by which a metric is measured. (e.g., hours).
*   **Technical Description:** A detailed explanation of the specification.
*   **Ideal Function:** The ultimate desired performance.
*   **Realized Function:** The actual performance achieved.

*   **Otto & Wood (2000):** In their discussion of reverse engineering and new product development, they highlight the importance of understanding existing product specifications as a basis for improvement or creating new products. Analyzing specifications of successful products can provide valuable insights into market expectations and technical feasibility.

### 1.5 Linking Specifications to Course Outcomes

This module lays the groundwork for several key course outcomes:

*   **CO1: Determine the life cycle of a product and product development process (Knowledge Level: K2):** Understanding product classification helps in identifying where a product fits within its lifecycle (e.g., a high-tech product might have a shorter lifecycle than a durable good). The development of specifications is a critical early step in the product development process.
*   **CO2: Develop knowledge of robust design and conceptual design (Knowledge Level: K2):** Clear specifications are essential for conceptual design, guiding the generation of initial ideas. Robust design aims to create products that perform consistently across a range of operating conditions, which requires well-defined performance specifications.
*   **CO3: Introduce the concept of Design for Manufacturing and Assembly (DFMA) in product design (Knowledge Level: K2):** Manufacturing and assembly specifications are directly derived from the product design and are fundamental to DFMA.
*   **CO4: Use value engineering in the development of product (Knowledge Level: K2):** Value engineering often involves analyzing existing product specifications to identify areas where cost can be reduced without sacrificing essential function or performance.
*   **CO5: Incorporate ergonomics and rapid prototyping in product development (Knowledge Level: K2):** Ergonomic specifications define comfort and ease of use, directly influencing physical and functional requirements. Rapid prototyping is a tool to test and validate product specifications early in the development cycle.

## Practice Questions and Exercises

**1. Multiple Choice Questions:**

a) Which of the following is NOT a classification of consumer products based on purchasing habits?
    i) Convenience Products
    ii) Industrial Products
    iii) Specialty Products
    iv) Unsought Products

b) A precise, measurable description of a required characteristic of a product is known as:
    i) Customer Need
    ii) Product Classification
    iii) Product Specification
    iv) Product Concept

c) The specification that describes *what* a product must do is called:
    i) Performance Specification
    ii) Physical Specification
    iii) Functional Specification
    iv) Reliability Specification

d) Which textbook is primarily referenced for the structured product development process?
    i) Product Design: Techniques in Reverse Engineering and new Product Development by Otto & Wood
    ii) Product Design & Development by Ulrich & Eppinger
    iii) Neither
    iv) Both equally

**2. Short Answer Questions:**

a) Differentiate between a "convenience product" and a "specialty product," providing an example of each.
b) Explain the importance of "performance specifications" in the product design process.
c) List three different ways in which products can be classified.
d) What is the relationship between "customer needs" and "target specifications"?

**3. Application Exercise:**

Imagine you are designing a new electric kettle.

a) Classify this product based on at least three different classification methods discussed in this module. Justify your classifications.
b) List at least five specific "performance specifications" for this electric kettle. Be as precise and measurable as possible. (e.g., Heating time, Water capacity, Power consumption).

## Answers

**1. Multiple Choice Answers:**

a) **ii) Industrial Products** (Industrial products are a separate major category from consumer products.)
b) **iii) Product Specification**
c) **iii) Functional Specification**
d) **ii) Product Design & Development by Ulrich & Eppinger** (While Otto & Wood is valuable, Ulrich & Eppinger is known for its structured approach to the entire development process.)

**2. Short Answer Answers:**

a)
    *   **Convenience Product:** Purchased frequently, with minimal effort and comparison. *Example:* Bottled water from a vending machine.
    *   **Specialty Product:** Has unique characteristics, and buyers make a special effort to purchase it. *Example:* A specific vintage guitar model sought by a collector.
b) Performance specifications quantify functional requirements, providing measurable targets that the design must achieve. They are crucial for evaluating the success of the design, guiding testing, and ensuring the product meets customer expectations for functionality.
c) Three classification methods are:
    1.  By Tangibility (Tangible vs. Intangible)
    2.  By Consumer Behavior (Consumer vs. Industrial)
    3.  By Durability and Tangibility (Nondurable vs. Durable)
    (Other valid answers include classification by technology, complexity, purpose, etc.)
d) Customer needs are the underlying desires and expectations of the user, often expressed in qualitative terms. Target specifications translate these qualitative needs into quantitative, measurable technical parameters that the product design aims to achieve.

**3. Application Exercise Answers:**

a) **Classification of an Electric Kettle:**
    *   **By Tangibility:** Tangible Product (it's a physical object).
    *   **By Consumer Behavior:** Consumer Product (typically purchased by individuals/households for personal use).
        *   Within Consumer Products: Likely a **Shopping Product** (consumers compare brands, features like capacity, temperature control, design, and price before purchasing).
    *   **By Durability and Tangibility:** Durable Good (it is intended for long-term use, not consumed in one or a few uses).
    *   **By Technology and Complexity:** Differentiated Product (different brands offer unique features, designs, and performance levels). Could also be considered Low-to-Medium Technology, depending on the features.

b) **Performance Specifications for an Electric Kettle:**
    *   **Water Capacity:** Minimum 1.7 liters, Maximum 1.7 liters (± 0.05 liters).
    *   **Heating Time:** To boil 1.7 liters of water from 20°C to 100°C in less than 5 minutes (specifically, 4 minutes 30 seconds ± 15 seconds).
    *   **Power Consumption:** Rated at 1800 watts (± 100 watts) when operating at 240V.
    *   **Temperature Control Accuracy (for variable temperature models):** Maintain set temperature within ± 2°C.
    *   **Auto Shut-off Function:** Activates within 5 seconds of reaching boiling point or when empty (detected by boil-dry protection sensor).
    *   **Cord Length:** Minimum 0.75 meters, Maximum 1.0 meter.
    *   **Durability:** Withstand at least 5,000 boiling cycles without failure.

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Product as a Solution:** Always view products as solutions to customer problems or fulfillers of desires.
*   **Customer-Centricity:** Understanding customer needs is the driving force behind classification and specification.
*   **Specifications are Dynamic:** Specifications are not set in stone; they evolve as the design process progresses.
*   **Trade-offs are Inevitable:** Balancing competing requirements (cost, performance, aesthetics) is a core aspect of product design.
*   **Clarity is Key:** Precise and unambiguous specifications prevent misunderstandings and errors in design and manufacturing.
*   **The "What" vs. The "How":** Functional specs focus on "what," while performance specs quantify that "what." Manufacturing specs focus on "how."

This foundational understanding of product classification and specification will be critical for tackling more complex topics in subsequent modules of Product Design and Innovation.
