# World Class Manufacturing: Module 3 - WCM Concepts & Tools

## Topic: Process Design & Process Design Tools

### 1. Introduction to Process Design in WCM

Process design is the fundamental framework upon which World Class Manufacturing (WCM) operations are built. It focuses on creating efficient, effective, and adaptable manufacturing processes that deliver high-quality products consistently. A well-designed process minimizes waste, optimizes resource utilization, and fosters a culture of continuous improvement.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Process:** A series of actions or steps taken in order to achieve a particular end. In manufacturing, this refers to the sequence of operations involved in transforming raw materials into finished goods.
*   **Process Design:** The systematic development and refinement of manufacturing processes to achieve desired outcomes in terms of quality, cost, delivery, and flexibility.
*   **Value Stream:** The entire set of activities, both value-adding and non-value-adding, required to bring a product or service from concept to customer.
*   **Waste (Muda):** Any activity that consumes resources but does not create value from the customer's perspective. Common types include overproduction, waiting, transportation, overprocessing, inventory, motion, and defects.

### 2. Principles of Effective Process Design in WCM

Effective process design in WCM is guided by several core principles aimed at achieving operational excellence.

**Key Principles:**

*   **Simplicity:** Processes should be as straightforward as possible, minimizing unnecessary steps and complexity. (Schonberger's core philosophy).
*   **Flow:** Products should move smoothly through the process with minimal interruptions or delays. This relates to the concept of continuous flow.
*   **Pull System:** Production is initiated by customer demand, not by forecasts, thereby reducing overproduction and inventory.
*   **Zero Defects:** Processes should be designed to prevent errors from occurring in the first place, rather than relying on inspection to catch them.
*   **Standardization:** Establishing clear, consistent procedures for all tasks ensures predictability and facilitates improvement.
*   **Flexibility:** The ability to adapt processes to changing customer needs, product variations, and production volumes.
*   **Customer Focus:** All design decisions should consider what the customer values.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

### 3. Key Process Design Tools in WCM

A variety of tools are employed to design, analyze, and improve manufacturing processes. These tools provide a structured approach to understanding current processes and identifying opportunities for enhancement.

#### 3.1. Value Stream Mapping (VSM)

**Definition:** A lean manufacturing tool used to visualize the entire flow of materials and information required to bring a product or service to a customer. It differentiates between value-adding and non-value-adding activities.

**How it's used:**

1.  **Current State Mapping:** Documenting the existing process, including all steps, lead times, cycle times, inventory levels, and information flows.
2.  **Future State Mapping:** Designing an ideal process that eliminates waste, improves flow, and meets customer demand more effectively.

**Benefits:**

*   Identifies bottlenecks and sources of waste.
*   Provides a holistic view of the entire process.
*   Facilitates communication and collaboration among teams.
*   Aids in prioritizing improvement efforts.

**Example:** A VSM for a widget manufacturing process might reveal long waiting times between machining and assembly, indicating an opportunity to co-locate or improve material handling.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO4: To Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM.** (VSM is a key categorization tool for process analysis.)
*   **CO5: Learn different concepts in Modern day Manufacturing.** (VSM is a cornerstone of lean and modern manufacturing.)

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018):** Likely to cover VSM as a primary lean tool.
*   **Frazelle (2016):** May discuss VSM in the context of material flow and its impact on manufacturing efficiency.

#### 3.2. Process Flow Charting (PFC)

**Definition:** A graphical representation of the sequence of operations, process steps, and decision points in a manufacturing process.

**How it's used:**

*   Different symbols represent different types of activities (e.g., operation, inspection, transportation, delay, storage).
*   Flow lines connect the symbols to show the sequence of steps.

**Benefits:**

*   Helps understand the current process clearly.
*   Identifies redundant steps or unnecessary complexity.
*   Facilitates brainstorming for process improvement.
*   Useful for training and documentation.

**Example:** A PFC for an order fulfillment process might show steps like "Receive Order," "Pick Items," "Pack Order," "Ship Order," with decision points like "Is item in stock?"

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO4: To Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM.** (PFC is a tool for process visualization.)
*   **CO5: Learn different concepts in Modern day Manufacturing.** (Process mapping is fundamental to understanding and improving manufacturing.)

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018):** Will likely present PFC as a basic process analysis tool.

#### 3.3. Cause and Effect Diagram (Fishbone/Ishikawa Diagram)

**Definition:** A brainstorming tool used to identify potential causes of a specific problem or effect. It categorizes potential causes into major branches.

**How it's used:**

*   The "head" of the fish represents the problem (effect).
*   Major "bones" represent categories of potential causes (e.g., People, Process, Machine, Material, Environment, Measurement - the 6 Ms).
*   Smaller "bones" branch off to list specific contributing causes within each category.

**Benefits:**

*   Systematically identifies root causes of problems.
*   Encourages thorough investigation.
*   Promotes teamwork in problem-solving.

**Example:** For a "high defect rate" problem, potential causes could include: Operator training (People), Machine calibration (Machine), Raw material quality (Material), or improper assembly sequence (Process).

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO4: To Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM.** (Categorizing causes is a key function of this tool.)

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018):** Essential tool for quality improvement and root cause analysis.

#### 3.4. Pareto Chart

**Definition:** A bar graph that displays the frequency or impact of problems or causes, ranked from most to least significant. It is based on the Pareto principle (80/20 rule), which suggests that a small number of causes often account for a large percentage of the effects.

**How it's used:**

*   Data is collected on various problems or defects.
*   The data is organized in descending order of frequency or cost.
*   Bars represent the frequency/cost of each item.
*   A cumulative percentage line shows the combined effect of the top items.

**Benefits:**

*   Helps prioritize improvement efforts by focusing on the most significant issues.
*   Visually communicates the magnitude of different problems.

**Example:** A Pareto chart of customer complaints might show that "late delivery" and "product damage" account for 80% of all complaints, directing efforts to address these issues first.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO4: To Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM.** (Categorizes problems by impact.)
*   **CO1: To familiarise with the basic concepts in World class Manufacturing.** (Prioritization is a key concept for efficiency.)

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018):** Fundamental tool for quality management and problem-solving.

#### 3.5. Process Capability Analysis (Cp/Cpk)

**Definition:** A statistical method used to determine if a process is capable of consistently producing output that meets specified requirements (tolerances).

*   **Cp (Process Potential):** Measures the potential capability of a process assuming it is centered within the specification limits.
*   **Cpk (Process Capability Index):** Measures the actual capability of a process, taking into account its centering relative to the specification limits.

**How it's used:**

*   Requires data from a stable process.
*   Compares process variation (e.g., standard deviation) to the specification limits.

**Benefits:**

*   Quantifies the ability of a process to meet customer requirements.
*   Identifies processes that need improvement to reduce variation or improve centering.
*   Supports decision-making regarding process control and capability.

**Example:** A Cpk value of 1.33 or higher is often considered acceptable, indicating that the process can consistently produce parts within specification.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO4: To Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM.** (It's a quantitative tool for process assessment.)
*   **CO5: Learn different concepts in Modern day Manufacturing.** (Statistical process control is a hallmark of modern manufacturing.)

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018):** Essential for understanding and implementing Statistical Process Control (SPC).

#### 3.6. Six Sigma Tools (DMAIC)

**Definition:** Six Sigma is a data-driven methodology for eliminating defects and reducing variation in processes. The DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control) framework is central to its application. While not a single tool, it's a systematic approach that utilizes many tools.

**DMAIC Phases & Associated Tools:**

*   **Define:**
    *   **Project Charter:** Outlines the problem, goals, scope, and team.
    *   **Voice of the Customer (VOC):** Capturing customer needs and expectations.
*   **Measure:**
    *   **Process Mapping:** Understanding the current process.
    *   **Data Collection Plans:** Ensuring accurate and relevant data.
    *   **Measurement System Analysis (MSA):** Verifying the accuracy and reliability of measurement systems.
*   **Analyze:**
    *   **Cause and Effect Diagrams:** Identifying root causes.
    *   **Pareto Charts:** Prioritizing causes.
    *   **Statistical Hypothesis Testing:** Validating cause-and-effect relationships.
    *   **Regression Analysis:** Quantifying relationships between variables.
*   **Improve:**
    *   **Design of Experiments (DOE):** Optimizing process parameters.
    *   **Brainstorming Techniques:** Generating solutions.
*   **Control:**
    *   **Statistical Process Control (SPC) Charts:** Monitoring process performance.
    *   **Control Plans:** Documenting how to maintain improved processes.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **CO4: To Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM.** (DMAIC is a framework that categorizes a suite of tools.)
*   **CO5: Learn different concepts in Modern day Manufacturing.** (Six Sigma is a dominant modern manufacturing philosophy.)

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018):** Will likely dedicate significant coverage to Six Sigma principles and tools.
*   **Jagdev, Brennan, & Browne (2013):** May discuss Six Sigma as a strategic approach to decision-making in manufacturing.

#### 3.7. Lean Manufacturing Tools (Kaizen, 5S, Kanban)

**Definition:** Lean manufacturing focuses on maximizing customer value while minimizing waste.

*   **Kaizen (Continuous Improvement):** A philosophy of ongoing, incremental improvements involving all employees.
*   **5S (Sort, Set in Order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain):** A methodology for workplace organization and standardization, creating a safe and efficient environment.
*   **Kanban:** A visual signaling system used to manage workflow and production in a pull system.

**How they're used:**

*   **Kaizen:** Regular team meetings to identify and implement small improvements.
*   **5S:** Implementing workplace organization principles to reduce search time, improve safety, and enhance visual management.
*   **Kanban:** Using cards or signals to trigger the next step in production, controlling work-in-progress.

**Benefits:**

*   **Kaizen:** Fosters employee engagement and a culture of improvement.
*   **5S:** Creates a clean, organized, and efficient workspace, reducing waste and improving safety.
*   **Kanban:** Prevents overproduction, reduces lead times, and improves responsiveness.

**Example:**
    *   **Kaizen:** A production line team identifies a way to reduce setup time by 5 minutes through a simple process change.
    *   **5S:** A workshop floor is organized with designated spots for tools, clear labeling, and visual cues for everything.
    *   **Kanban:** As a bin of parts at the assembly station empties, a Kanban card is sent to the preceding workstation to signal the need for replenishment.

### 6. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1 (CO1, CO5):**
Define "process" in the context of World Class Manufacturing. Provide two key principles of effective process design and explain why they are important.

**Answer:**
A "process" in WCM is a sequence of value-adding and non-value-adding activities required to deliver a product or service to a customer.
Two key principles are:
1.  **Simplicity:** Reducing complexity makes processes easier to understand, execute, and improve, leading to fewer errors and greater efficiency.
2.  **Flow:** Ensuring a smooth, uninterrupted movement of products through the process minimizes lead times, reduces inventory, and improves responsiveness.

**Question 2 (CO4):**
Explain the purpose of a Value Stream Map (VSM) and how it helps in process improvement. Name two other tools used in process design and briefly describe their function.

**Answer:**
A Value Stream Map (VSM) visualizes the entire flow of materials and information from raw material to the customer. Its purpose is to identify and eliminate waste, highlight bottlenecks, and design a more efficient "future state" process.
Other tools:
*   **Process Flow Charting (PFC):** Visually represents the sequence of operations and decision points in a process.
*   **Cause and Effect Diagram (Fishbone):** Used to brainstorm and identify potential root causes of a problem.

**Question 3 (CO4, CO1):**
What is the primary advantage of using a Pareto Chart in process analysis?

**Answer:**
The primary advantage of a Pareto Chart is its ability to help prioritize improvement efforts by visually highlighting the most significant causes of problems (often following the 80/20 rule), allowing teams to focus resources where they will have the greatest impact.

**Question 4 (CO3):**
How can tools like Kaizen and 5S contribute to employee involvement in process design and improvement?

**Answer:**
Kaizen encourages all employees, regardless of their role, to identify and implement small, continuous improvements, fostering a sense of ownership and empowerment. 5S creates an organized and efficient workplace, making it easier for employees to perform their jobs and contribute to identifying further improvements in their work area. Both foster a culture where employee input is valued.

**Question 5 (CO7):**
List three modern trends impacting process design in manufacturing.

**Answer:**
1.  Industry 4.0 / Digitalization
2.  Advanced Robotics & Automation
3.  Additive Manufacturing (3D Printing)

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Process Design is foundational:** It's the blueprint for efficient and effective manufacturing.
*   **Waste Elimination is Key:** Tools like VSM are critical for identifying and removing "muda."
*   **Customer Focus:** All process design decisions should ultimately serve the customer's needs and expectations.
*   **Holistic View:** Tools like VSM provide an end-to-end perspective, not just isolated improvements.
*   **Data-Driven Decisions:** Tools like Pareto Charts and Process Capability analysis provide quantitative insights for improvement.
*   **Continuous Improvement:** Process design is not a one-time activity but an ongoing effort driven by tools like Kaizen.
*   **Adaptability:** Modern manufacturing demands processes that are flexible and responsive to change.
*   **Integration:** Process design tools are often used in conjunction with each other (e.g., VSM might identify a problem, and a Fishbone diagram might be used to analyze its causes).
