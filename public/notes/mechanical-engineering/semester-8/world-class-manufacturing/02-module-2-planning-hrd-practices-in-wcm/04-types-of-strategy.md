# World Class Manufacturing (WCM)

## Module 2: Planning & HRD Practices in WCM

### Topic: Types of Strategy

This module delves into the strategic underpinnings of World Class Manufacturing (WCM), focusing on how planning and human resource development (HRD) practices contribute to achieving manufacturing excellence. Understanding different types of strategies is crucial for aligning organizational goals with operational capabilities, ultimately leading to a competitive advantage.

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Strategy:** A long-term plan of action designed to achieve a particular goal or set of goals. In WCM, strategy guides the decisions related to operations, technology, human resources, and customer focus. (Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar, 2018)
*   **Manufacturing Strategy:** A coherent set of decisions that guide the operations function in its quest to support the overall business strategy and achieve competitive advantage. (Jagdev, Brennan, & Browne, 2013)
*   **World Class Manufacturing (WCM):** A philosophy and a set of practices that aim to achieve superior performance in manufacturing by focusing on continuous improvement, customer satisfaction, and operational excellence. (Schonberger, 2013)
*   **Competitive Advantage:** A condition or circumstance that puts a company in a favorable or superior business position. In WCM, this is achieved through superior quality, cost, delivery, and flexibility.

### 1. Types of Strategy in WCM

Strategies in WCM are not monolithic. They can be categorized based on various perspectives, including their focus, scope, and approach. Understanding these types helps organizations to tailor their WCM journey effectively.

#### 1.1. Based on Competitive Priorities (Porter's Generic Strategies Adapted for Manufacturing)

Michael Porter's framework, often adapted for manufacturing, highlights how companies can compete. In WCM, these translate into:

*   **Cost Leadership Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** A strategy focused on becoming the lowest-cost producer in the industry while maintaining acceptable quality.
    *   **WCM Application:** Emphasizes efficiency, waste reduction (Lean principles), process optimization, economies of scale, and advanced automation.
    *   **Example:** Toyota's Production System (TPS) is a prime example of achieving cost leadership through relentless waste elimination and continuous improvement. (Schonberger, 2013)
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** Lean Manufacturing, Six Sigma, Standardized Work, Value Stream Mapping.

*   **Differentiation Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** A strategy focused on offering unique products or services that are perceived as superior by customers.
    *   **WCM Application:** Emphasizes superior quality, innovative features, excellent customer service, and customization.
    *   **Example:** Companies excelling in high-precision manufacturing or custom-engineered products often follow this strategy. Bose, known for its audio quality and innovative designs, exemplifies differentiation.
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** Total Quality Management (TQM), Statistical Process Control (SPC), Design for Manufacturing (DFM), Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP).

*   **Focus Strategy (Niche Strategy):**
    *   **Definition:** A strategy that concentrates on a narrow segment of the market and aims to serve that segment exceptionally well, either through cost leadership or differentiation within that niche.
    *   **WCM Application:** Specializing in specific product families, customer groups, or geographical regions, and optimizing operations to meet their unique needs.
    *   **Example:** A manufacturer specializing in highly customized medical devices for a specific patient population.
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS), Build-to-Order (BTO), close customer collaboration.

#### 1.2. Based on Evolutionary Approach (Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar, 2018)

Manufacturing operations evolve, and their strategies often reflect this evolution.

*   **Traditional/Conventional Strategy:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Reactive, focused on short-term gains, limited employee involvement, emphasis on functional specialization, batch production, and reliance on inspection for quality.
    *   **WCM Perspective:** Often seen as the starting point from which organizations need to evolve towards more advanced strategies.

*   **Modern Manufacturing Strategy:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Proactive, longer-term perspective, focus on customer needs, integration of technology, employee empowerment, continuous improvement, and process orientation.
    *   **WCM Application:** Embraces principles of Lean, Six Sigma, Agile, and advanced automation to achieve superior performance.

#### 1.3. Based on Core Competencies (Prahalad & Hamel)

While not exclusively WCM, understanding core competencies is vital for developing an effective manufacturing strategy.

*   **Leveraging Core Competencies:**
    *   **Definition:** Strategies built around the unique skills, knowledge, and technologies that provide a sustainable competitive advantage.
    *   **WCM Application:** WCM practices are often developed to enhance and leverage core manufacturing competencies like process control, material science expertise, or complex assembly.
    *   **Example:** A company with strong expertise in precision machining might develop a strategy to become the world leader in producing high-precision components for the aerospace industry.
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** Advanced Training & Skill Development, Knowledge Management systems, R&D investment.

#### 1.4. Based on Market Orientation (Market-Driven Manufacturing)

*   **Customer-Centric Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** Strategies driven by in-depth understanding of customer needs, preferences, and evolving demands.
    *   **WCM Application:** WCM principles like customer feedback loops, VOC (Voice of the Customer) analysis, and flexible production systems are key to this strategy.
    *   **Example:** A fashion manufacturer quickly adapting its production lines to new trends and customer demands.
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** CRM integration, Agile Manufacturing, Fast Response Systems.

#### 1.5. Based on Technology Adoption (Technology-Push vs. Market-Pull)

*   **Technology-Push Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** Developing and manufacturing products based on new technological capabilities, with the aim of creating new markets.
    *   **WCM Application:** Investing in advanced manufacturing technologies (e.g., AI, IoT, advanced robotics) to create innovative products and processes. (Frazelle, 2016)
    *   **Example:** The development of 3D printing technology leading to new possibilities in rapid prototyping and custom manufacturing.

*   **Market-Pull Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** Developing and manufacturing products in response to identified market needs and demands.
    *   **WCM Application:** Using WCM tools to efficiently and effectively meet existing customer requirements, focusing on quality, cost, and delivery.
    *   **Example:** Improving production efficiency to meet increased demand for an existing product.

#### 1.6. Based on Manufacturing System Design

*   **Lean Manufacturing Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** A strategy focused on eliminating waste in all its forms (overproduction, waiting, transportation, excess inventory, motion, over-processing, defects) to maximize customer value and minimize resource usage.
    *   **WCM Application:** This is a foundational strategy for many WCM initiatives.
    *   **Example:** Implementing Just-In-Time (JIT) delivery of components to reduce inventory costs. (Schonberger, 2013)
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** Kanban, Jidoka, Poka-Yoke, Kaizen.

*   **Agile Manufacturing Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** A strategy designed to respond quickly and effectively to unpredictable changes in customer demand and market conditions.
    *   **WCM Application:** Emphasizes flexibility, responsiveness, speed, and adaptability in manufacturing operations.
    *   **Example:** A company that can rapidly reconfigure its production lines to manufacture different product variants based on real-time market signals.
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** Modular production, cross-functional teams, rapid prototyping.

*   **Mass Customization Strategy:**
    *   **Definition:** Providing customized products or services at mass production prices and availability.
    *   **WCM Application:** Integrating flexibility and efficiency to offer tailored solutions to individual customers without compromising on cost or speed.
    *   **Example:** Dell's build-to-order computer systems.
    *   **Relevant WCM Tools:** Flexible Manufacturing Systems, modular product design, advanced IT systems.

### 2. Linking Strategy to Planning & HRD Practices in WCM

The chosen manufacturing strategy directly influences planning and HRD practices.

#### 2.1. Strategic Planning in WCM (CO2)

*   **Mission and Vision Alignment:** WCM strategies must align with the company's overall mission and vision.
*   **Competitive Priority Setting:** Deciding which competitive priorities (cost, quality, delivery, flexibility) will be emphasized.
*   **Long-Term Capacity Planning:** Ensuring the manufacturing capability can support the strategic goals.
*   **Technology Roadmapping:** Planning the adoption of new technologies to support the strategy.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Directing financial, human, and technological resources towards strategic objectives.
*   **Performance Measurement:** Establishing Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) that track progress towards strategic goals. (Jagdev, Brennan, & Browne, 2013)

#### 2.2. HRD Practices and Employee Involvement in WCM (CO3)

*   **Skill Development:** Training employees to operate new technologies and implement WCM methodologies (e.g., Lean, Six Sigma).
*   **Cross-Functional Training:** Developing employees with broader skill sets to improve team flexibility.
*   **Employee Empowerment:** Encouraging and enabling employees to identify and solve problems, participate in decision-making, and drive continuous improvement (Kaizen).
*   **Teamwork and Collaboration:** Fostering a culture of teamwork to support integrated processes.
*   **Performance Management:** Linking individual and team performance to strategic WCM objectives.
*   **Leadership Development:** Cultivating leaders who can champion WCM principles and drive change.
*   **Culture of Continuous Improvement:** Embedding a mindset where everyone is involved in finding ways to improve processes, quality, and efficiency. (Schonberger, 2013)

**Example:** If a WCM strategy focuses on **differentiation through quality**, HRD practices would emphasize rigorous training in SPC, problem-solving techniques, and quality awareness programs for all employees. Employee involvement would be crucial in identifying quality deviations and implementing corrective actions at the source.

### 3. Modern Day Trends and Terminology in WCM (CO5, CO7)

*   **Industry 4.0:** The integration of cyber-physical systems, IoT, cloud computing, and AI into manufacturing. This impacts strategy by enabling more data-driven decisions, predictive maintenance, and highly flexible production.
*   **Smart Manufacturing:** Using advanced technologies to create intelligent, connected, and autonomous manufacturing systems.
*   **Digital Transformation:** The application of digital technologies across all areas of a business, including manufacturing.
*   **Sustainable Manufacturing:** Strategies that focus on minimizing environmental impact, optimizing resource usage, and ensuring ethical production.
*   **Resilient Supply Chains:** Building manufacturing systems that can withstand disruptions and adapt to changing global conditions, often influencing location and sourcing strategies. (Hejaji, 2015)
*   **Servitization:** Shifting from selling products to selling services related to those products (e.g., maintenance contracts, performance guarantees), impacting manufacturing design and support strategies.

### 4. Indian Scenario & WCM (CO6)

*   **Adoption of WCM:** Indian manufacturing companies are increasingly adopting WCM principles to enhance competitiveness in global and domestic markets.
*   **Challenges:** Common challenges include resistance to change, lack of skilled manpower, infrastructure limitations, and the need for a strong management commitment.
*   **Opportunities:** Indian companies can leverage WCM for cost reduction, quality improvement, and better customer service, especially in sectors like automotive, pharmaceuticals, and textiles.
*   **Government Initiatives:** Programs promoting manufacturing excellence and skill development support WCM adoption.
*   **Hybrid Strategies:** Many Indian companies are adopting hybrid strategies, combining Lean principles with elements of Six Sigma and TQM, tailored to their specific contexts.

### 5. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1 (CO2):** A manufacturing company wants to compete on speed of delivery. What type of manufacturing strategy would be most appropriate, and what WCM tools would be essential for its successful implementation?

**Question 2 (CO3):** Explain how employee involvement and HRD practices differ between a company pursuing a cost leadership strategy and one pursuing a differentiation strategy in WCM.

**Question 3 (CO4):** Categorize the following WCM tools based on their primary focus:
    a) Kanban
    b) Six Sigma
    c) Poka-Yoke
    d) Standardized Work
    e) SPC

**Question 4 (CO5 & CO7):** How does the trend of Industry 4.0 influence the types of manufacturing strategies companies can adopt in modern manufacturing?

**Question 5 (CO6):** Discuss a potential challenge faced by Indian manufacturing companies when adopting a World Class Manufacturing strategy and suggest a mitigation approach.

---

### Answers

**Answer 1 (CO2):**
The most appropriate strategy would be an **Agile Manufacturing Strategy** or a **Fast Response Strategy**, aiming for rapid delivery. Essential WCM tools would include:
*   **Lean Manufacturing:** To eliminate all forms of waste that cause delays (e.g., waiting, excess inventory, inefficient transportation).
*   **Just-In-Time (JIT):** To ensure timely delivery of components and finished goods.
*   **Kanban:** For pull-based production, signaling when more units are needed.
*   **Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS):** To quickly reconfigure production lines for different products or order volumes.
*   **Efficient Production Planning and Scheduling Systems:** To optimize throughput and minimize lead times.

**Answer 2 (CO3):**
*   **Cost Leadership Strategy:**
    *   **Employee Involvement:** Focused on identifying and eliminating waste, improving process efficiency, and adhering to standardized procedures. Employees are encouraged to suggest incremental improvements (Kaizen).
    *   **HRD Practices:** Emphasis on standardized training, skill development in efficient operations, cost-consciousness awareness, and potentially performance-based incentives tied to productivity and waste reduction.
*   **Differentiation Strategy (Quality Focus):**
    *   **Employee Involvement:** Focused on identifying quality issues, root cause analysis, process control, and ensuring adherence to stringent quality standards. Employees are empowered to stop production if quality is compromised.
    *   **HRD Practices:** Emphasis on in-depth training in quality management systems (TQM, SPC), problem-solving techniques, product knowledge, and customer appreciation. Skills related to precision, attention to detail, and innovation are fostered.

**Answer 3 (CO4):**
*   **a) Kanban:** Focus: Flow control, Just-In-Time, Waste Reduction.
*   **b) Six Sigma:** Focus: Defect Reduction, Process Improvement, Variation Reduction.
*   **c) Poka-Yoke (Mistake-Proofing):** Focus: Defect Prevention, Error Reduction.
*   **d) Standardized Work:** Focus: Consistency, Efficiency, Quality, Waste Reduction.
*   **e) SPC (Statistical Process Control):** Focus: Process Monitoring, Variation Control, Quality Assurance.

**Answer 4 (CO5 & CO7):**
Industry 4.0 enables new strategic possibilities:
*   **Hyper-customization:** The integration of IoT, AI, and flexible automation allows for highly personalized products produced efficiently at scale (Mass Customization).
*   **Predictive Strategies:** AI and data analytics enable predictive maintenance, shifting from reactive to proactive strategies for equipment uptime and production continuity.
*   **Agile and Adaptive Strategies:** Real-time data from connected systems allows manufacturers to respond rapidly to market fluctuations and supply chain disruptions.
*   **Data-Driven Decision Making:** Strategies can be informed by vast amounts of operational data, leading to more optimized resource allocation and process design.
*   **Integration of Digital and Physical:** Strategies can focus on creating seamless integration between design, production, and customer feedback loops.

**Answer 5 (CO6):**
*   **Challenge:** Resistance to change and a lack of a strong quality- or efficiency-driven culture. Many Indian companies have historically relied on labor-intensive methods with less emphasis on standardized processes or continuous improvement.
*   **Mitigation Approach:**
    *   **Strong Management Commitment:** Visible and unwavering support from top leadership for WCM initiatives.
    *   **Pilot Projects:** Start with small, manageable pilot projects (e.g., a specific production line or a particular WCM tool like 5S) to demonstrate success and build buy-in.
    *   **Comprehensive Training and HRD:** Invest heavily in training all levels of employees on WCM principles, tools, and the benefits of adoption. Focus on behavioural change alongside technical skills.
    *   **Employee Engagement:** Create platforms for employee participation in problem-solving and decision-making. Recognize and reward contributions to WCM.
    *   **Phased Implementation:** Introduce WCM concepts gradually, building capabilities and confidence over time.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **No single "best" strategy:** The most effective strategy depends on the company's industry, market position, resources, and competitive environment.
*   **Strategy drives operations:** Manufacturing strategy must align with and support the overall business strategy.
*   **Integration is key:** WCM strategies require the integration of technology, processes, and people.
*   **HRD is a strategic enabler:** Effective HRD practices are crucial for the successful implementation of any WCM strategy, fostering a culture of continuous improvement and employee engagement.
*   **Continuous evolution:** Manufacturing strategies are not static; they need to evolve with market changes and technological advancements.
