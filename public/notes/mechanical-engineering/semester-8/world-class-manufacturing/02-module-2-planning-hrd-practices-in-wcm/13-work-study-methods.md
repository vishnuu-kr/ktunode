## World Class Manufacturing: Module 2 - Planning & HRD Practices in WCM: Work Study Methods

This module delves into the crucial role of **Work Study Methods** in achieving World Class Manufacturing (WCM). We will explore how these systematic approaches to improving productivity and efficiency are fundamental to WCM principles and how they integrate with planning and Human Resource Development (HRD) practices.

### 1. Introduction to Work Study in WCM

**Definition:** Work Study is a systematic approach to the investigation of all the factors affecting the efficiency of a given operation or task, with the object of:

*   Determining the most efficient, economical and effective method of performing the operation.
*   Determining the time required for a qualified worker to perform the operation at a defined level of performance.

**Objective in WCM:** In the context of WCM, Work Study is not merely about cost reduction but a fundamental enabler for achieving overall operational excellence, waste elimination, and continuous improvement. It supports WCM principles like simplicity, efficiency, and employee involvement.

**Connection to CO1 (Familiarise with basic concepts in WCM):** Work Study is a foundational concept that underpins many WCM philosophies, such as lean manufacturing and operational efficiency.

**Key Points:**

*   Work Study is a two-pronged approach: **Method Study** and **Work Measurement**.
*   It aims to improve productivity and efficiency.
*   It's a systematic and scientific approach.
*   It's crucial for waste identification and elimination, a core WCM tenet.

### 2. Method Study

**Definition:** Method Study is the systematic recording and critical examination of existing and proposed ways of carrying out work, and the development and introduction of the most satisfactory method.

**Objective:** To find the best way to do a job by examining the current method and developing an improved one.

**Connection to CO4 (Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM):** Method Study involves various tools and techniques that can be categorized.

**Connection to CO3 (Identify employee involvement in WCM):** The success of Method Study relies heavily on the input and collaboration of the shop floor workers.

**The Seven Steps of Method Study:**

This is a universally recognized framework for conducting Method Study:

1.  **Select the Job:** Choose a job or operation to study. Criteria for selection often include high cost, frequent repetition, safety hazards, or employee complaints.
    *   *Example:* Studying the assembly process for a high-volume product, or the method used for material handling in a specific warehouse section.
2.  **Get All the Facts:** Gather all relevant information about the current method. This involves observation, recording, and collecting data.
    *   *Tools:* Process charts, flow diagrams, operation process charts, multiple activity charts.
3.  **Critically Examine the Job:** Analyze the collected facts, questioning every detail of the operation to identify opportunities for improvement. The key questions asked are: **What? Where? When? Who? How? Why?**
    *   *Example:* For an assembly operation:
        *   **What** is being done?
        *   **Where** is it being done?
        *   **When** is it being done?
        *   **Who** is doing it?
        *   **How** is it being done?
        *   **Why** is it being done this way?
4.  **Develop the Best Method:** Based on the critical examination, devise a new, improved method. This often involves eliminating unnecessary steps, combining operations, rearranging sequences, or simplifying tasks.
    *   *Principles of Simplification:*
        *   **Elimination:** Remove unnecessary steps or operations.
        *   **Combination:** Combine two or more operations if feasible.
        *   **Rearrangement:** Change the sequence of operations.
        *   **Simplification:** Make the remaining operations easier.
    *   *Referencing Schonberger (2013):* Schonberger's emphasis on **simplicity** in WCM directly aligns with the core objective of Method Study. Finding the simplest, most direct way to perform a task is paramount.
5.  **Define the New Method:** Document the proposed improved method clearly and concisely. This documentation will be used for training and implementation.
6.  **Install the New Method:** Implement the improved method. This involves training personnel, procuring necessary tools or equipment, and managing the change process.
    *   *Crucial Element:* Employee buy-in and effective training are vital for successful installation.
7.  **Maintain the New Method:** Monitor the new method to ensure it's being followed correctly and continues to deliver benefits. Continuous feedback and refinement are key.

**Benefits of Method Study:**

*   Increased productivity and efficiency.
*   Reduced costs.
*   Improved quality.
*   Enhanced safety.
*   Better utilization of resources (men, machines, materials).
*   Elimination of waste (defects, overproduction, waiting, etc.).
*   Improved working conditions and employee morale.

**Example from Sahay, Saxena, Kumar (2018):** These authors likely discuss Method Study as a critical lean manufacturing tool, emphasizing its role in identifying and eliminating non-value-added activities, a core component of achieving WCM. They might provide case studies where improved methods led to significant reductions in cycle times or material waste.

### 3. Work Measurement

**Definition:** Work Measurement is the application of techniques designed to establish the time for a qualified worker to carry out a task at a defined rate of performance.

**Objective:** To determine the standard time for performing an operation, which forms the basis for planning, scheduling, costing, and incentive schemes.

**Connection to CO4 (Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM):** Work Measurement utilizes various techniques that can be categorized.

**Connection to CO2 (Understand Strategy planning in WCM):** Standard times derived from Work Measurement are crucial for strategic planning of production capacity and resource allocation.

**Key Techniques of Work Measurement:**

1.  **Time Study:**
    *   **Description:** Direct observation and measurement of the time required to perform a task using a stopwatch. The observed times are then adjusted by a performance rating and allowances.
    *   **Steps:**
        *   Define the task to be timed.
        *   Select a qualified worker.
        *   Time the operation using a stopwatch (recording multiple readings).
        *   Rate the worker's performance.
        *   Calculate the basic (or normal) time: `Basic Time = Observed Time × (Performance Rating / Standard Rating)`
        *   Add allowances (e.g., for fatigue, personal needs, unavoidable delays).
        *   Calculate the standard time: `Standard Time = Basic Time × (1 + Allowances)`
    *   *Example:* Timing an operator performing a welding task, rating their speed and accuracy, and adding allowances for breaks and personal needs.
2.  **Predetermined Motion Time Systems (PMTS):**
    *   **Description:** Systems that break down manual work into fundamental human motions (e.g., reach, move, grasp, position) and assign pre-determined times to each motion based on empirical data.
    *   *Examples:* Methods-Time Measurement (MTM), MOST (Maynard Operation Sequence Technique).
    *   *Advantage:* Can be used to establish time standards *before* the operation is performed, useful for new product development or process design.
    *   *Referencing Jagdev, Brennan, Browne (2013):* This textbook on Strategic Decision Making in Modern Manufacturing may discuss how PMTS can be integrated into early-stage product design and process planning to estimate labor requirements and production costs, influencing strategic decisions.
3.  **Work Sampling (Activity Sampling):**
    *   **Description:** A statistical technique where random observations are made of a worker or group of workers to determine the proportion of time spent on various activities or idle.
    *   *Application:* Useful for studying jobs with long cycle times, infrequent tasks, or when direct time study might be disruptive.
    *   *Example:* Observing assembly line workers at random intervals to determine the percentage of time they spend on actual assembly, material handling, or waiting for parts.
4.  **Standard Data:**
    *   **Description:** Time values for basic work elements that can be used to build up standard times for new jobs or operations that share similar elements. This reduces the need for new time studies for every task.

**Importance of Work Measurement:**

*   Setting realistic production targets and schedules.
*   Determining labor costs and setting piece rates or wages.
*   Benchmarking performance.
*   Capacity planning.
*   Identifying bottlenecks and areas for improvement.
*   Basis for performance appraisal and incentive schemes.

**Connection to CO7 (Identify various modern day trends and terminologies in WCM):** Concepts like standard time, performance rating, and allowances are key terminologies in manufacturing efficiency.

### 4. Work Study and Productivity Improvement in WCM

**Definition of Productivity:** Productivity is the ratio of output to input. In manufacturing, it often refers to output per labor hour, output per machine hour, or output per unit of material.

**How Work Study Drives Productivity:**

*   **Method Study:** By finding and implementing better, more efficient methods, Work Study directly reduces the input (time, effort, resources) required to produce a given output, thus increasing productivity. It eliminates non-value-added activities, a core principle of lean WCM.
*   **Work Measurement:** By establishing accurate standard times, Work Study provides a basis for measuring performance and identifying deviations from expected efficiency. This allows for targeted interventions to improve productivity.

**Referencing Schonberger (2013):** Schonberger's "lessons of simplicity" are inherently linked to Work Study. Simplifying processes through Method Study is a direct route to higher productivity. He might advocate for intuitive, simple methods that are easily understood and executed by the workforce, leading to fewer errors and faster cycle times.

**Referencing Frazelle (2016):** Frazelle's work on World Class Manufacturing and Material Handling would likely highlight how Work Study, particularly Method Study and Work Measurement, is applied to optimize material flow, layout, and handling operations. Efficient material handling is crucial for overall plant productivity. For instance, studying the method of picking parts from a warehouse or the time taken to transport materials can lead to significant productivity gains.

**Connection to CO5 (Learn different concepts in Modern day Manufacturing):** Work Study is a fundamental concept that has evolved and remains relevant in modern manufacturing, often integrated with other advanced techniques.

**Connection to CO6 (Analyse Indian scenario & WCM):** In the Indian context, where labor costs are a significant factor, Work Study can be particularly impactful in improving the efficiency of the workforce and making Indian manufacturing more competitive globally.

### 5. Employee Involvement in Work Study

**Importance:** While Work Study is a systematic process, its success is critically dependent on the active involvement and cooperation of the workforce.

**Roles of Employees:**

*   **Information Source:** Employees are often the best source of information about the current method's practicalities and potential improvements.
*   **Idea Generators:** They can suggest innovative solutions during the "Develop the Best Method" stage.
*   **Implementers:** They are the ones who will carry out the new method. Their understanding and acceptance are vital.
*   **Feedback Providers:** They can provide feedback on the effectiveness of the new method.

**Methods for Employee Involvement:**

*   **Participative Method Study:** Involve employees in the selection, fact-finding, and development stages.
*   **Suggestion Schemes:** Encourage employees to submit ideas for method improvements.
*   **Quality Circles/Improvement Teams:** Empower teams to study and improve their own work processes.
*   **Training:** Educate employees on Work Study principles and techniques.

**Connection to CO3 (Identify employee involvement in WCM):** This is a direct link. WCM strongly advocates for empowering and engaging the workforce, and Work Study is a key area where this engagement is critical.

### 6. Work Study and HRD Practices in WCM

**Interplay:** Work Study and Human Resource Development (HRD) are closely intertwined in WCM.

*   **Training:** Method Study requires training employees on new methods. Work Measurement may necessitate training in performance improvement. HRD programs can design and deliver this training effectively.
*   **Performance Management:** Work Measurement provides objective data for performance appraisal and feedback, which is a core HRD function.
*   **Skill Development:** Identifying improved methods might reveal a need for new skills, which HRD can address through training and development initiatives.
*   **Motivation and Incentives:** Standard times from Work Measurement can form the basis for fair incentive schemes, a key HRD tool for motivation.
*   **Change Management:** HRD plays a crucial role in managing the human aspects of implementing changes identified through Work Study, ensuring smooth transitions and minimizing resistance.

**Referencing Sahay, Saxena, Kumar (2018):** These authors might discuss HRD practices that support lean initiatives like Work Study, emphasizing how a skilled and motivated workforce is essential for sustained efficiency gains.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Multiple Choice Questions:**

1.  Work Study is broadly divided into:
    a) Method Study and Time Study
    b) Method Study and Work Measurement
    c) Work Measurement and Operations Research
    d) Process Improvement and Quality Control

    **Answer: b) Method Study and Work Measurement**

2.  Which of the following is the first step in the seven-step Method Study process?
    a) Critically Examine the Job
    b) Develop the Best Method
    c) Select the Job
    d) Get All the Facts

    **Answer: c) Select the Job**

3.  A technique that breaks down work into fundamental motions with pre-determined times is:
    a) Time Study
    b) Work Sampling
    c) Predetermined Motion Time Systems (PMTS)
    d) Operation Process Chart

    **Answer: c) Predetermined Motion Time Systems (PMTS)**

4.  The objective of Method Study is primarily to:
    a) Increase product variety
    b) Find the best way to do a job
    c) Reduce labor costs only
    d) Automate all processes

    **Answer: b) Find the best way to do a job**

**Short Answer Questions:**

1.  Define Work Study and state its primary objectives in the context of World Class Manufacturing.
    *   **Answer:** Work Study is a systematic approach to investigating factors affecting efficiency to determine the most efficient method and the time required for a qualified worker. In WCM, its objectives are to improve productivity, eliminate waste, enhance quality, and optimize resource utilization, ultimately contributing to competitive advantage.

2.  List the seven steps of Method Study.
    *   **Answer:** Select the Job, Get All the Facts, Critically Examine the Job, Develop the Best Method, Define the New Method, Install the New Method, Maintain the New Method.

3.  Briefly explain the difference between Time Study and Work Sampling.
    *   **Answer:** Time Study involves direct, detailed observation and timing of specific tasks using a stopwatch, often requiring performance rating and allowances to derive standard time. Work Sampling is a statistical technique using random observations to determine the proportion of time spent on various activities or idle, suitable for longer or intermittent tasks.

**Practical Exercise:**

Imagine you are observing an operator assembling a small electronic device.

1.  **Select the Job:** The task is "assembling the main circuit board."
2.  **Get All the Facts (Simulated):**
    *   Operator picks components from a bin.
    *   Operator uses a screwdriver to fasten components.
    *   Operator places the assembled board into a testing fixture.
    *   The process is repeated for each unit.
    *   The bin is located to the operator's right, about 1 meter away. The testing fixture is directly in front.
3.  **Critically Examine:**
    *   **What:** Assembling the circuit board.
    *   **Where:** On a workbench.
    *   **When:** Continuously throughout the shift.
    *   **Who:** A trained assembly operator.
    *   **How:** Picking, screwing, placing.
    *   **Why:** To produce functional units.
    *   *Questions for critical examination:* Could the components be closer? Is the screwdriver the most efficient tool? Is the placement into the fixture optimized? Is there any unnecessary movement?
4.  **Develop a Better Method (Brainstorming):**
    *   Move the component bin closer to the operator's left hand.
    *   Use a pneumatic screwdriver for faster fastening.
    *   Design a jig that automatically aligns the board with the testing fixture, reducing placement time.
    *   Combine component picking and placement steps if possible.
5.  **Define the New Method:** Document the proposed changes clearly.
6.  **Install & Maintain:** Train the operator, implement the new tools and jig, and monitor the new process.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Work Study is a dual approach:** Method Study (improving "how") and Work Measurement (determining "how long").
*   **Simplicity is key:** As highlighted by Schonberger, finding the simplest and most effective method is paramount.
*   **Employee involvement is non-negotiable:** For successful implementation and continuous improvement.
*   **Data-driven decisions:** Both Method Study and Work Measurement rely on systematic data collection and analysis.
*   **Foundation for other WCM tools:** Work Study provides essential insights that feed into other WCM practices like lean manufacturing, TQM, and Six Sigma.
*   **Continuous Improvement:** Work Study is not a one-time activity but an ongoing process of seeking better ways of working.
