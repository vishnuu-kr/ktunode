# HYBRID AND ELECTRIC VEHICLES - Module 1: Vehicle Fundamentals – General Description

## Topic: The Configuration of the Series Hybrid Electric Drive Train

This module introduces the fundamental architectures of hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs). This topic specifically focuses on the **Series Hybrid Electric Drive Train**, a key configuration in understanding the evolution and operation of electrified vehicles.

### Learning Outcomes Covered:

*   **Understanding the general architecture of HEVs:** This topic directly addresses the basic layout and components of a series HEV. (Aligns with CO1: K2)
*   **Explaining power flow and control:** While not explicitly detailed in this introductory topic, understanding the series configuration lays the groundwork for discussing power flow and control strategies later in the course.
*   **Differentiating between HEV configurations:** By understanding the series type, students can begin to compare it with other HEV architectures.

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Hybrid Electric Vehicle (HEV):** A vehicle that uses both an internal combustion engine (ICE) and an electric motor for propulsion.
*   **Series Hybrid Electric Drive Train:** A configuration where the ICE is *not* directly connected to the wheels. Instead, the ICE acts solely as a generator to produce electricity, which then powers an electric motor that drives the wheels.
*   **Internal Combustion Engine (ICE):** A heat engine where the combustion of a fuel occurs with an oxidizer (usually air) in a combustion chamber that is an integral part of the working fluid flow circuit.
*   **Generator:** A device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. In a series HEV, this is driven by the ICE.
*   **Electric Motor:** A machine that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy, used for propulsion in HEVs.
*   **Battery Pack:** Stores electrical energy to power the electric motor.
*   **Power Split Device:** In some HEV configurations, this device manages the power flow between the ICE, electric motor, and wheels. **Crucially, a pure series HEV does *not* have a power split device directly connecting the ICE to the wheels.**
*   **Generator Set:** Often used to refer to the combined ICE and generator unit.

### 1. General Description of the Series Hybrid Electric Drive Train:

The series hybrid electric drive train is the simplest of all hybrid configurations. In this architecture, the **internal combustion engine (ICE) is decoupled from the driven wheels**. Its sole purpose is to drive a generator, which produces electrical power. This electrical power is then used to:

*   **Power the electric traction motor(s):** The electric motor(s) are directly responsible for driving the wheels.
*   **Charge the battery pack:** The battery pack acts as an energy buffer, storing excess electricity generated by the ICE or capturing regenerative braking energy.

**Diagrammatic Representation (Conceptual):**

```
+-----------------+     +------------+     +-----------------+     +------------+     +-------+
| Internal        | --> | Generator  | --> | Electrical      | --> | Electric   | --> | Wheels|
| Combustion      |     |            |     | Power Distribution|     | Traction   |     |       |
| Engine (ICE)    |     |            |     | (Bus)           |     | Motor(s)   |     |       |
+-----------------+     +------------+     +-----------------+     +------------+     +-------+
                           ^                                              ^
                           |                                              |
                           |                                              |
                           +------------------+     +------------------+
                                              |     |                  |
                                              +-----| Battery Pack     |
                                                    +------------------+
```

**Key Functional Blocks:**

*   **Internal Combustion Engine (ICE):** Provides mechanical power. Typically designed to operate at its most efficient speed range.
*   **Generator:** Converts the ICE's mechanical power into electrical energy.
*   **Battery Pack:** Stores electrical energy and supplies power to the motor. It also captures energy from regenerative braking.
*   **Electric Traction Motor(s):** Converts electrical energy into mechanical torque to drive the wheels.
*   **Power Electronics:** (Not explicitly shown in the simplified diagram but crucial for operation) Includes inverters, converters, and controllers to manage power flow between the battery, generator, and motor.

### 2. How it Works:

1.  **Power Generation:** The ICE runs a generator. The ICE can be operated independently of the vehicle's speed, allowing it to be tuned for maximum efficiency.
2.  **Power Delivery:** The electricity generated can directly power the electric motor(s) driving the wheels.
3.  **Energy Storage:** If the generated power exceeds the demand from the traction motor, the excess electricity is used to charge the battery pack.
4.  **Battery Power:** When the ICE is off (e.g., at low speeds, during idle, or when sufficient battery charge is available), the electric motor is powered solely by the battery.
5.  **Regenerative Braking:** During braking, the electric motor acts as a generator, converting the vehicle's kinetic energy back into electrical energy, which is stored in the battery.

### 3. Advantages of the Series Hybrid Configuration:

*   **Optimized ICE Operation:** The ICE can be run at its most efficient speed and load, regardless of the vehicle's speed. This improves fuel economy and reduces emissions. (Husain, 2010; Ehsani et al., 2018)
*   **Simpler Mechanical Design:** No complex mechanical power-splitting devices are required between the ICE and the wheels. The drivetrain is mechanically simpler. (Larminie & Lowry, 2012)
*   **Electric-Only Operation:** Allows for quiet, zero-emission operation at low speeds or for short distances, powered solely by the battery.
*   **Flexibility in ICE Placement:** The ICE and generator can be located anywhere in the vehicle chassis, as they are not mechanically linked to the wheels. This can aid in weight distribution and packaging.
*   **Torque Vectoring (with multiple motors):** If multiple electric motors are used for propulsion (e.g., one per wheel), precise torque vectoring is possible for improved handling and traction.

### 4. Disadvantages of the Series Hybrid Configuration:

*   **Energy Conversion Losses:** There are two energy conversion steps from the ICE to the wheels: mechanical to electrical (generator) and electrical to mechanical (motor). Each conversion step incurs losses, leading to lower overall efficiency compared to parallel or series-parallel hybrids when the ICE is the primary power source. (Ehsani et al., 2018)
*   **Larger Electric Motor and Battery:** To provide sufficient power for acceleration and sustained operation, a larger and more powerful electric motor and battery pack are generally required compared to other hybrid configurations where the ICE can directly assist.
*   **ICE Running Constantly (in some strategies):** In some operating modes, the ICE may run continuously to charge the battery or provide power, even if the vehicle speed is low. This can lead to a perception of the engine running unnecessarily or being noisy in certain situations.
*   **Cost:** The need for a larger battery and motor, plus the generator, can increase the overall cost of the vehicle.

### 5. Examples of Series Hybrid Electric Vehicles:

*   **BMW i3 (with Range Extender - REX):** A classic example where a small gasoline engine powers a generator to extend the electric-only range.
*   **Chevrolet Volt (Gen 1):** While often considered a plug-in hybrid, its primary mode of propulsion for most of its operating range was electric, with the gasoline engine acting as a generator.
*   **Nissan e-Power:** Nissan's system uses the gasoline engine solely to generate electricity, which then powers the electric motor that drives the wheels.
*   **Range Extender Electric Vehicles (REEVs):** This term is often used synonymously with series hybrids, emphasizing the primary electric drive with an ICE acting as a generator for range extension.

### 6. Important Points to Remember:

*   **Decoupled ICE:** The defining characteristic of a series HEV is that the ICE *never* directly drives the wheels.
*   **Electric Drive:** The wheels are *always* driven by an electric motor(s).
*   **ICE as a Generator:** The ICE's sole function is to generate electricity.
*   **Battery as Energy Buffer:** The battery is crucial for storing energy and supplying power to the motor when the ICE is off or insufficient.
*   **Efficiency Gains:** Achieved by optimizing ICE operation, but offset by energy conversion losses.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises:

**Question 1:**
What is the primary role of the Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) in a series hybrid electric drive train?
a) To directly drive the wheels at high speeds.
b) To act solely as a generator to produce electricity.
c) To assist the electric motor during acceleration.
d) To power the vehicle's accessories only.

**Question 2:**
Which of the following is a significant advantage of the series hybrid configuration?
a) Higher overall energy efficiency due to direct mechanical linkage.
b) Simpler mechanical drivetrain design.
c) Lower requirement for battery capacity.
d) Ability to use a smaller electric motor.

**Question 3:**
Identify a potential disadvantage of a series hybrid electric drive train.
a) Inability to operate in electric-only mode.
b) Increased energy conversion losses from ICE to wheels.
c) Over-reliance on regenerative braking.
d) Complex power-splitting mechanisms.

**Question 4:**
Name one real-world example of a vehicle that utilizes a series hybrid or range-extender electric vehicle (REx) configuration.

---

### Answers:

**Answer 1:**
b) To act solely as a generator to produce electricity.
*   **Explanation:** In a series hybrid, the ICE's only function is to power the generator. It is never mechanically connected to the wheels.

**Answer 2:**
b) Simpler mechanical drivetrain design.
*   **Explanation:** The absence of direct mechanical coupling between the ICE and the wheels, and the lack of complex power-splitting devices, result in a mechanically simpler drivetrain.

**Answer 3:**
b) Increased energy conversion losses from ICE to wheels.
*   **Explanation:** The energy path involves two main conversion steps: mechanical to electrical (generator) and electrical to mechanical (motor). Each conversion step has inherent inefficiencies, leading to higher overall losses compared to direct mechanical drive.

**Answer 4:**
*   BMW i3 (with Range Extender)
*   Chevrolet Volt (Gen 1)
*   Nissan e-Power
