## Module 2: Energy Storage – Battery Basics

### Topic: Nickel-Cadmium Battery (NiCd)

### **1. Introduction to Nickel-Cadmium Batteries**

Nickel-Cadmium (NiCd) batteries are secondary (rechargeable) electrochemical cells that were historically significant in various applications, including early electric vehicles and portable electronics. While largely superseded by Nickel-Metal Hydride (NiMH) and Lithium-ion (Li-ion) batteries due to their limitations, understanding NiCd batteries provides a foundational understanding of rechargeable battery technology.

*   **Key Concept:** NiCd batteries are based on the electrochemical reaction between nickel oxide hydroxide and cadmium electrodes in an alkaline electrolyte, typically potassium hydroxide (KOH).

*   **Historical Significance:** NiCd batteries were one of the first commercially successful rechargeable batteries and played a crucial role in the development of portable devices and early electric vehicle prototypes.

*   **Textbook Reference:** Husain, "Electric and Hybrid Vehicles: Design Fundamentals" (2nd ed., 2010) may offer historical context on battery types used in early EVs. Ehsani et al., "Modern Electric, Hybrid Electric and Fuel Cell Vehicles" (3rd ed., 2018) might also touch upon the evolution of battery technologies.

### **2. Electrochemical Principles of NiCd Batteries**

The operation of a NiCd battery relies on reversible electrochemical reactions at its electrodes.

#### **2.1. Anode (Negative Electrode)**

*   **Material:** Cadmium (Cd)
*   **Reaction during Discharge:** Cadmium metal is oxidized to cadmium hydroxide.
    $Cd_{(s)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)} \rightarrow Cd(OH)_{2(s)} + 2e^-$
*   **Reaction during Charge:** Cadmium hydroxide is reduced back to cadmium metal.
    $Cd(OH)_{2(s)} + 2e^- \rightarrow Cd_{(s)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)}$

#### **2.2. Cathode (Positive Electrode)**

*   **Material:** Nickel(III) oxide-hydroxide ($NiO(OH)$)
*   **Reaction during Discharge:** Nickel(III) oxide-hydroxide is reduced to nickel(II) hydroxide.
    $NiO(OH)_{(s)} + H_2O_{(l)} + 2e^- \rightarrow Ni(OH)_{2(s)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)}$
*   **Reaction during Charge:** Nickel(II) hydroxide is oxidized back to nickel(III) oxide-hydroxide.
    $Ni(OH)_{2(s)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)} \rightarrow NiO(OH)_{(s)} + H_2O_{(l)} + 2e^-$

#### **2.3. Overall Cell Reaction**

*   **Discharge:** $Cd_{(s)} + 2NiO(OH)_{(s)} + 2H_2O_{(l)} \rightarrow Cd(OH)_{2(s)} + 2Ni(OH)_{2(s)}$
*   **Charge:** $Cd(OH)_{2(s)} + 2Ni(OH)_{2(s)} \rightarrow Cd_{(s)} + 2NiO(OH)_{(s)} + 2H_2O_{(l)}$

#### **2.4. Electrolyte**

*   **Composition:** Aqueous solution of potassium hydroxide (KOH) (alkaline electrolyte).
*   **Role:** Conducts ions between the electrodes. The hydroxide ions ($OH^-$) are crucial for the electrochemical reactions.

*   **Key Concepts:** Oxidation-Reduction (Redox) reactions, Electrodes (Anode, Cathode), Electrolyte, Ion conductivity.

*   **Textbook Reference:** Larminie & Lowry, "Electric Vehicle Technology Explained" (2nd ed., 2012) provides detailed explanations of battery electrochemistry. Dhameja, "Electric Vehicle Battery Systems" (2001) is a dedicated resource for battery systems and principles.

### **3. Characteristics and Performance of NiCd Batteries**

NiCd batteries have specific performance characteristics that dictated their suitability for certain applications.

#### **3.1. Voltage**

*   **Nominal Cell Voltage:** Approximately 1.2 V.
*   **Voltage Stability:** Exhibits a relatively flat discharge voltage curve, meaning the voltage remains nearly constant throughout most of the discharge cycle. This is advantageous for applications requiring a stable power supply.

#### **3.2. Energy Density**

*   **Gravimetric Energy Density:** Typically around 40-60 Wh/kg. This is significantly lower than modern Li-ion batteries.
*   **Volumetric Energy Density:** Also relatively low compared to newer technologies.

*   **Key Concept:** Energy density is the amount of energy stored per unit mass or volume. Lower energy density means a heavier and/or larger battery pack is required for the same amount of stored energy.

#### **3.3. Power Density**

*   **Definition:** The amount of power the battery can deliver per unit mass or volume.
*   **NiCd Performance:** NiCd batteries generally offer good power density, meaning they can deliver high currents for short periods. This was a key advantage for applications requiring rapid acceleration.

#### **3.4. Cycle Life**

*   **Definition:** The number of charge-discharge cycles a battery can undergo before its capacity significantly degrades.
*   **NiCd Performance:** Can typically achieve 500-1000 charge-discharge cycles.

#### **3.5. Temperature Performance**

*   **Operating Range:** Generally performs well over a wide temperature range, especially at lower temperatures where other battery types might struggle.
*   **Sensitivity:** Can be affected by high temperatures, which can lead to premature degradation.

#### **3.6. "Memory Effect"**

*   **Description:** A phenomenon where a NiCd battery, if repeatedly discharged only partially before recharging, appears to "remember" this shallow discharge depth. Subsequent full discharges will then stop at the depth of the previous partial discharges.
*   **Mitigation:** Requires occasional deep discharge cycles to recalibrate the battery's voltage reference.
*   **Impact:** Can reduce the usable capacity of the battery over time.

*   **Key Concepts:** Voltage stability, Energy density (gravimetric and volumetric), Power density, Cycle life, Temperature sensitivity, Memory effect.

*   **Textbook Reference:** All mentioned textbooks will likely discuss these performance characteristics in their respective battery sections. Husain and Ehsani et al. are particularly relevant for EV-specific applications.

### **4. Advantages of NiCd Batteries**

Despite their limitations, NiCd batteries offered certain advantages, which made them suitable for specific applications.

*   **Good Power Capability:** Can deliver high discharge currents, making them suitable for applications requiring high power output (e.g., starting electric motors).
*   **Good Cycle Life:** Relatively long cycle life compared to some older battery technologies.
*   **Wide Operating Temperature Range:** Performs reliably across a broader temperature spectrum than some other chemistries.
*   **Robust Construction:** Generally robust and tolerant to overcharging and deep discharge (though not ideal).
*   **Predictable Discharge Curve:** The flat voltage profile simplifies voltage regulation in some systems.

*   **Textbook Reference:** Denton, "Electric and hybrid vehicles" (2nd ed., 2020) might provide a comparative analysis of battery technologies and their pros/cons.

---

### **5. Disadvantages of NiCd Batteries**

The significant drawbacks of NiCd batteries led to their decline in most applications, especially in EVs.

*   **Low Energy Density:** Requires a larger and heavier battery pack for a given range compared to modern batteries.
*   **"Memory Effect":** As discussed, this can lead to reduced usable capacity if not managed properly.
*   **Cadmium Toxicity:** Cadmium is a toxic heavy metal, posing environmental and health concerns during manufacturing, use, and disposal. This is a major reason for their phase-out.
*   **Higher Self-Discharge Rate:** Compared to some newer chemistries, NiCd batteries tend to lose their charge faster when not in use.
*   **Cost:** While initially competitive, the environmental regulations and specialized recycling have increased the overall cost of ownership.
*   **Limited Scalability for High Power EVs:** While they offer good power, the low energy density limits the practical range achievable for modern EVs.

*   **Key Concepts:** Toxicity, Environmental impact, Self-discharge rate.

*   **Textbook Reference:** Ehsani et al. and Larminie & Lowry are excellent sources for understanding the practical limitations of battery technologies in EV design.

### **6. Applications in Hybrid and Electric Vehicles (Historical Context)**

NiCd batteries were used in some of the earlier generations of electric vehicles and hybrid vehicles.

*   **Early Electric Vehicles:** Their ability to deliver high power made them a candidate for the propulsion of early EVs. However, their low energy density limited the practical range.
*   **Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs):** In some early HEVs, NiCd batteries were used, often in conjunction with other energy storage systems or for specific functions.

*   **Important Point to Remember:** While NiCd batteries were an important step in the evolution of EV technology, they have been largely replaced by more advanced battery chemistries like NiMH and Li-ion due to their superior energy density, lack of memory effect, and reduced environmental impact.

*   **Textbook Reference:** Husain, "Electric and Hybrid Vehicles: Design Fundamentals" is likely to have historical information on battery choices for early EV designs.

---

### **7. Environmental and Safety Considerations**

The environmental and safety aspects of NiCd batteries are critical.

*   **Toxicity of Cadmium:** Cadmium is a known carcinogen and can cause severe health problems. Proper handling, disposal, and recycling are paramount.
*   **Recycling:** Specialized recycling processes are required to recover cadmium and other materials safely. Many regions have regulations mandating the return and recycling of NiCd batteries.
*   **Electrolyte Hazard:** The alkaline electrolyte (KOH) is corrosive and can cause burns.
*   **Thermal Runaway:** While less prone to thermal runaway than some Li-ion chemistries, improper charging or damage can still lead to hazardous conditions.

*   **Key Concepts:** Toxicity, Environmental regulations, Recycling, Corrosive electrolyte.

*   **Textbook Reference:** Dhameja, "Electric Vehicle Battery Systems" (2001) might offer insights into safety considerations of various battery types.

### **8. Comparison with Other Battery Technologies (Briefly)**

To understand why NiCd batteries are less prevalent today, it's useful to compare them briefly with their successors.

| Feature           | Nickel-Cadmium (NiCd) | Nickel-Metal Hydride (NiMH) | Lithium-ion (Li-ion)      |
| :---------------- | :-------------------- | :-------------------------- | :------------------------ |
| **Energy Density** | Low (40-60 Wh/kg)     | Medium (60-120 Wh/kg)       | High (150-250+ Wh/kg)     |
| **Voltage/Cell**  | 1.2 V                 | 1.2 V                       | 3.2-3.7 V (varies by type) |
| **Memory Effect** | Present               | Reduced/Negligible          | Negligible                |
| **Cadmium Toxicity**| High                  | None                        | None                      |
| **Power Density** | Good                  | Very Good                   | Excellent                 |
| **Cycle Life**    | Moderate (500-1000)   | Good (1000-2000)            | Very Good (1000-3000+)    |
| **Cost**          | Moderate              | Higher than NiCd            | High (but decreasing)     |
| **Self-Discharge**| Moderate              | Higher than NiCd            | Low                       |

*   **Key Point:** The advancements in energy density, elimination of the memory effect, and reduced environmental concerns of NiMH and Li-ion batteries have made them the preferred choice for modern EVs.

### **9. Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** What are the primary electrode materials in a Nickel-Cadmium battery?
    *   (a) Lithium and Carbon
    *   (b) Nickel oxide hydroxide and Cadmium
    *   (c) Lead and Sulfuric Acid
    *   (d) Nickel and Zinc

**Answer 1:** (b) Nickel oxide hydroxide and Cadmium

**Question 2:** Explain the "memory effect" in NiCd batteries and how it can be mitigated.

**Answer 2:** The "memory effect" occurs when a NiCd battery is repeatedly discharged only partially before recharging. The battery then "remembers" this shallow discharge depth and may stop discharging at that point in subsequent cycles, effectively reducing its usable capacity. Mitigation involves periodically performing a full deep discharge cycle to recalibrate the battery's voltage reference.

**Question 3:** What is the nominal cell voltage of a NiCd battery?
    *   (a) 1.0 V
    *   (b) 1.2 V
    *   (c) 2.0 V
    *   (d) 3.7 V

**Answer 3:** (b) 1.2 V

**Question 4:** Discuss two significant disadvantages of using NiCd batteries in electric vehicles compared to modern battery technologies.

**Answer 4:** Two significant disadvantages are:
    1.  **Low Energy Density:** This leads to shorter driving ranges and requires heavier, bulkier battery packs for a given range, negatively impacting vehicle performance and efficiency.
    2.  **Cadmium Toxicity:** Cadmium is a toxic heavy metal, posing serious environmental and health risks during production, use, and disposal. This necessitates specialized handling and recycling procedures, increasing complexity and cost.

**Question 5:** Write down the anode and cathode half-reactions during the discharge of a NiCd battery.

**Answer 5:**
    *   Anode (Negative Electrode) reaction: $Cd_{(s)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)} \rightarrow Cd(OH)_{2(s)} + 2e^-$
    *   Cathode (Positive Electrode) reaction: $NiO(OH)_{(s)} + H_2O_{(l)} + 2e^- \rightarrow Ni(OH)_{2(s)} + 2OH^-_{(aq)}$

### **10. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Electrode Materials:** Nickel oxide hydroxide (positive) and Cadmium (negative).
*   **Electrolyte:** Alkaline (Potassium Hydroxide).
*   **Nominal Voltage:** 1.2 V per cell.
*   **Key Limitation for EVs:** Low energy density.
*   **Major Drawback:** Cadmium toxicity and the environmental concerns associated with it.
*   **"Memory Effect":** A characteristic that requires occasional deep discharges.
*   **Historical Relevance:** Important in early EV development but largely replaced by NiMH and Li-ion.
