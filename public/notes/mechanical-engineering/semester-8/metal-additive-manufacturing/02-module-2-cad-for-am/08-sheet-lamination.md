# Metal Additive Manufacturing: Module 2 - CAD for AM: Sheet Lamination

## 1. Introduction to Sheet Lamination (K2)

Sheet lamination is a Metal Additive Manufacturing (MAM) process that builds parts by joining thin sheets of metal. These sheets are typically pre-formed and then fused together layer by layer. This method differs from powder-based processes where material is deposited in a molten or semi-molten state.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Layer-by-Layer Construction:** Parts are built by stacking and bonding individual metal sheets.
*   **Pre-formed Sheets:** The sheets are usually processed or shaped before being incorporated into the build.
*   **Bonding Mechanism:** The primary difference between various sheet lamination techniques lies in how the sheets are bonded.

**Relevance to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Basic MAM Techniques):** Introduces a fundamental MAM process distinct from powder-based methods.
*   **CO2 (Features and Control):** Understanding the core principles of sheet lamination is crucial for appreciating its features and controlling the build process.

**From Textbooks:**

*   **Additive Manufacturing of Metals (Yang & Michaleris):** Chapter 2 might discuss different MAM process categories, where sheet lamination would be classified. It will likely detail the basic principle of layering pre-formed materials.
*   **Additive Manufacturing Technologies (Gibson, Rosen, Stucker, Khorasani):** This book often categorizes AM processes broadly. Sheet Lamination will be presented as a distinct category, potentially with sub-classifications.

## 2. Types of Sheet Lamination Processes

Sheet lamination encompasses several distinct techniques, each with a unique bonding mechanism.

### 2.1. Laser Engineered Net Shaping (LENS) - *Note: LENS is primarily powder-fed, but variations can be considered in the broader context of deposition.*

While LENS is predominantly a powder-bed fusion or directed energy deposition (DED) process, it's important to understand its distinction from true sheet lamination. However, some interpretations might consider it as building up layers, albeit with molten powder.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Powder Feed:** Metal powder is delivered into a molten pool created by a laser.
*   **Fused Deposition:** The molten material solidifies to form the next layer.

**Distinction from Sheet Lamination:** LENS directly deposits and fuses molten material, not pre-formed sheets.

### 2.2. Electron Beam Additive Manufacturing (EBAM) - *Similar to LENS, often powder-based.*

EBAM utilizes an electron beam to melt and fuse metal powder. Again, it's important to differentiate this from true sheet lamination.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Electron Beam:** A high-energy electron beam melts the metal powder.
*   **Vacuum Environment:** Typically performed in a vacuum to prevent contamination.

**Distinction from Sheet Lamination:** EBAM melts and fuses powder, not pre-formed sheets.

### 2.3. Ultrasonic Additive Manufacturing (UAM)

UAM is a true sheet lamination process that uses ultrasonic vibrations to bond metal sheets.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Ultrasonic Welding:** High-frequency acoustic vibrations are used to create a solid-state bond between metal sheets.
*   **No Melting Required:** The process operates below the melting point of the metals.
*   **Composite Structures:** UAM can also bond dissimilar materials, including polymers and ceramics, to metal sheets.
*   **Machining Integration:** Often coupled with CNC machining to achieve final part dimensions and surface finish.

**Process Steps in UAM:**

1.  **Sheet Placement:** A thin sheet of metal is placed on the build platform or the previous layer.
2.  **Ultrasonic Horn Application:** An ultrasonic horn is pressed against the sheet.
3.  **Vibration and Pressure:** The horn vibrates at ultrasonic frequencies while applying pressure.
4.  **Friction and Diffusion Bonding:** The friction generated at the interface of the sheets causes plastic deformation and diffusion bonding, creating a metallurgical bond.
5.  **Layer Addition:** Subsequent sheets are placed and bonded in the same manner.
6.  **CNC Machining:** After each layer or a set of layers, CNC machining is often performed to control dimensions and remove excess material.

**Advantages of UAM:**

*   **Low Temperature Process:** Minimizes residual stresses and distortion compared to melting processes.
*   **Dissimilar Material Bonding:** Ability to create multi-material parts.
*   **Good Mechanical Properties:** Often achieves mechanical properties comparable to wrought materials.
*   **Reduced Porosity:** Solid-state bonding generally results in fewer internal defects.

**Disadvantages of UAM:**

*   **Limited Geometries:** Primarily suited for prismatic or "bread loaf" type structures, with limitations on complex internal features.
*   **Surface Finish:** Requires post-machining for precise tolerances and smooth surfaces.
*   **Material Limitations:** While versatile, some metal alloys may be more challenging to bond ultrasonically.
*   **Build Volume:** Can be limited by the size of the ultrasonic tooling and the available CNC machinery.
