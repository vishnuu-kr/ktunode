# Manufacturing Processes: Module 1 - General Classification of Manufacturing Processes

## Topic: Special Casting Processes

This module focuses on understanding the general classification of manufacturing processes. Within this module, we will delve into several **Special Casting Processes** that offer advantages over traditional sand casting for specific applications. These processes are characterized by their ability to produce castings with higher accuracy, better surface finish, and more complex geometries.

---

### 1. Shell Moulding

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Shell moulding is a casting process that uses a pattern and a core box coated with a thermosetting resin-sand mixture. The resin-sand mixture forms a thin, hard shell around the pattern, which is then used to create the mould cavity.
*   **Process Overview:**
    1.  **Pattern Preparation:** A metal pattern (usually cast iron or steel) is heated to a specific temperature (175-230°C).
    2.  **Shell Formation:** A mixture of fine sand and a thermosetting resin (e.g., phenolic resin) is prepared. This mixture is then applied to the heated pattern.
        *   **Investment Method:** The pattern is coated with the resin-sand mixture, and excess material is allowed to drip off.
        *   **Slurry Method:** The pattern is dipped into a slurry of resin-sand mixture.
    3.  **Curing:** The resin-coated pattern is placed in an oven or exposed to infrared radiation to cure the resin, forming a rigid shell mould.
    4.  **Ejection:** The cured shell mould is then ejected from the pattern.
    5.  **Assembly:** Two shell mould halves are assembled, and if a core is needed, a shell core is inserted. They are typically held together by clips or adhesives.
    6.  **Pouring:** Molten metal is poured into the assembled mould.
    7.  **Shakeout:** After solidification, the shell mould is broken away from the casting.

**Advantages:**

*   Good dimensional accuracy and surface finish.
*   Thin shells are light, reducing storage and handling space.
*   Reduced machining allowance required.
*   Suitable for both ferrous and non-ferrous metals.
*   Can produce intricate shapes and thin sections.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Higher initial tooling cost (patterns).
*   Limited to relatively small and medium-sized castings.
*   Requires careful control of resin-sand mixture and curing temperature.

**Applications:**

*   Automotive components (e.g., cylinder heads, engine blocks, manifolds).
*   Pump housings.
*   Gears and other intricate parts.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Ghosh & Mallik (2010):** Likely discusses the preparation of the resin-sand mixture and the heating/curing process.
*   **Kalpakjian & Schmid:** Might elaborate on the pattern design and the advantages of improved surface finish.
*   **P N Rao (Vol-1):** Could detail the different methods of shell formation and the types of resins used.

### 2. Precision Investment Casting (Lost-Wax Process)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Precision investment casting, also known as the lost-wax process, is a casting method used to produce highly accurate, intricate, and complex metal parts. It involves creating a wax pattern, coating it with a refractory material to form a mould, melting out the wax, and then pouring molten metal into the evacuated mould cavity.
*   **Process Overview:**
    1.  **Wax Pattern Creation:** Intricate wax patterns are produced by injection moulding wax into a metal die.
    2.  **Tree Assembly:** Multiple wax patterns are attached to a central wax runner system, forming a "tree."
    3.  **Ceramic Shell Formation:** The wax tree is dipped repeatedly into a slurry of refractory material (e.g., ceramic or silica) and fine stucco, with drying or stuccoeing between each dip. This builds up a thick ceramic shell.
    4.  **Dewaxing:** The ceramic shell is heated to melt and drain out the wax, leaving a hollow mould cavity.
    5.  **Preheating:** The ceramic mould is preheated to a high temperature to ensure uniform metal flow and prevent thermal shock.
    6.  **Pouring:** Molten metal is poured into the heated mould cavity.
    7.  **Shakeout:** After solidification, the ceramic shell is broken away to reveal the casting.

**Advantages:**

*   Excellent dimensional accuracy and surface finish.
*   Ability to produce highly complex shapes, including internal passages and thin sections.
*   Wide range of metals can be cast.
*   No parting lines on the casting.

**Disadvantages:**

*   High cost of tooling and labour.
*   Time-consuming process.
*   Limited casting size (though large parts are possible with specialized techniques).
*   Potential for porosity if dewaxing or pouring is not controlled.

**Applications:**

*   Aerospace components (e.g., turbine blades, impellers).
*   Medical implants (e.g., hip and knee replacements).
*   Jewellery.
*   Firearms components.
*   Precision machinery parts.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Ghosh & Mallik (2010):** Likely covers the historical roots of the process and the selection of refractory materials.
*   **Kalpakjian & Schmid:** Might focus on the design considerations for wax patterns and the importance of controlled heating.
*   **P N Rao (Vol-1):** Will provide detailed steps of shell formation, dewaxing, and pouring, potentially with diagrams.
*   **Foundry Technology by Peter Beeley:** A comprehensive resource for the metallurgical and refractory aspects of investment casting.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Classify casting techniques):** Investment casting is a specific and important casting category. (K2)
*   **CO5 (Develop products for socially relevant applications):** Medical implants are a prime example of socially relevant applications enabled by this process. (K3, K4, K5)

### 3. Die Casting

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Die casting is a metal casting process that involves pouring molten metal into a reusable metal mould (die) under high pressure. The die is typically made of hardened tool steel.
*   **Process Overview:**
    1.  **Die Preparation:** The die cavity is lubricated and cleaned.
    2.  **Injection:** Molten metal is injected into the die cavity under high pressure. There are two main types:
        *   **Hot-Chamber Die Casting:** The injection mechanism (plunger) is immersed in the molten metal. Suitable for low melting point alloys like zinc, lead, and tin.
        *   **Cold-Chamber Die Casting:** The molten metal is ladled into a shot sleeve, and then a plunger injects it into the die. Suitable for higher melting point alloys like aluminum, magnesium, and copper alloys.
    3.  **Solidification:** The metal solidifies rapidly under pressure.
    4.  **Die Opening and Ejection:** The die opens, and the casting is ejected.
    5.  **Trimming:** Excess material (flash) is removed.

**Advantages:**

*   High production rates.
*   Excellent dimensional accuracy and smooth surface finish.
*   Ability to produce thin-walled castings.
*   Good mechanical properties due to rapid solidification and fine grain structure.
*   Complex shapes can be achieved with minimal machining.

**Disadvantages:**

*   High initial tooling cost (dies).
*   Not suitable for all metals (limited by melting point and reactivity with die material).
*   Can trap gas porosity within the casting, affecting mechanical properties.
*   Limited casting size compared to sand casting.

**Applications:**

*   Automotive parts (e.g., engine blocks, transmission housings, door handles).
*   Electronics enclosures.
*   Appliance parts.
*   Toys.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Ghosh & Mallik (2010):** Will detail the differences between hot-chamber and cold-chamber processes and the materials used for dies.
*   **Kalpakjian & Schmid:** Provides extensive coverage on die casting machines, injection pressures, and the metallurgical implications of rapid solidification.
*   **P N Rao (Vol-1):** Offers a good overview of the process steps, die design considerations, and types of defects.
*   **Foundry Technology by Peter Beeley:** May discuss the fluid dynamics of metal injection and solidification.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**

### 4. Centrifugal Casting

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Centrifugal casting is a casting process where molten metal is poured into a rotating mould. The centrifugal force generated by the rotation causes the molten metal to be pressed against the mould walls, forming the casting.
*   **Types:**
    *   **True Centrifugal Casting:** Used to produce hollow cylindrical parts (pipes, tubes, sleeves). The mould rotates around its longitudinal axis.
    *   **Semi-Centrifugal Casting:** Used for disc-shaped or wheel-shaped parts. The mould rotates around a horizontal axis, and gravity also plays a role.
    *   **Centrifuging:** Used for smaller, complex castings. The mould rotates around an axis that is offset from the mould's center of gravity, forcing metal into all parts of the mould.
*   **Process Overview (True Centrifugal Casting):**
    1.  **Mould Preparation:** A cylindrical mould (often metal) is prepared and coated with refractory material.
    2.  **Rotation:** The mould is spun at high speed.
    3.  **Pouring:** Molten metal is poured into the rotating mould.
    4.  **Solidification:** The metal solidifies under centrifugal force, with denser impurities tending to collect on the inner surface.
    5.  **Cooling and Ejection:** The casting is cooled, and the mould rotation is stopped to remove the casting.
    6.  **Inner Surface Machining:** The inner surface, which may contain impurities, is often machined.

**Advantages:**

*   Produces dense, homogeneous castings with fine grain structure.
*   Good dimensional accuracy, especially for outer surfaces.
*   No central core is needed for hollow cylinders.
*   Can produce castings free from shrinkage porosity.
*   Good for producing pipes, tubes, and rings.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Limited to shapes that have rotational symmetry.
*   Inner surface can be rough and may require machining.
*   Higher initial equipment cost.
*   Can be challenging to control wall thickness accurately in true centrifugal casting.

**Applications:**

*   Pipes and tubes (cast iron, steel).
*   Cylinder liners.
*   Bushings and bearings.
*   Rolls for mills.
*   Brake drums.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Ghosh & Mallik (2010):** Likely explains the physics of centrifugal force and its application in different types of centrifugal casting.
*   **Kalpakjian & Schmid:** May detail the specific types of centrifugal casting and their applications, along with the equipment used.
*   **P N Rao (Vol-1):** Provides a good overview of the process steps, mould design, and the types of metals suitable for this method.
*   **Foundry Technology by Peter Beeley:** Offers in-depth discussion on mould materials, rotation speeds, and metal flow dynamics.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Classify casting techniques):** Centrifugal casting is a distinct and specialized casting method. (K2)

### 5. Continuous Casting

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Continuous casting is a process where molten metal is solidified into a semi-finished billet, bloom, or slab of a continuous cross-section. This process eliminates the need for individual moulds for each casting and allows for high production rates.
*   **Process Overview:**
    1.  **Molten Metal Supply:** Molten metal is supplied from a holding furnace or ladle.
    2.  **Tundish:** The molten metal flows into a tundish, which acts as a reservoir and distributor, ensuring a steady flow of metal into the mould.
    3.  **Water-Cooled Mould:** The molten metal enters a water-cooled copper mould, where the outer shell solidifies. The mould is typically oscillated vertically to prevent sticking.
    4.  **Secondary Cooling:** As the solidified shell emerges from the mould, it passes through a water spray cooling zone (secondary cooling) to complete solidification.
    5.  **Cutting:** The continuous strand of solidified metal is cut to desired lengths by a flying saw or shear.
    6.  **Straightening:** If the casting process involves bending (e.g., curved moulds), the strand is straightened.

**Advantages:**

*   High production rates.
*   Improved yield (less scrap compared to ingot casting).
*   Uniform microstructure and properties throughout the strand.
*   Eliminates secondary rolling operations for some semi-finished products.
*   Lower energy consumption compared to ingot casting.

**Disadvantages:**

*   High initial investment cost for the casting machine.
*   Limited to producing relatively simple cross-sections (billets, blooms, slabs).
*   Requires careful control of molten metal temperature and cooling rates.
*   Defects like surface cracks or internal segregation can occur if not controlled.

**Applications:**

*   Production of steel billets for re-rolling into bars, rods, and wire.
*   Production of steel slabs for rolling into plates and sheets.
*   Casting of aluminum, copper, and other non-ferrous metals into semi-finished shapes.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Ghosh & Mallik (2010):** Will likely discuss the importance of the tundish and mould design, as well as the cooling stages.
*   **Kalpakjian & Schmid:** Provides a comprehensive overview of the continuous casting process, including the different stages and the metallurgical aspects.
*   **P N Rao (Vol-1):** Offers a good explanation of the equipment used, the principles of solidification, and the types of products manufactured.
*   **Foundry Technology by Peter Beeley:** Could delve into the intricacies of mold oscillation and the heat transfer involved.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Classify casting techniques):** Continuous casting is a fundamentally different approach to casting compared to batch processes. (K2)

### 6. Squeeze Casting

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Squeeze casting, also known as liquid metal forging or shaped casting, is a process that combines casting and forging. Molten metal is poured into a die, and then a punch or die half applies high pressure to the molten metal as it solidifies.
*   **Process Overview:**
    1.  **Die Preparation:** The die cavity is preheated and lubricated.
    2.  **Pouring:** Molten metal is poured into the die.
    3.  **Pressing:** A punch or the upper die half is brought down with significant pressure onto the molten metal. This pressure is maintained until the metal solidifies.
    4.  **Solidification:** The pressure assists in feeding the casting as it shrinks and closes pores.
    5.  **Ejection:** The casting is ejected from the die.

**Advantages:**

*   Produces castings with superior mechanical properties (e.g., higher strength, ductility, fatigue life) due to fine grain structure, reduced porosity, and elimination of shrinkage voids.
*   Good dimensional accuracy and surface finish.
*   Reduced machining allowance.
*   Can produce complex shapes with integrated features.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Higher equipment cost (requires a hydraulic press or specialized machine).
*   Slower production rates compared to die casting.
*   Limited to metals that can withstand high pressure during solidification.
*   Die wear can be a concern.

**Applications:**

*   Automotive components requiring high strength and reliability (e.g., control arms, brake components, engine pistons).
*   Aerospace components.
*   Components for high-pressure applications.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Ghosh & Mallik (2010):** May describe the mechanics of applying pressure during solidification and its effect on porosity.
*   **Kalpakjian & Schmid:** Likely discusses the synergy between casting and forging and the resulting property improvements.
*   **P N Rao (Vol-1):** Might illustrate the process with diagrams and discuss the types of presses used.
*   **Cold and Hot Forging Fundamentals and Applications by Taylan Altan et al.:** While focusing on forging, it might touch upon squeeze casting as a hybrid process.
*   **ASM Metals Handbook, Vol. 14 (Forming and Forging):** Could offer detailed insights into the pressure application and its metallurgical consequences.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Classify casting techniques):** Squeeze casting is a unique hybrid process that should be classified accordingly. (K2)

### Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Briefly explain the principle of shell moulding and mention two advantages of this process over green sand casting.

**Answer:** Shell moulding involves creating a mould by coating a heated metal pattern with a thin shell of resin-bonded sand. The shell is then cured, baked, and assembled. Two advantages over green sand casting are:
1.  **Improved Surface Finish:** Due to the fine sand and smooth pattern surfaces.
2.  **Better Dimensional Accuracy:** Resulting in reduced machining allowance.

**Question 2:** What are the primary applications of precision investment casting? Give one specific example.

**Answer:** Precision investment casting is used for producing intricate, complex, and high-precision metal parts where dimensional accuracy and excellent surface finish are crucial.
*   **Example:** Turbine blades for jet engines, medical implants (like hip prosthetics).

**Question 3:** Differentiate between hot-chamber and cold-chamber die casting processes. For which types of alloys are they typically used?

**Answer:**
*   **Hot-Chamber Die Casting:** The injection cylinder and plunger are immersed in the molten metal. It's faster but limited to low melting point alloys (e.g., zinc, lead, tin) due to the risk of material seizing to the hot cylinder.
*   **Cold-Chamber Die Casting:** Molten metal is ladled into a separate shot sleeve, and then a plunger injects it into the die. This is suitable for higher melting point alloys (e.g., aluminum, magnesium, brass) as the injection mechanism is not constantly in contact with the molten metal.

**Question 4:** Describe the basic principle of centrifugal casting and state its main limitation.

**Answer:** In centrifugal casting, molten metal is poured into a rotating mould, and centrifugal force distributes the metal against the mould walls.
*   **Main Limitation:** It is primarily suitable for producing castings with rotational symmetry (e.g., pipes, tubes, rings).

**Question 5:** What is the main benefit of continuous casting in metal production?

**Answer:** The main benefit of continuous casting is its ability to produce semi-finished metal products (billets, blooms, slabs) of consistent cross-section and uniform microstructure at high production rates, leading to improved yield and reduced processing steps.

**Question 6:** How does squeeze casting improve the mechanical properties of a casting compared to conventional casting?

**Answer:** Squeeze casting applies high pressure during solidification, which helps to:
1.  **Reduce Porosity:** It closes shrinkage voids and gas pores, leading to a denser casting.
2.  **Promote Fine Grain Structure:** The pressure can refine the grain size.
These factors contribute to increased strength, ductility, and fatigue resistance of the casting.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Trade-offs:** Each special casting process has its own set of advantages and disadvantages, making them suitable for specific applications. The choice depends on factors like desired accuracy, surface finish, complexity of shape, production volume, and cost.
*   **Tooling Cost:** Many special casting processes (e.g., die casting, investment casting, shell moulding) involve higher initial tooling costs but offer benefits in terms of production efficiency and quality.
*   **Metallurgical Aspects:** The interaction between molten metal and the mould material, cooling rates, and solidification phenomena are critical for achieving the desired casting quality in all these processes.
*   **CO1 Focus:** This entire topic directly addresses CO1 by detailing and classifying various advanced casting techniques.
*   **CO5 Relevance:** Processes like investment casting are crucial for developing products in sectors like healthcare and aerospace, aligning with CO5.
