### **1. Introduction to Arc Welding**

Arc welding is a fusion welding process that utilizes an electric arc to generate intense heat, melting the base metals and filler material (if used) to form a molten pool. This molten pool solidifies to create a strong metallurgical bond.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Electric Arc:** A sustained electrical discharge between two electrodes through a conductive medium (usually ionized gas).
*   **Fusion Welding:** A welding process where the base metals are melted to form a molten pool, and filler material is added if necessary.
*   **Base Metal:** The parent material being joined.
*   **Filler Material:** Additional material added to the joint to fill the gap and contribute to the weld.

### **2. Classification of Arc Welding Processes**

Arc welding processes can be broadly classified based on the type of electrode used:

#### **2.1. Consumable Electrode Arc Welding**

In these processes, the electrode itself is consumed during welding, acting as both the source of the arc and a source of filler metal.

**Key Concept:** The electrode melts and deposits metal into the weld pool.

**Sub-Classification (based on shielding):**

*   **Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW):** Uses a flux-coated consumable electrode.
*   **Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW) / Metal Inert Gas (MIG) Welding:** Uses a bare wire consumable electrode with an external shielding gas.
*   **Flux Cored Arc Welding (FCAW):** Uses a tubular consumable electrode containing flux and often alloying elements, with or without external shielding gas.

#### **2.2. Non-Consumable Electrode Arc Welding**

In these processes, the electrode is not consumed during welding. It generates the arc, and filler material is supplied separately if required.

**Key Concept:** The electrode maintains its form and shape, while filler material is added to the weld pool.

**Sub-Classification (based on shielding):**

*   **Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW) / Tungsten Inert Gas (TIG) Welding:** Uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode with an external inert shielding gas.
*   **Submerged Arc Welding (SAW):** Uses a continuous bare wire consumable electrode, but the arc is submerged under a granular flux. While the electrode is consumable, the *process* itself is distinct due to the flux shielding, and the arc is contained. *Note: SAW is often discussed alongside consumable processes due to the wire electrode, but its shielding mechanism is unique.*
*   **Atomic Hydrogen Welding (AHW):** Uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode and a hydrogen atmosphere for shielding.
*   **Plasma Arc Welding (PAW):** Uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode and a constricted plasma arc for shielding.

**Important Point to Remember:** The fundamental difference lies in whether the electrode melts into the weld pool or not.

### **3. Key Arc Welding Processes**

Let's delve into the details of the commonly used arc welding processes:

#### **3.1. Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW)**

*   **Also Known As:** Stick welding.
*   **Electrode:** Consumable, flux-coated. The flux coating performs multiple functions:
    *   Provides shielding gas by vaporizing.
    *   Forms slag to protect the molten pool.
    *   Adds alloying elements.
    *   Stabilizes the arc.
*   **Power Source:** DC (DCEN - electrode negative, DCEP - electrode positive) or AC. DCEP is generally preferred for better penetration.
*   **Applications:** Widely used in construction, fabrication, repair, and maintenance. Suitable for various metals like carbon steel, stainless steel, cast iron, aluminum.
*   **Advantages:** Simple equipment, portable, versatile, low cost, can be used in various positions (flat, horizontal, vertical, overhead).
*   **Disadvantages:** Slower welding speed, requires skill to produce clean welds, slag removal is necessary, not ideal for thin materials.
*   **Textbook References:**
    *   *Manufacturing Science* by Ghosh and Mallik, Section on SMAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Engineering and Technology* by Kalpakjian and Schmid, Section on SMAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Technology Volume -1* by P N Rao, Section on SMAW.
*   **Reference Book Reference:** *ASM Metals Handbook, Vol. 14* provides detailed information on SMAW electrodes and techniques.

#### **3.2. Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW)**

*   **Also Known As:** TIG welding.
*   **Electrode:** Non-consumable tungsten electrode.
*   **Shielding:** Inert gas (e.g., Argon, Helium) supplied externally.
*   **Filler Material:** Added separately as a rod or wire, or the weld can be made without filler material (autogenous welding).
*   **Power Source:** AC for aluminum and magnesium, DC (DCEP or DCEN) for steel and other metals. DCEP is generally preferred for steel.
*   **Applications:** High-quality welds on a wide range of metals, including aluminum, stainless steel, titanium, magnesium, copper alloys, and carbon steel. Used in aerospace, automotive, piping, and precision fabrication.
*   **Advantages:** Produces very clean, high-quality welds with excellent appearance, precise heat control, no slag, can weld very thin materials, no spatter.
*   **Disadvantages:** Slower welding speed, requires high skill, sensitive to contamination (requires clean base metal and filler), more expensive equipment and consumables.
*   **Textbook References:**
    *   *Manufacturing Science* by Ghosh and Mallik, Section on GTAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Engineering and Technology* by Kalpakjian and Schmid, Section on GTAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Technology Volume -1* by P N Rao, Section on GTAW.
*   **Reference Book Reference:** *ASM Metals Handbook, Vol. 14* details GTAW applications and filler metals.

#### **3.3. Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW)**

*   **Also Known As:** MIG welding (Metal Inert Gas).
*   **Electrode:** Consumable bare wire electrode, fed continuously from a spool.
*   **Shielding:** Inert gas (e.g., Argon, Helium) or a mixture of inert gas and active gas (e.g., CO2, Oxygen) supplied externally. The choice of shielding gas affects the arc mode and weld properties.
*   **Power Source:** DC (DCEP is most common).
*   **Transfer Modes:**
    *   **Short-Circuiting Transfer:** Low voltage/amperage, good for thin materials and out-of-position welding.
    *   **Globular Transfer:** High voltage/amperage, unstable, large molten droplets, prone to spatter and defects.
    *   **Spray Transfer:** High voltage/amperage, fine molten droplets transferred across the arc, high deposition rates, good for thicker materials in flat position.
    *   **Pulsed Spray Transfer:** Similar to spray transfer but pulsed, allows for better control, good for out-of-position and thin materials.
*   **Applications:** Widely used in automotive, manufacturing, structural fabrication, and repair. Suitable for steel, stainless steel, aluminum, and copper alloys.
*   **Advantages:** High welding speed, good deposition rates, relatively easy to learn, continuous welding, minimal post-weld cleaning.
*   **Disadvantages:** Sensitive to drafts (shielding gas can be blown away), requires external gas supply, equipment can be more complex than SMAW, can have limited penetration in short-circuiting mode.
*   **Textbook References:**
    *   *Manufacturing Science* by Ghosh and Mallik, Section on GMAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Engineering and Technology* by Kalpakjian and Schmid, Section on GMAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Technology Volume -1* by P N Rao, Section on GMAW.
*   **Reference Book Reference:** *ASM Metals Handbook, Vol. 14* covers GMAW shielding gases and transfer modes.

#### **3.4. Submerged Arc Welding (SAW)**

*   **Electrode:** Consumable bare wire electrode, fed continuously.
*   **Shielding:** Arc is submerged under a granular flux. The flux melts to form a protective slag and shielding gas.
*   **Power Source:** AC or DC.
*   **Applications:** High-quality, high-deposition rate welding for thick plates. Widely used in shipbuilding, heavy fabrication, pressure vessel manufacturing, and pipe welding. Primarily used in the flat or horizontal position.
*   **Advantages:** Very high deposition rates, deep penetration, excellent weld quality, minimal fumes and spatter, can be automated.
*   **Disadvantages:** Limited to flat and horizontal positions, requires flux handling and recovery, not suitable for thin materials, not easily visible arc.
*   **Textbook References:**
    *   *Manufacturing Science* by Ghosh and Mallik, Section on SAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Engineering and Technology* by Kalpakjian and Schmid, Section on SAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Technology Volume -1* by P N Rao, Section on SAW.
*   **Reference Book Reference:** *ASM Metals Handbook, Vol. 14* details flux types and SAW process parameters.

#### **3.5. Atomic Hydrogen Welding (AHW)**

*   **Electrode:** Non-consumable tungsten electrode.
*   **Shielding:** Atomic hydrogen generated by an electric arc between two tungsten electrodes in a hydrogen atmosphere. The hydrogen molecules break down into highly reactive atomic hydrogen, which then shields the weld pool and reduces oxidation.
*   **Power Source:** AC.
*   **Applications:** Used for joining stainless steel, nickel alloys, and for surfacing. Less common today due to the advent of GTAW and GMAW.
*   **Advantages:** Produces clean welds with excellent ductility, no slag.
*   **Disadvantages:** Requires a hydrogen gas supply, can cause hydrogen embrittlement in some steels, not as widely used as other arc welding processes.
*   **Textbook References:**
    *   *Manufacturing Science* by Ghosh and Mallik, Section on AHW (may be limited).
    *   *Manufacturing Technology Volume -1* by P N Rao, Section on AHW (may be limited).
*   **Reference Book Reference:** Older editions of welding handbooks might provide more detail on AHW.

#### **3.6. Plasma Arc Welding (PAW)**

*   **Electrode:** Non-consumable tungsten electrode.
*   **Shielding:** Plasma gas (e.g., Argon, Nitrogen) and shielding gas (e.g., Argon). The plasma gas is passed through a constricted nozzle, creating a focused and hotter arc.
*   **Key Feature:** Arc constricting nozzle.
*   **Power Source:** DC.
*   **Applications:** Precision welding for high-quality joints, especially on thin materials and for keyhole welding on thicker sections. Used in aerospace, electronics, and medical device manufacturing.
*   **Advantages:** Very stable and concentrated arc, deeper penetration with less heat input compared to GTAW, faster welding speeds, can achieve keyhole welding, less sensitive to arc length variations.
*   **Disadvantages:** More complex and expensive equipment, requires careful setup, potential for nozzle erosion.
*   **Textbook References:**
    *   *Manufacturing Science* by Ghosh and Mallik, Section on PAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Engineering and Technology* by Kalpakjian and Schmid, Section on PAW.
    *   *Manufacturing Technology Volume -1* by P N Rao, Section on PAW.
*   **Reference Book Reference:** *ASM Metals Handbook, Vol. 14* provides information on PAW applications and parameters.

### **4. Applications of Arc Welding**

Arc welding processes are fundamental to modern manufacturing and are used across a vast range of industries.

**General Applications:**

*   **Construction:** Bridges, buildings, pipelines, offshore structures. SMAW, GMAW, SAW are common.
*   **Automotive:** Vehicle frames, chassis, exhaust systems, body panels. GMAW is dominant due to speed and automation.
*   **Aerospace:** Aircraft structures, engine components, fuel tanks. GTAW and PAW are used for high-quality, precise welds on exotic materials.
*   **Shipbuilding:** Hull construction, piping, structural components. SMAW, GMAW, SAW are used extensively.
*   **Heavy Fabrication:** Pressure vessels, tanks, machinery bases. SAW and GMAW are common for thick sections.
*   **Manufacturing:** General fabrication of metal products, machinery, appliances. SMAW, GMAW, GTAW.
*   **Repair and Maintenance:** Repairing broken machinery, tools, and structures. SMAW is very versatile for this.
*   **Pipelines:** Joining sections of pipelines for oil, gas, and water. SMAW and GTAW are often used for root passes, followed by other processes.

**Examples:**

*   **Building a skyscraper:** Thousands of steel beams are joined using SMAW.
*   **Manufacturing a car body:** Robotic GMAW ensures high speed and consistent quality.
*   **Building an airplane wing:** GTAW is used to join aluminum alloys for lightweight and strong structures.
*   **Fabricating a large storage tank:** SAW is employed for rapid and robust welding of thick steel plates.

### **5. Process Parameters in Arc Welding**

Controlling process parameters is crucial for achieving sound welds with desired properties.

**Key Parameters:**

1.  **Welding Current (Amperage):**
    *   **Effect:** Controls the heat input. Higher current leads to deeper penetration and wider bead.
    *   **Impact:** Too low: insufficient fusion, incomplete penetration. Too high: excessive penetration, burn-through, increased spatter, wider heat-affected zone (HAZ).
    *   **Relationship:** Often directly proportional to penetration and weld bead width.

2.  **Arc Voltage:**
    *   **Effect:** Controls the arc length and influences the arc shape and bead profile. Higher voltage generally leads to a longer arc and a wider, flatter bead.
    *   **Impact:** Too low: constricts the arc, can lead to poor wetting and cigar-shaped beads. Too high: wider arc, less penetration, increased spatter, potential for porosity.
    *   **Relationship:** Affects bead width and appearance more than penetration.

3.  **Welding Speed (Travel Speed):**
    *   **Effect:** Controls the amount of heat deposited per unit length of weld.
    *   **Impact:** Too slow: excessive heat input, wider HAZ, potential for burn-through or collapse of molten pool. Too fast: insufficient fusion, incomplete penetration, narrow bead.
    *   **Relationship:** Inverse relationship with heat input per unit length.

4.  **Electrode Diameter:**
    *   **Effect:** Larger diameter electrodes generally require higher current to melt at a similar rate.
    *   **Impact:** Affects deposition rate and penetration. Thicker electrodes are used for thicker materials.

5.  **Electrode Stick-out (GTAW/PAW) or Contact Tip to Work Distance (GMAW):**
    *   **Effect:** Influences arc stability, shielding effectiveness, and heat input.
    *   **Impact:** Too long: reduces penetration, can lead to oxidation if shielding is compromised. Too short: can cause short-circuiting and damage the electrode or nozzle.

6.  **Polarity (DC Welding):**
    *   **Direct Current Electrode Positive (DCEP/Reverse Polarity):** Most of the heat is generated at the workpiece, leading to deeper penetration. Used in SMAW and GMAW for steel.
    *   **Direct Current Electrode Negative (DCEN/Straight Polarity):** Most of the heat is generated at the electrode, leading to shallower penetration. Used in GTAW for aluminum and magnesium to improve cleaning action.

7.  **Shielding Gas Flow Rate (GTAW/GMAW/PAW):**
    *   **Effect:** Ensures adequate protection of the molten pool from atmospheric contamination.
    *   **Impact:** Too low: poor shielding, porosity, oxidation. Too high: turbulence in the weld pool, can draw in surrounding air, potentially causing contamination.

8.  **Wire Feed Speed (GMAW/SAW):**
    *   **Effect:** Directly controls the deposition rate. Higher feed speed means more filler metal deposited.
    *   **Impact:** Needs to be matched with welding current and voltage for stable operation.

9.  **Flux Type and Thickness (SAW):**
    *   **Effect:** Influences weld metallurgy, shielding quality, and slag properties.
    *   **Impact:** Choice of flux is critical for achieving desired weld mechanical properties.

**Heat Input Calculation:**

Heat input is a crucial parameter that influences the weld microstructure and mechanical properties, particularly the Heat Affected Zone (HAZ).

*   **Formula:**
    $HI = \frac{V \times I \times 60}{TS}$ (Joules/mm or J/in)

    Where:
    *   $HI$ = Heat Input
    *   $V$ = Arc Voltage (Volts)
    *   $I$ = Welding Current (Amperes)
    *   $TS$ = Travel Speed (mm/min or in/min)

*   **Efficiency Factor (k):** In reality, not all electrical energy is converted into heat in the weld pool. An efficiency factor (k) is often introduced, which depends on the welding process and shielding.
    $HI = k \times \frac{V \times I \times 60}{TS}$
    *   Typical values of k:
        *   SMAW: ~0.7 - 0.8
        *   GTAW: ~0.6 - 0.7
        *   GMAW: ~0.7 - 0.8
        *   SAW: ~0.8 - 0.9

### **6. Numerical Problems in Arc Welding**

These problems help in understanding the quantitative aspects of arc welding parameters.

**Example Problems:**

**Problem 1:**
A GMAW operation is carried out with the following parameters:
Voltage ($V$) = 24 V
Current ($I$) = 150 A
Travel Speed ($TS$) = 300 mm/min

Calculate the heat input per unit length of weld, assuming an efficiency factor ($k$) of 0.8.

**Solution:**
Using the heat input formula:
$HI = k \times \frac{V \times I \times 60}{TS}$
$HI = 0.8 \times \frac{24 \, \text{V} \times 150 \, \text{A} \times 60}{300 \, \text{mm/min}}$
$HI = 0.8 \times \frac{216000}{300}$
$HI = 0.8 \times 720$
$HI = 576 \, \text{J/mm}$

**Problem 2:**
In an SMAW process for joining steel plates, the arc voltage is 25 V and the welding current is 120 A. The desired heat input is 1.0 kJ/mm (1000 J/mm), and the efficiency factor ($k$) for SMAW is 0.75. What should be the travel speed?

**Solution:**
Rearranging the heat input formula to solve for travel speed:
$TS = k \times \frac{V \times I \times 60}{HI}$
$TS = 0.75 \times \frac{25 \, \text{V} \times 120 \, \text{A} \times 60}{1000 \, \text{J/mm}}$
$TS = 0.75 \times \frac{180000}{1000}$
$TS = 0.75 \times 180$
$TS = 135 \, \text{mm/min}$

**Problem 3:**
A GTAW process uses a travel speed of 250 mm/min, an arc voltage of 20 V, and a welding current of 100 A. The efficiency factor ($k$) for GTAW is 0.65. If the weld bead is 8 mm wide and the penetration depth is 3 mm, calculate the area of the cross-section of the weld bead.

**Solution:**
First, calculate the heat input:
$HI = k \times \frac{V \times I \times 60}{TS}$
$HI = 0.65 \times \frac{20 \, \text{V} \times 100 \, \text{A} \times 60}{250 \, \text{mm/min}}$
$HI = 0.65 \times \frac{120000}{250}$
$HI = 0.65 \times 480$
$HI = 312 \, \text{J/mm}$

Assuming the weld bead cross-section is roughly triangular or elliptical, an approximation can be made. However, a more direct approach based on energy deposition is to relate heat input to weld area for a given thickness.

For a simplified calculation of the weld bead cross-sectional area, if we assume a rectangular cross-section (which is a rough approximation):
Area $\approx$ Bead Width $\times$ Penetration Depth
Area $\approx 8 \, \text{mm} \times 3 \, \text{mm} = 24 \, \text{mm}^2$

*Note: A more accurate calculation of weld bead area from parameters is complex and often involves empirical relationships or geometric modeling. However, for understanding the impact of parameters, relating them to dimensions is key.*

**Answers to Practice Questions:**

1.  In consumable arc welding, the electrode melts and becomes part of the weld. In non-consumable arc welding, the electrode does not melt and filler metal is added separately if needed.
2.  **Advantages:** Simple equipment, portable, versatile, low cost, usable in various positions.
    **Disadvantages:** Slower welding speed, requires skill, slag removal needed, not ideal for thin materials.

4.  **GTAW:** Argon, Helium.
    **GMAW:** Argon, Helium, CO2, or mixtures of these gases.
5.  Short-Circuiting Transfer, Globular Transfer, Spray Transfer, Pulsed Spray Transfer.
6.  $HI = 0.75 \times \frac{28 \, \text{V} \times 150 \, \text{A} \times 60}{10 \, \text{in/min}} = 0.75 \times \frac{252000}{10} = 0.75 \times 25200 = 18900 \, \text{J/in} = 18.9 \, \text{kJ/in}$.

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Arc Stability:** Crucial for consistent weld quality. Affected by voltage, current, electrode type, and shielding.
*   **Heat Input Control:** The primary factor influencing the Heat Affected Zone (HAZ) size, microstructure, and mechanical properties of the weld.
*   **Shielding:** Essential to prevent atmospheric contamination (oxygen, nitrogen) of the molten weld pool, which can cause porosity and embrittlement.
*   **Polarity:** DCEP generally gives deeper penetration than DCEN.
*   **Material Compatibility:** The choice of welding process and consumables (electrodes, filler metals) must be compatible with the base metals being joined.
*   **Weld Defects:** Common defects include porosity, slag inclusions, lack of fusion, undercut, and cracks. Understanding process parameters helps prevent these.
*   **Automation:** Many arc welding processes, especially GMAW and SAW, are highly amenable to automation for increased productivity and consistency.
*   **Safety:** Arc welding involves intense light (UV/IR radiation), heat, fumes, and electrical hazards. Proper Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and ventilation are mandatory.
