# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 4: Value Analysis and Value Engineering

## Topic: Esteem Value

### 1. Introduction to Value Analysis and Value Engineering

Value Analysis (VA) and Value Engineering (VE) are systematic methods used to improve the "value" of a product, service, or project. Value is typically defined as the ratio of function to cost. The goal is to achieve the required functions at the lowest possible cost without sacrificing quality, reliability, or performance.

*   **Value Analysis (VA):** Applied to existing products or services, often in production. Focuses on reducing costs while maintaining or improving function.
*   **Value Engineering (VE):** Applied to new products or designs during the design and development phase. Proactive approach to prevent unnecessary costs.

This module focuses on the various types of value that are considered in these methodologies.

### 2. Types of Value in Value Analysis and Value Engineering

The core principle of VA/VE is to increase value by improving function or reducing cost. To achieve this, understanding different types of value is crucial.

#### 2.1. Use Value

*   **Definition:** The value a product or component contributes to its intended purpose or function. It is directly related to the performance and characteristics that fulfill a need.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Relates to the "what it does."
    *   Focuses on the functional contribution of an item.
    *   Objective and measurable based on performance criteria.
*   **Examples:**
    *   A bolt's use value is its ability to fasten two components together securely.
    *   A motor's use value is its ability to convert electrical energy into mechanical energy.
*   **Textbook/Reference Relevance:**
    *   **Thuesen & Fabrycky (1966):** Likely discusses functional analysis as a fundamental step in economic analysis of engineering alternatives.
    *   **Park (2001):** Emphasizes defining functions clearly as a prerequisite for value improvement.

#### 2.2. Esteem Value

*   **Definition:** The value derived from the prestige, reputation, aesthetic appeal, or emotional satisfaction associated with a product or its brand. It's the "what it is" value, often subjective.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Relates to the non-functional aspects, such as:
        *   **Aesthetics:** Appearance, style, design.
        *   **Brand Reputation:** Trust, perceived quality, status associated with the brand.
        *   **Exclusivity/Rarity:** Limited editions, unique features.
        *   **Emotional Appeal:** Nostalgia, sentimental value, perceived luxury.
    *   Often psychological and subjective.
    *   Can command a premium price even if functional value is similar to alternatives.
*   **Examples:**
    *   A luxury watch brand (e.g., Rolex) where a significant portion of the price is due to brand prestige and esteem, not just its timekeeping function.
    *   A designer handbag or a sports car with a high-profile brand name.
    *   The perceived value of owning a piece of art or an antique.
    *   The aesthetic design of a smartphone that makes it desirable beyond its basic communication functions.
*   **Textbook/Reference Relevance:**
    *   **Geetika, Ghosh & Chodhury (2015):** Might touch upon this in the context of marketing and pricing strategies, where brand equity and customer perception influence demand and willingness to pay.
    *   **Blank & Tarquin (7th Ed.):** Could discuss how perceived value, driven by factors beyond pure functionality, influences customer choice and market positioning.
    *   **Newman & Lavelle (2002):** May explore non-quantifiable benefits that customers associate with products, which can contribute to perceived value.

#### 2.3. Cost Value

*   **Definition:** The value of an item in terms of its production cost. It represents the resources (materials, labor, overhead) expended to create the product or service.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Relates to the "what it costs."
    *   Objective and quantifiable.
    *   Includes direct costs (materials, labor) and indirect costs (overhead, depreciation).
*   **Examples:**
    *   The manufacturing cost of a simple metal bracket.
    *   The cost of raw materials and labor to produce a basic chair.
*   **Textbook/Reference Relevance:**
    *   **Paneerselvam (2012):** Will extensively cover cost accounting principles, cost behaviors, and cost estimation methods, all relevant to understanding cost value.
    *   **Thuesen & Fabrycky (1966):** Essential for understanding the economic evaluation of alternatives, which heavily relies on cost analysis.

#### 2.4. Exchange Value

*   **Definition:** The value an item holds in the marketplace, i.e., what it can be traded or sold for. It is determined by the interaction of supply and demand.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Relates to the "what it will fetch."
    *   Market-driven and subject to fluctuations.
    *   Can be influenced by esteem value, use value, and competition.
*   **Examples:**
    *   The selling price of a new smartphone.
    *   The resale value of a used car.
    *   The market price of a commodity like oil.
*   **Textbook/Reference Relevance:**
    *   **Geetika, Ghosh & Chodhury (2015):** Directly relates to microeconomic principles of demand, supply, and market equilibrium, which determine exchange value.
    *   **Khan (2011):** Discusses financial markets and pricing mechanisms, which are core to understanding exchange value in a broader economic context.

### 3. The Role of Esteem Value in VA/VE

While VA/VE primarily aims to optimize the "function/cost" ratio, **esteem value plays a critical role in understanding customer perception and market success.**

*   **Impact on Pricing:** Products with high esteem value can command higher prices, even if their functional value is comparable to cheaper alternatives. This is because customers are willing to pay a premium for the associated prestige, brand image, or aesthetic appeal.
*   **Customer Loyalty:** Esteem value can foster strong brand loyalty. Customers may repeatedly purchase from a brand they associate with positive emotions or social status, even if functionally similar products are available.
*   **Marketing and Branding:** Esteem value is heavily influenced by marketing, advertising, and branding efforts. Companies invest significant resources in building and maintaining the esteem associated with their products.
*   **VA/VE Considerations:**
    *   **Preserving Esteem:** When performing VA/VE, it's crucial not to inadvertently reduce the esteem value of a product. For example, changing materials to cheaper ones might degrade the perceived quality or luxury feel, even if the function remains the same.
    *   **Leveraging Esteem:** Conversely, understanding esteem value can help identify opportunities. If a product lacks esteem value, VA/VE efforts might involve design improvements or material changes that enhance its aesthetic appeal or perceived quality, thereby increasing its exchange value.
    *   **Targeting Markets:** The relative importance of esteem value varies across different market segments and product categories. For luxury goods, esteem value is paramount. For commodity products, use value and cost value might be more dominant.

### 4. How Esteem Value is Created and Maintained

Engineers and product developers need to be aware of how esteem value is built to either preserve it during cost reduction efforts or to incorporate elements that enhance it.

*   **Design and Aesthetics:**
    *   Sleek, modern, or classic designs.
    *   Color choices, finishes, and materials used.
    *   Ergonomics and user experience.
*   **Brand Reputation and Heritage:**
    *   Long-standing history of quality and reliability.
    *   Association with innovation or excellence.
    *   Endorsements by reputable figures or organizations.
*   **Marketing and Communication:**
    *   Advertising campaigns that evoke desired emotions or lifestyles.
    *   Packaging and presentation.
    *   Public relations and media coverage.
*   **Customer Service and Experience:**
    *   High-quality pre-sale and post-sale support.
    *   Exclusive customer programs or privileges.
*   **Perceived Quality:**
    *   Materials chosen, even if not functionally critical.
    *   Precision in manufacturing and assembly.
    *   Durability and longevity perceived by the customer.

### 5. Incorporating Esteem Value in VA/VE Decision Making

When evaluating potential changes through VA/VE, engineers should consider the impact on esteem value.

*   **Scenario:** A team is considering replacing a high-quality, expensive metal casing on an electronic device with a cheaper plastic one to reduce cost.
    *   **Use Value:** The casing still protects the internal components.
    *   **Cost Value:** Significantly reduced.
    *   **Exchange Value:** Might initially decrease due to lower perceived quality.
    *   **Esteem Value:** Likely to decrease if the metal casing contributed to a premium, luxurious feel. Customers might perceive the plastic casing as less durable, less premium, and less desirable.
*   **Decision:** The VA/VE team must weigh the cost savings against the potential loss in esteem value. If the product targets a market where premium feel is crucial (e.g., high-end consumer electronics), the cost savings might not justify the loss in customer appeal and potential sales.
*   **Mitigation:** If a change is necessary, efforts can be made to mitigate the loss of esteem value, such as:
    *   Using high-quality, textured plastics that mimic the feel of metal.
    *   Investing in superior paint finishes.
    *   Enhancing other aspects of the product's design or branding.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Define Esteem Value and provide two distinct examples of products where Esteem Value significantly influences its market price.

**Answer 1:**
Esteem Value is the value derived from the prestige, reputation, aesthetic appeal, or emotional satisfaction associated with a product or its brand. It relates to the subjective perception of desirability beyond pure functionality.

*   **Example 1: Luxury Cars (e.g., Ferrari, Rolls-Royce):** A significant portion of their high price is attributed to the brand's reputation for exclusivity, performance, craftsmanship, and the social status they confer upon their owners. The driving function is similar to many less expensive cars, but the esteem value commands a premium.
*   **Example 2: High-End Fashion Apparel (e.g., Louis Vuitton, Chanel):** The brand name, designer's reputation, quality of materials (even beyond functional necessity), and exclusivity contribute immense esteem value, allowing these items to be priced far above their cost value or even their basic use value (e.g., providing warmth or covering).

**Question 2:**
During a Value Engineering study for a consumer appliance, the team proposes replacing a brushed aluminum control panel with a high-gloss plastic one to save manufacturing costs. Explain how Esteem Value might be affected and what other value types are relevant in this decision.

**Answer 2:**
*   **Impact on Esteem Value:** The brushed aluminum control panel likely contributes to the appliance's **esteem value** by conveying a sense of premium quality, durability, and modern aesthetics. Replacing it with a high-gloss plastic panel might reduce this esteem value if the plastic is perceived as less premium, more prone to scratches, or less robust. Customers may associate the aluminum with a higher-end product.
*   **Other Relevant Value Types:**
    *   **Use Value:** The control panel's function (operating the appliance) remains. However, if the plastic scratches easily or feels flimsy, its *perceived* use value might also be slightly affected.
    *   **Cost Value:** Will significantly decrease due to the lower cost of plastic and potentially simpler manufacturing processes.
    *   **Exchange Value:** The market price might need to be adjusted. If the loss of esteem value is significant, the appliance might sell for less or face reduced demand at the original price point, especially in markets where aesthetics and premium feel are highly valued.

The decision requires a trade-off analysis: weighing the cost savings against the potential negative impact on perceived quality and customer desirability (esteem value), and ultimately, the product's market performance (exchange value).

**Question 3 (Exercise):**
Consider a company manufacturing office chairs. They are looking to reduce costs. Identify one potential area where reducing cost might negatively impact Esteem Value, and suggest a mitigation strategy that attempts to preserve Esteem Value.

**Answer 3 (Exercise):**
*   **Potential Area Impacting Esteem Value:** Switching from a high-density, supportive foam in the seat cushion to a lower-density, cheaper foam. While the chair might still offer basic comfort (Use Value) and is cheaper to produce (Cost Value), customers who value long-term comfort and perceived quality might notice the difference. The chair might feel "cheaper" or less luxurious, reducing its Esteem Value.
*   **Mitigation Strategy:**
    *   **Design & Branding:** Emphasize ergonomic features in marketing and design documentation, using terms like "advanced contouring" or "supportive matrix."
    *   **Aesthetics:** Ensure the fabric used for the cushion is high-quality and visually appealing.
    *   **Customer Experience:** Offer a longer warranty on cushioning or a satisfaction guarantee to build confidence and offset concerns about reduced density.
    *   **Selective Application:** If possible, use the higher-density foam only in the most critical contact areas of the seat, while using the cheaper foam in less critical zones, optimizing cost reduction while maintaining a good perceived feel.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Value = Function / Cost:** This is the fundamental equation in VA/VE.
*   **Esteem Value is Subjective:** It's about perception, emotion, and psychological appeal, not just physical attributes.
*   **Marketing and Branding are Key Drivers:** Esteem value is heavily influenced by how a product is presented and perceived in the market.
*   **Avoid Sacrificing Esteem Unnecessarily:** Cost reduction efforts must consider the potential negative impact on customer perception and willingness to pay.
*   **Balance is Crucial:** VA/VE aims for optimal value, which means balancing all types of value, including the often intangible esteem value.
*   **Target Market Matters:** The importance of esteem value differs significantly across different product categories and customer segments.
