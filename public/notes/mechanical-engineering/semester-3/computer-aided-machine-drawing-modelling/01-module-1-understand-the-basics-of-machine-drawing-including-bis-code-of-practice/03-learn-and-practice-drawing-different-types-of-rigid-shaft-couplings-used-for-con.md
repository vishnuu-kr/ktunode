# Computer-Aided Machine Drawing & Modelling: Module 1 - Rigid Shaft Couplings

This module introduces the foundational principles of machine drawing using Computer-Aided Design (CAD) software, with a specific focus on understanding and drawing various types of rigid shaft couplings.

## Module Overview

This module aims to equip students with the essential knowledge and practical skills required for creating accurate and informative machine drawings. We will delve into the fundamental concepts of machine drawing, including drawing standards (BIS), line types, dimensioning techniques, scaling, sectional views, and geometric tolerancing. A significant portion of this module will be dedicated to the practical application of these principles in drawing different types of rigid shaft couplings using 2D drafting software.

## Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   Understand and apply the principles of the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) code of practice for technical drawings.
*   Differentiate and utilize various types of lines in technical drawings.
*   Apply appropriate dimensioning techniques to convey critical information.
*   Select and apply suitable scales for drawings.
*   Create and interpret sectional views to reveal internal features.
*   Understand the concepts and applications of geometric tolerances.
*   Explain the importance and role of Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T) in manufacturing.
*   Draw different types of rigid shaft couplings (e.g., Flange Coupling, Protected Flange Coupling) using 2D drafting software.

## Key Concepts and Definitions

### 1. Machine Drawing Basics

Machine drawing is the language of engineers. It's a graphical representation of mechanical components and assemblies that communicates design intent, manufacturing information, and inspection requirements.

### 2. BIS Code of Practice for Technical Drawings

*   **Purpose:** To ensure uniformity, clarity, and accuracy in engineering drawings across India. It provides guidelines for line types, dimensioning, lettering, scales, projection methods, and more.
*   **Key Standards (Examples):**
    *   **IS 10387:** Specifies general principles of dimensioning.
    *   **IS 919:** Deals with fits and tolerances.
    *   **IS 696:** Covers drawing instruments and their use.
*   **Importance:** Adherence to BIS codes is crucial for effective communication between designers, manufacturers, and quality control personnel.
    *   *(Reference: Fundamentals of Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing by Alex Krulikowski, while primarily focused on GD&T, emphasizes the need for standardized drawing practices for clear communication, which aligns with the spirit of BIS codes.)*

### 3. Types of Lines

Different line types convey specific information about the object being drawn.

| Line Type              | Appearance       | Application                                                                                                   |
| :--------------------- | :--------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Visible/Object Line** | Thick, Solid     | Outlines of visible features of an object.                                                                    |
| **Hidden/Dashed Line**  | Thick, Dashed    | Outlines of features that are not visible in the current view.                                                |
| **Center Line**        | Thin, Alternating Long and Short Dashes | Indicates the center of holes, cylindrical parts, and axes of symmetry. Used for dimensioning diameters and lengths. |
| **Dimension Line**     | Thin, Solid, with Arrowheads | Indicates the size or location of a feature.                                                                |
| **Extension Line**     | Thin, Solid      | Extends from the object to show the extent of a dimension.                                                    |
| **Leader Line**        | Thin, Solid, with Arrowhead | Points to a feature to which a note or dimension applies.                                                     |
| **Cutting Plane Line** | Thick, Dashed, with Arrowheads | Indicates the location and direction of a cutting plane for sectional views.                                  |
| **Section Line**       | Thin, Solid, at an angle | Used in sectional views to indicate the surface that has been cut.                                             |
| **Phantom Line**       | Thin, Alternating Long Dashes and Two Short Dashes | Indicates alternate positions of moving parts, repeated details, or adjacent parts.                           |

*   **Important Note:** Line thickness and spacing are critical for legibility and must conform to BIS standards.

### 4. Dimensioning

Dimensioning is the process of adding numerical values, tolerance information, and symbols to a drawing to define the size, location, and orientation of features.

*   **Types of Dimensions:**
    *   **Size Dimension:** Defines the physical size of a feature (e.g., diameter, length, width).
    *   **Location Dimension:** Defines the position of a feature relative to a datum or another feature.
*   **Dimensioning Methods:**
    *   **Chain Dimensioning:** Dimensions are placed in a continuous chain, end-to-end.
    *   **Baseline Dimensioning:** All dimensions originate from a common datum.
    *   **Coordinate Dimensioning:** Uses a coordinate system to locate features.
*   **Dimensioning Guidelines:**
    *   Avoid over-dimensioning or under-dimensioning.
    *   Dimensions should be clear and unambiguous.
    *   Dimension lines should not cross object lines or other dimension lines if possible.
    *   Use appropriate arrowheads or terminators.
    *   Place dimensions outside the object where possible.
*   *(Reference: Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing by James D. Meadows, extensively covers dimensioning practices and their importance in conveying design intent.)*

### 5. Scales of Drawing

A scale is the ratio between the size of an object on the drawing and its actual size.

*   **Types of Scales:**
    *   **Full Scale (1:1):** The drawing size is equal to the actual object size.
    *   **Enlarging Scale (e.g., 2:1, 5:1):** The drawing size is larger than the actual object size. Used for small components.
    *   **Reducing Scale (e.g., 1:2, 1:5, 1:10):** The drawing size is smaller than the actual object size. Used for large components.
*   **Representation:** Scales are typically indicated in the title block of the drawing.
*   **Selection:** The scale is chosen to ensure that all features are clearly visible and legibly dimensioned.

### 6. Sectional Views

Sectional views are used to reveal the internal features of an object that are hidden in the external views.

*   **Purpose:** To show the internal construction, shape, and relationships between internal parts.
*   **Cutting Plane:** A hypothetical plane that cuts through the object.
*   **Section Line:** Indicates the path of the cutting plane and is often thickened with arrowhead to show the direction of view.
*   **Sectional Lines/Hatching:** Thin, angled lines used to shade the surface exposed by the cut.
    *   **Standard Hatching:** Typically at 45 degrees.
    *   **Different Hatching Patterns:** Used to differentiate materials in assemblies.
*   **Types of Sections:**
    *   **Full Section:** The cutting plane passes entirely through the object.
    *   **Half Section:** Used for symmetrical objects, showing half in external view and half in section.
    *   **Revolved Section:** A section view rotated 90 degrees and placed directly on the drawing, often within the outline of the object.
    *   **Offset Section:** The cutting plane is bent to pass through features that are not in a straight line.
    *   **Broken-Out Section:** A portion of the object is removed to expose internal features.
*   **Important Point:** Hidden lines are generally omitted in sectional views of the sectioned part itself.

### 7. Geometric Tolerances (GT) and Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T)

*   **Geometric Tolerances (GT):** The allowable variation in the form, orientation, location, and profile of individual features of a part.
*   **Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T):** A symbolic language used on engineering drawings to communicate design requirements precisely, including allowable variations. It goes beyond simple dimensional tolerances to define how features should relate to each other.
*   **Importance of GD&T:**
    *   **Clear Communication:** Eliminates ambiguity and ensures everyone involved in the manufacturing process understands the exact requirements.
    *   **Interchangeability:** Guarantees that parts produced by different machines or at different times will fit and function together.
    *   **Reduced Scrap:** By specifying functional tolerances, it minimizes the number of parts that fail inspection.
    *   **Cost Reduction:** Optimizes manufacturing processes by allowing for the largest possible tolerances that still meet functional requirements.
    *   **Improved Functionality:** Ensures the part performs its intended function reliably.
*   **Key GD&T Concepts (Brief Introduction):**
    *   **Datums:** Theoretically exact geometric references (points, lines, planes) used to establish a coordinate system for tolerancing.
    *   **Feature Control Frame:** A rectangular box containing symbols to define the geometric characteristic, tolerance value, and datum references.
    *   **Geometric Characteristics:** Form (Straightness, Flatness, Circularity, Cylindricity), Orientation (Angularity, Perpendicularity, Parallelism, Profile of a Line/Surface), Location (Position, Concentricity, Symmetry), Runout, and Runout.
*   *(Reference: Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing by James D. Meadows, and Fundamentals of Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing by Alex Krulikowski are dedicated to these topics and provide comprehensive coverage.)*
*   **Course Outcome Alignment:** This section directly addresses **CO3: Practice GD & T in models as well as drawings**.

## Topic: Rigid Shaft Couplings

Rigid shaft couplings are used to connect two shafts to transmit power between them. They are designed to connect shafts with collinear axes and do not allow for any relative angular or parallel displacement between the shafts.

### Types of Rigid Shaft Couplings

We will focus on drawing two common types using 2D drafting software:

#### 1. Flange Coupling

*   **Description:** Consists of two flanges, one attached to the end of each shaft, which are then bolted together. The flanges are often provided with a keyway to secure them to the shaft.
*   **Components:**
    *   Two Flanges (each with a hub)
    *   Bolts and Nuts
    *   Keys (for shaft attachment)

*   **Drawing Considerations:**
    *   **Flange Diameter and Thickness:** Dictated by the shaft diameter and torque to be transmitted.
    *   **Hub Diameter and Length:** Should match the shaft diameter and provide sufficient bearing surface.
    *   **Bolt Circle Diameter (BCD):** The diameter on which the bolt holes are located.
    *   **Number and Size of Bolts:** Determined by the torque and shaft size.
    *   **Keyway Dimensions:** Standard dimensions for fitting keys.
    *   **Chamfers and Fillets:** For stress relief and ease of assembly.

*   **Drawing Practice (2D Drafting Software - e.g., AutoCAD, SolidWorks 2D):**

    1.  **Draw the Shaft:** Draw a circle for the shaft diameter and a rectangle for the keyway.
    2.  **Draw the Flange Hub:** Draw a concentric circle for the hub outer diameter. Draw a rectangle for the keyway in the hub, aligning it with the shaft keyway.
    3.  **Draw the Flange Body:** Draw the outer diameter of the flange and the recess for the mating flange.
    4.  **Bolt Holes:**
        *   Draw a circle representing the Bolt Circle Diameter (BCD).
        *   Divide the BCD into the required number of segments.
        *   Draw circles for the bolt holes at the intersection points.
    5.  **Bolt Holes in Mating Flange:** Draw the corresponding bolt holes in the second flange.
    6.  **Sectional View:** To show the keyway and bolt assembly clearly, a sectional view (e.g., half-section or full section) is often used.
        *   Draw a cutting plane line through the coupling.
        *   Draw the sectioned view, showing the shaft, hub, flange body, and bolts.
        *   Apply section lines to the cut surfaces.
    7.  **Dimensioning:** Add dimensions for shaft diameter, flange outer diameter, hub diameter, BCD, bolt hole diameters, keyway dimensions, and flange thickness.
    8.  **Hidden Lines:** Use hidden lines to represent features that are not visible in the current view.
    9.  **Assembly View:** Show the two flanges bolted together onto the shafts.

*   **Example Sketch (Conceptual):**

    ```
    +-----------------+
    |                 |
    |   (Shaft) o-----o-----o (Shaft)
    |       / \     / \
    |      /   \   /   \
    |     /     \ /     \
    |    |-------|-------|   (Hub)
    |    |_______|_______|
    |     /       \       \
    |    /         \       \
    |   /___________\_______\  (Flange)
    |  o  o  o  o  o  o  o  o (Bolt Holes)
    | / \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \
    | -----------------------  (Bolt Circle)
    | \ /\ /\ /\ /\ /\ /\ /\ /
    |  o  o  o  o  o  o  o  o
    |   \___________\_______\
    |    \         \       \
    |     \       /       /
    |      \     / \     /
    |       \   /   \   /
    |        \ /     \ /
    |         o-------o
    |
    +-----------------+
    ```

#### 2. Protected Flange Coupling

*   **Description:** A variation of the flange coupling where the flanges are covered by a protective sleeve. This sleeve prevents accidental contact with the rotating flanges and bolts, providing an additional safety feature.
*   **Additional Component:** A protective sleeve.

*   **Drawing Considerations:**
    *   The drawing will include the components of a standard flange coupling, plus the protective sleeve.
    *   The sleeve typically has a keyway or other features to prevent it from rotating with the shaft.
    *   The sleeve's outer diameter and length are designed to cover the flanges and bolts.

*   **Drawing Practice (2D Drafting Software):**

    1.  **Draw the Flange Coupling:** Follow the steps for drawing a standard flange coupling.
    2.  **Draw the Protective Sleeve:** Draw a cylindrical sleeve that encloses the flanges and bolts.
        *   Ensure the sleeve's inner diameter is sufficient to clear the outer diameter of the flanges.
        *   The sleeve might have features like a keyway or grub screw holes to secure it to the shaft.
    3.  **Sectional View:** A sectional view is essential to show how the sleeve covers the flanges and bolts.
    4.  **Dimensioning:** Include dimensions for the sleeve's outer diameter, inner diameter, and length, in addition to the flange coupling dimensions.
    5.  **Assembly View:** Show the sleeve fitted over the bolted flanges.

## Practice Questions/Exercises

**Exercise 1: Drawing a Simple Flange Coupling**

**Task:** Draw the front view and top view of a simple flange coupling connecting two shafts of 30mm diameter. The coupling has the following specifications:

*   Shaft Diameter: 30 mm
*   Flange Outer Diameter: 120 mm
*   Hub Diameter: 50 mm
*   Flange Thickness: 20 mm
*   Number of Bolts: 4
*   Bolt Circle Diameter (BCD): 90 mm
*   Bolt Diameter: 10 mm
*   Keyway for Shaft and Hub: 8 mm wide x 3 mm deep (assume standard rectangular keyway)

**Instructions:**

1.  Use a 2D drafting software of your choice.
2.  Draw the individual views (front and top) with appropriate line types.
3.  Add all necessary dimensions according to BIS standards.
4.  Draw a half-sectional front view to clearly show the keyways and bolts.
5.  Include a title block with the drawing title, scale (assume 1:1 for practice), and your name.

**Answer Guidance:**

*   **Front View:** Show the circular outline of the flanges and hubs, along with the bolt circles and bolt holes. The half-sectional view will reveal the keyway and the bolt passing through the flanges.
*   **Top View:** Show the rectangular outlines of the shafts with their keyways, and the flanges with their bolt holes.
*   **Dimensioning:** Ensure all critical dimensions are clearly indicated. For the half-section, use extension lines and dimension lines outside the object boundaries.
*   **Line Types:** Use object lines for visible edges, hidden lines for non-visible edges (unless in section), center lines for axes of rotation, and dimension lines.

**Exercise 2: Identifying GD&T Symbols (Conceptual)**

**Task:** Imagine a drawing of a flange coupling with GD&T applied. Which GD&T symbol would be most appropriate to control:

a)  The roundness of the bolt holes?
b)  The parallelism of the flange faces to the shaft axis?
c)  The location of the bolt holes relative to the center of the flange?
d)  The flatness of the mating faces of the flanges?

**Answer Guidance:**

a)  **Roundness:** The **Circularity** symbol (a circle within a circle).
b)  **Parallelism:** The **Parallelism** symbol (two parallel lines). This would likely be applied to the flange face with respect to the shaft axis or a datum derived from the shaft.
c)  **Location of Bolt Holes:** The **Position** symbol (a crosshair). This would control the true position of the bolt holes with respect to the center of the bolt circle and the center of the flange.
d)  **Flatness of Mating Faces:** The **Flatness** symbol (a horizontal rectangle). This would ensure that the faces where the two flanges meet are perfectly flat.

*(Reference: Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing by James D. Meadows, Chapter 5 covers Form Tolerances, Chapter 6 covers Orientation Tolerances, Chapter 7 covers Location Tolerances, which are directly relevant to these questions.)*

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Accuracy is Paramount:** Machine drawings must be accurate to ensure proper function and interchangeability of parts.
*   **Clarity and Legibility:** Use appropriate line types, dimensioning techniques, and lettering to make the drawing easy to understand.
*   **Consistency:** Maintain consistency in dimensioning style, line weights, and hatching patterns throughout the drawing.
*   **Understand the Function:** Before drawing, understand the purpose and function of the component to ensure all critical features are represented and dimensioned correctly.
*   **GD&T is Key:** GD&T provides a robust system for defining functional requirements and controlling manufacturing variations.
*   **CAD Software Proficiency:** Familiarize yourself with the tools and commands in your chosen 2D drafting software to create precise drawings efficiently.
*   **BIS Standards:** Always refer to the relevant BIS codes for specific drawing conventions.
