# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS: Module 1: Basic Economic Concepts

## Topic: Changes in Demand and Supply and Its Effects

This module introduces fundamental economic concepts crucial for engineers in decision-making. We will explore how changes in demand and supply influence market equilibrium and the implications for economic agents.

### 1. Recap: The Law of Demand and Supply

Before delving into changes, let's quickly revisit the core concepts:

*   **Demand:** The willingness and ability of consumers to purchase a good or service at various prices during a specific period.
    *   **Law of Demand:** Ceteris paribus (all other things being equal), as the price of a good or service increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa.
    *   **Demand Curve:** A graphical representation of the relationship between price and quantity demanded, typically downward sloping.
*   **Supply:** The willingness and ability of producers to offer a good or service for sale at various prices during a specific period.
    *   **Law of Supply:** Ceteris paribus, as the price of a good or service increases, the quantity supplied increases, and vice versa.
    *   **Supply Curve:** A graphical representation of the relationship between price and quantity supplied, typically upward sloping.
*   **Market Equilibrium:** The point where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. The corresponding price is the equilibrium price, and the quantity is the equilibrium quantity.

---

### 2. Factors Causing Changes in Demand (Determinants of Demand)

A **change in demand** refers to a shift of the entire demand curve, indicating a change in the quantity demanded at *every* price. This is distinct from a **change in quantity demanded**, which is a movement *along* the demand curve due to a price change.

**Key Determinants of Demand:**

*   **Consumer Income:**
    *   **Normal Goods:** As income rises, demand increases. As income falls, demand decreases.
        *   *Example:* Cars, designer clothing.
    *   **Inferior Goods:** As income rises, demand decreases. As income falls, demand increases.
        *   *Example:* Instant noodles, public transportation (for some).
    *   *Reference:* Geetika, Ghosh, & Chodhury (2015), Chapter 3: "Demand and Consumer Behaviour". They discuss income elasticity of demand, which categorizes goods into normal and inferior.
*   **Prices of Related Goods:**
    *   **Substitute Goods:** Goods that can be used in place of each other. If the price of a substitute increases, demand for the original good increases.
        *   *Example:* If the price of coffee increases, the demand for tea might increase.
    *   **Complementary Goods:** Goods that are used together. If the price of a complement increases, demand for the original good decreases.
        *   *Example:* If the price of gasoline increases, the demand for cars might decrease.
    *   *Reference:* Geetika, Ghosh, & Chodhury (2015), Chapter 3: "Demand and Consumer Behaviour", discusses cross-price elasticity of demand.
*   **Consumer Tastes and Preferences:** Changes in fashion, trends, advertising, or health concerns can shift demand.
    *   *Example:* Increased awareness of healthy eating can increase demand for organic produce.
    *   *Reference:* Geetika, Ghosh, & Chodhury (2015), Chapter 3.
*   **Consumer Expectations:** Beliefs about future prices, income, or product availability.
    *   *Example:* If consumers expect a price increase for a product next week, they might increase their demand today.
*   **Number of Buyers:** An increase in the population or market size leads to an increase in demand.
*   **Demographic Factors:** Changes in age distribution, gender, or location can affect demand for specific products.
    *   *Example:* An aging population might increase demand for healthcare services.

**Graphical Representation of a Demand Shift:**

*   **Increase in Demand:** The demand curve shifts to the **right**.
*   **Decrease in Demand:** The demand curve shifts to the **left**.

### 3. Factors Causing Changes in Supply (Determinants of Supply)

A **change in supply** refers to a shift of the entire supply curve, indicating a change in the quantity supplied at *every* price. This is distinct from a **change in quantity supplied**, which is a movement *along* the supply curve due to a price change.

**Key Determinants of Supply:**

*   **Input Prices (Cost of Production):** If the cost of resources (labor, raw materials, energy) used to produce a good increases, supply decreases.
    *   *Example:* If the price of steel (an input for car manufacturing) increases, the supply of cars will decrease.
    *   *Reference:* Geetika, Ghosh, & Chodhury (2015), Chapter 4: "Supply and Cost of Production".
*   **Technology:** Improvements in technology that make production more efficient typically increase supply.
    *   *Example:* The invention of the assembly line increased the supply of automobiles.
    *   *Reference:* Geetika, Ghosh, & Chodhury (2015), Chapter 4.
*   **Prices of Related Goods in Production:**
    *   **Joint Products:** Goods produced together. If the price of one joint product increases, the supply of the other might decrease as producers shift resources.
        *   *Example:* If the price of beef increases, the supply of leather (a byproduct) might increase.
    *   **Alternative Goods:** Goods that can be produced using the same resources. If the price of an alternative good increases, producers might shift production, decreasing the supply of the original good.
        *   *Example:* If the price of corn increases, farmers might plant less wheat, decreasing the supply of wheat.
*   **Producer Expectations:** Beliefs about future prices. If producers expect prices to rise, they might decrease current supply to sell later at a higher price.
    *   *Example:* Farmers might hold back some of their harvest if they anticipate higher prices in the future.
*   **Number of Sellers:** An increase in the number of producers in the market increases supply.
*   **Government Policies:**
    *   **Taxes:** Taxes on production increase costs, decreasing supply.
    *   **Subsidies:** Subsidies on production lower costs, increasing supply.
    *   **Regulations:** Stricter regulations can increase production costs, decreasing supply.
    *   *Reference:* Geetika, Ghosh, & Chodhury (2015), Chapter 4, discusses the impact of government policies.

**Graphical Representation of a Supply Shift:**

*   **Increase in Supply:** The supply curve shifts to the **right**.
*   **Decrease in Supply:** The supply curve shifts to the **left**.

### 4. Effects of Changes in Demand and Supply on Equilibrium

Changes in the determinants of demand and supply lead to shifts in the respective curves, altering the market equilibrium price and quantity.

**Scenario 1: Increase in Demand (Demand Curve Shifts Right)**

*   **Cause:** E.g., rise in consumer income (for a normal good), successful advertising campaign.
*   **Effect:**
    *   At the original equilibrium price, there is now a shortage (quantity demanded > quantity supplied).
    *   Prices are bid up.
    *   As price rises, quantity supplied increases (movement along the supply curve), and quantity demanded decreases (movement along the new demand curve).
    *   A new equilibrium is established at a **higher price** and a **higher quantity**.

```
        | /     .     .
        |/      .     .
        +--------------------> Quantity
              Q0    Q1
```

**Scenario 2: Decrease in Demand (Demand Curve Shifts Left)**

*   **Cause:** E.g., fall in consumer income (for a normal good), negative publicity.
*   **Effect:**
    *   At the original equilibrium price, there is now a surplus (quantity supplied > quantity demanded).
    *   Prices tend to fall.
    *   As price falls, quantity supplied decreases (movement along the supply curve), and quantity demanded increases (movement along the new demand curve).
    *   A new equilibrium is established at a **lower price** and a **lower quantity**.

**Scenario 3: Increase in Supply (Supply Curve Shifts Right)**

*   **Cause:** E.g., improved production technology, decrease in input prices.
*   **Effect:**
    *   At the original equilibrium price, there is now a surplus (quantity supplied > quantity demanded).
    *   Prices tend to fall.
    *   As price falls, quantity demanded increases (movement along the demand curve), and quantity supplied decreases (movement along the new supply curve).

### 5. Combined Shifts

When both demand and supply shift simultaneously, the impact on equilibrium price and quantity depends on the *magnitude* and *direction* of each shift.

*   **Demand Increases, Supply Decreases:** Price will definitely increase. The effect on quantity is indeterminate (depends on the relative size of the shifts).
*   **Demand Decreases, Supply Increases:** Price will definitely decrease. The effect on quantity is indeterminate.
*   **Both Demand and Supply Increase:** Quantity will definitely increase. The effect on price is indeterminate.
*   **Both Demand and Supply Decrease:** Quantity will definitely decrease. The effect on price is indeterminate.

*Reference:* Geetika, Ghosh, & Chodhury (2015), Chapter 5: "Market Equilibrium and Applications", discusses scenarios with simultaneous shifts.

### 6. Engineering Application and Relevance

Understanding these shifts is crucial for engineers:

*   **Cost Estimation and Forecasting:** Predicting the future price of materials or components requires understanding supply-side factors (input costs, technology).
*   **Product Development and Pricing:** Anticipating changes in consumer demand (tastes, income) helps in designing and pricing new products effectively.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Decisions about what projects to undertake or which materials to use often depend on current and projected market prices, which are driven by demand and supply.
*   **Project Feasibility Studies:** Understanding market demand and supply dynamics is essential for assessing the economic viability of engineering projects. For example, if a project aims to increase the supply of a certain good, the engineers need to understand how this increased supply will affect the market price and overall demand.
*   **Capital Budgeting:** Evaluating the long-term economic performance of capital investments requires considering how market conditions (driven by demand and supply) might change over the project's life.
    *   *Reference:* Engineering Economy by R. Paneerselvam (2012) and Engineering Economy by H. G. Thuesen & W. J. Fabrycky (1966) emphasize the importance of market analysis in engineering economic decisions.

---

### 7. Key Points to Remember

*   **Change in Demand vs. Change in Quantity Demanded:** Demand refers to the entire curve shift; quantity demanded refers to a movement along the curve due to price.
*   **Change in Supply vs. Change in Quantity Supplied:** Supply refers to the entire curve shift; quantity supplied refers to a movement along the curve due to price.
*   **Equilibrium:** Where demand and supply intersect, determining market price and quantity.
*   **Shifts Right:** Indicate an increase in demand or supply.
*   **Shifts Left:** Indicate a decrease in demand or supply.
*   **Direction of Price/Quantity Change:** Always analyze how the shift affects the balance between buyers and sellers at the original price to predict the movement to a new equilibrium.

### 8. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1:**

If the price of gasoline increases significantly, how would this likely affect the demand for SUVs and the supply of public transportation? Explain your reasoning using economic concepts.

**Answer:**

*   **Demand for SUVs:** SUVs are often seen as complements to gasoline (cars need fuel to operate). An increase in the price of gasoline makes owning and operating SUVs more expensive. Therefore, the demand for SUVs is likely to **decrease**, causing the demand curve for SUVs to shift to the **left**.
*   **Supply of Public Transportation:** Public transportation can be considered a substitute for private car ownership. If gasoline prices rise, more people may opt for public transport. This increased demand for public transportation would likely lead to a **rightward shift** in its demand curve. In response to higher demand, transit authorities might increase the supply of services.

**Question 2:**

A new, more efficient technology for producing microchips is developed. Simultaneously, consumer demand for smartphones (which use microchips) increases due to a popular new model. What are the likely effects on the equilibrium price and quantity of microchips?

**Answer:**

*   **Effect of New Technology:** Improved technology leads to lower production costs, increasing the supply of microchips. This shifts the supply curve to the **right**.
*   **Effect of Increased Consumer Demand:** Higher demand for smartphones increases the derived demand for microchips. This shifts the demand curve for microchips to the **right**.

    *   **Effect on Quantity:** Both shifts (increase in supply, increase in demand) lead to an **increase in the equilibrium quantity** of microchips.
    *   **Effect on Price:** The increase in supply tends to lower prices, while the increase in demand tends to raise prices. The net effect on the equilibrium price is **indeterminate** and depends on the magnitude of the shifts. If the demand shift is larger than the supply shift, the price will rise. If the supply shift is larger, the price will fall. If they are equal, the price might remain unchanged.

**Question 3 (Engineering Context):**

An engineering firm is considering a project to build a new solar panel manufacturing plant. The market price of solar panels is currently $0.50 per watt. However, there are expectations of a government subsidy for renewable energy and potential improvements in solar cell efficiency in the coming years.

*   How might the expected government subsidy affect the supply of solar panels?
*   How might the expected improvements in solar cell efficiency affect the supply of solar panels?
*   If consumer demand for solar panels also increases due to environmental awareness, what would be the combined effect on price and quantity?

**Answer:**

*   **Government Subsidy:** A government subsidy for renewable energy would lower the cost of production for solar panel manufacturers. This is equivalent to a decrease in input costs or an improvement in the production process. Consequently, the **supply of solar panels would increase**, shifting the supply curve to the **right**.
*   **Improvements in Solar Cell Efficiency:** More efficient technology typically lowers production costs per unit or increases output for the same cost. This also leads to an **increase in the supply of solar panels**, shifting the supply curve to the **right**.
*   **Combined Effect (Demand Increases, Supply Increases):**
    *   **Quantity:** With both demand and supply increasing, the equilibrium quantity of solar panels traded will definitely **increase**.
    *   **Price:** The increase in supply tends to put downward pressure on prices, while the increase in demand tends to put upward pressure on prices. The net effect on the equilibrium price is **indeterminate**. It depends on whether the increase in demand is greater or smaller than the increase in supply. If demand increases more than supply, the price will likely rise. If supply increases more than demand, the price will likely fall.
