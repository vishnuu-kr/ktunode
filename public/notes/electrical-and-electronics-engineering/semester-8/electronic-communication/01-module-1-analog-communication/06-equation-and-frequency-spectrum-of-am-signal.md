# Electronic Communication: Module 1 - Analog Communication

## Topic: Equation and Frequency Spectrum of AM Signal

This module delves into the fundamentals of analog communication, focusing on Amplitude Modulation (AM). We will explore the mathematical representation of an AM signal and analyze its frequency spectrum, which is crucial for understanding its bandwidth requirements and transmission characteristics.

---

### 1. Introduction to Amplitude Modulation (AM)

Amplitude Modulation is a modulation technique where the amplitude of a high-frequency carrier wave is varied in accordance with the instantaneous amplitude of the modulating signal (information signal).

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Carrier Wave ($c(t)$):** A high-frequency sinusoidal wave used to carry the information. It is typically represented as $c(t) = A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t)$, where $A_c$ is the carrier amplitude and $f_c$ is the carrier frequency.
*   **Modulating Signal ($m(t)$):** The information signal that needs to be transmitted. This could be audio, voice, or any other analog signal.
*   **Modulated Signal ($s(t)$):** The resulting signal after the carrier wave has been modulated by the modulating signal.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:** This section directly addresses the foundational understanding required for CO1 and CO2.

### 2. Mathematical Equation of an AM Signal

An AM signal is generated by varying the amplitude of the carrier signal based on the modulating signal.

**2.1. Standard AM (DSB-FC - Double Sideband Full Carrier)**

In standard AM, the amplitude of the carrier signal is varied linearly by the modulating signal. The modulated signal $s(t)$ can be expressed as:

$s(t) = [A_c + k_a m(t)] \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

Where:

*   $A_c$: Amplitude of the carrier wave.
*   $m(t)$: Instantaneous amplitude of the modulating signal.
*   $k_a$: Amplitude sensitivity of the modulator. This constant determines how much the carrier amplitude changes for a given change in the modulating signal amplitude.
*   $f_c$: Carrier frequency.

**Expanding the Equation:**

$s(t) = A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t) + k_a m(t) \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

This equation shows that the AM signal consists of two components:
1.  **Carrier Component:** $A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t)$ - This component carries no information but is essential for coherent demodulation.
2.  **Modulated Component:** $k_a m(t) \cos(2\pi f_c t)$ - This component contains the information.

**2.2. Normalized Amplitude and Modulation Index**

To ensure proper modulation and avoid over-modulation, we introduce the **modulation index ($\mu$)**. It represents the degree of variation of the carrier amplitude.

The modulating signal is usually normalized such that its maximum amplitude is less than or equal to the carrier amplitude. Let $m_{max} = \max|m(t)|$.

The modulation index is defined as:

$\mu = \frac{k_a m_{max}}{A_c}$

Using this, we can rewrite the AM equation as:

$s(t) = A_c \left[1 + \frac{k_a m(t)}{A_c}\right] \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

$s(t) = A_c \left[1 + \mu \frac{m(t)}{m_{max}}\right] \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

**Conditions of Modulation:**

*   **Under-modulation ($\mu < 1$):** The envelope of the modulated signal follows the modulating signal without distortion. Carrier component is dominant.
*   **Critical Modulation ($\mu = 1$):** The envelope exactly follows the modulating signal. The amplitude of the sidebands becomes equal to the carrier amplitude.
*   **Over-modulation ($\mu > 1$):** The envelope distorts the modulating signal, leading to undesirable effects like phase reversal during demodulation, which causes harmonic distortion.

**Example:**

Let $A_c = 10V$, $f_c = 1MHz$, and $m(t) = 2 \cos(2\pi \times 1kHz \times t) V$. Let $k_a = 3 V/V$.

Then $m_{max} = 2V$.
Modulation index $\mu = \frac{k_a m_{max}}{A_c} = \frac{3 \times 2}{10} = 0.6$.

The AM signal is:
$s(t) = [10 + 3 \times 2 \cos(2\pi \times 1kHz \times t)] \cos(2\pi \times 1MHz \times t)$
$s(t) = 10 \cos(2\pi \times 1MHz \times t) + 6 \cos(2\pi \times 1kHz \times t) \cos(2\pi \times 1MHz \times t)$

### 3. Frequency Spectrum of an AM Signal

The frequency spectrum of a signal shows its constituent frequencies and their amplitudes. We can determine the spectrum of an AM signal by analyzing its equation in the frequency domain.

**3.1. Spectrum of a Single-Tone Modulating Signal**

Consider a simple modulating signal that is a single sinusoid:

$m(t) = M \cos(2\pi f_m t)$

Where $M$ is the amplitude of the modulating signal and $f_m$ is the modulating frequency.

Substituting this into the AM equation:

$s(t) = A_c \left[1 + \mu \frac{M \cos(2\pi f_m t)}{M}\right] \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

$s(t) = A_c [1 + \mu \cos(2\pi f_m t)] \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

$s(t) = A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t) + A_c \mu \cos(2\pi f_m t) \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

Now, we use the trigonometric identity: $\cos A \cos B = \frac{1}{2} [\cos(A-B) + \cos(A+B)]$

Applying this to the second term:

$A_c \mu \cos(2\pi f_m t) \cos(2\pi f_c t) = \frac{A_c \mu}{2} [\cos(2\pi (f_c - f_m) t) + \cos(2\pi (f_c + f_m) t)]$

So, the complete AM signal equation becomes:

$s(t) = A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t) + \frac{A_c \mu}{2} \cos(2\pi (f_c - f_m) t) + \frac{A_c \mu}{2} \cos(2\pi (f_c + f_m) t)$

**Frequency Components:**

From this equation, we can identify the frequency components present in the AM signal:

1.  **Carrier Frequency ($f_c$):** With amplitude $A_c$.
2.  **Lower Sideband (LSB) Frequency ($f_c - f_m$):** With amplitude $\frac{A_c \mu}{2}$.
3.  **Upper Sideband (USB) Frequency ($f_c + f_m$):** With amplitude $\frac{A_c \mu}{2}$.

**Frequency Spectrum Representation:**

The frequency spectrum of a single-tone AM signal can be represented using a line spectrum:

*   A spike at $f_c$ with amplitude $A_c$.
*   A spike at $f_c - f_m$ with amplitude $\frac{A_c \mu}{2}$.
*   A spike at $f_c + f_m$ with amplitude $\frac{A_c \mu}{2}$.

**Bandwidth (BW):**

The bandwidth occupied by an AM signal is the difference between the highest and lowest frequencies present in its spectrum.

$BW = (f_c + f_m) - (f_c - f_m) = 2f_m$

This means the bandwidth of an AM signal is twice the maximum frequency component of the modulating signal.

**Example:**

If $f_c = 1MHz$ and $f_m = 5kHz$, the AM signal will have components at:
*   $1MHz$ (carrier)
*   $1MHz - 5kHz = 995kHz$ (LSB)
*   $1MHz + 5kHz = 1005kHz$ (USB)

The bandwidth is $2 \times 5kHz = 10kHz$.

### 4. Spectrum of a General Modulating Signal

When the modulating signal $m(t)$ is complex and contains multiple frequency components, its spectrum will be a continuous function in the frequency domain. Let the Fourier Transform of $m(t)$ be $M(f)$.

$M(f) = \mathcal{F}\{m(t)\}$

The AM signal equation is:

$s(t) = A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t) + k_a m(t) \cos(2\pi f_c t)$

Taking the Fourier Transform of both sides:

$S(f) = \mathcal{F}\{A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t)\} + \mathcal{F}\{k_a m(t) \cos(2\pi f_c t)\}$

We know that $\mathcal{F}\{\cos(2\pi f_c t)\} = \frac{1}{2}[\delta(f - f_c) + \delta(f + f_c)]$.

And using the frequency shifting property of Fourier Transform: $\mathcal{F}\{x(t) \cos(2\pi f_c t)\} = \frac{1}{2}[X(f - f_c) + X(f + f_c)]$.

So,

$S(f) = A_c \times \frac{1}{2}[\delta(f - f_c) + \delta(f + f_c)] + k_a \times \frac{1}{2}[M(f - f_c) + M(f + f_c)]$

$S(f) = \frac{A_c}{2}[\delta(f - f_c) + \delta(f + f_c)] + \frac{k_a}{2}[M(f - f_c) + M(f + f_c)]$

**Frequency Components:**

The spectrum of the AM signal consists of:

1.  **Carrier Component:** Two impulses at $+f_c$ and $-f_c$, representing the carrier signal.
2.  **Upper Sideband (USB):** A shifted version of the modulating signal's spectrum, $M(f)$, shifted by $+f_c$. This is represented by $M(f - f_c)$.
3.  **Lower Sideband (LSB):** A shifted version of the modulating signal's spectrum, $M(f)$, shifted by $-f_c$. This is represented by $M(f + f_c)$.

**Bandwidth (BW):**

If the bandwidth of the modulating signal $m(t)$ is $B_m$ (meaning the highest frequency component in $m(t)$ is $f_{m,max} = B_m$), then the bandwidth of the AM signal is:

$BW = (f_c + f_{m,max}) - (f_c - f_{m,max}) = 2 f_{m,max} = 2B_m$.

**Important Point:** The sidebands carry the information, while the carrier component does not. However, the carrier is necessary for simple demodulation techniques.

### 5. Power Content of an AM Signal

The total power of an AM signal can be calculated by considering the power of each component.

Recall the AM signal equation:
$s(t) = A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t) + \frac{A_c \mu}{2} \cos(2\pi (f_c - f_m) t) + \frac{A_c \mu}{2} \cos(2\pi (f_c + f_m) t)$

The power of a sinusoidal signal $A \cos(\omega t)$ is $P = \frac{A^2}{2}$ (assuming a 1-ohm resistor for simplicity in analysis, which is standard for spectral analysis).

1.  **Power of the Carrier Component ($P_c$):**
    $P_c = \frac{A_c^2}{2}$

2.  **Power of the Lower Sideband Component ($P_{LSB}$):**
    $P_{LSB} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{A_c \mu}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{A_c^2 \mu^2}{8}$

3.  **Power of the Upper Sideband Component ($P_{USB}$):**
    $P_{USB} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{A_c \mu}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{A_c^2 \mu^2}{8}$

**Total Power ($P_{Total}$):**

$P_{Total} = P_c + P_{LSB} + P_{USB}$
$P_{Total} = \frac{A_c^2}{2} + \frac{A_c^2 \mu^2}{8} + \frac{A_c^2 \mu^2}{8}$
$P_{Total} = \frac{A_c^2}{2} + \frac{A_c^2 \mu^2}{4}$
$P_{Total} = \frac{A_c^2}{2} \left(1 + \frac{\mu^2}{2}\right)$

**Power Distribution:**

*   **Carrier Power Fraction:**
    $\frac{P_c}{P_{Total}} = \frac{A_c^2/2}{\frac{A_c^2}{2} \left(1 + \frac{\mu^2}{2}\right)} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\mu^2}{2}}$

*   **Sideband Power Fraction:**
    $\frac{P_{LSB} + P_{USB}}{P_{Total}} = \frac{A_c^2 \mu^2 / 4}{\frac{A_c^2}{2} \left(1 + \frac{\mu^2}{2}\right)} = \frac{\mu^2 / 2}{1 + \frac{\mu^2}{2}}$

**Efficiency:**

The efficiency of an AM system is the ratio of the power transmitted in the sidebands (information-carrying components) to the total transmitted power.

Efficiency ($\eta$) = $\frac{\text{Power in Sidebands}}{\text{Total Power}} \times 100\%$
$\eta = \frac{P_{LSB} + P_{USB}}{P_{Total}} \times 100\% = \frac{\mu^2 / 2}{1 + \mu^2 / 2} \times 100\%$

**Analysis of Efficiency:**

*   The efficiency is always less than 1/3 (33.3%) because even for critical modulation ($\mu = 1$), the sideband power is $\frac{1}{1 + 1/2} = \frac{1}{3/2} = 2/3$ of the total power, meaning sidebands carry $2/3$ of the power, and carrier carries $1/3$.
*   For under-modulation ($\mu < 1$), the efficiency is even lower.
*   This low efficiency is a major drawback of standard AM, as a significant portion of the transmitted power is in the carrier, which does not carry information.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **AM Equation:** $s(t) = A_c [1 + \mu \frac{m(t)}{m_{max}}] \cos(2\pi f_c t)$
*   **Modulation Index ($\mu$):** $\mu = \frac{k_a m_{max}}{A_c}$. Crucial for avoiding over-modulation ($\mu \le 1$).
*   **Spectrum:** An AM signal contains a carrier frequency component ($f_c$) and two sideband components: Lower Sideband ($f_c - f_m$) and Upper Sideband ($f_c + f_m$).
*   **Bandwidth:** The bandwidth of an AM signal is twice the maximum frequency of the modulating signal ($BW = 2f_{m,max}$).
*   **Power:** $P_{Total} = \frac{A_c^2}{2} \left(1 + \frac{\mu^2}{2}\right)$. Most power is in the carrier for low $\mu$.
*   **Efficiency:** $\eta = \frac{\mu^2 / 2}{1 + \mu^2 / 2} \times 100\%$. Standard AM is inefficient (max $\sim$ 33.3%).
*   **Envelope:** For $\mu \le 1$, the envelope of the AM signal faithfully reproduces the modulating signal.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
A standard AM signal is generated with a carrier frequency of 100 kHz and a modulating signal $m(t) = 5 \cos(2\pi \times 2 kHz \times t)$. If the carrier amplitude $A_c$ is 10V and the amplitude sensitivity $k_a$ is 0.5 V/V, what is the modulation index?

**Answer 1:**
Given:
$A_c = 10V$
$m(t) = 5 \cos(2\pi \times 2 kHz \times t)$
$m_{max} = 5V$
$k_a = 0.5 V/V$

Modulation index $\mu = \frac{k_a m_{max}}{A_c} = \frac{0.5 \times 5}{10} = \frac{2.5}{10} = 0.25$.

---

**Question 2:**
For the AM signal in Question 1, what are the frequencies present in its spectrum? Also, calculate the bandwidth of this signal.

**Answer 2:**
Carrier frequency ($f_c$) = 100 kHz.
Modulating frequency ($f_m$) = 2 kHz.

The frequencies present in the AM signal spectrum are:
*   Carrier: $f_c = 100 kHz$
*   Lower Sideband: $f_c - f_m = 100 kHz - 2 kHz = 98 kHz$
*   Upper Sideband: $f_c + f_m = 100 kHz + 2 kHz = 102 kHz$

Bandwidth (BW) = $2 f_m = 2 \times 2 kHz = 4 kHz$.

---

**Question 3:**
An AM signal with $\mu = 0.8$ and $A_c = 12V$ is transmitted. Calculate the power in the carrier and the power in the sidebands. Assume a 1-ohm load resistor.

**Answer 3:**
Given:
$\mu = 0.8$
$A_c = 12V$

Power in the carrier ($P_c$) = $\frac{A_c^2}{2} = \frac{12^2}{2} = \frac{144}{2} = 72W$.

Total Power ($P_{Total}$) = $\frac{A_c^2}{2} \left(1 + \frac{\mu^2}{2}\right) = 72 \left(1 + \frac{0.8^2}{2}\right) = 72 \left(1 + \frac{0.64}{2}\right) = 72 (1 + 0.32) = 72 \times 1.32 = 95.04W$.

Power in the sidebands ($P_{SB}$) = $P_{Total} - P_c = 95.04W - 72W = 23.04W$.

Alternatively,
$P_{LSB} = P_{USB} = \frac{A_c^2 \mu^2}{8} = \frac{12^2 \times 0.8^2}{8} = \frac{144 \times 0.64}{8} = 18 \times 0.64 = 11.52W$.
$P_{SB} = P_{LSB} + P_{USB} = 11.52W + 11.52W = 23.04W$.

---

**Question 4:**
What is the maximum theoretical efficiency of standard AM? Under what condition is this maximum efficiency achieved?

**Answer 4:**
The maximum theoretical efficiency of standard AM is approximately 33.3%. This occurs at critical modulation, where the modulation index $\mu = 1$.
