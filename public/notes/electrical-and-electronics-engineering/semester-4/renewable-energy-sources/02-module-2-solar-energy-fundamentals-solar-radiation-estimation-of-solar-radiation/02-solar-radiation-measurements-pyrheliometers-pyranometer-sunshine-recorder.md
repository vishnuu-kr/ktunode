# Renewable Energy Sources: Module 2 - Solar Energy: Fundamentals; Solar Radiation; Estimation of Solar Radiation on Horizontal and Inclined Surfaces

## Topic: Solar Radiation Measurements - Pyrheliometers, Pyranometers, Sunshine Recorders

### Introduction to Solar Radiation Measurement

Accurate measurement of solar radiation is fundamental to understanding and utilizing solar energy effectively. These measurements help in:

*   **Designing solar energy systems:** Determining the optimal size and configuration for solar collectors, photovoltaic panels, etc.
*   **Performance monitoring:** Evaluating the efficiency of installed solar systems.
*   **Resource assessment:** Mapping solar potential for different locations.
*   **Climate studies:** Understanding the impact of solar radiation on weather patterns and the environment.

This module focuses on the key instruments used to measure solar radiation: Pyrheliometers, Pyranometers, and Sunshine Recorders.

### 1. Pyrheliometers

**Key Concept:** Pyrheliometers are instruments specifically designed to measure **direct beam solar radiation**. This is the solar radiation arriving from the sun as a parallel beam.

**Definition:** A pyrheliometer measures the intensity of solar radiation from the sun's disk, excluding diffuse radiation from the sky.

**Working Principle:**

*   Pyrheliometers typically use a thermopile detector, which generates a voltage proportional to the temperature difference between a blackened sensor and a reference temperature.
*   The sensor is positioned in a tube with a narrow aperture, allowing only direct solar radiation to reach it. This tube is often equatorially mounted and tracked to continuously point at the sun.
*   The narrow aperture and shading ring (or tube) effectively block diffuse and reflected radiation.

**Types:**

*   **Absolute Pyrheliometers:** These instruments directly measure the radiation absorbed by a known area and are used to calibrate other instruments.
*   **Relative Pyrheliometers:** These instruments are calibrated against absolute pyrheliometers and provide direct readings.

**Applications:**

*   Measuring direct normal irradiance (DNI) for concentrating solar power (CSP) systems.
*   Calibrating other solar radiation instruments.
*   Scientific research on atmospheric extinction.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Measures only **direct beam radiation**.
*   Requires **tracking** the sun.
*   Sensitive to **alignment** and **atmospheric conditions**.

### 2. Pyranometers

**Key Concept:** Pyranometers are instruments designed to measure **global solar radiation**, which includes both the direct beam radiation and the diffuse radiation from the sky.

**Definition:** A pyranometer measures the solar radiation incident on a flat surface from a hemisphere (the entire sky).

**Working Principle:**

*   Pyranometers also typically use a thermopile detector.
*   The sensing element is a blackened surface (usually a disc or dome) exposed to the entire sky.
*   A glass dome or bulb encloses the sensing element, protecting it from the environment while allowing solar radiation to pass through. The dome can also filter out certain wavelengths.
*   The thermopile measures the temperature difference between the blackened sensing surface and a reference junction, which is kept at ambient temperature.
*   The output signal (voltage) is proportional to the total incoming solar radiation.

**Types:**

*   **First Class Pyranometers:** High accuracy, often used for reference measurements and calibration. Examples include Eppley Precision Pyranometers.
*   **Second Class Pyranometers:** Lower accuracy, suitable for general monitoring and data logging.
*   **Silicon Pyranometers (Photovoltaic Pyranometers):** These use semiconductor photodiodes and are less accurate but more cost-effective, often used for general irradiance measurements.

**Measurement:**

*   **Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI):** When the pyranometer is mounted horizontally, it measures GHI. This is the total solar radiation received on a horizontal surface.
*   **Incident Radiation on Tilted Surfaces:** Pyranometers can be tilted to measure radiation on surfaces with specific orientations (e.g., the plane of the array for PV systems).

**Applications:**

*   Measuring GHI for photovoltaic (PV) power systems.
*   Assessing solar resources for solar thermal systems.
*   Meteorological and climatological studies.
*   Agriculture and environmental research.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Measures **global solar radiation** (direct + diffuse).
*   Measures radiation over a **hemisphere**.
*   Typically mounted **horizontally** but can be tilted.
*   Accuracy varies significantly between different classes.

### 3. Sunshine Recorders

**Key Concept:** Sunshine recorders provide a qualitative or semi-quantitative measure of the **duration of bright sunshine**. They indicate how long the sun has been shining directly and intensely enough to burn or melt something.

**Definition:** Sunshine recorders are instruments that record the periods of direct solar irradiance exceeding a certain threshold, typically indicating the duration of sunshine.

**Working Principle:**

The common types of sunshine recorders operate on the principle of focusing solar radiation onto a recording medium.

*   **Campbell-Stokes Sunshine Recorder (Common Type):**
    *   Consists of a glass sphere that focuses the sun's rays onto a special card placed in a groove behind the sphere.
    *   As the sun moves across the sky, the focused spot of light moves along the card, burning a trace.
    *   The length of the burned trace on the card indicates the duration of bright sunshine.
    *   The cards are designed to be read visually or by a scale.

*   **Jordan Sunshine Recorder:**
    *   Uses a series of lenses to create multiple spots of light on a rotating drum with a photographic film.
    *   The film records the periods of bright sunshine.

*   **Beckley Sunshine Recorder:**
    *   Similar to Campbell-Stokes but uses a metal hemisphere with a slot to define the beam.

**Applications:**

*   Meteorological observations.
*   Assessing the availability of direct sunlight for certain solar processes (e.g., solar drying).
*   Providing a historical record of sunshine duration.

**Limitations:**

*   They do not measure the intensity of solar radiation, only its duration.
*   Accuracy can be affected by clouds that partially obscure the sun, fog, and the angle of the sun.
*   Require daily maintenance (replacing cards/film).
*   Less precise than pyranometers for quantitative energy assessment.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Measures **duration of bright sunshine**.
*   Relies on **focusing solar radiation**.
*   Provides **qualitative or semi-quantitative** data.
*   **Campbell-Stokes** is the most common type.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** A solar energy engineer is designing a concentrated solar power (CSP) plant. Which instrument would be most crucial for accurately measuring the solar resource needed for this application?
    a) Pyranometer
    b) Sunshine Recorder
    c) Pyrheliometer
    d) Anemometer

**Answer:** c) Pyrheliometer
**Explanation:** CSP plants rely on the direct beam radiation from the sun. Pyrheliometers are specifically designed to measure direct normal irradiance (DNI), making them essential for CSP design.

**Question 2:** What type of solar radiation does a pyranometer measure?
    a) Direct beam radiation only
    b) Diffuse radiation only
    c) Direct and diffuse radiation combined
    d) Ultraviolet radiation only

**Answer:** c) Direct and diffuse radiation combined
**Explanation:** A pyranometer measures global solar radiation, which is the sum of direct beam solar radiation and diffuse solar radiation received on a surface from the entire sky hemisphere.

**Question 3:** A researcher wants to know the total number of hours the sun was bright enough to cast a sharp shadow in a particular location over a month. Which instrument would best provide this information?
    a) Pyranometer
    b) Pyrheliometer
    c) Sunshine Recorder
    d) Spectroradiometer

**Answer:** c) Sunshine Recorder
**Explanation:** Sunshine recorders are designed to measure the duration of bright sunshine, indicated by the burning trace on a card. They don't measure intensity but rather the time the sun is sufficiently unobstructed.

**Question 4:** Explain the difference between the measurements provided by a pyrheliometer and a pyranometer.

**Answer:** A pyrheliometer measures only the **direct beam solar radiation** from the sun's disk, often requiring tracking of the sun. A pyranometer measures **global solar radiation**, which is the sum of both the **direct beam radiation** and the **diffuse radiation** from the sky, typically on a horizontal surface or a tilted surface.

**Question 5:** List two applications for each of the following instruments:
    a) Pyrheliometer
    b) Pyranometer
    c) Sunshine Recorder

**Answer:**
    a) **Pyrheliometer:**
        *   Measuring Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI) for Concentrated Solar Power (CSP) systems.
        *   Calibrating other solar radiation measuring instruments.
    b) **Pyranometer:**
        *   Measuring Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI) for photovoltaic (PV) system performance assessment.
        *   Assessing solar resources for solar water heating systems.
    c) **Sunshine Recorder:**
        *   Meteorological data collection to track sunny days.
        *   Assessing the suitability of locations for solar drying processes.

### Important Points to Remember Summary

*   **Pyrheliometer:** Direct beam radiation, sun-tracking required, crucial for CSP.
*   **Pyranometer:** Global radiation (direct + diffuse), measures hemispherical input, essential for PV and solar thermal.
*   **Sunshine Recorder:** Duration of bright sunshine, qualitative/semi-quantitative, useful for meteorological and specific solar process assessments.
*   Understanding the **type of radiation** each instrument measures is key to selecting the appropriate tool for a given solar energy application.
