## 1. Fundamentals of Vehicle Motion

To understand vehicle performance, we must first understand the forces that govern its motion. A vehicle's ability to accelerate, maintain speed, and overcome external resistances is dictated by the interplay of these forces.

### 1.1 Forces Acting on a Vehicle

When a vehicle is in motion, it is subjected to several forces that resist its movement. To achieve acceleration or maintain a constant speed, the vehicle's powertrain must generate a propulsive force greater than or equal to the sum of these resisting forces.

*   **Tractive Force (F<sub>t</sub>):** This is the forward force generated by the wheels that propels the vehicle. It is the force that needs to overcome all resisting forces.

*   **Resisting Forces:**

    *   **Aerodynamic Drag (F<sub>d</sub>):** This is the resistance force due to air flowing around the vehicle. It increases with the square of the vehicle's speed.
        *   **Formula:** $F_d = \frac{1}{2} \rho C_d A v^2$
            *   $\rho$: Density of air (kg/m³)
            *   $C_d$: Coefficient of drag (dimensionless) - depends on the vehicle's shape.
            *   $A$: Frontal area of the vehicle (m²)
            *   $v$: Velocity of the vehicle (m/s)
        *   **Key Concept:** Aerodynamic drag is a significant factor at higher speeds. Sleek vehicle designs aim to minimize $C_d$. (Chau, 2015)

    *   **Rolling Resistance (F<sub>r</sub>):** This is the resistance encountered due to the deformation of tires and the road surface as the wheels roll. It is generally considered to be constant or slowly varying with speed.
        *   **Formula:** $F_r = C_r m g$
            *   $C_r$: Coefficient of rolling resistance (dimensionless) - depends on tire type, inflation pressure, and road surface.
            *   $m$: Mass of the vehicle (kg)
            *   $g$: Acceleration due to gravity (m/s²)
        *   **Key Concept:** Proper tire inflation and choosing tires with lower rolling resistance can improve fuel efficiency.

    *   **Gradient Resistance (F<sub>g</sub>):** This is the force that opposes motion when the vehicle is traveling uphill. It is the component of the gravitational force acting parallel to the road incline.
        *   **Formula:** $F_g = m g \sin(\theta)$
            *   $\theta$: Angle of the incline (radians or degrees)
        *   **Key Concept:** Climbing a hill requires additional tractive force to overcome gravity.

    *   **Inertial Resistance (F<sub>i</sub>):** This is the force required to accelerate the vehicle. It is related to the vehicle's mass and its acceleration.
        *   **Formula:** $F_i = m a$
            *   $a$: Acceleration of the vehicle (m/s²)
        *   **Key Concept:** Overcoming inertia is crucial for acceleration. Lighter vehicles require less force to accelerate.

### 1.2 Total Resisting Force

The total resisting force ($F_{res}$) is the sum of all these individual resisting forces:

$F_{res} = F_d + F_r + F_g + F_i$

For steady-state motion on a level road ($F_g = 0$), the tractive force must equal the sum of aerodynamic drag and rolling resistance ($F_t = F_d + F_r$). For acceleration ($a > 0$), the tractive force must be greater than the sum of resisting forces ($F_t > F_d + F_r + F_g$).

## 2. Vehicle Performance Parameters

Understanding vehicle performance involves analyzing how well a vehicle can meet certain operational requirements, such as acceleration, top speed, and gradeability.

### 2.1 Power and Torque Requirements

The powertrain of a vehicle is responsible for generating the necessary torque and power to overcome resisting forces and achieve desired performance.

*   **Power (P):** The rate at which work is done. In vehicle dynamics, power is the product of tractive force and velocity.
    *   **Formula:** $P = F_t \times v$
    *   **Units:** Watts (W) or Kilowatts (kW)
    *   **Key Concept:** Power determines how quickly a vehicle can do work. Higher power allows for faster acceleration and higher top speeds.

*   **Torque (T):** The rotational force that causes motion. Torque is delivered to the wheels, and it's the turning effect that produces the tractive force.
    *   **Formula:** $T = F_t \times r_w$
        *   $r_w$: Effective radius of the driven wheel (m)
    *   **Units:** Newton-meters (Nm)
    *   **Key Concept:** Torque is essential for starting from rest and for overcoming resistance at low speeds.

### 2.2 Key Performance Metrics

*   **Maximum Speed:** The highest speed a vehicle can achieve on a level road. This occurs when the tractive force equals the total resisting force, and acceleration is zero ($a=0$). At maximum speed, the powertrain is operating at its maximum power output. (Miller, 2010)

*   **Acceleration:** The rate of change of velocity. Higher acceleration indicates a quicker response and ability to reach desired speeds faster.
    *   **Example:** A sports car with a powerful engine can achieve 0-100 km/h in a short time.

*   **Gradeability:** The ability of a vehicle to climb an incline. It's usually expressed as the maximum gradient the vehicle can ascend at a certain speed.
    *   **Example:** A truck designed for heavy hauling will have good gradeability to climb steep hills with a load.

### 2.3 Calculating Power for a Given Performance

Let's consider the power required to achieve a certain acceleration on a level road.

*   **Scenario:** A vehicle of mass 'm' needs to accelerate at 'a' m/s² on a level road at a velocity 'v' m/s.
*   **Resisting Forces:** Aerodynamic drag ($F_d$) and rolling resistance ($F_r$).
*   **Tractive Force required:** $F_t = F_d + F_r + F_i = F_d + F_r + m a$
*   **Power required:** $P = F_t \times v = (F_d + F_r + m a) \times v$

**Important Note:** This formula assumes the engine's power is directly translated to tractive power. In reality, transmission losses and drivetrain inefficiencies need to be accounted for.

## 3. Conventional Vehicle Powertrain Basics

The powertrain is the system that converts fuel energy into mechanical energy to drive the wheels. Understanding its components and their roles is crucial for comparing it with EV powertrains.

### 3.1 Internal Combustion Engine (ICE)

*   **Function:** Converts chemical energy in fuel (gasoline or diesel) into mechanical rotational energy.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   **Torque-Speed Curve:** ICEs produce varying amounts of torque and power at different engine speeds. They have a "sweet spot" for optimal efficiency and performance.
    *   **Emissions:** Produce exhaust gases as a byproduct of combustion.
    *   **Noise and Vibration:** Inherently generate noise and vibration.
*   **Reference:** While this module focuses on basics, understanding ICE characteristics is a prerequisite for appreciating EV advantages. (Mi, Masrur, & Gao, 2011, Introduction to ICE)

### 3.2 Transmission

*   **Function:** Modifies the engine's output speed and torque to match the vehicle's speed and driving conditions. This is achieved through different gear ratios.
    *   **Low Gears:** Provide high torque for starting and climbing hills.
    *   **High Gears:** Provide lower torque but higher speeds for efficient cruising.
*   **Types:** Manual, Automatic, Continuously Variable Transmission (CVT).
*   **Key Concept:** The transmission is vital for keeping the engine operating within its efficient range.

### 3.3 Drivetrain Components

*   **Clutch (Manual Transmission):** Connects and disconnects the engine from the transmission to allow for gear changes and starting/stopping.
*   **Driveshaft:** Transmits rotational power from the transmission to the differential.
*   **Differential:** Splits torque to the drive wheels and allows them to rotate at different speeds when turning.
*   **Axles:** Transmit power from the differential to the wheels.

### 3.4 Powertrain Efficiency

*   **Definition:** The ratio of useful mechanical power delivered to the wheels to the energy consumed by the fuel.
*   **Losses:** Occur in the engine (thermal and mechanical), transmission, driveshaft, differential, and final drive.
*   **Typical ICE Powertrain Efficiency:** Can range from 20-30% for gasoline engines and 30-40% for diesel engines. This means a significant portion of the fuel's energy is lost as heat and friction. (Chau, 2015, Chapter 1)

## 4. Vehicle Efficiency and Factors Affecting It

Vehicle efficiency is a critical measure of how effectively a vehicle converts its energy source into useful motion.

### 4.1 Defining Efficiency

*   **Fuel Efficiency:** Typically measured in miles per gallon (MPG) or liters per 100 kilometers (L/100km). It represents the distance traveled per unit of fuel consumed.
*   **Energy Efficiency:** In the context of EVs, this might relate to kilowatt-hours per mile (kWh/mile).

### 4.2 Factors Affecting Efficiency

*   **Aerodynamic Drag:** As discussed, drag increases significantly with speed. Driving at lower, consistent speeds is more efficient.
*   **Rolling Resistance:** Tire pressure, tread pattern, and tire construction impact rolling resistance.
*   **Vehicle Weight:** Heavier vehicles require more energy to accelerate and overcome resistance.
*   **Driving Style:** Aggressive acceleration and braking are less efficient than smooth driving.
*   **Powertrain Efficiency:** As noted, ICE powertrains have inherent inefficiencies.
*   **Ancillary Loads:** Powering accessories like air conditioning, heating, lights, and infotainment systems consumes energy.
*   **Terrain:** Driving uphill or on rough surfaces reduces efficiency.

### 4.3 Efficiency in Different Driving Cycles

*   **Urban Driving:** Characterized by frequent stops, starts, and low speeds. This often leads to lower fuel economy due to engine inefficiency at low loads and energy lost during acceleration/deceleration.
*   **Highway Driving:** Characterized by higher, more constant speeds. Aerodynamic drag becomes a dominant factor, but engine efficiency can be higher.
*   **Combined Driving:** A mix of urban and highway conditions.

## Summary of Key Concepts and Takeaways

*   **Vehicle motion is governed by balancing tractive force with resisting forces (aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, gradient resistance, inertial resistance).**
*   **Power is the rate of doing work ($P = F_t \times v$) and is crucial for speed and acceleration.**
*   **Torque is the rotational force ($T = F_t \times r_w$) and is essential for starting and low-speed performance.**
*   **Conventional powertrains (ICE, transmission, drivetrain) convert fuel energy to mechanical energy with significant losses.**
*   **Vehicle efficiency is affected by aerodynamics, rolling resistance, weight, driving style, and powertrain design.**
*   **Understanding these conventional vehicle principles provides a baseline for appreciating the unique performance characteristics of electric vehicles.**

**Question 5 (Conceptual Application):**
A vehicle with a mass of 1500 kg is traveling at 20 m/s on a level road. The aerodynamic drag force at this speed is 300 N, and the rolling resistance is 200 N.
a) Calculate the total resisting force acting on the vehicle.
b) If the vehicle needs to accelerate at 2 m/s², what is the total tractive force required?
c) Calculate the power required to maintain the speed of 20 m/s (assuming no acceleration).
d) Calculate the additional power required to achieve the acceleration of 2 m/s² at 20 m/s.

**Solution:**

*   **Given:**
    *   $m = 1500$ kg
    *   $v = 20$ m/s
    *   $F_d = 300$ N
    *   $F_r = 200$ N
    *   $a = 2$ m/s²

*   **a) Total resisting force ($F_{res}$):**
    On a level road, $F_g = 0$.
    $F_{res} = F_d + F_r + F_i$ (where $F_i = 0$ for constant velocity)
    For calculating resisting force at 20 m/s without acceleration:
    $F_{res} = F_d + F_r = 300 \, N + 200 \, N = 500 \, N$

*   **b) Total tractive force required for acceleration ($F_t$):**
    $F_t = F_d + F_r + F_i$
    $F_i = m \times a = 1500 \, kg \times 2 \, m/s² = 3000 \, N$
    $F_t = 300 \, N + 200 \, N + 3000 \, N = 3500 \, N$

*   **c) Power required to maintain speed (P<sub>maintain</sub>):**
    $P_{maintain} = F_{res} \times v$
    $P_{maintain} = 500 \, N \times 20 \, m/s = 10000 \, W = 10 \, kW$

*   **d) Additional power for acceleration (P<sub>accel</sub>):**
    The additional tractive force needed for acceleration is $F_i = 3000 \, N$.
    $P_{accel} = F_i \times v$
    $P_{accel} = 3000 \, N \times 20 \, m/s = 60000 \, W = 60 \, kW$

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Speed Dependency:** Aerodynamic drag is highly speed-dependent ($v^2$), making it the dominant resisting force at highway speeds.
*   **Inertia Matters for Acceleration:** Overcoming inertia ($m \times a$) is the primary requirement for acceleration.
*   **Power vs. Torque:** Power is needed for high speeds and sustained acceleration, while torque is critical for initial acceleration and climbing hills.
*   **Powertrain Efficiency is Key:** Conventional powertrains lose a significant amount of energy, impacting overall efficiency. This is a major area where EVs offer an advantage.
*   **Driving Style Impact:** Smooth driving significantly improves efficiency in conventional vehicles.
