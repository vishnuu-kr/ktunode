# Module 4: Software Project Management - Agile Planning

## 1. Introduction to Agile Planning

Agile planning is a **highly iterative and incremental approach** to software project management that emphasizes **flexibility, collaboration, and customer feedback**. Unlike traditional, plan-driven approaches that aim for detailed upfront planning, agile planning embraces change and adapts to evolving requirements throughout the project lifecycle.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Iterative:** Development happens in short cycles, with each cycle building upon the previous one.
*   **Incremental:** Functionality is delivered in small, usable pieces, allowing for early and continuous feedback.
*   **Adaptive:** Plans are not fixed and are expected to change as the project progresses and new information becomes available.
*   **Customer Collaboration:** Continuous interaction with stakeholders to ensure the product meets their needs.
*   **Responding to Change:** Prioritizing adaptation over strict adherence to an initial plan.

**Relation to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Interpret software process models and core activities):** Agile planning is a core activity within agile process models.
*   **CO2 (Describe agile methods):** This section introduces the fundamental principles of agile planning, a key practice in agile methods.
*   **CO6 (Make use of software project management concepts):** Agile planning is a fundamental concept for project planning, estimation, and tracking.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sommerville (2015, 2020):** Discusses agile as an alternative to traditional approaches, highlighting its adaptive nature.
*   **Pressman (2014):** Explains the shift from upfront planning to iterative planning in agile development.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Agile planning is not about *no* planning; it's about *different* planning.
*   The goal is to deliver value quickly and continuously.

## 2. Principles of Agile Planning

Agile planning is guided by the values and principles of the Agile Manifesto.

**The Agile Manifesto (Agile Alliance, 2001):**

*   **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools.
*   **Working software** over comprehensive documentation.
*   **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation.
*   **Responding to change** over following a plan.

**Agile Planning Principles (Derived from the Manifesto):**

*   **Embrace Change:** Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
*   **Deliver Working Software Frequently:** From a couple of weeks to a couple of months, with a preference to the shorter timescale.
*   **Business People and Developers Must Work Together Daily:** Throughout the project.
*   **Build Projects Around Motivated Individuals:** Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done.
*   **The Most Efficient and Effective Method of Conveying Information:** To and within a development team is face-to-face conversation.
*   **Simplicity--the art of maximizing the amount of work not done--is essential.**
*   **Self-organizing teams:** Empower teams to determine the best way to accomplish their work.
*   **Regular reflection:** At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly.

**Relation to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO2 (Describe agile methods):** Directly relates to understanding the foundational principles of agile.
*   **CO6 (Make use of software project management concepts):** These principles guide how agile planning is executed for effective project management.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sommerville (2015, 2020):** Often dedicates sections to the Agile Manifesto and its impact on planning.
*   **Pressman (2014):** Explains how these principles translate into agile project management practices.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The Agile Manifesto provides the philosophical foundation for agile planning.
*   Focus on delivering value and adapting to feedback.

## 3. Levels of Agile Planning

Agile planning occurs at different levels of granularity, from high-level roadmapping to detailed sprint planning.

### 3.1. Release Planning

*   **Definition:** High-level planning that outlines the features and functionalities to be delivered in a specific release of the software. It typically spans several iterations or sprints.
*   **Purpose:** To provide a roadmap for the project, align stakeholders on priorities, and estimate the overall timeline and effort.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Defining the Vision and Goals:** What is the overall objective of this release?
    *   **Identifying Key Features/Epics:** Large chunks of functionality.
    *   **Prioritizing Features:** Based on business value, customer needs, and technical feasibility.
    *   **Estimating Release Scope and Timeline:** Rough estimates of effort and duration.
    *   **Identifying Dependencies:** Between features and external factors.
*   **Output:** A release plan, often visualized as a roadmap, product backlog of epics, and rough timelines.

### 3.2. Iteration Planning (Sprint Planning in Scrum)

*   **Definition:** Detailed planning for a short, time-boxed period (typically 1-4 weeks) called an iteration or sprint.
*   **Purpose:** To select a subset of features from the product backlog that can be completed within the iteration, define how they will be delivered, and commit to the work.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Product Owner Presents Prioritized Backlog:** The team reviews the most important items.
    *   **Team Selects Items for the Iteration:** Based on capacity and understanding.
    *   **Breaking Down Features into Tasks:** User stories are decomposed into smaller, actionable tasks.
    *   **Estimating Tasks:** Team members estimate the effort for each task.
    *   **Committing to the Iteration Goal:** The team collectively agrees on what will be delivered.
*   **Output:** An iteration backlog (or sprint backlog) containing selected user stories and their associated tasks, with estimates and assignments.

### 3.3. Daily Planning (Daily Stand-up/Scrum)

*   **Definition:** A short, daily synchronization meeting where the development team discusses progress, identifies impediments, and plans for the day.
*   **Purpose:** To ensure the team is aligned, aware of each other's progress, and can quickly address any blockers.
*   **Key Activities (for each team member):**
    *   What did I do yesterday that helped the development team meet the iteration goal?
    *   What will I do today to help the development team meet the iteration goal?
    *   Do I see any impediment that prevents me or the development team from meeting the iteration goal?
*   **Output:** A shared understanding of daily progress and immediate plans.

**Relation to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Interpret software process models and core activities):** This outlines the different planning activities within agile.
*   **CO6 (Make use of software project management concepts):** Demonstrates how planning, estimation, and tracking are integrated into agile workflows.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sommerville (2015, 2020):** Provides overviews of iterative and incremental planning.
*   **Pressman (2014):** Details the mechanics of release and iteration planning in various agile methods.
*   **Royce (1998):** While older, his work on iterative development provides a foundational understanding of phased planning.

**Example:**

Imagine a project to build an e-commerce website.

*   **Release Planning:** Might define that Release 1.0 will include core functionalities like user registration, product browsing, and a shopping cart. Release 2.0 might add order processing and payment integration.
*   **Iteration Planning:** For Release 1.0, an iteration might focus on building the user registration feature. The team breaks this down into tasks like "design database schema for users," "implement user registration form," "validate email address," and "create user authentication module."
*   **Daily Planning:** Team members might discuss yesterday's progress on the registration form, today's plan to integrate the email validation, and any issues with the authentication library.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Planning is continuous and refines as you learn more.
*   The Product Owner plays a crucial role in prioritizing at the release level.
*   The development team self-organizes and plans at the iteration and daily levels.

## 4. Agile Planning Techniques and Artifacts

Agile planning relies on specific techniques and artifacts to manage the flow of work and maintain visibility.

### 4.1. Product Backlog

*   **Definition:** A prioritized, dynamic list of all desired features, functionalities, requirements, enhancements, and fixes for the product. It's the single source of truth for what needs to be built.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **Ordered:** Items are ranked by priority, usually by business value.
    *   **Dynamic:** It evolves as the product is developed and stakeholder needs change.
    *   **Ever-changing:** It is continuously refined and updated.
    *   **Living Document:** It is never "complete."
*   **Contents:** Often contains User Stories, Epics, Bugs, and Technical Debt.
*   **Management:** The Product Owner is responsible for the Product Backlog, including its content, availability, and ordering.

### 4.2. User Stories

*   **Definition:** A concise, informal description of a feature told from the perspective of the person who desires the new capability, usually a user or customer of the system.
*   **Format (Commonly):** "As a [type of user], I want [some goal] so that [some reason]."
*   **INVEST Criteria:** Good user stories are Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, and Testable.
*   **Example:** "As a registered customer, I want to be able to reset my password so that I can regain access to my account if I forget it."

### 4.3. Epics

*   **Definition:** Large bodies of work that can be broken down into a number of smaller stories. They represent significant features or functionalities.
*   **Purpose:** To group related user stories and provide a higher-level view of functionality.
*   **Example:** "User Account Management," which could contain stories like "Register new user," "Log in," "Log out," "Reset password," and "Update profile."

### 4.4. Release Burndown Chart

*   **Definition:** A graphical representation of the work remaining in a release compared to the time remaining. It shows the progress towards completing the release.
*   **Purpose:** To provide visibility into the progress of the release and to help identify potential delays or scope creep.
*   **X-axis:** Time (days, weeks).
*   **Y-axis:** Remaining work (e.g., estimated effort in story points, remaining tasks).
*   **Ideal Line:** Represents a steady rate of progress to complete the work by the release deadline.
*   **Actual Line:** Tracks the actual progress.

### 4.5. Iteration Burndown Chart (Sprint Burndown Chart)

*   **Definition:** Similar to a release burndown chart, but tracks the work remaining within a single iteration or sprint.
*   **Purpose:** To monitor progress within the current iteration and identify if the team is on track to meet the sprint goal.

### 4.6. Velocity

*   **Definition:** A measure of the amount of work a team can complete during an iteration. It's typically expressed in story points.
*   **Purpose:** To help with forecasting and planning future iterations and releases. The team's average velocity over several iterations is used to estimate how many story points they can take on in future sprints.
*   **Calculation:** Sum of story points completed in a sprint.

**Relation to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO2 (Describe agile methods):** These are key artifacts and metrics used in agile development.
*   **CO3 (Prepare Software Requirement Specification):** User stories and epics are the building blocks of requirements in agile.
*   **CO6 (Make use of software project management concepts):** Velocity and burndown charts are vital for estimation, scheduling, and tracking.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sommerville (2015, 2020):** Often details user stories and the product backlog as core agile requirements engineering tools.
*   **Pressman (2014):** Provides in-depth explanations of these artifacts and their use in planning and tracking.
*   **Anderson (2010, 2003):** Discusses Kanban's approach to visualizing workflow and managing work-in-progress, which shares similarities with backlog management and burndown concepts.

**Example:**

A team's product backlog for an online bookstore might include epics like "Book Catalog Management" and "Customer Orders." Under "Book Catalog Management," there could be user stories like:

*   "As a shopper, I want to search for books by title so that I can find specific books quickly." (2 story points)
*   "As a shopper, I want to filter books by genre so that I can explore books within my preferred categories." (3 story points)
*   "As a librarian, I want to add new books to the catalog so that they are available for purchase." (5 story points)

If the team's velocity is typically 15 story points per sprint, they might plan to take on stories totaling 15 story points in their next sprint.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The Product Backlog is the foundation for all agile planning.
*   User stories capture requirements from the user's perspective.
*   Velocity and burndown charts are essential for forecasting and progress tracking.

## 5. Agile Planning Frameworks

While the principles are general, specific frameworks provide structured ways to implement agile planning.

### 5.1. Scrum

*   **Definition:** A framework for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products. It's an agile framework that uses iterative and incremental development.
*   **Key Roles:**
    *   **Product Owner:** Defines and prioritizes the product backlog.
    *   **Scrum Master:** Facilitates Scrum processes and removes impediments.
    *   **Development Team:** Self-organizing team responsible for delivering a potentially releasable Increment of "Done" product at the end of each Sprint.
*   **Key Events:**
    *   **Sprint Planning:** To plan the work for the Sprint.
    *   **Daily Scrum:** To synchronize daily activities.
    *   **Sprint Review:** To inspect the Increment and adapt the Product Backlog.
    *   **Sprint Retrospective:** To inspect and adapt the team's processes.
*   **Key Artifacts:**
    *   **Product Backlog:** Prioritized list of all work.
    *   **Sprint Backlog:** Set of Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, plus a plan for delivering the product Increment and realizing the Sprint Goal.
    *   **Increment:** The sum of all the Product Backlog items completed during a Sprint and the value of the increments of all previous Sprints.

**How Scrum supports Agile Planning:**

*   **Iterative Planning:** Sprint Planning defines the plan for each iteration.
*   **Adaptive Planning:** The Product Backlog is continuously refined.
*   **Progress Tracking:** Daily Scrums and Sprint Reviews monitor progress against the plan.

### 5.2. Kanban

*   **Definition:** A method for managing knowledge work with an emphasis on visualizing workflow, limiting work in progress (WIP), and maximizing efficiency.
*   **Key Principles:**
    *   **Visualize the workflow:** Use a Kanban board.
    *   **Limit Work in Progress (WIP):** Avoid overwhelming the system.
    *   **Manage Flow:** Focus on smooth, continuous delivery.
    *   **Make Policies Explicit:** Define how work is done.
    *   **Implement Feedback Loops:** Regularly review and improve.
    *   **Improve Collaboratively, Evolve Experimentally:** Foster a culture of continuous improvement.
*   **Key Artifacts:**
    *   **Kanban Board:** Visualizes the workflow stages and work items.
    *   **WIP Limits:** Constraints on the number of work items in each stage.
    *   **Kanban Metrics:** Lead time, cycle time, throughput.

**How Kanban supports Agile Planning:**

*   **Continuous Planning:** Kanban doesn't prescribe fixed iterations. Planning is an ongoing process of pulling work from the backlog as capacity becomes available.
*   **Flow-based Planning:** Focuses on smooth delivery of individual items rather than pre-defined batches.
*   **Data-Driven Planning:** Metrics like lead time and throughput help predict completion times and optimize the workflow.

### 5.3. Lean Software Development

*   **Definition:** A methodology focused on eliminating waste, amplifying learning, deciding late, delivering fast, empowering the team, building integrity in, and seeing the whole.
*   **Seven Principles (Mary Poppendieck):**
    1.  **Eliminate Waste:** Identify and remove non-value-adding activities (e.g., defects, unnecessary features, task switching).
    2.  **Build Quality In:** Prevent defects rather than fixing them later.
    3.  **Create Knowledge:** Encourage learning and experimentation.
    4.  **Defer Commitment:** Make decisions as late as possible to retain flexibility.
    5.  **Deliver Fast:** Speed up the delivery cycle.
    6.  **Empower the Team:** Trust and motivate the individuals doing the work.
    7.  **See the Whole:** Optimize the entire value stream, not just individual parts.

**How Lean supports Agile Planning:**

*   **Just-in-Time Planning:** Decisions are made closer to the point of execution to avoid wasted effort on premature or incorrect plans.
*   **Focus on Value Stream:** Planning aims to optimize the flow of value to the customer.
*   **Waste Reduction:** Planning processes themselves are scrutinized to ensure they add value.

**Relation to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO2 (Describe agile methods):** Scrum and Kanban are leading agile methodologies.
*   **CO6 (Make use of software project management concepts):** These frameworks provide practical application of planning, scheduling, and tracking in an agile context.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sommerville (2015, 2020):** Discusses Scrum and Kanban as popular agile frameworks.
*   **Pressman (2014):** Provides detailed explanations of Scrum and Kanban processes and their planning implications.
*   **Anderson (2010, 2003):** The primary source for understanding Kanban and its application.
*   **Poppendieck (2006):** The foundational text for Lean Software Development principles and their impact on project management.

**Example:**

*   **Scrum:** A team plans for a 2-week sprint, selects 5 user stories from the backlog, breaks them into tasks, and estimates each task. They commit to delivering these stories by the end of the sprint.
*   **Kanban:** A team uses a Kanban board with columns like "To Do," "In Progress," and "Done." They pull the highest priority item from "To Do" into "In Progress" when a developer is free. WIP limits ensure no more than 3 items are "In Progress" at any time.
*   **Lean:** A team identifies that lengthy code reviews are causing delays (waste). They decide to implement a policy of pair programming to catch defects earlier and reduce the need for extensive later reviews, thus speeding up delivery.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Scrum provides a structured, time-boxed approach to agile planning.
*   Kanban offers a flexible, flow-based approach focused on continuous delivery and waste reduction.
*   Lean principles guide the optimization of agile planning processes to maximize value and minimize waste.

## 6. Agile Planning: Planning Poker and Estimation

Accurate estimation is crucial for agile planning, and several techniques are used.

### 6.1. Planning Poker

*   **Definition:** A consensus-based technique used by agile teams to estimate the effort required for backlog items, typically user stories.
*   **Process:**
    1.  The Product Owner explains a user story.
    2.  The team discusses the story, asking questions for clarification.
    3.  Each team member privately selects a card representing their estimate of the effort (e.g., using Fibonacci sequence numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100, or "?" for uncertainty).
    4.  Everyone reveals their cards simultaneously.
    5.  If estimates are similar, the average is taken as the team's estimate.
    6.  If estimates differ significantly, the highest and lowest estimators explain their reasoning, and the team discusses until consensus is reached, often re-estimating.
*   **Purpose:** To leverage the collective intelligence of the team for more accurate and agreed-upon estimates, and to uncover hidden assumptions or complexities.

### 6.2. Story Points

*   **Definition:** A unit of measure for estimating the overall effort required to implement a feature or user story. It represents a combination of complexity, effort, and risk.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   **Relative Estimation:** Story points are not directly tied to time (hours/days) but are relative to other story points.
    *   **Abstract:** They represent a measure of "how much" rather than "how long."
    *   **Team-Specific:** What constitutes a "3" for one team might be a "5" for another.
*   **Purpose:** To provide a consistent way to estimate and compare the relative size of different work items, which is then used with velocity to forecast.

### 6.3. Affinity Estimating

*   **Definition:** A quick estimation technique for a large number of backlog items. The team groups items into relative size categories.
*   **Process:**
    1.  Large number of user stories are laid out.
    2.  Team members take turns picking up a story and placing it into a pre-defined size bucket (e.g., Small, Medium, Large, Extra Large).
    3.  Discussions happen if there's disagreement on placement.
*   **Purpose:** To get a rough estimate of the relative effort for many items efficiently.

**Relation to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO6 (Make use of software project management concepts):** Planning Poker, story points, and affinity estimating are key techniques for estimation within agile planning.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Pressman (2014):** Covers various estimation techniques used in agile, including Planning Poker.
*   **Sommerville (2015, 2020):** May discuss estimation as part of agile practices.

**Example:**

During Planning Poker for a story about implementing a "Wishlist" feature:

*   Developer A estimates 8 story points.
*   Developer B estimates 13 story points.
*   Developer C estimates 5 story points.
*   Developer D estimates 8 story points.

Developers B and C have the most different estimates. Developer B explains that they foresee significant challenges with database indexing for performance. Developer C mentions they've worked on similar features before and found them straightforward. The team discusses, perhaps agreeing to investigate the indexing further or acknowledging the uncertainty. They might re-estimate to an 8 or 13, or even add a spike (a time-boxed investigation task) to better understand the complexity.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Estimation in agile is about relative sizing, not absolute time.
*   Planning Poker encourages collaboration and discussion to achieve consensus.
*   Story points are a valuable metric when combined with team velocity for forecasting.

## 7. Managing Change in Agile Planning

Agile planning inherently embraces change. The goal is not to prevent change but to manage it effectively to deliver the most value.

### 7.1. Continuous Refinement (Backlog Grooming)

*   **Definition:** The ongoing process of reviewing, updating, and refining the Product Backlog.
*   **Purpose:** To ensure the backlog is accurate, prioritized, and contains well-understood items ready for development.
*   **Activities:**
    *   Adding new items.
    *   Removing obsolete items.
    *   Re-prioritizing items.
    *   Breaking down large items (epics) into smaller user stories.
    *   Adding details, acceptance criteria, and estimates to user stories.
*   **Frequency:** Typically done regularly (e.g., weekly or before each sprint planning meeting).

### 7.2. Embracing Feedback

*   **Definition:** Actively soliciting and incorporating feedback from stakeholders at the end of each iteration (e.g., during Sprint Review) and throughout the development process.
*   **Purpose:** To ensure the product is aligned with evolving customer needs and business priorities.
*   **Mechanism:** Sprint Reviews, demos, direct communication with the Product Owner.

### 7.3. Adapting the Plan

*   **Definition:** Adjusting the release plan and sprint backlog based on feedback, new information, or changing priorities.
*   **Mechanism:**
    *   **Product Backlog Updates:** The Product Owner can re-prioritize items at any time.
    *   **Scope Negotiation:** If new high-priority items emerge, the team and Product Owner negotiate what can be swapped out of the current sprint or upcoming sprints.
    *   **Retrospectives:** The team reflects on what went well and what could be improved, leading to adjustments in how they plan and execute.

**Relation to Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (Interpret software process models and core activities):** Handling changes is a core activity in agile.
*   **CO6 (Make use of software project management concepts):** This directly addresses change management in agile project planning.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Sommerville (2015, 2020):** Highlights the adaptive nature of agile planning and its mechanisms for change.
*   **Pressman (2014):** Discusses how agile methodologies are designed to manage change effectively.
*   **Royce (1998):** Emphasizes the benefits of iterative development for managing change.

**Example:**

During a Sprint Review, a stakeholder provides feedback that a particular feature, initially deemed low priority, is now critical for an upcoming marketing campaign. The Product Owner, in response, re-prioritizes this feature to the top of the product backlog. The team will then incorporate this new high-priority item into a future sprint, potentially by deferring less critical items.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Agile planning is not rigid; it's designed to be flexible.
*   Continuous refinement and feedback loops are key to managing change effectively.
*   The Product Owner is central to prioritizing changes.

**Question 1:**
Which of the following is NOT a core value of the Agile Manifesto?
a) Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
b) Comprehensive documentation over working software
c) Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
d) Responding to change over following a plan

**Answer:** b) Comprehensive documentation over working software. The Agile Manifesto values "Working software over comprehensive documentation."

**Question 2:**
What is the primary purpose of a Product Backlog in agile planning?
a) To list all the technical debt of the project.
b) To serve as a continuously updated, prioritized list of all desired work for the product.
c) To document the project's architecture.
d) To track the team's daily progress.

**Answer:** b) To serve as a continuously updated, prioritized list of all desired work for the product.

What does "velocity" typically refer to in Scrum?
a) The speed at which a single developer can complete tasks.
b) The amount of work a team can complete in a single iteration, usually measured in story points.

**Answer:** b) The amount of work a team can complete in a single iteration, usually measured in story points.

Explain the concept of "Planning Poker" and its role in agile estimation.

**Answer:** Planning Poker is a consensus-based technique used by agile teams to estimate the effort required for backlog items (like user stories). It involves team members privately selecting estimate cards (often using relative units like story points) and then revealing them simultaneously. Discrepancies are discussed until a consensus is reached. Its role is to leverage the collective intelligence of the team for more accurate and agreed-upon estimates, helping to identify hidden complexities and assumptions.

**Question 5:**
How does agile planning differ from traditional, plan-driven project planning in terms of handling change?

**Answer:** Traditional plan-driven planning aims for detailed, upfront planning and treats change as an exception to be controlled through formal change management processes. Agile planning, conversely, embraces change as an inherent part of software development. It uses iterative and incremental approaches with short cycles, continuous feedback, and a dynamic backlog to adapt to evolving requirements and priorities, making change a normal and expected part of the process.

**Question 6 (Exercise):**
Assume a development team has the following velocity history:
*   Sprint 1: 12 story points
*   Sprint 2: 16 story points
*   Sprint 3: 14 story points

If their average velocity is 14 story points per sprint, and they have a Product Backlog with the following estimated items for the next release:

*   Epic A: 20 story points
*   Epic B: 30 story points
*   Epic C: 15 story points

How many sprints would this team likely need to complete the release, assuming the backlog items are broken down into sprint-sized chunks and the velocity remains consistent?

**Answer:**
Total story points for the release = 20 + 30 + 15 = 65 story points.
Average velocity = 14 story points/sprint.
Number of sprints = Total story points / Average velocity
Number of sprints = 65 / 14 ≈ 4.64 sprints.

Therefore, the team would likely need **5 sprints** to complete the release, as you can't have a fraction of a sprint. They would aim to complete around 14 story points each sprint.

**Question 7 (Exercise):**
Create a user story for adding a "Forgot Password" functionality to a web application. Ensure it follows the common format and considers the INVEST criteria.

**Answer:**
**User Story:** "As a registered user, I want to be able to reset my password via email so that I can regain access to my account if I forget my password."

**Consideration of INVEST criteria:**

*   **Independent:** Can be developed and deployed without necessarily relying on other user stories.
*   **Negotiable:** The details of the email sending process, password complexity, and security measures can be discussed and refined.
*   **Valuable:** Directly provides value to the user by enabling account recovery.
*   **Estimable:** The team can estimate the effort based on the complexity of email integration, form handling, and security.
*   **Small:** It's a focused piece of functionality that can typically fit within an iteration.
*   **Testable:** Acceptance criteria can be defined to verify its functionality (e.g., user receives email, password is reset successfully).

## Summary of Key Takeaways

*   Agile planning is **adaptive, iterative, and incremental**, prioritizing flexibility and customer feedback.
*   It operates at multiple levels: **release planning, iteration planning, and daily planning.**
*   Key artifacts include the **Product Backlog, User Stories, and Epics.**
*   Metrics like **velocity and burndown charts** are crucial for tracking and forecasting.
*   Frameworks like **Scrum and Kanban** provide structured approaches to agile planning.
*   **Lean principles** emphasize eliminating waste and optimizing the flow of value.
*   Techniques like **Planning Poker and story points** are used for relative estimation.
*   **Continuous refinement and embracing feedback** are fundamental to managing change in agile planning.
