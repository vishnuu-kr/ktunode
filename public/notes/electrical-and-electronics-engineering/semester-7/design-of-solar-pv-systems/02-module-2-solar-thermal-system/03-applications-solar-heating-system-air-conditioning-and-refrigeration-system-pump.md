# DESIGN OF SOLAR PV SYSTEMS

## Module 2: Solar Thermal Systems - Applications

This module explores various applications of solar thermal energy, focusing on how solar heat can be harnessed for different purposes. While the overarching subject is Solar PV Systems, understanding solar thermal applications provides context for the broader utilization of solar energy.

---

### 1. Solar Heating Systems

Solar heating systems utilize solar collectors to capture solar radiation and transfer this thermal energy to a fluid (typically water or air). This heated fluid is then used for various heating applications.

**1.1. Types of Solar Heating Systems:**

*   **Solar Water Heating Systems:**
    *   **Description:** These are the most common solar thermal applications. They heat water for domestic use (bathing, washing, etc.) or for swimming pools.
    *   **Key Components:**
        *   **Solar Collectors:** Absorb solar radiation and transfer heat to a fluid.
            *   **Flat-plate collectors:** Most common, consist of an insulated box with a transparent cover, an absorber plate (usually blackened metal) with fluid passages.
            *   **Evacuated tube collectors:** Consist of glass tubes with a vacuum between the inner and outer walls, providing better insulation and higher temperatures.
        *   **Storage Tank:** Insulated tank to store heated water.
        *   **Circulation System:** Moves the fluid between the collector and the storage tank.
            *   **Natural Circulation (Thermosiphon):** Hot water rises naturally from the collector to the tank, which must be located above the collector.
            *   **Forced Circulation:** A pump is used to circulate the fluid, allowing more flexibility in system design.
        *   **Piping:** Connects components and transports the fluid.
    *   **Working Principle:** Solar radiation strikes the absorber plate, heating it. Heat is transferred to the fluid flowing through the passages in the plate. The heated fluid then circulates to the storage tank.
    *   **Example:** A domestic solar water heater on a rooftop providing hot water for a household.
    *   **Textbook Reference:** Solanki (3rd Edition) extensively covers solar collectors and water heating systems, detailing different collector types and system configurations. Tiwari's book provides in-depth design principles and performance analysis of solar water heaters.

*   **Solar Space Heating Systems:**
    *   **Description:** Used to heat buildings (homes, offices, etc.). They can be direct (solar energy directly heats the air that circulates in the building) or indirect (solar energy heats a fluid that then heats the air).
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Direct Gain:** Sunlight enters the building directly through south-facing windows and is absorbed by thermal mass (walls, floors).
        *   **Indirect Gain:** Sunlight heats a thermal mass (e.g., a wall or water container) located between the collector and the living space.
        *   **Isolated Gain:** Sunlight heats a collector, and the heated air or fluid is then distributed to the living space.
    *   **Key Components:** Solar collectors (often air collectors), ductwork, fans, storage systems (e.g., rock beds for air systems).
    *   **Example:** A passive solar home design where south-facing windows allow sunlight to warm the interior, or an active system with air collectors on the roof distributing warm air via ducts.

**1.2. Design Considerations for Solar Water Heaters (LO Covered: CO2 - Design of a standalone PV system, implies designing solar thermal systems too):**

*   **Location and Orientation:** Collectors should be oriented towards the equator (south in the Northern Hemisphere, north in the Southern Hemisphere) and tilted at an angle optimized for the latitude and seasonal usage.
*   **Collector Area:** Determined by the hot water demand and solar radiation availability.
*   **Storage Tank Sizing:** Based on daily hot water consumption and ensuring sufficient hot water during periods of low solar radiation.
*   **System Type (Thermosiphon vs. Forced Circulation):** Choice depends on site constraints, cost, and reliability requirements.
*   **Heat Transfer Fluid:** Water is common, but antifreeze solutions may be used in colder climates.
*   **Efficiency of Collectors:** Higher efficiency collectors will require a smaller area for the same hot water output.
*   **Integration with Conventional Heating:** Often coupled with conventional water heaters for backup during cloudy days or high demand.

**1.3. Performance Evaluation:**

*   **F-chart method:** A graphical method for estimating the long-term performance of solar heating systems.
*   **Utilization Factor:** Represents the fraction of collected solar energy that is actually used.

### 2. Solar Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Systems

Solar energy can be used to power air conditioning and refrigeration cycles, reducing reliance on electricity from the grid.

**2.1. Solar Absorption Systems:**

*   **Description:** These systems use a thermal-driven absorption refrigeration cycle. A heat source (solar collectors) is used to drive the cycle.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Solar Collectors:** Usually flat-plate or evacuated tube collectors providing medium-to-high temperatures.
    *   **Generator:** Where the refrigerant is boiled off from the absorbent using solar heat.
    *   **Condenser:** Cools and liquefies the refrigerant.
    *   **Evaporator:** Where the refrigerant evaporates, absorbing heat from the space to be cooled.
    *   **Absorber:** Where the refrigerant vapor is absorbed by the absorbent.
    *   **Pump:** Circulates the absorbent-solution.
*   **Working Principle:** Solar heat is used to drive the generator, separating the refrigerant from the absorbent. The refrigerant then undergoes the cooling cycle.
*   **Example:** A solar-powered absorption chiller for large buildings or industrial applications.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Solanki and Tiwari discuss solar cooling technologies, including absorption and adsorption systems, their principles, and design considerations.

**2.2. Solar Adsorption Systems:**

*   **Description:** Similar to absorption systems but use a solid adsorbent material (like silica gel or zeolite) and a refrigerant (like water or methanol).
*   **Working Principle:** Solar heat is used to desorb the refrigerant from the adsorbent. The refrigerant then condenses and evaporates, absorbing heat.
*   **Advantages:** Can operate at lower temperatures than absorption systems, making them suitable for flat-plate collectors.
*   **Example:** Small-scale solar refrigerators or air conditioners for residential use.

**2.3. Solar-Powered Mechanical Vapor Compression (MVC):**

*   **Description:** Solar PV systems power an electric compressor in a conventional vapor-compression refrigeration cycle.
*   **Working Principle:** Solar PV panels generate electricity, which drives a compressor, fans, and pumps in the refrigeration system.
*   **Example:** Rooftop solar PV panels powering a standard air conditioner.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Kothari & Jamil's "Grid Integration of Solar Photovoltaic Systems" is highly relevant here, as it focuses on PV systems powering electrical loads, including HVAC systems.

### 3. Pumping Systems

Solar thermal energy can be used for pumping water or other fluids.

**3.1. Solar Thermal Pumping:**

*   **Description:** Solar heat is used to power a thermal engine (e.g., Rankine cycle, Stirling engine) that drives a mechanical pump.
*   **Working Principle:** Solar collectors heat a working fluid, which then drives a turbine or piston, powering the pump.
*   **Example:** Pumping water for irrigation in remote agricultural areas where grid electricity is unavailable.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Tiwari's book covers solar thermal applications, including those driving mechanical systems.

**3.2. Solar PV Pumping (Indirect relevance to the overall course):**

*   **Description:** Solar PV panels generate electricity directly to power an electric pump.
*   **Working Principle:** PV panels convert sunlight into DC electricity, which is then used by a DC pump or an AC pump via an inverter.
*   **Example:** Solar-powered water pumps for boreholes in rural villages for drinking water.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Solanki's "Solar Photovoltaics" is the primary resource for understanding PV modules and their application in powering loads like pumps. Kothari & Jamil also discuss PV system design for various loads.

### 4. Solar Cookers

Solar cookers harness solar radiation to cook food. They are an eco-friendly and cost-effective alternative to traditional cooking methods.

**4.1. Types of Solar Cookers:**

*   **Box Cookers:**
    *   **Description:** An insulated box with a transparent lid. An inner reflective surface directs sunlight onto a dark cooking pot.
    *   **Working Principle:** Traps solar radiation and uses a greenhouse effect to heat the cooking chamber.
    *   **Key Features:** Simple design, can reach temperatures of 100-150°C.
    *   **Example:** A common household solar cooker for baking and boiling.
    *   **Textbook Reference:** Solanki and Tiwari discuss various solar cooking technologies and their thermal performance.

*   **Parabolic Cookers:**
    *   **Description:** Use a parabolic reflector to concentrate sunlight onto a cooking pot placed at the focal point.
    *   **Working Principle:** Concentrates solar radiation to achieve much higher temperatures (200-300°C or more).
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Parabolic Dish Cookers:** Most common, direct sunlight to a single focal point.
        *   **Parabolic Trough Cookers:** Concentrate sunlight onto a receiver tube.
    *   **Example:** A parabolic dish cooker used for frying or grilling.

*   **Panel Cookers:**
    *   **Description:** Use flat or slightly curved reflective panels to direct sunlight onto a cooking pot.
    *   **Working Principle:** Less concentrating than parabolic cookers but easier to manufacture and use.
    *   **Example:** A foldable panel cooker for portable use.

**4.2. Design Considerations:**

*   **Concentration Ratio:** The ratio of the aperture area to the collector area, affecting the temperature achieved.
*   **Reflector Efficiency:** The reflectivity and surface finish of the reflector.
*   **Absorber Properties:** The color and emissivity of the cooking pot.
*   **Heat Losses:** Minimizing heat loss through insulation and avoiding air leaks.

### 5. Solar Furnaces

Solar furnaces are specialized devices designed to generate extremely high temperatures by concentrating solar radiation to a very small focal area.

**5.1. Description and Working Principle:**

*   **Description:** Employ large arrays of heliostats (sun-tracking mirrors) or large parabolic dishes to reflect and concentrate sunlight onto a central receiver.
*   **Working Principle:** The concentrated solar flux creates very high temperatures (up to 3000°C or more) in the receiver, allowing for material testing, high-temperature chemical reactions, and energy conversion studies.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Heliostat Field:** A large array of steerable mirrors that track the sun.
    *   **Central Receiver:** A target that absorbs the concentrated solar radiation.
    *   **Tracking System:** To maintain precise alignment of heliostats towards the receiver.
*   **Example:** The Odeillo solar furnace in France is a well-known example.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Materials science (melting, heat treatment).
    *   Ceramics and refractories testing.
    *   Solar thermal power generation (receiver heats a fluid to drive a turbine).
    *   Chemical processing.
*   **Textbook Reference:** Tiwari's book may touch upon high-temperature solar applications, including solar furnaces, in the context of solar thermal power generation.

### 6. Solar Greenhouses

Solar greenhouses utilize solar energy for heating, lighting, and maintaining optimal conditions for plant growth.

**6.1. Description and Working Principle:**

*   **Description:** Structures designed to capture and retain solar radiation, creating a microclimate suitable for cultivating plants. They are often integrated with solar thermal systems for heating.
*   **Working Principle:**
    *   **Greenhouse Effect:** Transparent glazing allows solar radiation to enter, heating the interior. Heat is trapped due to the enclosure and reduced convection.
    *   **Thermal Mass:** Materials within the greenhouse (e.g., water barrels, stone walls) absorb solar heat during the day and release it at night.
    *   **Solar Thermal Heating:** Solar collectors can preheat air or water, which is then circulated within the greenhouse.
    *   **Solar PV Integration:** PV panels can power fans for ventilation, pumps for irrigation, or supplemental lighting.
*   **Design Elements:**
    *   **Glazing Material:** Glass, polycarbonate, or polyethylene films.
    *   **Orientation and Shape:** Optimizing solar gain.
    *   **Insulation:** Minimizing heat loss.
    *   **Ventilation and Shading:** Controlling temperature and humidity.
    *   **Thermal Storage:** Enhancing temperature stability.
*   **Example:** A greenhouse for year-round cultivation of vegetables in cooler climates.
*   **Textbook Reference:** While not a primary focus of PV systems textbooks, the principles of thermal management and energy utilization within a greenhouse are relevant to understanding solar thermal applications broadly. Tiwari's book might offer insights into controlled environment agriculture.

### 7. Design of Solar Water Heater (Detailed Breakdown)

This section elaborates on the design aspects of a solar water heater, which is a core application discussed earlier. This aligns with **Learning Outcome:** Design of solar water heater (part of the overall applications).

**7.1. Steps in Designing a Solar Water Heater:**

1.  **Determine Hot Water Requirements:**
    *   **Daily Water Consumption:** Estimate the number of people and their average hot water usage (e.g., liters per person per day for bathing, washing, etc.).
    *   **Peak Demand:** Consider the maximum hot water needed at any given time.

2.  **Site Assessment:**
    *   **Solar Resource Availability:** Obtain data on local solar insolation (average daily solar radiation) for the specific location. This is crucial for calculating the energy available.
    *   **Collector Location:** Identify a suitable, unshaded location for collector installation (e.g., rooftop).
    *   **Orientation and Tilt Angle:**
        *   **Orientation:** Facing true south (Northern Hemisphere) or true north (Southern Hemisphere).
        *   **Tilt Angle:** Typically set equal to the latitude for year-round performance. For seasonal optimization, adjust: latitude + 15° for winter, latitude - 15° for summer.

3.  **Collector Selection:**
    *   **Type:** Flat-plate collectors are common for domestic use. Evacuated tube collectors offer higher efficiency, especially in colder climates or when higher temperatures are needed.
    *   **Size (Area):** Calculated based on hot water demand, solar insolation, collector efficiency, and desired temperature rise.
        *   *Rule of Thumb:* 1 sq. meter of collector area per 1-2 persons for moderate climates.
        *   *Calculation involves energy balance:* `Energy Required = Energy Available × Collector Efficiency × System Efficiency`.

4.  **Storage Tank Sizing:**
    *   **Capacity:** Typically 1.5 to 2 times the daily hot water demand to ensure availability during cloudy periods.
    *   **Location:** For thermosiphon systems, the tank must be above the collectors. For forced circulation, it can be placed below or at the same level.
    *   **Insulation:** High-quality insulation is essential to minimize heat loss from the stored water.

5.  **Circulation System Design:**
    *   **Thermosiphon:** Requires higher tank placement and larger diameter pipes to facilitate natural convection.
    *   **Forced Circulation:**
        *   **Pump Selection:** Choose a pump with appropriate flow rate and head capacity to overcome system resistance.
        *   **Controller:** A differential temperature controller is used to activate the pump only when the collector temperature is sufficiently higher than the tank temperature.
        *   **Piping:** Select appropriate pipe sizes and materials (e.g., copper, PEX) and ensure proper insulation to minimize heat loss during circulation.

6.  **System Integration and Safety:**
    *   **Freeze Protection:** In colder climates, consider drain-back systems, antifreeze solutions (glycol), or electric heating elements.
    *   **Overheating Protection:** Pressure relief valves and expansion tanks are essential for safety.
    *   **Backup Heating:** Integrate with a conventional heater (electric or gas) for periods of low solar availability.

7.  **Performance Estimation:**
    *   Use methods like the F-chart or simulation software to predict the system's thermal performance and solar fraction (the percentage of total heating load met by solar energy).

**7.2. Example Design Problem (Simplified):**

**Problem:** Design a solar water heater for a family of 4 in a location with an average daily insolation of 5 kWh/m²/day. The daily hot water requirement is 200 liters at 50°C. Assume ambient temperature of 20°C.

**Solution Steps (Illustrative):**

1.  **Hot Water Demand:** 200 liters/day.
2.  **Site Assessment:** Assume a south-facing roof with no shading. Optimal tilt angle = latitude (e.g., if latitude is 30°, tilt is 30°).
3.  **Collector Area:**
    *   Assume a collector efficiency of 50% and system efficiency of 70%.
    *   Energy required to heat 200 kg of water by 30°C (from 20°C to 50°C): Q = m * c * ΔT = 200 kg * 4.18 kJ/kg°C * 30°C = 25,080 kJ/day ≈ 7 kWh/day.
    *   Solar energy available on 1 m² = 5 kWh/m²/day.
    *   Required collector area (approximate) = Energy Required / (Insolation * Collector Efficiency * System Efficiency)
    *   Area ≈ 7 kWh/day / (5 kWh/m²/day * 0.50 * 0.70) ≈ 4 m².
    *   *Note:* This is a simplified calculation. Detailed design involves more complex methods. A practical choice might be 4-5 m² of collector area.

4.  **Storage Tank Size:** 1.5 to 2 times daily demand = 300-400 liters. A 300-liter tank would be suitable.

5.  **Circulation System:** For a residential system, a forced circulation system with a small circulation pump and differential controller would be efficient and flexible.

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Solar Thermal Energy:** Heat derived from solar radiation.
*   **Solar Collector:** Device that absorbs solar radiation and converts it into thermal energy.
*   **Absorber Plate:** The component in a flat-plate collector that absorbs solar radiation and transfers heat to the fluid.
*   **Glazing:** Transparent cover (glass or plastic) that reduces heat loss from the collector.
*   **Thermosiphon:** Natural circulation of a fluid due to density differences caused by temperature variations.
*   **Absorption Refrigeration:** A refrigeration cycle powered by heat rather than mechanical work.
*   **Heliostat:** A mirror that tracks the sun and reflects its rays onto a fixed target.
*   **Greenhouse Effect:** The trapping of solar radiation within an enclosed space, leading to a temperature rise.
*   **Solar Fraction:** The proportion of a building's total heating or cooling demand that is met by solar energy.

---

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Site assessment (insolation, shading, orientation, tilt) is critical for all solar thermal designs.**
*   **Collector efficiency and system losses significantly impact the required collector area.**
*   **Proper insulation of collectors and storage tanks is essential to minimize heat loss.**
*   **Safety features (pressure relief, overheating protection) are mandatory.**
*   **Understanding user demand is paramount for sizing any solar thermal system.**
*   **Solar thermal systems can be integrated with conventional systems for reliable operation.**

3.  ** (CO1, K2) Describe the basic working principle of a solar absorption refrigeration system.**
    *   **Answer:** A solar absorption refrigeration system uses solar heat as its energy input. Solar collectors heat a solution of refrigerant and absorbent (e.g., lithium bromide and water, or ammonia and water). This heat drives a generator, separating the refrigerant into a vapor. The vapor is condensed, then evaporated to produce cooling, and finally reabsorbed by the absorbent to complete the cycle.

        *   **Ventilation and Shading:** Controlling overheating during sunny periods.
        *   **Water Management:** Efficient irrigation systems, possibly linked to solar-powered pumps.
