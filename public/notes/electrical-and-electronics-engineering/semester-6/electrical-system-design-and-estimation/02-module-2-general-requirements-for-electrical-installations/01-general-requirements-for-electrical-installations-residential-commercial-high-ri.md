# Module 2: General Requirements for Electrical Installations

## Topic: General Requirements for Electrical Installations, Load Survey, and Diversity Factor

This module delves into the fundamental requirements for designing safe and efficient electrical installations in various building types, along with the crucial processes of load surveying and applying diversity factors for accurate electrical system design.

---

### 1. General Requirements for Electrical Installations

This section outlines the overarching principles and standards governing electrical installations in different building categories. These requirements are crucial for ensuring safety, reliability, and compliance with national and international codes.

#### 1.1. Residential Buildings

Electrical installations in residential buildings aim to provide safe, convenient, and adequate power supply for domestic appliances, lighting, and communication.

*   **Key Requirements:**
    *   **Safety:** Protection against electric shock, fire, and overload. This includes proper earthing, insulation, overcurrent protection (fuses, circuit breakers), and residual current devices (RCDs). (Refer to IS 3043 for Earthing, IS 732 for Wiring Systems).
    *   **Adequacy:** Sufficient capacity to meet the intended use of the dwelling, considering future expansion.
    *   **Convenience:** Provision of adequate lighting and power outlets in accessible locations.
    *   **Wiring System:** Choice of wiring system (e.g., conduit wiring, surface wiring) based on cost, aesthetics, and safety. Types of cables (e.g., PVC insulated, XLPE insulated) and their appropriate use. (Refer to IS 732).
    *   **Earthing:** Effective earthing of all metallic parts of the installation to prevent dangerous voltage buildup. (Refer to IS 3043).
    *   **Lighting:** Adequate illumination levels for different rooms and activities. (Refer to National Lighting Code 2010).
    *   **Power Outlets:** Sufficient number of sockets for appliances, located strategically.
    *   **Special Considerations:** Provision for dedicated circuits for heavy loads like air conditioners, water heaters, and electric cookers. Provisions for telephone, internet, and television outlets.
    *   **Compliance:** Adherence to the National Building Code of India (NBC) 2016 and relevant IS codes.

*   **Examples:**
    *   A typical 3BHK apartment will require circuits for lighting, fans, exhaust fans, power outlets in bedrooms, living room, kitchen, bathrooms, and potentially dedicated circuits for AC units.
    *   Proper grounding of all metal fixtures and appliances is a mandatory safety requirement.

#### 1.2. Commercial Buildings

Commercial buildings (offices, shops, showrooms, restaurants) have higher power demands and require more complex electrical systems than residential buildings due to continuous operation and a higher concentration of equipment.

*   **Key Requirements:**
    *   **Higher Load Capacity:** Greater number of lighting points, power outlets, and specialized equipment (computers, printers, HVAC systems, elevators, escalators).
    *   **Reliability:** Uninterrupted power supply is often critical, requiring standby power systems (generators, UPS).
    *   **Safety:** Enhanced safety measures due to higher operating voltages and currents, including robust overcurrent protection, RCDs for all circuits, and emergency lighting.
    *   **Lighting Design:** Focused on creating appropriate ambiance and illumination levels for specific activities (e.g., task lighting in offices, accent lighting in showrooms). Energy-efficient lighting solutions are encouraged. (Refer to National Lighting Code 2010).
    *   **Power Distribution:** Efficient distribution network with main distribution boards (MDBs) and sub-distribution boards (SDBs). Proper cable sizing and routing are crucial.
    *   **Fire Safety:** Integration with fire alarm systems and sprinklers. Emergency exits and routes must be clearly illuminated by emergency lighting. (Refer to NBC 2016).
    *   **Earthing:** Comprehensive earthing system for all equipment and structures.
    *   **Compliance:** Adherence to NBC 2016, IS codes, and specific fire safety regulations.

*   **Examples:**
    *   An office building will need circuits for general lighting, task lighting at workstations, power for computers, printers, photocopiers, servers, HVAC systems, and elevators.
    *   A retail store will require lighting to highlight merchandise, power for point-of-sale systems, security systems, and display units.

#### 1.3. High-Rise Buildings

High-rise buildings present unique challenges due to their verticality, significant power demands, and increased fire risk. Electrical systems must be robust, reliable, and compliant with stringent safety standards.

*   **Key Requirements:**
    *   **Vertical Distribution:** Efficient vertical power distribution using risers and intermediate distribution panels.
    *   **Fire Resistance:** All electrical conduits, cables, and equipment must have fire-resistant properties to prevent the spread of fire. Fire-rated cables and containment systems are essential. (Refer to IS 732, IS 1708 for cable testing).
    *   **Emergency Power:** Robust emergency power supply for essential services like lighting in escape routes, fire fighting pumps, elevators for emergency personnel, and communication systems.
    *   **Lightning Protection:** Comprehensive lightning protection system for the entire building structure. (Refer to IS 2309).
    *   **Standby Power:** Generators of adequate capacity to power essential services during mains failure.
    *   **Ventilation:** Adequate ventilation for electrical rooms to prevent overheating of equipment.
    *   **Earthing:** Multi-point earthing system, including structural earthing.
    *   **Communication and Safety Systems:** Integration of public address systems, fire detection and alarm systems, and building management systems (BMS).
    *   **Specialized Lifts:** Dedicated power supplies and control systems for passenger and goods lifts.
    *   **Compliance:** Strict adherence to NBC 2016, IS codes, and specific building bye-laws for high-rise structures.

*   **Examples:**
    *   A 30-story residential tower will have main electrical risers originating from the basement or ground floor, feeding distribution panels on each floor.
    *   Fire pumps, emergency lighting in stairwells and corridors, and communication systems are critical for safety in high-rise buildings.

### 2. Method of Load Survey for Electrical Installation

A load survey is a systematic process of assessing and quantifying the electrical power demand of a building or an installation. It is crucial for accurate sizing of electrical equipment, conductors, and distribution systems.

*   **Purpose of Load Survey:**
    *   To determine the total connected load.
    *   To estimate the maximum demand (MD).
    *   To select appropriate capacity of transformers, switchgear, cables, and generators.
    *   To plan for future load growth.
    *   To ensure compliance with safety regulations and capacity limitations.

*   **Methods of Load Survey:**

    1.  **Sanctioned Load Method:**
        *   **Concept:** Based on the sanctioned electrical load from the utility provider for a particular consumer. This is typically used for new installations where the intended usage is clearly defined.
        *   **Procedure:** Obtain details of the sanctioned load from the electricity board. Identify the connected load for each type of equipment (lighting, fans, power outlets, heavy appliances) based on their rated power.
        *   **Limitations:** May not accurately reflect the actual usage pattern and can be an overestimation or underestimation if the sanctioned load is not in line with actual consumption.

    2.  **Connected Load Method:**
        *   **Concept:** This involves enumerating all electrical appliances and equipment installed in the building and summing up their rated power consumption.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   **Inventory:** Create a detailed list of all electrical points (lighting points, fan points, socket outlets) and the appliances connected to them.
            *   **Nameplate Data:** Obtain the rated power (in Watts or kW) from the nameplate of each appliance/equipment.
            *   **Calculation:** Sum up the power ratings of all connected loads.
        *   **Example:**
            *   Lights: 10 points x 10W = 100W
            *   Fans: 5 points x 75W = 375W
            *   5A Sockets: 15 points x 100W (estimated for common use) = 1500W
            *   15A Sockets: 5 points x 1500W (estimated for AC, geyser) = 7500W
            *   Total Connected Load = 100 + 375 + 1500 + 7500 = 9475W = 9.475 kW
        *   **Limitations:** This method represents the theoretical maximum load if all appliances are switched on simultaneously, which is rarely the case. It does not account for the diversity of usage.

    3.  **Demand Survey (Actual Load Measurement):**
        *   **Concept:** This is the most accurate method, involving direct measurement of the electrical load over a period of time using energy meters or clamp meters.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   **Monitoring Period:** Install energy meters or loggers to record the power consumption at various points in the distribution system (e.g., main incomer, sub-distribution boards) over a representative period (e.g., 24 hours, 7 days, or even a month, depending on the type of building and its usage patterns).
            *   **Data Analysis:** Analyze the recorded data to identify peak demand, average demand, and load variations throughout the day, week, and year.
        *   **Tools:**
            *   **Energy Meter:** Measures energy consumption (kWh). Can be used to infer demand from rate of consumption.
            *   **Clamp-on Meter:** Directly measures current, voltage, and power. Can be used to take instantaneous readings at different circuits.
            *   **Power Quality Analyzers/Data Loggers:** Advanced instruments for continuous monitoring and detailed analysis of power consumption and quality.
        *   **Importance:** Essential for existing installations to understand actual usage patterns and optimize the electrical system.

    4.  **Tabulation of Appliances and Their Ratings:**
        *   **Concept:** A structured approach to gather information about all electrical loads within the installation.
        *   **Procedure:** Create a table listing:
            *   Location of the point (e.g., Living Room, Kitchen, Office Desk)
            *   Type of Point (e.g., Light, Fan, 5A Socket, 15A Socket)
            *   Number of Points
            *   Appliance/Equipment Connected (e.g., LED Bulb, Ceiling Fan, Refrigerator, AC, Computer)
            *   Rated Power (Watts/kW) of each appliance.
            *   Number of Appliances
            *   Total Power for the Item (Rated Power x Number of Appliances)
        *   **Example Table Snippet:**

        | Location     | Point Type | Number of Points | Appliance       | Rated Power (W) | Number of Appliances | Total Power (W) |
        |--------------|------------|------------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------------|-----------------|
        | Living Room  | Light      | 4                | LED Bulb        | 10              | 4                    | 40              |
        | Living Room  | Fan        | 2                | Ceiling Fan     | 75              | 2                    | 150             |
        | Living Room  | 5A Socket  | 3                | -               | 100 (Est.)      | 3                    | 300             |
        | Kitchen      | Light      | 2                | LED Bulb        | 10              | 2                    | 20              |
        | Kitchen      | 15A Socket | 1                | Refrigerator    | 200             | 1                    | 200             |
        | Kitchen      | 15A Socket | 1                | Geyser          | 2000            | 1                    | 2000            |
        | Bedroom 1    | Light      | 2                | LED Bulb        | 10              | 2                    | 20              |
        | Bedroom 1    | Fan        | 1                | Ceiling Fan     | 75              | 1                    | 75              |
        | Bedroom 1    | 15A Socket | 1                | Air Conditioner | 1500            | 1                    | 1500            |

        *   **Total Connected Load = Sum of Total Power (W)**

### 3. Diversity Factor

The diversity factor (also known as the **coincidence factor** or **demand factor**) is a ratio used in electrical system design to account for the fact that not all connected loads in an installation operate simultaneously at their full rated capacity. It helps in sizing equipment more economically.

*   **Definition:**
    Diversity Factor = (Sum of individual maximum demands of various subdivisions of a system) / (Maximum demand of the whole system)

*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Maximum Demand (MD):** The highest average power consumed over a specified interval (e.g., 15 minutes, 30 minutes) within a given period.
    *   **Connected Load (CL):** The sum of the rated power of all electrical equipment connected to the supply.
    *   **Diversity Factor is always greater than 1.**

*   **Purpose of Diversity Factor:**
    *   **Economical Design:** Allows for the sizing of supply cables, switchgear, and transformers at a capacity lower than the total connected load, thus reducing capital costs.
    *   **Realistic Sizing:** Reflects the actual operating conditions and avoids over-sizing, which leads to inefficiency and higher costs.

*   **Factors Affecting Diversity Factor:**
    *   **Type of Building:** Residential buildings generally have lower diversity factors than commercial or industrial buildings.
    *   **Usage Pattern:** The lifestyle and habits of occupants influence when appliances are used.
    *   **Number of Circuits:** A larger number of circuits generally leads to a higher diversity factor.
    *   **Type of Appliances:** Appliances with intermittent usage (e.g., washing machines, electric irons) contribute to diversity.
    *   **Temperature/Climate:** Seasonal variations in the use of appliances like air conditioners or heaters can affect diversity.
    *   **Reliability of Supply:** In areas with unreliable power, the diversity factor might be lower as people might switch on multiple appliances when power is available.

*   **Application:**
    *   Maximum Demand (MD) = Total Connected Load x Diversity Factor (This is a simplified approach. For detailed calculations, specific diversity factors are applied to different categories of loads).
    *   A more accurate calculation involves applying diversity factors to different groups of loads:
        *   Total Maximum Demand = Sum of (Individual Load Category Connected Load x Diversity Factor for that category)

*   **Typical Diversity Factors (as per IS and NBC, these can vary):**
    *   **Residential Buildings:**
        *   First 7kVA (or 7000W): 1.0
        *   Next 8kVA (or 8000W): 0.5
        *   Balance kVA (or W): 0.25
        *   (Note: For lighting and fans, a diversity factor of 0.5 might be applied to the total connected lighting and fan load, and for power sockets, another factor might be used).
    *   **Commercial Buildings (Offices):**
        *   Lighting: 0.7 - 0.8
        *   Fans: 0.6 - 0.7
        *   Power Sockets: 0.4 - 0.5
        *   Specific equipment (e.g., computers): 0.8 - 0.9
    *   **High-Rise Buildings:** Diversity factors will be applied to different zones and services, considering the combined load of multiple dwelling units or commercial spaces.
    *   **General Rule:** Lower diversity factors are applied to loads that are more likely to operate simultaneously or have higher power ratings (e.g., motors, heating elements).

*   **Example Calculation (Residential - Simplified Approach):**
    *   Connected Load (Lighting & Fans): 3 kW
    *   Connected Load (Appliances - Refrigerator, TV, etc.): 2 kW
    *   Connected Load (Heavy Loads - AC, Geyser): 5 kW
    *   Total Connected Load = 3 kW + 2 kW + 5 kW = 10 kW

    *   **Applying Diversity Factors (Illustrative, as per IS methods):**
        *   Let's assume for lighting & fans, DF = 0.5; for appliances, DF = 0.7; for heavy loads, DF = 0.9 (these are illustrative, actual values are more detailed).
        *   Demand for Lighting & Fans = 3 kW x 0.5 = 1.5 kW
        *   Demand for Appliances = 2 kW x 0.7 = 1.4 kW
        *   Demand for Heavy Loads = 5 kW x 0.9 = 4.5 kW
        *   Total Maximum Demand = 1.5 kW + 1.4 kW + 4.5 kW = 7.4 kW

    *   **Using the IS 3043 based approach (for residential - simplified):**
        *   Connected Load = 10 kW
        *   First 7 kW: Demand = 7 kW (DF of 1.0)
        *   Remaining 3 kW: Demand = 3 kW * 0.5 = 1.5 kW
        *   Total Maximum Demand = 7 kW + 1.5 kW = 8.5 kW
        *   This shows how different methods yield different results and the importance of using the correct standard.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
What is the primary purpose of conducting a load survey for an electrical installation?
(a) To determine the aesthetic appeal of the wiring.
(b) To estimate the maximum electrical demand and size equipment appropriately.
(c) To assess the internet speed in the building.
(d) To check for radio frequency interference.

**Answer:** (b)

**Question 2:**
Define Diversity Factor and explain its importance in electrical system design. Provide a scenario where a higher diversity factor would be applicable.

**Answer:**
*   **Definition:** Diversity Factor is the ratio of the sum of individual maximum demands of various subdivisions of a system to the maximum demand of the whole system.
*   **Importance:** It allows for economical sizing of electrical components (cables, transformers, switchgear) by not assuming all loads will operate at their full capacity simultaneously. This prevents over-sizing and reduces capital costs.
*   **Scenario:** An office building with many individual workstations. Each workstation has a computer, monitor, and desk lamp. While each workstation has a connected load, it's unlikely that all computers will be running at full processing power, all monitors will be at maximum brightness, and all lamps will be on simultaneously for the entire duration of the workday. Therefore, a higher diversity factor can be applied to the total load of all workstations to arrive at a more realistic maximum demand for the office floor.

**Question 3:**
Calculate the total connected load for a residential flat with the following:
*   4 Lights @ 10W each
*   3 Fans @ 75W each
*   5 x 5A Socket outlets (estimated load per outlet = 100W)
*   1 x 15A Socket outlet for AC (rated 1500W)
*   1 x 15A Socket outlet for Geyser (rated 2000W)

**Answer:**
*   Lights: 4 x 10W = 40W
*   Fans: 3 x 75W = 225W
*   5A Sockets: 5 x 100W = 500W
*   AC Socket: 1 x 1500W = 1500W
*   Geyser Socket: 1 x 2000W = 2000W
*   **Total Connected Load = 40W + 225W + 500W + 1500W + 2000W = 4265W = 4.265 kW**

**Question 4:**
Which of the following building types typically requires the most robust electrical safety measures and emergency power provisions?
(a) Small residential bungalow
(b) Single-story retail shop
(c) High-rise apartment building
(d) Small office building

**Answer:** (c)

**Question 5 (Conceptual):**
If a building's total connected load is 50 kW, and the calculated maximum demand after applying diversity factors is 25 kW, what is the overall diversity factor for this installation?

**Answer:**
Overall Diversity Factor = Maximum Demand / Total Connected Load
Overall Diversity Factor = 25 kW / 50 kW = 0.5

**Wait!** This result (0.5) contradicts the definition of diversity factor (always > 1). This indicates a misunderstanding in the application. The calculation should be:

Maximum Demand = Sum of (Individual Load Category Connected Load x Diversity Factor for that category)

Let's rephrase the question to be more appropriate for diversity factor:

**Revised Question 5:**
If a building has two distinct load categories:
*   Category A (e.g., lighting and fans): Connected Load = 20 kW, Maximum Demand = 12 kW
*   Category B (e.g., motors and appliances): Connected Load = 30 kW, Maximum Demand = 20 kW

Calculate the Diversity Factor for each category and the overall Diversity Factor for the installation.

**Answer:**
*   **Diversity Factor for Category A:** Max Demand A / Connected Load A = 12 kW / 20 kW = 0.6. **Wait again!** This is the **Demand Factor**, not Diversity Factor. The question is about the *diversity factor for the installation*.

Let's use the standard definition and approach for diversity factor applied to Maximum Demand calculation.

**Corrected Approach for Question 5:**
Suppose the connected load for different parts of a building is as follows:
*   Residential Units (10 units): Total Connected Load = 100 kW
*   Common Area Lighting: Connected Load = 5 kW
*   Elevators: Connected Load = 15 kW
*   Water Pumps: Connected Load = 10 kW

And let's assume the following diversity factors are applied as per standards for residential blocks:
*   Residential Units: First 10 kW (DF=1.0), Next 20 kW (DF=0.7), Balance (DF=0.4)
*   Common Area Lighting: DF = 0.7
*   Elevators: DF = 0.8
*   Water Pumps: DF = 0.6

Calculate the Maximum Demand of the building.

**Answer:**
*   **Max Demand for Residential Units:**
    *   First 10 kW: 10 kW * 1.0 = 10 kW
    *   Next 20 kW: 20 kW * 0.7 = 14 kW
    *   Balance (100 kW - 10 kW - 20 kW = 70 kW): 70 kW * 0.4 = 28 kW
    *   Total Max Demand for Residential Units = 10 + 14 + 28 = 52 kW
*   **Max Demand for Common Area Lighting:** 5 kW * 0.7 = 3.5 kW
*   **Max Demand for Elevators:** 15 kW * 0.8 = 12 kW
*   **Max Demand for Water Pumps:** 10 kW * 0.6 = 6 kW

*   **Total Maximum Demand for the Building = 52 kW + 3.5 kW + 12 kW + 6 kW = 73.5 kW**

**(Note: This revised Question 5 is more representative of how diversity factors are applied in practice. The initial formulation of Question 5 was flawed in its premise.)**

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Safety First:** All electrical installations must prioritize safety, adhering to the National Building Code of India (NBC) and relevant Bureau of Indian Standards (IS) codes.
*   **Load Survey is Crucial:** An accurate load survey is the foundation for designing a safe, efficient, and cost-effective electrical system.
*   **Diversity Factor Saves Costs:** Understanding and applying diversity factors correctly leads to smaller, less expensive equipment without compromising service.
*   **Building Type Matters:** General requirements, load densities, and diversity factors vary significantly between residential, commercial, and high-rise buildings.
*   **High-Rise Challenges:** Fire resistance, vertical distribution, and emergency power are paramount in high-rise building electrical design.
*   **Actual vs. Connected Load:** Connected load represents the theoretical maximum, while maximum demand (derived using diversity factors or measurements) reflects actual usage.
*   **IS Codes are Your Guide:** Familiarize yourself with key IS codes for wiring, earthing, and safety (e.g., IS 3043, IS 732).
