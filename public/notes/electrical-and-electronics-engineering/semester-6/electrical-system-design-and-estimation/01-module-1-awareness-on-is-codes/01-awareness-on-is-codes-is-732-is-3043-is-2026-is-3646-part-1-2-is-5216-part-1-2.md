# Module 1: Awareness on IS Codes

## Topic: Awareness on IS Codes - IS 732, IS 3043, IS 2026 - IS 3646-part 1 & 2 - IS 5216 part 1 & 2

This module introduces fundamental Indian Standards (IS) codes that are crucial for the design, installation, and safety of electrical systems. Understanding these codes ensures efficient energy usage, compliance with regulations, and protection of life and property.

---

### **1. IS 732: Code of Practice for Electrical Wiring Installations**

*   **Purpose:** This IS code provides guidelines for the safe and efficient electrical wiring installations in buildings and other structures. It covers various aspects of wiring, from cable selection and routing to protection and testing.
*   **Key Concepts & Definitions:**
    *   **Wiring System:** The arrangement of electrical conductors and associated equipment for distributing electrical energy.
    *   **Circuit:** A combination of electrical equipment forming a path for current.
    *   **Conductor:** A material that readily permits the flow of electric current.
    *   **Insulation:** A non-conductive material used to prevent the flow of electric current.
    *   **Protection:** Measures taken to safeguard electrical installations and persons against hazards.
    *   **Earthing/Grounding:** A protective measure for electrical installations, connecting the non-current-carrying metal parts to the earth.
*   **Scope:**
    *   General requirements for wiring installations.
    *   Selection of conductors (e.g., copper, aluminum), their sizes, and types of insulation.
    *   Wiring methods (e.g., surface wiring, concealed wiring, conduit wiring).
    *   Principles of protection against electric shock, overcurrent, and overvoltage.
    *   Requirements for switches, sockets, junction boxes, and other accessories.
    *   Testing and inspection procedures for completed installations.
*   **Relevance to Course Outcomes:**
    *   **CO1 (K2):** Provides foundational knowledge on safe and efficient energy usage in wiring installations.
    *   **CO2 (K3):** Directly applicable to designing electrical wiring for residential and commercial consumers.
    *   **CO3 (K3):** Provides basic principles for industrial and high-rise building installations, which build upon residential/commercial wiring.
*   **Important Points to Remember:**
    *   Adherence to IS 732 is mandatory for all electrical wiring installations in India.
    *   Proper selection of conductor size based on current carrying capacity and voltage drop is crucial.
    *   Earthing is a vital safety requirement and must be implemented as per the code.
*   **Example:** When designing wiring for a residential building, IS 732 dictates the minimum conductor size for lighting circuits, power circuits, and the type of insulation required for concealed wiring to prevent damage and fire hazards.

### **2. IS 3043: Code of Practice for Earthing**

*   **Purpose:** This IS code provides comprehensive guidelines for the design, installation, and maintenance of earthing systems to ensure safety and proper functioning of electrical equipment.
*   **Key Concepts & Definitions:**
    *   **Earthing Electrode:** A conductor or system of conductors in intimate contact with the earth.
    *   **Earth Resistance:** The resistance of the path between the earth electrode and the general mass of the earth.
    *   **Earth Continuity:** The low resistance path provided by the earthing system to ensure that all metallic parts that could become live are connected to the earth.
    *   **Bonding:** The connection of conductive parts to create a conductive path.
    *   **Types of Earthing:**
        *   **Protective Earthing:** Connecting non-current-carrying metal parts to the earth.
        *   **System Earthing:** Connecting one of the current-carrying conductors of the system to earth (e.g., Neutral earthing).
        *   **Functional Earthing:** Earthing for specific operational purposes (e.g., signal grounding).
*   **Scope:**
    *   Requirements for earthing electrodes (materials, dimensions, types).
    *   Methods of earthing (e.g., rod electrode, plate electrode, strip electrode, pipe electrode).
    *   Design of earthing grids and networks.
    *   Interconnection of different earthing systems.
    *   Testing procedures for earth resistance and continuity.
    *   Maintenance and inspection of earthing systems.
*   **Relevance to Course Outcomes:**
    *   **CO1 (K2):** Essential for understanding safe energy usage by ensuring proper grounding.
    *   **CO5 (K3):** Directly addresses the design of various earthing methods and protection. This is a core outcome for this code.
*   **Important Points to Remember:**
    *   Low earth resistance is critical for effective earthing.
    *   All metallic enclosures of electrical equipment must be earthed.
    *   Regular testing and maintenance of the earthing system are vital.
*   **Example:** For a factory with heavy machinery, IS 3043 would guide the selection of multiple earth pits, the type of earth electrodes (e.g., copper-bonded rods), the size of earthing conductors, and the required low earth resistance value (e.g., less than 1 ohm for substations).

### **3. IS 2026: Power Transformers (Parts 1 to 4)**

*   **Purpose:** This IS code specifies the requirements, design, manufacturing, testing, and performance of power transformers used in electrical power systems.
*   **Key Concepts & Definitions:**
    *   **Power Transformer:** A static device that transforms electrical energy from one voltage level to another through electromagnetic induction.
    *   **Rating:** The capacity of a transformer to deliver power, usually expressed in kVA or MVA.
    *   **Vector Group:** A designation that indicates the phase displacement between the primary and secondary voltages and the winding connections.
    *   **Impedance Voltage:** The voltage required to circulate full-load current in the windings when the other winding is short-circuited.
    *   **Cooling Methods:** Methods used to dissipate heat generated during operation (e.g., ONAN, ONAF, OFAF).
*   **Scope:**
    *   **Part 1: General:** General requirements, ratings, performance, and testing.
    *   **Part 2: Cooling:** Classification and requirements for different cooling methods.
    *   **Part 3: Terminal Characteristics and Conditions:** Specifies terminal markings, connection diagrams, and voltage/frequency variations.
    *   **Part 4: Insulation Levels and Dielectric Tests:** Defines insulation requirements and associated tests for different voltage levels.
*   **Relevance to Course Outcomes:**
    *   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding transformer design and performance is crucial for efficient energy distribution.
    *   **CO4 (K4):** Directly relevant to analyzing electrical system conditioning equipment, as transformers are a key component.
*   **Important Points to Remember:**
    *   The choice of transformer rating and vector group depends on the system load and connection requirements.
    *   Proper cooling is essential for the longevity and efficiency of transformers.
    *   Dielectric strength of insulation is critical for preventing electrical breakdowns.
*   **Example:** When selecting a transformer for a substation, IS 2026 would guide the engineer in determining the transformer's MVA rating, the appropriate vector group (e.g., Dyn11 for step-down applications), the cooling method (e.g., ONAN/ONAF for larger ratings), and the required insulation levels for the operating voltage.

### **4. IS 3646: Code of Practice for Interior Illumination (Part 1 & 2)**

*   **Purpose:** This IS code provides guidelines for the design of effective and energy-efficient lighting systems for various interior environments.
*   **Key Concepts & Definitions:**
    *   **Illuminance:** The amount of light falling on a surface, measured in lux (lx).
    *   **Luminance:** The brightness of a surface, measured in candelas per square meter (cd/m²).
    *   **Light Source:** The device that produces light (e.g., LED, fluorescent lamp, incandescent lamp).
    *   **Luminaire:** A complete unit comprising a light source and its associated components (e.g., reflector, diffuser).
    *   **Uniformity Ratio:** The ratio of minimum illuminance to average illuminance in a space, indicating the evenness of lighting.
    *   **Glare:** Visual discomfort caused by excessive brightness or contrast.
*   **Scope:**
    *   **Part 1: General:** Principles of lighting design, recommended illuminance levels for different tasks and spaces, uniformity requirements, and glare control.
    *   **Part 2: Specific Areas:** Detailed recommendations for lighting in specific environments like offices, educational institutions, hospitals, industrial areas, and residential spaces.
*   **Relevance to Course Outcomes:**
    *   **CO1 (K2):** Essential for understanding efficient energy usage through optimized lighting design.
    *   **CO2 (K3):** Directly applicable to designing lighting for residential and commercial consumers.
*   **Important Points to Remember:**
    *   Adequate illuminance is crucial for visual comfort and productivity.
    *   Uniformity of lighting is as important as the average illuminance level.
    *   Minimizing glare improves visual performance and reduces eye strain.
    *   Consideration of energy-efficient light sources (e.g., LEDs) is encouraged.
*   **Example:** For an office space, IS 3646 (Part 2) would specify a recommended illuminance level of 300-500 lux on the work surface, a uniformity ratio of at least 0.6, and guidelines for the placement of luminaires to avoid glare on computer screens.

### **5. IS 5216: Recommendations for the Safety Procedures for Working in Energised Electrical Installations (Part 1 & 2)**

*   **Purpose:** This IS code provides guidelines and safety procedures for personnel working on or near energized electrical installations to prevent accidents and ensure their safety.
*   **Key Concepts & Definitions:**
    *   **Energised Installation:** An electrical installation that is connected to a source of electrical energy and intended to supply energy.
    *   **Live Working:** Any work performed on or near electrical conductors or circuit parts that are or are likely to be energized.
    *   **Dead Working:** Work performed on electrical equipment that has been de-energized and made safe.
    *   **Safety Clearance:** The minimum safe distance to be maintained from energized conductors.
    *   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Equipment worn by workers to protect them from electrical hazards (e.g., insulated gloves, boots, helmets).
*   **Scope:**
    *   **Part 1: General:** Principles of safety, risk assessment, methods of working (live vs. dead), requirements for authorization and training of personnel.
    *   **Part 2: Specific Aspects:** Details on safe working practices, use of insulated tools and equipment, safety procedures for specific tasks like testing, fault finding, and emergency response.
*   **Relevance to Course Outcomes:**
    *   **CO1 (K2):** Crucial for understanding safe practices in electrical system design and maintenance, contributing to efficient and safe energy usage.
    *   **CO3 (K3):** Essential for safety procedures in industrial and high-rise building electrical installations where maintenance on energized systems might be necessary.
*   **Important Points to Remember:**
    *   The primary principle is to de-energize and isolate equipment before working on it.
    *   Live working should only be performed by trained and authorized personnel using appropriate safety equipment and procedures.
    *   Thorough risk assessment must be conducted before any work commences.
*   **Example:** Before performing maintenance on a live electrical panel, IS 5216 would mandate that the worker wears insulating gloves, safety glasses, and uses insulated tools. It would also outline the procedure for establishing a safe working zone and ensuring no unintended contact with live parts.

### **Practice Questions:**

1.  According to IS 732, what are the primary safety requirements for concealed wiring installations?
2.  Explain the difference between protective earthing and system earthing as defined in IS 3043.
3.  What are the key parameters specified in IS 2026 for a power transformer that an electrical engineer must consider during design?
4.  As per IS 3646, why is uniformity of illuminance important in an office environment, and how is it measured?
5.  Under what circumstances would working on an energized electrical installation be permissible according to IS 5216, and what are the essential precautions?

---

### **Answers:**

1.  According to IS 732, primary safety requirements for concealed wiring include proper insulation of conductors, ensuring no damage to insulation during installation, using non-combustible conduits where necessary, and providing adequate ventilation to prevent heat buildup.
2.  **Protective Earthing (as per IS 3043):** This involves connecting the non-current-carrying metal parts of electrical equipment (like the metal casing of a motor or a switchboard) to the earth. Its purpose is to prevent electric shock if a fault occurs and the casing becomes live.
    **System Earthing (as per IS 3043):** This involves earthing one of the current-carrying conductors of the power system itself, typically the neutral point of a transformer. This helps in controlling overvoltages and facilitating the operation of protective devices during earth faults.
3.  Key parameters specified in IS 2026 for a power transformer include:
    *   **Rating:** MVA capacity.
    *   **Voltage Ratio:** Primary and secondary voltage levels.
    *   **Vector Group:** Connection of windings and phase displacement.
    *   **Impedance (%):** Affects voltage regulation and fault current.
    *   **Cooling Method:** ONAN, ONAF, etc.
    *   **Insulation Level:** Impulse withstand voltage.
    *   **Tap Changer Type and Range:** For voltage regulation.
4.  According to IS 3646, uniformity of illuminance is important in an office environment to ensure that there are no excessively dark or bright areas, which can lead to eye strain, reduce visual comfort, and decrease productivity. It is measured by the **Uniformity Ratio**, which is the ratio of the minimum illuminance to the average illuminance in a given area. A higher ratio indicates more uniform lighting.
5.  According to IS 5216, working on an energized electrical installation is permissible only when it is absolutely impossible to de-energize the equipment, and such work must be performed by **authorized and trained personnel** using **specialized tools and techniques**. Essential precautions include:
    *   Performing a thorough risk assessment.
    *   Using appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) like insulated gloves, safety shoes, and fire-retardant clothing.
    *   Using insulated tools and testing equipment.
    *   Maintaining safe working clearances from live parts.
    *   Having trained supervisors and support personnel present.
    *   Ensuring the work area is properly isolated and marked.
