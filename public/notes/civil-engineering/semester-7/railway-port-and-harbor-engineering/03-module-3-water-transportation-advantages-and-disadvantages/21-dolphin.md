### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

**Dolphin (in Port and Harbor Engineering):**

A dolphin is a **structure** consisting of a group of piles (usually driven into the seabed) or a single large pile, used for **mooring ships**, **guiding vessels**, or acting as **navigational aids**. They are typically located away from the main quay or berth and are not intended for cargo transfer operations.

**Distinction from Quay/Berth:**

While quays and berths are designed for direct cargo handling and passenger embarkation/disembarkation, dolphins are primarily for **mooring, fendering, and guiding**. They can be integrated with a quay structure but often stand alone.

**Piles:**

Vertical structural elements driven into the ground or seabed to provide support. In dolphins, piles are typically made of steel, concrete, or timber.

**Mooring:**

The act of securing a vessel to a structure.

**Fendering:**

Protective devices installed on structures or vessels to absorb impact energy during berthing operations and prevent damage.

**Navigational Aid:**

Any structure or device that helps mariners navigate safely, such as buoys or lights.

### 3. Functions and Purposes of Dolphins

Dolphins serve several critical functions in port operations:

*   **Mooring Dolphins:**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide strong points for securing vessels using mooring lines (ropes or wires).
    *   **How it works:** Ships are moored to bollards or cleats attached to the dolphin. This is particularly useful for:
        *   **Holding vessels in position:** Especially in areas with strong currents or winds.
        *   **Adding mooring capacity:** Supplementing the mooring points on the main quay.
        *   **Transfer operations:** Holding pipelines or hoses for liquid or gas transfer.
        *   **Temporary mooring:** For vessels not actively loading/unloading.

*   **Lead-in/Lead-out Dolphins:**
    *   **Purpose:** To guide vessels safely into or out of a berth, especially in confined or busy waterways.
    *   **How it works:** Placed at the approach to a berth to help the pilot steer the vessel accurately, preventing collisions with other structures or vessels.

*   **Fender Dolphins:**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide protection to the main quay or berth structure from the impact of berthing vessels.
    *   **How it works:** Fitted with large fenders (e.g., pneumatic fenders, rubber fenders) that absorb the berthing energy. The vessel makes contact with the fender dolphin instead of the quay face.

*   **Navigational Dolphins:**
    *   **Purpose:** To serve as navigational markers, indicating channels, hazards, or the limits of safe navigation.
    *   **How it works:** Often equipped with lights, radar reflectors, or other signaling devices. They can mark the edge of a dredged channel or a submerged obstruction.

*   **Loading/Unloading Dolphins (Specialized):**
    *   **Purpose:** While not for general cargo, some dolphins are designed to support specific transfer equipment.
    *   **Example:** Pipelines for oil or LNG transfer may be supported by dolphins extending from the seabed to the surface, allowing ships to connect directly.

### 4. Types of Dolphins and Their Construction

Dolphins can be classified based on their structural form and the materials used.

**Based on Structural Form:**

*   **Single Pile Dolphin:**
    *   **Description:** Consists of a single large-diameter pile, often heavily reinforced, driven into the seabed.
    *   **Construction:** A large diameter steel pipe pile or pre-stressed concrete pile is driven or vibrated into the seabed. The top is often fitted with a cap for mooring fixtures or fendering.

*   **Multiple Pile Dolphin:**
    *   **Description:** Composed of a cluster of several piles arranged in a circular or polygonal pattern.
    *   **Construction:** Piles (steel, concrete, or timber) are driven in a specific configuration. They are often connected at the top by bracing or a concrete cap to distribute loads.
    *   **Advantages:** Offers greater stability and load-carrying capacity than a single pile dolphin.

*   **Cellular Dolphin (Less Common for Mooring):**
    *   **Description:** A large cylindrical structure made of interlocking sheet piles driven into the seabed and filled with sand or gravel.
    *   **Construction:** Sheet piles are driven to form a watertight cell, then filled with granular material. Often used as quay walls or breakwaters, but smaller versions can serve as dolphins.

*   **Gravity Dolphins:**
    *   **Description:** Structures that rely on their own weight to resist forces.
    *   **Construction:** Typically a concrete structure built on the seabed, often on a prepared foundation. Less common for mooring due to significant seabed preparation required.

**Materials Used:**

*   **Steel Piles:**
    *   **Types:** Pipe piles, H-piles.
    *   **Advantages:** High strength, ease of driving, resistance to corrosion (with protective coatings).
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can be susceptible to corrosion without proper coatings, higher initial cost.

*   **Concrete Piles:**
    *   **Types:** Precast concrete piles, cast-in-situ piles.
    *   **Advantages:** High compressive strength, good durability, less susceptible to corrosion than uncoated steel.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can be heavier and more difficult to drive, potential for cracking under impact loads if not adequately reinforced.

*   **Timber Piles:**
    *   **Types:** Treated hardwood.
    *   **Advantages:** Lower cost, readily available in some regions.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Susceptible to marine borers and decay if not properly treated, lower strength and durability compared to steel or concrete.

**Typical Construction Process:**

1.  **Site Investigation:** Geotechnical surveys to determine seabed conditions.
2.  **Pile Driving/Installation:** Piles are driven or vibrated into the seabed to the required depth and capacity.
3.  **Capping/Connecting:** Piles are capped with a concrete structure or connected with bracing to form a stable unit.
4.  **Mooring Fixtures/Fenders:** Bollards, cleats, or fenders are installed on the cap.
5.  **Navigational Aids:** Lights or other markers are attached if required.

### 5. Advantages of Using Dolphins

Dolphins offer several benefits in port and harbor operations:

*   **Enhanced Mooring Capabilities:**
    *   Provide additional, robust mooring points, especially for larger vessels or in challenging environmental conditions (strong currents, high winds).
    *   Allow for more flexible mooring arrangements, distributing stresses on the vessel.

*   **Protection of Quay Structures:**
    *   Fender dolphins effectively absorb berthing impacts, significantly reducing the risk of damage to the valuable quay structure, which is crucial for cargo operations.
    *   This allows for more aggressive berthing by pilots who are less concerned about hitting the quay face.

*   **Improved Vessel Maneuvering:**
    *   Lead-in and lead-out dolphins guide ships into and out of berths, improving safety and efficiency, especially in narrow channels or alongside crowded quays.

*   **Facilitation of Specialized Operations:**
    *   Essential for offshore terminals, single-point moorings (SPMs), and pipeline transfers, where direct quay access is not feasible or necessary.
    *   Allows for safe connection of hoses or pipelines for bulk liquid and gas transfer.

*   **Cost-Effectiveness in Certain Scenarios:**
    *   For specific purposes like mooring a few vessels or providing fender protection, dolphins can be more cost-effective than constructing a full quay wall.
    *   Can be strategically placed to augment existing facilities.

*   **Navigational Safety:**
    *   As navigational aids, they clearly mark channels, hazards, and operational areas, contributing to overall port safety.

### 6. Disadvantages and Limitations of Dolphins

Despite their advantages, dolphins also have drawbacks:

*   **Limited Cargo Handling Capability:**
    *   Dolphins, by definition, are not designed for direct cargo transfer. Any cargo handling would require supplementary structures or a connection to a quay.

*   **Increased Exposure to Environmental Forces:**
    *   Isolated structures are more exposed to wave action, current forces, and ice loads (in relevant climates), requiring robust design.
    *   Mooring forces from vessels can also be significant and directly applied to the dolphin.

*   **Maintenance Requirements:**
    *   Piles and connections are susceptible to corrosion, abrasion, and impact damage, requiring regular inspection and maintenance.
    *   Fender systems require periodic inspection and replacement.

*   **Potential for Navigation Hazards (if not properly marked):**
    *   If not clearly marked with lights or other aids, dolphins can become submerged hazards, especially during high tides or adverse weather.

*   **Space Utilization:**
    *   While they can improve operational efficiency, the placement of dolphins can also occupy valuable maneuvering space within the harbor basin.

*   **Installation Challenges:**
    *   Driving piles or constructing structures in a marine environment can be complex, expensive, and dependent on seabed conditions.

*   **Collateral Damage Risk:**
    *   While fender dolphins protect the quay, if a vessel makes contact directly with a mooring dolphin or a poorly fendered dolphin, damage to the vessel or the dolphin can still occur.

### 7. Key Considerations for Design and Placement

Effective design and placement of dolphins require careful consideration of several factors:

*   **Seabed Conditions:**
    *   **Geotechnical Properties:** Soil type, bearing capacity, and presence of obstructions are critical for determining pile type, size, and driving requirements.
    *   **Water Depth:** Affects the required pile length and structural stability.

*   **Environmental Loads:**
    *   **Wave and Current Forces:** The magnitude and direction of expected wave and current loads on the dolphin structure and moored vessels.
    *   **Wind Loads:** Particularly important for moored vessels.
    *   **Ice Loads:** In cold climates, ice can exert significant forces.
    *   **Mooring Loads:** The maximum anticipated forces from mooring lines based on vessel size, environmental conditions, and mooring arrangement.

*   **Vessel Characteristics:**
    *   **Size and Type of Vessels:** Determines the required mooring capacity and fender specification.
    *   **Berthing Speeds:** Influences the design of fender systems for fender dolphins.

*   **Operational Requirements:**
    *   **Purpose of the Dolphin:** Mooring, fendering, guiding, or navigation.
    *   **Frequency of Use:** High-frequency use might necessitate more robust materials and easier maintenance.
    *   **Proximity to Other Structures:** To avoid interference and ensure safe maneuvering.

*   **Material Selection:**
    *   Durability, strength, cost, availability, and maintenance considerations guide the choice of steel, concrete, or timber.
    *   Corrosion protection (coatings, cathodic protection) is essential for steel piles.

*   **Fender System Design (for Fender Dolphins):**
    *   **Type of Fender:** Pneumatic, rubber, foam-filled, etc.
    *   **Energy Absorption Capacity:** Must be sufficient to safely absorb the berthing energy.
    *   **Reaction Force:** The force exerted by the fender on the vessel and the dolphin.

*   **Mooring Fixture Design (for Mooring Dolphins):**
    *   **Bollard Capacity:** Must be designed to withstand the maximum anticipated mooring loads.
    *   **Placement:** Strategic placement to optimize mooring line angles and reduce chafe.

*   **Navigational Marking:**
    *   Appropriate lighting, racons, or other aids must be installed and maintained for navigational dolphins.

*   **Regulatory Requirements and Standards:**
    *   Compliance with relevant port regulations, national and international maritime standards.

### 9. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
A dolphin is a structure, often consisting of a group of piles, used for mooring ships, guiding vessels, or acting as navigational aids. It is typically located away from the main quay and is not designed for cargo transfer.
Three primary functions:
1.  Mooring ships.
2.  Protecting quays (as fender dolphins).
3.  Guiding vessels (lead-in/lead-out dolphins).
4.  Acting as navigational aids.

**Answer 2:**
The main difference is their primary purpose. A **quay** is designed for direct cargo handling and passenger access, featuring a deck at or near the water level for cargo transfer equipment. A **dolphin**, on the other hand, is primarily for **mooring, fendering, or guiding**, and does not typically facilitate direct cargo operations.

**Answer 3:**
A multiple-pile dolphin consists of a cluster of several piles (e.g., steel or concrete) arranged in a pattern (circular or polygonal). These piles are driven into the seabed and often connected at the top by bracing or a concrete cap.
**Advantages:**
*   Provides greater stability and load-carrying capacity compared to a single-pile dolphin.
*   Distributes loads more effectively.
*   Can be more robust against lateral forces.

**Answer 4:**
A fender dolphin is placed in front of the quay structure. It is fitted with fenders (e.g., large rubber or pneumatic devices). When a vessel berths, it makes contact with the fenders on the dolphin first, absorbing the berthing energy. This prevents the vessel from directly impacting the quay face, thereby protecting the quay structure from damage caused by the impact forces.

**Answer 5:**
Two disadvantages:
1.  **Limited Cargo Handling Capability:** Dolphins are not designed for direct cargo transfer, limiting their utility for general port operations.
2.  **Maintenance Requirements:** Exposed structures are prone to corrosion and impact damage, necessitating regular inspection and maintenance.
3.  **Space Utilization:** Their placement can sometimes occupy valuable maneuvering space within the harbor.
4.  **Potential Navigation Hazard:** If not adequately marked, they can pose a risk to navigation.

**Answer 6:**
For mooring large tankers at an oil terminal, a **robust mooring dolphin** would be most suitable. This could be either:
*   **A single, large-diameter, heavily reinforced steel pipe pile dolphin** capable of withstanding high mooring loads.
*   **A multiple-pile dolphin** using steel or pre-stressed concrete piles, arranged to provide excellent stability and mooring capacity.

The dolphin would need to be designed with significant bollard capacity to handle the large mooring forces from tankers in various weather conditions. Additionally, its placement would need to allow for safe connection of hoses for oil transfer, possibly in conjunction with a separate loading platform or an articulated pipeline support structure.

### 10. Important Points to Remember

*   **Dolphins are specialized structures for mooring, fendering, and guidance, not for cargo transfer.**
*   **They significantly enhance safety by protecting quay structures and improving vessel maneuverability.**
*   **The choice between single-pile and multiple-pile dolphins depends on the required load capacity and stability.**
*   **Material selection (steel, concrete, timber) and proper protection are crucial due to the harsh marine environment.**
*   **Thorough site investigation and analysis of environmental and operational loads are essential for dolphin design and placement.**
*   **Regular inspection and maintenance are vital for the long-term performance and safety of dolphins.**
