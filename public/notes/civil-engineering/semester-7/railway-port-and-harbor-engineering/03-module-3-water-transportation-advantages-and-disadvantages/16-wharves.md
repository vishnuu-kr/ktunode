# RAILWAY, PORT AND HARBOR ENGINEERING

## Module 3: Water Transportation: Advantages and Disadvantages

### Topic: Wharves

---

### 1. Introduction to Wharves

*   **Definition:** A wharf is a structure built parallel to the bank of a waterway, river, or harbor, used for the berthing of ships and boats. It serves as a docking and loading/unloading facility.
*   **Purpose:** To provide a stable and accessible platform for cargo transfer between land and water, and for passengers embarking and disembarking.
*   **Contrast with Piers:** While often used interchangeably, a pier typically extends perpendicular to the shore into the water, whereas a wharf is parallel to the shore.

---

### 2. Classification of Wharves

Wharves can be classified based on their construction, function, and location.

#### 2.1 By Construction Type

*   **Open Wharves (Pile Wharves):**
    *   **Description:** Constructed using vertical piles (timber, concrete, or steel) driven into the seabed, supporting a deck structure. The area below the deck is open to water.
    *   **Advantages:** Lower construction costs, minimal obstruction to water flow, suitable for areas with soft seabed.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Less stable in rough conditions, vulnerable to marine borers (for timber piles), requires continuous maintenance.
    *   **Examples:** Many older wharves, particularly those for smaller vessels or in sheltered waters.

*   **Closed Wharves (Solid/Gravity Wharves):**
    *   **Description:** Constructed with solid, impermeable structures that extend from the seabed to the deck level. Common types include:
        *   **Gravity Walls:** Built using mass concrete or masonry, relying on their own weight to resist hydrostatic and soil pressures.
        *   **Caisson Wharves:** Constructed by sinking pre-fabricated boxes (caissons) filled with ballast.
        *   **Sheet Pile Wharves:** Constructed using interlocking steel sheets driven into the seabed, forming a continuous wall, often supported by anchors.
    *   **Advantages:** High stability, resistant to wave action, provide protection from currents, can support heavier loads.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher construction costs, can obstruct water flow and sediment transport, require significant engineering expertise for construction.
    *   **Examples:** Modern container terminals, bulk cargo facilities, and busy passenger terminals often utilize gravity or caisson wharves for their robustness.

#### 2.2 By Function

*   **Cargo Wharves:** Designed for the loading and unloading of goods.
    *   **Container Wharves:** Equipped with specialized cranes (e.g., gantry cranes) for handling containers.
    *   **Bulk Cargo Wharves:** Designed for handling loose materials like coal, grain, ore, and oil, often with specialized loading and unloading equipment (conveyors, grabbers, pipelines).
    *   **General Cargo Wharves:** Suitable for a variety of packaged or non-bulk goods.

*   **Passenger Wharves:** Designed for the embarkation and disembarkation of passengers, often associated with ferry terminals or cruise ship ports.

*   **Fishing Wharves/Landing Stages:** Used by fishing vessels for unloading their catch and for repairs.

*   **Ro-Ro (Roll-on/Roll-off) Wharves:** Feature ramps to allow vehicles to drive directly on and off ships.

#### 2.3 By Location

*   **Frontage Wharves:** Built along the edge of the land.
*   **Jetty Wharves:** Structures projecting out from the shore, often serving as a connection to deeper water for mooring. (While a pier is more commonly used, jetties can function similarly in some contexts).

---

### 3. Advantages of Wharves in Water Transportation

*   **Efficient Cargo Handling:** Provide a stable and accessible platform for the efficient loading and unloading of goods and passengers.
*   **Increased Throughput:** Allow multiple vessels to berth simultaneously, increasing the volume of goods and passengers handled.
*   **Passenger Convenience:** Facilitate easy and safe access for passengers to embark and disembark.
*   **Specialized Equipment Integration:** Can be designed to accommodate specialized cargo handling equipment (cranes, conveyors, etc.).
*   **Shelter and Protection:** Can offer some protection to vessels from minor wave action and currents.
*   **Deeper Draft Access:** Allow vessels with deeper drafts to berth closer to shore, facilitating the use of larger ships.
*   **Infrastructure Integration:** Connect seamlessly with land-based transportation networks (roads, railways) for efficient intermodal transport.

---

### 4. Disadvantages of Wharves in Water Transportation

*   **High Construction and Maintenance Costs:** Building and maintaining wharves, especially solid structures, is expensive and requires ongoing upkeep.
*   **Environmental Impact:**
    *   **Obstruction of Water Flow:** Solid wharves can alter natural current patterns, affecting sediment transport and potentially leading to siltation or erosion.
    *   **Habitat Disruption:** Construction can disrupt marine ecosystems and habitats.
    *   **Pollution:** Potential for pollution from cargo handling, spills, and wastewater.
*   **Limited Flexibility:** Once built, wharves are generally fixed structures and cannot be easily relocated or adapted to changing port needs.
*   **Depth Limitations:** The depth of water alongside a wharf dictates the size of vessels that can berth, requiring dredging for larger ships.
*   **Vulnerability:** Open wharves can be susceptible to damage from severe weather, ice, or collisions with vessels.
*   **Space Constraints:** Require significant waterfront space, which can be a limiting factor in urban or densely populated areas.

---

### 5. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Berthing:** The act of mooring a vessel alongside a wharf or pier.
*   **Dredging:** The excavation of material from a body of water, typically to increase depth or remove unwanted sediment.
*   **Seabed:** The ground or floor of the sea or other body of water.
*   **Piles:** Long, slender structural elements driven into the ground to provide support.
*   **Caissons:** Watertight structures, often made of concrete or steel, used in underwater construction.
*   **Gravity Wall:** A retaining wall that relies on its own weight to resist lateral forces.
*   **Intermodal Transport:** The transportation of freight in an intermodal container or vehicle, using multiple modes of transportation (e.g., ship, rail, truck) without any handling of the freight itself when changing modes.
*   **Throughput:** The rate at which goods or passengers are processed through a facility.
*   **Marine Borers:** Organisms (like barnacles and shipworms) that bore into submerged wood, causing damage.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Describe the main differences between an open wharf and a closed wharf in terms of construction and typical applications.

**Question 2:**
List three advantages of using wharves for cargo handling in water transportation.

**Question 3:**
What are some of the potential environmental concerns associated with the construction and operation of wharves?

**Question 4:**
A port authority is planning to build a new facility for handling large container ships. What type of wharf construction would likely be most suitable and why?

---

### 7. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
*   **Open Wharves (Pile Wharves):** Constructed using piles driven into the seabed, supporting a deck. The area below the deck is open to water. They are generally less expensive and have less obstruction to water flow but are less stable and vulnerable to marine borers.
*   **Closed Wharves (Solid/Gravity Wharves):** Constructed with solid structures (gravity walls, caissons, sheet piles) that are impermeable. They are more stable, resistant to wave action, and can support heavier loads but are more expensive and can obstruct water flow.

**Answer 2:**
Three advantages of using wharves include:
1.  **Efficient Cargo Handling:** Providing a stable platform for loading and unloading.
2.  **Increased Throughput:** Allowing multiple vessels to berth, increasing operational capacity.
3.  **Passenger Convenience:** Facilitating safe and easy access for passengers.
    *(Other valid answers: Specialized equipment integration, deeper draft access, infrastructure integration).*

**Answer 3:**
Potential environmental concerns include:
1.  **Alteration of Water Flow:** Solid wharves can change current patterns and affect sediment transport.
2.  **Habitat Disruption:** Construction can damage or destroy marine ecosystems.
3.  **Pollution:** Risk of pollution from cargo spills or operational activities.
    *(Other valid answers: Obstruction of navigation for smaller vessels, visual impact).*

**Answer 4:**
For large container ships, a **closed wharf** (such as a gravity wall or caisson wharf) would likely be most suitable. This is because:
*   **Stability:** Large container ships are heavy and require a very stable structure to berth safely.
*   **Load Bearing Capacity:** These wharves can support the significant weight of container cranes and the heavy loads of containers.
*   **Resistance to Wave Action:** Container terminals are often located in open harbors where wave action can be significant, and closed wharves provide better resistance.
*   **Durability:** They are generally more durable and require less frequent maintenance compared to open wharves, especially in harsh marine environments.

---

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Wharves are essential infrastructure for waterborne trade and passenger transport.**
*   The **choice of wharf construction** depends on factors like seabed conditions, expected loads, vessel types, environmental considerations, and budget.
*   **Maintenance is crucial** for ensuring the safety and functionality of wharves.
*   **Environmental impact assessment and mitigation strategies** are vital during the planning and construction phases of wharves.
*   **Integration with land transportation networks** is key to maximizing the efficiency of port operations.
