# FORMWORK ENGINEERING: Module 2 - Basic Principles of Formwork Design

## Topic: Basic Principles of Formwork Design

---

### **Learning Outcomes:**

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Understand the fundamental purpose and requirements of formwork.
*   Identify and explain the main types of formwork systems.
*   Recognize the importance of structural stability and load-bearing capacity of formwork.
*   Explain the role of material selection in formwork design.
*   Understand the principles of formwork erection and dismantling.
*   Discuss the factors influencing the cost-effectiveness of formwork.

### **1. Fundamental Purpose and Requirements of Formwork**

#### **1.1 Purpose of Formwork:**

*   **Shape and Support:** Formwork is a temporary mold or structure that provides the desired shape and support for freshly poured concrete until it gains sufficient strength to support itself and the applied loads.
*   **Containment:** It acts as a container to hold the liquid concrete in its intended position.
*   **Surface Finish:** It influences the surface texture and finish of the hardened concrete.

#### **1.2 Key Requirements of Formwork:**

*   **Strength:** Must be strong enough to withstand all temporary and permanent loads imposed during construction without excessive deflection or failure.
*   **Rigidity:** Must be sufficiently rigid to prevent excessive deformation (deflection) under load, which could affect the final dimensions and shape of the concrete member.
*   **Stability:** Must be stable against overturning, buckling, and sliding.
*   **Accuracy:** Must be constructed to precise dimensions and alignment to achieve the required shape, size, and location of the concrete member.
*   **Water-Tightness:** Should be reasonably water-tight to prevent leakage of cement paste, which can lead to honeycombing and reduced strength.
*   **Smoothness:** The inner surfaces should be smooth to facilitate easy removal and provide a good surface finish to the concrete.
*   **Ease of Erection and Dismantling:** Should be designed for quick, efficient, and safe assembly and disassembly.
*   **Reusability:** Where possible, formwork should be designed for multiple uses to improve cost-effectiveness.
*   **Economy:** Should be as economical as possible in terms of material, labor, and time.

#### **1.3 Loads on Formwork:**

*   **Dead Load:**
    *   Self-weight of the formwork system.
    *   Self-weight of fresh concrete.
    *   Weight of reinforcing steel.
*   **Live Load:**
    *   Weight of workmen.
    *   Weight of equipment and materials on the formwork.
    *   Impact loads (e.g., from concrete vibrators).
*   **Lateral Pressure of Fresh Concrete:** This is a critical and often dynamic load.
    *   It depends on the rate of pouring, temperature, slump (workability) of concrete, and height of the pour.
    *   The pressure is hydrostatic at the bottom of the pour and decreases as concrete sets.
*   **Wind Load:** Especially important for tall structures or formwork exposed to wind.
*   **Seismic Load:** Applicable in earthquake-prone regions.

**Important Point to Remember:** The lateral pressure of fresh concrete is often the most critical load to consider in formwork design, especially for walls and columns.

### **2. Main Types of Formwork Systems**

Formwork systems can be broadly classified based on their application, materials, and complexity.

#### **2.1 Based on Application:**

*   **Vertical Formwork:**
    *   **Wall Formwork:** Used for casting concrete walls (e.g., retaining walls, building walls, basement walls).
    *   **Column Formwork:** Used for casting concrete columns.
    *   **Shaft Formwork:** For vertical openings like elevator shafts, stairwells.
*   **Horizontal Formwork:**
    *   **Slab Formwork:** For casting concrete floors, roofs, and bridges.
    *   **Beam Formwork:** For casting concrete beams.
*   **Special Formwork:**
    *   **Arch Formwork:** For casting arches.
    *   **Dome Formwork:** For casting domed structures.
    *   **Tunnel Formwork:** Specialized formwork for tunnel construction.

#### **2.2 Based on Materials:**

*   **Timber Formwork:**
    *   **Pros:** Easily workable, readily available, good surface finish (if properly lined).
    *   **Cons:** Susceptible to moisture, warping, swelling, less reusable, can be labor-intensive for erection/dismantling.
    *   **Components:** Plywood (formply), timber planks, battens, props, shores.
*   **Steel Formwork:**
    *   **Pros:** Strong, durable, highly reusable, good dimensional accuracy, can be fabricated into modular panels, provides a smooth finish.
    *   **Cons:** Heavy, higher initial cost, requires skilled labor for erection/dismantling, can be prone to rust.
    *   **Components:** Steel panels, frames, ties, walers, props, shores.
*   **Aluminum Formwork:**
    *   **Pros:** Lightweight (easier handling), good strength-to-weight ratio, reusable, corrosion-resistant.
    *   **Cons:** Higher initial cost than steel, susceptible to damage if mishandled.
*   **Plastic/Composite Formwork:**
    *   **Pros:** Lightweight, waterproof, reusable, easy to clean, good surface finish.
    *   **Cons:** Can be less rigid than steel, limited load-carrying capacity for heavy structural elements, higher cost than timber.
    *   **Examples:** Modular plastic panels for walls and slabs.

#### **2.3 Based on Complexity/System:**

*   **Traditional Formwork (Site-Built):** Constructed on-site using raw materials like timber. Flexible but can be time-consuming.
*   **System Formwork (Prefabricated):**
    *   **Panel Systems:** Modular units (panels) of steel, aluminum, or plastic that are assembled on-site. Examples: Peri, Doka, Paschal systems.
    *   **Shoring and Scaffolding Systems:** Used to support horizontal formwork for slabs and beams.
        *   **Adjustable Steel Props:** Extendable steel tubes to provide vertical support.
        *   **Trusses and Towers:** For spanning larger distances or supporting heavier loads.
        *   **Ring-lock/Cup-lock Scaffolding:** Modular scaffolding systems for complex support structures.
    *   **Climbing Formwork:** Formwork that can be raised or "climbed" to successive levels as the concrete structure grows upwards. Used for high-rise walls, columns, and towers.
    *   **Slip Formwork:** Continuous movement of formwork vertically by means of jacks. Used for tall structures with constant cross-sections (e.g., chimneys, silos, bridge piers).

**Example:** For casting a typical residential concrete slab, one might use timber formwork for the edges (formply and timber battens) and steel props and timber joists as supporting members, topped with plywood. For a high-rise building's shear walls, a modular steel panel system with integrated climbing mechanisms would be more efficient.

### **3. Structural Stability and Load-Bearing Capacity**

#### **3.1 Importance of Stability:**

*   **Safety:** Prevents collapse during construction, protecting workers and the public.
*   **Dimensional Accuracy:** Prevents unwanted movement that can lead to incorrect dimensions or misaligned elements.
*   **Load Transfer:** Ensures loads are safely transferred to the ground or supporting structure.

#### **3.2 Key Considerations for Stability:**

*   **Foundation/Base Support:** The formwork must be supported on a stable base that can safely carry the applied loads.
*   **Bracing:** Diagonal bracing is crucial to prevent lateral movement and buckling of vertical supports (props, standards).
    *   **Horizontal Bracing:** Ties formwork members together to resist outward forces.
    *   **Diagonal Bracing:** Resists lateral loads and prevents racking.
*   **Connections:** All connections between formwork components (e.g., ties, bolts, wedges) must be strong and secure.
*   **Vertical Alignment:** Props and standards must be plumb to ensure vertical stability and proper load distribution.
*   **Load Distribution:** Loads must be distributed evenly to the supporting members to avoid overstressing any single component.
*   **Removal of Supports:** Shores and props must be removed in a planned sequence to ensure that the concrete has gained sufficient strength to carry its own weight and applied loads.

#### **3.3 Load-Bearing Capacity Calculations (Simplified):**

While detailed structural analysis is done by qualified engineers, basic principles involve:

*   **Determining the critical loads:** Lateral pressure of concrete, superimposed loads, self-weight.
*   **Calculating the maximum forces:** Bending moments, shear forces, axial forces in formwork members.
*   **Selecting members with adequate strength and stiffness:** This involves checking against material properties (e.g., bending strength of plywood, buckling strength of steel props).
*   **Deflection Control:** Ensuring that deflections are within acceptable limits as specified by codes.

**Example:** In slab formwork, the joists supporting the plywood deck will experience bending. The props supporting these joists will be in compression and need to be checked for buckling. The spacing of props and joists is determined by the load and the strength/stiffness of the materials used.

**Important Point to Remember:** Formwork is a temporary structure, but its design requires the same rigor as permanent structures. Failure to account for all loads and ensure stability can have catastrophic consequences.

### **4. Role of Material Selection in Formwork Design**

The choice of material significantly impacts the formwork's performance, cost, and suitability for a specific project.

#### **4.1 Factors Influencing Material Selection:**

*   **Type of Concrete Element:** Walls, slabs, columns, beams, curved structures.
*   **Magnitude of Loads:** Heavy loads require stronger materials.
*   **Required Surface Finish:** Smooth surfaces may favor steel or plastic; timber can provide good finish if lined.
*   **Reusability:** Projects with repetitive elements or multiple uses favor durable materials like steel or aluminum.
*   **Cost:** Initial cost vs. cost per use.
*   **Availability:** Local availability of materials and skilled labor.
*   **Site Conditions:** Exposure to moisture, extreme temperatures.
*   **Weight of Formwork:** Ease of handling and erection.
*   **Fire Resistance:** Sometimes a consideration.

#### **4.2 Properties of Common Formwork Materials:**

| Material    | Pros                                                                 | Cons                                                                   |
| :---------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Timber**  | Easily workable, good surface finish, relatively low initial cost. | Not very durable, susceptible to moisture, low reusability, labor-intensive. |
| **Plywood** | Good surface finish, relatively lightweight, available in large sheets. | Can delaminate, susceptible to moisture damage, specific grades needed (formply). |
| **Steel**   | High strength, durable, high reusability, good dimensional accuracy. | Heavy, high initial cost, prone to rust, requires skilled labor.       |
| **Aluminum**| Lightweight, good strength-to-weight ratio, reusable, corrosion-resistant. | Higher initial cost than steel, susceptible to damage if mishandled. |
| **Plastic** | Lightweight, waterproof, reusable, easy to clean, good surface finish. | Lower stiffness, limited load capacity, higher cost than timber.       |

**Example:** For casting a few simple concrete piers for a small bridge, timber formwork might be the most economical choice. For the core of a high-rise building, modular steel formwork would be preferred for its speed, accuracy, and reusability.

### **5. Principles of Formwork Erection and Dismantling**

#### **5.1 Erection Principles:**

*   **Following Design Drawings:** Formwork must be erected strictly according to approved design drawings and erection plans.
*   **Cleanliness:** Formwork surfaces should be clean and free from debris, nails, and projections before concrete is poured.
*   **Release Agents:** Apply form release agents to prevent concrete from sticking to the formwork and to protect the formwork material itself.
*   **Joints and Ties:** Formwork panels should be tightly joined, and ties should be installed at specified locations and tensions to prevent bulging under concrete pressure.
*   **Plumb and Level:** Vertical formwork must be plumb, and horizontal formwork must be level to achieve correct dimensions.
*   **Support and Bracing:** Adequate bracing and propping must be installed to ensure stability during the erection and concreting process.
*   **Reinforcement Placement:** Ensure that formwork does not obstruct the proper placement and tying of reinforcing steel.
*   **Access and Safety:** Provide safe access for workers during erection.

#### **5.2 Dismantling Principles:**

*   **Concrete Strength:** Formwork and supports should only be removed when the concrete has reached sufficient strength to support its own weight and any applied loads without damage. The minimum curing period depends on concrete mix design, ambient temperature, and specified strength requirements.
*   **Order of Removal:**
    *   **Side Formwork:** Usually removed first (e.g., walls, columns) as they don't carry the primary load of the concrete member.
    *   **Shoring/Props:** Removed in a specific sequence, often starting from the center of a span and moving outwards, or from the edges inwards, depending on the structural design and load transfer path.
*   **Careful Removal:** Formwork should be removed carefully without damaging the concrete surface or edges.
*   **Protection of Concrete:** Any partially supported concrete members (e.g., beams before props are removed) must be protected from shock or excessive vibration.
*   **Reuse of Formwork:** Clean and inspect formwork components after dismantling for future reuse.
*   **Safety:** Ensure safe working practices during dismantling, as formwork components can be heavy and unstable.

**Important Point to Remember:** The timing of formwork removal is critical. Premature removal can lead to cracking or failure, while delayed removal can impact construction schedules and costs.

### **6. Factors Influencing the Cost-Effectiveness of Formwork**

Cost-effectiveness is achieved when the total cost of formwork (materials, labor, time) is minimized while meeting all performance and safety requirements.

#### **6.1 Key Cost Factors:**

*   **Material Costs:** The price of timber, plywood, steel, aluminum, and accessories.
*   **Labor Costs:** The amount of labor required for erection, dismantling, and handling.
*   **Design Complexity:** Intricate shapes or complex geometries increase design, fabrication, and erection time.
*   **Reusability:** Highly reusable formwork systems (e.g., steel systems) have a higher initial cost but lower cost per use over multiple projects.
*   **Erection and Dismantling Time:** Faster systems reduce labor and overall project duration costs.
*   **Formwork Surface Finish:** Achieving a high-quality surface finish may require more expensive materials or additional labor for finishing.
*   **Site Conditions:** Difficult site access or adverse weather can increase costs.
*   **Waste:** Minimizing material waste during cutting and erection.
*   **Formwork Management:** Efficient planning, scheduling, and inventory control of formwork.
*   **Rental vs. Purchase:** Deciding whether to rent or buy formwork systems.

#### **6.2 Strategies for Cost-Effectiveness:**

*   **Standardization:** Using standard panel sizes and components.
*   **Modularization:** Employing prefabricated or system formwork.
*   **Optimized Design:** Designing formwork to minimize material usage and labor while maintaining structural integrity.
*   **Efficient Erection/Dismantling Methods:** Using specialized tools and techniques.
*   **Proper Maintenance and Repair:** Extending the lifespan of reusable formwork.
*   **Planning and Scheduling:** Integrating formwork planning into the overall project schedule.
*   **Material Selection:** Choosing the most appropriate material for the project's duration and requirements.

**Example:** For a project requiring a large number of identical walls, investing in a reusable steel system formwork might be more cost-effective in the long run than using disposable timber formwork repeatedly.

### **Practice Questions/Exercises:**

1.  **Identify and explain the two primary purposes of formwork in concrete construction.**
    *   **Answer:**
        1.  **Shape:** To provide the desired shape and dimensions for the concrete element.
        2.  **Support:** To hold the fresh concrete in its intended position until it gains sufficient strength.

2.  **List three critical loads that formwork must be designed to withstand.**
    *   **Answer:**
        1.  Lateral pressure of fresh concrete.
        2.  Self-weight of the formwork and fresh concrete.
        3.  Superimposed loads (workmen, equipment).

3.  **What are the advantages of using steel formwork compared to timber formwork for a large infrastructure project?**
    *   **Answer:** Steel formwork offers higher strength, greater durability, better dimensional accuracy, and significantly higher reusability, leading to lower cost per use over the project's life. It also provides a smoother surface finish and is generally faster to erect and dismantle for repetitive elements.

4.  **Why is bracing crucial in formwork design, especially for vertical elements like walls?**
    *   **Answer:** Bracing provides stability against lateral loads (wind, unbalanced concrete pressure) and prevents buckling of vertical supports. Without adequate bracing, formwork can rack, deform, or even collapse.

5.  **Discuss one strategy for improving the cost-effectiveness of formwork on a construction project.**
    *   **Answer:** One strategy is to utilize standardized or modular system formwork. This reduces design complexity, speeds up erection and dismantling, and increases reusability, thereby lowering the overall cost per use compared to site-built traditional formwork.

### **Important Points to Remember Summary:**

*   Formwork is a **temporary** structure but requires **rigorous design** for safety and performance.
*   The **lateral pressure of fresh concrete** is a critical and dynamic load to consider.
*   **Strength, rigidity, stability, and accuracy** are fundamental requirements.
*   **Material selection** is a trade-off between cost, performance, and reusability.
*   **Bracing and proper support** are essential for structural stability.
*   **Timing of formwork removal** is critical for concrete strength and preventing damage.
*   **Cost-effectiveness** is achieved through planning, standardization, reusability, and efficient labor practices.
