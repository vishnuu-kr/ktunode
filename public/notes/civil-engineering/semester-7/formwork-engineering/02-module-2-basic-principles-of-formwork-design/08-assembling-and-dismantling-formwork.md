# Formwork Engineering: Module 2 - Basic Principles of Formwork Design
## Topic: Assembling and Dismantling Formwork

---

### **Introduction**

This module delves into the critical aspects of assembling and dismantling formwork. Proper execution of these stages is paramount for the safety of workers, the integrity of the structure, and the efficiency of the construction process. This topic will cover the fundamental principles, procedures, safety considerations, and best practices associated with these essential formwork operations.

---

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

2.  **Identify and describe the key steps involved in the assembly of various types of formwork.**

### **1. Importance of Proper Assembly and Dismantling Procedures**

Proper assembly and dismantling are not merely procedural steps; they are fundamental to successful and safe construction.

*   **Structural Integrity:** Incorrect assembly can lead to formwork failure under the load of fresh concrete, causing collapses, damaged structures, and severe injuries.
*   **Safety:** The most critical aspect. Unstable or improperly erected formwork poses significant risks to workers. Conversely, premature or incorrect dismantling can lead to the collapse of partially cured concrete.
*   **Concrete Quality:** Accurate assembly ensures that the formwork holds the concrete in the correct shape and alignment, leading to a structurally sound and aesthetically pleasing final product.
*   **Efficiency and Cost-Effectiveness:** Well-planned assembly and dismantling streamline the construction process, reduce labor costs, and minimize material wastage.
*   **Durability of Formwork:** Proper handling during both stages extends the lifespan of the formwork system, reducing replacement costs.

### **2. Key Steps Involved in the Assembly of Various Types of Formwork**

The assembly process varies depending on the type of formwork. However, common principles apply.

#### **2.1 General Assembly Principles**

*   **Site Preparation:**
    *   **Cleaning:** Ensure the area where formwork will be erected is clean and free from debris.
    *   **Leveling and Compaction:** The ground or supporting structure must be level and adequately compacted to prevent settlement.
    *   **Layout and Marking:** Precise layout of formwork lines and dimensions according to drawings is crucial.
*   **Component Preparation:**
    *   **Inspection:** All formwork components (panels, props, beams, ties, braces) should be inspected for damage, wear, and cleanliness before assembly.
    *   **Lubrication:** Apply release agents to surfaces that will be in contact with concrete. This aids in easy dismantling and prevents concrete from sticking.
*   **Erection and Bracing:**
    *   **Sequential Erection:** Formwork is typically erected in a logical sequence, often starting with supporting elements and then the facing panels.
    *   **Plumbness and Alignment:** All vertical elements must be plumb, and horizontal elements must be level and in correct alignment.
    *   **Tightness of Joints:** Joints between formwork panels should be tight to prevent grout leakage, which can weaken the concrete and create unsightly surface defects.
    *   **Bracing and Propping:** Adequate bracing and temporary supports are essential to maintain the formwork's stability during erection and before concrete pouring.
*   **Fixing:**
    *   **Ties and Spacers:** Use appropriate formwork ties and spacers to maintain the designed dimensions and prevent the formwork from bulging outwards under concrete pressure.
    *   **Support Systems:** Ensure all props, shores, and scaffolding are securely placed and adequately braced.

#### **2.2 Assembly of Common Formwork Types**

*   **Timber Formwork:**
    *   **Cutting and Shaping:** Timber elements are cut to size and shape according to the design.
    *   **Assembly of Panels:** Boards are nailed or screwed to battens to form panels.
    *   **Erection of Walls:** Studs and wales are erected, followed by the sheathing. Bracing is critical.
    *   **Slab Formwork:** Joists and bearers are supported by props, and the decking is laid.
    *   **Example:** For a simple concrete wall, timber studs are erected vertically, braced, and then timber boards are attached horizontally to form the face.

*   **Steel Formwork (Modular Systems):**
    *   **Component Handling:** Steel formwork panels are typically lighter and easier to handle than timber.
    *   **Connection Systems:** Panels are connected using wedge pins, clamps, or bolts. The connection system is designed for rapid assembly and dismantling.
    *   **Panel Erection:** Panels are erected in sequence, ensuring correct alignment and plumbness.
    *   **Bracing:** Integral bracing or separate bracing systems are used to maintain stability.
    *   **Example:** A modular steel formwork system for a circular column would involve erecting curved steel panels and locking them together with specialized clamps.

*   **Aluminum Formwork:**
    *   **Lightweight:** Aluminum formwork is significantly lighter than steel, facilitating faster assembly by fewer workers.
    *   **Interlocking Systems:** Panels usually feature integrated locking mechanisms for quick connection.
    *   **Precision:** Aluminum systems are manufactured to tight tolerances, ensuring high accuracy.
    *   **Example:** For a concrete slab, aluminum joists and bearers are erected on props, and then aluminum decking panels are placed and locked together.

*   **Plywood and Plastic Formwork:**
    *   **Panel Assembly:** Similar to timber, plywood or plastic faced panels are assembled, often with timber or steel frames.
    *   **Connection:** Panel edges are joined using clamps, ties, or screwing.
    *   **Example:** Plywood faced panels with timber backing are commonly used for walls and columns, assembled using timber walings and tie rods.

#### **2.3 Assembly of Specific Structural Elements**

*   **Walls:** Formwork is erected on both sides of the intended wall, with tie rods or spacers to maintain the correct thickness. Bracing is essential to resist lateral pressure from concrete.
*   **Columns:** Vertical formwork is erected, ensuring it is plumb and securely braced. For square/rectangular columns, corner pieces are critical for maintaining sharp corners.
*   **Slabs:** A supporting structure of props, beams, and joists is erected. The decking (plywood, steel, or plastic panels) is then laid and secured. Edge formwork (risers) is added to contain the concrete.
*   **Beams:** Similar to slabs, beams require supporting formwork for the soffit and sides, with adequate bracing.
*   **Stairs:** Formwork for stairs requires precise angle and level setting for each tread and riser.

### **3. Critical Factors Influencing the Dismantling of Formwork**

Dismantling requires careful consideration of several factors to ensure the concrete has gained sufficient strength.

*   **Concrete Strength:** The primary factor. Formwork should only be removed when the concrete has reached a specified minimum strength to support its own weight and any superimposed loads without distortion or damage. This strength is typically expressed as a percentage of the characteristic compressive strength (e.g., 70% for slab soffits, 100% for supporting props).
*   **Type of Cement:** Different cement types have varying rates of strength development. Rapid-hardening cements allow for earlier dismantling than ordinary Portland cement.
*   **Curing Conditions:** Temperature and humidity significantly affect the rate of concrete hardening. Warmer temperatures and adequate moisture accelerate strength gain.
*   **Type of Structure and Loading:**
    *   **Slabs and Beams:** Require significant strength before removing soffit support, as they are significantly loaded.
    *   **Walls and Columns:** Can typically be stripped earlier as they primarily support vertical loads.
    *   **Superimposed Loads:** If construction loads are applied, a higher concrete strength is required before dismantling.
*   **Span and Depth:** Longer spans and deeper sections in slabs and beams require longer curing periods due to higher internal stresses.
*   **Rate of Loading:** If a structure is to be loaded quickly after pouring, higher initial strength is needed before formwork removal.
*   **Protection of Edges and Corners:** Edges and corners of concrete elements are more vulnerable to damage during dismantling, so special care is needed.

### **4. Procedures and Precautions for Safe Dismantling of Formwork**

Dismantling is often more dangerous than assembly if not done correctly.

#### **4.1 General Dismantling Procedures**

*   **Inspection:**
    *   **Concrete Strength Verification:** Visually inspect the concrete for apparent hardness. If doubt exists, cylinder tests or rebound hammer tests may be required to confirm strength.
    *   **Structural Stability:** Ensure the structure is stable and self-supporting.
*   **Planned Sequence:**
    *   **Stripping of Loose Items First:** Remove wedges, clamps, ties, and any loose bracing.
    *   **Dismantling of Walls and Sides:** Side forms for walls, columns, and beams can usually be removed before soffit supports.
    *   **Removal of Soffit Supports:** This is the most critical step for slabs and beams. Supports are removed in a planned sequence, often from mid-span towards the supports, to gradually transfer the load.
    *   **Removal of Props:** Props are usually the last to be removed, and this should be done gradually.
*   **Controlled Release of Load:**
    *   **Gradual Loosening:** Wedges and jacks should be gradually loosened to avoid sudden stress changes in the concrete.
    *   **Systematic Removal of Props:** Remove props in a predetermined order, often starting from the center of the span.
*   **Tool Usage:** Use appropriate tools for release (e.g., hammers, wrenches, pry bars). Avoid excessive force that could damage the concrete.

#### **4.2 Precautions for Safe Dismantling**

*   **Clear Working Area:** Ensure the area below and around the formwork is clear of personnel and equipment.
*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Hard hats, safety glasses, safety boots, and gloves are mandatory.
*   **Scaffolding and Access:** Provide safe access to all parts of the formwork for dismantling.
*   **Communication:** Establish clear communication signals between workers.
*   **Never Stand Underneath:** Prohibit personnel from standing directly beneath formwork that is being dismantled.
*   **Controlled Dropping:** If components need to be dropped, ensure they land in a designated safe area.
*   **Avoid Impact:** Do not hammer or force components violently, as this can damage the concrete.
*   **Formwork Stability During Dismantling:** Ensure that the remaining formwork and the structure itself remain stable as sections are removed.
*   **Worker Training:** Ensure all workers involved in dismantling are adequately trained and experienced.
*   **Supervision:** Always have a competent supervisor overseeing the dismantling process.

#### **4.3 Dismantling of Specific Structural Elements**

*   **Walls and Columns:** Side forms can usually be stripped once the concrete has achieved sufficient early strength (e.g., a few hours or a day, depending on the concrete mix and ambient temperature).
*   **Slabs and Beams:**
    *   **Side Forms:** Can be stripped after 24-48 hours.
    *   **Soffit Support:** The removal of props supporting the soffit is crucial. For typical concrete, supports might be left in place for 1-3 weeks depending on the span and load. **Important:** Some temporary props are often left in place under slabs and beams for a longer period to support subsequent floors during construction, especially if the upper floors are cast before the lower floors have gained full strength. This is known as **reshoring**.
*   **Stairs:** Side forms can be removed relatively early. The soffit support must remain until the stairs gain adequate strength.

### **5. Common Problems Encountered During Assembly and Dismantling and Their Solutions**

| Problem                                | Cause                                                                                                  | Solution                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| :------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Formwork Collapse during Assembly**  | Inadequate bracing, improper prop placement, loose connections, over-tightening tie rods.                | Rigorous adherence to erection plans, thorough bracing, double-checking connections, using proper torque for tie rods.                                                                                                             |
| **Grout Leakage**                      | Poorly fitting panels, insufficient sealing of joints, damaged formwork edges.                         | Ensure tight joints, use sealing tapes or gaskets, repair or replace damaged formwork.                                                                                                                                           |
| **Bulging Formwork**                   | Insufficient tie rods/walers, improper spacing of supports, high concrete pressure.                      | Increase the number and strength of tie rods, strengthen walers and supports, consider formwork release mechanisms for vibration.                                                                                                   |
| **Damaged Concrete Surface**           | Rough handling during assembly, striking formwork too early, failure to use release agents.              | Careful handling of components, adhere to recommended stripping times, proper application of release agents, use appropriate tools for dismantling.                                                                             |
| **Difficulty in Stripping Formwork**   | Insufficient release agent, concrete sticking to formwork, improper design of formwork joints.           | Ensure thorough application of release agents, use mechanical aids for stripping where necessary, ensure formwork is designed for easy release.                                                                                  |
| **Formwork Tipping During Dismantling** | Removal of too many supports at once, inadequate bracing of remaining formwork, unstable ground conditions. | Follow a planned, gradual dismantling sequence, ensure remaining formwork is adequately braced, check ground stability.                                                                                                          |
| **Premature Formwork Removal leading to Structural Damage** | Incorrect assessment of concrete strength, reliance on visual cues rather than testing.      | Always verify concrete strength through appropriate testing before removing critical supports. Follow project-specific guidelines.                                                                                               |
| **Damage to Newly Cast Concrete**      | Dropping tools or materials onto fresh concrete, heavy impacts during dismantling.                     | Maintain a clean work area, use designated drop zones, careful handling of tools and components.                                                                                                                                  |

### **6. Role of Quality Control and Inspection in Formwork Assembly and Dismantling**

Quality control (QC) and inspection are integral to ensuring safety and achieving the desired results.

*   **Pre-Assembly Inspection:**
    *   **Material Quality:** Checking formwork materials (timber, steel, plywood, plastic) for defects, damage, and suitability.
    *   **Component Condition:** Ensuring all panels, props, clamps, ties, and braces are in good working order.
*   **During Assembly Inspection:**
    *   **Dimensional Accuracy:** Verifying that the formwork dimensions, plumbness, and alignment match the approved drawings.
    *   **Stability and Bracing:** Checking that all supports are adequately placed and securely braced.
    *   **Tie Rod and Clamp Tightness:** Ensuring connections are secure and tie rods are correctly tensioned.
    *   **Release Agent Application:** Confirming proper and uniform application of release agents.
*   **Pre-Dismantling Inspection:**
    *   **Concrete Strength:** Verifying concrete has reached the required strength.
    *   **Structural Integrity:** Assessing the self-supporting capacity of the structure before formwork removal.
*   **During Dismantling Inspection:**
    *   **Controlled Removal:** Monitoring that dismantling follows the planned sequence and is carried out safely.
    *   **Prevention of Damage:** Ensuring no damage is inflicted on the concrete or adjacent structures.
*   **Post-Dismantling Inspection:**
    *   **Concrete Surface Finish:** Checking for any defects that might require remediation.
    *   **Formwork Condition:** Inspecting formwork components for reusability.
*   **Documentation:** Maintaining records of inspections, any deviations, and corrective actions taken.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Safety First:** Always prioritize safety during all stages of formwork assembly and dismantling.
*   **Follow the Plan:** Adhere strictly to the approved formwork drawings and erection/dismantling sequences.
*   **Adequate Bracing:** Never underestimate the importance of proper bracing for stability.
*   **Know Your Concrete:** Understand the strength development characteristics of the concrete mix being used.
*   **Gradual Loading/Unloading:** Apply and remove loads (concrete, supports) gradually and systematically.
*   **Cleanliness:** Keep formwork clean and free from debris.
*   **Release Agents:** Use appropriate release agents for easy stripping and protection of formwork and concrete.
*   **Experienced Personnel:** Ensure that personnel involved in formwork operations are trained and supervised.
*   **Inspection is Key:** Regular inspections at all stages are crucial for identifying and rectifying potential problems.
*   **Reshoring:** Understand the concept and necessity of reshoring for multi-storey construction.

---

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

1.  **(b) Structural integrity, safety, and concrete quality**
    *   **Explanation:** While aesthetics and cost are considerations, the paramount reasons are ensuring the structure's stability and the safety of workers and the public, as well as achieving the desired concrete shape and quality.

2.  **(c) Wedges and clamps**
    *   **Explanation:** Loose elements like wedges, clamps, and ties are typically removed first, followed by side forms, and then the soffit supports (props) in a controlled manner.

3.  **(c) Concrete strength**
    *   **Explanation:** While ambient temperature and cement type influence strength gain, it is the **actual achieved strength** of the concrete that dictates when formwork can be safely removed.

4.  **Consequences of inadequate bracing:**
    *   **Formwork collapse:** This can happen during concrete pouring due to the immense pressure exerted by the fresh concrete, leading to serious injury, death, and significant structural damage.
    *   **Distorted concrete:** Formwork can bulge or deform, resulting in incorrect dimensions and shapes for the structural element, affecting its load-bearing capacity and appearance.
    *   **Instability during construction:** The partially constructed element might become unstable and collapse due to external forces like wind or vibration.

5.  **Reshoring:** Reshoring is the process of placing temporary shores or supports under a concrete slab or beam after the original formwork has been removed.
    *   **Why it's used:** It is used in multi-storey construction to support the load of freshly poured upper floors, especially before the lower floors have achieved their full structural strength. This prevents excessive deflection and potential collapse of the lower, immature concrete elements under the superimposed load.

6.  **Common Problems and Solutions during Formwork Dismantling:**
    *   **Problem:** Difficulty in stripping formwork.
        *   **Solution:** Ensure adequate and even application of release agents; use mechanical aids like pry bars or formwork removers carefully; design formwork for easy release.
    *   **Problem:** Damage to newly cast concrete edges or corners.
        *   **Solution:** Careful use of tools; avoid forceful hammering or prying against the concrete; ensure formwork is removed in a controlled sequence.
    *   **Problem:** Formwork tipping or becoming unstable during dismantling.
        *   **Solution:** Follow a planned dismantling sequence; ensure remaining formwork is adequately braced; do not remove too many supports at once.

7.  **Importance of Release Agents:**
    *   **Easy Stripping:** Release agents create a barrier between the concrete and the formwork surface, preventing the concrete from bonding to the formwork. This allows for the formwork to be stripped easily without damaging the concrete surface.
    *   **Protection of Formwork:** Prevents concrete from sticking to and damaging the formwork, thus extending the lifespan and reusability of the formwork components.
    *   **Improved Surface Finish:** Contributes to a smoother and cleaner concrete surface finish, often reducing the need for further surface treatment.
