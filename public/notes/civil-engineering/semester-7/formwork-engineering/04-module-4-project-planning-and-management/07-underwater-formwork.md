# Formwork Engineering: Module 4 - Project Planning and Management

## Topic: Underwater Formwork

This module focuses on the planning and management aspects of formwork engineering, with a specific emphasis on the unique challenges and techniques associated with **underwater formwork**.

### 1. Necessity and Applications of Underwater Formwork

#### 1.1 What is Underwater Formwork?

Underwater formwork refers to temporary or permanent structures used to contain and shape concrete when it is being cast in an underwater environment. Unlike conventional formwork, it must contend with water pressure, currents, and the need for precise placement without direct human access during the casting process.

#### 1.2 Applications

Underwater formwork is essential for various civil engineering projects, including:

*   **Bridge Piers and Abutments:** Constructing foundations and supports for bridges in rivers, lakes, or oceans.
    *   *Example:* Building piers for a bridge crossing a wide river where dewatering is impractical or uneconomical.
*   **Dams and Spillways:** Creating water-retaining structures and overflow sections.
    *   *Example:* Building concrete sections of a dam that are submerged during construction due to water levels.
*   **Offshore Structures:** Foundations for oil rigs, wind turbines, and artificial islands.
    *   *Example:* Constructing the base of an offshore wind turbine that rests on the seabed.
*   **Tunnels and Culverts:** Pouring concrete for submerged tunnel sections or culverts under waterways.
    *   *Example:* Creating submerged concrete elements for an immersed tunnel.
*   **Marine Structures:** Seawalls, breakwaters, jetties, and caissons.
    *   *Example:* Constructing caissons for port development that are sunk into position and then filled with concrete underwater.
*   **Pipeline Crossings:** Encasing pipelines laid across riverbeds or seabeds.
    *   *Example:* Building concrete sleeves around a pipeline to protect it from erosion and damage.

---

### 2. Challenges of Underwater Formwork

Working with formwork underwater presents a unique set of challenges compared to conventional construction:

*   **Water Pressure (Hydrostatic Pressure):** The formwork must withstand significant pressure exerted by the surrounding water column. This pressure increases with depth.
    *   *Key Concept:* Hydrostatic pressure ($P = \rho \times g \times h$), where $\rho$ is the density of water, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $h$ is the depth.
*   **Water Currents:** Flowing water can exert lateral forces on the formwork, potentially causing displacement or damage.
*   **Buoyancy:** The formwork and the wet concrete can be subject to upward forces (buoyancy), requiring anchoring or weighting.
*   **Accessibility:** Direct human access for assembly, inspection, and adjustment is limited or impossible, requiring specialized equipment and techniques.
*   **Concrete Placement and Consolidation:** Achieving proper concrete placement and consolidation without vibration or direct inspection is difficult.
*   **Material Selection:** Formwork materials must be durable, resistant to water ingress, and capable of withstanding the harsh underwater environment.
*   **Anchoring and Stability:** Securely anchoring the formwork to the seabed or existing structures is crucial to prevent movement.
*   **Sealing:** Ensuring watertight joints to prevent concrete washout and maintain the integrity of the concrete mix.
*   **Visibility:** Poor visibility underwater can hinder precise placement and inspection.
*   **Environmental Impact:** Minimizing disturbance to the aquatic ecosystem during construction.

### 3. Types of Underwater Formwork Systems and Materials

Various systems and materials are employed, often a combination of both, to address the challenges:

#### 3.1 Types of Systems

*   **Pre-cast Concrete Units:** Large, prefabricated concrete sections that are lowered into place and then concreted internally or connected.
    *   *Example:* Using pre-cast concrete boxes that are sealed and sunk into position to form a foundation.
*   **Steel Sheet Piles (Cofferdams):** Driven into the ground to create an enclosure, allowing dewatering and conventional formwork within. While this often involves dewatering, it can also be used in conjunction with underwater techniques if complete dewatering isn't feasible.
*   **Caissons:** Watertight, prefabricated structures that are sunk to the seabed and then filled with concrete. They can be open, pneumatic, or floating.
    *   *Open Caissons:* Open at the top and bottom, sunk by excavating material from within.
    *   *Pneumatic Caissons:* Use compressed air to keep water out, allowing workers to excavate.
    *   *Floating Caissons:* Constructed on land or in a dry dock, then floated to the site, sunk, and filled.
*   **Inflatable Formwork (Air-Inflated Formwork):** Flexible membranes inflated with air or water to create the desired shape. Once concrete is placed, they remain as permanent formwork and membrane.
    *   *Example:* Using large inflatable tubes to form a concrete barrier wall underwater.
*   **Rigid Steel or Timber Formwork with Watertight Seals:** Traditional formwork that is meticulously sealed at all joints to prevent water ingress. This is often used for smaller or more accessible underwater concrete pours.
*   **Steel Cylinder Formwork:** Large diameter steel cylinders used for constructing bridge piers or foundations.
    *   *Example:* Driving steel cylinders into the seabed and filling them with concrete to create robust bridge supports.
*   **Geotextile Bags/Sleeves:** Fabric bags filled with a concrete-sand grout mixture, then placed underwater to form structural elements or erosion protection.
    *   *Example:* Using large geotextile bags filled with grout to stabilize the seabed around a pipeline.

#### 3.2 Materials

*   **Steel:**
    *   **Advantages:** High strength, durability, watertight (when properly fabricated and sealed), reusable.
    *   **Applications:** Sheet piles, caissons, cylinder formwork, custom-fabricated panels.
    *   *Note:* Requires corrosion protection.
*   **Timber:**
    *   **Advantages:** Relatively inexpensive, easy to cut and shape, can be sealed with membranes.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Less durable than steel, susceptible to water absorption and deformation, not easily reusable for multiple cycles.
    *   **Applications:** Smaller pours, temporary internal formwork, often used in conjunction with other methods.
*   **Reinforced Concrete:**
    *   **Advantages:** Can be pre-cast, strong, durable.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Heavy, requires careful handling and placement.
    *   **Applications:** Caissons, pre-cast units.
*   **Geotextiles (Synthetic Fabrics):**
    *   **Advantages:** Flexible, permeable to water but impermeable to cement particles, can form complex shapes.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Single-use, susceptible to damage during handling.
    *   **Applications:** Geotextile bags/sleeves for grout or concrete placement.
*   **Inflatable Membranes (PVC, Neoprene):**
    *   **Advantages:** Lightweight, can form complex shapes, reusable.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Susceptible to puncture, requires constant pressure to maintain shape during concreting.
    *   **Applications:** Inflatable formwork for tunnels, piers.
*   **Sealing Compounds and Gaskets:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure watertight joints between formwork panels, around penetrations, and at the base.
    *   *Examples:* Rubber gaskets, neoprene seals, polymeric joint fillers.

### 4. Principles of Design and Construction

#### 4.1 Design Considerations

*   **Structural Integrity:**
    *   **Water Pressure:** Design formwork to withstand the maximum hydrostatic pressure at the deepest point.
    *   **Current Forces:** Account for lateral forces from water currents.
    *   **Buoyancy:** Ensure the formwork is adequately weighted or anchored to counteract buoyancy.
    *   **Concrete Pressure:** Consider the pressure exerted by the wet concrete itself, especially during placement.
    *   **Anchoring System:** Design a robust system to fix the formwork to the seabed or existing structures.
*   **Watertightness:**
    *   Design for minimal joints or use effective sealing methods at all connections and penetrations.
*   **Ease of Installation and Removal:**
    *   Consider the logistics of handling and placing heavy or complex formwork underwater.
    *   Plan for any necessary dismantling or leaving the formwork in place as permanent work.
*   **Material Selection:** Choose materials appropriate for the intended lifespan, water conditions, and structural requirements.
*   **Connection Details:** Design secure and robust connections between formwork elements, lifting points, and anchoring systems.
*   **Deformation Control:** Minimize any deflection or distortion under load.
*   **Accommodation of Reinforcement:** Ensure adequate space and access for placing reinforcement.

#### 4.2 Construction Procedures

*   **Site Investigation and Preparation:**
    *   Assess seabed conditions, water depth, currents, and potential obstructions.
    *   Clear the seabed of debris.
*   **Fabrication:**
    *   Formwork panels or units are typically fabricated on land or in a sheltered area.
    *   Precision in fabrication is crucial for achieving watertightness.
*   **Assembly (if applicable):**
    *   Assemble large formwork units on land or floating platforms before sinking.
*   **Transportation and Lowering:**
    *   Use barges, cranes, or specialized lifting equipment to transport formwork to the site.
    *   Controlled lowering using winches or ballast systems is essential to prevent damage.
*   **Positioning and Anchoring:**
    *   Use GPS, sonar, and divers (if applicable) for precise positioning.
    *   Securely anchor the formwork to the seabed using piles, ballast, or directly to existing foundations.
*   **Sealing:**
    *   Apply sealing compounds and gaskets to all joints before or during placement.
*   **Inspection (Pre-pour):**
    *   Remote sensing, divers, or cameras are used to verify correct positioning and integrity before concrete placement.

### 5. Concrete Placement and Curing Underwater

#### 5.1 Concrete Placement Methods

Achieving a cohesive and high-quality concrete pour underwater without segregation is paramount.

*   **Tremie Pipe Method:**
    *   **Description:** A watertight pipe (tremie pipe) with a funnel at the top is used. The pipe is lowered to the seabed, and concrete is fed into it. The end of the pipe is kept embedded in the fresh concrete being placed, ensuring continuous flow and minimizing wash-out. As concrete is placed, the tremie pipe is gradually lifted and moved horizontally.
    *   **Key Principle:** The end of the tremie pipe must always be submerged in the placed concrete (at least 1-2m deep) to prevent the ingress of water into the pipe and the segregation of concrete.
    *   *Example:* Pouring concrete for a bridge pier foundation using a tremie pipe lowered from a barge.
*   **Chute Method:**
    *   Similar to the tremie pipe, but uses a larger diameter pipe or chute.
    *   Less common for deep water due to control issues.
*   **Pump Method:**
    *   Concrete is pumped directly through a pipeline to the desired location. The discharge end of the pump line is kept submerged in the placed concrete.
*   **Bucket Method (for smaller pours):**
    *   Concrete is placed in a bottom-dumping bucket that is lowered to the seabed. The bucket is opened at the bottom to discharge the concrete. The bucket must be withdrawn carefully to avoid disturbance.
*   **Grout or Slurry Injection:**
    *   For geotextile bags or some caisson types, a flowable grout or concrete slurry is injected under pressure.

#### 5.2 Concrete Mix Design Considerations

*   **Low Water-Cement Ratio:** To achieve higher strength and durability and reduce permeability.
*   **Plasticizers/Superplasticizers:** To improve workability and flowability without increasing the water content.
*   **Coarse Aggregate Size:** Typically, the maximum aggregate size is limited to avoid segregation and ensure flowability through the tremie pipe.
*   **Admixtures:**
    *   **Anti-washout Admixtures (AWAs):** These polymers increase the viscosity of the mix, helping to prevent the cement from washing out in contact with water. This is a critical admixture for underwater concrete.
    *   **Air-Entraining Agents:** To improve freeze-thaw resistance and workability.
*   **Cement Content:** Often higher to ensure proper hydration and strength development.
*   **Slump:** High slump is generally required for ease of placement.

#### 5.3 Curing Underwater

*   **Self-Curing:** The surrounding water provides a moist environment for the concrete to cure.
*   **Heat of Hydration:** The mass of concrete and the surrounding water can help dissipate the heat generated during hydration, reducing thermal cracking.
*   **Protection:** The formwork itself provides the primary protection for the curing concrete.

### 6. Inspection, Maintenance, and Removal

#### 6.1 Inspection

*   **Pre-Pour Inspection:**
    *   Verification of formwork position, integrity, and watertightness using divers, remote-controlled vehicles (ROVs), or sonar.
*   **During Pour Inspection:**
    *   Monitoring of concrete placement from the surface (e.g., by observing the tremie pipe).
    *   Divers or ROVs may monitor the progress and quality of the pour if visibility permits and it's safe.
*   **Post-Pour Inspection:**
    *   Assessing the quality of the hardened concrete, checking for voids or defects (often using non-destructive testing methods like sonar or core samples).
    *   Verifying the stability and integrity of the formwork after concrete placement.

#### 6.2 Maintenance

*   Maintenance is generally limited during the pour due to inaccessibility.
*   Any maintenance required would be to the equipment used for placement (e.g., cleaning tremie pipes) or ensuring the stability of support structures.

#### 6.3 Removal (or Non-Removal)

*   **Permanent Formwork:** In many underwater applications, the formwork is designed to remain in place permanently, becoming part of the structure. This is common for geotextile bags, some types of caissons, or steel casings.
*   **Temporary Formwork:** If temporary formwork is used, removal requires careful planning:
    *   **Dismantling:** If possible, formwork can be dismantled underwater by divers or ROVs.
    *   **Washing Out:** Formwork might be designed to be washed out or broken apart.
    *   **Controlled Sinking/Disposal:** In some cases, formwork might be released and allowed to sink to a designated disposal area (subject to environmental regulations).

### 7. Safety and Environmental Considerations

#### 7.1 Safety

*   **Working at Heights:** For personnel operating on barges or platforms.
*   **Diving Operations:** Strict safety protocols for divers are essential, including decompression schedules and clear communication.
*   **Heavy Lifting:** Safe operation of cranes and lifting equipment.
*   **Equipment Malfunction:** Risk of failure in hoisting, pumping, or sealing equipment.
*   **Environmental Hazards:** Currents, waves, and poor visibility can pose risks to personnel and equipment.
*   **Confined Spaces:** For divers working within formwork structures.
*   **Communication:** Reliable communication systems between surface operations and divers are crucial.

#### 7.2 Environmental Considerations

*   **Minimizing Seabed Disturbance:** Careful placement of formwork and materials to reduce damage to marine habitats.
*   **Pollution Prevention:** Preventing the release of concrete washout (cementitious fines) into the water, which can be harmful to aquatic life.
    *   *Mitigation:* Using anti-washout admixtures, proper tremie pipe operation, and containment measures.
*   **Noise and Vibration:** Controlling noise and vibration from construction activities, especially in sensitive marine areas.
*   **Material Disposal:** Proper disposal of any temporary formwork materials or waste products.
*   **Impact on Water Quality:** Monitoring and managing any potential changes in water turbidity or chemistry.

### Answers to Practice Questions

1.  **Explanation of Water Pressure:** Underwater formwork must be designed to withstand the hydrostatic pressure exerted by the column of water surrounding it. This pressure increases with depth and can cause the formwork to buckle or collapse if not adequately designed and braced.
2.  **Tremie Pipe Principle:** The fundamental principle of the tremie pipe method is to keep the discharge end of the pipe continuously submerged within the freshly placed concrete. This prevents the ingress of water into the pipe, thereby preventing the segregation of cement and aggregates and ensuring the placement of a cohesive concrete mass.
3.  **Applications of Underwater Formwork:**
    *   Bridge piers and abutments
    *   Dams and spillways
    *   Offshore structures (e.g., oil rig foundations)
    *   Submerged tunnels and culverts
    *   Marine structures (e.g., caissons, seawalls)
4.  **Advantages of Steel vs. Timber:** Steel offers higher strength and stiffness, is more durable, more watertight (when properly fabricated), and is generally reusable for multiple cycles, making it more economical in the long run for demanding underwater applications. Timber is less durable, more susceptible to deformation, and generally single-use in harsh underwater conditions.
5.  **Role of Anti-Washout Admixtures (AWAs):** AWAs are polymers that increase the viscosity of the concrete mix. This increased viscosity helps to bind the cement particles together and resist dispersion into the surrounding water, preventing the washout of cement and ensuring a more homogeneous and durable concrete structure.
6.  **Inspection and Watertightness Challenges:** Without dewatering, inspection relies on divers, remote-controlled vehicles (ROVs), or sonar. Ensuring watertightness is achieved through meticulous design of joints, the use of robust sealing compounds and gaskets, and pre-pour checks using these specialized methods. Any leak can lead to concrete washout and compromise the structural integrity.
7.  **Environmental Concern and Mitigation:** The primary environmental concern is the potential washout of cementitious materials into the water, which can harm aquatic life by increasing turbidity and altering pH levels. Mitigation measures include using anti-washout admixtures, ensuring proper operation of placement equipment (like tremie pipes), and sometimes using temporary containment systems.
8.  **Permanent vs. Temporary Formwork:**
    *   **Permanent Formwork:** Designed to remain in place permanently as part of the finished structure. *Example:* Geotextile bags filled with grout that become a barrier or foundation element.
    *   **Temporary Formwork:** Designed to be removed after the concrete has hardened. *Example:* Steel panels used to form a concrete pier that are later unbolted and retrieved.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Hydrostatic pressure is the primary design consideration.**
*   **Watertightness is paramount.**
*   **Tremie pipe operation requires strict adherence to keeping the pipe end submerged.**
*   **Anti-washout admixtures (AWAs) are crucial for concrete quality.**
*   **Accessibility limitations necessitate remote or diver-assisted operations.**
*   **Environmental protection is a key aspect of planning and execution.**
*   **Pre-planning and precise execution are critical for success.**
