# Advanced Geotechnical Investigation: Module 2 - Sounding Methods

## Topic: Standard Penetration Test (SPT)

This module focuses on the Standard Penetration Test (SPT), a widely used in-situ testing method for evaluating the geotechnical properties of soil. We will cover its procedure, factors influencing its results, methods to ensure reliability, corrections, correlations with soil properties, and practical field applications.

### 1. Standard Penetration Test (SPT) Procedure

The SPT is a dynamic penetration test that measures the resistance of the soil to penetration by a standard split-spoon sampler driven by a hammer of a specified weight and drop height.

#### Key Concepts:

*   **Split-Spoon Sampler:** A cylindrical steel sampler with a split barrel, designed to collect soil samples.
*   **Drive Weight (Hammer):** A standard hammer, typically weighing 63.5 kg (140 lbs).
*   **Drop Height:** The free fall of the hammer, typically 760 mm (30 inches).
*   **Blow Count (N-value):** The number of blows required to drive the sampler a specified distance.

#### Procedure Steps:

1.  **Borehole Preparation:**
    *   Drill a borehole to the desired depth using a standard rotary drilling method.
    *   Maintain a stable borehole using drilling fluid (mud) or casing to prevent collapse.
    *   The borehole diameter is typically 65 mm to 150 mm.

2.  **Sampler Assembly:**
    *   The split-spoon sampler is attached to the drill rods.
    *   A driving shoe (or tip) is attached to the bottom of the sampler.

3.  **Lowering the Sampler:**
    *   The sampler is lowered to the bottom of the borehole.
    *   The sampler is seated on undisturbed soil by seating blows.

4.  **Driving the Sampler:**
    *   The standard hammer is lifted to the specified drop height (760 mm) and released to strike the anvil on top of the drill rods.
    *   The blows are applied continuously.

5.  **Measuring Blow Counts:**
    *   The number of blows required to drive the sampler the following distances is recorded:
        *   **Initial 150 mm (6 inches):** This is the seating penetration and is *not* counted towards the N-value. This helps overcome any disturbance at the borehole bottom.
        *   **Next 300 mm (12 inches):** The number of blows for this penetration is recorded.
        *   **Final 300 mm (12 inches):** The number of blows for this penetration is recorded.
    *   The **Standard Penetration Resistance (N-value)** is the sum of the blows for the last two 300 mm (12-inch) penetrations.

    *   **Example:** If it takes 10 blows for the first 150mm, 20 blows for the next 300mm, and 25 blows for the final 300mm, the N-value is 20 + 25 = 45.

6.  **Sample Recovery:**
    *   After driving, the sampler is retrieved from the borehole.
    *   The split barrel is separated, and the soil sample is examined and potentially stored for laboratory testing.

7.  **Repetition:**
    *   The SPT is typically performed at regular intervals (e.g., every 1.5 m to 3 m) or at changes in soil strata.

### 2. Factors Influencing SPT Results

The reliability of SPT results is heavily influenced by several factors, both in the testing procedure and the soil conditions.

#### **A. Factors Related to Testing Equipment and Procedure:**

*   **Hammer Type and Energy Transfer:**
    *   **Automatic Trip Hammer:** Generally provides more consistent energy delivery than a manual trip hammer.
    *   **Energy Ratio (E_m):** The ratio of the energy delivered by the hammer to the standard energy. Different hammers and rope/pulley systems have different energy efficiencies.
*   **Drop Height and Hammer Weight:** Deviations from the standard 760 mm drop height and 63.5 kg hammer weight directly affect the energy input.
*   **Drill Rods:**
    *   **Length and Weight:** Longer and heavier rods absorb more energy.
    *   **Type of Connection:** Loose or worn connections can lead to energy loss.
*   **Borehole Size and Condition:**
    *   **Larger Boreholes:** May require more blows due to increased friction and resistance.
    *   **Mud Weight and Viscosity:** High mud density or viscosity can increase resistance.
    *   **Borehole Stability:** Collapsed walls can lead to extra blows to drive the sampler.
*   **Sampler Condition:**
    *   **Dents or Damage:** Can increase penetration resistance.
    *   **Plugged Sampler:** If the sampler gets plugged with clay, it might yield artificially high N-values.
*   **Rate of Driving:** A very rapid rate of driving can lead to "pore water pressure buildup" in fine-grained soils, potentially giving higher N-values than actual.

#### **B. Factors Related to Soil Conditions:**

*   **Soil Type:**
    *   **Cohesionless Soils (Sands and Gravels):** SPT is most reliable in these soils. N-values generally increase with density and grain size.
    *   **Cohesive Soils (Clays):** SPT can be unreliable in clays due to potential for pore pressure buildup and remolding of the sample. N-values in clays are often correlated with undrained shear strength.
*   **Soil Density/Consistency:** Higher density (in sands) or stiffer consistency (in clays) will result in higher N-values.
*   **Water Table Depth and Pore Water Pressure:**
    *   **High Water Table:** Can reduce the effective stress, leading to lower N-values in saturated sands.
*   **Overburden Pressure:** The weight of the overlying soil influences the soil's resistance.
*   **Soil Stratification:** Testing at the interface of different soil layers can give misleading results.
*   **Presence of Cobbles or Boulders:** Can cause the sampler to bounce or deviate, leading to erratic N-values.
*   **Soil Sensitivity:** Highly sensitive clays can lose strength upon disturbance, potentially affecting N-values.

### 3. Precautions to Obtain Reliable SPT Results

Implementing specific precautions during the SPT is crucial for obtaining accurate and representative data.

#### **Key Precautions:**

*   **Use Standardized Equipment:** Ensure the hammer weight, drop height, and split-spoon sampler dimensions conform to ASTM D1586 or equivalent standards.
*   **Maintain Consistent Energy Transfer:** Use a reliable hammer (preferably automatic) and check the condition of drill rods and connections regularly.
*   **Control Borehole Conditions:**
    *   Maintain a clean borehole bottom, free from accumulated cuttings.
    *   Use drilling fluid of appropriate density and viscosity. If the mud is too heavy, it can influence the N-value.
    *   Use casing to support unstable boreholes.
*   **Ensure Proper Sampler Seating:** Allow for the initial 150 mm seating penetration and ensure the sampler is resting on firm ground, not loose fill or accumulated debris.
*   **Minimize Disturbance to Soil:** Avoid excessive over-drilling before lowering the sampler.
*   **Maintain a Consistent Driving Rate:** Aim for a rate of 30-45 blows per minute. Avoid very rapid or very slow driving.
*   **Accurate Blow Count Recording:** Ensure the blow counts for each 150 mm (6 inch) interval are accurately recorded by the field technician.
*   **Record Relevant Data:** Document all critical information:
    *   Borehole number and location.
    *   Depth of test.
    *   Hammer type and drop height.
    *   Drill rod type and number of rods.
    *   Borehole diameter and method.
    *   Mud weight and viscosity (if applicable).
    *   Blow counts for each 150 mm penetration.
    *   Sampler condition.
    *   Water table depth.
    *   Description of soil strata at the test location.
*   **Test at Regular Intervals:** Conduct SPTs at consistent vertical intervals (e.g., every 1.5m or 2m) and at significant changes in soil type.
*   **Verify Water Table:** Measure the water table depth accurately after drilling and stabilization.
*   **Avoid Testing in Cobble/Boulder Zones:** If encountered, carefully try to bypass or note the difficulties.

### 4. Corrections to be Applied to Observed N Values

Raw SPT N-values are often modified to account for various factors that affect the energy delivered and the measured resistance, making them more comparable across different testing conditions.

#### **Key Corrections:**

*   **Correction for Hammer Energy (E_m):** This is the most significant correction.
    *   **Formula:** $N_{60} = N_{raw} \times E_m \times C_m$
        *   $N_{60}$: Standardized SPT N-value (energy corrected).
        *   $N_{raw}$: Observed blow count.
        *   $E_m$: Hammer energy ratio (ratio of delivered energy to standard energy).
            *   **Donut Hammer (not recommended):** $E_m \approx 0.45 - 0.50$
            *   **Safety (Automatic Trip) Hammer:** $E_m \approx 0.60 - 0.80$ (typically around 0.70)
            *   **Japanese Automatic Hammer:** $E_m \approx 0.80$
        *   $C_m$: Correction factor for the sampler (typically $C_m = 1.0$ for standard split-spoon samplers).

    *   **Example:** If $N_{raw} = 30$ and an automatic trip hammer with an energy ratio of 0.75 is used, then $N_{60} = 30 \times 0.75 = 22.5$.

*   **Correction for Borehole Diameter:**
    *   **Formula:** $N_{60} = N_{raw} \times C_{b}$
        *   $C_b$: Correction factor for borehole diameter.
            *   Standard diameter (65-115 mm): $C_b = 1.0$
            *   150 mm diameter borehole: $C_b \approx 0.95 - 1.0$
            *   Larger diameter boreholes: $C_b$ may be lower.

*   **Correction for Rod Length:**
    *   **Formula:** $N_{60} = N_{raw} \times C_{r}$
        *   $C_r$: Correction factor for rod length.
            *   Rods length 4-6 m: $C_r = 1.0$
            *   Rods length 6-10 m: $C_r \approx 1.0$
            *   Rods length 10-20 m: $C_r \approx 1.05$
            *   Rods length > 20 m: $C_r \approx 1.15$

*   **Correction for Sampler Type:**
    *   The standard split-spoon sampler has $C_s = 1.0$. Non-standard samplers require corrections.

*   **Combined Correction (Simplified):**
    *   A common approach is to use the energy correction and then adjust for borehole diameter and rod length if significant. However, the energy correction is paramount.

*   **Correction for Overburden Pressure (for correlations):** While not a correction to the raw N-value itself, when *correlating* N-values to engineering properties, the overburden pressure must be considered. A common correction is:
    *   **Formula:** $N'_{60} = C_N \times N_{60}$
        *   $N'_{60}$: Corrected N-value for overburden pressure.
        *   $C_N$: Correction factor for overburden pressure, which typically decreases with depth. Numerous charts and formulas exist, often based on the effective overburden pressure $(\sigma'_v)$. For example, Peck, Hanson, and Thornburn (1974) suggest $C_N \approx (9.77/\sigma'_v + 1.73)^{-1}$, where $\sigma'_v$ is in kN/m².

#### **Important Note:**

*   The primary goal of corrections is to bring the N-value to a **standardized energy (N60)**.
*   Always use the same correction factors or formulas throughout a project for consistency.
*   The most critical correction is for hammer energy efficiency.

### 5. Correlations of N Value with Various Engineering and Index Properties of Soils

SPT N-values, especially after corrections, can be empirically correlated with various soil properties, allowing engineers to estimate these properties without extensive laboratory testing. These correlations are empirical and site-specific conditions can influence their accuracy.

#### **A. Correlations with Index Properties:**

*   **Relative Density ($D_r$) in Sands:**
    *   $D_r$ indicates the degree of compactness of a sand deposit.
    *   **General Correlation (after Terzaghi & Peck, Peck, Hanson & Thornburn):**
        *   $N < 5$: Very loose
        *   $5 \le N < 15$: Loose
        *   $15 \le N < 30$: Medium dense
        *   $30 \le N < 50$: Dense
        *   $N > 50$: Very dense
    *   **More quantitative correlations exist, e.g., by Meyerhof (1976) or Seed et al. (1986), often using $N_{60}$ corrected for overburden pressure ($N'_{60}$):**
        *   $D_r \approx \sqrt{N'_{60}/15.8}$ (simplified) or using more complex empirical equations.

*   **Consistency of Clays:**
    *   N-values in clays are less reliable but can be correlated with consistency.
    *   $N < 2$: Very soft
    *   $2 \le N < 5$: Soft
    *   $5 \le N < 10$: Medium stiff
    *   $10 \le N < 20$: Stiff
    *   $N > 20$: Very stiff to hard

#### **B. Correlations with Engineering Properties:**

*   **Shear Strength Parameters:**
    *   **Angle of Internal Friction ($\phi$) in Sands:**
        *   Meyerhof (1976) proposed: $\phi = 20^\circ + \sqrt{25N}$ (for $N > 5$, $N$ is average blow count)
        *   Seed et al. (1986) also provided correlations based on $N_{60}$ and relative density.
        *   **Important:** This correlation is sensitive to grain size, angularity, and effective stress.

    *   **Undrained Shear Strength ($c_u$) in Clays:**
        *   In normally consolidated or lightly overconsolidated clays, $c_u$ can be estimated.
        *   **Correlation:** $c_u \approx c \times N$
            *   Where $c$ is a constant (e.g., 1-4 kPa for soft clays, 5-12 kPa for stiff clays).
            *   **Example:** If $N=8$ in a stiff clay, $c_u$ could be estimated as $10 \times 8 = 80$ kPa.
        *   **Note:** This correlation is highly empirical and depends heavily on clay type and plasticity.

*   **Modulus of Elasticity ($E$):**
    *   The stiffness of the soil.
    *   **Correlation:** $E = c_{mod} \times N_{60}$
        *   $c_{mod}$ is a modulus coefficient that varies with soil type and stress.
        *   For sands: $c_{mod}$ can range from 5000 to 18000 kPa.
        *   For clays: $c_{mod}$ can range from 2000 to 6000 kPa.

*   **Bearing Capacity:**
    *   SPT N-values are extensively used in empirical formulas for estimating the bearing capacity of shallow foundations.
    *   **Meyerhof's Bearing Capacity Formula for Sands:**
        *   For square footing: $q_u = 40 N_{avg} D_f (B/D_f)^2$ (for $B/D_f \le 4$) and $q_u = 40 N_{avg} D_f$ (for $B/D_f > 4$)
        *   For strip footing: $q_u = 60 N_{avg} D_f$
        *   Where $q_u$ is ultimate bearing capacity, $N_{avg}$ is the average corrected SPT N-value in the influence zone, $D_f$ is embedment depth, and $B$ is footing width.
        *   **Note:** These are simplified forms, and many variations exist with safety factors.

*   **Settlement Analysis:**
    *   SPT N-values are also used to estimate the settlement of foundations, particularly in sands, by correlating them with soil compressibility.

#### **Important Considerations for Correlations:**

*   **Empirical Nature:** These correlations are based on observed data and can have significant scatter.
*   **Site Specificity:** Correlations derived for one geological region or soil type may not be directly applicable to another. It's best to calibrate correlations with local experience.
*   **Correction Factors:** Use corrected N-values ($N_{60}$ and $N'_{60}$) for correlations whenever possible.
*   **Soil Type:** The reliability of correlations varies significantly between cohesionless and cohesive soils.
*   **Overburden Pressure:** Crucial for correlations involving strength and stiffness.
*   **Laboratory vs. Field:** Field correlations should be used cautiously and ideally validated with laboratory tests.

### 6. Field Study from Sites - Field Visit and Analysis of Data

A crucial part of advanced geotechnical investigation is applying the knowledge gained from tests like the SPT to real-world projects and analyzing the data collected.

#### **A. Planning a Field Visit/Study:**

1.  **Objective Definition:** Clearly define what needs to be achieved during the visit (e.g., assess soil conditions for a proposed building, evaluate a slope for stability, investigate a foundation settlement issue).
2.  **Site Reconnaissance:**
    *   **Visual Inspection:** Observe topography, existing structures, drainage patterns, evidence of past landslides, vegetation, and any visible soil types.
    *   **Review Existing Data:** Gather geological maps, previous geotechnical reports, aerial photographs, and utility information.
3.  **Sampling and Testing Plan:**
    *   **Number and Location of Boreholes:** Determine the optimal placement and density of boreholes based on the project scope and expected variability.
    *   **Test Depths:** Decide how deep boreholes need to be drilled to reach competent strata or investigate the relevant influence zone of a foundation.
    *   **SPT Interval:** Specify the depth interval for performing SPTs (e.g., every 1.5m, or at changes in strata).
    *   **Other Tests:** Plan for other in-situ tests (e.g., CPT, vane shear) and laboratory tests (e.g., Atterberg limits, shear tests, consolidation tests) to complement SPT data.
4.  **Logistics and Safety:**
    *   Arrange for drilling equipment, personnel, and necessary permits.
    *   Ensure adherence to all safety protocols on-site.

#### **B. Conducting the Field Work (SPT Execution):**

*   **Supervision:** A qualified geotechnical engineer or technician should supervise the SPT operations to ensure adherence to the procedure and to identify any anomalies.
*   **Real-time Data Recording:** Crucial data (as listed in precautions) should be recorded meticulously at the time of testing.
*   **On-site Observations:** Note any unusual observations during drilling or sampling (e.g., washouts, presence of gas, water inflow).
*   **Sample Examination:** Briefly examine recovered samples on-site for visual classification (e.g., soil type, color, presence of gravel/cobbles, moisture content).

#### **C. Field Data Analysis:**

1.  **Data Compilation:** Organize all SPT logs, sample descriptions, and other relevant field data into a structured format.
2.  **Quality Control:** Review the field logs for completeness and consistency. Check for any potential errors in recording blow counts or other parameters.
3.  **Correction Application:** Apply appropriate corrections to the raw SPT N-values ($N_{60}$, $N'_{60}$) based on the equipment used and overburden pressure.
4.  **Soil Stratigraphy Development:**
    *   Combine SPT data with visual classification of samples and other in-situ test results to create a detailed soil profile for each borehole.
    *   Draw borehole logs clearly showing depth, soil strata, N-values, sample recovery, and water levels.
5.  **Correlation and Interpretation:**
    *   Use the corrected N-values to estimate engineering properties (e.g., $D_r$, $\phi$, $c_u$, $E$) using established correlations.
    *   Critically assess the applicability of these correlations to the specific site conditions. Consider local experience if available.
6.  **Groundwater Analysis:** Analyze the groundwater levels recorded in the boreholes.
7.  **Identification of Problematic Zones:** Highlight areas with low N-values (indicating soft or loose soils), erratic N-values, or potential liquefaction susceptibility (in sands).
8.  **Foundation Design Parameters:** Based on the analyzed data, establish design parameters for foundations, slopes, earthworks, etc.
9.  **Reporting:** Prepare a geotechnical investigation report summarizing the methodology, findings, interpreted soil properties, and recommendations for design and construction.

#### **Example of Field Data Analysis:**

**Scenario:** Investigating the bearing capacity for a proposed 10m x 10m footing for a 3-story building on a site with layered sands.

*   **Field Work:** Conducted 4 boreholes to 15m depth, with SPTs performed every 1.5m.
*   **Collected Data (Hypothetical):**
    *   **Borehole 1:**
        *   0-2m: Fine sand, loose, $N$ values (0.5m intervals): 4, 6, 8
        *   2-7m: Medium sand, medium dense, $N$ values: 12, 15, 18, 20, 22
        *   7-12m: Medium sand, dense, $N$ values: 28, 30, 33, 35, 38
        *   12-15m: Coarse sand with gravel, very dense, $N$ values: 50, 55, 60
    *   Water table at 3m depth.
    *   Used an automatic trip hammer with $E_m \approx 0.75$.

*   **Analysis:**
    1.  **Correction:** Calculate $N_{60}$ for each reading (e.g., for $N=18$ at 3.5m, $N_{60} = 18 \times 0.75 = 13.5$).
    2.  **Overburden Correction:** Calculate $N'_{60}$ for each $N_{60}$ value using a suitable correlation for overburden pressure.
    3.  **Average $N'_{60}$:** Determine the average $N'_{60}$ value in the influence zone of the footing (typically 1.5B to 2B below the foundation base, so for a 10m footing, investigate down to ~15-20m). Let's assume the average $N'_{60}$ in the critical zone (say, 2m to 12m) is 25.
    4.  **Correlation:**
        *   Estimate relative density: With $N'_{60} = 25$, the sand is likely medium dense to dense.
        *   Estimate $\phi$: Using a correlation like $\phi = 20^\circ + \sqrt{25N'_{60}} = 20^\circ + \sqrt{25 \times 25} = 20^\circ + 25^\circ = 45^\circ$. (This seems high, possibly indicating a need for a more conservative correlation or site-specific calibration). A more typical range for dense sand might be $\phi = 35^\circ - 40^\circ$.
        *   Estimate bearing capacity: Using Meyerhof's formula for a 10m x 10m footing with $D_f = 2m$ and $N_{avg}$ (e.g., representing $N'_{60}$ appropriately for bearing capacity) and $\phi$.

### Answers to Practice Questions

1.  **Procedure:**
    *   Drill borehole to desired depth.
    *   Lower the standard split-spoon sampler.
    *   Drive the sampler with a 63.5 kg hammer falling from 760 mm.
    *   Record blows for initial 150 mm penetration (seating), next 300 mm, and final 300 mm.
    *   The N-value is the sum of blows for the last two 300 mm penetrations.
    *   **Significance of initial 150 mm:** To overcome any disturbance at the borehole bottom (e.g., loose cuttings) and seat the sampler properly, ensuring the subsequent blows represent penetration into the undisturbed soil.
2.  **Factors:**
    *   **Hammer Energy Efficiency:** Different hammers deliver different amounts of energy.
    *   **Borehole Diameter:** Larger boreholes can create more resistance.
    *   **Rod Length:** Longer rods absorb more energy.
    *   **Water Table:** High water tables reduce effective stress, influencing resistance.
    *   **Drilling Fluid Properties:** Density and viscosity of drilling mud.
    *   *(Any three of these or similar valid factors)*
3.  **Precautions:**
    *   **Accurate Blow Count Recording:** Ensure the blows for each 150mm interval are precisely recorded.
    *   **Consistent Energy Delivery:** Verify the hammer is falling from the correct height and the equipment is in good condition.
    *   **Clean Borehole Bottom:** Ensure the sampler is seated on undisturbed soil, not debris.
    *   **Proper Hammer Operation:** Ensure the hammer is properly seated on the anvil before release.
    *   *(Any two of these or similar valid precautions)*
4.  **Corrections:**
    *   $N_{60} = N_{raw} \times E_m$
    *   $N_{60} = 40 \times 0.5 = 20$
    *   *(Note: The borehole diameter correction is usually minor for 100mm, often $C_b \approx 1.0$, so $N_{60}$ is usually considered the primary correction for energy.)*
5.  **Correlations:**
    *   A "loose" deposit of sand typically corresponds to SPT N-values generally between 5 and 15 (or sometimes up to 10).
    *   In cohesive soils (clays), SPT N-value is most often correlated with **undrained shear strength ($c_u$)** and **consistency**.
6.  **Field Analysis:**
    *   **Density:** An average $N'_{60}$ of 35 in sand indicates a **dense** deposit.
    *   **Angle of Internal Friction:** Using a simplified correlation like $\phi = 20^\circ + \sqrt{25N'_{60}}$: $\phi = 20^\circ + \sqrt{25 \times 35} = 20^\circ + \sqrt{875} \approx 20^\circ + 29.6^\circ \approx 49.6^\circ$. However, it's crucial to acknowledge that such high values are unusual and may require more conservative empirical relationships or site-specific calibration. A more typical range for dense sands would be around $35^\circ - 40^\circ$.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **SPT is empirical:** Results are best used for comparative purposes and for estimating properties via empirical correlations.
*   **Corrections are essential:** Standardize N-values to $N_{60}$ and consider overburden effects ($N'_{60}$) for reliable correlations.
*   **Site-specific calibration:** Local experience and laboratory testing are vital to validate SPT correlations.
*   **Document everything:** Meticulous record-keeping in the field is paramount.
*   **Understand limitations:** SPT is less reliable in very soft clays, highly organic soils, or soils with large cobbles/boulders.
