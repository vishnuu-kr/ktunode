# Advanced Geotechnical Investigation - Module 1: Planning an Investigation Programme

## Topic: Methods of Exploration and Boring

This module introduces fundamental methods used in geotechnical investigations to understand subsurface soil and rock conditions. Effective planning of an investigation program is crucial for successful and economical geotechnical design.

### Learning Outcomes:

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

1.  **Differentiate between various methods of subsurface exploration.**
2.  **Describe the purpose and application of open pits, trenches, shafts, and boreholes.**
3.  **Explain the principles and techniques of different boring methods.**
4.  **Identify the advantages and disadvantages of each exploration and boring method.**
5.  **Select appropriate exploration and boring methods based on project requirements and subsurface conditions.**

---

### 1. Methods of Subsurface Exploration

Subsurface exploration aims to determine the physical characteristics of the soil and rock strata underlying a site. This information is vital for foundation design, slope stability analysis, excavation planning, and other geotechnical applications.

#### 1.1. Open Pits and Trenches

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Open Pits (or Excavations):** Large excavations that allow for direct visual inspection of soil and rock layers over a significant area and depth.
*   **Trenches (or Test Pits):** Narrower, shallower excavations compared to open pits, typically used for observing shallow soil profiles.

**Purpose and Application:**
*   **Direct Visual Inspection:** Allows for detailed observation of soil stratigraphy, layering, color, texture, moisture content, and presence of groundwater.
*   **Sampling:** Facilitates the collection of large, undisturbed soil samples, which are crucial for laboratory testing.
*   **In-situ Testing:** Enables various in-situ tests to be performed directly within the excavated material.
*   **Shallow Investigations:** Particularly useful for shallow foundations, road construction, utility trenches, and basement excavations.

**Advantages:**
*   Provides high-quality visual information and allows for direct observation of soil conditions.
*   Facilitates the collection of large, relatively undisturbed samples.
*   Enables in-situ testing under natural conditions.
*   Relatively simple and cost-effective for shallow depths.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Limited Depth:** Practical limitations on depth due to stability of excavation walls, dewatering requirements, and cost.
*   **Site Disturbance:** Significant surface disturbance.
*   **Safety Hazards:** Requires stringent safety measures to prevent collapses and falls.
*   **Groundwater Issues:** Can be problematic in areas with high groundwater tables, requiring extensive dewatering.
*   **Limited Lateral Extent:** The information obtained is localized to the pit/trench area.

**Examples:**
*   **Construction of a building foundation:** Digging test pits to examine the soil layers for bearing capacity.
*   **Highway embankment construction:** Trenches excavated along the proposed alignment to check for suitable fill material and compressible soils.
*   **Underground utility installation:** Excavating trenches to verify existing subsurface utilities and soil conditions.

#### 1.2. Shafts

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Shafts:** Vertical or near-vertical excavations of significant depth and cross-sectional area, allowing access for detailed examination and testing of subsurface materials.

**Purpose and Application:**
*   **Deep Visual Inspection:** Provides direct access to deep soil and rock strata for visual inspection.
*   **Extensive Sampling:** Allows for the collection of large, undisturbed samples from greater depths than open pits.
*   **In-situ Testing:** Enables a wide range of in-situ tests to be performed in a controlled environment.
*   **Underground Structures:** Essential for investigations related to tunnels, underground power stations, and deep foundations.
*   **Difficult Ground Conditions:** Can be used when borehole methods are insufficient or infeasible.

**Advantages:**
*   Provides excellent access for visual inspection and detailed examination of deep formations.
*   Allows for comprehensive sampling and in-situ testing at depth.
*   Can penetrate through problematic layers to reach competent strata.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Extremely Expensive:** Significantly more costly than boreholes or shallow excavations due to the volume of excavation, support, and personnel required.
*   **Time-Consuming:** Takes considerable time to construct.
*   **Safety Hazards:** High safety risks associated with deep excavations, including collapses, ventilation issues, and worker access.
*   **Groundwater Management:** Requires significant dewatering and support systems if groundwater is encountered.
*   **Limited Lateral Extent:** Like pits and trenches, the information is localized.

**Examples:**
*   **Investigating the site for a major underground railway station.**
*   **Assessing rock conditions for a tunnel boring machine start point.**
*   **Geotechnical investigations for a deep mine shaft.**

#### 1.3. Boreholes

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Boreholes:** Cylindrical holes drilled into the ground to obtain subsurface information, primarily through drilling, sampling, and in-situ testing.

**Purpose and Application:**
*   **Subsurface Profiling:** To obtain a continuous or semi-continuous record of the subsurface soil and rock strata.
*   **Sampling:** To collect soil and rock samples for laboratory testing.
*   **In-situ Testing:** To perform various tests directly within the borehole (e.g., SPT, CPT, Vane Shear Test).
*   **Groundwater Monitoring:** To install piezometers for groundwater level monitoring.
*   **Foundation Investigations:** The most common method for site investigations for buildings, bridges, dams, and other structures.

**Advantages:**
*   **Versatility:** Can be drilled to great depths.
*   **Cost-Effective (relatively):** Generally more cost-effective than shafts or open pits for deep investigations.
*   **Minimal Surface Disturbance:** Less disruptive to the site compared to excavations.
*   **Access to Remote Areas:** Can be performed in various locations, including water.
*   **Continuous Data:** Provides a continuous log of subsurface materials encountered.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Indirect Visual Inspection:** Relies on samples and testing, not direct visual observation of large sections.
*   **Disturbed Samples:** Samples obtained can be disturbed to varying degrees depending on the sampling method.
*   **Limited In-situ Testing in Borehole:** While some tests are possible, the options might be more limited than in an open excavation.
*   **Difficulty in Certain Strata:** Can be challenging to drill through very hard rock or very soft, unstable soils without specialized equipment.

**Examples:**
*   **Foundation investigation for a high-rise building:** Drilling boreholes to determine the depth to bedrock or a suitable bearing stratum.
*   **Slope stability analysis:** Drilling boreholes to collect soil samples and perform in-situ tests to assess shear strength parameters.
*   **Tunnel alignment investigation:** Drilling boreholes ahead of the tunnel face to identify potential geological hazards.

### 2. Methods of Boring

Boring is the process of drilling a hole into the ground. The method chosen depends on the soil type, depth, required sample quality, and budget.

#### 2.1. Auger Boring

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Auger Boring:** A manual or mechanical method where a rotating helical screw (auger) penetrates the soil, bringing samples to the surface.

**Types of Augers:**
*   **Hand Augers (Post Hole Augers):** Used for shallow depths (typically up to 5-10 meters) and soft to stiff soils.
*   **Power Augers (Rotary Augers):** Mechanized augers capable of drilling to greater depths and through a wider range of soil types, including some softer rock.
*   **Continuous Flight Augers:** Long, helical flights that continuously remove soil as they penetrate.
*   **Discontinuous Flight Augers:** Augers with short flights, used for obtaining samples or for drilling through harder materials.

**Purpose and Application:**
*   **Shallow Investigations:** Primarily for soil profiling and sampling in shallow depths.
*   **Preliminary Investigations:** Useful for quick, preliminary assessments of soil conditions.
*   **Environmental Sampling:** Used for soil and groundwater sampling.

**Advantages:**
*   **Simple and Relatively Inexpensive:** Easy to operate and cost-effective for shallow depths.
*   **Quick Soil Profiling:** Provides a continuous sample for visual logging.
*   **Minimal Site Disturbance:** Less disruptive than open excavations.
*   **No Need for Drilling Fluid:** Does not require drilling mud or water, simplifying the process.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Limited Depth:** Primarily suitable for shallow to moderate depths.
*   **Limited Sample Quality:** Samples can be disturbed, especially in saturated or loose soils.
*   **Difficult in Hard Materials:** Ineffective in hard soils, dense gravel, or rock.
*   **Difficult with High Groundwater:** Can be challenging to maintain borehole stability in saturated conditions.

**Examples:**
*   **Geotechnical investigation for a residential foundation in sandy soil.**
*   **Sampling soil for agricultural or environmental assessments.**
*   **Installing shallow monitoring wells.**

#### 2.2. Wash Boring (or Mud Rotary Drilling)

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Wash Boring:** A method where a rotating hollow drill stem with a cutting bit at the end is advanced into the soil. Water or a drilling fluid is pumped down the drill stem, exiting through the bit and flushing loosened soil particles to the surface via the annular space between the drill stem and the borehole wall.

**Process:**
1.  A drill bit is attached to a hollow drill rod.
2.  Water or drilling fluid (mud) is pumped down the drill rod.
3.  The fluid exits through openings in the drill bit, eroding and lifting the soil cuttings to the surface.
4.  The cuttings are collected in a settling tank or sump and the fluid is recirculated.
5.  Samples are collected from the flushed cuttings or by driving a sampler into the base of the borehole.

**Purpose and Application:**
*   **General Soil Investigations:** A common method for drilling boreholes in a wide range of soil types.
*   **Sampling:** Used to collect disturbed and sometimes relatively undisturbed samples.
*   **Penetration:** Can penetrate most soils and soft to medium-hard rocks.

**Advantages:**
*   **Relatively Fast:** Can be faster than percussion drilling in many soil types.
*   **Can Drill to Significant Depths:** Suitable for deeper investigations.
*   **Maintains Borehole Stability:** The drilling fluid helps to stabilize the borehole walls, especially in unstable soils.
*   **Cuttings Removal:** Efficiently removes cuttings from the borehole.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Disturbs Soil Structure:** The washing action significantly disturbs the natural soil structure, making samples highly disturbed.
*   **Sample Recovery Issues:** Difficult to recover true undisturbed samples.
*   **Groundwater Contamination:** The drilling fluid can potentially contaminate groundwater.
*   **Identification of Strata:** Identifying precise changes in soil layers can be challenging due to the mixing of cuttings.
*   **Inefficient in Very Hard Rock:** Can be slow and inefficient in very hard or fractured rock.

**Examples:**
*   **Investigating a site for a multi-story building where SPT is required.**
*   **Drilling boreholes for foundation piles where soil samples are needed for laboratory testing.**
*   **Installing piezometers to monitor groundwater levels.**

#### 2.3. Percussion Drilling (Cable Tool Drilling)

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Percussion Drilling:** A method where a heavy chisel-like bit is repeatedly dropped onto the ground from a height, breaking up the soil and rock through impact. The loosened material is then removed, typically by bailing.

**Process:**
1.  A heavy, sharp-edged bit is suspended from a cable or rope.
2.  The bit is repeatedly lifted and dropped, shattering the subsurface material.
3.  A bailer (a hollow cylinder with a flap valve at the bottom) is lowered to collect the loosened debris.
4.  The bailer is then raised to the surface to empty the contents.
5.  Water is often added to help in the pulverization and removal of cuttings.

**Purpose and Application:**
*   **Hard Soil and Rock:** Effective in drilling through hard soils, boulders, and consolidated rock formations.
*   **Well Drilling:** Historically used for water well drilling.
*   **Deep Investigations:** Can reach considerable depths.

**Advantages:**
*   **Effective in Hard Formations:** Excellent for drilling through hard, dense, or fractured materials.
*   **Good for Rock Coring:** Can be adapted for obtaining rock cores.
*   **Simple Equipment:** Relatively simple drilling rig and tools.
*   **Borehole Stability:** The process generally maintains good borehole stability.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Slow and Laborious:** Significantly slower than rotary drilling.
*   **Significant Vibration:** Can cause vibrations, which might be undesirable near existing structures.
*   **Disturbed Samples:** Samples recovered are often significantly disturbed due to the crushing action.
*   **Difficult in Soft Soils:** Not ideal for very soft or cohesionless soils where the bit can get stuck.
*   **Limited Use of Drilling Fluids:** Primarily relies on water, which can lead to issues with mud formation in certain strata.

**Examples:**
*   **Drilling for water wells in bedrock.**
*   **Investigating a quarry site for rock quality.**
*   **Drilling for anchor bolts in rock slopes.**

#### 2.4. Rotary Drilling

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Rotary Drilling:** A method where a rotating drill bit, attached to a drill string, cuts into the ground. A drilling fluid (mud or water) is circulated to cool the bit, flush cuttings, and stabilize the borehole.

**Types of Rotary Drilling:**
*   **Rotary Wash Boring:** (Already described under Wash Boring)
*   **Rotary Core Drilling:** Uses a hollow, cylindrical drill bit (core barrel) with diamond or carbide cutters to extract intact cylindrical samples of rock or soil (core samples).
*   **Rotary Open Hole Drilling:** Uses various types of bits (roller bits, drag bits) to advance the borehole without necessarily obtaining continuous cores.

**Process (Rotary Core Drilling):**
1.  A core barrel with a cutting bit is attached to a drill rod.
2.  The drill string is rotated at high speed.
3.  Water or drilling mud is pumped down the drill string to lubricate and cool the bit and flush out cuttings.
4.  As the bit cuts into the rock, the material is retained in the core barrel.
5.  Periodically, the drill string is pulled out to recover the core sample.

**Purpose and Application:**
*   **Obtaining Undisturbed Rock Cores:** The primary method for obtaining high-quality rock samples for laboratory testing.
*   **Deep Investigations:** Can reach very great depths.
*   **Hard Rock Penetration:** Highly effective in hard and abrasive rock formations.
*   **Soil Coring:** Can be adapted for obtaining relatively undisturbed soil samples in some conditions.

**Advantages:**
*   **High-Quality Samples:** Excellent for obtaining intact rock cores.
*   **Fast Penetration in Rock:** Very efficient in drilling through rock.
*   **Good Borehole Stability:** Drilling fluid aids in borehole stability.
*   **Versatile:** Can be used for both soil and rock investigations with appropriate bits and techniques.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Expensive:** Generally the most expensive drilling method due to specialized equipment and skilled operators.
*   **Requires Drilling Fluid:** Requires careful management of drilling fluid.
*   **Potential for Core Loss:** In fractured or highly weathered rock, core loss can occur.
*   **Less Efficient in Soft Soils:** Can be less efficient in very soft or unconsolidated soils if not adapted properly.

**Examples:**
*   **Investigating a dam site for rock mass properties.**
*   **Core drilling for tunnels in hard rock.**
*   **Geotechnical investigations for high-rise buildings on rock.**
*   **Mineral exploration drilling.**

### 4. Factors Influencing Method Selection

The choice of exploration and boring methods depends on several factors:

*   **Project Requirements:** The type and scale of the project (e.g., a small residential building versus a major infrastructure project).
*   **Geological Conditions:** The expected soil and rock types, stratigraphy, presence of groundwater, and potential hazards (e.g., boulders, soft clays).
*   **Required Information:** The type and quality of data needed (e.g., visual observation, laboratory testing of samples, in-situ strength parameters).
*   **Depth of Investigation:** The depth to which the subsurface needs to be investigated.
*   **Budget:** The financial resources available for the investigation.
*   **Time Constraints:** The project schedule and the time available for the investigation.
*   **Site Constraints:** Accessibility of the site, presence of existing structures, environmental considerations.
*   **Safety:** The inherent risks associated with each method and the site conditions.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
You are investigating a site for a new multi-story office building. The project manager requires detailed information on the bearing capacity of the soil and wants to obtain undisturbed samples for laboratory testing. The estimated depth of investigation is 30 meters. Which primary boring method would be most suitable, and why?

**Answer:**
Rotary drilling, specifically rotary core drilling if bedrock is expected at a significant depth, or rotary wash boring followed by specialized sampling techniques (like Thin-walled tube sampling) in soil strata, would be most suitable.
*   **Reasoning:** Rotary drilling can reach the required depth, and techniques exist for obtaining relatively undisturbed samples in soil (e.g., tube sampling) and excellent intact core samples in rock. Wash boring is efficient for penetration and allows for SPT testing, which is standard for assessing bearing capacity. The ability to obtain samples for laboratory testing is crucial for this project.

**Question 2:**
Describe a scenario where an open pit excavation would be preferred over boreholes for a geotechnical investigation.

**Answer:**
An open pit excavation would be preferred when investigating the suitability of a soil layer for shallow foundations, such as for a small residential building or a garden patio, where the depth of interest is less than 2-3 meters.
*   **Reasoning:** In such cases, the cost-effectiveness and direct visual inspection capabilities of an open pit provide superior information for assessing near-surface soil conditions, identifying roots, organic matter, and precise layering, which might be missed or poorly represented by disturbed samples from boreholes. It also allows for easy collection of large, undisturbed samples for simple tests.

**Question 3:**
What are the main advantages and disadvantages of percussion drilling compared to rotary drilling?

**Answer:**
*   **Advantages of Percussion Drilling:**
    *   More effective in hard soils, boulders, and consolidated rock.
    *   Simpler equipment.
    *   Good for well drilling.
*   **Disadvantages of Percussion Drilling:**
    *   Significantly slower than rotary drilling.
    *   Causes more vibration.
    *   Samples are typically more disturbed.
    *   Less efficient in soft soils.

**Question 4:**
Explain the role of drilling fluid in wash boring and rotary drilling.

**Answer:**
In both wash boring and rotary drilling, the drilling fluid (usually a bentonite-based mud or water) serves multiple critical functions:
1.  **Cuttings Removal:** It flushes the loosened soil or rock particles away from the cutting bit and up to the surface through the annular space.
2.  **Borehole Stabilization:** It forms a filter cake on the borehole walls, preventing collapse, especially in unconsolidated or unstable soils.
3.  **Cooling and Lubrication:** It cools the drill bit and bit assembly, reducing wear and increasing drilling efficiency.
4.  **Hydrostatic Pressure:** It maintains hydrostatic pressure within the borehole, helping to counteract formation pressures and prevent blowouts.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **No single method is universally best.** The selection of exploration and boring methods is a critical planning step based on project needs, site conditions, and budget.
*   **Direct observation (pits, shafts) provides the highest quality visual data but is limited in depth and costly.**
*   **Boreholes are versatile and cost-effective for deeper investigations, but rely on samples and in-situ tests for information.**
*   **Sample disturbance is a key consideration.** Auger boring and wash boring generally produce more disturbed samples than careful rotary core drilling.
*   **The purpose of the investigation dictates the choice of method.** Is it for shallow foundations, deep foundations, tunnels, or slope stability?
*   **Always consider safety and environmental implications** when planning and executing exploration programs.
