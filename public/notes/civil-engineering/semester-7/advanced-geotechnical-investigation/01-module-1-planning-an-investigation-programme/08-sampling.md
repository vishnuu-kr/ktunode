# Advanced Geotechnical Investigation: Module 1 - Planning an Investigation Programme

## Topic: Sampling

### 1. Introduction to Sampling in Geotechnical Investigations

Sampling is a fundamental aspect of geotechnical investigations. It involves extracting representative samples of soil and rock from the ground to allow for laboratory testing and analysis. The quality and representativeness of these samples directly impact the reliability of the geotechnical design and the overall success of a project.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Representativeness:** The degree to which a sample accurately reflects the properties of the in-situ soil or rock mass.
*   **Disturbance:** Alteration of the soil or rock fabric and stress state during the sampling process.
*   **Soil vs. Rock Sampling:** Distinct methods are required due to the different properties and behavior of soil and rock.
*   **Purpose of Sampling:**
    *   Laboratory testing (physical properties, strength, compressibility).
    *   Visual classification and description.
    *   Documentation of subsurface conditions.
    *   Verification of initial assumptions.

**Important Point to Remember:** The goal of sampling is to obtain samples that are as undisturbed as possible, allowing for the determination of the original in-situ properties of the soil or rock.

### 2. Learning Outcomes and Key Concepts

#### 2.1. To understand the importance of sampling in a geotechnical investigation programme.

*   **Foundation of Geotechnical Design:** Laboratory test results from samples are crucial inputs for all geotechnical design calculations (e.g., bearing capacity, settlement, slope stability).
*   **Understanding In-situ Conditions:** Samples provide direct evidence of soil/rock types, layering, and variations that might not be apparent from other investigation methods.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Obtaining good quality samples helps reduce uncertainty and the risk of unexpected ground conditions leading to project delays or failures.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** While good sampling can be costly, it is generally far more cost-effective than dealing with unforeseen ground problems during construction.

#### 2.2. To identify and differentiate between disturbed and undisturbed samples.

**Disturbed Samples:**

*   **Definition:** Samples that have undergone significant alteration in their natural structure, fabric, and/or stress history during the sampling process.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Soil structure (e.g., particle arrangement, layering, presence of fissures) is destroyed.
    *   Water content may change.
    *   Stress history can be altered.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   Identification and classification tests (e.g., sieve analysis, Atterberg limits).
    *   Determination of basic physical properties.
    *   Can be useful for preliminary identification, but not for strength or compressibility testing.
*   **Sampling Methods (Examples):**
    *   **Auger Sampling:** Using hand or powered augers, often resulting in highly disturbed material.
    *   **Grab Samples:** Collected from the surface or open excavations, typically highly disturbed.
    *   **Split-Spoon Sampler (SPT):** While primarily used for in-situ testing, the recovered material is considered disturbed.

**Undisturbed Samples:**

*   **Definition:** Samples that retain as much as possible of their natural structure, fabric, and in-situ stress conditions.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Preservation of soil fabric (e.g., stratification, particle orientation, void structure).
    *   Maintained water content.
    *   Minimally altered stress history.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   Strength tests (e.g., triaxial compression, direct shear).
    *   Consolidation tests (settlement prediction).
    *   Permeability tests (where fabric is critical).
    *   Detailed microstructural analysis.
*   **Sampling Methods (Examples):**
    *   **Thin-walled (Shelby) Tube Samplers:** Driven or pushed into the soil, minimizing disturbance.
    *   **Piston Samplers:** Use a piston to create a vacuum and reduce friction during extraction, ideal for soft soils.
    *   **Block Samples:** Cut manually from exposed faces in excavations, allowing for very high-quality sampling of specific layers.
    *   **Rock Core Sampling:** Achieved using rotary drilling with core barrels.

**Example:** Imagine trying to measure the stiffness of a delicate sponge. If you squeeze it vigorously and tear it apart (disturbed), you'll get a poor indication of its original springiness. If you carefully extract a section without deforming it (undisturbed), you can accurately measure its stiffness.

#### 2.3. To describe various types of soil sampling techniques and their suitability for different soil types and investigation objectives.

**Categorization of Techniques:**

*   **Based on Sample Disturbance:** (As discussed above: disturbed vs. undisturbed)
*   **Based on Method of Installation:**
    *   **Driven Samplers:** Hammered into the ground (e.g., Split-Spoon, thin-walled tubes driven by hammer).
    *   **Pushed Samplers:** Slowly pushed into the ground (e.g., thin-walled tubes pushed by hydraulic jack, piston samplers).
    *   **Rotary Core Samplers:** Used for rock, involve drilling and extracting a core.

**Common Soil Sampling Techniques:**

*   **Split-Spoon Sampler (SPT Sampler):**
    *   **Description:** A split tube sampler driven by a standard drop hammer.
    *   **Suitability:** Primarily used for Standard Penetration Test (SPT) for N-value measurement. Recovered soil is **disturbed**. Suitable for most soil types, from soft clays to dense sands.
    *   **Limitations:** High disturbance, especially in softer soils. Sample recovery can be poor in very loose sands or very stiff clays.

*   **Thin-Walled Tube Samplers (Shelby Tubes):**
    *   **Description:** Seamless steel tubes with a sharp cutting edge at the tip. Can be pushed or driven.
    *   **Suitability:** Primarily for **undisturbed** sampling. Excellent for cohesive soils (clays and silts), especially soft to stiff ones. Lower disturbance than SPT samplers.
    *   **Limitations:** Can deform or buckle in dense sands or gravels. Can be difficult to recover in soils with cobbles or boulders.

*   **Piston Samplers:**
    *   **Description:** Incorporates a piston that remains stationary at the bottom of the borehole while the tube is pushed down, creating a vacuum and reducing friction.
    *   **Suitability:** For obtaining highly **undisturbed** samples, especially in soft to firm cohesive soils and sensitive clays.
    *   **Limitations:** More complex and expensive than thin-walled tubes. Not suitable for dense soils or sands.

*   **Block Sampling:**
    *   **Description:** Manual excavation of large, cubical blocks of soil from exposed faces.
    *   **Suitability:** For obtaining the highest quality **undisturbed** samples, particularly for detailed structural analysis or when very specific layers need to be preserved. Suitable for cohesive soils.
    *   **Limitations:** Only possible in excavations (e.g., trenches, shafts) and is labor-intensive and expensive. Limited to accessible depths.

*   **Augers (Hand Augers, Power Augers):**
    *   **Description:** Spiral augers that excavate and bring soil to the surface.
    *   **Suitability:** Primarily for obtaining **disturbed** samples for preliminary identification and classification. Useful for shallow investigations where precise sampling is not critical.
    *   **Limitations:** High degree of disturbance. Can mix soil layers. Limited depth.

**Example:** For a project requiring precise settlement analysis of a soft clay layer, pushing thin-walled tubes or using a piston sampler would be the preferred method to obtain undisturbed samples. For a preliminary assessment of granular fill, SPT sampling might be sufficient.

#### 2.4. To describe various types of rock sampling techniques and their suitability for different rock types and investigation objectives.

**Rock Sampling Techniques:**

*   **Rotary Core Drilling:**
    *   **Description:** The most common method. A rotating cutting bit on a core barrel drills into the rock, extracting a cylindrical core.
    *   **Types of Coring:**
        *   **Single Tube Core Barrels:** Simple, but can cause significant sample disturbance and loss, especially in fractured or weathered rock.
        *   **Double Tube Core Barrels (Inner/Outer):** The inner tube rotates with the bit, while the outer tube remains stationary, significantly reducing sample disturbance and improving core recovery. **Standard for geotechnical investigations.**
        *   **Triple Tube Core Barrels:** Similar to double tube but with an additional stationary liner inside the inner tube, offering the highest level of sample preservation. Used for very sensitive or fractured rock.
    *   **Suitability:** Applicable to a wide range of rock types, from weathered to hard, intact rock. Used for obtaining **undisturbed** rock samples.
    *   **Objectives:** Rock mass characterization, strength testing, weathering assessment, identification of discontinuities.
    *   **Core Catchers:** Devices at the bottom of the core barrel to retain the core as it's brought to the surface.

*   **Cable Tool Drilling (Percussion Drilling):**
    *   **Description:** A heavy bit is repeatedly dropped onto the rock, breaking it up. The debris is then bailed to the surface.
    *   **Suitability:** Primarily for obtaining **disturbed** samples (cuttings) for identification and preliminary assessment.
    *   **Limitations:** Provides no structural information. Not suitable for strength or deformation testing.

*   **Rock Hand-Cuts/Block Samples:**
    *   **Description:** Similar to soil block sampling, but requires specialized tools (rock saws, chisels) to extract intact blocks of rock, usually from exposed faces or shafts.
    *   **Suitability:** For obtaining very high-quality, virtually **undisturbed** samples of specific rock features or intact rock specimens for detailed laboratory testing.
    *   **Limitations:** Only feasible in accessible exposures. Labor-intensive.

**Example:** To assess the compressive strength of a granitic rock for a tunnel foundation, double-tube rotary core drilling would be the standard method. If investigating a highly fractured shale where structural fabric is crucial, a triple-tube core barrel or rock hand-cuts might be considered.

#### 2.5. To understand the factors influencing the choice of sampling method.

**Key Factors:**

*   **Soil/Rock Type:**
    *   **Cohesive Soils (Clays, Silts):** Piston samplers, thin-walled tubes are good for undisturbed. SPT for disturbed.
    *   **Cohesionless Soils (Sands, Gravels):** SPT is common for disturbed. Vibratory or rotary methods for pushing tubes might be needed for undisturbed, but disturbance is inherent.
    *   **Soft/Sensitive Clays:** Piston samplers offer the best chance for undisturbed.
    *   **Firm/Stiff Clays:** Thin-walled tubes are effective.
    *   **Dense Sands/Gravels:** Driving thin-walled tubes is difficult. SPT is common. Rotary drilling with core barrels (for rock-like gravels) or specialized sampling tools might be necessary.
    *   **Rock:** Rotary core drilling with double or triple tube barrels is standard.

*   **Investigation Objective:**
    *   **Strength/Deformation Parameters:** Undisturbed samples are essential (triaxial, consolidation).
    *   **Classification/Identification:** Disturbed samples are often sufficient (SPT, augers).
    *   **Environmental Testing:** Specific sampling methods might be required to preserve volatile components.
    *   **Groundwater Contamination:** Sampling techniques must avoid cross-contamination.

*   **Required Sample Quality (Disturbance Level):**
    *   High-quality undisturbed samples (e.g., for sensitive clay behavior) require specialized methods.
    *   Routine index testing can use disturbed samples.

*   **Depth of Investigation:**
    *   Shallow depths might allow for block sampling.
    *   Deeper investigations typically require borehole drilling and coring/sampling.

*   **Cost and Time Constraints:**
    *   Undisturbed sampling methods are generally more expensive and time-consuming than disturbed sampling.
    *   The budget will dictate the level of detail and quality of sampling achievable.

*   **Site Accessibility and Conditions:**
    *   Limited access may restrict the type of drilling rig or sampling equipment that can be used.
    *   Presence of obstructions (e.g., boulders, existing utilities) can influence method selection.

*   **Presence of Groundwater:**
    *   Sampling below the water table requires special considerations to prevent sample flushing or collapse.

**Example:** If a project requires precise measurement of the shear strength of a soft, organic clay layer to ensure slope stability, the priority is obtaining undisturbed samples, likely using a piston sampler, even though it's more expensive. If the project is a simple house foundation and preliminary soil classification is the main goal, SPT sampling might be sufficient.

### 3. Best Practices in Sampling

*   **Careful Handling and Transport:**
    *   Immediately seal samples to prevent moisture loss or gain (e.g., wax sealing, plastic bags within containers).
    *   Label samples clearly with borehole number, depth, date, sample type, and project information.
    *   Minimize jarring and vibration during transport.
    *   Keep samples cool and out of direct sunlight.

*   **Accurate Documentation:**
    *   Record the sampling method, depth of sample, recovery length, and visual description on the borehole log.
    *   Note any observations about the soil or rock during sampling (e.g., presence of water, odor, color changes).

*   **Appropriate Sample Containers:**
    *   For undisturbed samples, use sample tubes or containers that maintain the sample's dimensions and prevent deformation.
    *   For disturbed samples, use sturdy bags or containers.

*   **Minimizing Disturbance:**
    *   Use the lowest possible drive or push force required.
    *   Clean the borehole before sampling.
    *   Use appropriate core barrel sizes relative to the borehole diameter.

*   **Maximizing Sample Recovery:**
    *   Ensure drill bits and core barrels are sharp and well-maintained.
    *   Use appropriate drilling fluids or casing to support boreholes.

*   **Quality Control:**
    *   Regularly inspect sampling equipment.
    *   Train personnel in proper sampling techniques.

**Important Point to Remember:** Proper sample handling and documentation are as critical as the sampling technique itself for ensuring the validity of the investigation results.

### 4. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1:**
Which sampling method is generally considered best for obtaining undisturbed samples of soft, sensitive clays and why?

**Question 2:**
You are investigating a dense sand layer for a bridge foundation. What sampling method would you most likely employ, and what type of sample would it produce?

**Question 3:**
List three key factors that influence the choice of a sampling method for a geotechnical investigation.

**Question 4:**
What are the main differences between a disturbed and an undisturbed sample, and what types of laboratory tests are typically performed on each?

**Question 5:**
A contractor is excavating a tunnel. What sampling technique could be used to obtain high-quality, undisturbed samples of the rock mass encountered at the tunnel face?

---

### Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
The **piston sampler** is generally best for obtaining undisturbed samples of soft, sensitive clays. This is because the piston creates a vacuum as the tube is pushed down, significantly reducing friction against the borehole walls and minimizing soil disturbance and remolding, thereby preserving the delicate soil fabric and moisture content.

**Answer 2:**
For a dense sand layer, the **Split-Spoon Sampler (SPT Sampler)** is typically employed. This method involves driving the sampler into the ground, and it produces a **disturbed sample**. While it's not ideal for precise strength testing, the SPT N-value obtained during sampling provides an indication of the sand's relative density and strength.

**Answer 3:**
Three key factors influencing the choice of sampling method are:
1.  **Soil/Rock Type:** The physical properties of the soil or rock dictate which methods can be used effectively and how much disturbance is likely.
2.  **Investigation Objective:** The required quality of the sample (disturbed vs. undisturbed) and the specific laboratory tests planned determine the method.
3.  **Required Sample Quality (Disturbance Level):** Projects requiring accurate strength or deformation parameters necessitate undisturbed sampling, while basic classification may allow for disturbed samples.
(Other valid answers include depth, cost, site accessibility, groundwater conditions).

**Answer 4:**
*   **Disturbed Samples:** Have their natural structure, fabric, and stress history altered during sampling. They are typically used for **index tests** such as sieve analysis, Atterberg limits, and visual classification.
*   **Undisturbed Samples:** Retain their original structure, fabric, and stress history as much as possible. They are used for **strength tests** (e.g., triaxial compression, direct shear) and **deformation tests** (e.g., consolidation tests) to determine engineering properties.

**Answer 5:**
To obtain high-quality, undisturbed samples of the rock mass at the tunnel face, **rock block sampling** could be used. This involves carefully excavating or cutting out intact blocks of rock from the exposed face. If borehole sampling is also being conducted in conjunction with the tunnel, **triple-tube rotary core drilling** would be the preferred method for obtaining the highest quality undisturbed rock core samples.
