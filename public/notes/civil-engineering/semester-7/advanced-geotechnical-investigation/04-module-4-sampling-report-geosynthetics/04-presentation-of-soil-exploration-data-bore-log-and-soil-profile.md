### 1. Introduction: The Importance of Presenting Soil Exploration Data

Effective communication of soil exploration findings is crucial for successful geotechnical engineering projects. The raw data collected from site investigations (e.g., borings, CPTs, lab tests) needs to be organized, interpreted, and presented in a clear, concise, and standardized format. This ensures that:

*   **Design engineers can accurately assess site conditions:** This includes understanding soil stratigraphy, material properties, and potential engineering challenges.
*   **Clients and stakeholders can understand the site's characteristics:** Non-technical audiences need accessible information.
*   **Future investigations and projects can benefit from the existing data:** A well-documented site history is invaluable.
*   **Regulatory compliance and quality assurance are maintained:** Standardized reporting ensures adherence to industry best practices.

The primary tools for presenting this data are the **Borehole Log** and the **Soil Profile**.

---

### 2. The Borehole Log: A Detailed Record of a Single Boring

A borehole log is a graphical and textual representation of the soil and rock encountered at a specific boring location. It acts as a detailed diary of what was found during the drilling process and the subsequent testing.

#### 2.1 Purpose of a Borehole Log:

*   To document the depth and nature of different soil and rock strata.
*   To record the results of in-situ testing (e.g., SPT N-values, CPT data, piezocone readings).
*   To list samples collected at various depths and their descriptions.
*   To note groundwater levels and any observed changes during drilling.
*   To provide essential data for geotechnical engineers to interpret soil behavior and properties.
