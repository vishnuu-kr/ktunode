# Advanced Geotechnical Investigation: Module 4 - Sampling, Report & Geosynthetics

## Topic: Soil Sampling

This module delves into the critical aspects of soil sampling, ensuring the collected samples are suitable for laboratory testing and ultimately lead to accurate geotechnical designs. We will cover the fundamental principles of soil sampling, types of samples, sampler characteristics, and specialized techniques for sampling in challenging conditions.

---

### 1. Disturbed vs. Undisturbed Soil Samples

Understanding the difference between disturbed and undisturbed samples is fundamental to geotechnical investigations. The objective of sampling is to obtain soil that accurately represents the in-situ conditions for laboratory testing.

#### 1.1. Disturbed Soil Samples

*   **Definition:** Soil samples in which the original soil structure, stratification, and some of the stress history have been altered or destroyed.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Soil grains are displaced, and the soil fabric is disrupted.
    *   Natural moisture content may change.
    *   Original stress conditions are generally lost.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   **Identification and Classification:** Visual inspection, grain size analysis, Atterberg limits.
    *   **Approximate Strength/Compressibility:** Unconfined compressive strength (UCS), compaction tests.
    *   **Chemical Analysis:** pH, soluble salts, sulfates.
*   **Methods of Collection:**
    *   **Augers:** Hand augers, power augers.
    *   **Shovels and Picks:** For shallow depths and surface soils.
    *   **Drive Sampling:** Using a sampling spoon (e.g., Split Spoon Sampler) where significant disturbance is acceptable.
*   **When Used:**
    *   When detailed soil structure or stress history is not critical for the intended tests.
    *   For initial site reconnaissance.
    *   In granular soils where achieving truly undisturbed samples is difficult.

#### 1.2. Undisturbed Soil Samples

*   **Definition:** Soil samples in which the natural structure, stratification, moisture content, and in-situ stress conditions are preserved as closely as possible.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Retains its natural soil fabric (arrangement of particles).
    *   Maintains its natural moisture content.
    *   Attempts to preserve the in-situ stress conditions.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   **Shear Strength:** Triaxial compression tests, direct shear tests.
    *   **Consolidation Characteristics:** Oedometer tests.
    *   **Permeability:** Permeability tests.
    *   **Laboratory Compaction Parameters:** Modified Proctor test (though this is technically a compaction test, undisturbed samples might be used for evaluating the effect of soil structure on compaction).
*   **Methods of Collection:**
    *   **Thin-Walled Tube Samplers:** Shelby tubes, piston samplers.
    *   **Block Sampling:** For cohesive soils at shallow depths.
    *   **Stationary Piston Samplers:** Particularly effective for soft clays.
*   **When Used:**
    *   When the soil structure significantly influences its engineering behavior (e.g., clays, silts).
    *   For critical design parameters like shear strength and compressibility.

### 2. Representative and Non-Representative Samples

The quality of geotechnical investigation hinges on the representativeness of the collected samples.

#### 2.1. Representative Samples

*   **Definition:** Soil samples that accurately reflect the properties and conditions of the soil stratum from which they were obtained.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Collected from the correct location and depth.
    *   Preserves the in-situ properties (structure, moisture content, density) as much as possible, especially for undisturbed samples.
    *   The volume of the sample is adequate for the intended laboratory tests.
    *   Free from contamination.
*   **Importance:** Essential for accurate laboratory testing and reliable geotechnical design. Non-representative samples can lead to erroneous conclusions and potentially unsafe structures.

#### 2.2. Non-Representative Samples

*   **Definition:** Soil samples that do not accurately reflect the properties and conditions of the soil stratum from which they were obtained.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Sampling Errors:** Improper sampling techniques, using the wrong sampler, incorrect operation.
    *   **Contamination:** Mixing with soils from other strata, mixing with drilling fluid.
    *   **Loss of Fines:** In granular soils during sampling or retrieval.
    *   **Sample Disturbance:** Excessive friction, impact, or deformation during sampling.
    *   **Improper Handling/Storage:** Drying out, changing moisture content, damaging structure.
    *   **Sampling from the Wrong Location/Depth:** Not targeting the intended soil layer.
*   **Consequences:** Inaccurate test results, misinterpretation of soil conditions, flawed design parameters, increased project risks.

### 3. Chunk and Tube Samples

These terms describe the physical form of the collected sample, primarily related to the sampling method.

#### 3.1. Chunk Samples

*   **Definition:** Soil samples collected as large, irregular pieces or "chunks" of soil.
*   **Collection Method:** Typically obtained by hand from excavations (e.g., trenches, pits, shafts) or by carefully excavating around a cohesive soil mass.
*   **Characteristics:** Can be relatively undisturbed if care is taken during excavation. The size and shape are not standardized.
*   **Purpose:** Useful for visual inspection, description, and laboratory testing when undisturbed samples are required from shallow depths, especially in cohesive soils.
*   **Limitations:** Not suitable for deeper excavations or when precise geometric control is needed.

#### 3.2. Tube Samples

*   **Definition:** Soil samples collected by driving or pushing a cylindrical tube into the soil.
*   **Collection Method:** Using various types of tube samplers (e.g., thin-walled tube samplers, split spoon samplers).
*   **Characteristics:** The sample is contained within a cylinder, allowing for better preservation of its natural form and easier handling. The size and type of tube dictate the sample's quality and suitability for different tests.
*   **Purpose:** Widely used for obtaining both disturbed and undisturbed samples from various depths via drilling or direct pushing.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Open Drive Samplers:** Like the Split Spoon Sampler.
    *   **Thin-Walled Tube Samplers:** Shelby Tubes, Piston Samplers.

### 4. Sampler Geometry and Parameters

Understanding sampler dimensions and their impact on sample quality is crucial for selecting the appropriate sampler.

#### 4.1. Area Ratio Clearance ($A_r$)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the area of the sampler's cutting edge to the area of the soil cylinder that would be displaced by the sampler, expressed as a percentage. It quantizes how much the soil outside the sampler is deformed during penetration.
*   **Formula:**
    $$A_r = \frac{D_o^2 - D_i^2}{D_i^2} \times 100\%$$
    Where:
    *   $D_o$ = Outside diameter of the sampler cutting edge
    *   $D_i$ = Inside diameter of the sampler cutting edge

*   **Significance:**
    *   **Low Area Ratio (e.g., < 10%):** Indicates minimal soil disturbance, which is desirable for obtaining undisturbed samples. Thin-walled tube samplers typically have low area ratios.
    *   **High Area Ratio:** Implies significant soil disturbance due to the displacement of a larger volume of soil. Split spoon samplers generally have higher area ratios.
*   **Impact:** A higher area ratio generally leads to greater disturbance of the soil within the sampler, particularly affecting the soil structure and pore water pressure.

#### 4.2. Outside Clearance (Cutting Edge Relief)

*   **Definition:** The difference between the inside diameter of the sampler tube and the diameter of the cutting edge, expressed as a radial clearance. It's the space that allows the sampler to enter the soil without significant wedging or over-stressing the soil inside the tube.
*   **Formula:**
    $$Outside\;Clearance = \frac{D_c - D_i}{2}$$
    Where:
    *   $D_c$ = Inside diameter of the sampler cutting edge
    *   $D_i$ = Inside diameter of the sampler barrel (where the soil sample is contained)

    *Note: Sometimes referred to as "relief" or "kerf." It's crucial that the cutting edge diameter ($D_c$) is slightly smaller than the barrel diameter ($D_i$) to prevent the tube from binding as it enters the soil.*

*   **Significance:**
    *   **Proper Clearance:** Facilitates penetration with reduced friction and less disturbance.
    *   **Too Little Clearance:** Can cause the tube to wedge into the soil, leading to over-consolidation and sample disturbance.
    *   **Too Much Clearance:** Can lead to remolding of the soil ahead of the sampler.
*   **Ideal:** A small, positive outside clearance (typically a few millimeters) is ideal for minimizing disturbance.

### 5. Recovery Ratio

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the length of the recovered soil sample to the length of the borehole or interval over which the sampler was driven. It quantizes how much of the soil that was supposed to be sampled was actually obtained.
*   **Formula:**
    $$Recovery\;Ratio = \frac{L_s}{L_b} \times 100\%$$
    Where:
    *   $L_s$ = Length of the soil sample recovered
    *   $L_b$ = Length of the borehole interval the sampler was driven into

*   **Significance:**
    *   **Recovery Ratio close to 100%:** Indicates successful sampling.
    *   **Recovery Ratio significantly less than 100%:** Suggests sample loss, which can occur due to:
        *   Very soft soils that deform or wash out.
        *   Dense or very stiff soils that the sampler cannot penetrate sufficiently.
        *   Borehole instability.
        *   Improper operation of the sampler.
    *   **Recovery Ratio greater than 100%:** This is usually an indication of oversampling or compaction of the soil within the sampler, suggesting significant disturbance.
*   **Interpretation:** A low recovery ratio can make it difficult to assess the continuity of soil layers. A recovery ratio greater than 100% flags potential over-compaction and disturbance.

### 6. Handling and Transportation of Samples

Proper handling and transportation are vital to preserve the integrity of collected soil samples, especially undisturbed ones.

#### 6.1. General Principles

*   **Minimize Disturbance:** Handle samples with care to avoid jarring, dropping, or applying excessive pressure.
*   **Maintain Moisture Content:** Prevent samples from drying out or gaining moisture.
*   **Preserve Structure:** Avoid actions that can break down the soil fabric.
*   **Labeling:** Accurate and immediate labeling of each sample with essential information is critical.

#### 6.2. Handling Procedures

*   **Disturbed Samples:**
    *   Place in clean, sturdy plastic bags or containers.
    *   Label clearly with borehole number, sample depth, sample type, date, and sampler used.
    *   Seal bags to prevent moisture loss or contamination.
*   **Undisturbed Samples (Tube Samples):**
    *   **Sealing the Ends:** Immediately after retrieval, the ends of the sampler tube must be sealed to prevent moisture loss. This is typically done with:
        *   **Wax sealing:** Dipping the ends in molten wax (paraffin).
        *   **Plastic caps/Plugs:** Securely fitting caps.
        *   **Polythene sheeting:** Wrapped and sealed tightly.
    *   **Protection:** The tube should be protected from physical damage.
    *   **Vertical Storage:** Always store undisturbed samples vertically to prevent sample movement within the tube.
*   **Undisturbed Samples (Block Samples):**
    *   Carefully wrap in plastic sheeting or cloth and secure with tape.
    *   Place in a rigid container (e.g., wooden box) with cushioning material.
    *   Label clearly.

#### 6.3. Transportation

*   **Protection:** Transport samples in suitable containers to protect them from shock, vibration, and temperature extremes.
*   **Minimize Time:** Transport samples to the laboratory as quickly as possible.
*   **Temperature Control:** Avoid extreme temperatures that can affect soil properties (e.g., freezing, excessive heat).
*   **Secure Storage:** Ensure samples are secured during transit to prevent movement and damage.

### 7. Types of Samplers

Different samplers are designed for specific soil types and sampling objectives.

#### 7.1. Thin-Walled Samplers (Shelby Tubes)

*   **Description:** Cylindrical tubes with a thin wall, typically made of seamless steel. The cutting edge is usually square-edged or slightly beveled to reduce friction.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **Low Area Ratio:** Generally less than 10%, minimizing disturbance.
    *   **Thin Wall:** Reduces the volume of soil displaced.
    *   **Sample Size:** Common diameters range from 50 mm to 100 mm.
    *   **Driven by:** Pushing (preferred for soft to medium clays) or sometimes by light hammering.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Produces high-quality undisturbed samples in cohesive soils.
    *   Relatively inexpensive.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Not suitable for very soft or sensitive clays that can deform easily.
    *   Difficult to use in very dense or cemented soils.
    *   Can be easily damaged in granular soils or if driven by heavy impact.
*   **Usage:** Primarily for soft to stiff cohesive soils for triaxial compression, consolidation, and permeability tests.

#### 7.2. Piston Samplers (Stationary Piston Samplers)

*   **Description:** A specialized type of thin-walled sampler that incorporates a piston inside the sampler tube. The piston can be fixed or moved.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **Fixed Piston:** The piston is fixed at the bottom of the tube, creating a vacuum behind the sample as it enters, which helps prevent ingress of drilling fluid and reduces suction forces.
    *   **Moving Piston:** The piston can be extended beyond the cutting edge to help initiate sampling and then held stationary as the tube is pushed into the soil, creating a vacuum to facilitate sample entry and minimize disturbance.
    *   **High Quality Samples:** Renowned for obtaining very high-quality undisturbed samples, especially in very soft, sensitive clays and silts.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Excellent for obtaining highly undisturbed samples.
    *   Reduces sample disturbance due to suction and pressure differentials.
    *   Provides positive control during sampling.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   More complex and expensive than standard thin-walled samplers.
    *   Requires careful operation.
*   **Usage:** Ideal for highly sensitive clays, soft silts, and other delicate soil structures.

#### 7.3. Split Spoon Sampler (Standard Penetration Test - SPT Sampler)

*   **Description:** A thick-walled cylindrical sampler consisting of two halves that are bolted together. It has a hardened cutting edge and a coupling at the top to attach to drill rods.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **Thick Wall:** Higher area ratio (typically 15-30%), leading to significant disturbance.
    *   **Driven by:** Hammer blows (typically a 63.5 kg hammer falling 760 mm).
    *   **Sample Type:** Primarily yields disturbed, remolded samples.
    *   **Purpose:** Primarily used for the Standard Penetration Test (SPT) to determine the "N-value" (number of blows for 150 mm penetration), which is an index of soil density and strength.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Robust and can be used in a wide range of soil conditions, including sands and gravels.
    *   Provides a useful in-situ strength index (N-value).
    *   Can collect disturbed samples for classification.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Causes significant disturbance, making it unsuitable for tests requiring preserved soil structure (e.g., triaxial compression, consolidation).
    *   Sample recovery can be poor in clean sands below the water table due to washing out.

### 8. Methods for Collection of Sand Samples from Beneath the Water Table

Sampling sands below the water table presents a significant challenge due to the potential for fines washing out, sample collapse, and loss of fines content, which influences the soil's behavior.

#### 8.1. Challenges

*   **Loss of Fines:** Water flow during sampling can wash away fine sand particles.
*   **Sample Dilatancy/Collapse:** Upon retrieval, the removal of pore water pressure can cause the sand structure to collapse, altering its density and void ratio.
*   **Contamination:** Drilling fluid ingress can saturate and displace the sample.
*   **Difficulty in Penetration:** Dense sands require significant force, potentially causing over-compaction.

#### 8.2. Effective Sampling Methods

*   **Split Spoon Sampler (SPT):**
    *   **Procedure:** Driven by hammer blows. The driving action compacts the sand and often leads to a recovery ratio greater than 100% (over-compaction).
    *   **Mitigation for Fines Loss:** The cutting edge is designed to retain soil, and rapid retrieval helps minimize washing. However, it's still a disturbed sample.
    *   **Purpose:** Primarily for SPT "N-value" determination, which is well-calibrated for sands. Disturbed samples are collected for classification.

*   **Thin-Walled Tube Samplers (Shelby Tubes):**
    *   **Procedure:** Pushed into the soil. Requires a clean borehole free of drilling fluid.
    *   **Mitigation for Fines Loss:** The slow pushing action can reduce fines loss compared to driven samplers. Careful sealing is paramount.
    *   **Limitations:** Difficult to use in very dense sands; can lead to over-penetration and disturbance in looser sands if not pushed carefully.

*   **Stationary Piston Samplers:**
    *   **Procedure:** The piston creates a vacuum that aids in drawing the sample into the tube and minimizing water flow.
    *   **Mitigation for Fines Loss:** Highly effective in retaining fines due to the vacuum.
    *   **Usage:** Can provide better quality samples in sands than standard tube samplers, but still challenging in very dense or coarse sands.

*   **Core Sampling (with liner tubes):**
    *   **Procedure:** A sampling barrel with an inner liner tube is advanced. The soil enters the liner.
    *   **Mitigation for Fines Loss:** The liner tube helps retain the sample. Careful sealing is critical.
    *   **Usage:** Can be effective for maintaining sample integrity.

*   **Vibratory Sampling:**
    *   **Procedure:** Uses high-frequency vibrations to advance the sampler, which can be beneficial in granular soils.
    *   **Mitigation for Fines Loss:** The continuous vibration can help prevent bridging and aid in sample entry.
    *   **Usage:** Less common but can be effective in certain sand conditions.

*   **Crucial Considerations for Sand Sampling Below Water Table:**
    *   **Minimize Borehole Disturbance:** Ensure the borehole is stable and as clean as possible before sampling.
    *   **Rapid Retrieval:** Once the sampler is filled, retrieve it as quickly as possible to minimize water flow and fines loss.
    *   **Immediate Sealing:** Seal the sample ends immediately upon retrieval.
    *   **Record Blow Counts/Penetration Resistance:** Even for tube samples, record any resistance encountered.
    *   **Visual Inspection:** Note the appearance of the recovered sample for signs of disturbance or fines loss.
    *   **Consider Density:** The SPT is often preferred for sands because its "N-value" is directly related to the relative density of the sand, which is a key parameter.

### Practice Questions & Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary difference between a disturbed and an undisturbed soil sample?
**Answer:** A disturbed sample has its natural soil structure, stratification, and stress history altered or destroyed, while an undisturbed sample aims to preserve these in-situ characteristics as much as possible.

**Question 2:** A sampler has an outside diameter of 70 mm and an inside diameter of 60 mm. Calculate its area ratio clearance.
**Answer:**
$D_o = 70 \text{ mm}$
$D_i = 60 \text{ mm}$
$A_r = \frac{D_o^2 - D_i^2}{D_i^2} \times 100\% = \frac{70^2 - 60^2}{60^2} \times 100\% = \frac{4900 - 3600}{3600} \times 100\% = \frac{1300}{3600} \times 100\% \approx 36.1\%$

**Question 3:** A split spoon sampler was driven into the ground for 450 mm, but only 300 mm of soil was recovered. What is the recovery ratio?
**Answer:**
$L_s = 300 \text{ mm}$
$L_b = 450 \text{ mm}$
$Recovery\;Ratio = \frac{L_s}{L_b} \times 100\% = \frac{300}{450} \times 100\% \approx 66.7\%$

**Question 4:** Why is it important to seal the ends of undisturbed soil samples immediately after collection?
**Answer:** To prevent moisture loss, which would alter the soil's physical properties and potentially cause shrinkage or changes in pore water pressure, making subsequent laboratory tests unreliable.

**Question 5:** Which sampler is generally considered to produce the highest quality undisturbed samples in very soft clays?
**Answer:** Stationary Piston Sampler.

**Question 6:** You are sampling a medium dense sand below the water table using an SPT sampler. You notice a recovery ratio of 120%. What does this likely indicate?
**Answer:** This likely indicates that the sand has been compacted or over-driven within the sampler during the SPT process, suggesting significant disturbance and an increased density compared to the in-situ condition.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Objective of Sampling:** To obtain soil that accurately represents in-situ conditions for laboratory testing.
*   **Disturbance is Inevitable:** The goal is to *minimize* disturbance, especially for undisturbed samples.
*   **Area Ratio is Key for Disturbance:** Low area ratio samplers are preferred for undisturbed sampling.
*   **Proper Handling is Crucial:** Seal, protect, and transport samples carefully to preserve their integrity.
*   **Sampler Selection:** Choose the sampler appropriate for the soil type and the required quality of sample.
*   **SPT is Primarily for Strength Index:** While it yields disturbed samples, the "N-value" is invaluable for sands.
*   **Sand Sampling Below Water Table:** Requires special care to prevent fines loss and sample collapse; SPT is often used due to its correlation with relative density.
*   **Accurate Labeling:** Never underestimate the importance of clear and complete sample labeling.
