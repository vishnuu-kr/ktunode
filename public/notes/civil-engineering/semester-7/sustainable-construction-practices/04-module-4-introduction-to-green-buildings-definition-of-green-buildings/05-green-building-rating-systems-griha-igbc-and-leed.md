# SUSTAINABLE CONSTRUCTION PRACTICES

## Module 4: Introduction to Green Buildings

### Topic: Green Building Rating Systems – GRIHA, IGBC, and LEED

---

### 1. Introduction to Green Buildings

A **green building** is a building that is designed, constructed, and operated in a way that minimizes its negative impact on the environment and human health, while maximizing its positive contributions. It considers the entire lifecycle of the building, from site selection and design to construction, operation, maintenance, renovation, and deconstruction.

**Key Principles of Green Buildings:**

*   **Resource Efficiency:** Minimizing the consumption of water, energy, and materials.
*   **Environmental Protection:** Reducing pollution, waste, and greenhouse gas emissions.
*   **Occupant Health and Well-being:** Creating healthy, comfortable, and productive indoor environments.
*   **Lifecycle Impact:** Considering the environmental effects throughout the building's existence.

---

### 2. Green Building Rating Systems

Green building rating systems are voluntary frameworks that provide a structured approach to assessing and certifying the sustainability performance of buildings. They offer a set of criteria and benchmarks against which a building's environmental, social, and economic aspects are evaluated. These systems aim to:

*   **Promote Sustainable Design and Construction:** Encourage the adoption of green building principles and practices.
*   **Provide a Benchmark:** Establish clear standards for sustainable performance.
*   **Facilitate Communication:** Offer a common language for discussing and evaluating green buildings.
*   **Recognize and Reward Excellence:** Certify buildings that achieve high levels of sustainability.
*   **Drive Market Transformation:** Increase the demand for and supply of green buildings.

---

### 3. GRIHA (Green Rating for Integrated Habitat Assessment)

GRIHA is India's indigenous green building rating system, developed by The Energy and Resources Institute (TERI). It is designed to be relevant to Indian climatic conditions, building types, and socio-economic contexts.

**Key Concepts and Features of GRIHA:**

*   **Holistic Approach:** Evaluates buildings across a broad spectrum of sustainability criteria.
*   **Life Cycle Perspective:** Considers the environmental impact of a building from inception to deconstruction.
*   **Adaptability:** Designed to be adaptable to different building types and stages of development (new construction, existing buildings, interim stages).
*   **Scoring System:** Uses a point-based system, with a maximum of 100 points. Buildings are rated on a scale from "1-Star" to "5-Star" based on their total score.
*   **Focus Areas (Categories):**
    *   **Site Selection and Planning:** Location, accessibility, site analysis, minimizing ecological impact.
    *   **Conservation and Efficiency in Resources:**
        *   **Water Conservation:** Rainwater harvesting, efficient water fixtures, water recycling.
        *   **Energy Efficiency:** Passive design strategies, energy-efficient equipment, renewable energy integration.
        *   **Materials and Construction Practices:** Use of sustainable materials, recycled content, local sourcing, waste reduction.
    *   **Building Operation and Maintenance:** Indoor environmental quality, waste management, occupant comfort.
    *   **Innovation and Management:** Novel approaches, social and economic aspects, occupant education.

**GRIHA Rating Levels:**

| Score Range | Rating       |
| :---------- | :----------- |
| 40-49       | 1-Star       |
| 50-59       | 2-Star       |
| 60-69       | 3-Star       |
| 70-79       | 4-Star       |
| 80+         | 5-Star       |

**Examples of GRIHA Application:**

*   **Residential Buildings:** Houses, apartments.
*   **Commercial Buildings:** Offices, retail spaces.
*   **Institutional Buildings:** Schools, hospitals.
*   **Industrial Buildings:** Factories, warehouses.

**Important Points to Remember about GRIHA:**

*   **India-centric:** Tailored to Indian environmental and cultural contexts.
*   **Comprehensive:** Covers a wide range of sustainability aspects.
*   **Scalable:** Applicable to various building types and sizes.

### 4. IGBC (Indian Green Building Council)

IGBC is a consensus-based, market-driven, and participatory green building rating system in India, developed by the Confederation of Indian Industry (CII). It is also adapted to Indian conditions and aims to facilitate the adoption of green building practices.

**Key Concepts and Features of IGBC:**

*   **Performance-based:** Focuses on measurable performance outcomes.
*   **Market-driven:** Responds to the needs and preferences of the building industry.
*   **User-friendly:** Aims for ease of use and understanding.
*   **Adaptable:** Offers various rating schemes for different building typologies.
*   **Scoring System:** Similar to GRIHA, it uses a point-based system. Buildings are rated from "Certified" to "Platinum" based on their performance.
*   **Focus Areas (Categories):**
    *   **Site Selection and Planning:** Location, site conservation, transportation.
    *   **Water Efficiency:** Water-saving fixtures, rainwater harvesting, wastewater treatment.
    *   **Energy Efficiency:** Energy performance, renewable energy, efficient lighting and HVAC.
    *   **Materials and Resources:** Sustainable materials, construction waste management, recycled content.
    *   **Indoor Environmental Quality:** Indoor air quality, thermal comfort, natural lighting and ventilation.
    *   **Innovation:** Novel green building concepts and technologies.

**IGBC Rating Levels:**

| Score Range (Typical - varies slightly by scheme) | Rating      |
| :------------------------------------------------ | :---------- |
| 50-54                                             | Certified   |
| 55-64                                             | Silver      |
| 65-74                                             | Gold        |
| 75+                                               | Platinum    |

**IGBC Schemes:**

*   IGBC Green Homes (for residential buildings)
*   IGBC Green New Buildings (for commercial, institutional, industrial buildings)
*   IGBC Green Existing Buildings
*   IGBC Green Factory Buildings
*   IGBC Green Schools
*   IGBC Green Healthcare Facilities

**Examples of IGBC Application:**

*   Infosys campuses across India often achieve high IGBC ratings.
*   Many corporate offices and residential projects in major Indian cities are IGBC certified.

**Important Points to Remember about IGBC:**

*   **Developed by CII:** A prominent industry body in India.
*   **Focus on Performance:** Emphasizes measurable results.
*   **Diverse Rating Schemes:** Caters to a wide range of building types.

### 5. LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)

LEED is a globally recognized green building rating system developed by the U.S. Green Building Council (USGBC). It is one of the most widely used green building certification programs in the world.

**Key Concepts and Features of LEED:**

*   **Global Standard:** Recognized internationally, making it suitable for projects with global aspirations.
*   **Performance-based:** Rewards projects for achieving specific sustainability goals.
*   **Comprehensive Criteria:** Covers a wide range of environmental, social, and economic aspects.
*   **Flexible:** Adaptable to different project types, sizes, and phases of the building lifecycle.
*   **Scoring System:** Uses a point-based system, with a maximum of 110 points (including innovation and regional credits). Buildings are rated from "Certified" to "Platinum."
*   **Prerequisites and Credits:**
    *   **Prerequisites:** Mandatory requirements that all projects must meet.
    *   **Credits:** Optional requirements that projects can earn points for.
*   **Focus Areas (Categories):**
    *   **Location and Transportation (LT):** Promoting sustainable site selection, access to public transport, and reducing automobile dependence.
    *   **Sustainable Sites (SS):** Protecting and restoring natural habitats, minimizing site disturbance, managing stormwater.
    *   **Water Efficiency (WE):** Reducing potable water consumption.
    *   **Energy and Atmosphere (EA):** Optimizing energy efficiency, reducing greenhouse gas emissions, and utilizing renewable energy.
    *   **Materials and Resources (MR):** Using sustainable materials, reducing waste, and promoting responsible sourcing.
    *   **Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ):** Enhancing indoor air quality, thermal comfort, lighting, and acoustics for occupant well-being.
    *   **Innovation (IN):** Recognizing innovative strategies not covered by existing credits.
    *   **Regional Priority (RP):** Addressing specific environmental issues relevant to the project's location.

**LEED Rating Levels (for LEED v4.1):**

| Score Range | Rating      |
| :---------- | :---------- |
| 40-49       | Certified   |
| 50-59       | Silver      |
| 60-79       | Gold        |
| 80+         | Platinum    |

**LEED Rating Systems:**

*   LEED for Building Design and Construction (BD+C) - for new buildings.
*   LEED for Interior Design and Construction (ID+C) - for interiors.
*   LEED for Building Operations and Maintenance (O+M) - for existing buildings.
*   LEED for Neighborhood Development (ND).

**Examples of LEED Application:**

*   The Empire State Building underwent a major renovation to achieve LEED Gold certification.
*   Many multinational corporations worldwide adopt LEED for their new office buildings.

**Important Points to Remember about LEED:**

*   **Globally Recognized:** A leading international standard.
*   **Rigorous and Comprehensive:** Covers a broad range of sustainability aspects.
*   **Adaptable:** Available for various building types and project phases.

### 6. Comparison of GRIHA, IGBC, and LEED

While all three rating systems aim to promote sustainable buildings, they have distinct characteristics:

| Feature             | GRIHA (India)                               | IGBC (India)                               | LEED (Global)                                |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| **Origin**          | TERI (India)                                | CII (India)                                | USGBC (USA)                                  |
| **Contextualization**| Highly adapted to Indian conditions       | Adapted to Indian conditions             | Global, with regional credits             |
| **Emphasis**        | Integrated approach, resource efficiency    | Performance-based, market-driven         | Performance-based, comprehensive           |
| **Approach**        | Holistic, scoring based on criteria       | Performance-oriented, points system      | Prerequisite-driven, points system         |
| **Application**     | All building types, adaptable to existing | Diverse specific schemes for building types | Broad range of building types & phases     |
| **Recognition**     | India                                       | India                                      | Global                                       |

**Choosing a Rating System:**

The choice of rating system often depends on:

*   **Project Location:** GRIHA and IGBC are ideal for projects in India. LEED is suitable globally and for Indian projects seeking international recognition.
*   **Project Goals:** What specific sustainability targets does the project aim to achieve?
*   **Client Preferences:** Some clients may have a preference for a particular rating system.
*   **Market Trends:** The prevalent rating systems in the local market.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Which of the following is an indigenous green building rating system developed in India?
A. LEED
B. GRIHA
C. BREEAM
D. EDGE

**Answer:** B. GRIHA

**Question 2:**
What are the main categories evaluated by green building rating systems like GRIHA, IGBC, and LEED? (List at least five)

**Answer:**
*   Site Selection and Planning
*   Water Efficiency
*   Energy Efficiency
*   Materials and Resources
*   Indoor Environmental Quality
*   (Others could include: Location and Transportation, Innovation, etc.)

**Question 3:**
Explain the primary difference in origin and contextualization between GRIHA and LEED.

**Answer:** GRIHA originated in India and is specifically designed to be relevant to Indian climatic conditions and socio-economic contexts. LEED originated in the USA and is a globally recognized standard, though it allows for regional adaptations.

**Question 4:**
Imagine you are designing a new commercial office building in Mumbai. Which Indian green building rating system might you consider, and why?

**Answer:** You might consider both GRIHA and IGBC.
*   **GRIHA:** Because it's an Indian system, it's well-suited to local environmental conditions and building practices. It offers a holistic approach.
*   **IGBC:** Because it's also adapted to Indian conditions and is known for being performance-based and having specific schemes for different building types like commercial offices.

**Question 5:**
What does the "E" in LEED stand for?
A. Environment
B. Energy
C. Excellence
D. Environmental

**Answer:** C. Excellence (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)

**Exercise:**

Research a recently completed building in your region (city/state) that has achieved a certification from GRIHA, IGBC, or LEED. Identify the rating achieved and try to find out which specific green building strategies were implemented in its design and construction.

### 8. Summary of Key Takeaways

*   **Green buildings** prioritize resource efficiency, environmental protection, and occupant health.
*   **Green building rating systems** provide a framework to assess and certify the sustainability of buildings.
*   **GRIHA** is India's indigenous rating system, tailored to local conditions.
*   **IGBC** is another prominent Indian rating system, developed by CII, focusing on performance.
*   **LEED** is a globally recognized and widely used rating system.
*   All three systems evaluate buildings across key categories like site, water, energy, materials, and indoor quality.
*   The choice of rating system depends on project location, goals, and market preferences.
