# PAVEMENT DESIGN AND CONSTRUCTION - Module 3: Construction of Flexible Pavement

## Topic: Construction of Cement Concrete Pavement

This module focuses on the construction of Rigid Pavements, specifically Cement Concrete Pavements (PCC). While the module title is "Construction of Flexible Pavement," this topic deviates to cover the construction of rigid pavements, which are distinct in their materials and construction methods.

---

### **Introduction to Cement Concrete Pavements (PCC)**

Cement Concrete Pavements (PCC), also known as Rigid Pavements, are constructed using Portland Cement Concrete as the primary structural layer. They distribute traffic loads through slab action, transferring stresses to the subgrade over a wider area. This makes them more resistant to deformation compared to flexible pavements.

---

### **1. Material Characterization for PCC Pavements**

Proper selection and characterization of materials are crucial for the performance and durability of PCC pavements.

#### **1.1. Cement**

*   **Definition:** Portland cement is a binder that, when mixed with water, undergoes a chemical reaction (hydration) to form a hardened paste that binds aggregates together.
*   **Types and Specifications:** Various types of Portland cement are available, each suited for different environmental conditions and performance requirements. Key types include:
    *   **Type I (Ordinary Portland Cement):** General-purpose cement.
    *   **Type II (Modified Portland Cement):** Moderate sulfate resistance and moderate heat of hydration.
    *   **Type III (High Early Strength Portland Cement):** Achieves high strength rapidly, useful for fast-track construction.
    *   **Type IV (Low Heat of Hydration Portland Cement):** Generates less heat during hydration, suitable for mass concrete structures.
    *   **Type V (High Sulfate Resistance Portland Cement):** High resistance to sulfate attack.
*   **Key Properties to Check:**
    *   **Fineness:** Affects rate of hydration and strength development.
    *   **Soundness:** Ability to retain volume stability after setting.
    *   **Setting Time:** Time taken for cement paste to harden.
    *   **Compressive Strength:** Ability to resist compressive forces.
*   **Importance:** The quality and type of cement directly influence the strength, durability, and setting characteristics of the concrete.

#### **1.2. Aggregates**

*   **Definition:** Aggregates are granular materials (sand, gravel, crushed stone) that form the bulk of the concrete mix. They are divided into fine aggregate (sand) and coarse aggregate (gravel, crushed stone).
*   **Key Properties to Check:**
    *   **Gradation:** Distribution of particle sizes, which affects workability, density, and strength. Well-graded aggregates are preferred.
        *   *Example:* Gradation curves (e.g., sieve analysis) are used to assess aggregate gradation.
    *   **Particle Shape and Texture:** Affects workability and strength. Rounded aggregates are more workable than angular ones. Rough texture improves bond with cement paste.
    *   **Strength:** Should be stronger than the cement paste.
    *   **Durability:** Resistance to weathering and abrasion.
    *   **Cleanliness:** Free from clay, silt, organic matter, and other deleterious substances.
*   **Importance:** Aggregates constitute about 70-80% of the concrete volume and significantly impact its mechanical properties, cost, and durability.

#### **1.3. Water**

*   **Definition:** Water is essential for the hydration of cement and for providing workability to the concrete mix.
*   **Key Properties to Check:**
    *   **Purity:** Should be free from impurities like salts, acids, alkalis, organic matter, and oils.
    *   **Chloride and Sulfate Content:** Excessive amounts can lead to corrosion of reinforcing steel and affect concrete durability.
*   **Importance:** The quality and quantity of water are critical. Too much water weakens the concrete (lowers strength), while too little reduces workability and can lead to incomplete hydration. The water-cement ratio is a key design parameter.

#### **1.4. Admixtures**

*   **Definition:** Admixtures are ingredients other than cement, water, and aggregates added to concrete during or before mixing. They are used to modify the properties of fresh or hardened concrete.
*   **Common Types and Their Functions:**
    *   **Air-entraining admixtures:** Improve resistance to freezing and thawing cycles and de-icing salts by entraining microscopic air bubbles.
    *   **Water-reducing admixtures (Plasticizers/Superplasticizers):** Reduce the amount of mixing water required for a given slump, leading to higher strength or improved workability.
    *   **Retarding admixtures:** Slow down the rate of hydration, delaying the setting time. Useful in hot weather or for long hauls.
    *   **Accelerating admixtures:** Speed up the rate of hydration, shortening the setting time and increasing early strength. Useful in cold weather or for rapid construction.
    *   **Mineral admixtures (Supplementary Cementitious Materials - SCMs):** Such as fly ash, silica fume, and ground granulated blast furnace slag (GGBS). They react with cementitious materials or contribute to binding, improving strength, durability, and reducing heat of hydration.
*   **Importance:** Admixtures allow for tailoring concrete properties to specific project requirements and environmental conditions.

#### **1.5. Reinforcing Steel**

*   **Definition:** Steel bars (rebar) or welded wire fabric (WWF) used to enhance the tensile strength of concrete, which is inherently weak in tension.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Deformed Bars:** Have ribs or lugs to improve bond with concrete.
    *   **Welded Wire Fabric (WWF):** A grid of steel wires welded at intersections, often used for crack control.
    *   **Tie Bars:** Used across contraction joints to hold the slab faces together.
    *   **Dowel Bars:** Smooth, plain bars used across expansion joints and contraction joints to transfer load between adjacent slabs.
*   **Key Properties to Check:**
    *   **Yield Strength:** The stress at which the steel begins to deform plastically.
    *   **Tensile Strength:** The maximum stress the steel can withstand before breaking.
    *   **Ductility:** Ability to deform without fracturing.
    *   **Bond Strength:** Ability to transfer stress to the concrete.
*   **Importance:** Reinforcement prevents or controls cracking due to thermal expansion/contraction, shrinkage, and traffic loads.

### **2. Preparation of Subgrade and Base**

The subgrade and base layers are critical for the performance of PCC pavements. They provide support and help distribute loads.

#### **2.1. Subgrade Preparation**

*   **Definition:** The subgrade is the natural soil formation upon which the pavement structure is built.
*   **Steps:**
    *   **Clearing and Grubbing:** Removal of vegetation, topsoil, roots, and other organic matter.
    *   **Excavation and Filling:** Excavating unsuitable material and filling with suitable compacted soil to achieve the required formation level.
    *   **Moisture Conditioning:** Adjusting the moisture content of the subgrade soil to achieve optimum moisture content (OMC) for compaction.
    *   **Compaction:** Densifying the soil to increase its strength, stiffness, and reduce its susceptibility to moisture changes and settlement.
        *   *Methods:* Rollers (e.g., sheepsfoot, pneumatic, vibratory) are used.
        *   *Specifications:* Compaction is typically specified by a minimum relative compaction (e.g., 95% of Standard Proctor Density).
    *   **Proof Rolling:** Rolling the prepared subgrade with a heavy roller to detect any soft spots that might require removal and recompaction.
    *   **Surface Finishing:** Achieving the required grade and cross-slope.

*   **Importance:** A well-prepared and compacted subgrade provides a stable and uniform foundation for the pavement structure, preventing excessive settlements and stress concentrations.

#### **2.2. Base Course Construction**

*   **Definition:** The base course is a layer of granular material or stabilized material placed directly below the PCC slab. It provides structural support, acts as a drainage layer, and prevents pumping.
*   **Types of Base Courses:**
    *   **Granular Base:** Typically composed of crushed stone or gravel.
    *   **Stabilized Base:** Granular material stabilized with cement, lime, or asphalt.
    *   **Lean Concrete Base (LCB):** A low-strength concrete mix.
*   **Construction Steps (for Granular Base):**
    *   **Material Placement:** Spreading the granular material in layers.
    *   **Moisture Conditioning:** Adding water to achieve OMC.
    *   **Compaction:** Compacting each layer to specified density.
        *   *Methods:* Vibratory rollers are commonly used for granular bases.
        *   *Specifications:* Similar to subgrade compaction, specified by relative compaction.
    *   **Surface Finishing:** Ensuring proper grade and smoothness.
*   **Importance:** The base course distributes traffic loads, prevents subgrade pumping (where fines are pumped out of the base/subgrade due to repeated traffic loading and pore water pressure), and provides a stable platform for concrete paving.

### **3. Presetting Reinforcement in Joints and PCC Slab Construction**

Reinforcement plays a crucial role in PCC pavements, especially at joints.

#### **3.1. Types of Joints in PCC Pavements**

Joints are intentionally created to control cracking caused by shrinkage and thermal stresses.

*   **Contraction Joints (Dummy Joints):**
    *   **Purpose:** To create weakened planes where the concrete can crack in a controlled manner due to shrinkage and thermal contraction.
    *   **Construction:** Grooves are formed in the slab, typically to 1/4 to 1/3 of the slab depth.
    *   **Reinforcement:**
        *   **Tie Bars:** Deformed steel bars are placed across contraction joints to hold the slab faces together, preventing excessive opening and infiltration of incompressible materials. They resist the pulling forces caused by contraction.
        *   **No Load Transfer Devices:** Tie bars do not provide load transfer; they only maintain aggregate interlock and hold the faces together.
*   **Expansion Joints:**
    *   **Purpose:** To accommodate expansion of concrete due to temperature increases and moisture gain, preventing compressive stresses that could cause blow-ups.
    *   **Construction:** Full-depth gaps are created, filled with a compressible material (e.g., fiberboard, cork, rubberized material).
    *   **Reinforcement:**
        *   **Dowel Bars:** Smooth, round steel bars (often coated to prevent bonding with concrete) are inserted across expansion joints. They are typically installed in sleeves on one side to allow for movement.
        *   **Function:** Dowel bars allow for horizontal movement of the slabs while transferring loads between adjacent slabs, preventing faulting.
*   **Construction Joints:**
    *   **Purpose:** Formed when concrete pouring is stopped and resumed, typically at the end of a day's work.
    *   **Reinforcement:**
        *   **Tie Bars:** Usually used to tie the new slab to the previously placed slab.
        *   **Dowel Bars:** May also be used for load transfer, depending on design.

#### **3.2. Presetting Reinforcement**

*   **Definition:** The process of accurately placing and securing reinforcement (tie bars and dowel bars) before concrete placement.
*   **Methodology:**
    *   **Tie Bars:** Placed at mid-depth of the slab and secured to pre-fabricated chairs or stakes to ensure they remain in position during concrete pouring. They are typically installed across contraction joints.
    *   **Dowel Bars:** Inserted into pre-drilled holes in forms or placed in a support system (e.g., cages) and accurately positioned at the joint location. For expansion joints, dowels are inserted into sleeves on one end to allow longitudinal movement.
*   **Importance:** Accurate placement of reinforcement is critical for effective load transfer and crack control. Misplaced reinforcement will not perform its intended function.

#### **3.3. PCC Slab Construction**

*   **Definition:** The process of mixing, transporting, placing, compacting, finishing, and curing the concrete to form the pavement slab.
*   **Methods of Construction:**

    *   **a) Conventional Paving (Fixed Form Paving):**
        *   **Process:**
            1.  **Formwork Setup:** Metal or wooden forms are set up to define the edges of the slab and joints.
            2.  **Reinforcement Placement:** Tie bars and dowel bars are placed and secured in position.
            3.  **Concrete Placement:** Concrete is transported (e.g., by truck mixers) and placed within the forms, often using a paver or spreader.
            4.  **Consolidation:** Concrete is vibrated to remove entrapped air and ensure a dense mix.
            5.  **Finishing:** The surface is leveled and smoothed using screeds, floats, and trowels.
            6.  **Joint Cutting:** Contraction joints are cut shortly after the concrete has hardened sufficiently to prevent uncontrolled cracking.
            7.  **Curing:** The concrete surface is kept moist and at a suitable temperature to ensure proper hydration and strength development.
        *   **Advantages:** Suitable for various slab thicknesses and geometries, good surface finish.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Slower, requires formwork.

    *   **b) Slipform Paving:**
        *   **Process:**
            1.  **Slipform Paver Machine:** A specialized machine that extrudes concrete into the desired slab shape as it moves forward. It has continuous side forms that move with the machine.
            2.  **Reinforcement Placement:** Tie bars are often automatically inserted by the paver, while dowel bars are pre-set or manually placed.
            3.  **Concrete Placement:** Concrete is supplied directly to the paver's hopper from concrete trucks.
            4.  **Consolidation:** Internal vibrators within the paver consolidate the concrete.
            5.  **Finishing:** The rear of the paver includes finishing devices (e.g., floats, texturing equipment).
            6.  **Joint Cutting:** Sawing of joints is still required for contraction joints.
        *   **Advantages:** Faster, continuous operation, no need for fixed forms (except at edges or when joining existing pavement), generally more economical for large projects.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Requires precise subgrade preparation, less flexibility in shaping complex geometries, can be sensitive to variations in concrete consistency.

    *   **c) Other Methods (Less Common for Highway Pavements):**
        *   **Precast Concrete Pavements:** Slabs are manufactured off-site and then transported and placed on the prepared base. Used for specific applications like bridge decks or rapid repair.

#### **3.4. Curing of PCC Pavements**

*   **Definition:** The process of maintaining adequate moisture content and temperature in concrete for a specific period to ensure proper hydration and strength development.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Water Curing:** Ponding, spraying, wet coverings (burlap, cotton mats).
    *   **Membrane Curing:** Application of liquid curing compounds that form a continuous film to prevent moisture evaporation.
    *   **Steam Curing:** Used in precast concrete production for rapid strength gain.
*   **Importance:** Proper curing is vital for achieving the designed strength, durability, and resistance to surface abrasion and cracking. Inadequate curing can lead to premature failure.

### **Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:**
What is the primary purpose of dowel bars in expansion joints of PCC pavements?
a) To tie the slab faces together and prevent widening.
b) To transfer load between adjacent slabs and allow for longitudinal movement.
c) To control cracking due to shrinkage.
d) To increase the flexural strength of the slab.

**Answer:** b) To transfer load between adjacent slabs and allow for longitudinal movement.

**Question 2:**
Which type of admixture is used to improve the resistance of concrete to freezing and thawing cycles?
a) Water-reducing admixture
b) Retarding admixture
c) Air-entraining admixture
d) Accelerating admixture

**Answer:** c) Air-entraining admixture

**Question 3:**
Describe the key differences between conventional paving and slipform paving for concrete pavements.

**Answer:**
*   **Conventional Paving:** Uses fixed formwork to define the slab edges, requiring more labor and time for form setup and removal.
*   **Slipform Paving:** Uses a machine with moving side forms that extrudes the concrete into the desired shape. It is faster, continuous, and does not require fixed forms, leading to economies of scale for large projects.

**Question 4:**
Why is proper compaction of the subgrade and base layers essential before placing a PCC slab?

**Answer:** Proper compaction increases the strength and stiffness of the subgrade and base, reduces their susceptibility to moisture changes and settlement, and prevents pumping. This provides a stable and uniform foundation for the PCC slab, ensuring its long-term performance and preventing premature failure.

**Question 5:**
What is the function of tie bars in contraction joints, and how do they differ in function from dowel bars?

**Answer:** Tie bars are used in contraction joints to hold the slab faces together, preventing excessive opening due to shrinkage and thermal contraction. They ensure that aggregate interlock is maintained. Dowel bars, on the other hand, are used in expansion joints and some construction joints to transfer loads between adjacent slabs while allowing for longitudinal movement. Tie bars do not facilitate load transfer; they only tie the slabs together.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Material Quality:** The performance of a PCC pavement is heavily dependent on the quality of all its constituent materials (cement, aggregates, water, admixtures, reinforcement).
*   **Subgrade/Base Support:** A well-prepared and compacted subgrade and base are fundamental to distributing loads and preventing pavement distress.
*   **Joint Design and Placement:** Joints are critical for managing stresses. The type of joint and the correctly placed reinforcement (tie bars and dowel bars) are essential for their function.
*   **Construction Method Selection:** The choice between conventional and slipform paving depends on project size, complexity, and cost considerations.
*   **Curing is Crucial:** Never underestimate the importance of proper curing for achieving the intended strength and durability of the concrete.
