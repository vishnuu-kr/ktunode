# PAVEMENT DESIGN AND CONSTRUCTION: Module 1 - Pavement: Functions and Characteristics

## Topic: Requirements and Tests on Aggregates, Bitumen, and Subgrade Soil (CBR Value)

### 1. Aggregates

Aggregates are granular materials, such as sand, gravel, and crushed stone, that form the bulk of pavement layers. Their quality significantly influences the strength, durability, and overall performance of the pavement.

#### 1.1. Requirements for Aggregates in Pavement Construction

The suitability of aggregates depends on their physical and chemical properties. Key requirements include:

*   **Strength:** Aggregates must be able to withstand the loads and stresses applied by traffic without crushing or disintegration.
*   **Durability:** They should resist weathering, abrasion, and chemical degradation, especially in adverse climatic conditions.
*   **Hardness:** Resistance to scratching and wear.
*   **Toughness:** Resistance to fracture under impact.
*   **Shape and Texture:**
    *   **Shape:** Angular or flaky aggregates are generally preferred for interlocking and better stability in base and sub-base layers. Rounded aggregates are less desirable but can be used in certain applications.
    *   **Texture:** Rough or gritty texture provides better adhesion with bitumen.
*   **Cleanliness:** Aggregates should be free from deleterious materials like clay, silt, organic matter, and mica, which can weaken the pavement structure and reduce the effectiveness of bitumen binder.
*   **Gradation (Particle Size Distribution):** A well-graded aggregate mixture, containing a range of particle sizes, is crucial for achieving dense packing, good stability, and minimizing voids. This ensures efficient load distribution and reduced permeability.
*   **Absorption and Adhesion:** Aggregates should have low absorption capacity and good adhesion properties with bitumen to ensure a strong bond.

#### 1.2. Tests on Aggregates

Various tests are conducted to evaluate the properties of aggregates:

*   **Sieve Analysis:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the particle size distribution (gradation) of the aggregate.
    *   **Procedure:** Aggregates are passed through a series of sieves with progressively smaller openings. The weight retained on each sieve is measured and expressed as a percentage of the total weight.
    *   **Importance:** Essential for designing aggregate blends and ensuring compliance with specifications for different pavement layers.
*   **Impact Value Test (for toughness):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the resistance of aggregates to sudden shock or impact.
    *   **Procedure:** A standard aggregate sample is subjected to repeated blows from a standard hammer in a testing machine. The percentage of fine material passing a 2.36 mm sieve after impact is the impact value.
    *   **Acceptable Limit (typical):** For wearing courses (surface layer), impact value should not exceed 20%. For other layers, it can be up to 30%.
*   **Crushing Value Test (for strength):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the resistance of aggregates to crushing under gradual compressive load.
    *   **Procedure:** A cylindrical sample of aggregate is subjected to a gradually increasing load in a compression testing machine. The percentage of material passing a 2.36 mm sieve after applying a load of 40 tonnes is the crushing value.
    *   **Acceptable Limit (typical):** For wearing courses, crushing value should not exceed 30%. For other layers, it can be up to 50%.
*   **Abrasion Value Test (for hardness/resistance to wear):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the resistance of aggregates to wear by friction.
    *   **Procedure:** A sample of aggregate is placed in a Los Angeles Abrasion Machine (a rotating steel cylinder with steel balls) and rotated for a specified number of revolutions. The percentage of material passing a 1.70 mm sieve after the test is the abrasion value.
    *   **Acceptable Limit (typical):** For wearing courses, abrasion value should not exceed 30%. For other layers, it can be up to 40%.
*   **Water Absorption Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the amount of water an aggregate can absorb.
    *   **Procedure:** Aggregate is dried, weighed, and then immersed in water for a specified period. The difference in weights (dry vs. saturated surface dry) indicates absorption.
    *   **Importance:** High absorption can affect the bitumen-aggregate bond and the overall performance of asphalt mixes.
*   **Flakiness and Elongation Index Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To measure the proportion of flaky (thickness less than 0.6 times its width) and elongated (length greater than 1.8 times its width) particles in the aggregate.
    *   **Procedure:** Aggregates are passed through specific slotted sieves to separate flaky and elongated particles.
    *   **Importance:** Excessive flaky and elongated particles can lead to poor workability, reduced density, and increased potential for cracking in asphalt mixes.

**Example:** For a surface course of an asphalt pavement, you would select aggregates that have a low impact value (e.g., < 20%), low crushing value (e.g., < 30%), and low abrasion value (e.g., < 30%).

### 2. Bitumen (Asphalt Binder)

Bitumen is a black, viscous, semi-solid or solid cementitious material, which is soluble in carbon disulfide and is a byproduct of petroleum distillation. It acts as the binder that holds the aggregates together in asphalt pavements.

#### 2.1. Types of Bitumen and Uses

Bitumen is primarily classified based on its penetration value or viscosity.

*   **Penetration Grade Bitumen:**
    *   **Description:** Classified by the distance (in tenths of a millimeter) a standard needle penetrates vertically into the bitumen at a temperature of 25°C and a load of 100 g for 5 seconds. Common grades are 30/40, 60/70, 80/100, 150/200.
    *   **Uses:**
        *   **Penetration Grade 30/40 & 40/50:** High viscosity, used for hot weather conditions and heavy traffic roads in tropical countries. Also suitable for seal coats and mastic asphalt.
        *   **Penetration Grade 60/70 & 70/80:** Medium viscosity, widely used for general purpose asphalt concrete mixes for wearing courses and binder courses.
        *   **Penetration Grade 80/100 & 100/120:** Lower viscosity, used for regions with cold weather conditions and lighter traffic, or for producing cutbacks.
*   **Viscosity Grade Bitumen (VG):**
    *   **Description:** Classified based on the kinematic viscosity of the bitumen at 60°C. Common grades are VG-10, VG-20, VG-30, VG-40. This is a more modern classification.
    *   **Uses:**
        *   **VG-10:** Suitable for spray applications and seal coats.
        *   **VG-20:** Used for surface dressing and single/multiple coat seals.
        *   **VG-30:** Suitable for open-graded premixes and requires careful handling due to its viscosity.
        *   **VG-40:** Used for asphalt concrete mixes in areas with heavy traffic and high temperatures.
*   **Modified Bitumen:**
    *   **Description:** Bitumen modified with polymers (e.g., Styrene-Butadiene-Styrene - SBS, Ethylene Vinyl Acetate - EVA) or other additives to improve its performance characteristics like elasticity, resistance to rutting, fatigue cracking, and temperature susceptibility.
    *   **Uses:** High-traffic roads, airport runways, bridges, and areas requiring enhanced durability and performance.
*   **Blown Bitumen (Oxidized Bitumen):**
    *   **Description:** Produced by blowing air through heated bitumen. This process increases its viscosity and softens it.
    *   **Uses:** Primarily used for roofing felt, waterproofing, and electrical insulation.

#### 2.2. Tests on Bitumen

*   **Penetration Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To measure the hardness or consistency of bitumen.
    *   **Procedure:** A standard needle is allowed to penetrate the bitumen sample under specified temperature (25°C), load (100g), and time (5s). The depth of penetration is measured.
    *   **Importance:** Used for classifying bitumen into penetration grades.
*   **Softening Point Test (Ring and Ball Method):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the temperature at which bitumen becomes soft and starts to deform under its own weight.
    *   **Procedure:** A bitumen sample is placed in a ring, and two steel balls are placed on top. The ring assembly is heated at a standard rate. The temperature at which the balls descend and touch a needle is recorded.
    *   **Importance:** Indicates the temperature susceptibility of bitumen. Higher softening points are desirable for hot climates to prevent rutting.
*   **Ductility Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To measure the ability of bitumen to deform or stretch before breaking.
    *   **Procedure:** A briquette of bitumen is pulled apart at a standard speed and temperature (25°C). The distance to which it can be stretched before breaking is the ductility.
    *   **Importance:** A long ductile thread indicates good binding properties and resistance to cracking.
*   **Viscosity Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To measure the resistance to flow of bitumen at a specified temperature.
    *   **Procedure:** Various methods like Saybolt Furol, Kinematic Viscosity (using capillary viscometers).
    *   **Importance:** Crucial for designing asphalt mixes and determining appropriate mixing and compaction temperatures.
*   **Flash and Fire Point Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the temperature at which bitumen vapors ignite in the presence of a flame (flash point) and sustain combustion (fire point).
    *   **Procedure:** The bitumen sample is heated, and a flame is applied periodically until ignition occurs.
    *   **Importance:** Safety precaution during handling and heating of bitumen. Higher flash points are safer.
*   **Loss on Heating Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the amount of volatile constituents (e.g., moisture, light oils) lost when bitumen is heated at a specified temperature (163°C) for 5 hours.
    *   **Procedure:** A sample of bitumen is heated in an oven, and the weight loss is measured.
    *   **Importance:** Excessive loss can indicate loss of valuable binding material and affect the performance of the asphalt mix.

**Example:** For an asphalt concrete mix in a hot climate, you would typically use a bitumen with a higher softening point and a lower penetration grade (e.g., 60/70 or VG-30/VG-40) compared to a mix for a cold climate.

### 3. Subgrade Soil and CBR Value

The subgrade is the natural ground or engineered fill that supports the pavement layers. Its strength and stability are critical for the overall performance and longevity of the pavement.

#### 3.1. Requirements for Subgrade Soil

*   **Adequate Strength:** The subgrade must be able to support the loads transmitted from the pavement layers without excessive deformation or failure.
*   **Good Drainage:** Should allow water to drain away effectively to prevent saturation, which can significantly reduce its strength.
*   **Stability:** Should be resistant to volume changes due to moisture fluctuations (swelling or shrinkage), especially in expansive soils.
*   **Resistance to Compaction:** Should be amenable to compaction to achieve desired density and stiffness.

#### 3.2. California Bearing Ratio (CBR) Value

The CBR test is a widely used empirical test to determine the strength and load-bearing capacity of subgrade soils, sub-base, and base course materials.

*   **Purpose:** To evaluate the relative strength of a soil by comparing the load required to penetrate it with a standard plunger to the load required to penetrate a standard crushed stone base.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  A soil sample is compacted in a mold to a standard density.
    2.  The compacted sample is soaked in water for a specified period (typically 4 days) to simulate saturated conditions.
    3.  A standard plunger (typically 50 mm diameter) is forced into the soaked soil sample at a standard rate.
    4.  The load at which the plunger penetrates to specific depths (2.5 mm and 5.0 mm) is recorded.
    5.  The **CBR value** is calculated as the ratio of the load at a given penetration to the standard load for that penetration, expressed as a percentage:
        $CBR (\%) = \frac{\text{Load in kN at specified penetration}}{\text{Standard Load in kN at specified penetration}} \times 100$
        *   Standard Load for 2.5 mm penetration = 1370 kN (or 1395 kgf)
        *   Standard Load for 5.0 mm penetration = 2055 kN (or 2097 kgf)
    6.  If the load at 5.0 mm penetration is greater than the load at 2.5 mm penetration, the CBR value at 5.0 mm is used. If the ratio of load at 5.0 mm to load at 2.5 mm is less than 1.0, a correction factor is applied.

*   **Importance:**
    *   **Pavement Thickness Design:** The CBR value is a primary input for designing the thickness of flexible pavement layers. Lower CBR values require thicker pavement structures.
    *   **Material Selection:** Helps in selecting suitable materials for sub-base and base courses.
    *   **Quality Control:** Used to ensure the strength of compacted subgrade and base materials.

*   **Typical CBR Value Requirements (examples, actual values vary by design standards):**
    *   **Subgrade:** CBR values can range from less than 10% (poor) to over 50% (excellent). A minimum CBR of 3-5% is often required for pavement construction.
    *   **Sub-base/Base Course:** Higher CBR values are required, typically > 80% for base courses and > 50% for sub-base courses.

**Example:** If a soil sample requires a load of 500 kN to penetrate the plunger to 2.5 mm, its CBR value would be calculated as (500 kN / 1370 kN) * 100% = 36.5%. This soil would be considered a good subgrade material.

### 4. Bituminous Emulsions and Cutbacks

These are modified forms of bitumen used for specific pavement applications, particularly when working at lower temperatures or for surface treatments.

#### 4.1. Bituminous Emulsions

*   **Description:** A stable dispersion of very fine bitumen globules in water, with the aid of an emulsifying agent (emulsifier).
*   **Types (based on charge of bitumen globules):**
    *   **Anionic Emulsion:** Bitumen globules have a negative charge (e.g., RS-1, RS-2, MS-1, SS-1). The emulsifying agent is an anionic surfactant.
    *   **Cationic Emulsion:** Bitumen globules have a positive charge (e.g., CRS-1, CRS-2, CMS-2, CSS-1). The emulsifying agent is a cationic surfactant.
    *   **Non-ionic Emulsion:** Bitumen globules have no net charge.
*   **Uses:**
    *   **Prime Coat:** Applied to an untreated granular base course to bind dust particles and provide a surface for adhesion of the subsequent layer. Usually uses slow-setting (SS) or slow-setting cationic (CSS) emulsions.
    *   **Tack Coat:** Applied between successive layers of asphalt pavement to ensure good bonding. Usually uses rapid-setting (RS) or rapid-setting cationic (CRS) emulsions.
    *   **Surface Dressing (Chip Seal):** Application of emulsion followed by a layer of aggregate.
    *   **Slurry Seal:** A mix of fine aggregate, emulsion, water, and additives.
    *   **Cold Mix Asphalt:** For road repairs and construction in cooler climates.
*   **Advantages:** Lower application temperatures, safer to handle, environmentally friendlier due to water as a diluent.

#### 4.2. Cutback Bitumen

*   **Description:** Bitumen dissolved in a volatile petroleum solvent (e.g., kerosene, naphtha, diesel). The solvent evaporates after application, leaving the bitumen binder.
*   **Types (based on viscosity and solvent type):**
    *   **Rapid Curing (RC) Cutbacks:** Use light, volatile solvents (like naphtha). Evaporate quickly, suitable for sprayed seal coats and tack coats where fast setting is needed. (e.g., RC-70, RC-250)
    *   **Medium Curing (MC) Cutbacks:** Use medium volatile solvents (like kerosene). Evaporate at a medium rate, suitable for prime coats and mixed-in-place construction. (e.g., MC-70, MC-250)
    *   **Slow Curing (SC) Cutbacks:** Use heavy, low-volatile solvents (like diesel fuel). Evaporate slowly, suitable for dust palliatives and some low-traffic road surface treatments. (e.g., SC-70, SC-250)
*   **Uses:** Similar to emulsions – prime coats, tack coats, surface dressing, cold mixes.
*   **Disadvantages:** Higher cost due to solvents, environmental concerns due to volatile organic compounds (VOCs), fire hazard during handling.

**Example:** For a prime coat on a base layer, you might use an MC-70 cutback bitumen or a CSS-1 emulsion, both designed for good penetration and binding of fine particles.

## Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of the Sieve Analysis test performed on aggregates?
**Answer:** The primary purpose of the Sieve Analysis test is to determine the particle size distribution (gradation) of the aggregate. This information is crucial for designing asphalt mixes and ensuring compliance with specifications for different pavement layers, as it affects density, stability, and void content.

**Question 2:** A bitumen sample shows a penetration of 70 at 25°C and 100g load. What does this indicate about its consistency, and what grade of bitumen is it likely to be?
**Answer:** A penetration of 70 indicates that the bitumen is of medium consistency. It is likely to be a Penetration Grade 60/70 or 70/80 bitumen. This grade is commonly used for asphalt concrete mixes for wearing and binder courses in moderate climatic conditions.

**Question 3:** Explain why the CBR value of a subgrade soil is critical in pavement design.
**Answer:** The CBR value of a subgrade soil is critical because it represents the soil's load-bearing capacity. A lower CBR value indicates weaker soil, requiring a thicker pavement structure to distribute traffic loads effectively and prevent excessive deformation or failure of the pavement. It is a key parameter used in calculating the required pavement thickness.

**Question 4:** What is the difference between a Rapid Curing (RC) cutback and a Cationic Rapid Setting (CRS) emulsion in terms of their composition and setting mechanism?
**Answer:**
*   **RC Cutback:** Composed of bitumen dissolved in a volatile petroleum solvent (e.g., naphtha). It sets by the evaporation of the solvent.
*   **CRS Emulsion:** Composed of bitumen globules dispersed in water with a cationic emulsifier (positive charge). It sets through a combination of solvent evaporation (if present, less common in CRS) and primarily by the breaking of the emulsion due to loss of water and the coagulation of bitumen particles onto the aggregate surface, often accelerated by the positive charge of the bitumen globules interacting with negatively charged aggregate surfaces.

**Question 5:** Name three essential tests performed on aggregates to assess their suitability for pavement construction and briefly state their purpose.
**Answer:**
1.  **Impact Value Test:** To assess the toughness or resistance to sudden shock.
2.  **Crushing Value Test:** To assess the strength or resistance to crushing under gradual load.
3.  **Abrasion Value Test:** To assess the hardness or resistance to wear by friction.

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Aggregate Quality:** The performance of any pavement layer is heavily dependent on the quality and properties of the aggregates used. Always check for compliance with specified tests.
*   **Bitumen Grade Selection:** The choice of bitumen grade (penetration or viscosity) should be based on climatic conditions, traffic volume, and type of pavement layer.
*   **CBR is Key for Subgrade:** The subgrade's strength, as indicated by its CBR value, dictates the overall pavement thickness and cost.
*   **Emulsions vs. Cutbacks:** Emulsions are generally preferred over cutbacks due to environmental and safety reasons (lower VOCs, lower application temperatures).
*   **Adhesion:** Proper adhesion between bitumen binder and aggregates is crucial for a durable asphalt pavement. This is influenced by aggregate cleanliness, texture, and bitumen properties.
*   **Temperature Susceptibility:** Bitumen's properties change significantly with temperature. Tests like softening point and penetration help characterize this behavior.
*   **Gradation is Crucial:** Well-graded aggregates lead to denser mixes with better stability and reduced voids.
