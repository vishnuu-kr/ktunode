# Air and Noise Pollution Control Engineering: Module 3 - Control Principles

## Topic: Fabric Filters

### 1. Introduction to Fabric Filters

Fabric filters, also known as baghouses or bag filters, are widely used air pollution control devices that remove particulate matter from industrial exhaust gases. They operate by passing the gas stream through a fabric material, which captures the particles.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Particulate Matter (PM):** Microscopic solid or liquid particles suspended in the air.
*   **Collection Efficiency:** The percentage of particulate matter removed from the gas stream.
*   **Pressure Drop:** The resistance to airflow caused by the fabric filter, measured as the difference in pressure between the inlet and outlet.
*   **Cake Resistance:** The resistance to airflow offered by the accumulated dust layer on the fabric.

**How they work:**

1.  **Gas Inlet:** Dirty gas enters the filter housing.
2.  **Fabric Filtration:** The gas passes through fabric bags or cartridges. Particles are retained on the fabric surface.
3.  **Clean Gas Outlet:** Cleaned gas exits the filter.
4.  **Dust Removal (Cleaning Cycle):** Periodically, the collected dust is removed from the fabric to maintain airflow and efficiency.

**Learning Outcome 1: Explain the working principles of fabric filters.**

Fabric filters primarily operate on two mechanisms:

*   **Inertial Impaction:** Larger particles, due to their inertia, cannot follow the tortuous path of the gas stream around the fabric fibers and collide with them.
*   **Interception:** Medium-sized particles, while following the gas flow, come into contact with the fabric fibers as they pass by and adhere to them.
*   **Diffusion (Brownian Motion):** Very small particles (sub-micron) move randomly due to collisions with gas molecules. This increased random movement leads to a higher probability of them colliding with and adhering to the fabric fibers.
*   **Sieving:** Particles larger than the pores in the fabric are physically trapped. This mechanism is more significant after a dust cake has formed.

**The Formation of the "Dust Cake":**

Initially, the fabric itself provides the filtration. However, as dust accumulates, it forms a layer called the "dust cake" on the fabric surface. This dust cake becomes the primary filtering medium, offering much higher efficiency for smaller particles than the bare fabric alone. The dust cake builds up a porous structure that effectively traps fine particles through sieving and surface deposition.

**Learning Outcome 2: Describe the basic components of a fabric filter.**

A typical fabric filter system consists of the following key components:

*   **Housing:** The outer structure that encloses the filter bags, cages, and cleaning mechanism. It prevents leakage of dirty gas and protects the internal components.
*   **Filter Bags (or Cartridges):** The core filtration media. These are typically cylindrical bags made of woven or felted fabric. Cartridges are folded structures that offer a larger filter area in a more compact design.
    *   **Material:** Selection depends on temperature, chemical composition of the gas, and particle characteristics (e.g., polyester, polypropylene, fiberglass, PTFE, P84).
*   **Support Cages (for Pulse-Jet and Shaker-type):** Internal metal cages that support the filter bags, preventing them from collapsing during operation and aiding in dust cake removal.
*   **Hopper:** A conical or pyramidal section at the bottom of the housing where collected dust accumulates.
*   **Dirty Gas Inlet:** The ductwork that directs the dust-laden gas into the filter housing.
*   **Clean Gas Outlet:** The ductwork that carries the filtered gas out of the housing.
*   **Cleaning Mechanism:** Devices used to remove the accumulated dust cake from the filter bags. Common types include:
    *   **Shaker Mechanism:** Mechanical shakers that vibrate the bags to dislodge the dust cake.
    *   **Pulse-Jet Mechanism:** A blast of compressed air (pulse) injected into the bag from the top, creating a shockwave that dislodges the dust.
    *   **Reverse Air Mechanism:** A flow of clean air is blown in the reverse direction through the bags to collapse them and release the dust.
*   **Dust Discharge System:** Mechanisms to remove the dislodged dust from the hopper, such as screw conveyors, rotary valves, or airlocks.
*   **Bag/Cartridge Removal and Replacement Mechanism:** Access points and procedures for replacing worn-out filter bags or cartridges.

**Learning Outcome 3: Discuss the different types of fabric filters and their operational differences.**

Fabric filters are primarily classified based on their cleaning mechanism:

**1. Shaker-Type Fabric Filters:**

*   **Mechanism:** Filter bags are hung vertically and are mechanically shaken by a rotating arm or linkage system.
*   **Operation:** Gas flows from outside to inside the bags (or inside to outside). When cleaning is initiated, the gas flow is typically stopped or reduced, and the bags are shaken. The dust cake falls into the hopper.
*   **Advantages:** Relatively simple design, lower initial cost.
*   **Disadvantages:** Lower cleaning efficiency compared to pulse-jet, potential for bag abrasion due to shaking, requires periodic shutdown for cleaning, limited to moderate temperatures.
*   **Applications:** Wood processing, cement plants, grain handling.

**2. Pulse-Jet Fabric Filters:**

*   **Mechanism:** Filter bags are typically mounted horizontally or vertically with the dirty gas flowing from inside to outside (most common). A blast of compressed air is injected into the top of each bag for a very short duration.
*   **Operation:** The compressed air creates a shockwave that travels down the bag, flexing it and dislodging the dust cake. Cleaning occurs continuously or in discrete intervals without significantly interrupting gas flow.
*   **Advantages:** High cleaning efficiency, continuous operation possible, no internal moving parts in the gas stream, higher dust loading capacity, can handle higher temperatures (depending on fabric).
*   **Disadvantages:** Higher initial cost due to compressed air system, higher operating cost (compressed air consumption), potential for bag damage from over-pulsing.
*   **Applications:** Power plants (fly ash removal), asphalt plants, steel manufacturing, chemical processing.

**3. Reverse Air Fabric Filters:**

*   **Mechanism:** Gas flows from outside to inside the bags. During cleaning, a fan blows clean air in the reverse direction (inside to outside) through the bags, causing them to collapse and dislodge the dust.
*   **Operation:** Typically, a compartment containing a group of bags is isolated from the main gas flow, and reverse air is applied.
*   **Advantages:** No moving parts in contact with dust (except the fan), gentle cleaning action, can handle sticky or abrasive dust.
*   **Disadvantages:** Requires multiple compartments for continuous operation, lower gas-to-cloth ratio, higher capital cost for multiple compartments.
*   **Applications:** Lime kilns, cement plants, gypsum plants.

**Example:**

Imagine a cement plant producing fine cement dust. A pulse-jet baghouse would be a good choice here because cement dust is abrasive and sticky. The strong pulse of air effectively removes the cake without damaging the bags, and the continuous operation ensures high throughput.

**Learning Outcome 4: Explain the factors affecting the performance of fabric filters.**

Several factors significantly influence the performance (efficiency, pressure drop, lifespan) of fabric filters:

*   **Gas Flow Rate:** Higher flow rates can lead to increased pressure drop and reduced residence time for effective particle capture.
*   **Temperature:** Exceeds the thermal limits of the fabric, leading to degradation and loss of filtration efficiency. High temperatures also reduce gas density, affecting pressure drop.
*   **Moisture Content:** Excessive moisture can cause dust to cake and become sticky, leading to blinding of the fabric and increased pressure drop. Condensation can also cause corrosion.
*   **Dust Characteristics:**
    *   **Particle Size Distribution:** Smaller particles are harder to capture and can lead to higher pressure drops.
    *   **Particle Shape and Surface Properties:** Sticky, oily, or fibrous particles can adhere strongly to the fabric, making cleaning difficult and leading to blinding.
    *   **Abrasiveness:** Abrasive dust can wear down the fabric, reducing its lifespan.
    *   **Dust Loading:** Higher dust concentrations increase the rate of cake formation, leading to higher pressure drops and more frequent cleaning cycles.
*   **Fabric Properties:**
    *   **Fiber Material:** Affects temperature resistance, chemical resistance, and filtration characteristics.
    *   **Weave/Construction (woven vs. felted):** Felted fabrics generally offer higher efficiency due to their thicker, porous structure and better dust cake adhesion.
    *   **Permeability (Air-to-Cloth Ratio):** Directly impacts pressure drop and the required cleaning frequency. A lower air-to-cloth ratio generally means lower pressure drop but requires a larger filter area.
*   **Cleaning Cycle Effectiveness:** Inadequate cleaning leads to excessive pressure drop and reduced filter capacity. Over-cleaning can lead to premature bag wear.
*   **Bag Installation and Maintenance:** Proper installation and regular inspection of bags are crucial to prevent bypass and leaks.

**Learning Outcome 5: Discuss the selection criteria for fabric filters.**

Choosing the right fabric filter system involves considering various factors:

*   **Emission Standards and Required Efficiency:** Regulatory requirements dictate the maximum allowable particulate emissions.
*   **Gas Stream Characteristics:**
    *   **Temperature:** Determines the fabric material and housing design.
    *   **Chemical Composition:** Corrosive gases may require specific fabric treatments or materials.
    *   **Moisture Content:** High moisture may necessitate special handling or pre-conditioning.
    *   **Flammability/Explosivity:** Requires spark detection, explosion venting, and appropriate materials.
*   **Dust Characteristics:** (as discussed in factors affecting performance)
    *   **Particle Size and Loading:** Influences the required filter area and cleaning mechanism.
    *   **Abrasiveness, Stickiness, or Corrosiveness:** Guides fabric selection and cleaning strategy.
*   **Operational Requirements:**
    *   **Continuous vs. Intermittent Operation:** Dictates the need for multiple compartments.
    *   **Pressure Drop Tolerance:** Influences the air-to-cloth ratio selection.
    *   **Space Availability:** Cartridge filters offer a more compact solution.
    *   **Maintenance Requirements:** Ease of bag replacement and accessibility.
*   **Capital and Operating Costs:** Budget constraints play a significant role.
*   **Reliability and Lifespan:** The expected operational life of the system.

**Example:**

For a hot gas stream from a steel furnace with abrasive dust, fiberglass fabric with a special coating and a pulse-jet cleaning system would likely be selected. Fiberglass can withstand higher temperatures, the coating improves abrasion resistance, and the pulse-jet offers efficient cleaning of abrasive material.

**Learning Outcome 6: Describe the cleaning methods employed in fabric filters.**

As discussed earlier, the three primary cleaning methods are:

*   **Mechanical Shaking:**
    *   **How it works:** Bags are vigorously shaken, causing the dust cake to break and fall into the hopper.
    *   **Pros:** Simple mechanism, lower cost.
    *   **Cons:** Less effective for sticky dust, can cause bag abrasion, requires shutdown.
*   **Pulse Jet Cleaning:**
    *   **How it works:** A short burst of high-pressure compressed air is directed down the inside of the bag, creating a shockwave that dislodges the dust.
    *   **Pros:** Highly effective, continuous operation, no mechanical stress on bags.
    *   **Cons:** Requires compressed air supply, higher initial cost.
*   **Reverse Air Cleaning:**
    *   **How it works:** Clean air is blown in the reverse direction through the bag, causing it to collapse and shed the dust.
    *   **Pros:** Gentle on bags, effective for sticky dust.
    *   **Cons:** Requires multiple compartments for continuous operation, less effective for very fine dust unless combined with other methods.

**Important Note:** The choice of cleaning method is crucial and depends heavily on the specific application and dust characteristics.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Explain the difference between inertial impaction and diffusion in particle capture by fabric filters.

**Answer:**
*   **Inertial Impaction:** Larger particles, due to their mass and momentum, cannot easily follow the gas flow around the filter fibers and collide with them.
*   **Diffusion:** Very small particles move randomly due to collisions with gas molecules (Brownian motion). This random movement increases their chance of hitting and adhering to the filter fibers.

**Question 2:** List the main components of a fabric filter system and briefly describe the function of each.

**Answer:**
*   **Housing:** Encloses the system.
*   **Filter Bags/Cartridges:** The filtration media.
*   **Support Cages:** Support the bags and aid cleaning.
*   **Hopper:** Collects the dislodged dust.
*   **Inlet/Outlet Ducts:** Direct gas flow.
*   **Cleaning Mechanism:** Removes accumulated dust.
*   **Dust Discharge System:** Removes dust from the hopper.

**Question 3:** Compare and contrast pulse-jet and shaker-type fabric filters, highlighting their advantages and disadvantages.

**Answer:**
*   **Pulse-Jet:**
    *   *Advantages:* High cleaning efficiency, continuous operation, no mechanical stress on bags.
    *   *Disadvantages:* Higher initial cost, requires compressed air.
*   **Shaker-Type:**
    *   *Advantages:* Simpler design, lower initial cost.
    *   *Disadvantages:* Lower cleaning efficiency, potential for bag abrasion, requires shutdown for cleaning.

**Question 4:** A cement plant needs to control particulate emissions. What factors would you consider when selecting a fabric filter for this application, and what type of fabric filter would you recommend? Justify your choice.

**Answer:**
Factors to consider:
*   **Dust Characteristics:** Cement dust is fine, abrasive, and can be somewhat sticky.
*   **Gas Temperature:** Usually moderate in cement plants.
*   **Emission Standards:** Strict for cement plants.
*   **Operational Continuity:** Continuous operation is desirable.

**Recommendation:** A **pulse-jet fabric filter** would be a suitable choice.
*   **Justification:** The pulse-jet system provides effective cleaning for abrasive and potentially sticky dust without causing excessive bag wear. Its continuous operation is advantageous for the high throughput of cement plants. The bags are typically made of materials like polyester or acrylic felt, which are suitable for moderate temperatures and offer good filtration of fine particles.

**Question 5:** How does the formation of a "dust cake" affect the performance of a fabric filter?

**Answer:**
The dust cake, once formed, significantly enhances the filtration efficiency, particularly for sub-micron particles, by acting as the primary filtering medium. However, it also increases the resistance to airflow, leading to a higher pressure drop. Effective cleaning is essential to manage this pressure drop and maintain performance.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Fabric filters are highly efficient for removing fine particulate matter.**
*   **The "dust cake" is crucial for high-efficiency filtration but also increases pressure drop.**
*   **Fabric material selection is critical and depends on temperature, chemical composition, and particle characteristics.**
*   **The cleaning mechanism is the key differentiator between fabric filter types.**
*   **Proper selection and operation are essential to meet emission standards and ensure long-term performance.**
*   **Regular maintenance and inspection are vital for optimal operation and longevity.**
