### **1. Introduction to Particulate Matter (PM) Emission**

#### **1.1. What is Particulate Matter?**

*   **Definition:** Particulate matter (PM) refers to a complex mixture of solid and liquid particles suspended in the air. These particles vary in size, shape, chemical composition, and origin.
*   **Classification by Size:**
    *   **Coarse PM (PM10):** Particles with an aerodynamic diameter less than or equal to 10 micrometers ($\mu$m).
        *   *Sources:* Dust, construction activities, road dust, industrial grinding.
    *   **Fine PM (PM2.5):** Particles with an aerodynamic diameter less than or equal to 2.5 $\mu$m.
        *   *Sources:* Combustion processes (engines, power plants), industrial emissions, secondary aerosols formed in the atmosphere.
    *   **Ultrafine PM (UFP):** Particles with diameters less than 0.1 $\mu$m.
        *   *Sources:* Combustion, industrial processes, atmospheric reactions.

#### **1.2. Sources of Particulate Emissions**

*   **Natural Sources:**
    *   Volcanic eruptions
    *   Wildfires
    *   Dust storms
    *   Sea salt spray
    *   Pollen
*   **Anthropogenic (Man-made) Sources:**
    *   **Industrial Processes:**
        *   Combustion (coal, oil, natural gas in power plants, boilers)
        *   Manufacturing (cement production, steel mills, foundries, chemical plants)
        *   Mining and quarrying
        *   Agricultural activities (tilling, harvesting)
        *   Wood processing
    *   **Transportation:**
        *   Diesel and gasoline engines (exhaust, tire wear, brake wear)
    *   **Residential Sources:**
        *   Wood burning stoves and fireplaces
        *   Residential boilers

#### **1.3. Impacts of Particulate Matter**

*   **Health Impacts:**
    *   **Respiratory System:** Inhalation of PM can lead to irritation, inflammation, reduced lung function, asthma exacerbation, bronchitis, and emphysema.
    *   **Cardiovascular System:** Fine and ultrafine particles can enter the bloodstream, contributing to heart attacks, strokes, and other cardiovascular diseases.
    *   **Other Impacts:** Some PM components can be toxic, carcinogenic, or mutagenic.
*   **Environmental Impacts:**
    *   **Visibility Reduction (Haze):** PM scatters and absorbs sunlight, reducing visibility.
    *   **Climate Change:** Certain PM components (e.g., black carbon) absorb solar radiation, contributing to warming, while others (e.g., sulfates) can have a cooling effect by scattering sunlight.
    *   **Damage to Vegetation:** PM deposition on plant surfaces can reduce photosynthesis and growth.
    *   **Acid Rain:** Sulfates and nitrates in PM can contribute to acid deposition.
    *   **Soiling of Buildings and Structures:** PM can accumulate on surfaces, causing aesthetic damage.

### **2. Principles of Particulate Control**

#### **2.1. Physical Principles of Particle Collection**

Particulate control devices utilize various physical forces to separate particles from a gas stream. The key principles include:

*   **Gravitational Settling:** Particles settle out of the gas stream due to gravity. This is effective for larger particles.
*   **Inertial Impaction:** Particles, due to their inertia, tend to continue in a straight line when the gas stream changes direction, leading to collision with a collecting surface. This is effective for larger and denser particles.
*   **Interception:** Particles following the gas streamline come close enough to a collecting surface to touch and adhere. This is effective for intermediate-sized particles.
*   **Diffusion (Brownian Motion):** Very small particles (< 0.1 $\mu$m) exhibit random motion due to collisions with gas molecules, increasing their probability of colliding with a collecting surface.
*   **Electrostatic Attraction:** Particles are charged and then attracted to oppositely charged collecting surfaces.
*   **Filtration:** Particles are physically blocked or captured by a porous medium.

#### **2.2. Particle Size and Collection Efficiency**

*   The effectiveness of a control device is often expressed as its **collection efficiency**, which is the fraction of incoming particulate mass removed.
*   **Collection efficiency is strongly dependent on particle size.** Different devices are designed to be more effective for specific size ranges.
*   **Cut-off Diameter (or Cut Point):** The particle size at which a device achieves a specific collection efficiency (often 50%). A smaller cut-off diameter indicates a more effective device for smaller particles.

### **4. Detailed Description of Particulate Control Technologies**

#### **4.1. Mechanical Collectors**

##### **4.1.1. Settling Chambers**

*   **Princ:** Relies on gravitational settling. Gas flows horizontally through a large chamber, allowing larger particles to settle to the bottom.
*   **Mechanism:** Reduced gas velocity in a large cross-sectional area decreases the upward drag force on particles, allowing gravity to dominate.
*   **Design:** Simple, rectangular box with inlet and outlet ducts. Particles settle on the floor and are removed by a hopper.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Efficiency:** Low, typically effective for particles > 50 $\mu$m.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple design, low capital cost, low operating cost, no moving parts, can handle hot gases and corrosive materials.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Low efficiency for fine particles, large footprint, high residence time required.
*   **Example:** Pre-cleaners for very coarse dust in mining or quarrying operations.

##### **4.1.2. Cyclone Separators**

*   **Princ:** Utilizes centrifugal force to separate particles from the gas stream.
*   **Mechanism:** Gas enters tangentially into a cylindrical or conical body. The swirling motion creates a centrifugal force that throws larger particles outward towards the walls, where they slide down into a collection hopper. The cleaned gas exits through a central vortex finder.
*   **Design:**
    *   **Inlet:** Tangential entry.
    *   **Body:** Cylindrical or conical.
    *   **Vortex Finder:** Central tube for clean gas exit.
    *   **Dust Outlet/Hopper:** Collects separated particles.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Standard Cyclone:** Most common design.
        *   **High-Efficiency Cyclone:** Shorter, smaller diameter for increased centrifugal force.
        *   **Long Cyclone:** For better efficiency but higher pressure drop.
        *   **Reverse-Flow Cyclone:** Gas enters tangentially, spins, and exits in the opposite direction from the inlet.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Efficiency:** Moderate, typically effective for particles > 10-15 $\mu$m. Efficiency decreases significantly for particles < 10 $\mu$m.
    *   **Advantages:** Relatively low capital and operating costs, simple design, compact, can handle high temperatures and corrosive/abrasive materials, no moving parts, can be used in series for higher efficiency.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Limited efficiency for fine particles, high pressure drop in high-efficiency designs, wear can be an issue with abrasive dust.
*   **Example:** Dust collection in woodworking shops, grain elevators, cement plants (as primary collectors).

#### **4.2. Fabric Filters (Baghouses)**

*   **Princ:** Uses porous fabric bags or envelopes to physically filter particles from the gas stream.
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  **Inertial Impaction & Interception:** Larger particles collide with the fabric fibers.
    2.  **Entanglement:** Particles are caught within the weave of the fabric.
    3.  **Diffusion:** Smaller particles collide with fibers due to Brownian motion.
    4.  **Fabric Dust Cake:** A layer of collected dust builds up on the fabric surface, which becomes the primary filtering medium and significantly increases collection efficiency.
*   **Design:**
    *   **Housing:** Enclosure for the filter bags.
    *   **Filter Bags:** Cylindrical bags made of various fabrics (e.g., polyester, fiberglass, PTFE, P84) depending on temperature, chemical environment, and particle characteristics.
    *   **Support Cages:** Metal cages to support the bags and prevent collapse.
    *   **Dirty Gas Inlet:** Where flue gas enters.
    *   **Clean Gas Outlet:** Where filtered gas exits.
    *   **Cleaning Mechanism:** Crucial for maintaining airflow and preventing excessive pressure drop.
        *   **Shaker Cleaning:** Mechanical shaking of the bags.
        *   **Reverse Air Cleaning:** Flow of clean air in the reverse direction.
        *   **Pulse-Jet Cleaning:** Short bursts of compressed air injected into the bag from the top.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Efficiency:** Very high, typically > 99.9% for most particle sizes, including fine particles. Can achieve PM2.5 removal efficiencies of > 99%.
    *   **Advantages:** Excellent collection efficiency for all particle sizes, reliable, can handle varying gas flow rates, relatively compact compared to settling chambers, can recover valuable products.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High capital cost, can be expensive to operate and maintain (bag replacement), sensitive to moisture and sticky/oily dust (can blind the bags), high pressure drop can increase fan power requirements, fire and explosion hazards with combustible dusts.
*   **Example:** Coal-fired power plants, cement kilns, asphalt plants, steel mills, chemical manufacturing.

#### **4.3. Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs)**

*   **Princ:** Uses electrostatic forces to remove particles.
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  **Charging:** Particles in the gas stream are passed through a strong electric field created by high-voltage discharge electrodes. This imparts an electrical charge to the particles (typically negative).
    2.  **Collection:** The charged particles are then passed through a region with collecting electrodes (plates or pipes) that are maintained at a high positive potential. The electrostatic force between the charged particles and the collecting electrodes causes the particles to migrate and deposit on the collecting surfaces.
    3.  **Removal:** The collected dust (called "dust cake") is periodically removed from the collecting electrodes by rapping or vibrating mechanisms, causing it to fall into hoppers below.
*   **Design:**
    *   **Discharge Electrodes (Coronas):** Typically thin wires or sharp points that generate a corona discharge and ionize the gas molecules, which then charge the particles.
    *   **Collecting Electrodes:** Large plates or pipes that attract the charged particles.
    *   **Power Supply:** High-voltage DC power supply.
    *   **Rapping System:** Mechanisms to dislodge the collected dust.
    *   **Hopper:** For dust collection.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Plate ESPs:** Most common, with parallel plates.
        *   **Tubular ESPs:** Cylindrical collecting electrodes with discharge electrodes inside.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Efficiency:** Very high, typically 95-99.9%, and can be increased by adding more stages. Excellent for fine particles.
    *   **Advantages:** Very high collection efficiency for fine particles, can handle large volumes of flue gas, low pressure drop (saving fan power), can operate at high temperatures, continuous operation possible.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High capital cost, sensitive to gas resistivity (very high or very low resistivity can reduce efficiency), can be affected by high moisture content, risk of re-entrainment of dust during rapping, require high voltage.
*   **Example:** Large coal-fired power plants, cement plants, pulp and paper mills.

#### **4.4. Wet Scrubbers**

*   **Princ:** Uses a liquid (usually water) to capture particles.
*   **Mechanism:**
    *   **Impaction:** Particles collide with liquid droplets or a liquid film.
    *   **Interception:** Particles follow the liquid stream and get trapped.
    *   **Diffusion:** Small particles are captured by liquid droplets.
    *   **Absorption:** Gases may also be dissolved in the scrubbing liquid.
*   **Types of Wet Scrubbers:**
    *   **Spray Towers:** Gas passes through a cloud of atomized liquid droplets.
    *   **Venturi Scrubbers:** Gas is forced through a narrow venturi throat where it meets a high-velocity spray of liquid. The high turbulence and shear forces lead to efficient droplet-particle contact and particle de-entrainment.
    *   **Packed Bed Scrubbers:** Gas passes through a bed of packing material wetted by a liquid.
    *   **Plate Scrubbers:** Gas bubbles through liquid on trays.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Efficiency:** Moderate to high, depending on the type and operating parameters. Venturi scrubbers are very effective for fine particles (e.g., > 1 $\mu$m with high pressure drop). Other types are generally better for larger particles.
    *   **Advantages:** Simultaneously removes particles and gaseous pollutants (e.g., SO2), cools hot gases, can handle sticky or explosive dusts, relatively compact.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Produces a wet sludge or wastewater that requires treatment and disposal, can cause corrosion, can lead to plume reheating issues (visible steam plumes), higher operating cost due to water usage and fan power (especially for Venturi scrubbers).
*   **Example:** Power plants (especially for SO2 and PM removal), chemical plants, metal processing.

#### **4.5. Particulate Filters (e.g., HEPA)**

*   **Princ:** Similar to fabric filters but use much finer and denser media, often made of fiberglass or synthetic materials.
*   **Mechanism:** Primarily diffusion and interception due to the very small pore sizes.
*   **Performance:** Extremely high efficiency (e.g., > 99.97% for 0.3 $\mu$m particles for HEPA filters).
*   **Advantages:** Very high removal efficiency for even ultrafine particles.
*   **Disadvantages:** Very high pressure drop, requires frequent replacement, suitable for low gas flow rates and clean air applications, not typically used for large industrial flue gas treatment.
*   **Example:** Clean rooms, biological containment, laboratory fume hoods, some HVAC systems.

### **5. Factors Affecting Device Selection**

When selecting a particulate control device, several factors must be considered:

*   **Particle Size Distribution:** The dominant particle size in the emission stream is critical. Fine particles require more efficient devices.
*   **Required Removal Efficiency:** Regulatory limits dictate the necessary efficiency.
*   **Gas Flow Rate and Temperature:** Affects the size and type of device. High temperatures may limit fabric choices for baghouses.
*   **Gas Composition:** Presence of corrosive gases or moisture can influence material selection and device type (e.g., favouring ESPs or specific wet scrubbers over baghouses).
*   **Particle Properties:**
    *   **Abrasiveness:** Can cause wear in cyclones and baghouses.
    *   **Stickiness/Tackiness:** Can blind baghouse filters or cause rapping issues in ESPs.
    *   **Combustibility/Explosiveness:** Requires careful design considerations (e.g., explosion vents, inerting for baghouses).
    *   **Electrical Resistivity (for ESPs):** High or low resistivity significantly impacts ESP performance.
*   **Cost:**
    *   **Capital Cost:** Initial purchase and installation cost.
    *   **Operating Cost:** Energy consumption (fans, power), maintenance, consumables (bags, scrubbing liquid), waste disposal.
*   **Space Availability:** Some devices require more space than others.
*   **Simultaneous Removal of Gaseous Pollutants:** Wet scrubbers offer this advantage.

### **7. Practice Questions & Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Which of the following particulate control devices is most effective for removing particles with an aerodynamic diameter of 0.5 $\mu$m?
A. Settling Chamber
B. Cyclone Separator
C. Fabric Filter
D. All are equally effective

**Answer:** C. Fabric Filters are known for their excellent efficiency in removing fine and ultrafine particles due to the mechanisms of diffusion and the formation of a dust cake.

**Question 2:**
Describe the primary operational principle of a cyclone separator and list two of its main advantages and disadvantages.

**Answer:**
*   **Princ:** Cyclone separators use centrifugal force generated by a swirling gas flow to separate particles from the gas stream. Larger, denser particles are thrown towards the outer walls and settle into a hopper, while the cleaned gas exits centrally.
*   **Advantages:**
    1.  Low capital and operating costs.
    2.  Simple design, robust, and requires minimal maintenance.
    3.  Can handle high temperatures and corrosive/abrasive materials.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    1.  Low efficiency for fine particles (< 10 $\mu$m).
    2.  Wear can be an issue with abrasive dust.

**Question 3:**
A power plant burning coal needs to control particulate emissions to meet stringent environmental regulations requiring very high removal efficiency for PM2.5.
a) Suggest two suitable particulate control devices for this application.
b) For each suggested device, briefly explain its operating principle for PM2.5 removal.

**Answer:**
a) Two suitable devices are Fabric Filters (Baghouses) and Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs).

b)
*   **Fabric Filter (Baghouse):**
    *   **Operating Principle for PM2.5:** Fine particles are captured through a combination of diffusion (random motion of small particles colliding with fibers), interception (particles following gas streamlines and touching fibers), and inertial impaction. The buildup of a "dust cake" on the fabric surface acts as an extremely effective filter medium, enhancing the removal of even the smallest particles.
*   **Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP):**
    *   **Operating Principle for PM2.5:** Flue gas containing PM2.5 passes through discharge electrodes that create a corona discharge, imparting a negative charge to the particles. These charged particles are then drawn towards positively charged collecting plates due to electrostatic forces. The strong electrostatic attraction effectively removes the fine particles from the gas stream.

**Question 4:**
Compare and contrast Wet Scrubbers and Fabric Filters in terms of their primary advantages and disadvantages for particulate control.

**Answer:**
*   **Wet Scrubbers:**
    *   **Primary Advantages:** Can simultaneously remove gaseous pollutants (like SO2) along with particulate matter; cooling of hot gases.
    *   **Primary Disadvantages:** Produce wastewater requiring disposal; potential for corrosion; can result in visible steam plumes.
*   **Fabric Filters (Baghouses):**
    *   **Primary Advantages:** Very high collection efficiency for all particle sizes, including PM2.5 and ultrafine particles; reliable and proven technology.
    *   **Primary Disadvantages:** High capital cost; sensitive to moisture and sticky dust; no simultaneous removal of gaseous pollutants.

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Particle size is the most critical factor** determining the effectiveness of particulate control devices.
*   **Collection efficiency is particle-size dependent.**
*   **Fabric filters and ESPs are the workhorses for fine particulate control** in large industrial applications.
*   **Mechanical collectors (cyclones) are good pre-cleaners** but are not sufficient for fine PM control alone.
*   **Wet scrubbers offer the advantage of dual removal** of PM and gaseous pollutants.
*   **Device selection is a multi-faceted decision** involving technical, economic, and operational considerations.
*   **Maintenance and proper operation** are crucial for the long-term performance of any particulate control device.
