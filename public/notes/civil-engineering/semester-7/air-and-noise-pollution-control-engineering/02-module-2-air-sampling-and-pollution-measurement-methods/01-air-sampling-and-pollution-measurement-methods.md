# AIR AND NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL ENGINEERING

## Module 2: Air Sampling and Pollution Measurement Methods

---

### Introduction to Air Sampling and Pollution Measurement

This module focuses on the fundamental principles and techniques used to measure the concentration and nature of air pollutants. Accurate measurement is crucial for understanding the extent of pollution, assessing its impact on human health and the environment, and evaluating the effectiveness of control strategies.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   **Understand the principles of air sampling and measurement.**
*   **Categorize different types of air pollutants and their measurement techniques.**
*   **Differentiate between direct-reading and indirect-reading instruments.**
*   **Explain the working principles of various sampling and measurement instruments.**
*   **Discuss the importance of quality assurance and quality control (QA/QC) in air monitoring.**
*   **Understand the concepts of ambient air monitoring and source emission monitoring.**

### 1. Principles of Air Sampling and Measurement

**1.1 Purpose of Air Sampling and Measurement:**

*   **Assessing ambient air quality:** To determine the concentration of pollutants in the general atmosphere and compare it with regulatory standards.
*   **Evaluating source emissions:** To measure the amount of pollutants released from industrial stacks, vehicles, or other sources.
*   **Monitoring occupational exposure:** To assess the concentration of hazardous substances in the workplace.
*   **Evaluating the effectiveness of control technologies:** To determine if pollution control devices are functioning as intended.
*   **Research and scientific studies:** To understand the sources, transport, and fate of air pollutants.
*   **Legal and regulatory compliance:** To ensure adherence to air quality standards and regulations.

**1.2 Key Concepts:**

*   **Pollutant:** A substance or energy introduced into the environment that has undesired effects, or that adversely affects the usefulness of a resource.
*   **Concentration:** The amount of a substance present in a given volume or mass of air. Typically expressed as:
    *   Parts per million (ppm)
    *   Parts per billion (ppb)
    *   Micrograms per cubic meter ($\mu g/m^3$)
    *   Milligrams per cubic meter ($mg/m^3$)
    *   Percentage (%)
*   **Sampling:** The process of collecting a representative portion of air or gaseous emissions for analysis.
*   **Measurement/Analysis:** The process of determining the quantity or concentration of a specific pollutant in the collected sample or directly in the air.
*   **Accuracy:** The degree of closeness of measurements of a quantity to that quantity's actual (true) value.
*   **Precision:** The degree to which repeated measurements of the same quantity under the same conditions agree with each other.
*   **Sensitivity:** The minimum concentration of a substance that an instrument can detect.
*   **Selectivity:** The ability of an instrument to measure a specific substance without interference from other substances.
*   **Response Time:** The time it takes for an instrument to reach a steady reading after a change in concentration.

### 2. Categories of Air Pollutants and Their Measurement

Air pollutants can be broadly categorized, and their measurement techniques often depend on their physical and chemical properties.

**2.1 Gaseous Pollutants:**

*   **Examples:** Sulfur dioxide ($SO_2$), nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$), carbon monoxide ($CO$), ozone ($O_3$), volatile organic compounds (VOCs), hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$), ammonia ($NH_3$).
*   **Measurement Principles:**
    *   **Absorption/Chemisorption:** Pollutants are absorbed into a liquid or solid sorbent, which then undergoes chemical analysis.
    *   **Spectroscopy:** Interaction of electromagnetic radiation with the pollutant molecule.
    *   **Electrochemical Detection:** Pollutants react with electrodes to produce an electrical signal.
    *   **Chromatography:** Separation of compounds based on their physical or chemical properties, followed by detection.

**2.2 Particulate Matter (PM):**

*   **Definition:** Microscopic solid or liquid particles suspended in the air.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Primary Particulates:** Emitted directly from a source (e.g., soot, dust).
    *   **Secondary Particulates:** Formed in the atmosphere through chemical reactions (e.g., sulfates, nitrates).
*   **Measurement Principles:**
    *   **Gravimetric Analysis:** Collecting particles on a filter and measuring their mass. This is the reference method for PM concentration.
    *   **Optical Methods:** Measuring light scattering or absorption by particles.
    *   **Beta Attenuation:** Measuring the attenuation of beta radiation as it passes through a collected particulate sample.
    *   **Light Scattering/Nephelometry:** Measuring the amount of light scattered by particles.

### 3. Direct-Reading vs. Indirect-Reading Instruments

**3.1 Direct-Reading Instruments (Real-time Analyzers):**

*   **Definition:** Instruments that provide an immediate, continuous measurement of pollutant concentration in the air without requiring a separate sampling and laboratory analysis step.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Provide real-time data.
    *   Useful for monitoring fluctuating concentrations and identifying emission events.
    *   Often used for continuous ambient air quality monitoring and process control.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Infrared (IR) Analyzers:** For $CO$, $CO_2$.
    *   **Chemiluminescence Analyzers:** For $NO_x$, $O_3$.
    *   **Flame Ionization Detectors (FID):** For VOCs.
    *   **Electrochemical Sensors:** For $SO_2$, $CO$, $H_2S$.
    *   **Particle Counters:** For counting and sizing particles.

**3.2 Indirect-Reading Instruments (Sampling/Integrative Methods):**

*   **Definition:** Instruments that collect a sample of air over a period of time, which is then analyzed in a laboratory or by a separate analytical procedure.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Provide averaged concentrations over a sampling period.
    *   Can be more sensitive and selective.
    *   Often used for regulatory compliance, specialized analysis, and source emission monitoring where direct-reading is not feasible.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **High-Volume Air Samplers (Hi-Vol):** For measuring total suspended particulates (TSP) and $PM_{10}$.
    *   **Impinger Trains:** Air is bubbled through liquid solutions to absorb gaseous pollutants (e.g., $SO_2$ with sodium tetrachloromercurate).
    *   **Filter-based samplers:** For collecting particulate matter for gravimetric or chemical analysis.
    *   **Adsorbent Tubes:** For collecting VOCs or specific gaseous pollutants.

### 4. Working Principles of Various Sampling and Measurement Instruments

This section delves into the operational principles of common air monitoring instruments.

**4.1 Gravimetric Analysis for Particulate Matter:**

*   **Princ:** Air is drawn through a pre-weighed filter at a known flow rate for a specific duration. The collected particulate matter is weighed, and the concentration is calculated.
*   **Instrumentation:**
    *   **High-Volume Air Sampler (Hi-Vol):** Uses a large diameter filter (e.g., 8-inch x 10-inch) and a high flow rate pump. Primarily for TSP and $PM_{10}$ analysis.
    *   **Personal Monitoring Devices:** Smaller, portable samplers used to measure personal exposure.
    *   **Micro-Orifice Uniform Deposit Impactor (MOUDI):** Collects particles in different size fractions.
*   **Calculation:**
    $PM Concentration = \frac{(Weight\ of\ filter\ with\ sample) - (Weight\ of\ blank\ filter)}{Volume\ of\ air\ sampled}$

**4.2 Gas Chromatography (GC):**

*   **Princ:** A mixture of compounds is separated by passing it through a stationary phase (column) under the influence of a mobile phase (carrier gas). Different components elute from the column at different times and are detected by a detector.
*   **Applications:** Analysis of VOCs, sulfur compounds, and other organic pollutants.
*   **Detectors:** Flame Ionization Detector (FID), Photoionization Detector (PID), Electron Capture Detector (ECD), Mass Spectrometer (MS).

**4.3 Spectroscopy-Based Instruments:**

*   **Princ:** Based on the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with pollutant molecules. Different molecules absorb or emit radiation at specific wavelengths.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Infrared (IR) Spectroscopy:**
        *   **Non-Dispersive Infrared (NDIR):** Used for gases like $CO$ and $CO_2$. Measures the absorption of IR radiation at specific wavelengths.
        *   **Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR):** Provides a broad spectrum of absorption, allowing for the identification and quantification of multiple gases simultaneously.
    *   **Ultraviolet (UV) Absorption Spectroscopy:**
        *   Used for $O_3$ and $SO_2$. Measures the absorption of UV radiation.
    *   **Chemiluminescence:**
        *   **Princ:** Light is emitted as a result of a chemical reaction.
        *   **For $NO_x$:** Reaction of $NO$ with $O_3$ produces excited $NO_2^*$, which emits light upon relaxation. $NO_2$ is converted to $NO$ in a converter before analysis.
        *   **For $O_3$:** Reaction of $O_3$ with ethylene produces formaldehyde and light.
    *   **Fluorescence:**
        *   **For $SO_2$:** $SO_2$ absorbs UV radiation and then re-emits it at a longer wavelength.

**4.4 Electrochemical Sensors:**

*   **Princ:** The pollutant diffuses to an electrode where it undergoes an electrochemical reaction (oxidation or reduction), generating a current proportional to the pollutant concentration.
*   **Applications:** Portable monitors for $CO$, $SO_2$, $H_2S$, $NO$, $NO_2$, $O_2$.
*   **Advantages:** Small, low power consumption, relatively inexpensive.
*   **Limitations:** Can be affected by temperature, humidity, and cross-interference from other gases.

**4.5 Impinger Sampling:**

*   **Princ:** Air is bubbled through a liquid absorbent in a glass vessel (impinger). The pollutant reacts with or dissolves in the absorbent.
*   **Absorbents:**
    *   $SO_2$: Sodium tetrachloromercurate (Mercuric chloride solution).
    *   $NO_x$: Sulfanilic acid, followed by N-(1-Naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride (Griess-Saltzman reagent).
    *   $NH_3$: Dilute sulfuric acid.
*   **Analysis:** The absorbed pollutant is then analyzed using wet chemistry methods (e.g., colorimetric analysis, titrations).
*   **Sampling Train:** Often includes a particulate filter upstream of the impingers to remove PM.

### 5. Quality Assurance and Quality Control (QA/QC) in Air Monitoring

QA/QC is essential to ensure the reliability, accuracy, and validity of air monitoring data.

**5.1 Quality Assurance (QA):**

*   A system of activities intended to ensure that the monitoring program meets the intended objectives and that the data generated are of the required quality.
*   Includes planning, documentation, training, and procedures.

**5.2 Quality Control (QC):**

*   The routine application of procedures and techniques to monitor and maintain the quality of the monitoring process and the data.
*   Includes:
    *   **Calibration:** Adjusting instrument readings to match known standards.
        *   **Zero Calibration:** Using a "zero air" gas (free of the target pollutant) to set the baseline.
        *   **Span Calibration:** Using a calibration gas of known concentration to verify the instrument's response.
    *   **Performance Audits:** Independent evaluation of the monitoring system's performance using audit gases or samples.
    *   **Field Blanks/Zero Spikes:** Samples collected in the field to check for contamination or instrumental drift.
    *   **Duplicate Samples:** Collecting two samples simultaneously from the same location to assess precision.
    *   **Data Validation:** Reviewing data for completeness, consistency, and adherence to protocols.
    *   **Instrument Maintenance:** Regular servicing and repair of monitoring equipment.

**5.3 Important QA/QC Elements:**

*   **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs):** Detailed written instructions for all monitoring activities.
*   **Training and Certification:** Ensuring personnel are adequately trained.
*   **Documentation:** Maintaining accurate records of all calibration, maintenance, and sampling activities.
*   **Traceability:** Ensuring calibration standards are traceable to national or international standards.

### 6. Ambient Air Monitoring vs. Source Emission Monitoring

**6.1 Ambient Air Monitoring:**

*   **Objective:** To measure the concentration of pollutants in the general atmosphere that people breathe.
*   **Location:** Typically conducted at fixed sites in urban or rural areas, downwind from potential pollution sources.
*   **Instruments:** Continuous analyzers, Hi-Vols, personal monitors.
*   **Parameters:** Often focuses on criteria pollutants ($SO_2$, $NO_x$, $CO$, $O_3$, $PM_{2.5}$, $PM_{10}$).
*   **Focus:** Long-term trends, compliance with air quality standards, public health.

**6.2 Source Emission Monitoring:**

*   **Objective:** To measure the concentration and flow rate of pollutants directly from emission sources (e.g., industrial stacks, vehicle exhaust).
*   **Location:** At the point of emission.
*   **Instruments:**
    *   **Continuous Emission Monitoring Systems (CEMS):** Permanently installed instruments that continuously measure pollutant concentrations and stack gas flow rates.
    *   **Isokinetic Sampling:** A manual sampling technique for particulate matter where the sampling velocity is matched to the gas velocity in the stack to ensure a representative sample of particle sizes.
    *   **Baghouses/Filters:** Used to collect particulate matter from stacks.
    *   **Stack Impingers:** For collecting gaseous pollutants from stacks.
*   **Parameters:** Can include a wider range of pollutants depending on the source (e.g., mercury, heavy metals, hazardous air pollutants).
*   **Focus:** Compliance with emission limits, process control, material balance.

**6.3 Isokinetic Sampling for Particulate Matter:**

*   **Princ:** To collect a representative sample of particulate matter from a moving gas stream (like in a stack), the velocity of the gas entering the sampling nozzle must be equal to the velocity of the gas stream itself. This is achieved by adjusting the sampling flow rate.
*   **Why it's important:** If the sampling velocity is too high, smaller, lighter particles will be swept past the nozzle. If it's too low, larger, heavier particles may not enter the nozzle.
*   **Procedure:** Involves measuring the stack gas velocity and temperature, and then setting the sampling pump flow rate accordingly.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**

What is the primary difference between direct-reading and indirect-reading air monitoring instruments? Provide one example of each.

**Answer:**
Direct-reading instruments provide immediate, real-time measurements of pollutant concentrations without a separate sampling and analysis step. Indirect-reading instruments collect samples over a period, which are then analyzed.
*   **Example of Direct-Reading:** Chemiluminescence analyzer for $NO_x$.
*   **Example of Indirect-Reading:** High-Volume Air Sampler for $PM_{10}$.

**Question 2:**

A Hi-Vol sampler is used to measure $PM_{10}$. After sampling for 24 hours, the filter weighs 150 mg before sampling and 200 mg after sampling. The average flow rate was 1.5 $m^3/min$. Calculate the average $PM_{10}$ concentration in $\mu g/m^3$.

**Answer:**
*   Weight of collected PM = 200 mg - 150 mg = 50 mg
*   Sampling time = 24 hours * 60 min/hour = 1440 minutes
*   Volume of air sampled = Flow rate * Time = 1.5 $m^3/min$ * 1440 min = 2160 $m^3$
*   $PM_{10}$ concentration = $\frac{50\ mg}{2160\ m^3} = 0.02315\ mg/m^3$
*   Convert to $\mu g/m^3$: $0.02315\ mg/m^3 \times 1000\ \mu g/mg = 23.15\ \mu g/m^3$

**Question 3:**

Explain the working principle of a chemiluminescence analyzer used for measuring nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$).

**Answer:**
A chemiluminescence analyzer for $NO_x$ works by reacting nitric oxide ($NO$) with ozone ($O_3$). This reaction produces excited nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2^*$), which then emits light as it relaxes to its ground state. The intensity of this emitted light is directly proportional to the concentration of $NO$. To measure total $NO_x$, any nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2$) present in the sample is first converted to $NO$ (usually by passing it through a catalytic converter) before being reacted with ozone.

**Question 4:**

What is the significance of isokinetic sampling for particulate matter measurements in stacks?

**Answer:**
Isokinetic sampling is crucial for accurate particulate matter measurement in stacks because it ensures that the velocity of the air entering the sampling nozzle is equal to the velocity of the gas stream in the stack. This matching of velocities prevents bias in the sample, ensuring that the collected particulate matter is representative of the actual particle size distribution and concentration in the stack. If sampling is not isokinetic, lighter particles might be missed or heavier particles might be preferentially collected, leading to inaccurate concentration measurements.

**Question 5:**

List at least three quality control measures that should be implemented during air monitoring.

**Answer:**
Three quality control measures are:
1.  **Calibration:** Regular zero and span calibrations of instruments.
2.  **Field Blanks/Spikes:** Analyzing blank samples collected in the field or spiking samples with known concentrations to check for contamination or instrumental drift.
3.  **Duplicate Samples:** Collecting and analyzing multiple samples from the same location simultaneously to assess the precision of the measurement method.
4.  **Performance Audits:** Periodic checks by independent auditors.
5.  **Instrument Maintenance:** Regular cleaning, repair, and preventive maintenance of equipment.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Representative Sampling:** The most critical aspect of air monitoring is ensuring that the collected sample accurately reflects the air quality or emission characteristics of the area or source being studied.
*   **Instrument Selection:** The choice of sampling and measurement instrument depends on the pollutant of interest, its concentration range, the required accuracy, the sampling location (ambient vs. source), and regulatory requirements.
*   **Calibration is Key:** All analytical instruments must be regularly calibrated using certified gas standards to ensure accurate measurements.
*   **QA/QC is Non-Negotiable:** Robust QA/QC procedures are vital for data credibility and defensibility, especially for regulatory compliance and public health decisions.
*   **Understanding Limitations:** Every instrument and method has limitations (e.g., cross-sensitivity, interferences, response time). It's important to be aware of these when interpreting data.
*   **Particulate Matter Size:** The size of particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}$, $PM_{10}$, TSP) significantly impacts its health effects and requires specific sampling techniques.
