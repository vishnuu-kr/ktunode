# AIR AND NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL ENGINEERING

## Module 2: Air Sampling and Pollution Measurement Methods

### Topic: The Air Act

---

### **1. Introduction to the Air Act**

*   **Purpose:** The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, is a comprehensive legislation enacted in India to provide for the prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution.
*   **Objective:** To maintain and restore the normal condition of air and to prevent any person from carrying on any industry or operation which may cause or be likely to cause air pollution.
*   **Evolution:** Originally enacted in 1981, it has been amended subsequently to strengthen its provisions and adapt to changing environmental needs. It aligns with the Stockholm Declaration of 1972 on the Human Environment.

---

### **2. Key Provisions of the Air Act**

This section will cover the essential components of the Air Act relevant to air sampling and pollution measurement.

#### **2.1. Definitions**

*   **Air Pollutant:** Any solid, liquid, or gaseous substance including noise introduced into the atmosphere which is injurious to human beings or other living creatures or plants or property or interferes with the enjoyment of life or property.
*   **Air Pollution:** The presence in the atmosphere of air pollutant.
*   **State Board:** The State Pollution Control Board constituted under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974.
*   **Industry:** Any operation or process carried out in any mine or quarry or in any site of construction or demolition or any other operation or process specified by the State Government.
*   **Occupier:** The person who has the control of the affairs of a factory or an establishment.

#### **2.2. Powers and Functions of the Central and State Boards**

*   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):**
    *   Advise the Central Government on matters relating to the prevention and control of air pollution.
    *   Coordinate the activities of the State Boards.
    *   Provide technical assistance and guidance to State Boards.
    *   Lay down standards for the quality of air.
    *   Collect and disseminate information related to air pollution.
    *   Undertake, assist, and encourage studies and investigations relating to the prevention and control of air pollution.
*   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):**
    *   **Prevention and Control of Air Pollution:** Plan the prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution in the respective states.
    *   **Advisory Functions:** Advise the State Government on matters relating to the prevention and control of air pollution.
    *   **Laying Down Standards:** May, with the approval of the CPCB, lay down standards for the quality of air for the State.
    *   **Consent Mechanism:** Grant or refuse consent to industries for operating and discharging pollutants into the atmosphere, subject to conditions.
    *   **Inspection and Monitoring:** Inspect air pollution control equipment and control measures, and collect samples of air and emissions from any specified source.
    *   **Public Information:** Publish information relating to air pollution and its prevention and control.
    *   **Enforcement:** Take legal action against defaulting industries.

#### **2.3. Standards for Air Quality and Emission Standards**

*   **Ambient Air Quality Standards:** The Act empowers the CPCB and SPCBs to lay down standards for air quality in different areas. These standards specify the maximum permissible concentrations of various air pollutants in the ambient atmosphere.
    *   *Example:* National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) specify limits for pollutants like Particulate Matter (PM10, PM2.5), Sulphur Dioxide (SO2), Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2), Carbon Monoxide (CO), Ozone (O3), Lead (Pb), etc.
*   **Emission Standards:** The Act also empowers the boards to lay down standards for emissions from industrial units and other sources. These standards specify the maximum permissible concentration or rate of discharge of pollutants from specific sources.
    *   *Example:* Emission standards for thermal power plants might specify limits for SO2, NOx, and particulate matter per unit of power generated.

#### **2.4. Control of Air Pollution from Industries and Sources**

*   **Consent to Operate:** No person can establish or operate any industrial plant without the consent of the State Board. This consent is granted subject to compliance with prescribed emission standards and installation of air pollution control equipment.
*   **Furnishing of Information:** Occupiers of certain industries are required to furnish information to the State Board regarding the quality and composition of any air pollutant discharged from their premises.
*   **Power to Take Samples:** The State Board has the power to take samples of air or emissions from any specified source for the purpose of analysis. This is a crucial aspect directly related to air sampling and pollution measurement methods.
*   **Power to Give Directions:** The State Board can issue directions to any person or authority for the prevention, control, or abatement of air pollution.
*   **Emergency Provisions:** In case of imminent danger to public health or environment due to air pollution, the State Government or the State Board can take measures to control or mitigate the pollution.

#### **2.5. Penalties and Prosecution**

*   **Offences:** Contravention of any provision of the Act, failure to comply with directions, or carrying on any activity without consent are considered offences.
*   **Punishment:** Penalties include imprisonment and fines, which can be substantial and increase for subsequent offences.
    *   *Example:* If an industry continues to emit pollutants beyond the prescribed limits after receiving a warning, it can face legal prosecution and penalties.

#### **2.6. Sampling and Analysis**

*   **Methodology:** The Act mandates that samples of air and emissions be collected and analysed according to prescribed methods. These methods ensure the accuracy and reliability of the data.
*   **Prescribed Methods:** The Central Government, in consultation with the CPCB, prescribes the methods for sampling and analysis of air pollutants. These methods are detailed in various guidelines and manuals.
    *   *Example:* For particulate matter sampling, the gravimetric method using an isokinetic sampler is often prescribed. For gaseous pollutants, specific absorption techniques followed by chemical analysis or instrumental methods are used.
*   **Role of Laboratories:** Government-approved laboratories play a crucial role in analysing the collected samples.

### **3. Role of the Air Act in Air Sampling and Pollution Measurement**

The Air Act provides the legal framework and mandates the activities related to air sampling and pollution measurement:

*   **Legal Basis for Monitoring:** It empowers regulatory bodies (SPCBs) to conduct air quality monitoring and source emission monitoring.
*   **Standard Setting:** It enables the establishment of ambient air quality standards and emission standards, which are the benchmarks against which measured data is compared.
*   **Data Collection:** The Act necessitates the collection of data on air pollutants through sampling and analysis to assess compliance and identify pollution hotspots.
*   **Enforcement:** The measured data is used to enforce the provisions of the Act, leading to actions against non-compliant industries.
*   **Public Awareness:** By mandating reporting and making information available, the Act contributes to public awareness about air quality.

---

### **4. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Precautionary Principle:** The Act is based on the precautionary principle, emphasizing the need to prevent pollution even in the absence of complete scientific certainty.
*   **Polluter Pays Principle:** While not explicitly stated in the 1981 Act, its enforcement aligns with the polluter pays principle, where polluters are responsible for the costs of pollution control.
*   **Centralized vs. Decentralized Approach:** The Act establishes a balance between the central coordination by CPCB and the decentralized implementation by SPCBs.
*   **Citizen's Suits:** The Act allows for citizen's suits, empowering citizens to initiate legal action against polluters or the government if they fail to perform their duties under the Act.
*   **Inter-State Air Pollution:** The Act also addresses the issue of air pollution that may cross state boundaries.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:** What is the primary objective of the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981?

**Answer:** The primary objective is to provide for the prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution and to maintain and restore the normal condition of air.

---

**Question 2:** Explain the role of State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) in air pollution management as per the Air Act.

**Answer:** SPCBs are responsible for planning the prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution within their states. They advise the State Government, lay down air quality standards (with CPCB approval), grant consent to industries for operation, inspect pollution control equipment, collect samples, and enforce the Act.

---

**Question 3:** Differentiate between ambient air quality standards and emission standards under the Air Act. Provide an example for each.

**Answer:**
*   **Ambient Air Quality Standards:** These are limits for the concentration of pollutants in the general atmosphere. They define the maximum permissible levels of pollutants in the outdoor air we breathe.
    *   *Example:* The NAAQS limit for PM2.5 in residential areas is 60 µg/m³ (annual average).
*   **Emission Standards:** These are limits for the concentration or rate of discharge of pollutants from specific sources, such as industrial chimneys or vehicle exhausts.
    *   *Example:* An emission standard for a thermal power plant might limit SO2 emissions to 600 mg/Nm³ from its boiler.

---

**Question 4:** What power does the Air Act grant to the State Board regarding sampling of air pollutants from industrial sources?

**Answer:** The Air Act grants the State Board the power to enter any building, premises, or vehicle, and to take samples of air or emissions from any specified source for analysis.

---

**Question 5:** What are the implications for an industry if it fails to obtain consent to operate from the State Pollution Control Board?

**Answer:** An industry operating without the required consent from the State Pollution Control Board is liable for prosecution under the Air Act, leading to penalties which can include fines and/or imprisonment. They would also be subject to directions from the Board to cease operations.

---

### **6. Conclusion**

The Air Act, 1981, is a cornerstone legislation in India's environmental protection framework. It provides the essential legal and regulatory backbone for managing air pollution. Understanding its provisions is crucial for anyone involved in air pollution control engineering, particularly concerning the methods and importance of air sampling and pollution measurement. The Act empowers regulatory bodies to set standards, monitor compliance, and take enforcement actions, ultimately aiming to safeguard public health and the environment from the adverse effects of air pollution.
