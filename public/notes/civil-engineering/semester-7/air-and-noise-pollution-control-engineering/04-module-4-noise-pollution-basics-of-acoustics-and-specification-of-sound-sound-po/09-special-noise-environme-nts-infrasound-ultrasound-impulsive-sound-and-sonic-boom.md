# AIR AND NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL ENGINEERING

## Module 4: Noise Pollution - Basics of Acoustics and Specification of Sound; Sound Power

### Topic: Special Noise Environments: Infrasound, Ultrasound, Impulsive Sound, and Sonic Boom

---

### 1. Introduction to Special Noise Environments

While much of noise pollution control focuses on audible sound, certain phenomena operate outside the typical human hearing range or have distinct characteristics that require specialized understanding and control strategies. These "special noise environments" include infrasound, ultrasound, impulsive sound, and sonic booms. Understanding their generation, propagation, effects, and measurement is crucial for comprehensive noise pollution management.

---

### 2. Infrasound

#### 2.1 Definition

*   **Infrasound:** Sound waves with frequencies below the lower limit of human hearing, typically considered to be **below 20 Hz (Hertz)**.

#### 2.2 Characteristics and Properties

*   **Low Frequency:** Characterized by very long wavelengths (many meters).
*   **Long Wavelength:** Allows infrasound to travel long distances with minimal attenuation (absorption). It can diffract around obstacles and penetrate buildings more easily than audible sound.
*   **Low Attenuation:** Less affected by atmospheric conditions like wind and temperature compared to higher frequencies.
*   **Perception:** While not consciously heard, infrasound can be felt as vibrations or a sense of unease. It can affect the human body psychologically and physiologically.

#### 2.3 Sources of Infrasound

*   **Natural Sources:**
    *   **Volcanic eruptions:** The release of seismic energy.
    *   **Earthquakes:** Ground vibrations.
    *   **Thunderstorms:** Large-scale atmospheric pressure fluctuations.
    *   **Ocean waves and surf:** Large water movements.
    *   **Wind gusts:** Turbulence and pressure variations in strong winds.
    *   **Auroras:** Charged particle interactions in the atmosphere.
*   **Man-made Sources:**
    *   **Large industrial machinery:** Fans, compressors, pumps, turbines.
    *   **Vehicles:** Heavy trucks, aircraft engines, ships.
    *   **Wind turbines:** Rotating blades can generate low-frequency noise.
    *   **Explosions:** Demolition activities, seismic surveys.
    *   **HVAC systems:** Large ventilation and air conditioning units.
    *   **Subways and trains:** Passing through tunnels.

#### 2.4 Effects of Infrasound on Humans

*   **Physiological Effects:**
    *   Nausea, dizziness, disorientation.
    *   Fatigue, headaches.
    *   Vibrations in the chest and abdomen.
    *   Potential effects on inner ear balance.
*   **Psychological Effects:**
    *   Irritability, anxiety, unease, fear.
    *   Difficulty concentrating.
    *   Sleep disturbances.

#### 2.5 Measurement and Control of Infrasound

*   **Measurement:** Requires specialized microphones and instrumentation capable of detecting very low frequencies. Standard sound level meters may not accurately capture infrasound levels. Measurement units often use decibels (dB) referenced to specific low frequencies (e.g., dB(G) for overall sound pressure level, including infrasound).
*   **Control:**
    *   **Source Reduction:** Designing machinery to minimize infrasound generation (e.g., slower fan speeds, larger impellers).
    *   **Path Treatment:**
        *   **Isolation:** Mounting machinery on vibration isolators.
        *   **Barriers:** While less effective than for audible sound, large mass barriers can offer some attenuation.
        *   **Enclosures:** Heavy, airtight enclosures can help reduce transmission.
    *   **Building Design:** Using massive construction materials, double-glazed windows, and careful sealing can improve isolation from external infrasound.

### 3. Ultrasound

#### 3.1 Definition

*   **Ultrasound:** Sound waves with frequencies above the upper limit of human hearing, typically considered to be **above 20 kHz (kilohertz)**.

#### 3.2 Characteristics and Properties

*   **High Frequency:** Characterized by very short wavelengths.
*   **Short Wavelength:** Ultrasound is highly directional and attenuates rapidly in the air due to absorption by air molecules. It is easily blocked by barriers.
*   **Perception:** Not consciously heard by humans.

#### 3.3 Sources of Ultrasound

*   **Natural Sources:**
    *   **Bats:** Echolocation.
    *   **Dolphins and Whales:** Communication and echolocation.
    *   **Insects:** Some insects produce ultrasonic calls.
*   **Man-made Sources:**
    *   **Medical Equipment:** Ultrasound imaging (sonography), therapeutic ultrasound.
    *   **Industrial Equipment:** Ultrasonic cleaners, welding machines, non-destructive testing equipment.
    *   **Pest Repellents:** Devices emitting ultrasonic frequencies to deter rodents and insects.
    *   **Sonar Systems:** Used in marine applications.
    *   **High-frequency electronic devices:** Some electronics can emit faint ultrasound.

#### 3.4 Effects of Ultrasound on Humans

*   **Threshold of Perception:** Humans generally do not perceive ultrasound consciously.
*   **Potential Nuisance:** While not heard, very high-intensity ultrasound can cause:
    *   Headaches, nausea, dizziness.
    *   Tinnitus (ringing in the ears).
    *   Discomfort, particularly in individuals with heightened sensitivity or in specific occupations.
*   **Biological Effects:** High-intensity therapeutic ultrasound can have significant biological effects, but this is controlled in medical applications. The concern for noise pollution is from unintended exposure.

#### 3.5 Measurement and Control of Ultrasound

*   **Measurement:** Requires specialized microphones sensitive to high frequencies and appropriate measurement units (e.g., dB(A) or specific frequency band measurements).
*   **Control:**
    *   **Source Reduction:** Designing equipment to minimize ultrasonic emissions.
    *   **Enclosure:** Encasing ultrasonic devices in sound-attenuating materials.
    *   **Shielding:** Using barriers to block the propagation of ultrasound. Due to its directivity and attenuation, simple shielding can be effective.
    *   **Distance:** Increasing distance from the source significantly reduces exposure due to rapid attenuation.

### 4. Impulsive Sound (Impact Noise)

#### 4.1 Definition

*   **Impulsive Sound:** Sound characterized by a **rapid rise in sound pressure, followed by a rapid decay**, often repeated. These are sudden, short-duration sounds.

#### 4.2 Characteristics and Properties

*   **Short Duration:** Typically lasts for milliseconds to a few seconds.
*   **High Peak Sound Pressure:** The peak sound pressure level can be very high, even if the overall energy is low.
*   **Broad Frequency Spectrum:** Impulsive sounds often contain a wide range of frequencies.
*   **Perception:** Perceived as sharp, distinct "bangs" or "thumps."
*   **Damage Potential:** High peak pressures can cause auditory fatigue or damage more readily than continuous noise of the same overall energy, especially if repeated.

#### 4.3 Sources of Impulsive Sound

*   **Industrial:**
    *   Hammering, forging.
    *   Punch presses, stamping machines.
    *   Explosions (controlled demolitions, mining blasts).
    *   Pneumatic tools (riveters, nail guns).
*   **Transportation:**
    *   Aircraft sonic booms (though often considered a separate category).
    *   Car backfiring.
*   **Recreational/Other:**
    *   Firearms.
    *   Fireworks.
    *   Loud music concerts (transient effects).
    *   Slamming doors.

#### 4.4 Effects of Impulsive Sound on Humans

*   **Auditory Effects:**
    *   Temporary Threshold Shift (TTS): A temporary reduction in hearing sensitivity.
    *   Permanent Threshold Shift (PTS) / Noise-Induced Hearing Loss (NIHL): Permanent hearing damage, especially with repeated exposure or very high peak levels.
    *   Tinnitus.
*   **Non-Auditory Effects:**
    *   Startle response, annoyance.
    *   Stress and psychological discomfort.

#### 4.5 Measurement and Control of Impulsive Sound

*   **Measurement:**
    *   Requires instruments with fast response times (e.g., "impulse" or "peak" weighting).
    *   Metrics like Peak Sound Pressure Level (SPLp) are used.
    *   Impulse noise regulations often consider the number of impulses and their peak levels.
*   **Control:**
    *   **Source Reduction:**
        *   Modifying machinery to reduce impact forces (e.g., using pneumatic rather than mechanical presses where possible).
        *   Mufflers and silencers on pneumatic tools.
    *   **Path Treatment:**
        *   **Barriers:** Effective for blocking line-of-sight propagation.
        *   **Enclosures:** Sound-insulating enclosures for noisy machinery.
        *   **Resilient Mounts:** Isolating vibrating components.
    *   **Administrative Controls:**
        *   Limiting exposure time.
        *   Rotating workers.
        *   Using hearing protection.

### 5. Sonic Boom

#### 5.1 Definition

*   **Sonic Boom:** A **distinctive loud sound that is heard when an object traveling through the air at supersonic speed (faster than the speed of sound) produces shock waves**.

#### 5.2 Characteristics and Properties

*   **Supersonic Travel:** The fundamental cause is exceeding Mach 1 (the speed of sound).
*   **Shock Waves:** As an object travels faster than sound, it cannot "get out of its own way." This creates compression waves that coalesce into strong shock waves.
*   **"N-Wave" Signature:** The pressure signature of a sonic boom often resembles the letter "N." It starts with a rapid positive pressure rise (bow shock), followed by a rapid negative pressure drop (tail shock), and then a return to ambient pressure. This "N-wave" is what creates the characteristic "boom-boom" sound.
*   **Directional:** The boom is heard most strongly in a "boom carpet" or "concorde track" that follows the aircraft's flight path.
*   **Magnitude:** Can be very loud, comparable to explosions, and cause significant annoyance and potential damage.

#### 5.3 Sources of Sonic Boom

*   **Supersonic Aircraft:**
    *   Military fighter jets.
    *   Supersonic transport aircraft (e.g., Concorde, though now retired).
    *   Hypersonic vehicles.
*   **Spacecraft Re-entry:** Re-entering the atmosphere at supersonic speeds.
*   **Meteoroids:** Some large meteors create sonic booms as they travel through the atmosphere.

#### 5.4 Effects of Sonic Boom on Humans and Structures

*   **Human Effects:**
    *   **Startle response:** Significant psychological impact.
    *   **Annoyance:** High levels of public complaint.
    *   **Sleep disturbance.**
    *   **Potential temporary hearing impairment** (though less common for typical overland flight).
*   **Structural Effects:**
    *   **Window breakage:** Can occur if the boom intensity is high enough.
    *   **Damage to plaster or other brittle materials.**
    *   Vibrations in buildings.

#### 5.5 Measurement and Control of Sonic Boom

*   **Measurement:**
    *   Requires specialized microphones and data acquisition systems to record the rapid pressure changes.
    *   Metrics include peak overpressure (often in pounds per square foot, psf, or Pascals, Pa) and loudness metrics (e.g., Perceived Level).
*   **Control:**
    *   **Flight Planning and Operations:**
        *   **Altitude:** Flying at higher altitudes reduces the intensity of the boom reaching the ground.
        *   **Speed:** Avoiding supersonic speeds over populated areas.
        *   **Flight Path:** Directing supersonic flight over unpopulated areas (oceans, deserts).
    *   **Aircraft Design (Low-Boom Design):** Ongoing research aims to design aircraft shapes that minimize the strength of the shock waves, creating a "low boom" or even a "sonic thump" instead of a sharp boom. This involves shaping the aircraft to spread the pressure rise over a longer duration and reduce the peak overpressure.
    *   **Regulation:** Implementing regulations that restrict supersonic flight over land or set limits on boom intensity.

### 6. Key Concepts and Definitions Summary

*   **Infrasound:** Sound < 20 Hz. Long wavelength, travels far, felt as vibration.
*   **Ultrasound:** Sound > 20 kHz. Short wavelength, directional, attenuates rapidly.
*   **Impulsive Sound:** Sudden, short-duration sounds with high peak pressure (e.g., bang, thump).
*   **Sonic Boom:** Sound produced by objects exceeding the speed of sound, characterized by shock waves.
*   **Mach 1:** Speed of sound (approximately 343 m/s at sea level, 15°C).
*   **N-Wave:** Typical pressure signature of a sonic boom.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Which of the following characteristics is NOT typical of infrasound?
a) Long wavelength
b) High attenuation in air
c) Ability to penetrate buildings
d) Potential to be felt as vibration

**Answer 1:**
b) High attenuation in air. Infrasound actually has low attenuation in air.

**Question 2:**
Bats use which type of sound for echolocation?
a) Infrasound
b) Audible sound
c) Ultrasound
d) Sonic boom

**Answer 2:**
c) Ultrasound. Bats emit ultrasonic pulses and interpret the returning echoes.

**Question 3:**
A rapid rise and fall in sound pressure, often heard as a distinct "bang," describes:
a) Ultrasound
b) Infrasound
c) Continuous noise
d) Impulsive sound

**Answer 3:**
d) Impulsive sound.

**Question 4:**
What is the primary cause of a sonic boom?
a) High-frequency vibrations from an engine
b) An object traveling faster than the speed of sound
c) Large industrial machinery operating at low frequencies
d) The resonant frequency of the atmosphere

**Answer 4:**
b) An object traveling faster than the speed of sound. This creates shock waves.

**Question 5 (Application):**
Imagine a new industrial facility is planned near a residential area. The facility includes large cooling fans and a high-speed pneumatic stamping machine.
a) What types of special noise environments might be of concern from this facility, and why?
b) What specific control measures could be implemented for each potential concern?

**Answer 5 (Application):**
a)
*   **Infrasound:** From the large cooling fans, especially if they are large diameter or operate at low RPM. The low frequencies can travel long distances and penetrate buildings.
*   **Impulsive Sound:** From the high-speed pneumatic stamping machine due to the impact of the stamping process. These are short, sharp noises with high peak pressures.

b)
*   **For Infrasound from fans:**
    *   **Source Control:** Select fans with optimized blade design and consider variable speed drives to operate at lower RPMs if possible.
    *   **Path Control:** Enclose the fans in heavy, acoustically lined enclosures. Mount fans and ductwork on vibration isolators.
*   **For Impulsive Sound from stamping machine:**
    *   **Source Control:** Optimize the stamping process itself to reduce impact forces if feasible. Use silencers or mufflers on pneumatic exhaust.
    *   **Path Control:** Enclose the stamping machine in a sound-insulating enclosure with good sealing. Install the machine on resilient mounts to prevent vibration transmission.
    *   **Administrative Control:** Implement strict hearing protection policies for operators and nearby workers.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Frequency Range is Key:** Differentiate between infrasound (<20 Hz), audible sound (20 Hz - 20 kHz), and ultrasound (>20 kHz).
*   **Propagation Differences:** Low frequencies (infrasound) travel far with little attenuation, while high frequencies (ultrasound) are directional and attenuate quickly.
*   **Impulse vs. Continuous:** Impulsive noise is characterized by its transient nature and high peak pressures, which can be more damaging than continuous noise of equivalent energy.
*   **Sonic Boom Mechanism:** Understand it's a consequence of exceeding the speed of sound, creating shock waves.
*   **Measurement Challenges:** Each special noise environment may require specialized equipment and metrics for accurate assessment.
*   **Control Strategies Vary:** Solutions for infrasound, ultrasound, and impulsive noise differ based on their unique properties. Source control, path treatment (enclosures, barriers, isolation), and administrative controls are all important tools.
