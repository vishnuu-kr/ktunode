# AIR AND NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL ENGINEERING

## Module 1: Air Pollutants

### Topic: Reactions of Pollutants in the Atmosphere and Their Effects

---

### 1. Introduction to Atmospheric Reactions of Pollutants

*   **Atmosphere as a Chemical Reactor:** The Earth's atmosphere is a complex and dynamic system where emitted pollutants undergo various physical and chemical transformations. These reactions can alter the properties, toxicity, and transport of pollutants, leading to significant environmental and health impacts.
*   **Key Drivers of Atmospheric Reactions:**
    *   **Sunlight (Photochemistry):** Solar radiation provides the energy to initiate many chemical reactions, particularly in the troposphere.
    *   **Presence of Oxidants:** Highly reactive chemical species like hydroxyl radicals (•OH), ozone (O₃), and nitrate radicals (NO₃•) are crucial for transforming pollutants.
    *   **Temperature and Pressure:** These physical conditions influence reaction rates and equilibria.
    *   **Presence of Catalysts:** Certain surfaces (e.g., dust particles) can catalyze reactions.
    *   **Water Vapor:** Plays a role in hydrolysis and the formation of acidic aerosols.

---

### 2. Major Atmospheric Reactions of Key Pollutants

This section details the primary reactions and their resulting products for common air pollutants.

#### 2.1. Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)

*   **Oxidation to Sulfur Trioxide (SO₃):** This is the most significant atmospheric reaction for SO₂.
    *   **Gas-phase Oxidation:**
        *   **Reaction with Hydroxyl Radicals (•OH):** The dominant pathway, especially in polluted air.
            ```
            SO₂ + •OH → HOSO₂•
            HOSO₂• + O₂ → HO₂• + SO₃
            ```
            *   **Rate:** Relatively fast.
        *   **Reaction with Ozone (O₃):** Less significant in the gas phase.
            ```
            SO₂ + O₃ → SO₃ + O₂
            ```
            *   **Rate:** Very slow in the gas phase.
        *   **Reaction with Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂):** Can occur in the presence of catalysts or at higher concentrations.
            ```
            SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO
            ```
    *   **Liquid-phase Oxidation (in clouds and fog):** Crucial for SO₂ removal.
        *   **Oxidation by Hydrogen Peroxide (H₂O₂):** Very efficient, especially at lower pH.
            ```
            SO₂ (aq) + H₂O₂ → H₂SO₄ (aq)
            ```
        *   **Oxidation by Ozone (O₃):** Also contributes to liquid-phase oxidation.
            ```
            SO₂ (aq) + O₃ (aq) → SO₄²⁻ (aq) + O₂ (aq) + H⁺ (aq)
            ```
        *   **Catalytic Oxidation by Metal Ions:** Transition metal ions (e.g., Fe³⁺, Mn²⁺) can catalyze SO₂ oxidation.
            ```
            2SO₂ (aq) + O₂ (aq) + 4Mⁿ⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ (aq) + 4M⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾⁺
            ```
*   **Formation of Sulfuric Acid (H₂SO₄):** SO₃ readily reacts with water vapor to form sulfuric acid.
    ```
    SO₃ (g) + H₂O (g) → H₂SO₄ (g)
    ```
    Sulfuric acid then typically condenses onto existing aerosol particles or forms new particles.
*   **Effects:**
    *   **Acid Rain:** H₂SO₄ is a major component of acid rain, leading to:
        *   Acidification of lakes and rivers, harming aquatic life.
        *   Damage to forests and vegetation.
        *   Corrosion of buildings, statues, and infrastructure.
        *   Leaching of nutrients from soils.
    *   **Respiratory Problems:** Fine sulfuric acid aerosols can penetrate deep into the lungs, exacerbating asthma and other respiratory conditions.
    *   **Reduced Visibility:** Sulfate aerosols scatter and absorb light, contributing to haze.

#### 2.2. Nitrogen Oxides (NOx = NO + NO₂)

*   **Nitric Oxide (NO) Oxidation:**
    *   **Reaction with Ozone (O₃):** The primary gas-phase pathway for NO conversion to NO₂.
        ```
        NO + O₃ → NO₂ + O₂
        ```
        *   **Rate:** Fast. This reaction is critical in the formation of tropospheric ozone.
    *   **Reaction with Peroxy Radicals (RO₂•, HO₂•):** Occurs in the presence of organic compounds.
        ```
        NO + RO₂• → NO₂ + RO•
        NO + HO₂• → NO₂ + OH•
        ```
*   **Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂) Reactions:**
    *   **Photolysis (Photodissociation):** Under sunlight, NO₂ breaks down into NO and atomic oxygen.
        ```
        NO₂ + hv (λ < 420 nm) → NO + O (atom)
        ```
        *   **Significance:** This is a key step in the formation of tropospheric ozone. The atomic oxygen then reacts with molecular oxygen.
        ```
        O + O₂ + M → O₃ + M (M is a third body like N₂ or O₂)
        ```
    *   **Reaction with Hydroxyl Radicals (•OH):** Forms nitric acid (HNO₃), a stable and soluble compound.
        ```
        NO₂ + •OH → HNO₃
        ```
        *   **Rate:** Fast.
    *   **Reaction with Hydrocarbons (in the presence of radicals):** Contributes to smog formation.
    *   **Dimerization:** At low temperatures and high concentrations, NO₂ can form dinitrogen tetroxide (N₂O₄).
        ```
        2NO₂ ⇌ N₂O₄
        ```
*   **Formation of Nitric Acid (HNO₃):** A significant end product of NOx chemistry.
    *   **Gas-phase reaction:** NO₂ + •OH → HNO₃
    *   **Aqueous-phase reaction:** NO₂ dissolving in water can form nitrous acid (HNO₂) and nitric acid.
        ```
        2NO₂ (aq) + H₂O (l) ⇌ HNO₂ (aq) + HNO₃ (aq)
        ```
*   **Effects:**
    *   **Smog Formation:** NO₂ is a precursor to photochemical smog, characterized by its brownish color and respiratory irritant effects.
    *   **Acid Rain:** HNO₃ contributes to acid rain.
    *   **Eutrophication:** Nitrogen deposition can lead to nutrient enrichment in ecosystems, causing algal blooms in water bodies.
    *   **Respiratory Problems:** NO₂ itself is an irritant and can worsen asthma and bronchitis.
    *   **Reduced Visibility:** Nitrate aerosols, formed from HNO₃, contribute to haze.

#### 2.3. Carbon Monoxide (CO)

*   **Primary Reaction: Oxidation to Carbon Dioxide (CO₂):** The main removal mechanism for CO.
    *   **Reaction with Hydroxyl Radicals (•OH):** The dominant pathway.
        ```
        CO + •OH → CO₂ + H
        H + O₂ + M → HO₂• + M
        ```
        *   **Rate:** Slow, giving CO a long atmospheric lifetime (months to years), allowing for global transport.
    *   **Reaction with Atomic Oxygen (O):** Occurs in the upper atmosphere but is less significant in the troposphere.
        ```
        CO + O → CO₂
        ```
*   **Effects:**
    *   **Health Impacts:** CO binds to hemoglobin in the blood with a much higher affinity than oxygen, reducing the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity. This can lead to headaches, dizziness, impaired judgment, and in severe cases, death.
    *   **Indirect Climate Forcing:** While CO₂ is a greenhouse gas, CO itself has a minor direct warming effect. However, its presence influences the concentration of methane (CH₄) and tropospheric ozone, both potent greenhouse gases, by reacting with •OH radicals.

#### 2.4. Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)

*   **Reactions with Oxidants:** VOCs are highly reactive and undergo complex photochemical reactions.
    *   **Reaction with Hydroxyl Radicals (•OH):** The most important reaction, initiating the breakdown of most VOCs.
        ```
        R-H + •OH → R• + H₂O
        ```
        Where R-H represents a VOC molecule. The resulting radicals (R•) undergo further reactions.
    *   **Reaction with Ozone (O₃):** Particularly important for VOCs containing double bonds (e.g., alkenes).
        ```
        O₃ + Alkene → Ozonides → Aldehydes, Ketones, Carboxylic Acids, etc.
        ```
    *   **Reaction with Nitrate Radicals (NO₃•):** Important at night, especially for alkenes and substituted aromatics.
        ```
        VOC + NO₃• → Products
        ```
*   **Products of VOC Oxidation:**
    *   **Oxygenated VOCs:** Aldehydes (e.g., formaldehyde, acetaldehyde), ketones, organic acids.
    *   **Peroxyacyl Nitrates (PANs):** Formed from reactions involving NO₂ and peroxyacyl radicals, important in smog.
    *   **Secondary Organic Aerosols (SOAs):** Low-volatility organic compounds formed from oxidation can condense to form fine particulate matter.
*   **Effects:**
    *   **Smog Formation:** VOCs are critical precursors to tropospheric ozone and secondary organic aerosols, major components of photochemical smog.
    *   **Health Impacts:** Some VOCs are directly toxic (e.g., benzene is a carcinogen). Others contribute to respiratory irritation.
    *   **Secondary Organic Aerosols:** Contribute to reduced visibility and respiratory problems.
    *   **Formation of Ozone:** Contributes to the formation of ground-level ozone, which is harmful to human health and vegetation.

#### 2.5. Particulate Matter (PM)

*   **Formation:** PM can be primary (emitted directly) or secondary (formed through atmospheric reactions).
    *   **Secondary PM:** Formed from the condensation of gaseous pollutants like sulfates (SO₄²⁻), nitrates (NO₃⁻), and ammonium (NH₄⁺) onto existing particles or through nucleation to form new particles.
    *   **Organic Aerosols:** Formed from the oxidation of VOCs (SOAs).
*   **Reactions on Particle Surfaces (Heterogeneous Reactions):**
    *   **SO₂ and NO₂ Adsorption and Oxidation:** Pollutants can adsorb onto particle surfaces and react, often catalyzed by the surface material.
    *   **Formation of Nitrates and Sulfates:** NO₂ can react with aerosol water and ions to form nitrates, and SO₂ can be oxidized to sulfates.
*   **Effects:**
    *   **Health Impacts:** Fine and ultrafine particles (PM₂.₅ and PM₀.₁) can penetrate deep into the lungs and even enter the bloodstream, causing respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, and premature death.
    *   **Reduced Visibility (Haze):** Aerosols scatter and absorb light.
    *   **Climate Forcing:** Aerosols can directly scatter or absorb solar radiation, influencing the Earth's energy balance. They also affect cloud formation and properties, leading to indirect climate effects.
    *   **Deposition:** Can contaminate soils and water bodies.

### 3. Key Atmospheric Processes and Concepts

*   **Photochemistry:** Chemical reactions initiated by sunlight.
    *   **Photodissociation:** The breaking of chemical bonds by absorption of photons.
    *   **Photooxidation:** Oxidation reactions driven by light.
*   **Radicals:** Highly reactive chemical species with unpaired electrons (e.g., •OH, HO₂•, NO₃•, RO•). They are key intermediates in atmospheric reactions.
    *   **Hydroxyl Radical (•OH):** Often called the "detergent of the atmosphere" due to its role in oxidizing many pollutants. It's primarily formed by the photolysis of ozone in the presence of water vapor.
        ```
        O₃ + hv (λ < 320 nm) → O₂ + O(¹D)
        O(¹D) + H₂O → 2•OH
        ```
    *   **Peroxy Radicals (RO₂•, HO₂•):** Formed from the reaction of organic radicals with oxygen. They play a crucial role in chain reactions and the formation of ozone and secondary pollutants.
    *   **Nitrate Radical (NO₃•):** Formed from the reaction of NO₂ with ozone. It's a strong oxidant but is photochemically unstable, existing primarily during nighttime.
*   **Chain Reactions:** A series of reactions where intermediates are regenerated, allowing a small initial perturbation to lead to a large overall transformation (e.g., smog formation).
*   **Gas-Phase vs. Liquid-Phase Reactions:** Reactions can occur in the gaseous state or dissolved in atmospheric water droplets (clouds, fog, aerosols). Liquid-phase reactions are often faster for certain species (e.g., SO₂ oxidation).
*   **Heterogeneous Reactions:** Reactions occurring at the interface between different phases, such as on the surface of aerosol particles.
*   **Atmospheric Lifetime:** The average time a pollutant molecule exists in the atmosphere before being removed by chemical reaction or deposition. Pollutants with long lifetimes can be transported globally.

### 4. Effects of Atmospheric Reactions

*   **Formation of Secondary Pollutants:**
    *   **Ozone (O₃):** Ground-level ozone is formed from NOx and VOCs under sunlight and is a major component of smog.
    *   **Sulfuric Acid (H₂SO₄) and Nitric Acid (HNO₃):** Lead to acid deposition.
    *   **Secondary Organic Aerosols (SOAs):** Contribute to PM₂.₅ and haze.
    *   **Peroxyacyl Nitrates (PANs):** Phytotoxic and eye irritants in smog.
*   **Transformation and Transport:** Reactions can alter the chemical form and physical properties of pollutants, affecting their transport distances and deposition patterns.
*   **Health Impacts:**
    *   **Respiratory and Cardiovascular Diseases:** Directly from irritant pollutants (NO₂, O₃) or secondary particles.
    *   **Carcinogenesis:** From specific VOCs or their transformation products.
*   **Environmental Impacts:**
    *   **Acidification:** Damage to ecosystems, buildings.
    *   **Eutrophication:** Nitrogen deposition.
    *   **Crop Damage:** Ozone and acid rain.
    *   **Reduced Visibility:** Haze from aerosols.
*   **Climate Change:**
    *   **Greenhouse Gas Effects:** CO₂, CH₄, tropospheric O₃, and aerosols all influence Earth's radiative balance.
    *   **Indirect Effects:** Aerosols influence cloud formation and radiative properties.

---

### 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Sunlight and Oxidants are Key:** Photochemistry and the presence of radicals like •OH are the primary drivers of atmospheric transformations.
*   **SO₂ and NOx Lead to Acid Rain:** SO₂ → SO₃ → H₂SO₄; NOx → NO₂ → HNO₃.
*   **Smog is a Cocktail:** Ground-level ozone and secondary organic aerosols are the result of NOx and VOC reactions under sunlight.
*   **Atmospheric Reactions are Complex:** A single pollutant can undergo multiple reactions, forming a cascade of products.
*   **Secondary Pollutants are Often More Harmful:** Many secondary pollutants (e.g., ozone, fine particles) have significant health and environmental impacts.
*   **Aerosols are Crucial:** They act as surfaces for heterogeneous reactions, influence visibility, and have climate impacts.
*   **Atmospheric Lifetime Matters:** Determines a pollutant's ability to travel long distances and exert regional or global effects.

### 7. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
(c) Hydroxyl Radical (•OH)

**Answer 2:**
The primary conversion of SO₂ to sulfuric acid involves oxidation to SO₃, which then reacts with water.
*   **Gas-phase:** The dominant pathway is the reaction of SO₂ with hydroxyl radicals (•OH).
    ```
    SO₂ + •OH → HOSO₂•
    HOSO₂• + O₂ → HO₂• + SO₃
    ```
*   **Liquid-phase:** In clouds and fog, SO₂ is efficiently oxidized by hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and ozone (O₃).
    ```
    SO₂ (aq) + H₂O₂ (aq) → H₂SO₄ (aq)
    ```
    SO₃ then readily reacts with water vapor to form sulfuric acid:
    ```
    SO₃ (g) + H₂O (g) → H₂SO₄ (g)
    ```

**Answer 3:**
NOx and VOCs react in the presence of sunlight to form photochemical smog.
1.  **NOx Photolysis:** NO₂ is photolyzed by sunlight:
    ```
    NO₂ + hv → NO + O
    O + O₂ + M → O₃ + M
    ```
    This produces ozone.
2.  **VOC Oxidation:** VOCs react with •OH, producing peroxy radicals (RO₂•).
3.  **Ozone Formation Cycle:** These peroxy radicals react with NO, converting it to NO₂, which then leads to more ozone formation.
    ```
    RO₂• + NO → RO• + NO₂
    ```
    The overall process leads to a build-up of ozone and other oxidants.
**Major secondary pollutants:** Ground-level ozone (O₃), secondary organic aerosols (SOAs), peroxyacyl nitrates (PANs).

**Answer 4:**
The environmental effects of acid rain caused by SO₂ emissions (leading to sulfuric acid) include:
*   **Acidification of aquatic ecosystems:** Harm to fish and other aquatic life in lakes and rivers.
*   **Damage to forests:** Weakening trees, making them susceptible to disease and pests, and damaging leaves.
*   **Damage to buildings and materials:** Corrosion of metals, stone (e.g., marble, limestone), and concrete.
*   **Leaching of nutrients from soil:** Affecting plant growth.
*   **Release of toxic metals:** From soil and sediments.

**Answer 5:**
*   **Role in Atmospheric Chemistry:**
    *   **Secondary Pollutant Formation:** PM₂.₅ and PM₀.₁ are often formed from the condensation of gaseous pollutants like sulfates and nitrates.
    *   **Heterogeneous Reactions:** PM surfaces can catalyze or act as reaction sites for gaseous pollutants, facilitating their transformation.
    *   **Nucleation Sites:** Particles can act as condensation nuclei for forming clouds and fog.
*   **Health Impacts:**
    *   Fine and ultrafine particles can be inhaled deep into the lungs.
    *   They can cause inflammation, exacerbate respiratory and cardiovascular diseases (e.g., asthma, bronchitis, heart attacks), and lead to premature death.
    *   Some particles may carry toxic compounds that can cause cancer.
