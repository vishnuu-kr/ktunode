# Environmental Health and Safety: Module 3 - Safety in the Construction Industry

## Topic: Concreting

---

### Introduction to Concreting Safety

Concreting is a fundamental process in the construction industry, involving the mixing, placing, and finishing of concrete. While essential, it presents a range of potential hazards that require careful management to ensure the health and safety of workers and the surrounding environment. This module focuses on identifying and mitigating these risks associated with concreting operations.

---

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

*   **Identify hazards associated with concreting operations.**
*   **Describe control measures to mitigate identified hazards.**
*   **Explain the importance of personal protective equipment (PPE) for concreting.**
*   **Discuss safe work practices for concrete mixing, pouring, and finishing.**
*   **Recognize the risks of cement burns and their prevention.**
*   **Understand the safety considerations for working with concrete pumps and vibrators.**
*   **Address environmental considerations related to concreting.**

### 1. Hazards Associated with Concreting Operations

Concreting activities can expose workers to various physical, chemical, and ergonomic hazards.

*   **Physical Hazards:**
    *   **Slips, Trips, and Falls:** Wet concrete, tools, equipment, and debris on the work surface.
        *   *Example:* A worker tripping over a stray rebar while carrying a bucket of concrete.
    *   **Falling Objects:** Tools, materials, or partially set concrete falling from elevated work areas.
        *   *Example:* A trowel dropped from scaffolding by a worker finishing a concrete slab.
    *   **Contact with Moving Machinery:** Mixers, concrete pumps, vibrators, and other equipment.
        *   *Example:* A worker's hand getting caught in the rotating drum of a concrete mixer.
    *   **Noise Exposure:** From concrete mixers, pumps, and vibrators, leading to hearing damage.
        *   *Example:* Prolonged exposure to the high decibel noise of a concrete vibrator without hearing protection.
    *   **Vibration Exposure:** From concrete vibrators and other powered tools, leading to Hand-Arm Vibration Syndrome (HAVS) or Whole-Body Vibration (WBV).
        *   *Example:* A worker experiencing tingling and numbness in their hands after prolonged use of a concrete vibrator.
    *   **Electrical Hazards:** From faulty equipment or contact with live wires when working with wet concrete.
        *   *Example:* A worker receiving an electric shock from a damaged extension cord powering a concrete mixer.
    *   **Confined Spaces:** Working within concrete structures like manholes or large pipes can pose risks of oxygen deficiency or hazardous atmospheres.
        *   *Example:* A worker entering a newly constructed concrete chamber without proper ventilation and gas monitoring.

*   **Chemical Hazards:**
    *   **Cement Burns (Alkaline Burns):** Wet cement is highly alkaline and can cause severe skin burns, eye damage, and respiratory irritation.
        *   *Key Concept:* Cement contains chromium compounds and calcium hydroxide, which are corrosive.
        *   *Example:* Skin redness, itching, and blistering after prolonged contact with wet concrete without gloves.
    *   **Silica Dust:** During cutting, grinding, or demolition of hardened concrete, crystalline silica dust can be released, leading to silicosis, a serious lung disease.
        *   *Key Concept:* Silicosis is an irreversible fibrotic lung disease.
        *   *Example:* A worker developing a persistent cough and shortness of breath after years of dry-cutting concrete without respiratory protection.
    *   **Chemical Additives:** Some concrete additives (admixtures) can be hazardous if not handled properly.
        *   *Example:* Skin irritation or allergic reactions from contact with certain plasticizers or accelerators.

*   **Ergonomic Hazards:**
    *   **Manual Handling:** Lifting and carrying heavy bags of cement, tools, or concrete.
        *   *Example:* A worker experiencing back strain from improperly lifting a 50kg bag of cement.
    *   **Repetitive Motions:** During finishing tasks like troweling or screeding.
        *   *Example:* Developing carpal tunnel syndrome from repetitive wrist movements during manual troweling.
    *   **Awkward Postures:** Working in confined spaces or reaching to place concrete.
        *   *Example:* A worker experiencing shoulder pain from prolonged overhead work to pour concrete into formwork.

### 2. Control Measures to Mitigate Identified Hazards

Effective hazard control follows the hierarchy of controls: Elimination, Substitution, Engineering Controls, Administrative Controls, and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).

*   **Elimination/Substitution:**
    *   Where possible, use pre-mixed concrete delivered to site to reduce manual handling of cement bags.
    *   Consider using concrete mixes with lower alkalinity or less hazardous additives.

*   **Engineering Controls:**
    *   **Ventilation:** Ensure adequate ventilation when working in confined spaces or areas where dust is generated.
    *   **Machine Guarding:** Ensure all rotating machinery (mixers, pumps) are properly guarded.
    *   **Noise Reduction:** Use quieter equipment where possible, or implement sound barriers.
    *   **Dust Control:** Employ wet methods for cutting or grinding concrete, or use dust extraction systems.
    *   **Ergonomic Equipment:** Utilize mechanical aids for lifting and moving materials.

*   **Administrative Controls:**
    *   **Safe Work Procedures (SWPs):** Develop and implement clear SWPs for all concreting tasks.
    *   **Training and Supervision:** Ensure workers are adequately trained on hazards and safe practices. Provide regular supervision.
    *   **Housekeeping:** Maintain a clean and tidy work area to prevent slips, trips, and falls.
    *   **Job Safety Analysis (JSA)/Risk Assessments:** Conduct thorough risk assessments before starting any concreting operation.
    *   **Work Rotation:** Rotate tasks to reduce exposure to repetitive motions or prolonged vibration.
    *   **Hygiene Facilities:** Provide adequate washing facilities with soap and clean water.

*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** (Covered in detail in the next section)

### 3. Importance of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for Concreting

PPE is the last line of defence and is crucial for protecting workers from direct exposure to hazards.

*   **Eye Protection:**
    *   **Safety Glasses/Goggles:** Protect from dust, splashes, and flying debris.
        *   *Requirement:* Must meet Australian Standard AS/NZS 1337.
    *   **Face Shields:** Provide additional protection for the face during tasks like grinding or when there's a high risk of splashing.

*   **Skin Protection:**
    *   **Waterproof Gloves:** Alkali-resistant gloves (e.g., nitrile, PVC) are essential when handling wet concrete or cement.
        *   *Requirement:* Must be long enough to cover the wrist and forearm.
    *   **Protective Clothing:**
        *   **Long-sleeved shirts and long trousers:** Made from durable, woven fabric to prevent skin contact.
        *   **Waterproof Aprons/Overalls:** Provide an additional barrier against wet concrete.
        *   **Waterproof Boots:** With steel toes and puncture-resistant soles.

*   **Respiratory Protection:**
    *   **Dust Masks (P2/P3):** Required when there is a risk of inhaling silica dust or cement dust.
        *   *Requirement:* Fit-tested and properly worn.
    *   **Respirators with appropriate cartridges:** For higher concentrations of dust or chemical fumes.

*   **Hearing Protection:**
    *   **Earplugs or Earmuffs:** Essential when working with noisy equipment like concrete mixers, pumps, or vibrators.
        *   *Requirement:* Must provide adequate Noise Reduction Rating (NRR).

*   **Head Protection:**
    *   **Hard Hats:** Protect from falling objects.
        *   *Requirement:* Must meet Australian Standard AS/NZS 1801.

**Important Point to Remember:**
*   PPE must be **correctly selected, fitted, maintained, and worn** at all times when required.
*   Workers must be **trained** on the proper use and limitations of their PPE.

### 4. Safe Work Practices for Concrete Mixing, Pouring, and Finishing

Adhering to safe work practices significantly reduces the risk of incidents.

*   **Concrete Mixing:**
    *   **Manual Mixing:**
        *   Use appropriate tools (shovels, picks) to avoid overexertion.
        *   Wear full PPE, especially gloves and eye protection.
        *   Add water gradually to avoid splashing.
    *   **Mechanical Mixing (Mixers):**
        *   Ensure the mixer is on stable ground and properly maintained.
        *   Keep guards in place.
        *   Never place hands or tools into the rotating drum while it's operating.
        *   Be aware of pinch points and crushing hazards.

*   **Concrete Pouring:**
    *   **Planning:**
        *   Ensure the formwork is securely in place and can withstand the pressure of wet concrete.
        *   Have all necessary tools and equipment ready.
        *   Establish clear communication channels.
    *   **Using Buckets/Wheelbarrows:**
        *   Ensure safe pathways are clear of obstructions.
        *   Workers should not overfill buckets or wheelbarrows.
    *   **Using Concrete Pumps:**
        *   Ensure the pump operator is trained and licensed.
        *   Inspect the pump and hoses for damage before use.
        *   Maintain a safe distance from the pump and hoses during operation (explosion/burst risk).
        *   Never stand in the path of the concrete discharge.
        *   Bleed the line safely to remove excess concrete.
    *   **Manual Placement:**
        *   Use long-handled tools to direct concrete.
        *   Avoid prolonged contact with wet concrete.
    *   **Working at Heights:**
        *   Use appropriate edge protection (guardrails, safety nets).
        *   Ensure stable working platforms.
        *   Use fall arrest systems if necessary.

*   **Concrete Finishing:**
    *   **Troweling:**
        *   Use power trowels with appropriate guarding.
        *   Be aware of trip hazards from the cord of electric trowels.
        *   Avoid prolonged, repetitive motions that can lead to HAVS.
    *   **Screeding:**
        *   Use long-handled tools to maintain a safe distance.
        *   Be aware of uneven surfaces.
    *   **Curing:**
        *   Some curing compounds may be volatile or irritating. Follow manufacturer safety instructions.

### 5. Risks of Cement Burns and Their Prevention

Cement is a highly alkaline substance that can cause severe skin and eye burns.

*   **How Cement Burns Happen:**
    *   **Direct Contact:** Wet cement adhering to skin for extended periods.
    *   **Permeation:** Moisture on the skin allows the alkaline chemicals in cement to penetrate protective clothing if not waterproof.
    *   **Trapped Moisture:** Sweat trapped under gloves or clothing can react with cement particles, exacerbating the burn.

*   **Symptoms of Cement Burns:**
    *   Redness, itching, and pain.
    *   Blistering.
    *   Dry, cracked skin.
    *   Severe burns can penetrate to deeper tissue layers and take a long time to heal.

*   **Prevention of Cement Burns:**
    *   **Wear Appropriate PPE:**
        *   **Waterproof, alkali-resistant gloves:** Long enough to cover wrists.
        *   **Waterproof boots:** With long shafts.
        *   **Long sleeves and trousers:** Made of dense fabric.
        *   **Waterproof apron or overalls.**
    *   **Keep Skin Dry:**
        *   Change wet clothing immediately.
        *   Dry hands thoroughly before putting on gloves.
        *   Ensure gloves are not worn for extended periods without breaks if sweating.
    *   **Good Hygiene Practices:**
        *   **Wash thoroughly with soap and water:** After any contact with cement or concrete, and before eating, drinking, or smoking.
        *   **Do not use solvents or abrasives:** To clean cement from skin, as this can damage the skin and increase absorption.
    *   **Awareness and Training:**
        *   Educate workers about the risks of cement burns and how to prevent them.

**Important Point to Remember:**
*   Cement burns can be slow to develop and may not be immediately painful, leading to underestimation of the severity. **Always treat even minor contact as potentially harmful.**

### 6. Safety Considerations for Working with Concrete Pumps and Vibrators

These are specialized pieces of equipment with specific safety requirements.

*   **Concrete Pumps:**
    *   **Operator Competency:** Only trained and authorized personnel should operate concrete pumps.
    *   **Pre-Use Inspection:** Thoroughly inspect the pump, hoses, couplings, and outriggers for wear, damage, or leaks.
    *   **Hose Management:**
        *   Secure hoses properly to prevent whipping if they burst.
        *   Never stand in front of or directly behind hoses under pressure.
        *   When disconnecting hoses, ensure they are properly drained and depressurized.
    *   **Stability:** Ensure the pump is set up on stable, level ground and outriggers are properly deployed.
    *   **Communication:** Maintain clear communication between the pump operator and the crew on the receiving end.
    *   **Electric Shock Hazard:** Be aware of proximity to overhead power lines.

*   **Concrete Vibrators:**
    *   **Operator Training:** Workers should be trained on the correct use and potential hazards.
    *   **Vibration Exposure:**
        *   Limit continuous exposure time.
        *   Use vibration-dampening gloves.
        *   Take breaks to reduce the risk of HAVS.
    *   **Electrical Safety:**
        *   Ensure electric vibrators are regularly inspected for damage to cords and plugs.
        *   Use Ground Fault Circuit Interrupters (GFCIs) for added protection.
        *   Avoid using electric vibrators in excessively wet conditions unless specifically designed for it.
    *   **Mechanical Hazards:**
        *   Keep guards in place on petrol-driven vibrators.
        *   Be aware of moving parts and hot exhaust.
    *   **Ergonomics:** Use vibrators as efficiently as possible to minimize strain.

### 7. Environmental Considerations Related to Concreting

Concreting operations can have environmental impacts that need to be managed.

*   **Water Pollution:**
    *   **Wash Water:** Water used to clean tools, equipment, and mixers can contain cement, admixtures, and other pollutants.
    *   **Runoff:** Rainwater running over fresh concrete can carry cement particles into waterways.
    *   **Control Measures:**
        *   Collect and contain wash water.
        *   Use designated wash-down areas with settlement ponds or filtration systems.
        *   Prevent concrete slurry from entering stormwater drains or natural watercourses.
        *   Use temporary barriers (e.g., sandbags) to prevent runoff.

*   **Air Pollution:**
    *   **Dust:** Generation of silica dust during cutting, grinding, or demolition of hardened concrete.
    *   **Emissions:** From petrol or diesel-powered equipment.
    *   **Control Measures:**
        *   Use wet methods for dust-generating activities.
        *   Employ dust suppression systems.
        *   Maintain engines to reduce emissions.

*   **Waste Management:**
    *   **Concrete Waste:** Excess or rejected concrete.
    *   **Packaging:** Bags, plastic sheeting, formwork materials.
    *   **Control Measures:**
        *   Plan concrete quantities to minimize waste.
        *   Recycle or reuse formwork materials where possible.
        *   Dispose of concrete waste appropriately (e.g., crushing for aggregate).
        *   Ensure proper disposal of all packaging and other waste.

*   **Resource Consumption:**
    *   Water, cement, aggregates, and energy are consumed.
    *   **Control Measures:**
        *   Use water-efficient methods.
        *   Consider using recycled aggregates or supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs) to reduce cement content.

### Answers to Practice Questions

1.  The primary chemical hazards are **cement burns** (alkaline burns) caused by the highly alkaline nature of wet cement and **respiratory irritation** from cement dust.
2.  Three physical hazards include: **slips, trips, and falls** (due to wet surfaces or debris), **falling objects** (tools, materials), and **contact with moving machinery** (mixers, pumps). Others include noise and vibration exposure.
3.  Crucial PPE for preventing cement burns includes **waterproof, alkali-resistant gloves** (long enough to cover wrists), **waterproof boots** (with long shafts), and **protective clothing** (long sleeves, long trousers, waterproof apron/overalls).
4.  Good housekeeping is essential for concreting safety as it helps to **prevent slips, trips, and falls** by removing debris, spilled materials, and tools from walkways. It also reduces fire hazards and makes the work area safer and more efficient.
5.  The main risks when working with concrete vibrators are **Hand-Arm Vibration Syndrome (HAVS)** due to prolonged vibration exposure and **ergonomic strain** from the tool's weight and operation. Mitigation includes **limiting exposure time, using vibration-dampening gloves, taking regular breaks, and ensuring proper tool maintenance.**
6.  Wash water from concreting operations can **pollute waterways** by introducing cement particles, admixtures, and other chemicals, potentially harming aquatic life and affecting water quality. Measures to control this include **collecting and containing wash water, using designated wash-down areas with settlement systems, and preventing runoff into drains or natural watercourses.**
7.  Maintaining a clean work area is important to **prevent slips, trips, and falls** from materials, tools, or wet concrete. It also helps in **identifying and addressing potential hazards** more easily and contributes to an overall safer and more organized work environment.

---

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Wet cement is hazardous:** It is corrosive and can cause severe burns to skin and eyes.
*   **PPE is non-negotiable:** Always wear the correct PPE for the task.
*   **Housekeeping is crucial:** A clean site prevents many common accidents.
*   **Training and communication:** Ensure all workers understand the risks and safe procedures.
*   **Hierarchy of controls:** Prioritize eliminating or reducing hazards before relying on PPE.
*   **Environmental responsibility:** Manage waste and prevent pollution from concreting activities.
*   **Vibration and noise:** Be aware of the long-term health effects and take precautions.
