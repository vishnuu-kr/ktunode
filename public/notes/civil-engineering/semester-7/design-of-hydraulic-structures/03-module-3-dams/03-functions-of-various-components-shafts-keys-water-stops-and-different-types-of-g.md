# Design of Hydraulic Structures - Module 3: Dams

## Topic: Functions of Various Components: Shafts, Keys, Water Stops, and Different Types of Galleries

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   Describe the functions of shafts in dams.
*   Explain the purpose and types of keys used in dam construction.
*   Understand the role and importance of water stops in ensuring watertightness.
*   Differentiate between various types of galleries found in dams and their specific functions.

---

### 1. Shafts in Dams

**Definition:** Shafts in dams are vertical or near-vertical openings or passages constructed within the dam body or foundation. They serve multiple purposes related to the dam's operation, maintenance, and monitoring.

**Key Functions:**

*   **Access for Inspection and Maintenance:**
    *   Provide a means for engineers and technicians to access various parts of the dam for visual inspection, routine maintenance, and repairs.
    *   Examples: Checking structural integrity, inspecting embedded instruments, cleaning internal conduits.
*   **Housing for Machinery and Equipment:**
    *   Can house operational machinery such as gate operating mechanisms, valve controls, and lifting equipment for spillway gates or outlet works.
    *   Example: A gate shaft housing the hoist machinery for an intake gate.
*   **Conduits for Services:**
    *   Can be used to route electrical cables, drainage pipes, and other service lines within the dam structure.
    *   Example: A service shaft carrying power cables to operate instruments or pumps.
*   **Passageways for Personnel:**
    *   Allow for safe passage of personnel within the dam structure for inspection, maintenance, or emergency response.
    *   Example: A vertical shaft connecting different levels of galleries.
*   **Drainage and Ventilation:**
    *   Some shafts might be incorporated into drainage systems or provide ventilation for enclosed spaces within the dam.

**Types of Shafts (based on function):**

*   **Gate Shafts:** Primarily for housing gate operating mechanisms.
*   **Intake Shafts:** Leading to water intake structures.
*   **Service Shafts:** For general access, services, and equipment.
*   **Drainage Shafts:** Part of the dam's drainage system.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Shafts can introduce stress concentrations, so their design requires careful consideration of structural integrity.
*   Proper sealing and drainage around shafts are crucial to prevent water ingress.
*   The size and location of shafts are dictated by their intended function and the type of dam.

### 2. Keys in Dams

**Definition:** Keys are projecting or recessed interlocking features incorporated at the contact surfaces between different sections of a dam or between the dam and the abutments. They are designed to resist shear forces and prevent differential movement.

**Key Functions:**

*   **Resisting Shear Forces:**
    *   The primary function is to transfer shear forces between the dam body and the foundation (in abutment keys) or between different monoliths of the dam.
    *   This is particularly important in gravity dams to ensure the monolithic behavior of the structure.
*   **Preventing Differential Settlement and Sliding:**
    *   Keys help to anchor the dam to the foundation, reducing the risk of sliding along the base.
    *   They also help to prevent relative movement between different blocks of a concrete dam, especially under seismic loading.
*   **Improving Watertightness:**
    *   When used at construction joints between concrete lifts or monoliths, keys can help to create a more continuous and less permeable barrier.
*   **Facilitating Construction (in some cases):**
    *   While primarily structural, the interlocking nature can also provide a degree of stability during the construction of successive lifts.

**Types of Keys:**

*   **Foundation Keys (Sole Keys/Keyways):**
    *   These are incorporated into the foundation rock or concrete at the base of the dam.
    *   The dam body has a corresponding projection that fits into the foundation key.
    *   **Purpose:** To prevent sliding of the dam along the foundation contact.
    *   **Example:** A U-shaped keyway cut into the rock foundation.
*   **Abutment Keys:**
    *   Projecting features at the sides of the dam that interlock with the abutment rock or concrete.
    *   **Purpose:** To resist lateral movement and shear forces at the abutments.
    *   **Example:** A projecting rib on the dam that fits into a groove in the abutment.
*   **Construction Joint Keys (Monolith Keys):**
    *   Used between successive lifts of concrete or between adjacent concrete monoliths.
    *   These are typically trapezoidal or rectangular projections and recesses.
    *   **Purpose:** To transfer shear stresses between monoliths and improve watertightness of construction joints.
    *   **Example:** A trapezoidal key cast at the interface of two concrete blocks.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The size and shape of keys are determined by the shear forces they are designed to resist.
*   The contact surfaces of keys must be clean and properly prepared to ensure effective shear transfer.
*   The radius at the root of keys is important to avoid stress concentrations.
*   Keys are particularly critical in gravity dams and arch dams for ensuring structural stability.

### 3. Water Stops

**Definition:** Water stops are flexible, continuous strips or profiles made of rubber, PVC, neoprene, or other waterproof materials, embedded in construction joints or expansion joints within a dam to prevent the passage of water.

**Key Functions:**

*   **Preventing Water Seepage:**
    *   Their primary and most crucial function is to form a continuous, impermeable barrier across joints that might otherwise allow water to leak through the dam.
    *   This is vital for maintaining the integrity and efficiency of the reservoir and preventing uncontrolled seepage through the dam body.
*   **Accommodating Joint Movement:**
    *   Water stops are designed to be flexible and can accommodate the opening and closing of joints due to thermal expansion/contraction, elastic shortening, or settlement without losing their sealing capability.
    *   This is particularly important in concrete dams where joints are inevitable.
*   **Ensuring Watertightness of Construction Joints:**
    *   In large concrete dams, construction is often carried out in lifts or blocks, creating construction joints. Water stops are placed at these interfaces to ensure watertightness.
*   **Sealing Expansion Joints:**
    *   Expansion joints are deliberately created to manage thermal movements. Water stops are essential for sealing these joints to prevent leakage.

**Types of Water Stops (based on material and configuration):**

*   **Ribbed Water Stops:**
    *   Feature a series of ribs along their length to increase the path of seepage and improve adhesion to the concrete.
    *   **Configuration:** Typically flat with integral ribs.
*   **Webbed Water Stops:**
    *   Have a central web that allows for greater elongation and movement.
    *   **Configuration:** Flat with a central webbed section.
*   **Bulb Water Stops:**
    *   Feature a central bulb that helps to accommodate significant joint movement and provide a more robust seal.
    *   **Configuration:** Often circular or dumbbell-shaped in cross-section.
*   **Specialized Water Stops:**
    *   **Through-wall water stops:** Extend through the entire thickness of the dam.
    *   **Center-bulb water stops:** Designed for large movements.
    *   **Combinations:** Some water stops combine features like ribs and bulbs.

**Materials:**

*   **Rubber:** Durable, flexible, and resistant to water and chemicals.
*   **PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride):** Cost-effective, easy to handle, and good for moderate movements.
*   **Neoprene:** Good chemical resistance and UV stability.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The correct selection of water stop type and material is crucial based on the expected joint movement, water pressure, and environmental conditions.
*   Proper installation and splicing of water stops are critical to ensure a continuous and effective seal.
*   Water stops are often embedded in the center of the concrete joint to provide a better seal against water pressure from either side.
*   The integrity of the water stop is paramount for the overall watertightness of the dam.

### 4. Different Types of Galleries in Dams

**Definition:** Galleries are horizontal or inclined passages constructed within the dam body or along its foundation. They serve as vital conduits for various operational, monitoring, and maintenance functions.

**Key Functions (General):**

*   **Inspection and Monitoring:**
    *   Allow access to embedded instruments (e.g., piezometers, strain gauges, thermometers) for reading and calibration.
    *   Provide means for visual inspection of the dam's internal condition.
*   **Drainage:**
    *   Facilitate the collection and discharge of seepage water from the dam body and foundation.
*   **Access and Maintenance:**
    *   Provide access to operational equipment such as valves, gates, and pumps.
    *   Facilitate repairs and maintenance within the dam structure.
*   **Service Conduits:**
    *   Route electrical cables, plumbing, and other service utilities.

**Types of Galleries:**

*   **Foundation Gallery:**
    *   **Location:** Constructed in the foundation rock or concrete immediately below the dam.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Collects seepage water from the foundation.
        *   Allows inspection of the foundation and grout curtains.
        *   Houses foundation drainage systems (e.g., drainage holes, wells).
        *   May house instrumentation for foundation monitoring.
    *   **Example:** A gallery at the base of a concrete gravity dam, connected to a system of drainage holes drilled into the foundation.

*   **Toe Drain Gallery (or Drainage Gallery at Toe):**
    *   **Location:** Located at the downstream toe of the dam.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Collects seepage water that has passed through the dam body and foundation.
        *   Discharges this water safely downstream, preventing uplift pressures and ensuring stability.
        *   May also house drainage pipes.
    *   **Example:** A gallery at the downstream edge of an embankment dam or concrete dam, designed to collect and discharge seepage.

*   **Mid-level Gallery / Intermediary Gallery:**
    *   **Location:** Situated at intermediate levels within the dam body, particularly in high dams.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Facilitates inspection of internal dam components and the dam body at various heights.
        *   Provides access for maintenance of embedded instruments.
        *   Can house service equipment or drainage collection points.
        *   Allows for the placement of expansion joint fillers or sealing materials.
    *   **Example:** A gallery within a large concrete gravity dam, allowing access to piezometers installed at different elevations.

*   **Service Gallery:**
    *   **Location:** Can be at various levels, often integrated with other galleries.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Primarily houses operational machinery (e.g., gate hoists, valve controls).
        *   Routes electrical cables and service pipes.
        *   Provides access for routine maintenance of these services.
    *   **Example:** A gallery housing the mechanical equipment for operating the spillway gates.

*   **Crest Gallery:**
    *   **Location:** Located near the top of the dam, often along the crest road.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Provides access for inspecting the spillway gates or crest structure.
        *   May house control panels or instrumentation for the spillway.
        *   Offers access for maintenance of the crest road and associated features.
    *   **Example:** A gallery above the operational level of a spillway, providing access to the gate operating mechanisms.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The type and number of galleries depend on the dam's type, height, complexity, and specific design requirements.
*   Proper ventilation and lighting are essential in all galleries.
*   Galleries must be adequately sealed to prevent uncontrolled water ingress or egress.
*   The design of galleries must consider structural stability, preventing them from becoming weak points in the dam.
*   Drainage galleries play a critical role in controlling uplift pressures, which is fundamental for the stability of concrete dams.

### Practice Questions and Answers:

**Question 1:** What is the primary function of a foundation gallery in a concrete gravity dam?
**Answer:** The primary function of a foundation gallery is to collect seepage water from the foundation and the base of the dam, and to allow for inspection and maintenance of the foundation and any foundation drainage or grouting systems.

**Question 2:** Name two types of water stops and describe their key characteristics.
**Answer:**
1.  **Ribbed Water Stop:** Characterized by ribs along its length, which increase the seepage path and improve adhesion to the concrete.
2.  **Bulb Water Stop:** Features a central bulb that allows for greater joint movement and provides a more robust seal against water pressure.

**Question 3:** Explain the purpose of a key in a concrete dam. Provide an example of where keys are typically used.
**Answer:** A key in a concrete dam is an interlocking feature designed to resist shear forces and prevent differential movement between different sections of the dam or between the dam and its foundation. An example of where keys are typically used is at the construction joints between concrete monoliths or at the base of the dam where it interfaces with the foundation (foundation key).

**Question 4:** Differentiate between a service shaft and a gate shaft.
**Answer:** A **service shaft** is primarily used for general access, routing services like electrical cables and pipes, and housing general equipment. A **gate shaft** is specifically designed to house the machinery and mechanisms required for operating gates, such as hoists and valve controls.

**Question 5:** Why are toe drain galleries important for the stability of a dam?
**Answer:** Toe drain galleries are crucial for stability because they collect seepage water from the dam body and foundation. By collecting and discharging this water, they reduce the hydrostatic pressure (uplift pressure) within the dam and foundation, which is a major factor contributing to the stability of dams, especially concrete gravity dams.

### Summary of Key Concepts:

*   **Shafts:** Vertical passages for access, machinery, services, and personnel.
*   **Keys:** Interlocking features resisting shear and preventing movement at joints.
*   **Water Stops:** Impermeable barriers across joints to prevent seepage.
*   **Galleries:** Horizontal/inclined passages for inspection, drainage, and services.
    *   **Foundation Gallery:** Deals with foundation seepage.
    *   **Toe Drain Gallery:** Manages seepage at the downstream toe.
    *   **Mid-level Gallery:** Internal inspection and service access.
    *   **Service Gallery:** Houses operational equipment and services.
    *   **Crest Gallery:** Access to crest structures and spillway gates.
