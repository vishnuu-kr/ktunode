# DESIGN OF HYDRAULIC STRUCTURES: Module 3: Dams - Grouting

## 1. Introduction to Grouting in Dams

Grouting is a fundamental technique used in the construction and maintenance of dams to improve their properties and ensure their long-term stability and performance. It involves injecting a fluid material (grout) into the pores, fissures, and cavities within the foundation soil or rock mass, or within the dam material itself.

### 1.1. Purpose of Grouting in Dams

*   **Impermeability:** To reduce seepage through the dam foundation, abutments, and the dam body itself, preventing excessive water loss and uplift pressures.
*   **Strengthening:** To fill voids, consolidate weak zones, and improve the mechanical properties (e.g., shear strength, stiffness) of the foundation and dam materials.
*   **Stabilization:** To prevent erosion and piping within the dam and its foundation.
*   **Water Control:** To create impermeable barriers or cut-off walls to manage groundwater flow.
*   **Repair and Rehabilitation:** To seal cracks and fissures in existing dams.

### 1.2. Types of Grouting Based on Purpose

*   **Grout Curtain:** A continuous vertical barrier of grout injected into the foundation to intercept seepage.
*   **Grout Consolidation:** Grouting applied to strengthen and improve the mechanical properties of weak rock or soil.
*   **Grout Treatment:** Grouting for general repair or sealing of minor fissures.

## 2. Grouting Materials (Grout Mixes)

The selection of grout material is critical and depends on the permeability of the material to be grouted, the size of the voids, and the desired properties of the grout.

### 2.1. Cementitious Grouts

*   **Description:** The most common type, consisting of cement, water, and sometimes admixtures.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Cement:** Portland cement is standard. Special cements may be used for specific conditions (e.g., rapid hardening, sulfate resistance).
    *   **Water:** Crucial for workability and strength development. Water-cement ratio (w/c) is a key parameter affecting fluidity and strength.
    *   **Admixtures (Optional):**
        *   **Plasticizers/Superplasticizers:** To reduce water content while maintaining fluidity, leading to higher strength and lower permeability.
        *   **Retarders:** To slow down setting time, especially in hot weather or for deep holes.
        *   **Accelerators:** To speed up setting time in cold weather or for rapid sealing.
        *   **Fillers (e.g., sand, fly ash):** To reduce shrinkage, improve workability, and reduce cost in larger voids.
        *   **Expansive agents:** To counteract shrinkage.
*   **Properties:**
    *   Good strength and durability.
    *   Relatively low cost.
    *   Can be modified to achieve a wide range of viscosities and setting times.
*   **Applications:** Widely used for grout curtains, consolidation grouting, and filling larger voids.

### 2.2. Chemical Grouts (Suspension Grouts)

*   **Description:** Grout mixes that undergo a chemical reaction to solidify. They are typically more fluid than cementitious grouts, allowing them to penetrate finer fissures.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Silicate Grouts:** Based on sodium silicate reacting with a hardening agent (e.g., calcium chloride, organic esters). They can set rapidly and are effective in fine fissures.
    *   **Acrylamide Grouts:** Based on acrylamide monomers, forming a stable gel. They are very fluid and can penetrate very fine cracks, but are generally more expensive and have environmental concerns.
    *   **Urethane Grouts:** Two-component systems that react to form a flexible or rigid foam or solid. They offer good adhesion and water resistance.
*   **Properties:**
    *   Low viscosity, enabling penetration into very fine fissures.
    *   Can set quickly.
    *   May offer higher tensile strength or elasticity compared to cement.
    *   Often more expensive than cementitious grouts.
    *   Some chemical grouts can have environmental or health concerns.
*   **Applications:** Sealing fine cracks, stabilizing soils with very low permeability, and in situations where cement grouts might wash out before setting.

## 3. Grouting Methods and Techniques

The success of grouting depends on applying the correct method and technique to achieve the desired grout penetration and sealing.

### 3.1. Grouting Equipment

*   **Grout Mixer:** To prepare the grout mix.
*   **Grout Pump:** To deliver the grout under pressure. Various types exist, including piston pumps, diaphragm pumps, and centrifugal pumps.
*   **Grout Packer (Bridge or Bridge Packer):** A device inserted into the borehole to isolate a specific section for grouting.
*   **Drill Rig:** For drilling the boreholes.
*   **Pressure Gauges and Flow Meters:** To monitor injection pressure and grout flow.

### 3.2. Grouting Schemes and Patterns

The pattern in which boreholes are drilled and grouted significantly influences the effectiveness of the grout curtain or treatment.

*   **Primary Grouting:** The initial grouting phase, typically with wider hole spacing.
*   **Secondary Grouting:** Grouting in holes drilled between primary holes, to fill zones missed by primary grouting.
*   **Tertiary Grouting (and subsequent stages):** Further stages with progressively closer hole spacing to ensure complete coverage and achieve the desired low permeability.

Common patterns include:

*   **Single Line Pattern:** For a basic grout curtain.
*   **Staggered Pattern:** More effective for a complete grout curtain.
*   **Multiple Line Patterns:** For critical structures or very high permeability zones.

### 3.3. Grouting Procedures

*   **Drilling:**
    *   **Hole Diameter:** Depends on the grout mix and desired penetration. Larger diameters are used for cementitious grouts in wider fissures.
    *   **Drilling Method:** Percussive drilling, rotary drilling, or core drilling.
    *   **Drilling Fluid:** Water is common; air flush can be used if it doesn't affect the rock.
*   **Cleaning:** Thorough cleaning of boreholes after drilling is crucial to remove debris that could impede grout flow. This can involve flushing with water or air.
*   **Grouting Execution:**
    *   **Single Hole Grouting:**
        *   **Perfusion Grouting:** Grout is injected from the bottom of the hole upwards, often using packers to isolate sections.
        *   **Downstage Grouting:** Grouting is done in stages from the bottom, advancing the packer upwards as grouting progresses.
    *   **Packer Grouting:** Isolating a specific length of the borehole with a packer and injecting grout. This allows for targeted grouting of specific fissures or zones.
    *   **Reverse Circulation Grouting:** Grouting is done from the top downwards, with return flow of excess grout and drilling fluid. This is less common for dams.
*   **Monitoring:**
    *   **Injection Pressure:** Controlled to prevent hydraulic fracturing of the rock mass.
    *   **Grout Take (Quantity):** The volume of grout injected into a specific section.
    *   **Grout Viscosity and Setting Time:** Monitored at the mixing plant.
    *   **Water Pressure Tests (Lugeon Tests):** Performed before, during, and after grouting to assess permeability and the effectiveness of the grout curtain.
*   **Curing:** Allowing sufficient time for the grout to set and gain strength.

## 4. Design Considerations for Grouting

### 4.1. Foundation Grouting

*   **Geological Investigation:** Detailed site investigation is paramount to identify faults, fissures, joints, bedding planes, and the permeability characteristics of the foundation rock or soil.
*   **Permeability Assessment:**
    *   **Water Pressure Tests (Lugeon Tests):** Standard method to measure the in-situ permeability of rock masses. The Lugeon unit (LU) is a measure of water flow per unit length of borehole under a specified pressure.
    *   **Pumping Tests:** For larger areas or soil foundations.
*   **Grout Curtain Design:**
    *   **Depth:** Based on seepage calculations and desired reduction in flow.
    *   **Thickness:** Related to the grout pattern and desired barrier continuity.
    *   **Spacing of Holes:** Decreases in successive stages to ensure full coverage.
    *   **Permissible Lugeon Values:** Target permeability values for the grouted zones, typically expressed in LU.
*   **Uplift Pressure Mitigation:** Grouting is essential to reduce uplift pressures acting on the dam base, which can reduce its stability.
*   **Erosion Control:** Grouting prevents internal erosion (piping) within the foundation.

### 4.2. Dam Body Grouting

*   **Roller Compacted Concrete (RCC) Dams:** Grouting is used to fill voids between layers of RCC, especially in the heart of the dam, to improve its impermeability and strength.
*   **Concrete Dams:** Grouting can be used to fill construction joints, cracks, and honeycombed areas.
*   **Embankment Dams (Earthfill and Rockfill):** Grouting is less common in the bulk material but might be used for specific features like concrete cores or abutment treatment.

### 4.3. Economic Considerations

*   **Cost of Materials:** Cement, chemical additives, water.
*   **Cost of Equipment:** Drill rigs, pumps, mixers.
*   **Labor Costs:** Skilled personnel are required.
*   **Effectiveness vs. Cost:** Balancing the desired level of permeability reduction with the overall project budget.

## 5. Performance Monitoring and Evaluation

### 5.1. Grouting Effectiveness Monitoring

*   **Post-Grouting Water Pressure Tests:** Comparing Lugeon values before and after grouting to quantify the reduction in permeability.
*   **Seepage Measurement:** Monitoring the actual seepage through the dam foundation and body after impoundment.
*   **Piezometer Readings:** Measuring water levels in piezometers installed in the foundation and downstream of the dam to assess uplift pressures and seepage patterns.

### 5.2. Long-Term Performance

*   **Regular Inspections:** Visual inspection of the dam and downstream areas for signs of erosion or leakage.
*   **Periodic Piezometer Monitoring:** Continuing to monitor water levels.
*   **Secondary Grouting (if necessary):** If seepage or uplift pressures increase significantly over time, further grouting may be required for rehabilitation.

## 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** What are the primary objectives of grouting in dam construction?
**Answer:** The primary objectives are to reduce seepage through the foundation and dam body, improve the mechanical strength and stability of the foundation, and to prevent erosion and piping.

**Question 2:** Briefly describe the main components of a cementitious grout mix and explain the role of admixtures.
**Answer:** The main components are cement and water. Admixtures can be used to modify properties like fluidity (plasticizers), setting time (retarders/accelerators), and to reduce shrinkage or improve workability.

**Question 3:** What is a "grout curtain" and how is it typically constructed?
**Answer:** A grout curtain is a continuous vertical barrier of grout injected into the foundation to intercept seepage. It is typically constructed by drilling a series of closely spaced boreholes in a specific pattern (e.g., staggered line) and injecting grout under pressure, often in multiple stages (primary, secondary, etc.).

**Question 4:** Explain the purpose of a Lugeon test in dam grouting.
**Answer:** A Lugeon test is a water pressure test performed in a borehole to measure the in-situ permeability of a rock mass. It's used to assess the initial permeability of the foundation before grouting and to evaluate the effectiveness of the grout curtain after grouting by comparing the Lugeon values.

**Question 5:** Name two types of chemical grouts and one advantage they might offer over cementitious grouts.
**Answer:** Two types of chemical grouts are silicate grouts and acrylamide grouts. An advantage they might offer is their lower viscosity, allowing them to penetrate finer fissures than cementitious grouts.

**Question 6:** How is the effectiveness of grouting monitored after construction?
**Answer:** Effectiveness is monitored through post-grouting water pressure tests (Lugeon tests), measurement of actual seepage through the dam, and monitoring of piezometric levels in the foundation.

## 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Thorough geological investigation is the foundation of successful grouting.** Understand the rock/soil mass thoroughly.
*   **Cleanliness of boreholes is paramount.** Debris can block fissures and prevent effective grout penetration.
*   **Control injection pressure.** Exceeding the rock's fracture toughness can lead to unwanted hydraulic fracturing, widening fissures instead of sealing them.
*   **Grouting is an iterative process.** Multiple stages with decreasing hole spacing are often necessary to achieve the desired low permeability.
*   **Lugeon tests are critical for design and performance evaluation.** They provide a quantitative measure of permeability.
*   **Proper mixing and pumping are essential.** Inconsistent grout properties or inadequate pressure will lead to poor results.
*   **Long-term monitoring is crucial.** Grouting effectiveness can change over time, and periodic checks are important for dam safety.
