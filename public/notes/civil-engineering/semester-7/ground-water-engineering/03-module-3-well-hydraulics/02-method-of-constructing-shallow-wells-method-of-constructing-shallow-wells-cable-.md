# Ground Water Engineering: Module 3 - Well Hydraulics

## Topic: Method of Constructing Shallow Wells

This module delves into the practical aspects of constructing wells, focusing on shallow wells, and the crucial steps involved in ensuring their efficient and long-lasting performance.

---

### **Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   Understand the principles and methods of constructing shallow wells.
*   Differentiate between the Cable Tool, Rotary, and Reverse Rotary drilling methods.
*   Explain the concept and importance of well completion.
*   Design a gravel-packed well.
*   Discuss various well development techniques.
*   Understand the principles and methods of well rehabilitation.

### **1. Methods of Constructing Shallow Wells**

Shallow wells are typically constructed to access the unconfined aquifer or the shallowest confined aquifer. The choice of construction method depends on factors such as aquifer characteristics, geological formations, cost, and available equipment.

#### **1.1 Cable Tool Method (Churn Drilling)**

This is one of the oldest and simplest methods for drilling wells. It's particularly well-suited for unconsolidated formations and moderately hard rock.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Cable Tool Rig:** A derrick-like structure equipped with a heavy, reciprocating string of drilling tools suspended on a flexible cable.
*   **Jars:** Heavy links in the drill string designed to absorb shock and deliver a sharp upward blow to the bit.
*   **Auger:** A screw-like tool used to remove cuttings from the borehole.
*   **Bailer:** A cylindrical bucket with a valve at the bottom, used to remove loose cuttings from the borehole.
*   **Driving:** The process of forcing the well casing into the ground as the drilling progresses.

**Process:**

1.  **Setup:** The rig is set up over the desired well location.
2.  **Drilling:** A heavy chisel-like bit attached to the drilling tools is repeatedly lifted and dropped onto the formation.
3.  **Cuttings Removal:** Periodically, the bit is withdrawn, and the loose cuttings are removed from the bottom of the hole using a bailer or auger.
4.  **Casing Advancement:** The well casing is driven into the ground concurrently with the drilling, or after a certain depth is reached. This prevents the borehole from collapsing and seals off unwanted water-bearing zones.
5.  **Formation Penetration:** The process continues, with drilling and bailing, until the desired aquifer is reached.

**Advantages:**

*   Simple and relatively inexpensive to operate.
*   Good for driving casing simultaneously.
*   Produces a relatively clean and stable borehole, especially in unconsolidated materials.
*   Can be effective in hard rock formations.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Slow drilling rate compared to rotary methods.
*   Requires a significant amount of water for operation (though less than some rotary methods).
*   Difficult to control the borehole stability in soft, unconsolidated formations without casing.
*   Limited depth capability for large diameter wells.

**Example:** In areas with thick layers of sand and gravel, the cable tool method can be effective for constructing shallow domestic water supply wells.

---

#### **1.2 Rotary Method (Direct Circulation Rotary Drilling)**

This is a common and efficient method for drilling wells, especially in various geological formations.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Rotary Rig:** A drilling rig that uses a rotating drill string with a rotating bit at the end.
*   **Drill String:** A series of connected pipes that transmits rotational and downward force to the drill bit.
*   **Drill Bit:** The cutting tool at the end of the drill string, designed to break up the formation.
*   **Drilling Fluid (Mud):** A mixture of water, clay, and additives circulated down the drill string and up the annulus to:
    *   Cool and lubricate the drill bit.
    *   Carry cuttings to the surface.
    *   Stabilize the borehole walls (by forming a filter cake).
    *   Control formation pressures.
*   **Annulus:** The space between the drill string and the borehole wall.
*   **Circulation:** The continuous flow of drilling fluid from the surface, down the drill string, and back up the annulus.
*   **Mud Pit/Circulating Tank:** Where the drilling fluid is stored and prepared.

**Process:**

1.  **Setup:** The rotary rig is set up over the well location.
2.  **Drilling:** The drill string with the bit is rotated at the surface and lowered into the ground.
3.  **Circulation:** Drilling fluid is pumped down the drill string, out of the bit, and up the annulus.
4.  **Cuttings Removal:** The drilling fluid carries the pulverized formation material (cuttings) to the surface, where they are separated from the fluid in the mud pits.
5.  **Casing Advancement:** Casing is typically installed *after* drilling to the desired depth. The annulus is then sealed with cement.
6.  **Formation Penetration:** The drilling continues until the target aquifer is reached.

**Advantages:**

*   Faster drilling rates compared to the cable tool method.
*   Can drill to greater depths.
*   Better control over borehole stability due to the drilling fluid.
*   Can drill through a wider range of formations, including harder rocks.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Higher initial equipment cost.
*   Requires a continuous supply of drilling fluid and a system for its circulation and treatment.
*   Potential for formation damage if drilling fluid invades the aquifer pores.
*   Casing installation is separate from drilling, which can add time and complexity.

**Example:** For municipal water supply wells or irrigation wells that need to penetrate deeper aquifers, the rotary method is often preferred.

---

#### **1.3 Reverse Rotary Method (Reverse Circulation Rotary Drilling)**

In this method, the drilling fluid and cuttings are circulated *up* the drill string, rather than up the annulus.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Hollow Drill Pipe:** The drill string is hollow, allowing fluid and cuttings to flow upwards.
*   **Swivel (Rotating Sub):** A specialized fitting at the top of the drill string that allows the fluid to be pumped into the hollow pipe while it rotates.
*   **Water Swivel:** A device used to introduce the drilling fluid into the hollow drill pipe.
*   **Cuttings Removal:** Cuttings are carried up the hollow drill pipe and discharged into a collection trough.

**Process:**

1.  **Setup:** The reverse rotary rig is set up.
2.  **Drilling:** The drill string with the bit is rotated.
3.  **Circulation:** Drilling fluid is pumped down the *annulus* between the drill string and the borehole wall. The fluid then flows *up* through the hollow drill pipe along with the cuttings.
4.  **Cuttings Discharge:** The fluid and cuttings are discharged at the surface into a trough, and the fluid is recirculated after removing the cuttings.
5.  **Casing Advancement:** Similar to the direct rotary method, casing is usually installed after drilling.

**Advantages:**

*   Excellent for drilling large diameter holes and in unconsolidated formations, as it minimizes the risk of borehole collapse.
*   Reduces the risk of formation damage because the drilling fluid is not forced into the formation from the annulus.
*   Effective for sampling formations because cuttings are brought to the surface cleanly.
*   Less water is required compared to direct circulation methods in some situations.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Requires specialized equipment and is generally more expensive.
*   Slower drilling rates than direct circulation rotary drilling in many formations.
*   Not as efficient in hard rock formations.
*   Can be problematic if a continuous flow of fluid cannot be maintained up the drill string.

**Example:** This method is often used for constructing large-diameter water supply wells, especially where formations are prone to collapse or where minimal formation damage is desired. It's also effective for drilling through gravel and sand.

### **2. Well Completion**

Well completion refers to the process of installing the well screen, casing, and associated components in the borehole to allow for the efficient and safe withdrawal of water from the aquifer. Proper completion is crucial for the long-term performance of the well.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Well Casing:** The watertight pipe installed in the borehole to prevent collapse, isolate different water-bearing zones, and provide a conduit for water to reach the surface. Typically made of steel, PVC, or other durable materials.
*   **Well Screen:** A slotted or perforated section of pipe installed in the aquifer zone to allow water to enter the well while preventing sediment and aquifer materials from entering. The slot size is critical and depends on the grain size of the aquifer material.
*   **Gravel Pack:** A layer of clean, uniformly graded gravel placed in the annulus between the well screen and the borehole wall.
*   **Gravel Retainer:** A material (like a finer mesh screen or filter) placed below the gravel pack to prevent it from washing out.
*   **Plugs (Cement, Bentonite):** Used to seal off undesirable water-bearing zones or to secure the casing in place.
*   **Annular Seal (Cement Grout):** A cement slurry pumped into the annulus between the casing and the borehole wall, typically from the bottom of the casing to the surface, to prevent surface water contamination and provide structural support.

**Steps in Well Completion:**

1.  **Borehole Cleaning:** Thoroughly cleaning the borehole of all drilling fluids and cuttings.
2.  **Screen and Casing Installation:** Lowering the well screen and casing string into the borehole.
3.  **Gravel Packing (if applicable):** Placing the gravel pack around the well screen.
4.  **Annular Seal Installation:** Cementing or sealing the annulus between the casing and the borehole wall.
5.  **Development:** Performing activities to clean and prepare the aquifer and well components for optimal water flow (discussed later).
6.  **Installation of Wellhead Equipment:** Installing pumps, valves, and other surface facilities.

### **3. Design of Gravel Packed Well**

Gravel packing is a well completion technique used to improve the efficiency and longevity of wells, particularly in formations with fine-grained materials (like sand) that could otherwise clog the well screen.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Formation Material:** The natural sediment or rock surrounding the well.
*   **Well Screen Slot Size:** The opening size of the well screen, designed to retain the formation material.
*   **Gravel Pack Size:** The size of the gravel used for packing, chosen to be larger than the slot size of the screen but smaller than the formation material it's meant to support.
*   **Uniformity Coefficient (Uc):** A measure of the uniformity of grain size in the gravel pack. A low Uc (e.g., 1.5 to 2.5) indicates a well-graded, uniform material, which is desirable. Uc = D60 / D10 (where D60 and D10 are the grain sizes for which 60% and 10% of the material is finer, respectively).
*   **Grain Size Distribution:** The range of particle sizes present in the formation and the gravel pack.

**Design Steps:**

1.  **Analyze Formation Material:** Obtain representative samples of the aquifer material. Determine its grain size distribution (using sieve analysis). Identify the effective size (D10) and D60 of the formation.
2.  **Select Well Screen Slot Size:**
    *   **General Rule:** The slot size should be approximately 40-50% of the D10 size of the formation material.
    *   **More Rigorous Approach (Darcy's Law based):** Slot size is chosen such that the cumulative percentage of formation material finer than the slot size is between 30% and 50%.
    *   **Crucially, the slot size must be smaller than the smallest particle of the gravel pack.**
3.  **Select Gravel Pack Material:**
    *   **Size:** The gravel pack should be significantly coarser than the formation material to prevent it from passing through the pack.
    *   **Uniformity:** A well-graded gravel pack (low Uc) is preferred for better hydraulic conductivity and to prevent bridging and clogging.
    *   **Rule of Thumb for Gravel Pack Size:**
        *   The D10 of the gravel pack should be 4 to 6 times larger than the D10 of the formation material.
        *   The D50 of the gravel pack should be 2 to 4 times larger than the D50 of the formation material.
        *   The D90 of the gravel pack should be 1.5 to 2 times larger than the D90 of the formation material.
    *   **Uniformity Coefficient (Uc) of Gravel Pack:** Typically between 1.5 and 2.5.
    *   **Compatibility:** The slot size of the screen must be smaller than the D5 of the gravel pack (the smallest particle of the gravel pack).

4.  **Calculate Required Gravel Pack Thickness:** The thickness of the gravel pack is usually between 15 cm (6 inches) and 60 cm (2 feet), depending on the formation characteristics and well diameter. A thicker pack provides better support and filtration.

**Example Design:**

*   **Formation Material Analysis:** Sieve analysis reveals:
    *   D10 = 0.3 mm
    *   D50 = 0.7 mm
    *   D60 = 0.9 mm
    *   Uc (formation) = 0.9 / 0.3 = 3.0 (relatively poorly sorted formation)

*   **Well Screen Slot Size Selection:**
    *   Using the 40-50% rule for D10:
        *   0.40 * 0.3 mm = 0.12 mm
        *   0.50 * 0.3 mm = 0.15 mm
    *   Let's choose a slot size of **0.12 mm**.

*   **Gravel Pack Material Selection:**
    *   Desired D10 (gravel) = 4-6 * D10 (formation) = 4-6 * 0.3 mm = 1.2 mm - 1.8 mm
    *   Desired D50 (gravel) = 2-4 * D50 (formation) = 2-4 * 0.7 mm = 1.4 mm - 2.8 mm
    *   Desired D90 (gravel) = 1.5-2 * D90 (formation) = 1.5-2 * 0.9 mm = 1.35 mm - 1.8 mm
    *   Let's target a gravel pack with a D10 of 1.5 mm.
    *   We need to ensure the gravel pack is uniform. Let's aim for a Uc of 2.0.
    *   If D10 = 1.5 mm and Uc = 2.0, then D60 = Uc * D10 = 2.0 * 1.5 mm = 3.0 mm.
    *   We also need to check D50 and D90. A typical well-graded gravel might have D50 ≈ 2.0 mm and D90 ≈ 3.5 mm. These values fall within the desired ranges.
    *   So, we select a gravel pack with a mean grain size of around 1.5-2.0 mm and a low Uc.
    *   **Crucially, check compatibility:** The smallest particle in the gravel pack (D5) should be larger than the slot size (0.12 mm). A gravel with D10 = 1.5 mm will have D5 significantly larger than 0.12 mm.

*   **Gravel Pack Thickness:** Let's specify a thickness of 30 cm (approx. 1 foot).

**Important Note:** The actual selection process often involves referencing established charts and guidelines based on extensive field experience, especially for different types of geological formations.

### **4. Well Development**

Well development is a crucial step after construction and completion. It's the process of removing fine materials, drilling fluid residues, and other obstructions from the well and the immediate surrounding aquifer zone to improve the hydraulic conductivity and efficiency of the well.

**Goals of Well Development:**

*   Remove drilling fluid filter cake.
*   Remove fine particles from the gravel pack.
*   Remove fine particles from the aquifer formation adjacent to the screen.
*   Improve the permeability of the aquifer near the well.
*   Maximize well yield and minimize drawdown.
*   Reduce pumping energy costs.

**Different Methods of Well Development:**

#### **4.1 Mechanical Methods:**

*   **Surging:**
    *   **Concept:** Involves creating rapid fluctuations in water level and pressure within the well to dislodge fine particles.
    *   **How it works:** A surge block (a tight-fitting plunger) or a bailer is rapidly moved up and down in the well, forcing water into and out of the well screen and aquifer.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Very effective in removing fine particles and improving permeability.
    *   **Considerations:** Can be labor-intensive.

*   **Backwashing:**
    *   **Concept:** Forcing water *up* from the bottom of the well through the screen and gravel pack.
    *   **How it works:** Water is pumped into the well at high flow rates, creating an upward velocity that flushes out fine particles.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Good for removing fines.
    *   **Considerations:** Requires a pump capable of producing high flow rates and a way to manage the discharged water.

*   **Jetting:**
    *   **Concept:** Using high-pressure water jets directed at the well screen and formation.
    *   **How it works:** A jetting nozzle is lowered into the well and directed at the screen openings. High-pressure water is pumped through the nozzle, creating turbulence and dislodging fine particles.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Effective in cleaning screen openings and the immediate formation.
    *   **Considerations:** Can be done during pumping or independently.

#### **4.2 Hydraulic Methods:**

*   **Pumping:**
    *   **Concept:** Simply pumping water from the well at progressively higher rates.
    *   **How it works:** As water is pumped, fines are carried out of the screen and formation.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Less effective on its own for thorough development but is a standard step.
    *   **Considerations:** Can be combined with other methods. The discharge water should be monitored for turbidity and sediment.

*   **Hydraulic Fracturing:**
    *   **Concept:** Injecting fluid at very high pressure to create small fractures in the formation, opening up new flow paths.
    *   **How it works:** High-pressure fluid is injected into the well, exceeding the formation's fracture pressure.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Can significantly improve permeability, especially in low-permeability formations.
    *   **Considerations:** Requires specialized equipment and expertise. Can sometimes lead to undesirable water-bearing zone connections if not done carefully.

#### **4.3 Chemical Methods:**

*   **Acidizing:**
    *   **Concept:** Injecting acids to dissolve mineral coatings or precipitates that may be clogging the pore spaces.
    *   **How it works:** Acids (e.g., hydrochloric acid for carbonate rocks, hydrofluoric acid for siliceous rocks) are injected into the well and allowed to react with the formation.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Very effective for carbonate and sandstone formations with mineral scaling.
    *   **Considerations:** Requires careful selection of acid, concentration, and contact time. Potential for formation damage if not done correctly. Neutralization and flushing are essential.

*   **Enzymes and Surfactants:**
    *   **Concept:** Using chemicals to break down organic matter, slime, or oil that might be clogging the formation.
    *   **How it works:** These chemicals are injected into the well and allowed to react.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Useful in specific situations where organic blockage is an issue.
    *   **Considerations:** Less common than acidizing for general development.

**Typical Development Sequence:**

A common sequence involves a combination of methods:

1.  **Initial Pumping:** To remove gross debris.
2.  **Surging/Backwashing:** To dislodge and flush out fine particles.
3.  **High-Rate Pumping:** To carry away loosened material and evaluate well performance.
4.  **Chemical Treatment (if needed):** For specific types of clogging.
5.  **Final Pumping:** Until discharged water is clear and well yield stabilizes.

**Monitoring Development:**

*   **Turbidity:** Measured in the discharged water. Should decrease significantly.
*   **Flow Rate:** Should increase as development progresses.
*   **Drawdown:** Should decrease for a given pumping rate.
*   **Specific Capacity:** (Flow rate / Drawdown) should increase.

### **5. Well Rehabilitation**

Well rehabilitation refers to the techniques used to restore the performance of an aging or underperforming well. Over time, wells can suffer from a decline in yield due to various factors.

**Reasons for Well Performance Decline:**

*   **Clogging of Screen and Formation:**
    *   **Incrustation:** Precipitation of minerals (e.g., calcium carbonate, iron hydroxides) within the screen openings and pore spaces.
    *   **Biofouling:** Growth of bacteria and slime that clogs pores and screens.
    *   **Silting:** Accumulation of fine sediment.
*   **Scaling:** Hard deposits on well surfaces.
*   **Corrosion:** Deterioration of metal components (casing, screen).
*   **Damage to Well Components:** Cracked casing, damaged screen.
*   **Changes in Aquifer Properties:** Depletion of the aquifer, a decrease in water levels.

**Methods of Well Rehabilitation:**

These methods are often similar to well development techniques but are applied to existing wells experiencing problems.

1.  **Mechanical Cleaning:**
    *   **Brushing/Scraping:** Using mechanical brushes or scrapers to remove incrustations and deposits from the casing and screen.
    *   **Jetting:** High-pressure water jetting to clean screens and surrounding formation.
    *   **Surging:** Similar to development, to dislodge accumulated fine material.
    *   **Bailing/Vacuuming:** Removing accumulated sediment from the bottom of the well.

2.  **Chemical Treatment:**
    *   **Acidizing:** Most common method to remove mineral incrustations (e.g., carbonate scale, iron deposits) from carbonate or sandstone formations.
    *   **Caustic Washing:** Using caustic soda to break down organic matter and iron deposits.
    *   **Chlorination:** To kill bacteria and control biofouling.
    *   **Sequestering Agents:** To prevent the precipitation of dissolved minerals.

3.  **Re-gravel Packing:** In some cases, the existing gravel pack may become clogged or disturbed. It can be removed and replaced with a new, properly graded gravel pack.

4.  **Screen Replacement:** If the existing screen is severely corroded or damaged, it may need to be replaced.

5.  **Re-drilling/Re-aming:** In extreme cases, the well may be re-drilled to a larger diameter, or a section of the borehole may be reamed to remove damaged sections.

**Process for Rehabilitation:**

1.  **Diagnosis:** Identify the cause of the performance decline (e.g., water analysis, caliper logs, video inspection, flow testing).
2.  **Selection of Method:** Based on the diagnosis, choose the most appropriate rehabilitation technique.
3.  **Application:** Carefully apply the chosen method(s).
4.  **Development/Flushing:** After treatment, develop the well again to remove loosened debris.
5.  **Testing:** Conduct pumping tests to assess the effectiveness of the rehabilitation.

**Example:** If a well's yield has decreased, and water analysis shows high levels of dissolved iron that are precipitating, a chemical treatment with a mild acid or a sequestering agent followed by surging and pumping might be employed.

---

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Formation Characterization is Key:** Understanding the geology and hydrogeology of the site is fundamental to selecting the appropriate drilling method and completion design.
*   **Screen Slot Size and Gravel Pack Design:** These are critical for efficient water entry and longevity. Incorrect sizing leads to clogging or excessive drawdown.
*   **Well Development is Not Optional:** It's an integral part of the construction process that significantly impacts well performance.
*   **Rehabilitation is Cost-Effective:** Restoring an existing well is often more economical than drilling a new one.
*   **Preventive Maintenance:** Regular monitoring and maintenance can extend the life of a well and reduce the need for costly rehabilitation.
*   **Water Quality:** Consider the potential for contamination and design the well completion (e.g., annular seal) to prevent it.

### **Practice Questions and Exercises:**

**Question 1:** Compare and contrast the Cable Tool Method, Direct Rotary Method, and Reverse Rotary Method for constructing shallow wells, highlighting their advantages and disadvantages.

**Answer:**
*   **Cable Tool:** Simple, good for driving casing, slower, limited depth for large diameters.
*   **Direct Rotary:** Faster, deeper drilling, good borehole stability with mud, higher equipment cost, potential formation damage.
*   **Reverse Rotary:** Excellent for large diameter/unconsolidated formations, minimizes formation damage, requires specialized equipment, slower in hard rock.

**Question 2:** A well is to be constructed in a sand formation with the following grain size distribution: D10 = 0.2 mm, D50 = 0.5 mm, D60 = 0.7 mm. Design a suitable well screen slot size and a gravel pack, specifying their properties.

**Answer:**
*   **Well Screen Slot Size:** Using the 40-50% of D10 rule, the slot size would be between 0.08 mm (40% of 0.2) and 0.10 mm (50% of 0.2). Let's choose **0.10 mm slot size**.
*   **Gravel Pack:**
    *   **Size:** D10 (gravel) should be 4-6 times D10 (formation) = 0.8 mm - 1.2 mm. Let's target D10 = 1.0 mm.
    *   **Uniformity:** Aim for a Uc of 1.5 - 2.5. Let's select Uc = 2.0.
    *   **Gravel Pack Properties:**
        *   D10 = 1.0 mm
        *   Uc = 2.0
        *   D60 = Uc * D10 = 2.0 * 1.0 mm = 2.0 mm
        *   This gravel pack will have particles significantly larger than the 0.10 mm screen slot, preventing clogging.

**Question 3:** What is the primary purpose of well development, and describe at least two common methods used for this process.

**Answer:**
The primary purpose of well development is to remove fine particles, drilling fluid residues, and other obstructions from the well and the surrounding aquifer zone to improve the well's hydraulic conductivity and efficiency.
Two common methods are:
1.  **Surging:** Rapidly moving a surge block or bailer up and down to create pressure fluctuations that dislodge fine particles and force them out of the screen and aquifer.
2.  **Backwashing:** Pumping water upwards through the screen and gravel pack at high rates to flush out fine particles.

**Question 4:** List three common causes for the decline in well performance over time and a corresponding rehabilitation method for each.

**Answer:**
1.  **Cause:** Incrustation (mineral precipitation) on screen and formation.
    **Rehabilitation Method:** Acidizing (to dissolve mineral deposits).
2.  **Cause:** Biofouling (bacterial growth).
    **Rehabilitation Method:** Chlorination or Caustic Washing (to kill bacteria and break down organic matter).
3.  **Cause:** Silting (accumulation of fine sediment).
    **Rehabilitation Method:** Jetting or Surging followed by pumping (to flush out accumulated fines).
