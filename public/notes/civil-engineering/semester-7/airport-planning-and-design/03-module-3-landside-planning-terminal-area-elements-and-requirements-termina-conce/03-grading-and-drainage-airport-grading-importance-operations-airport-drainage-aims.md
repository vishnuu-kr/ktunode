# Airport Planning and Design: Module 3 - Landside Planning (Part 2)

## Topic: Grading and Drainage

---

### 1. Airport Grading: Importance and Operations

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Airport Grading:** The process of shaping the airport's surface to achieve specific elevations, slopes, and contours. This involves earthwork, excavation, filling, and compaction.
*   **Importance:** Crucial for ensuring safe and efficient airport operations, passenger comfort, and structural integrity.
*   **Operations:** Directly impacts ground movement of aircraft, vehicles, and passengers.

**Why is Airport Grading Important?**

*   **Aircraft Operations:**
    *   **Runway and Taxiway Gradients:** Prevents excessive slopes that can be dangerous for landing, takeoff, and ground maneuvering.
    *   **Surface Water Runoff:** Directs water away from critical operational areas, preventing standing water, ice formation, and erosion.
    *   **Visibility:** Ensures clear sightlines for pilots and ground personnel.
    *   **Aircraft Stability:** Affects the stability of aircraft during ground operations, especially in crosswinds.
*   **Terminal Area Operations:**
    *   **Access and Egress:** Facilitates smooth movement of vehicles (buses, taxis, private cars) to and from the terminal.
    *   **Passenger Comfort:** Prevents uneven surfaces, potholes, and water accumulation around the terminal and access routes.
    *   **Service Vehicles:** Supports the efficient movement of baggage carts, catering trucks, fuel trucks, etc.
*   **Drainage System Integration:** Proper grading is the foundation for an effective drainage system.
*   **Stormwater Management:** Controls the flow of stormwater to prevent flooding and erosion.
*   **Environmental Considerations:** Mitigates potential environmental impacts like soil erosion and sediment runoff.
*   **Aesthetics and Landscaping:** Contributes to the overall visual appeal of the airport.

**Key Grading Considerations:**

*   **Longitudinal Gradients:** Slopes along the length of runways and taxiways. Typically kept very flat (e.g., < 1%).
*   **Transverse Gradients:** Slopes across the width of runways and taxiways to promote drainage. Typically around 1% to 1.5%.
*   **Crossfall:** The slope of the apron and terminal area to direct water towards drainage inlets.
*   **Contour Lines:** Imaginary lines connecting points of equal elevation, used to represent the topography.
*   **Earthwork Balance:** Aiming to balance excavation and filling to minimize the cost of transporting soil.

**Example:**

A runway is graded with a slight crossfall (e.g., 1% from the center outwards) to ensure that any rainwater on the surface is directed towards the shoulders and drainage ditches, preventing water from pooling on the runway itself. Similarly, the apron area around the gates will have a crossfall to drain water from aircraft de-icing or cleaning operations.

### 2. Airport Drainage: Aims, Functions, Special Characteristics, and Basic Requirements

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Airport Drainage:** The system designed to collect and dispose of surface and subsurface water from the airport environment.
*   **Aims:** To maintain dry and safe operational surfaces, protect infrastructure, and manage stormwater.

**Aims of Airport Drainage:**

*   **Ensure Safety:** Prevent hydroplaning, ice formation, and slippery surfaces on runways, taxiways, and aprons.
*   **Maintain Operational Efficiency:** Prevent water accumulation that can hinder aircraft and vehicle movement.
*   **Protect Infrastructure:** Prevent erosion, structural damage to pavements, and subsurface saturation that can weaken the foundation.
*   **Stormwater Management:** Control the quantity and quality of stormwater runoff from paved surfaces and open areas.
*   **Environmental Protection:** Prevent pollution of receiving water bodies from contaminated runoff (e.g., fuel spills, de-icing fluids).

**Functions of Airport Drainage:**

*   **Surface Water Collection:** Gathers rainwater, snowmelt, and other surface water.
*   **Water Conveyance:** Transports collected water away from operational areas to safe discharge points.
*   **Stormwater Treatment (often integrated):** May include features to remove pollutants before discharge.
*   **Groundwater Control:** Manages the groundwater table to protect pavement structures.

**Special Characteristics of Airport Drainage:**

*   **Large Paved Areas:** Extensive runways, taxiways, and aprons generate significant runoff.
*   **High Traffic Loads:** Requires robust drainage systems that can withstand the weight of aircraft and vehicles.
*   **Critical Safety Requirements:** Any failure in the drainage system can have immediate and severe safety implications.
*   **Presence of Contaminants:** Runoff may contain fuel, oils, de-icing fluids, and tire debris, requiring special consideration for treatment.
*   **Sensitivity to Gradients:** Precise grading is essential to guide water flow effectively.
*   **Integration with Utilities:** Drainage systems must be carefully coordinated with other underground utilities.
*   **Environmental Regulations:** Must comply with stringent environmental standards for discharge.

**Basic Requirements of Airport Drainage Systems:**

*   **Adequate Capacity:** Must be sized to handle peak storm events without overwhelming the system.
*   **Efficiency:** Effectively collect and convey water away from critical areas.
*   **Durability:** Constructed with materials that can withstand heavy loads and environmental exposure.
*   **Low Maintenance:** Designed for minimal ongoing maintenance requirements.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Balance performance with construction and maintenance costs.
*   **Environmental Compliance:** Meet all applicable environmental discharge standards.
*   **Safety:** Prevent hazards to aircraft and personnel.
*   **Integration:** Coordinate with grading and other airport infrastructure.

### 3. Surface and Subsurface Drainage Systems

**3.1. Surface Drainage Systems**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Surface Drainage:** Systems designed to collect and convey water that flows over the airport surfaces (pavements, open areas).
*   **Gravity Flow:** Relies on natural slopes to move water.

**Components of Surface Drainage Systems:**

*   **Crown and Crossfall:** The primary method of directing surface water towards drainage inlets.
    *   **Runways/Taxiways:** Typically have a longitudinal grade and a slight crossfall.
    *   **Aprons/Gate Areas:** Designed with crossfalls or crowns to direct water towards the edge or collection points.
*   **Open Ditches (Shoulder Ditches):**
    *   **Function:** Linear channels along the sides of pavements to collect and convey surface runoff.
    *   **Characteristics:** Can be grassed, concrete-lined, or French drains.
    *   **Considerations:** Need to be sized appropriately, protected from erosion, and positioned to avoid obstruction to aircraft or vehicle movement.
*   **Gutter Inlets/Catch Basins:**
    *   **Function:** Structures placed at low points or along the edges of paved areas to intercept surface runoff and direct it into underground pipes.
    *   **Types:** Various designs exist, including slotted inlets and grate inlets.
    *   **Considerations:** Grates must be designed to prevent aircraft tires from getting caught. Sizing is crucial to prevent blockage.
*   **Curbs and Pavement Edges:**
    *   **Function:** Define the pavement edge and can guide water towards inlets or ditches.
    *   **Considerations:** Design can influence how water flows off the pavement.
*   **In-Pavement Drainage (less common for runways/taxiways, more for aprons):**
    *   **Function:** Drainage elements integrated directly into the pavement surface.
    *   **Example:** Grooved pavements or specialized permeable surfaces.

**Example:**

Along a runway, the paved surface has a slight cross-slope. Rainwater flows off the runway surface towards the shoulders, where it enters open grassed ditches. These ditches convey the water away from the runway to a larger storm drain system or a retention pond. At the apron, water from rain or aircraft operations flows towards the edge of the apron, where it is collected by a trench drain or a series of catch basins connected to underground pipes.

**3.2. Subsurface Drainage Systems**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Subsurface Drainage:** Systems designed to collect and remove groundwater or water that has infiltrated the pavement structure.
*   **Purpose:** To prevent saturation of the pavement's subgrade and base layers, which can lead to structural failure.

**Components of Subsurface Drainage Systems:**

*   **French Drains (In-ground Drains):**
    *   **Function:** Perforated pipes laid in trenches filled with granular material (gravel/crushed stone) to collect and convey groundwater.
    *   **Location:** Can be placed along pavement edges, under pavements, or in areas with high groundwater tables.
*   **Perforated Pipes:**
    *   **Function:** Pipes with holes that allow groundwater to enter and be drained away.
    *   **Material:** Typically PVC or corrugated plastic.
    *   **Installation:** Buried and often surrounded by filter fabric and gravel.
*   **Underdrains:**
    *   **Function:** A general term for pipes or systems installed below the surface to intercept and remove groundwater.
    *   **Types:** Can include trench drains, collector pipes, or systems integrated into pavement layers.
*   **Filter Fabric:**
    *   **Function:** Placed around granular material and perforated pipes to prevent fine soil particles from clogging the drainage system.
*   **Positive Outlets:**
    *   **Function:** The destination for drained water, which can include ditches, storm sewers, detention/retention ponds, or natural water bodies.

**Example:**

Beneath a high-traffic taxiway, a subsurface drainage system might consist of perforated pipes laid in gravel-filled trenches along the edges of the pavement. These pipes collect any groundwater that infiltrates the pavement base layers. The collected water is then conveyed via gravity to a storm sewer system. This prevents water from weakening the subgrade and compromising the taxiway's structural integrity.

### 4. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Explain three critical reasons why proper airport grading is essential for safe aircraft operations.

**Answer:**
1.  **Control of Surface Water:** Prevents water accumulation on runways and taxiways, reducing the risk of hydroplaning and ice formation.
2.  **Gradient Limits:** Ensures that slopes on runways and taxiways are within safe limits for landing, takeoff, and ground maneuvering, preventing aircraft instability.
3.  **Visibility:** Creates clear sightlines for pilots and ground personnel by managing slopes and preventing uneven surfaces that could obstruct views.

---

**Question 2:**
What are the primary aims of an airport drainage system, and name two special characteristics that differentiate it from urban drainage systems?

**Answer:**
Primary aims:
*   Ensure safety by preventing hazardous surface conditions (hydroplaning, ice).
*   Maintain operational efficiency by keeping surfaces dry.
*   Protect airport infrastructure from water damage and erosion.
*   Manage stormwater runoff and potentially treat contaminated water.

Two special characteristics:
*   **Large Paved Areas:** Airports have significantly larger expanses of paved surfaces generating higher runoff volumes.
*   **Critical Safety Requirements:** Any failure has immediate and severe safety implications for aircraft operations.
*   **Presence of Contaminants:** Runoff can contain fuel, oils, and de-icing fluids, requiring specific management.

---

**Question 3:**
Differentiate between surface drainage and subsurface drainage in an airport context. Provide an example of a component for each system.

**Answer:**
*   **Surface Drainage:** Focuses on collecting and conveying water that flows over the airport's exposed surfaces (pavements, aprons, open areas).
    *   **Example Component:** Open ditches along the shoulders of a runway.
*   **Subsurface Drainage:** Focuses on collecting and removing groundwater or water that infiltrates the pavement structure to prevent saturation and structural damage.
    *   **Example Component:** Perforated pipes laid in gravel-filled trenches beneath taxiways.

**Question 4 (Exercise):**

Imagine you are designing the grading for a new apron area at an airport. The prevailing winds are from the north. Describe how you would incorporate grading principles to facilitate both aircraft operations and drainage.

**Solution Exercise:**

*   **Grading for Drainage:** I would design a gentle crossfall sloping away from the aircraft parking positions towards the edges of the apron. This would direct rainwater, aircraft wash water, and potentially de-icing fluid runoff towards drainage inlets or collection ditches at the apron's perimeter. The crossfall would typically be around 1-1.5%.
*   **Grading for Aircraft Operations:** The grading would ensure a smooth and level surface at the aircraft parking positions to prevent any rocking or instability of the aircraft. The overall slope would be kept minimal to facilitate the movement of ground support equipment and passenger transport vehicles.
*   **Consideration of Wind:** While the primary function of grading is drainage and providing a level surface, the slight crossfall for drainage would be oriented to assist in directing any surface water away from the apron, regardless of wind direction, but it doesn't directly mitigate wind effects on aircraft itself. The primary consideration for wind would be the placement of structures and the design of taxiway approaches, not the apron grading itself in this context.

### 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Grading is the first step:** Effective drainage relies heavily on well-executed grading.
*   **Safety First:** All grading and drainage decisions must prioritize the safety of aircraft and passengers.
*   **Hydraulics Matter:** Drainage systems must be hydraulically designed to handle expected rainfall intensities and runoff volumes.
*   **Environmental Regulations:** Compliance with environmental discharge standards is mandatory.
*   **Maintenance:** While low maintenance is desired, regular inspection and cleaning of drainage components are crucial.
*   **Coordination:** Grading and drainage plans must be integrated with other airport infrastructure development (e.g., utilities, pavement design).
*   **Contaminant Management:** Be aware of potential contaminants in runoff and design systems accordingly.
