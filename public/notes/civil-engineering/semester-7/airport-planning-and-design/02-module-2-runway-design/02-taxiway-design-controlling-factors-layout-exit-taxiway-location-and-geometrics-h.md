# AIRPORT PLANNING AND DESIGN - MODULE 2: RUNWAY DESIGN

## Topic: Taxiway Design

---

### **Learning Outcomes:**

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Identify the controlling factors influencing taxiway design.
*   Understand different taxiway layouts and their advantages/disadvantages.
*   Explain the purpose and design considerations for exit taxiways.
*   Determine the optimal location and geometric features of taxiways.
*   Describe the function and design of holding aprons.
*   Understand the necessity and design of aircraft turn-around facilities.

### **1. Controlling Factors in Taxiway Design**

Taxiway design is governed by a complex interplay of factors to ensure safe, efficient, and effective aircraft movement on the ground. These include:

*   **Aircraft Characteristics:**
    *   **Wingspan and Tail Height:** Dictates the required separation distances from other taxiways, runways, and obstacles. Larger aircraft require wider clearances.
    *   **Turning Radius:** Determines the minimum curve radius needed for aircraft to navigate turns safely without overshooting or requiring excessive maneuver space.
    *   **Gear Span and Tread:** Influences the required width of the taxiway to accommodate the aircraft's landing gear.
    *   **Engine Thrust and Downdraft:** Affects the design of the taxiway surface and separation from surrounding areas (e.g., avoiding excessive dust/debris being kicked up).
    *   **Operating Speed:** Influences the required straight sections and the allowable bank angles on curves.
*   **Airport Master Plan & Air Traffic Control (ATC) Requirements:**
    *   **Operational Volume and Flow:** The number of aircraft movements dictates the capacity and layout of the taxiway system. High-traffic airports need more complex and efficient systems.
    *   **Desired Turn-Around Times:** Efficient taxiway design aims to minimize ground movement times.
    *   **Runway Configuration:** The orientation and number of runways significantly impact taxiway routing.
    *   **Apron Layout:** Taxiways must connect the apron areas efficiently to the runway system.
    *   **Future Expansion Plans:** Taxiways should be designed with flexibility to accommodate anticipated growth in aircraft size and traffic volume.
*   **Topography and Site Constraints:**
    *   **Terrain:** Slopes and natural features can limit potential taxiway alignments and necessitate costly earthworks.
    *   **Environmental Considerations:** Noise abatement, drainage, and proximity to sensitive areas can influence routing.
    *   **Existing Infrastructure:** Utilities, buildings, and other airport facilities need to be factored in.
*   **Safety Standards and Regulations:**
    *   **ICAO Annex 14 and FAA Advisory Circulars:** These provide minimum standards for taxiway widths, clearances, lighting, and markings.
    *   **Separation Distances:** Critical for preventing collisions between aircraft and between aircraft and obstacles.

### **2. Taxiway Layout**

The layout of taxiways aims to create an efficient and safe network for aircraft ground movement. Common layouts include:

*   **Parallel Taxiways:**
    *   Run parallel to the runway, allowing aircraft to enter or exit the runway without occupying it.
    *   **Advantages:** High capacity, improved runway utilization, reduced runway incursions.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires significant land area.
    *   **Example:** A single parallel taxiway on one side of the runway or parallel taxiways on both sides.
*   **Center Taxiways:**
    *   Located in the center of the runway complex, connecting aprons to runways at both ends.
    *   **Advantages:** Efficient for airports with parallel runways, can reduce crossing movements.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can increase complexity for ATC, potential for confusion.
*   **High-Speed Exit Taxiways:**
    *   Designed with gentle curves to allow aircraft to exit the runway at higher speeds, freeing up the runway sooner.
    *   **Advantages:** Increases runway capacity, reduces delays.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires more space than standard exit taxiways.
*   **Apron Taxiways:**
    *   Connect aircraft parking stands to the main taxiway system.
    *   **Considerations:** Wide enough for maneuvering at parking stands, adequate visibility for ground controllers.
*   **Connecting Taxiways:**
    *   Link different runways or taxiways.
    *   **Considerations:** Proper geometric design to accommodate aircraft turning radii.

**Key Concept:** **Operational Efficiency** is paramount in taxiway layout. The system should minimize taxiing distances and time, reduce conflicting movements, and provide clear routing for pilots and ground controllers.

### **3. Exit Taxiways**

Exit taxiways are critical for efficiently clearing runways.

*   **Purpose:** To allow aircraft to leave the runway after landing, thereby freeing it up for the next aircraft.
*   **Types:**
    *   **45-degree Exit Taxiways:** Standard exit at a 45-degree angle to the runway.
    *   **60-degree Exit Taxiways:** Exit at a 60-degree angle, allowing for slightly higher exit speeds.
    *   **High-Speed Exit Taxiways:** Designed with larger radius curves and longer straight sections to allow aircraft to exit at speeds up to 60 knots (or higher depending on aircraft category).
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   **Entry Angle:** The angle at which the exit taxiway meets the runway is crucial for safe and efficient departure.
    *   **Radius of Curvature:** Larger radii are required for higher-speed exits.
    *   **Length of Curve and Straight Sections:** Adequate length is needed to decelerate and transition smoothly.
    *   **Visibility:** Pilots must have good visibility of the exit taxiway from the runway.
    *   **Clearances:** Adequate clearance from obstacles and other taxiways.
    *   **Surface Markings and Lighting:** Essential for guiding pilots.

**Example:** A busy airport might have a mix of 45-degree and high-speed exits to optimize runway throughput.

### **4. Taxiway Location and Geometrics**

The precise placement and geometric features of taxiways are vital for safety and operational efficiency.

*   **Location:**
    *   **Separation from Runways:** Adequate distance is required to prevent engine backwash from affecting aircraft on the runway and to minimize the risk of runway incursions. Standard separation distances are defined by ICAO and FAA.
    *   **Separation from Obstacles:** Clearances from buildings, terrain, and other airport infrastructure must be maintained.
    *   **Relationship to Aprons and Hangars:** Logical connection to service areas.
    *   **Consideration of Wind:** While runways are aligned with prevailing winds, taxiways may need to account for crosswinds during turns.
*   **Geometrics:**
    *   **Width:** Varies based on the aircraft category the taxiway is designed to serve. Wider taxiways provide more clearance for larger aircraft.
    *   **Shoulder Width:** Provides a safety buffer on either side of the taxiway pavement.
    *   **Turning Radii:** The inner and outer radii of curves are critical. Designed based on the turning radius of the largest aircraft using the taxiway, with allowances for pilot judgment.
        *   **Minimum Turning Radius:** The tightest curve an aircraft can execute.
        *   **Design Turning Radius:** A larger radius that ensures safe and comfortable turning.
    *   **Grades (Slopes):** Gentle slopes are preferred to avoid excessive braking or acceleration. Steep grades can be problematic for aircraft with tail-dragger configurations or during adverse weather.
    *   **Visibility:** Pilots need sufficient visibility along the taxiway and at intersections. Sightlines can be affected by terrain and structures.
    *   **Intersections:** Designed to facilitate smooth flow and minimize potential conflict points. Angles of intersection are important.

**Key Concept:** **Aircraft Reference Code (ARC)** is used to categorize aircraft based on their wingspan and tail height. Taxiway geometrics are designed to accommodate the specific ARC of aircraft expected to use them.

### **5. Holding Aprons**

Holding aprons (also known as holding bays or waiting areas) are designated areas where aircraft wait before entering a runway or taxiing across an active runway.

*   **Purpose:**
    *   To provide a safe waiting area for aircraft.
    *   To prevent aircraft from encroaching onto the runway.
    *   To facilitate efficient sequencing of aircraft by ATC.
*   **Location:**
    *   Adjacent to runway entrances.
    *   At designated points for runway crossings.
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   **Size:** Sufficient space to accommodate the largest aircraft expected to use it, with adequate clearance from the runway edge.
    *   **Surface:** Paved and clearly marked.
    *   **Visibility:** Pilots should have good visibility of the runway and ATC tower.
    *   **Lighting:** Adequate lighting for nighttime operations.
    *   **Markings:** Clear painted lines indicating the holding point.

**Example:** At the end of a runway, aircraft might hold at a designated line on the apron before being cleared for takeoff.

---

### **6. Turn Around Facility**

Turn-around facilities are essential for aircraft that need to reverse direction on a runway or taxiway.

*   **Purpose:**
    *   To allow aircraft to turn around when they cannot make a standard 180-degree turn using the runway width.
    *   To facilitate aircraft maneuvering in confined areas like taxiways or aprons.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Runway Turnaround Pads:** Designated paved areas at the ends of runways for larger aircraft.
    *   **90-degree Turnarounds:** Using a wider section of a taxiway or apron.
    *   **U-Turn Taxiways:** Dedicated sections of taxiway designed for U-turns.
    *   **Spike/De-Spike Areas:** Areas where aircraft can be pushed back by tugs.
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   **Turning Radius:** Must accommodate the turning radius of the intended aircraft.
    *   **Surface Strength:** Capable of withstanding the weight of the aircraft.
    *   **Clearances:** Adequate space around the turnaround facility.
    *   **Markings:** Clear markings to guide pilots.

**Example:** A Boeing 747 might require a specific runway turnaround pad due to its large wingspan and turning radius, whereas a smaller aircraft might be able to execute a turn on a standard runway.

---

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Aircraft Characteristics are Primary Drivers:** Always consider the specific aircraft that will operate at the airport.
*   **Safety First:** All taxiway design decisions must prioritize safety, with a strong emphasis on clearances and preventing runway incursions.
*   **Efficiency is Key:** A well-designed taxiway system minimizes ground delays and maximizes airport operational capacity.
*   **Regulations are Mandatory:** Adhere strictly to ICAO and national aviation authority standards.
*   **Future-Proofing:** Design for anticipated growth in aircraft size and traffic volume.
*   **Clear Communication:** Effective signage, lighting, and markings are crucial for pilot guidance.

### **Answers to Practice Questions:**

1.  **Aircraft Characteristics:**
    *   **Wingspan and Tail Height:** Influence required clearances from other aircraft, structures, and along taxiway edges.
    *   **Turning Radius:** Determines the minimum curve radius needed for safe navigation without encroaching on adjacent areas.
    *   **Gear Span and Tread:** Dictates the required width of the taxiway pavement.
2.  **Parallel Taxiways Advantage:** The main advantage is increased runway capacity and operational efficiency. Aircraft can exit or enter the runway via the parallel taxiway without occupying the active runway, allowing for quicker sequential operations.
3.  **High-Speed Exit Taxiways Importance:** They allow aircraft to leave the runway at higher speeds, significantly reducing the time an aircraft occupies the runway after landing. This frees up the runway sooner for subsequent arrivals or departures, thereby increasing the airport's overall runway capacity and reducing arrival delays.
4.  **Key Geometric Features and Importance:**
    *   **Width:** Crucial for providing adequate clearance for the aircraft's wingspan and landing gear, preventing wingtip strikes or damage to the pavement.
    *   **Turning Radii:** Essential for ensuring aircraft can safely negotiate turns on taxiways and at intersections without overshooting, veering off the pavement, or experiencing excessive side forces.
5.  **Purpose of Holding Apron:** The purpose of a holding apron is to provide a safe and designated waiting area for aircraft before they are cleared to enter an active runway or to cross it. This prevents aircraft from encroaching on the runway while awaiting clearance from Air Traffic Control (ATC).
6.  **Turn-Around Facility Necessity Example:** A turn-around facility is necessary when an aircraft, particularly a large one with a wide wingspan, needs to reverse its direction on a runway or taxiway but cannot execute a standard 180-degree turn within the available paved width. For instance, a cargo aircraft at the end of a runway might require a specially designed turnaround pad to reverse direction before taxiing back to the terminal.
