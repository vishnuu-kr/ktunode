# Economics for Engineers: Module 1 - Basic Economic Problems

## Topic: Changes in Demand and Supply and Its Effects

---

### **Introduction**

This topic explores how shifts in the forces of demand and supply impact market equilibrium. Understanding these dynamics is crucial for engineers who often deal with resource allocation, pricing, and forecasting in their projects. Changes in demand or supply can signal shifts in consumer preferences, technological advancements, input costs, or government policies, all of which have tangible economic consequences.

---

### **Learning Outcomes**

Upon completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   **LO1:** Differentiate between a movement along a demand/supply curve and a shift of the demand/supply curve.
*   **LO2:** Identify the key determinants of demand and supply.
*   **LO3:** Analyze the effects of shifts in demand and supply on equilibrium price and quantity.
*   **LO4:** Apply economic principles to real-world scenarios involving changes in demand and supply.

### **1. Movements vs. Shifts in Demand and Supply**

It's critical to distinguish between two ways the market equilibrium can change:

#### **1.1 Movement Along the Demand Curve (Change in Quantity Demanded)**

*   **Definition:** A movement along the demand curve occurs when there is a change in the **price** of the good itself, assuming all other factors (determinants of demand) remain constant.
*   **Cause:** Exclusively a change in the **price** of the product.
*   **Effect:** Leads to a change in the **quantity demanded** at that new price.
*   **Graphical Representation:** A movement from one point to another *on the same* demand curve.
    *   **Increase in Price:** Movement *upwards and to the left* along the demand curve.
    *   **Decrease in Price:** Movement *downwards and to the right* along the demand curve.

#### **1.2 Shift of the Demand Curve (Change in Demand)**

*   **Definition:** A shift of the demand curve occurs when there is a change in any of the **determinants of demand**, *other than the price of the good itself*. This leads to a new relationship between price and quantity demanded at *every* price level.
*   **Cause:** Changes in factors *other than the price of the good*.
*   **Effect:** Leads to a change in **demand** itself.
*   **Graphical Representation:** The entire demand curve moves to the right or to the left.
    *   **Increase in Demand (Rightward Shift):** At every price, consumers are willing and able to buy more.
    *   **Decrease in Demand (Leftward Shift):** At every price, consumers are willing and able to buy less.

#### **1.3 Movement Along the Supply Curve (Change in Quantity Supplied)**

*   **Definition:** A movement along the supply curve occurs when there is a change in the **price** of the good itself, assuming all other factors (determinants of supply) remain constant.
*   **Cause:** Exclusively a change in the **price** of the product.
*   **Effect:** Leads to a change in the **quantity supplied** at that new price.
*   **Graphical Representation:** A movement from one point to another *on the same* supply curve.
    *   **Increase in Price:** Movement *upwards and to the right* along the supply curve.
    *   **Decrease in Price:** Movement *downwards and to the left* along the supply curve.

#### **1.4 Shift of the Supply Curve (Change in Supply)**

*   **Definition:** A shift of the supply curve occurs when there is a change in any of the **determinants of supply**, *other than the price of the good itself*. This leads to a new relationship between price and quantity supplied at *every* price level.
*   **Cause:** Changes in factors *other than the price of the good*.
*   **Effect:** Leads to a change in **supply** itself.
*   **Graphical Representation:** The entire supply curve moves to the right or to the left.
    *   **Increase in Supply (Rightward Shift):** At every price, producers are willing and able to sell more.
    *   **Decrease in Supply (Leftward Shift):** At every price, producers are willing and able to sell less.

### **2. Determinants of Demand and Supply**

Understanding what causes shifts is key to analyzing market changes.

#### **2.1 Determinants of Demand (Factors that Shift the Demand Curve)**

These are factors that, if they change, cause the entire demand curve to shift.

*   **Consumer Income:**
    *   **Normal Goods:** As income increases, demand increases (rightward shift). As income decreases, demand decreases (leftward shift).
        *   *Example:* Demand for new cars or restaurant meals.
    *   **Inferior Goods:** As income increases, demand decreases (leftward shift). As income decreases, demand increases (rightward shift).
        *   *Example:* Demand for instant noodles or public transport.
*   **Prices of Related Goods:**
    *   **Substitutes:** Goods that can be used in place of each other. If the price of a substitute increases, demand for the original good increases (rightward shift).
        *   *Example:* If the price of coffee increases, demand for tea might increase.
    *   **Complements:** Goods that are often consumed together. If the price of a complement increases, demand for the original good decreases (leftward shift).
        *   *Example:* If the price of gasoline increases, demand for SUVs might decrease.
*   **Consumer Tastes and Preferences:** Changes in fashion, trends, or advertising can influence demand.
    *   *Example:* A health trend could increase demand for organic food (rightward shift).
*   **Consumer Expectations:** Expectations about future prices, income, or availability can affect current demand.
    *   *Example:* If consumers expect a product's price to rise soon, they might buy more now (rightward shift).
*   **Number of Buyers (Market Size):** An increase in the number of consumers in the market increases demand (rightward shift).
    *   *Example:* Population growth or a new market opening up.
*   **Demographics:** Changes in the age, gender, or distribution of the population can affect demand for certain products.
    *   *Example:* An aging population might increase demand for healthcare services.

#### **2.2 Determinants of Supply (Factors that Shift the Supply Curve)**

These are factors that, if they change, cause the entire supply curve to shift.

*   **Input Prices (Costs of Production):** If the cost of resources used to produce a good increases, supply decreases (leftward shift). If costs decrease, supply increases (rightward shift).
    *   *Example:* An increase in the price of steel would decrease the supply of cars.
*   **Technology:** Improvements in technology usually lower production costs and increase efficiency, leading to an increase in supply (rightward shift).
    *   *Example:* A new manufacturing process that reduces waste increases the supply of widgets.
*   **Government Policies:**
    *   **Taxes:** Taxes on production increase costs, leading to a decrease in supply (leftward shift).
    *   **Subsidies:** Subsidies for production lower costs, leading to an increase in supply (rightward shift).
    *   **Regulations:** Stricter regulations can increase production costs, decreasing supply (leftward shift).
*   **Expectations of Producers:** Producers' expectations about future prices can influence current supply.
    *   *Example:* If producers expect the price of their product to fall in the future, they might try to sell more now (rightward shift of current supply).
*   **Number of Sellers:** An increase in the number of producers in the market increases supply (rightward shift).
    *   *Example:* New firms entering the market.
*   **Natural Events/Disasters:** Weather conditions, natural disasters, or disease outbreaks can significantly impact the supply of agricultural products or affect production facilities.
    *   *Example:* A drought would decrease the supply of wheat.

### **3. Effects of Shifts on Equilibrium Price and Quantity**

When either the demand or supply curve shifts, the market moves to a new equilibrium point.

#### **3.1 Effect of a Demand Shift:**

*   **Increase in Demand (Rightward Shift of Demand Curve):**
    *   **Effect:** Equilibrium Price **increases**, Equilibrium Quantity **increases**.
    *   **Reasoning:** At the original equilibrium price, there is now excess demand (quantity demanded > quantity supplied). Consumers are willing to pay more, driving prices up. As prices rise, suppliers are incentivized to produce more, leading to a higher equilibrium quantity.

*   **Decrease in Demand (Leftward Shift of Demand Curve):**
    *   **Effect:** Equilibrium Price **decreases**, Equilibrium Quantity **decreases**.
    *   **Reasoning:** At the original equilibrium price, there is now excess supply (quantity supplied > quantity demanded). Sellers have unsold goods and must lower prices to sell them. As prices fall, producers are willing to supply less, leading to a lower equilibrium quantity.

#### **3.2 Effect of a Supply Shift:**

*   **Increase in Supply (Rightward Shift of Supply Curve):**
    *   **Effect:** Equilibrium Price **decreases**, Equilibrium Quantity **increases**.
    *   **Reasoning:** At the original equilibrium price, there is now excess supply (quantity supplied > quantity demanded). Producers have more goods than consumers want at that price, forcing them to lower prices to clear inventory. As prices fall, consumers are incentivized to buy more, leading to a higher equilibrium quantity.

*   **Decrease in Supply (Leftward Shift of Supply Curve):**
    *   **Effect:** Equilibrium Price **increases**, Equilibrium Quantity **decreases**.
    *   **Reasoning:** At the original equilibrium price, there is now excess demand (quantity demanded > quantity supplied). Consumers want more than is available, leading to competition and driving prices up. As prices rise, producers are willing to supply less (due to higher costs or scarcity), leading to a lower equilibrium quantity.

#### **3.3 Simultaneous Shifts:**

When both demand and supply shift, the effect on either price or quantity can be indeterminate without knowing the magnitude of the shifts.

*   **Demand Increases, Supply Decreases:** Price **definitely increases**. The effect on quantity depends on the relative size of the shifts.
*   **Demand Decreases, Supply Increases:** Price **definitely decreases**. The effect on quantity depends on the relative size of the shifts.
*   **Both Demand and Supply Increase:** Quantity **definitely increases**. The effect on price depends on the relative size of the shifts.
*   **Both Demand and Supply Decrease:** Quantity **definitely decreases**. The effect on price depends on the relative size of the shifts.

### **4. Applications for Engineers**

*   **Product Design and Development:** Understanding how consumer preferences (demand) and input costs/technology (supply) change can inform decisions about what products to develop and how to design them for cost-effectiveness.
*   **Pricing Strategies:** Engineers involved in project costing or product pricing need to anticipate how market changes will affect prices and demand.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Knowledge of supply and demand helps in understanding the availability and cost of materials, labor, and energy, guiding efficient resource allocation.
*   **Forecasting:** Predicting future market conditions for materials, components, or end products requires understanding the determinants of supply and demand.
*   **Policy Analysis:** Engineers may need to assess the economic impact of government policies (taxes, subsidies, regulations) on their industry.

---

### **Key Concepts to Remember**

*   **Movement along the curve:** Caused *only* by a change in the price of the good itself. Changes **quantity demanded** or **quantity supplied**.
*   **Shift of the curve:** Caused by changes in the *determinants* of demand or supply (other than the good's own price). Changes **demand** or **supply**.
*   **Law of Demand:** As price increases, quantity demanded decreases (and vice versa), holding other factors constant.
*   **Law of Supply:** As price increases, quantity supplied increases (and vice versa), holding other factors constant.
*   **Equilibrium:** The point where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, determining equilibrium price and quantity.
*   **Normal vs. Inferior Goods:** Distinguish how income changes affect demand for different types of goods.
*   **Substitutes vs. Complements:** Understand how the price of related goods affects demand.
*   **Impact of Shifts:**
    *   **Demand shifts:** Affect both equilibrium price and quantity in the same direction as the shift.
    *   **Supply shifts:** Affect equilibrium price in the opposite direction of the shift and equilibrium quantity in the same direction.
