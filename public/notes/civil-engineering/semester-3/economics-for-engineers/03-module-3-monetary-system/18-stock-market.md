## 1. Understanding the Stock Market: Purpose and Functions

### What is a Stock Market?

*   A **stock market** (also known as a stock exchange) is a marketplace where buyers and sellers trade **shares of ownership (stocks)** in publicly traded companies.
*   It provides a platform for companies to raise capital by issuing new stocks and for investors to buy and sell these stocks.

### Key Functions of a Stock Market:

*   **Capital Raising:**
    *   Companies can sell stock to the public to raise money for expansion, research and development, debt repayment, or other business needs.
    *   This is a crucial way for businesses to finance growth without relying solely on debt.
*   **Liquidity:**
    *   The stock market provides a liquid market for investors, meaning they can buy and sell stocks relatively easily and quickly at prevailing market prices.
    *   This liquidity is essential for investors to access their capital when needed.
*   **Price Discovery:**
    *   Through the continuous interaction of buyers and sellers, the stock market helps determine the fair market value of companies.
    *   Supply and demand forces dictate stock prices, reflecting investor sentiment and company performance.
*   **Facilitating Investment:**
    *   It allows individuals and institutions to invest their savings in productive assets, potentially earning returns through capital appreciation and dividends.
*   **Economic Barometer:**
    *   The performance of the stock market is often seen as an indicator of the overall health of the economy. Rising stock markets can signal economic optimism, while falling markets can suggest economic concerns.

## 2. Primary vs. Secondary Markets

### Primary Market:

*   **Definition:** The market where **new securities (stocks and bonds) are issued for the first time directly from the issuer to investors.**
*   **Purpose:** To allow companies and governments to raise capital.
*   **Key Event:** Initial Public Offering (IPO) – the first time a private company offers its shares to the public.
*   **Participants:** Issuing company, investment banks (underwriters), and initial investors.
*   **Flow of Funds:** Investors pay money to the issuing company.
*   **Example:** When Company X decides to go public, it works with an investment bank to issue shares for the first time. The money raised from the sale of these IPO shares goes directly to Company X.

### Secondary Market:

*   **Definition:** The market where **previously issued securities are traded among investors.** The issuing company is not directly involved in these transactions.
*   **Purpose:** To provide liquidity for investors and allow for price discovery.
*   **Key Activity:** Trading of stocks on stock exchanges (e.g., NYSE, Nasdaq, London Stock Exchange).
*   **Participants:** Investors (individual and institutional), brokers, dealers, and stock exchanges.
*   **Flow of Funds:** Investors buy and sell securities from and to other investors. The issuing company does not receive funds from these trades.
*   **Example:** After Company X's IPO, investors can buy and sell shares of Company X on the stock exchange. When one investor sells their shares, another investor buys them. Company X itself does not receive money from these secondary market trades.

## 3. Types of Financial Instruments Traded

The stock market primarily trades **equity securities**, which represent ownership in a company. The most common types are:

### Common Stock (Ordinary Shares):

*   **Definition:** Represents **actual ownership in a company.** Common stockholders are the residual owners of the company.
*   **Key Features:**
    *   **Voting Rights:** Typically, common stockholders have the right to vote on important company matters, such as electing the board of directors. Each share usually equals one vote.
    *   **Dividends:** Companies may pay dividends to common stockholders, which are a portion of the company's profits. However, dividends are not guaranteed and are paid only after preferred stockholders receive their dividends.
    *   **Capital Appreciation:** Investors hope the stock price will increase over time, allowing them to sell their shares for a profit.
    *   **Risk:** Generally considered riskier than preferred stock because they are paid last in the event of liquidation and dividends are not guaranteed.
*   **Example:** Owning 100 shares of Apple (AAPL) common stock means you own a small piece of Apple and have voting rights on certain company decisions.
