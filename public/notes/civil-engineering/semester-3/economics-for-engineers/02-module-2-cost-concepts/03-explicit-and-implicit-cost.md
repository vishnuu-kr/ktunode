# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 2: Cost Concepts

## Topic: Explicit and Implicit Costs

---

### **1. Introduction to Cost Concepts in Engineering Economics**

*   **Why are costs important for engineers?**
    *   Informed decision-making in project selection, design, and resource allocation.
    *   Understanding the true economic cost of a decision, not just the accounting cost.
    *   Maximizing profitability and minimizing waste.
*   **The concept of "opportunity cost":** The value of the next best alternative that must be forgone to pursue a certain action. This is central to understanding economic costs.

---

### **2. Explicit Costs**

*   **Definition:** Explicit costs, also known as **accounting costs** or **out-of-pocket costs**, are direct, measurable payments made by a firm or individual for the use of resources. These are costs that involve an actual monetary transaction.

*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Involve a direct disbursement of cash.
    *   Are easily identifiable and quantifiable from accounting records.
    *   Are typically recorded in financial statements.

*   **Examples:**
    *   **Wages and Salaries:** Payments to employees for their labor.
    *   **Rent:** Payments made for the use of land, buildings, or equipment.
    *   **Raw Materials:** Cost of materials purchased for production.
    *   **Utilities:** Payments for electricity, water, gas, etc.
    *   **Interest Payments:** Cost of borrowing money.
    *   **Taxes:** Payments to government bodies.
    *   **Machinery Purchases:** The cash paid to acquire new equipment.
    *   **Consultant Fees:** Payments to external experts.

*   **Relevance for Engineers:**
    *   Crucial for budgeting and financial reporting.
    *   Help in understanding the immediate cash outflow associated with a project.
    *   Essential for calculating accounting profit.

---

### **3. Implicit Costs**

*   **Definition:** Implicit costs, also known as **economic costs** or **opportunity costs**, represent the value of resources that are owned and used by the firm but for which no direct monetary payment is made. These costs arise from the forgone opportunities to use these resources in their next-best alternative use.

*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Do not involve a direct cash outlay.
    *   Represent the value of forgone earnings or benefits.
    *   Are often subjective and require estimation.
    *   Are essential for making true economic decisions.

*   **Examples:**
    *   **Owner's Salary (Foregone):** If an engineer owns their own consulting firm and chooses to work there instead of taking a higher-paying job elsewhere, the forgone salary from the alternative job is an implicit cost.
    *   **Owner's Capital (Foregone Interest/Return):** If an engineer invests their own savings into their business instead of investing it in stocks or bonds that would yield a certain return, the forgone interest or return on investment is an implicit cost.
    *   **Owner's Time:** The value of the owner's time spent managing the business, if that time could have been used productively in another endeavor.
    *   **Use of Owner's Property:** If an engineer uses their own building for their business, the implicit cost is the rent they could have earned by renting it out to someone else.
    *   **Depreciation of Owner's Assets:** While depreciation is an accounting concept, the portion of depreciation that represents the consumption of the owner's capital, which could have been used elsewhere, can be viewed as an implicit cost from an economic perspective.

*   **Relevance for Engineers:**
    *   Critical for **make-or-buy decisions**, **project evaluation**, and **long-term strategic planning**.
    *   Helps in understanding the **true economic profitability** of a venture.
    *   Ensures that the decision-maker considers all relevant costs, not just those that appear on an accounting ledger.

---

### **4. Economic Costs vs. Accounting Costs**

*   **Accounting Cost = Explicit Costs Only**
    *   Used for financial reporting, tax purposes, and measuring accounting profit.
    *   Focuses on past transactions and monetary outlays.

*   **Economic Cost = Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs**
    *   Used for decision-making, evaluating the true profitability of a venture, and comparing alternatives.
    *   Considers both direct payments and forgone opportunities.
    *   **Economic Profit = Total Revenue - Economic Costs**
    *   **Accounting Profit = Total Revenue - Accounting Costs**

*   **Relationship:** Economic Cost $\ge$ Accounting Cost

---

### **5. Importance of Differentiating Costs for Engineers**

*   **Investment Decisions:** When evaluating whether to invest in a new project or technology, engineers must consider not only the explicit purchase costs but also the implicit costs, such as the forgone profits from alternative investments or the value of their own time.
*   **Project Management:** Understanding implicit costs helps in efficiently allocating scarce resources. For example, if a project requires an engineer's specialized expertise, the implicit cost of that expertise (what they could earn elsewhere) should be considered.
*   **Make-or-Buy Decisions:** When deciding whether to manufacture a component in-house or purchase it from an external supplier, implicit costs (e.g., the opportunity cost of using existing machinery for this purpose) play a significant role.
*   **Resource Optimization:** By recognizing implicit costs, engineers can identify opportunities to free up resources that are being used inefficiently or could generate greater returns elsewhere.

### **7. Practice Questions & Exercises**

**Question 1:**
An engineer decides to start their own consulting business. They invest $50,000 of their own savings into the business. If they had invested this money in a mutual fund, they would have earned a 7% annual return. The engineer also works full-time for the business and forgoes a salary of $80,000 per year from their previous job.

Identify and quantify the explicit and implicit costs associated with the engineer's first year of operation, assuming the business has no other expenses.

**Answer 1:**
*   **Explicit Costs:** $0 (Based on the information provided, there are no direct monetary payments made for resources in the first year.)
*   **Implicit Costs:**
    *   **Forgone Interest on Savings:** $50,000 * 0.07 = $3,500
    *   **Forgone Salary:** $80,000
    *   **Total Implicit Costs:** $3,500 + $80,000 = $83,500
*   **Total Economic Cost:** $0 (Explicit) + $83,500 (Implicit) = $83,500

**Question 2:**
A manufacturing company is considering using its own idle factory space to produce a new product. The factory space could be rented out to another company for $15,000 per month. The company will incur explicit costs of $10,000 per month for raw materials and labor to produce the new product.

What is the total economic cost of using the factory space for one month for the new product?

**Answer 2:**
*   **Explicit Costs:** $10,000 (Raw materials and labor)
*   **Implicit Cost:** $15,000 (Forgone rent from leasing the factory space)
*   **Total Economic Cost:** $10,000 + $15,000 = $25,000

**Question 3:**
Distinguish between accounting profit and economic profit. Provide a scenario where accounting profit is positive but economic profit is negative.

**Answer 3:**
*   **Accounting Profit:** Calculated as Total Revenue minus Explicit Costs. It reflects the financial performance based on actual monetary transactions.
*   **Economic Profit:** Calculated as Total Revenue minus Economic Costs (Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs). It reflects the true profitability by considering the opportunity cost of all resources used.

*   **Scenario:**
    Suppose an engineer starts a small software development company.
    *   Total Revenue in Year 1: $150,000
    *   Explicit Costs (salaries for employees, rent, software licenses, etc.): $100,000
    *   Accounting Profit = $150,000 - $100,000 = $50,000

    However, the engineer also invested $200,000 of their own savings into the business, which could have earned them 10% per year in the stock market. The forgone salary from a previous job was $90,000.
    *   Implicit Cost (Forgone Interest): $200,000 * 0.10 = $20,000
    *   Implicit Cost (Forgone Salary): $90,000
    *   Total Implicit Costs: $20,000 + $90,000 = $110,000
    *   Total Economic Costs = $100,000 (Explicit) + $110,000 (Implicit) = $210,000
    *   Economic Profit = $150,000 (Revenue) - $210,000 (Economic Costs) = -$60,000

    In this scenario, the company shows an accounting profit of $50,000, suggesting financial success. However, from an economic perspective, the company is losing $60,000 because the resources used could have generated a higher return elsewhere. This highlights that a venture might be accounting profitable but economically unprofitable.

### **8. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Always consider opportunity cost** when making economic decisions.
*   **Explicit costs are always part of economic costs.**
*   **Implicit costs represent forgone benefits** and are often subjective but crucial for accurate economic analysis.
*   **Economic profit is a more comprehensive measure of profitability** than accounting profit.
*   Engineers must use economic cost analysis to make **sound, long-term investment and project decisions.**
*   Ignoring implicit costs can lead to **suboptimal or even detrimental business decisions.**
