# ADVANCED DESIGN OF STEEL STRUCTURES - MODULE 2: STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF WELD METAL

## Topic: Beam to-Column Connections - Stiffened Beam Seat Connection, Web Angle and End Plate Connections

This module delves into the behavior and design of common beam-to-column connections used in steel structures. We will explore the structural characteristics and design principles of stiffened beam seat connections, web angle connections, and end plate connections, focusing on their load-carrying mechanisms, failure modes, and design considerations according to relevant standards.

---

### 1. Introduction to Beam-to-Column Connections

*   **Definition:** Beam-to-column connections are critical structural elements that transmit forces (shear, moment, axial force) between beams and columns, ensuring the stability and load-carrying capacity of the entire structure.
*   **Importance:**
    *   Efficient load transfer.
    *   Maintaining structural integrity and stability.
    *   Influencing the overall behavior (e.g., frame action, sway).
    *   Affecting constructability and cost.
*   **Classification based on Moment Resistance:**
    *   **Simply Supported (Pinned):** Assumed to transfer only shear forces, no moment resistance.
    *   **Fixed (Rigid):** Assumed to transfer shear and moment, with negligible relative rotation.
    *   **Partial Restraint:** Transfers shear and a certain degree of moment, exhibiting some flexibility.

### 2. Stiffened Beam Seat Connection

#### 2.1 Description and Configuration

*   **Concept:** A beam seat connection where the primary support for the beam is provided by a horizontal plate welded to the column web. This seat plate is often stiffened by a vertical web perpendicular to it, extending upwards to the beam flange.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Seat Plate:** A horizontal plate welded to the column web, providing vertical support for the beam's bottom flange.
    *   **Stiffener (Web):** A vertical plate welded to the seat plate and the column web, reinforcing the seat plate and transferring shear to the column.
    *   **Cleats (Optional):** Small vertical plates welded to the beam flange and column web to provide some lateral restraint and minor moment resistance.
*   **Typical Configuration:**
    *   The beam rests on the seat plate.
    *   The stiffener is typically located at the end of the beam or slightly set back.
    *   The seat plate and stiffener are welded to the column web.
    *   The beam is also typically welded to the column web with fillet welds for shear transfer.

#### 2.2 Load Transfer Mechanism

*   **Vertical Shear:** Primarily transferred through:
    *   The fillet welds connecting the beam web to the column web.
    *   The bearing of the beam's bottom flange on the seat plate.
    *   The shear resistance of the stiffener and its welds to the column web.
*   **Moment Resistance:** Limited. The primary function is shear transfer. The cleats, if present, contribute to some minor moment resistance and lateral restraint.
*   **Axial Force:** If present, can be transferred through direct bearing or welds.

#### 2.3 Structural Behavior and Failure Modes

*   **Behavior:** Predominantly a shear connection. Provides limited moment restraint.
*   **Failure Modes:**
    *   **Yielding of the Seat Plate:** Excessive vertical load can cause the seat plate to yield.
    *   **Web Crippling of the Column:** Local buckling of the column web at the point of concentrated load from the seat plate.
    *   **Shear Yielding of the Stiffener:** The stiffener may yield in shear.
    *   **Block Shear Failure:** Failure of the column web and flange material around the stiffener and seat plate welds.
    *   **Weld Failure:** Shear or tensile failure of the welds connecting the seat plate, stiffener, and cleats to the column, or the beam web to the column.
    *   **Punching Shear Failure of the Seat Plate:** The seat plate might fail by punching shear if the beam bearing area is not adequately distributed.

#### 2.4 Design Considerations

*   **Seat Plate Design:**
    *   **Thickness:** Determined by the applied shear load and bearing pressure under the beam flange.
    *   **Projection:** Sufficient projection to support the beam flange.
    *   **Weld Size:** Adequate fillet welds to connect the seat plate to the column web.
*   **Stiffener Design:**
    *   **Size and Thickness:** Determined by the shear force transmitted to the column and to prevent web crippling of the column.
    *   **Weld Size:** Adequate fillet welds to connect the stiffener to the seat plate and the column web.
*   **Column Web Strength:**
    *   **Bearing Strength:** Column web must resist the concentrated load from the seat plate without yielding or buckling.
    *   **Web Crippling Strength:** The column web needs to be checked for crippling under the load from the seat plate.
*   **Weld Strength:** All welds must be designed to resist the factored loads.
*   **Beam Properties:** Beam flange width and thickness are crucial for bearing.

#### 2.5 Example Application

*   Used in industrial buildings, warehouses, or situations where high moment resistance is not required.
*   Efficient for supporting large shear forces from heavy beams.

### 3. Web Angle Connection

#### 3.1 Description and Configuration

*   **Concept:** A connection where the beam web is connected to the column web using one or two angle cleats.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Angle Cleats:** L-shaped steel sections, typically bolted or welded to both the beam web and the column web.
    *   **Bolts/Welds:** Fasteners connecting the angles to the beam and column.
*   **Typical Configuration:**
    *   **Single Angle:** An angle bolted or welded to the beam web and column web.
    *   **Double Angle:** Two angles, one on each side of the beam web, bolted or welded to the beam web and column web. This is more common for heavier loads.
*   **Moment Resistance:** Can provide some degree of moment resistance, especially double angle connections, but is generally considered semi-rigid or flexible.

#### 3.2 Load Transfer Mechanism

*   **Vertical Shear:** Primarily transferred through:
    *   **Bolts in Shear:** The bolts connecting the angles to the beam and column are in shear.
    *   **Welds (if used):** Fillet welds connecting the angles to the beam and column are in shear.
    *   **Bearing of Angles:** Angles bear against the beam web and column web.
*   **Moment Resistance:** Provided by the eccentricity of the connection. The forces in the bolts or welds are distributed to resist the applied moment.
    *   **Tensile Forces:** Bolts/welds on the tension side of the connection experience tensile forces.
    *   **Shear Forces:** Bolts/welds on the shear side experience shear forces.
*   **Axial Force:** Can be transferred through direct bearing or via bolts/welds.

#### 3.3 Structural Behavior and Failure Modes

*   **Behavior:** Generally considered a flexible or semi-rigid connection.
*   **Failure Modes:**
    *   **Bolt Shear:** Failure of the bolts in shear.
    *   **Bolt Bearing:** Bearing failure of the bolt holes in the angle, beam web, or column web.
    *   **Angle Yielding/Rupture:** Yielding or tensile rupture of the angle itself.
    *   **Block Shear Failure:** Failure of the angle and connected plate material (beam web or column web).
    *   **Weld Failure:** Failure of welds connecting angles to the beam or column.
    *   **Net Section Fracture:** Fracture of the beam web or column web at the net section (reduced area due to bolt holes).
    *   **Stiffener Failure (if used):** If stiffeners are used on the column web to enhance load transfer, they can fail by yielding or buckling.

#### 3.4 Design Considerations

*   **Bolt Group Design:**
    *   Shear capacity of bolts.
    *   Bearing capacity of bolts and connected plates.
    *   Tensile capacity of bolts (for moment resistance).
    *   Arrangement of bolts to resist shear and moment.
*   **Angle Cleat Design:**
    *   Size and thickness of angles to accommodate bolt holes and transfer forces.
    *   Weld size and length if welded connections are used.
*   **Beam Web and Column Web Strength:**
    *   Net section capacity of the beam web and column web.
    *   Bearing capacity of the web material.
*   **Connection Eccentricity:** The distance from the neutral axis of the beam to the centroid of the bolt group in the connection significantly affects moment resistance.
*   **Moment Capacity:** Calculated based on the tensile and shear capacities of the bolt group and the applied moment.

#### 3.5 Example Application

*   Commonly used in multi-story buildings, commercial buildings, and frames where moderate moment restraint is acceptable.
*   Cost-effective and relatively easy to fabricate and erect.

### 4. End Plate Connection

#### 4.1 Description and Configuration

*   **Concept:** A connection where a steel plate (end plate) is welded to the end of the beam flange(s) and then bolted to the column.
*   **Components:**
    *   **End Plate:** A flat steel plate welded to the beam flange(s) at the beam end.
    *   **Bolts:** High-strength bolts connect the end plate to the column flange or web.
    *   **Column:** Typically a wide flange section, but can also be a hollow section.
*   **Types of End Plate Connections:**
    *   **Extended End Plate:** The end plate extends beyond the beam flange, with bolts arranged to resist moment. Can be designed to resist significant moments.
    *   **Flush End Plate:** The end plate is flush with the beam flange. Generally provides lower moment resistance than extended end plates.
    *   **End Plate Welded to Column Web:** Less common, but possible.
*   **Moment Resistance:** Can be designed to provide full or partial moment restraint, making them suitable for rigid frame construction.

#### 4.2 Load Transfer Mechanism

*   **Vertical Shear:**
    *   Transferred through the bolts connecting the end plate to the column, acting in shear.
    *   Can also be transferred through the beam web connection to the end plate and then to the column.
*   **Moment Resistance:**
    *   The bolts in the end plate act as a bolt group to resist the applied moment.
    *   **Tensile Force:** Bolts on the tension side of the connection are in tension.
    *   **Shear Force:** Bolts on the compression side of the connection are in shear.
    *   The bending of the end plate itself is also a critical factor.
*   **Axial Force:** Transferred through the bolts or bearing.

#### 4.3 Structural Behavior and Failure Modes

*   **Behavior:** Can be designed to be rigid (moment-resisting), semi-rigid, or flexible depending on the end plate dimensions and bolt arrangement.
*   **Failure Modes:**
    *   **Bolt Rupture:** Tensile rupture of bolts on the tension side.
    *   **Bolt Shear:** Shear failure of bolts.
    *   **Bolt Bearing:** Bearing failure of the bolt holes in the end plate or column flange/web.
    *   **End Plate Yielding/Rupture:**
        *   **Bending of the end plate:** The end plate can yield or fracture in bending between the bolt lines.
        *   **Tension field action:** The end plate can develop tension field action.
    *   **Column Flange/Web Failure:**
        *   **Column flange yielding:** Yielding of the column flange where the bolts are located.
        *   **Column web panel yielding:** Yielding of the column web in shear due to forces transferred from the end plate.
        *   **Column web panel buckling:** Buckling of the column web panel.
    *   **Weld Failure:** Failure of welds connecting the end plate to the beam flange.
    *   **Block Shear Failure:** Of the column web or flange.

#### 4.4 Design Considerations

*   **End Plate Design:**
    *   **Thickness:** Crucial for resisting bending and preventing yielding. Determined by analysis of the bending stresses in the end plate.
    *   **Width and Height:** To accommodate the bolt pattern and ensure adequate connection to the column.
    *   **Weld Details:** Welds connecting the end plate to the beam flange must be designed for the applied forces.
*   **Bolt Arrangement:**
    *   **Number of bolts:** To carry tensile and shear loads.
    *   **Spacing of bolts:** Affects the bending of the end plate and column web panel.
    *   **Bolt grade:** High-strength bolts are typically used.
*   **Column Strength:**
    *   **Column flange bending:** Column flange must resist bending due to bolt forces.
    *   **Column web panel shear yielding:** The column web must resist shear forces transferred from the end plate.
    *   **Column web panel buckling:** May require stiffeners.
*   **Moment Capacity:** Calculated based on the capacity of the bolt group, the bending capacity of the end plate, and the strength of the column web panel.
*   **Eurocode 3 (EN 1993-1-8) Classification:** End plate connections are often classified into various "components" for detailed design analysis.

#### 4.5 Example Application

*   Widely used in moment-resisting frames (MRFs) for seismic-resistant structures, high-rise buildings, and bridges.
*   Offers high moment capacity and rigidity, contributing to frame stability and reducing storey drift.

### 5. Comparison of Connection Types

| Feature            | Stiffened Beam Seat Connection | Web Angle Connection   | End Plate Connection      |
| :----------------- | :----------------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------ |
| **Primary Function** | Shear transfer                 | Shear transfer, partial moment | Shear & significant moment |
| **Moment Capacity** | Low                            | Moderate               | High                      |
| **Rigidity**       | Flexible                       | Semi-rigid/Flexible    | Rigid/Semi-rigid          |
| **Complexity**     | Moderate                       | Simple                 | Complex                   |
| **Cost**           | Moderate                       | Low                    | High                      |
| **Applications**   | Industrial buildings           | General building frames | MRFs, seismic design      |
| **Load Transfer**  | Bearing, shear welds           | Bolts/welds in shear/tension | Bolts in tension/shear, plate bending |

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Describe the primary load transfer mechanism for a stiffened beam seat connection. What is the main limitation of this connection type in terms of its moment-resisting capacity?

**Answer 1:**
The primary load transfer mechanism for a stiffened beam seat connection involves the beam's bottom flange bearing on the horizontal seat plate, with vertical shear transferred through fillet welds connecting the beam web to the column web and through the stiffener to the column web. The main limitation is its low moment-resisting capacity, as it's primarily designed for shear.

**Question 2:**
For a web angle connection, list at least three potential failure modes related to the bolts.

**Answer 2:**
Three potential failure modes related to the bolts in a web angle connection are:
1.  **Bolt Shear:** The bolts fail in shear due to the applied shear force.
2.  **Bolt Bearing:** The bolt holes in the angle, beam web, or column web fail in bearing under the pressure exerted by the bolts.
3.  **Bolt Rupture:** The bolts fail in tension due to the applied tensile force (particularly relevant when moment resistance is considered).

**Question 3:**
What is the key component that distinguishes an extended end plate connection from a flush end plate connection in terms of its ability to resist moment? Explain why.

**Answer 3:**
The key component is the **extension of the end plate beyond the beam flange** in an extended end plate connection. This extension allows for a wider spacing of bolts, creating a larger lever arm for the tensile and shear forces generated by the applied moment. This larger lever arm significantly increases the connection's moment capacity compared to a flush end plate where the bolts are closer to the beam flange.

**Question 4:**
Consider a beam-to-column connection using double angles. If the applied shear force is transmitted through bolts connecting the angles to the column web, what checks are typically performed on these bolts?

**Answer 4:**
The following checks are typically performed on the bolts connecting the angles to the column web:
1.  **Shear Capacity of Bolts:** The total shear resistance of the bolt group must exceed the applied shear force.
2.  **Bearing Capacity of Bolts and Connected Plates:** The bearing strength of the holes in the angles, beam web, and column web must exceed the forces acting on them.
3.  **Tensile Capacity of Bolts:** If the connection is designed for moment, the tensile capacity of the bolts must be checked.
4.  **Block Shear Capacity:** The combined shear and tension capacity of the column web and flange around the bolt group (block shear) must be checked.
5.  **Net Section Fracture:** The tensile capacity of the connected element (angle, beam web, or column web) at the net section must be checked.

**Question 5:**
For an end plate connection designed as a moment-resisting connection, what are the critical failure modes associated with the end plate itself?

**Answer 5:**
Critical failure modes associated with the end plate itself in a moment-resisting connection include:
1.  **Yielding/Rupture of the End Plate in Bending:** The end plate can yield or fracture in bending between the lines of bolts due to the tensile and shear forces.
2.  **Tensile Rupture of the End Plate at the Net Section:** If the end plate is bolted through its width, the net section can fail in tension.
3.  **Lamellar Tearing:** For connections with significant through-thickness forces, lamellar tearing of the end plate material can occur, especially in thicker plates.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Connection Type Selection:** The choice of connection depends on the required load transfer (shear vs. moment), rigidity, cost, and constructability.
*   **Load Path:** Understanding how forces are transferred through the connection is crucial for accurate design.
*   **Failure Modes:** Always consider all potential failure modes to ensure a safe and robust connection.
*   **Weld Details:** The strength and quality of welds are paramount for the performance of welded connections.
*   **Bolt Properties:** Bolt grade, diameter, spacing, and edge distances are critical design parameters.
*   **Material Properties:** The yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of the steel plates, angles, and bolts must be considered.
*   **Column Capacity:** The column web and flanges must have adequate strength to resist the forces transferred from the beam connections.
*   **Design Codes:** Always refer to the relevant design codes (e.g., Eurocodes, AISC) for specific design rules and equations.
*   **Stiffeners:** Used to enhance the capacity of a connection or to prevent local buckling of the column web or flange.
*   **Moment Resistance:** Connections designed for moment resistance often involve eccentricities and specific bolt arrangements to create lever arms.
