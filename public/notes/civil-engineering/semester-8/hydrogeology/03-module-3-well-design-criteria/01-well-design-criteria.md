# Hydrogeology: Module 3 - Well Design Criteria

## 1. Introduction to Well Design Criteria

**Objective:** To understand the fundamental principles guiding the design of water wells, ensuring efficient, sustainable, and safe water extraction.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Well Yield:** The rate at which water can be withdrawn from a well.
*   **Sustainable Yield:** The maximum rate of water extraction that can be maintained without depleting the aquifer or causing adverse environmental impacts.
*   **Aquifer Properties:** The characteristics of the underground geological formation that stores and transmits groundwater (e.g., transmissivity, storativity, hydraulic conductivity).
*   **Water Quality:** The suitability of groundwater for its intended use (e.g., potability, irrigation, industrial).
*   **Well Efficiency:** The ratio of the theoretical drawdown to the actual drawdown for a given pumping rate, indicating how well the well is performing.
*   **Aquifer Depletion:** The reduction of groundwater levels due to excessive pumping.
*   **Groundwater Contamination:** The introduction of harmful substances into the groundwater system.

**Importance of Well Design:**

*   Maximizing water extraction efficiency.
*   Ensuring long-term sustainability of the groundwater resource.
*   Preventing well damage and premature failure.
*   Maintaining water quality.
*   Minimizing operational costs.

## 2. Aquifer Characterization and Hydrogeological Investigations

**Objective:** To determine the hydrogeological properties of the aquifer to inform well design decisions.

**Key Concepts & Methods:**

*   **Geological Mapping:** Identifying the rock types, stratigraphy, and structural features (faults, fractures) that define the aquifer.
    *   *Example:* Identifying a sandstone aquifer versus a fractured limestone aquifer will dictate different well construction methods.
*   **Borehole Geophysical Logging:** Using downhole instruments to measure physical properties of the subsurface.
    *   **Spontaneous Potential (SP) log:** Measures natural electrical potential differences, useful for identifying permeable zones and lithology.
    *   **Resistivity log:** Measures the electrical resistance of the formation, indicating porosity and water content.
    *   **Gamma ray log:** Measures natural radioactivity, useful for identifying shale (low permeability) versus sand or limestone (higher permeability).
    *   **Natural Gamma Ray Spectrometry (NGS) log:** Provides more detailed elemental composition.
    *   **Neutron Porosity log:** Measures hydrogen content, indicative of pore fluids.
    *   **Density log:** Measures bulk density, used to calculate porosity.
    *   **Caliper log:** Measures borehole diameter, important for casing and gravel pack design.
*   **Pumping Tests (Aquifer Tests):** In situ tests to determine aquifer hydraulic properties.
    *   **Constant-Rate Pumping Test:** Pumping at a constant rate and monitoring water level drawdown.
        *   **Theis Equation/Cooper-Jacob Method:** Used to calculate transmissivity (T) and storativity (S).
            *   *T = Transmissivity (L²/T)* – The rate at which water can be transmitted through a unit width of aquifer under a unit hydraulic gradient.
            *   *S = Storativity (dimensionless)* – The volume of water released from storage per unit surface area of aquifer per unit decline in hydraulic head.
        *   *Example:* A high transmissivity indicates a high-yielding aquifer.
    *   **Step-Test Pumping Test:** Pumping at successively increasing rates to determine well losses and specific capacity.
    *   **Recovery Test:** Monitoring water level recovery after pumping stops.
*   **Slug Tests:** Rapid introduction or removal of a slug of water (or solid object) from the well to estimate hydraulic conductivity (K) and specific storage (Ss) in the immediate vicinity of the well.
    *   *K = Hydraulic Conductivity (L/T)* – The rate at which water can move through a unit cross-sectional area of aquifer under a unit hydraulic gradient.
*   **Water Quality Analysis:** Assessing the chemical and physical characteristics of the groundwater.
    *   *Parameters:* pH, dissolved solids, major ions (Ca, Mg, Na, K, Cl, SO₄, HCO₃), trace elements, microbial content.
    *   *Importance:* Identifying potential treatment needs or limitations on use.

**Practice Question:**
What is the primary difference between transmissivity and hydraulic conductivity, and how are they related?

**Answer:** Transmissivity (T) represents the ability of the entire aquifer layer to transmit water, while hydraulic conductivity (K) represents the ability of the porous material itself to transmit water. The relationship is T = K * b, where 'b' is the aquifer thickness.

## 3. Well Type Selection

**Objective:** To choose the most appropriate well type based on aquifer characteristics, intended use, and economic factors.

**Key Well Types:**

*   **Shallow Wells (Dug Wells, Hand-Dug Wells):**
    *   **Construction:** Excavated manually or with simple machinery. Often lined with stone, brick, or concrete.
    *   **Aquifer Type:** Typically used in shallow, unconfined aquifers, often in unconsolidated sediments.
    *   **Pros:** Low construction cost, simple technology.
    *   **Cons:** Limited depth, lower yield, susceptible to surface contamination, prone to collapse.
    *   *Example:* Traditional wells in rural areas for domestic water supply.
*   **Bored Wells (Drilled Wells):**
    *   **Construction:** Drilled using rotary, cable-tool, or auger drilling methods. Can be cased with PVC, steel, or other materials.
    *   **Aquifer Type:** Can access both unconfined and confined aquifers, in both unconsolidated and consolidated formations.
    *   **Pros:** Can reach greater depths, higher yields, less susceptible to surface contamination compared to dug wells.
    *   **Cons:** Higher construction cost than dug wells, requires specialized equipment.
    *   *Example:* Most common type of modern water well for domestic, agricultural, and municipal supply.
*   **Screened Wells:**
    *   **Construction:** The lower portion of the casing is perforated with slots, screens, or manufactured wire-wrapped screens to allow water entry while preventing sediment.
    *   **Aquifer Type:** Primarily used in unconsolidated aquifers (sand and gravel).
    *   **Pros:** Efficient water intake, prevents fine sediment from entering the well, prolongs well life.
    *   **Cons:** Screens can clog or corrode, higher cost than simple perforated casing.
    *   *Example:* Wells screened in clean sand formations.
*   **Gravel-Packed Wells (Gravel-Packed Gravel Screens):**
    *   **Construction:** A well screen is placed in the borehole, and the annular space between the screen and the borehole wall is filled with a specially graded gravel (gravel pack).
    *   **Aquifer Type:** Used in fine-grained unconsolidated aquifers where the natural formation is too fine to be effectively screened directly.
    *   **Pros:** Excellent sediment control, provides good hydraulic connection, can be used in formations prone to fines migration.
    *   **Cons:** Higher construction cost and complexity.
    *   *Example:* Wells in fine silty sands where natural screens would clog quickly.
*   **$.C. Wells (Limited Entry Wells):**
    *   **Construction:** Perforations or screens are placed only in the most productive zones of the aquifer, avoiding less permeable or problematic zones.
    *   **Aquifer Type:** Multi-layered aquifers or aquifers with significant variations in permeability.
    *   **Pros:** Maximizes yield from the best zones, can help manage water quality by avoiding contaminated zones, can reduce drawdown.
    *   **Cons:** Requires detailed aquifer characterization to identify optimal zones.
    *   *Example:* A well designed to tap into specific high-permeability fractures in a bedrock aquifer.
*   **Collector Wells (Ranney Wells):**
    *   **Construction:** Large-diameter wells with multiple horizontal perforated laterals extending into the aquifer.
    *   **Aquifer Type:** High-yield, permeable aquifers, often near rivers or lakes (induced recharge).
    *   **Pros:** Very high yields, efficient water collection.
    *   **Cons:** High construction cost, requires specific geological conditions, specialized construction.
    *   *Example:* Municipal water supply wells in broad alluvial valleys.

**Practice Question:**
When would you choose a gravel-packed well over a simple screened well?

**Answer:** You would choose a gravel-packed well when the aquifer formation is too fine-grained (e.g., silty sand or fine sand) to effectively support a direct-set screen without significant clogging or fines migration. The gravel pack acts as a coarser filter medium.

## 4. Well Construction Components and Design Considerations

**Objective:** To understand the function and design of each component of a water well.

**Key Components:**

*   **Borehole:**
    *   **Diameter:** Determined by pumping equipment size, well screen size, and annular space requirements for gravel packing or grout.
    *   **Depth:** Based on aquifer depth, desired yield, and drawdown considerations.
    *   **Alignment:** Verticality is crucial for efficient pump operation and preventing screen damage.
*   **Well Casing:**
    *   **Function:** Prevents borehole collapse, supports the well screen, seals off shallow or contaminated zones, and provides a conduit for pumping.
    *   **Materials:**
        *   **Steel:** Strong, durable, but susceptible to corrosion.
        *   **PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride):** Lightweight, corrosion-resistant, cost-effective, but less mechanically strong than steel.
        *   **HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene):** Similar to PVC but more flexible and durable.
        *   **Stainless Steel:** High corrosion resistance, but expensive.
    *   **Wall Thickness:** Selected based on borehole pressure, external loads (e.g., from gravel pack), and drilling method.
    *   **Above-Ground Projection:** Extends above the land surface to prevent surface water contamination and provide access for maintenance.
*   **Well Screen (Gravel Pack Screen):**
    *   **Function:** The primary intake zone, allowing water entry while retaining the aquifer material or gravel pack.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Slotted Casing:** Cut slots in the casing. Less precise slot size control, prone to clogging.
        *   **Perforated Casing:** Holes drilled in the casing.
        *   **Wire-Wrapped Screens:** Stainless steel or other alloys wrapped around support rods. Offers precise slot sizing and high open area.
        *   **Bridge Slotted Screens:** Interlocking metal bridges create slots.
        *   **Louvered Screens:** Shaped slots that direct water flow.
    *   **Slot Size:** Crucial for efficient filtration. Determined by the grain size distribution of the aquifer material or gravel pack (using the **Fin Filter Recommendation** or **Open Area Ratio** method).
        *   *Fin Filter Recommendation:* Select a slot size that retains at least 50% (by weight) of the aquifer material.
        *   *Open Area Ratio:* The percentage of the screen surface area that is open to water flow. Higher open area generally means higher inflow efficiency.
    *   **Screen Length:** Placed to encompass the productive zones of the aquifer.
    *   **Screen Material:** Must be corrosion-resistant (e.g., stainless steel, PVC, fiberglass).
*   **Gravel Pack:**
    *   **Function:** Acts as a filter medium to prevent fine sediment from reaching the well screen, improves hydraulic efficiency, and supports the borehole wall.
    *   **Gravel Selection:**
        *   **Gradation:** Uniformly graded, clean gravel with a specific grain size distribution (often specified as a range).
        *   **Size:** Typically 3-5 times the effective size of the formation material or the screen slot size.
        *   **Material:** Rounded, hard, inert gravel.
    *   **Placement:** Placed in the annular space between the screen and the borehole wall using a tremie pipe.
*   **Annular Seal (Grout/Clay Seal):**
    *   **Function:** Seals off the annular space above the screen and/or gravel pack to prevent the infiltration of shallow groundwater, surface water, or contaminants into the aquifer and well.
    *   **Materials:**
        *   **Cement Grout:** Most common, forms a rigid, impermeable barrier. Mix proportions are critical.
        *   **Bentonite Clay:** Can be used as a slurry or in pellet form to create a flexible seal.
    *   **Placement:** Placed from the top of the gravel pack or screen up to the land surface using a tremie pipe.
    *   **Thickness:** Sufficient to provide an effective seal, typically at least 0.5-1 meter.
*   **Wellhead Completion:**
    *   **Surface Seal:** A reinforced concrete pad around the wellhead to prevent surface water intrusion.
    *   **Well Cap:** Securely sealed to the casing to prevent entry of contaminants, debris, and pests. Should be vented to prevent pressure build-up.
    *   **Road Box/Protective Casing:** Protects the wellhead from damage.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Compatibility:** All materials used must be compatible with the groundwater chemistry to prevent corrosion or degradation.
*   **Screen/Gravel Pack Sizing:** This is critical for well performance and longevity.
*   **Annular Seal:** Essential for protecting groundwater quality.
*   **Borehole Stability:** The drilling and construction process must maintain borehole integrity.

**Practice Question:**
Describe the purpose of the annular seal and explain why it's important to place it using a tremie pipe.

**Answer:** The annular seal (grout or clay) prevents the movement of undesirable water (surface water, shallow contaminated groundwater) down the annulus into the aquifer. Placing it with a tremie pipe ensures it's placed from the bottom up, avoiding bridging and creating a continuous, impermeable barrier.

## 5. Well Development and Rehabilitation

**Objective:** To optimize well performance and restore efficiency after construction or over time.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Well Development:** A process carried out immediately after construction to remove drilling fluids, fines, and clay from the pore spaces around the well screen/gravel pack.
    *   **Purpose:** To improve the hydraulic connection between the aquifer and the well, thereby increasing well yield and efficiency.
    *   **Methods:**
        *   **Surging:** Rapidly changing the direction of water flow into and out of the well to dislodge fine particles.
        *   **Backwashing:** Forcing water backward through the screen.
        *   **High-Velocity Jetting:** Using high-pressure water jets.
        *   **Chemical Treatment:** Using dispersants or acids to remove drilling mud or scale.
        *   **Pumping:** Pumping at varying rates.
*   **Well Rehabilitation:** A process to restore the efficiency and performance of a well that has deteriorated over time.
    *   **Causes of Decline:**
        *   **Clogging:** Accumulation of fine sediment, biological growth (biofouling), or mineral precipitates (scaling) in the screen or aquifer.
        *   **Corrosion:** Degradation of well materials.
        *   **Incrustation:** Deposition of mineral scales (e.g., calcium carbonate, iron oxides).
        *   **Biological Growth:** Slimes and bacterial colonies.
    *   **Methods:**
        *   **Mechanical Cleaning:** Brushing, scraping, jetting.
        *   **Chemical Treatment:** Acidizing (to dissolve carbonate scales), chelating agents (to remove metal oxides), dispersants, and biocides.
        *   **Disinfecting:** Chlorination to kill bacteria.
        *   **Surging/Backwashing:** Can also be effective for rehabilitation.

**Practice Question:**
What is the difference between well development and well rehabilitation?

**Answer:** Well development is performed immediately after construction to optimize initial performance. Well rehabilitation is performed on existing wells to restore or improve performance that has declined over time due to clogging, scaling, or other issues.

## 6. Well Yield, Specific Capacity, and Pumping Tests

**Objective:** To quantify well performance and assess aquifer productivity.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Well Yield (Q):** The rate of discharge from the well, typically measured in liters per minute (L/min) or gallons per minute (gpm).
*   **Drawdown (s):** The difference between the static water level (before pumping) and the pumping water level.
*   **Static Water Level (h₀):** The water level in the well when no pumping is occurring.
*   **Pumping Water Level (h):** The water level in the well while pumping at a specific rate.
*   **Total Dynamic Head (TDH):** The total head that the pump must overcome to discharge water at the surface, including static lift, drawdown, friction losses in the pipe, and velocity head.
    *   *TDH = (h₀ - h) + (h₀ - static discharge level) + friction losses*
*   **Specific Capacity (SC):** The yield per unit of drawdown.
    *   *SC = Q / s*
    *   **Units:** L/min/m or gpm/ft.
    *   **Significance:** A measure of well efficiency. Higher specific capacity generally indicates a more efficient well. It can be used to estimate yield at different drawdown levels or to compare the performance of different wells.
*   **Well Efficiency (E):** The ratio of the theoretical drawdown (based on aquifer properties) to the actual drawdown, expressed as a percentage.
    *   *E = (Theoretical Drawdown / Actual Drawdown) * 100%*
    *   *Theoretical Drawdown = Q / (T * a)* where 'a' is a geometric factor.
    *   Alternatively, comparing actual drawdown to theoretical drawdown from pumping tests.
*   **Well Losses (lw):** The additional drawdown caused by inefficient water flow within the wellbore itself (turbulent flow, friction).
    *   *Actual Drawdown (s_actual) = Theoretical Drawdown (s_theoretical) + Well Losses (l_w)*
    *   Well losses are typically proportional to the square of the pumping rate ($l_w = C * Q^2$, where C is the well loss coefficient).
    *   This relationship is used in the **Jacob Method** for analyzing pumping test data, where the drawdown is plotted against the logarithm of time. The slope of the line relates to aquifer properties, while the intercept relates to well losses.
*   **Theis Recovery Method:** Used to determine aquifer properties from recovery data after pumping stops.

**Practice Question:**
A well yields 100 L/min with a drawdown of 5 meters. Calculate its specific capacity. If the static water level was 15 meters below ground, what is the pumping water level?

**Answer:**
*   Specific Capacity (SC) = Q / s = 100 L/min / 5 m = **20 L/min/m**.
*   Pumping Water Level = Static Water Level - Drawdown = 15 m - 5 m = **10 meters below ground**.

## 7. Well Performance Monitoring and Maintenance

**Objective:** To ensure the continued efficient and sustainable operation of a well.

**Key Aspects:**

*   **Regular Monitoring:**
    *   **Water Level:** Tracking static and pumping water levels to detect declines.
    *   **Pumping Rate:** Ensuring consistent yield.
    *   **Water Quality:** Periodic sampling and analysis for changes in chemical and physical parameters.
    *   **Pump Performance:** Monitoring pressure, amperage, and flow rate.
*   **Preventative Maintenance:**
    *   **Cleaning:** Scheduled cleaning to remove accumulated sediment or biological growth.
    *   **Disinfection:** Periodic disinfection.
    *   **Inspection:** Visual inspection of the wellhead and casing for damage.
    *   **Pump Servicing:** Regular maintenance of pumping equipment.
*   **Troubleshooting:**
    *   Identifying causes of reduced yield, poor water quality, or pump failure.
    *   Implementing appropriate rehabilitation or repair strategies.

**Importance:**

*   Detecting problems early, preventing costly repairs.
*   Maintaining water quality and supply reliability.
*   Extending the lifespan of the well and associated infrastructure.

## Summary of Key Well Design Criteria:

*   **Aquifer Characteristics:** Understand the geology, hydraulics, and water quality of the aquifer.
*   **Water Demand:** Determine the required yield and quality of water.
*   **Well Type Selection:** Choose the most suitable construction method.
*   **Borehole Design:** Proper diameter, depth, and verticality.
*   **Casing and Screen Design:** Appropriate materials, slot sizes, and placement for effective filtration and durability.
*   **Gravel Pack:** If needed, select proper gradation and size.
*   **Annular Seal:** Essential for groundwater protection.
*   **Well Development:** Crucial for initial performance optimization.
*   **Monitoring and Maintenance:** Essential for long-term sustainability.
