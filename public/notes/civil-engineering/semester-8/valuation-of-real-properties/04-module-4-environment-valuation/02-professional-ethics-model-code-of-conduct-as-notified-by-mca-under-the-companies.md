# VALUATION OF REAL PROPERTIES - Module 4: Environment & Valuation

## Topic: Professional Ethics - Model Code of Conduct and Ethical Considerations under Terms of Engagement

This module focuses on the crucial role of professional ethics in real property valuation, specifically examining the Model Code of Conduct notified by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, and the ethical considerations that arise within the terms of engagement for valuation services.

### Learning Outcomes Covered:

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   Understand the importance of professional ethics in the valuation profession.
*   Explain the scope and purpose of the Model Code of Conduct for Registered Valuers.
*   Identify the key principles and duties outlined in the Model Code of Conduct.
*   Analyze the ethical considerations involved in drafting and adhering to terms of engagement.
*   Recognize and avoid conflicts of interest in valuation assignments.
*   Understand the importance of independence, objectivity, and due diligence.
*   Discuss the implications of ethical breaches and professional misconduct.

### 1. Introduction to Professional Ethics in Valuation

Professional ethics form the bedrock of trust and credibility in any profession. For real property valuers, adherence to a strong ethical framework is paramount for several reasons:

*   **Public Interest:** Valuations often impact significant financial decisions for individuals, businesses, and financial institutions. Ethical conduct ensures that these decisions are based on sound, unbiased, and reliable information.
*   **Credibility and Reputation:** The reputation of individual valuers and the profession as a whole relies on consistent ethical behavior.
*   **Legal and Regulatory Compliance:** Many regulations, including the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, mandate ethical conduct.
*   **Client Confidence:** Clients engage valuers with the expectation of honesty, integrity, and competence.

### 2. The Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 and the Model Code of Conduct

The MCA, through the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, has established a framework for registered valuers, including a **Model Code of Conduct**. This code sets out the standards of behavior and professional practice expected of individuals registered under these rules.

#### 2.1 Scope and Purpose of the Model Code of Conduct

*   **Purpose:** To ensure that registered valuers conduct themselves with integrity, impartiality, and competence, thereby upholding the standards of the valuation profession and protecting the interests of stakeholders.
*   **Scope:** Applies to all individuals registered as valuers under the Companies Act, 2013, and the rules made thereunder. It governs their professional conduct in all valuation assignments, regardless of the context (e.g., company law, insolvency, or other statutory requirements).

#### 2.2 Key Principles and Duties of a Registered Valuer (as per Model Code of Conduct)

The Model Code of Conduct emphasizes several core principles and duties:

*   **Integrity and Honesty:**
    *   **Definition:** Acting with truthfulness, sincerity, and rectitude in all dealings.
    *   **Duty:** A valuer must be honest and forthright in all communications and representations. They should not misrepresent facts or mislead others.
*   **Objectivity and Impartiality:**
    *   **Definition:** Maintaining a neutral and unbiased stance, free from personal interests, pre-existing relationships, or external pressures that could influence the valuation.
    *   **Duty:** Valuers must conduct their work without prejudice and base their opinions solely on evidence and professional judgment. They must avoid any situation that compromises their objectivity.
*   **Professional Competence and Due Care:**
    *   **Definition:** Possessing the necessary knowledge, skills, and experience to perform valuation services and exercising reasonable care and diligence in carrying out their professional duties.
    *   **Duty:** Valuers must only accept assignments for which they are qualified. They must maintain and enhance their professional knowledge and skills through continuing professional development (CPD).
*   **Confidentiality:**
    *   **Definition:** Keeping information obtained during a professional engagement secret and not disclosing it to unauthorized third parties.
    *   **Duty:** Valuers have a strict obligation to maintain the confidentiality of client information, unless legally required to disclose it or with the client's express consent.
*   **Professional Conduct:**
    *   **Definition:** Adhering to all applicable laws, regulations, and professional standards.
    *   **Duty:** Valuers must conduct themselves in a manner that reflects creditably on the profession and avoid actions that could bring disrepute to the valuation profession. This includes avoiding misleading advertising or touting for business.
*   **Disclosure of Conflict of Interest:**
    *   **Definition:** Identifying and disclosing any actual or potential conflicts of interest that could impair their independence or objectivity.
    *   **Duty:** Valuers must proactively identify and disclose any situation that might present a conflict of interest to their clients and relevant authorities.
*   **Independence:**
    *   **Definition:** Being free from any influence, bias, or relationship that could compromise the valuer's ability to act with objectivity and integrity.
    *   **Duty:** Valuers must maintain both independence of mind (the state of mind that permits the expression of a conclusion without being affected by influences that compromise professional judgment) and independence in appearance (the avoidance of facts and circumstances that are significant enough to lead a reasonable and informed third party, having weighed all the specific information, to conclude that a threat to the valuer's compliance with the principles of integrity, objectivity and professional competence and due care, or the existence of a relationship that could impair the valuer's independence, has not been eliminated or reduced to an acceptable level).
*   **Due Diligence in Valuation:**
    *   **Definition:** The systematic process of gathering, analyzing, and verifying information relevant to the valuation.
    *   **Duty:** Valuers must exercise due diligence in collecting all necessary information, performing thorough analysis, and ensuring the accuracy and reliability of their valuation.

### 3. Ethical Considerations Under Terms of Engagement

The **Terms of Engagement (ToE)**, often documented in a Letter of Engagement or Engagement Agreement, is a crucial document that defines the scope, responsibilities, and expectations of both the valuer and the client. Ethical considerations are woven into every aspect of the ToE.

#### 3.1 Key Elements of Terms of Engagement and Ethical Implications

*   **Clear Definition of Scope of Work:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Ensures the valuer and client have a mutual understanding of what the valuation entails, preventing misunderstandings and potential disputes. It also ensures the valuer is undertaking a task they are competent to perform.
    *   **Example:** Clearly stating the property being valued, the purpose of the valuation (e.g., sale, mortgage, litigation), the valuation date, and the specific assets or liabilities to be included.
*   **Valuation Methodology and Standards:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Commits the valuer to using appropriate and accepted valuation methodologies and standards (e.g., RICS Valuation Standards, IVSC Standards, or specific legal requirements). This upholds professional competence and objectivity.
    *   **Example:** Specifying that the valuation will be conducted in accordance with the International Valuation Standards (IVS) or the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017.
*   **Fee Structure and Payment Terms:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Transparency in fees is essential to avoid any perception of undue influence or quid pro quo. Contingency fees, which are based on the outcome of the valuation, are generally considered unethical as they compromise independence.
    *   **Example:** Clearly stating the basis of the fee (e.g., fixed fee, hourly rate) and payment schedule. Explicitly stating that the fee is not contingent on the valuation outcome.
*   **Timelines and Deliverables:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Committing to realistic timelines and delivering the valuation report within the agreed period demonstrates professional conduct and respect for the client's needs.
    *   **Example:** Agreeing on a specific date for the submission of the draft valuation report and the final report.
*   **Confidentiality Clause:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Reinforces the valuer's duty of confidentiality, protecting sensitive client information.
    *   **Example:** A clause stating that all information provided by the client and obtained during the engagement will be kept strictly confidential.
*   **Client Responsibilities:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Outlines what the client is expected to provide, such as access to the property, relevant documentation, and timely responses. This ensures the valuer can perform their due diligence effectively.
    *   **Example:** The client agreeing to provide all requested documents, such as title deeds, lease agreements, and previous valuation reports.
*   **Limitations and Assumptions:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Transparently stating any limitations in the scope of work or assumptions made during the valuation. This ensures the client understands the basis and potential constraints of the valuation.
    *   **Example:** An assumption that the property will be sold in the open market by a willing seller to a willing buyer, or a limitation due to restricted access to certain parts of the property.
*   **Indemnity and Liability:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** While professional indemnity insurance is standard, the ToE should clarify the extent of the valuer's liability, ensuring it is reasonable and aligned with professional standards.
    *   **Example:** A clause stating the valuer's liability is limited to the amount of their professional indemnity insurance cover for negligence.
*   **Termination Clause:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** Outlines the conditions under which either party can terminate the engagement, ensuring a professional and orderly process.
    *   **Example:** Allowing the client to terminate the engagement with notice, with the valuer entitled to fees for work completed.

#### 3.2 Avoiding Conflicts of Interest in Terms of Engagement

Conflicts of interest are a significant ethical challenge. The ToE should actively address this:

*   **Disclosure of Relationships:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** The valuer must disclose any existing or past relationships with the client, the subject property, or any related parties that could influence their objectivity.
    *   **Example:** A valuer is asked to value a property owned by their cousin. They must disclose this familial relationship to the client.
*   **Prohibition of Contingency Fees:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** As mentioned earlier, fees tied to the valuation outcome create a direct conflict. The ToE should explicitly state that such arrangements are not accepted.
    *   **Example:** Refusing an engagement where the fee is a percentage of the value determined by the valuation.
*   **Independence of Valuation:**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** The ToE should confirm that the valuation will be conducted independently and objectively, free from undue influence.
    *   **Example:** A clause stating that the valuer's opinion will be based on their professional judgment and the available evidence, irrespective of any desired outcome by the client.
*   **Non-Compete Clauses (with caveats):**
    *   **Ethical Implication:** While legitimate, overly restrictive non-compete clauses can limit a valuer's ability to practice and may raise ethical concerns. They should be reasonable and specific.
    *   **Example:** A clause preventing a valuer from undertaking any valuation within a specific geographical area for a prolonged period might be considered unreasonable.

### 4. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
A registered valuer is asked to value a commercial property for a potential sale. The client, who is also the owner of the property, mentions that they are hoping for a valuation that will facilitate a quick sale at a high price. The valuer has a good personal relationship with the client. What ethical considerations should the valuer be mindful of, and how should they be addressed in the Terms of Engagement?

**Answer:**
The valuer must prioritize **integrity**, **objectivity**, and **impartiality**.
*   **Conflict of Interest:** The personal relationship with the client and the client's stated desire for a high valuation create a potential conflict of interest.
*   **Addressing in ToE:**
    *   **Disclosure:** The valuer must disclose their personal relationship with the client to ensure transparency.
    *   **Scope of Work:** The ToE should clearly define that the valuation will be based on market evidence and established valuation principles, not on the client's desired outcome.
    *   **Fee Structure:** The fee should be fixed or based on time spent, not contingent on the valuation amount.
    *   **Independence:** The ToE should reiterate the valuer's commitment to independent and objective valuation.

**Question 2:**
You are a registered valuer appointed to value an asset for a company undergoing insolvency proceedings. The insolvency professional who appointed you also requests you to provide a separate opinion on the marketing strategy for the asset. What ethical considerations arise, and how should you proceed?

**Answer:**
This presents a potential **conflict of interest** and a question of **professional competence**.
*   **Conflict of Interest:** Providing advice on marketing strategy may be perceived as influencing the valuation outcome, especially if the marketing strategy is designed to achieve a higher price. It also blurs the lines between valuation expertise and marketing advisory services.
*   **Professional Competence:** While some valuers may have marketing knowledge, it is not typically their core competency unless specifically engaged for such a service. The primary duty is to provide an unbiased valuation.
*   **Proceeding:**
    *   **Decline the additional service:** The most ethical approach is to politely decline the request to advise on the marketing strategy, explaining that it falls outside the scope of the valuation assignment and could create a conflict of interest or compromise objectivity.
    *   **Suggest alternative:** The valuer could suggest that the insolvency professional engage a separate marketing specialist.
    *   **Clear ToE:** If, for some reason, the valuer were to undertake both, separate Terms of Engagement would be absolutely critical, clearly delineating the scope of each service and ensuring the valuation remains independent.

**Question 3:**
Which of the following is generally considered an unethical fee arrangement for a registered valuer?
a) A fixed fee for the valuation report.
b) An hourly rate based on the time spent on the assignment.
c) A fee that is a percentage of the determined value of the property.
d) A fee based on the complexity of the valuation.

**Answer:**
c) A fee that is a percentage of the determined value of the property.

**Explanation:** Contingency fees (a percentage of the determined value) compromise a valuer's independence and objectivity, as they create an incentive to inflate the valuation to increase their fee.

### 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Integrity and Objectivity are Non-Negotiable:** These are the cornerstones of professional ethics for valuers.
*   **Terms of Engagement are Crucial:** A well-drafted ToE protects both the valuer and the client and clarifies ethical obligations.
*   **Disclose All Conflicts:** Never hide or downplay potential conflicts of interest. Transparency is key.
*   **Competence is Paramount:** Only accept assignments you are qualified to perform and stay updated through CPD.
*   **Confidentiality is a Duty:** Treat all client information with the utmost discretion.
*   **Adherence to Rules:** The Model Code of Conduct under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, is a binding framework.
*   **Reputation Matters:** Upholding ethical standards builds trust and enhances the reputation of the individual valuer and the profession.

This module provides a foundational understanding of professional ethics in real property valuation. Continuous learning and a commitment to ethical principles are essential for maintaining the integrity and credibility of the valuation profession.
