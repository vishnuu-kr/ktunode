# Valuation of Real Properties - Module 3: Building FSI – Plot Coverage – Types of Structure

## Topic: Valuation of Apartment - FSI – Super Built-up Area, Undivided Share of Land

This module delves into the crucial concepts of Floor Space Index (FSI), Plot Coverage, and Types of Structures, particularly as they apply to the valuation of apartments. Understanding these elements is vital for accurately assessing the value of individual apartment units within a larger building complex.

---

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this topic, you will be able to:

*   **Understand the concept of Floor Space Index (FSI) and its significance in property development and valuation.**
*   **Differentiate between various types of built-up areas, specifically Super Built-up Area, and their impact on valuation.**
*   **Explain the concept of Undivided Share of Land (UDS) and its role in apartment ownership and valuation.**
*   **Apply FSI, Super Built-up Area, and UDS principles to value apartment units.**
*   **Recognize how plot coverage and types of structures influence the overall valuation of apartments.**

### Key Concepts and Definitions

#### 1. Floor Space Index (FSI) / Floor Area Ratio (FAR)

*   **Definition:** FSI is the ratio of the **total permissible built-up area** on a plot of land to the **total area of the plot**. It is a regulatory tool used by urban planning authorities to control the density and intensity of development.
    
    $$ \text{FSI} = \frac{\text{Total Permissible Built-up Area}}{\text{Plot Area}} $$
    
*   **Significance in Valuation:**
    *   **Development Potential:** FSI dictates the maximum extent to which a plot can be developed. A higher FSI generally implies a greater development potential and, therefore, a higher land value.
    *   **Cost of Construction:** It directly influences the quantum of construction possible, impacting the cost of developing apartments.
    *   **Supply of Units:** A higher FSI allows for more units to be built, potentially increasing the supply of apartments in an area.
*   **Factors Influencing FSI:**
    *   **Zoning Regulations:** Different zones (residential, commercial, industrial) have different FSI norms.
    *   **Road Width:** The width of the adjoining road often influences the permissible FSI.
    *   **Amenities:** Presence of public amenities or the provision of specific amenities by the developer can sometimes lead to enhanced FSI.
    *   **Carpet Area vs. Built-up Area:** It's crucial to understand what constitutes "built-up area" as defined by local regulations, as this directly affects the FSI calculation.

#### 2. Plot Coverage

*   **Definition:** Plot coverage refers to the **maximum percentage of the plot area that can be occupied by the building at the ground level**. This includes the main building structure, but often excludes projections like balconies (depending on regulations).
    
    $$ \text{Plot Coverage (\%)} = \frac{\text{Ground Floor Footprint Area}}{\text{Plot Area}} \times 100 $$
*   **Significance in Valuation:**
    *   **Open Spaces:** Plot coverage determines the amount of open space left on the plot, which can be used for amenities like gardens, parking, or recreation. This impacts the desirability and, consequently, the value of apartments.
    *   **Light and Ventilation:** Higher plot coverage can lead to reduced light and ventilation for lower floors.
    *   **Aesthetics and Livability:** Adequate open spaces contribute to the aesthetic appeal and livability of a residential complex.
*   **Example:** If a plot is 1000 sq. meters and the permissible plot coverage is 40%, the building can occupy a maximum of 400 sq. meters at the ground level.

#### 3. Types of Structures

While not explicitly defined for apartment valuation in detail within this topic, understanding the general types of structures provides context. For apartments, the most common type is a **multi-storey residential building**.

*   **Common Types Relevant to Apartments:**
    *   **Apartment Buildings/Condominiums:** Buildings containing multiple individual housing units.
    *   **High-Rise Buildings:** Buildings exceeding a certain height (often 12 stories or 75 feet).
    *   **Mid-Rise Buildings:** Buildings of moderate height.
    *   **Low-Rise Buildings:** Buildings with fewer stories.
*   **Significance in Valuation:**
    *   **Construction Costs:** The type and height of the structure directly influence construction costs.
    *   **Amenities and Facilities:** Higher buildings often incorporate more amenities (e.g., lifts, dedicated parking, clubhouses) which add to the property value.
    *   **Market Demand:** The type of structure and its amenities can cater to different market segments.
    *   **Regulatory Compliance:** Different structures have different building codes and safety regulations that affect their construction and marketability.

#### 4. Built-up Area Measurement for Apartments

This is where the nuances of apartment valuation become critical. Different definitions of built-up area exist, and their inclusion in valuation is crucial.

*   **Carpet Area:**
    *   **Definition:** The actual usable floor area within an apartment, excluding the area of external walls, internal partition walls, balconies, terraces, and exclusive open spaces. This is the area you can walk on.
    *   **Significance:** It represents the true living space and is the most customer-centric measure.
    *   **Valuation Impact:** Direct correlation with usability and comfort.

*   **Built-up Area:**
    *   **Definition:** Includes the carpet area plus the area of internal walls and the area of balconies and terraces attached to the unit. Some definitions may also include a portion of common areas.
    *   **Significance:** Provides a more comprehensive measure of the enclosed space.
    *   **Valuation Impact:** Higher than carpet area, contributing to the overall price.

*   **Super Built-up Area (SBA) / Saleable Area:**
    *   **Definition:** This is the most common and often controversial measure used in apartment sales. It is calculated by adding the built-up area of the apartment to a proportionate share of the common areas (lobbies, lifts, staircases, clubhouses, gyms, swimming pools, corridors, etc.) and other amenities.
        
        $$ \text{Super Built-up Area} = \text{Built-up Area of Apartment} + (\text{Built-up Area of Apartment} \times \text{Common Area Ratio}) $$
        
        The "Common Area Ratio" is typically calculated as:
        
        $$ \text{Common Area Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Common Area of Building}}{\text{Total Built-up Area of all Apartments}} $$
    *   **Significance in Valuation:**
        *   **Developer's Pricing Strategy:** Developers use SBA to price apartments, as it includes the cost and utility of common amenities.
        *   **Market Practice:** It's the area on which most property transactions are based.
        *   **Consumer Perception:** Buyers often associate SBA with the total value they are getting, including shared facilities.
    *   **Valuation Impact:** SBA is always larger than the Built-up Area, leading to a higher sale price per unit of SBA compared to per unit of Built-up Area. This differential needs to be accounted for by the valuer.

#### 5. Undivided Share of Land (UDS)

*   **Definition:** When a plot of land is developed into an apartment complex, the ownership of the land is divided among the apartment owners. The UDS represents the proportion of the **total land area** that an apartment owner is entitled to, **in proportion to the area of their apartment**. It's an undivided right over the common areas and the land itself.
    
    $$ \text{UDS} = \frac{\text{Area of Individual Apartment (usually Carpet Area)}}{\text{Total Saleable Area of all Apartments}} \times \text{Total Plot Area} $$
    
    *Note: The basis for UDS calculation can vary slightly based on legal agreements and local practices (e.g., using built-up area or SBA instead of carpet area). It's crucial to check the sale deed/agreement.*
*   **Significance in Valuation:**
    *   **Land Value Component:** UDS is the direct link between the apartment and the underlying land value. The value of the land is a significant component of the overall property value.
    *   **Capital Appreciation:** As land values appreciate, the UDS also contributes to the capital appreciation of the apartment.
    *   **Resale Value:** A larger UDS can be a positive factor in resale value, as it implies a greater claim on the land.
    *   **Legal Ownership:** It defines the legal ownership stake in the land.
*   **Example:**
    *   Plot Area: 1000 sq. meters
    *   Total Saleable Area (SBA) of all apartments: 8000 sq. meters
    *   An apartment with a Saleable Area of 100 sq. meters.
    *   UDS for this apartment = (100 sq. meters / 8000 sq. meters) * 1000 sq. meters = 12.5 sq. meters.
    *   This means the owner of this apartment has a right to 12.5 sq. meters of the total land area.

### Valuation of Apartments - Integrating FSI, SBA, and UDS

The valuation of an apartment is a multi-faceted process that considers its individual attributes and its share of common elements.

#### 1. Valuation Approach

*   **Sales Comparison Approach:** This is the most common and effective method for apartment valuation. It involves comparing the subject apartment with similar apartments that have recently been sold or are currently listed in the same locality.
*   **Cost Approach:** Less common for individual apartments but can be used for the building as a whole. It involves estimating the cost to replace the building new, less depreciation, plus the land value.
*   **Income Capitalization Approach:** Relevant if the apartment is rented out. It involves capitalizing the net operating income generated by the apartment.

#### 2. Key Valuation Adjustments

When using the Sales Comparison Approach, the valuer must make adjustments for differences between the comparable properties and the subject apartment. These adjustments often involve the concepts learned:

*   **Adjustments for Area:**
    *   **SBA:** The most common unit of comparison is price per Super Built-up Area (₹/sq. m or ₹/sq. ft).
    *   **Carpet Area:** Some adjustors prefer to work with price per Carpet Area for a more direct comparison of usable space. The difference in price per SBA and price per Carpet Area can be attributed to common area loadings.
*   **Adjustments for UDS:**
    *   While SBA is the primary basis for comparison, the underlying UDS is critical for understanding the land value component.
    *   The value per square meter of UDS can be a separate benchmark. If two apartments have the same SBA but different UDS, the one with higher UDS is generally more valuable.
*   **Adjustments for Amenities:**
    *   Presence or absence of specific amenities (gym, pool, security, power backup) will necessitate adjustments.
*   **Adjustments for Location, Floor, View, Age, Condition:** Standard valuation adjustments apply.

#### 3. How FSI, SBA, and UDS Interact in Valuation

*   **FSI's Indirect Role:** FSI directly impacts the **density and potential of the land**. Developers use FSI to determine the total permissible built-up area. This influences the number of units they can construct and the overall feasibility of the project. Higher FSI translates to more units and potentially higher land value, which in turn affects the SBA and UDS allocated per unit.
*   **SBA as the Basis:** The **Super Built-up Area** is the primary basis for calculating the **sale price** of an apartment. The valuer will often derive the value per SBA from comparable sales and multiply it by the subject apartment's SBA.
    
    $$ \text{Apartment Value} \approx \text{Price per SBA} \times \text{Subject Apartment's SBA} $$
*   **UDS for Land Value Component:** The **Undivided Share of Land** is crucial for determining the **land value component** of the apartment.
    *   The valuer can estimate the value per square meter of UDS in the locality.
    *   $$ \text{Land Value Component} = \text{Subject Apartment's UDS} \times \text{Value per sq. m of UDS} $$
    *   This land value component can be used to validate the overall valuation derived from the SBA method or as a primary valuation metric if land is the dominant factor.

#### 4. Practical Valuation Steps

1.  **Determine the Subject Apartment's Details:**
    *   Carpet Area
    *   Built-up Area
    *   Super Built-up Area (SBA)
    *   Undivided Share of Land (UDS)
    *   Floor level, view, internal specifications, age, condition.
2.  **Identify Comparable Apartments:** Find similar apartments in the same or comparable locality that have been recently transacted.
3.  **Gather Comparable Sales Data:** Obtain the sale prices and the corresponding SBA, Carpet Area, and UDS for comparable apartments.
4.  **Calculate Price per SBA for Comparables:**
    *   $$ \text{Price per SBA (Comparable)} = \frac{\text{Sale Price of Comparable}}{\text{SBA of Comparable}} $$
5.  **Make Adjustments:** Adjust the comparable prices for differences in location, floor level, view, amenities, age, and condition relative to the subject apartment.
6.  **Derive an Adjusted Price per SBA:** Based on the adjusted comparable sales, determine a market-based price per SBA for the subject property.
7.  **Calculate the Subject Apartment's Value:**
    *   $$ \text{Valuation by SBA} = \text{Adjusted Price per SBA} \times \text{Subject Apartment's SBA} $$
8.  **Cross-check with UDS Valuation (Optional but Recommended):**
    *   Calculate the Value per sq. m of UDS for comparable properties.
    *   $$ \text{Value per sq. m of UDS (Comparable)} = \frac{\text{Sale Price of Comparable}}{\text{UDS of Comparable}} $$
    *   Make necessary adjustments for differences.
    *   $$ \text{Land Value Component} = \text{Adjusted Value per sq. m of UDS} \times \text{Subject Apartment's UDS} $$
    *   This can be compared to the total valuation obtained by the SBA method to ensure consistency.

### Examples

**Example 1: Calculating UDS**

*   **Plot Area:** 500 sq. meters
*   **Total Permissible FSI:** 2.0 (Meaning total built-up area on all floors can be 500 * 2 = 1000 sq. meters, assuming no plot coverage restrictions on FSI calculation)
*   **Permissible Plot Coverage:** 30% (Ground floor footprint can be 500 * 0.30 = 150 sq. meters)
*   **Total Saleable Area (SBA) of all apartments in the building:** 900 sq. meters (The developer might not utilize the full FSI or might have common areas that don't form part of apartment SBA).
*   **Apartment A's SBA:** 75 sq. meters
*   **Apartment B's SBA:** 100 sq. meters

**Calculation of UDS:**

*   **UDS for Apartment A:** (75 sq. m / 900 sq. m) * 500 sq. m = **41.67 sq. meters**
*   **UDS for Apartment B:** (100 sq. m / 900 sq. m) * 500 sq. m = **55.56 sq. meters**

This means Apartment A has a legal entitlement to 41.67 sq. meters of the total land, and Apartment B to 55.56 sq. meters.

**Example 2: Apartment Valuation using SBA**

*   **Subject Apartment's SBA:** 80 sq. meters
*   **Comparable Apartment 1:**
    *   SBA: 70 sq. meters
    *   Sale Price: ₹70,00,000
    *   Floor: Same as subject
    *   Amenities: Similar
*   **Comparable Apartment 2:**
    *   SBA: 90 sq. meters
    *   Sale Price: ₹85,00,000
    *   Floor: One floor lower than subject
    *   Amenities: Slightly better

**Valuation Steps:**

1.  **Price per SBA for Comparables:**
    *   Comparable 1: ₹70,00,000 / 70 sq. m = ₹1,00,000/sq. m
    *   Comparable 2: ₹85,00,000 / 90 sq. m = ₹94,444/sq. m (approx.)
2.  **Adjustments:**
    *   **Comparable 1:** No adjustment needed for floor or amenities.
    *   **Comparable 2:** Subject apartment is on a higher floor. Assume an upward adjustment of 5% for the higher floor of the subject compared to Comparable 2.
        *   Adjusted Price per SBA (Comparable 2) = ₹94,444 * 1.05 = ₹99,167/sq. m (approx.)
3.  **Derive Adjusted Price per SBA:**
    *   The prices are very close: ₹1,00,000/sq. m and ₹99,167/sq. m. Let's take an average or a slightly conservative figure, say ₹99,500/sq. m.
4.  **Calculate Subject Apartment's Value:**
    *   Subject Apartment Value = ₹99,500/sq. m * 80 sq. m = **₹79,60,000**

**Considering UDS for Validation:**

*   Assume the UDS for Comparable 1 is 50 sq. m.
*   Assume the UDS for Comparable 2 is 65 sq. m.
*   Value per sq. m of UDS (Comparable 1) = ₹70,00,000 / 50 sq. m = ₹1,40,000/sq. m.
*   Value per sq. m of UDS (Comparable 2) = ₹85,00,000 / 65 sq. m = ₹130,769/sq. m (approx.).
*   Let's assume the subject apartment has a UDS of 55 sq. m.
*   Adjusting Comparable 1's UDS value (assuming no floor adjustment needed for land value): ₹1,40,000/sq. m.
*   Adjusting Comparable 2's UDS value (for higher floor): ₹130,769 * 1.05 = ₹137,307/sq. m (approx.).
*   Let's take an average UDS value of ₹138,500/sq. m.
*   Land Value Component = ₹138,500/sq. m * 55 sq. m = ₹76,17,500.

This land value component is a significant portion of the total estimated value of ₹79,60,000, indicating consistency in the valuation. The difference between the total value and the land value component represents the value attributed to the built structure and common amenities.

### Answers to Practice Questions

1.  **FSI (Floor Space Index) or FAR (Floor Area Ratio) is the ratio of the total permissible built-up area on a plot to the total area of the plot. It is crucial for controlling development density. In valuation, it indicates the development potential of land, influencing land value and the scale of construction, which in turn affects the number of units and their total value.**
2.  **Super Built-up Area (SBA) is the total area of an apartment plus its proportionate share of common areas (lobbies, stairs, amenities, etc.). It's calculated as: SBA = Built-up Area + (Built-up Area × Common Area Ratio). SBA is the primary basis for apartment pricing because it reflects the total area a buyer "buys" and includes the shared facilities provided by the developer.**
3.  **Undivided Share of Land (UDS) is the proportionate share of the total land area that an apartment owner is entitled to. It is calculated based on the apartment's area relative to the total saleable area of all units. UDS is significant because it represents the ownership stake in the land itself, a key component of property value that appreciates over time and provides legal title to a portion of the land.**
4.  **Calculation of UDS:**
    *   Total Plot Area = 800 sq. meters
    *   Total Saleable Area (SBA) = 1800 sq. meters
    *   Subject Apartment's SBA = 90 sq. meters
    *   UDS = (Subject Apartment's SBA / Total SBA) × Total Plot Area
    *   UDS = (90 sq. m / 1800 sq. m) × 800 sq. m
    *   UDS = 0.05 × 800 sq. m = **40 sq. meters**
5.  **Valuation using SBA:**
    *   **Comp A:**
        *   Price per SBA = ₹80,00,000 / 75 sq. m = ₹1,06,667/sq. m (approx.)
    *   **Comp B:**
        *   Price per SBA = ₹88,00,000 / 85 sq. m = ₹1,03,529/sq. m (approx.)
    *   **Adjustment for Comp B:** Assume the subject apartment is one floor higher than Comp B. This warrants a 3% upward adjustment to Comp B's price per SBA.
        *   Adjusted Price per SBA (Comp B) = ₹1,03,529 × 1.03 = ₹1,06,635/sq. m (approx.)
    *   **Derive Adjusted Price per SBA:** Both comparables, after adjustment, suggest a price per SBA around ₹1,06,650/sq. m.
    *   **Estimated Value of Subject Apartment:** ₹1,06,650/sq. m × 80 sq. m = **₹85,32,000** (approx.)
6.  **FSI dictates the total buildable area on a plot, influencing the number and size of apartments. Developers use this to plan the project and derive the total saleable area. SBA is the metric used for selling units, encompassing individual unit space plus a share of common areas. UDS is the proportional ownership of the land, derived from the unit's SBA relative to the total SBA and total plot area. In valuation, FSI indirectly drives the potential for SBA, while SBA is the basis for market price, and UDS provides the critical land value component that underpins the overall asset value.**

### Important Points to Remember

*   **FSI is a regulatory control, not a direct measure of an apartment's area but influences the project's scale.**
*   **Super Built-up Area (SBA) is the primary metric for pricing and trading apartments, but it can be opaque due to the arbitrary allocation of common areas.**
*   **Undivided Share of Land (UDS) is crucial for understanding the underlying land value and long-term appreciation potential.**
*   **Always verify the definitions and calculation methods for SBA and UDS as per local bye-laws and sale agreements.**
*   **When valuing apartments, use the Sales Comparison Approach and make meticulous adjustments for all differentiating factors, including area basis (SBA/Carpet), floor, amenities, and UDS.**
*   **A valuer should cross-check SBA-based valuation with UDS-based land value estimations for a more robust assessment.**
*   **The value per sq. m of SBA will always be higher than the value per sq. m of built-up area or carpet area due to the inclusion of common area loadings.**
