# Valuation of Real Properties: Module 1 - Role of Valuer

## Topic: Role of Valuer - Classification - Valuers' Functions & Responsibilities

---

## Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   Understand the fundamental role of a valuer in the real estate market.
*   Identify and explain different classifications of valuers.
*   Describe the core functions and responsibilities of a valuer.
*   Recognize the ethical considerations and professional standards governing valuers.

---

## 1. The Fundamental Role of a Valuer

A valuer, also known as an appraiser or surveyor, is a professional who estimates the market value of real property. They play a crucial role in various financial and legal transactions involving land and buildings.

### Key Concepts & Definitions:

*   **Real Property:** Land and anything permanently attached to it, including buildings, trees, and minerals.
*   **Market Value:** The most probable price a property would bring in a competitive and open market under all conditions requisite to a fair sale, the buyer and seller each acting prudently and knowledgeably, and assuming the price is not affected by undue stimulus. (This is a common definition, variations may exist depending on jurisdiction).
*   **Valuation:** The process of estimating the value of an asset, in this case, real property.

### Why is Valuation Important?

*   **Financial Transactions:** Mortgages, loans, property sales, leases, insurance.
*   **Legal & Estate Matters:** Probate, divorce settlements, property disputes, expropriation/eminent domain.
*   **Investment & Development:** Feasibility studies, portfolio management, development planning.
*   **Taxation:** Property taxes, capital gains tax.
*   **Decision Making:** For buyers, sellers, lenders, investors, and governments.

**Example:** A bank needs to know the market value of a property before approving a mortgage loan to ensure the loan amount is not more than the property's worth.

## 2. Classification of Valuers

Valuers can be classified based on various criteria, including their specialization, professional affiliation, and geographical area of practice.

### Common Classifications:

*   **By Specialization:**
    *   **General Practice Valuers:** Handle a wide range of property types (residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural).
    *   **Specialist Valuers:** Focus on specific property types or valuation methods.
        *   **Residential Valuers:** Specialize in houses, apartments, condominiums.
        *   **Commercial Valuers:** Specialize in retail, office, and mixed-use properties.
        *   **Industrial Valuers:** Specialize in factories, warehouses, and manufacturing facilities.
        *   **Agricultural Valuers:** Specialize in farms, ranches, and plantations.
        *   **Plant and Machinery Valuers:** Value movable assets attached to land.
        *   **Contents Valuers:** Value movable contents within a property.
*   **By Professional Affiliation/Qualification:**
    *   **Chartered Surveyors/Valuers:** Members of recognized professional bodies (e.g., RICS - Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors). These often have rigorous educational and experience requirements.
    *   **Licensed Appraisers:** Holding a license issued by a government regulatory body, often with specific educational and examination requirements.
    *   **Certified Valuers:** Holding certifications from professional organizations, often signifying expertise in specific areas.
*   **By Employment:**
    *   **Private Practice Valuers:** Work for independent valuation firms.
    *   **In-House Valuers:** Employed by banks, insurance companies, property developers, or government agencies.
    *   **Government Valuers:** Employed by local or national government bodies for taxation, land acquisition, or public asset management.

**Example:** A valuer specializing in shopping malls would be a Commercial Valuer, likely with extensive experience in retail property analysis.

## 3. Valuers' Functions

Valuers perform a variety of functions, all centered around the estimation of property value.

### Key Functions:

*   **Property Valuation:** The primary function, estimating market value, investment value, or other defined values.
*   **Feasibility Studies:** Assessing the viability of a proposed development project.
*   **Rent Reviews:** Determining fair rental values for leased properties.
*   **Compulsory Acquisition/Eminent Domain Valuations:** Estimating compensation for properties taken by the government.
*   **Insurance Valuations:** Determining the replacement cost or indemnity value of a property for insurance purposes.
*   **Property Portfolio Analysis:** Valuing multiple properties within an investment portfolio.
*   **Due Diligence:** Investigating and reporting on the physical condition and marketability of a property.
*   **Advisory Services:** Providing expert advice on property market trends, investment strategies, and development opportunities.
*   **Expert Witness Testimony:** Providing expert opinions in legal proceedings related to property disputes.

**Example:** A valuer conducting a rent review for an office building would analyze comparable rental data and the building's current condition to recommend a new lease rate.

## 4. Valuers' Responsibilities

Beyond performing functions, valuers have significant responsibilities to their clients, the public, and the profession.

### Key Responsibilities:

*   **Accuracy and Objectivity:** To provide unbiased and accurate valuations based on sound principles and data.
*   **Professional Competence:** To possess the necessary knowledge, skills, and experience to undertake valuations. This includes keeping up-to-date with market trends and valuation methodologies.
*   **Confidentiality:** To maintain the privacy of client information and valuation details.
*   **Ethical Conduct:** To adhere to a strict code of ethics, avoiding conflicts of interest and maintaining integrity.
*   **Due Diligence:** To thoroughly investigate all relevant factors affecting property value, including physical condition, legal encumbrances, and market conditions.
*   **Clear Communication:** To present valuation reports in a clear, concise, and understandable manner, detailing the methodology, assumptions, and conclusions.
*   **Compliance with Standards:** To adhere to relevant professional standards, guidelines, and legal requirements (e.g., International Valuation Standards (IVS), local valuation acts).
*   **Disclosure of Conflicts of Interest:** To inform clients of any potential conflicts that might compromise their impartiality.
*   **Professional Development:** To engage in continuous learning and professional development to maintain and enhance their expertise.

**Example:** A valuer must disclose if they have a personal interest in a property they are valuing, as this could influence their professional judgment and create a conflict of interest.

---

## 5. Ethical Considerations & Professional Standards

Professionalism and ethics are paramount for valuers to maintain public trust and ensure the integrity of the valuation process.

### Key Ethical Principles:

*   **Integrity:** Honesty and uprightness in all dealings.
*   **Objectivity:** Impartiality and freedom from bias.
*   **Confidentiality:** Protecting client information.
*   **Competence:** Possessing and maintaining the required expertise.
*   **Professional Behavior:** Acting in a manner that upholds the reputation of the profession.
*   **Independence:** Being free from undue influence or pressure.

### Professional Standards:

*   **International Valuation Standards (IVS):** Published by the International Valuation Standards Council (IVSC), providing a global framework for valuation practice.
*   **National Valuation Standards:** Many countries have their own professional bodies that set specific standards and codes of conduct.
*   **Legal Requirements:** Adherence to local laws and regulations governing property valuation and the practice of the profession.

**Example:** A valuer must refuse a valuation assignment if they believe they do not have the necessary expertise for that specific property type or if a conflict of interest cannot be resolved.

## Practice Questions & Exercises

1.  **Question:** Briefly explain the importance of a valuer's role in the mortgage lending process.
    **Answer:** Valuers are crucial in mortgage lending to ensure the loan amount is adequately secured by the property's market value. This protects the lender from financial loss if the borrower defaults and the property has to be sold.

2.  **Question:** Provide two examples of how a valuer's functions extend beyond simply determining market value.
    **Answer:**
    *   **Feasibility Studies:** Valuers assess the potential financial viability of new property developments.
    *   **Rent Reviews:** Valuers determine appropriate rental values for existing leases, ensuring fairness for both landlord and tenant.

3.  **Question:** What is the primary responsibility of a valuer regarding the accuracy of their report?
    **Answer:** The primary responsibility is to provide an accurate and objective valuation based on sound principles, data, and thorough due diligence.

4.  **Question:** Imagine you are asked to value a property that is being sold by a close family member. What ethical responsibility must you consider and what action should you take?
    **Answer:** You must consider the responsibility of **objectivity** and avoiding **conflicts of interest**. You should inform your client (the family member) of the potential conflict and discuss whether you can proceed impartially. In most cases, it would be prudent to decline the assignment to maintain professional integrity.

5.  **Question:** Name one professional body that sets valuation standards and the name of the standards they publish.
    **Answer:** The International Valuation Standards Council (IVSC) publishes the International Valuation Standards (IVS). (Other valid answers might include RICS and their Red Book).

## Important Points to Remember:

*   **Valuers are essential intermediaries** in the real estate market, facilitating fair transactions and informed decision-making.
*   **Market value is the most common but not the only type of value** a valuer might determine.
*   **Classification helps understand specializations and professional standing.**
*   **Functions are the "what"** of a valuer's work, while **responsibilities are the "how" and "why"** they must perform those functions.
*   **Ethics and professional standards are non-negotiable** for maintaining trust and credibility.
*   **Continuous learning and adherence to evolving standards** are vital for any practicing valuer.
