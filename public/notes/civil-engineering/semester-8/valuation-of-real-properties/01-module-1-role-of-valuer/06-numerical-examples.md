## VALUATION OF REAL PROPERTIES - Module 1: Role of Valuer

### Topic: Numerical Examples

This module focuses on the foundational role of a valuer and how their expertise translates into practical numerical applications. While the core of this topic is about understanding the valuer's function, numerical examples within this context help illustrate the importance of accurate data, careful calculations, and the application of valuation principles.

### 1. The Valuer's Primary Function in the Real Estate Market

The valuer acts as an **impartial expert** who provides an opinion of a property's value. This opinion is crucial for various stakeholders:

*   **Buyers and Sellers:** To negotiate a fair price.
*   **Lenders (Banks, Financial Institutions):** To assess collateral for loans.
*   **Investors:** To make informed investment decisions.
*   **Governments:** For taxation (property tax, capital gains tax) and compulsory acquisition.
*   **Legal Professionals:** For probate, divorce settlements, and dispute resolution.

**Numerical Aspect:** The valuer's primary output is a numerical figure – the **valuation amount** or **market value**. This numerical output is the culmination of a complex process.

---

### 2. The Importance of Data Collection and its Impact on Valuation Outcomes

Accurate and comprehensive data is the bedrock of a reliable valuation. Errors or omissions in data collection will directly lead to inaccurate valuation figures.

**Key Data Types:**

*   **Property Specific Data:**
    *   Physical characteristics (size, age, construction, condition, number of rooms, amenities).
    *   Location (neighbourhood, accessibility, views, proximity to services).
    *   Tenure (freehold, leasehold).
    *   Planning permissions and zoning.
*   **Market Data:**
    *   **Sales Comparables:** Prices of recently sold similar properties.
    *   **Rental Comparables:** Rental rates of similar properties.
    *   **Inquiry Data:** Information on properties currently on the market.
    *   **Economic Data:** Interest rates, inflation, employment rates, economic growth.
    *   **Demographic Data:** Population growth, income levels, household composition.

**Numerical Aspect:** Many of these data points are inherently numerical or can be converted into numerical metrics.

*   **Example:**
    *   **Property Size:** 100 sq. meters (numerical).
    *   **Age of Property:** 25 years (numerical).
    *   **Sales Price of Comparable:** $500,000 (numerical).
    *   **Rental Income:** $2,000 per month (numerical).

**Impact on Valuation:**

*   **Underestimating property size:** Leads to a lower valuation.
*   **Using outdated sales comparables:** Can result in a valuation that doesn't reflect current market conditions.
*   **Ignoring negative market trends:** Might lead to an overvaluation.

---

### 3. The Role of Numerical Calculations in Determining Property Value

The valuer uses various numerical calculations to process the collected data and arrive at a valuation figure. These calculations are guided by established valuation methodologies.

**Common Numerical Operations:**

*   **Arithmetic Operations:** Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division.
*   **Percentage Calculations:** For adjustments, depreciation, or yield.
*   **Ratio Calculations:** Price per square meter, rent per square meter.
*   **Discounting and Capitalization:** For income-based valuations.

**Numerical Aspect:** The entire valuation process is underpinned by numerical computations.

---

### 4. Identifying Common Numerical Inputs and Outputs in the Valuation Process

**Key Numerical Inputs:**

*   **Square Meterage/Footage:** The usable area of the property.
*   **Purchase Price of Comparables:** The price paid for similar properties.
*   **Rental Income:** The amount generated by the property.
*   **Vacancy Rates:** The percentage of time a property is vacant.
*   **Operating Expenses:** Costs associated with owning and operating the property (e.g., property taxes, insurance, maintenance).
*   **Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate):** The rate of return on a real estate investment property.
*   **Discount Rate:** Used in discounted cash flow analysis.
*   **Interest Rates:** For mortgage calculations and investment analysis.
*   **Inflation Rates:** To adjust future cash flows.
*   **Depreciation Factors:** To account for the wear and tear of property.

**Key Numerical Outputs:**

*   **Market Value:** The most probable price a property would sell for in an open and competitive market.
*   **Rental Value:** The estimated rent a property could command.
*   **Yield:** The rate of return on investment.
*   **Price per Square Meter/Foot:** A common metric for comparing property values.
*   **Mortgageable Value:** The value determined for lending purposes.

### 5. Basic Understanding of How Different Valuation Approaches Utilize Numerical Data

The three main approaches to valuation (Cost, Income, Sales Comparison) all rely on numerical inputs and calculations.

#### a) Sales Comparison Approach (Market Approach)

This approach compares the subject property to similar properties (comparables) that have recently sold. Adjustments are made to the comparables' sale prices to account for differences.

**Numerical Steps:**

1.  **Identify Sales Comparables:** Find properties similar to the subject property that have sold recently.
2.  **Gather Sales Data:** Record the sale price, date of sale, and key features of comparables.
3.  **Analyze Features:** Identify differences between comparables and the subject property (e.g., size, condition, location, amenities).
4.  **Quantify Adjustments:** Assign a monetary value to each difference (e.g., adjust for an extra bathroom, a better view).
    *   **Example:** If a comparable has an extra bedroom and the subject property does not, and an extra bedroom is valued at $15,000, you would *subtract* $15,000 from the comparable's sale price to make it more similar to the subject.
5.  **Reconcile Values:** Arrive at a final valuation by averaging or weighting the adjusted sale prices.

**Numerical Example:**

*   **Subject Property:** 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, 1 garage, good condition.
*   **Comparable 1:** Sold for $550,000. 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, 1 garage, good condition. (No adjustment needed)
*   **Comparable 2:** Sold for $530,000. 3 bedrooms, 1 bathroom, 1 garage, good condition. (Needs adjustment for an extra bathroom)
*   **Comparable 3:** Sold for $575,000. 4 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, 1 garage, good condition. (Needs adjustment for an extra bedroom)

**Quantifying Adjustments (Hypothetical):**

*   Value of an extra bathroom: +$20,000
*   Value of an extra bedroom: +$15,000

**Calculations:**

*   **Comparable 1 Adjusted Price:** $550,000
*   **Comparable 2 Adjusted Price:** $530,000 + $20,000 (for extra bathroom) = $550,000
*   **Comparable 3 Adjusted Price:** $575,000 - $15,000 (for extra bedroom) = $560,000

**Reconciliation:**

Average adjusted price = ($550,000 + $550,000 + $560,000) / 3 = $553,333

**Estimated Market Value:** Approximately $553,333.

---

#### b) Income Capitalization Approach

This approach is used for income-producing properties. It converts the expected income stream into a capital value.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Gross Potential Income (GPI):** The total rent that could be collected if the property were fully occupied.
*   **Vacancy and Credit Loss:** An allowance for periods when the property is vacant or tenants fail to pay rent.
*   **Gross Effective Income (GEI):** GPI minus vacancy and credit loss.
*   **Operating Expenses (OE):** Costs of owning and operating the property (e.g., property taxes, insurance, maintenance, management fees).
*   **Net Operating Income (NOI):** GEI minus OE. This is the property's profit before debt service and income taxes.
*   **Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate):** The rate used to capitalize NOI into value. It represents the investor's required rate of return.

**Numerical Formula:**

**Value = Net Operating Income (NOI) / Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)**

**Numerical Example:**

*   **Subject Property:** An apartment building.
*   **Gross Potential Rent:** $120,000 per year.
*   **Vacancy and Credit Loss:** 5% of GPI.
*   **Operating Expenses:** $40,000 per year.
*   **Capitalization Rate:** 8% (or 0.08).

**Calculations:**

1.  **Vacancy and Credit Loss Amount:** $120,000 * 0.05 = $6,000
2.  **Gross Effective Income (GEI):** $120,000 - $6,000 = $114,000
3.  **Net Operating Income (NOI):** $114,000 - $40,000 = $74,000
4.  **Value:** $74,000 / 0.08 = $925,000

**Estimated Market Value:** $925,000.

---

#### c) Cost Approach

This approach is typically used for new or special-purpose properties where comparable sales or income data are scarce. It's based on the principle of substitution – a buyer wouldn't pay more for a property than the cost of acquiring a comparable substitute.

**Numerical Steps:**

1.  **Estimate Land Value:** Determine the value of the land as if it were vacant and available for its highest and best use.
2.  **Estimate Replacement Cost New:** Calculate the cost of constructing a replica of the existing building using current materials and standards.
3.  **Estimate Cost of New Construction (Less Depreciation):** Subtract accumulated depreciation (physical deterioration, functional obsolescence, economic obsolescence) from the replacement cost new.
4.  **Add Land Value:** Sum the depreciated cost of the building and the estimated land value.

**Numerical Formulas/Concepts:**

*   **Depreciation:** Can be calculated using age-life methods, straight-line depreciation, or observed depreciation.
*   **Observed Depreciation:**
    *   **Physical Deterioration:** Wear and tear.
    *   **Functional Obsolescence:** Outdated design or features (e.g., too few bathrooms, poor layout).
    *   **Economic Obsolescence:** External factors (e.g., changing neighbourhood, increased traffic).

**Numerical Example (Simplified):**

*   **Subject Property:** A small office building.
*   **Estimated Land Value:** $300,000.
*   **Cost to Build New (Replacement Cost New):** $800,000.
*   **Accrued Depreciation (Physical, Functional, Economic):** $200,000.

**Calculations:**

1.  **Depreciated Cost of Building:** $800,000 - $200,000 = $600,000
2.  **Total Value:** $600,000 (Building) + $300,000 (Land) = $900,000

**Estimated Market Value:** $900,000.

### Practice Questions/Exercises:

**Question 1:**

A valuer is using the Sales Comparison Approach. They have identified a comparable property that sold for $650,000. The comparable property has 4 bedrooms, while the subject property has 3 bedrooms. The valuer estimates the market value of an additional bedroom to be $25,000. What is the adjusted sale price of the comparable property for the subject property?

**Question 2:**

An investor is considering purchasing an apartment building. The building is expected to generate a Gross Potential Income of $250,000 per year. The estimated vacancy and credit loss is 7% of GPI. Operating expenses are $70,000 per year. The investor requires a capitalization rate of 9%. Calculate the estimated market value of the apartment building using the Income Capitalization Approach.

**Question 3:**

Using the Cost Approach, estimate the market value of a property where the estimated land value is $400,000, the cost to build the structure new is $1,200,000, and the total accrued depreciation is $350,000.

---

### Answers to Practice Questions:

**Answer 1:**

*   Since the comparable has an *extra* bedroom compared to the subject property, we need to *subtract* the value of that bedroom from the comparable's sale price to make it comparable to the subject.
*   Adjusted Sale Price = Sale Price of Comparable - Value of Extra Bedroom
*   Adjusted Sale Price = $650,000 - $25,000 = **$625,000**

**Answer 2:**

1.  **Vacancy and Credit Loss Amount:** $250,000 * 0.07 = $17,500
2.  **Gross Effective Income (GEI):** $250,000 - $17,500 = $232,500
3.  **Net Operating Income (NOI):** $232,500 - $70,000 = $162,500
4.  **Value:** $162,500 / 0.09 = **$1,805,555.56** (approximately)

**Answer 3:**

1.  **Depreciated Cost of Building:** $1,200,000 - $350,000 = $850,000
2.  **Total Value:** $850,000 (Building) + $400,000 (Land) = **$1,250,000**

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **The Valuer's Role:** To provide an independent and objective opinion of value.
*   **Data is King:** Accuracy and completeness of data are paramount for a reliable valuation.
*   **Numerical Inputs Drive Outputs:** Every valuation involves transforming numerical data through specific calculations.
*   **Each Approach Has a Numerical Basis:** The Sales Comparison, Income Capitalization, and Cost approaches all rely on distinct numerical methodologies.
*   **Adjustments are Crucial:** In the Sales Comparison Approach, accurately quantifying adjustments for differences is key.
*   **NOI is the Foundation of Income Valuation:** Understanding how to derive NOI is essential for the Income Capitalization Approach.
*   **Depreciation is Key in the Cost Approach:** Accurately estimating accrued depreciation is critical for the Cost Approach.
*   **The Goal is a Reliable Numerical Output:** The ultimate aim is to arrive at a defensible and accurate valuation figure.
