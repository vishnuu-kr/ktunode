# VALUATION OF REAL PROPERTIES: Module 1 - Role of the Valuer

## 1.1 Purpose of Valuation

The **purpose of a valuation** dictates the entire valuation process, influencing the methodology, data gathered, and the final report. It's the fundamental question: "Why is this valuation being undertaken?"

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Defining the Purpose:** Clearly understanding and documenting the objective of the valuation.
*   **User of the Valuation:** Identifying who will rely on the valuation report and for what decisions.
*   **Legal and Commercial Implications:** The purpose often has significant legal or financial consequences.

**Common Purposes of Valuation:**

*   **Sale and Purchase:** Determining a fair market price for a property.
    *   *Example:* A seller wants to know the market value of their house before listing it for sale. A buyer wants to ensure they are not overpaying.
*   **Mortgage Lending/Secured Lending:** Assessing the collateral value of a property for a loan.
    *   *Example:* A bank requires a valuation of a property before approving a mortgage for a borrower.
*   **Rating and Taxation:** Establishing the value of property for local government taxes.
    *   *Example:* Municipal authorities periodically revalue properties to determine property tax liabilities.
*   **Investment Analysis:** Evaluating the potential return on a property as an investment.
    *   *Example:* An investor needs to assess the rental income and capital growth potential of a commercial building.
*   **Compulsory Acquisition/Eminent Domain:** Determining compensation for property taken by government.
    *   *Example:* A government agency needs to acquire private land for a new road and must pay fair compensation to the owner.
*   **Insurance:** Establishing the replacement cost or indemnity value of a property.
    *   *Example:* An insurance company needs to know the cost to rebuild a damaged property after a fire.
*   **Leasehold Enfranchisement/Lease Extensions:** Valuing the freehold interest or the right to extend a lease.
    *   *Example:* A leaseholder in the UK may have the right to buy the freehold of their property or extend their lease, requiring a valuation.
*   **Company Accounts/Balance Sheets:** Reporting the value of property owned by a company for accounting purposes.
    *   *Example:* A company needs to report the value of its office building on its annual balance sheet.
*   **Divorce Settlements/Probate:** Dividing property assets in legal disputes or after a death.
    *   *Example:* In a divorce, a property may need to be valued to determine the equitable distribution of assets between spouses.
*   **Development Viability:** Assessing the potential profit from a development project.
    *   *Example:* A developer needs to value the potential sale prices of apartments and the costs of construction to determine if a project is financially feasible.
*   **Expert Witness:** Providing expert opinion on property value in legal proceedings.
    *   *Example:* A valuer may be called to court to give evidence on the value of a disputed property.

**Important Point to Remember:** The purpose of the valuation directly influences the **standard of value** to be adopted (e.g., Market Value, Investment Value).

## 1.2 Doctrine of Estate

The "Doctrine of Estate" is a fundamental concept in property law, particularly in common law jurisdictions, that defines the nature and extent of an owner's interest in land. It's about the **duration** and **type** of rights an individual holds in a property.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Estate:** Refers to the quantum of interest a person has in land, not the physical land itself. It's a bundle of rights.
*   **Freehold Estate:** The highest form of ownership, representing an indefinite and perpetual interest in land. It's often referred to as "ownership."
    *   *Characteristics:* Absolute ownership, inheritable, transferable.
    *   *Examples:* Owning a house outright.
*   **Leasehold Estate:** A possessory right in land for a defined period, granted by the freeholder (landlord) to a leaseholder (tenant).
    *   *Characteristics:* Fixed term (e.g., 99 years, 999 years), rent payable, conditions and covenants in the lease.
    *   *Examples:* Owning an apartment on a long lease, renting a commercial property.
*   **Other Estates (Less Common in Basic Valuation Contexts but relevant to broader property law):**
    *   **Life Estate:** An interest in land for the duration of a person's life.
    *   **Fee Tail:** An estate that can only be inherited by lineal descendants (largely abolished in many jurisdictions).

**Relevance to Valuation:**

*   **Interest to be Valued:** The valuer must identify precisely *what* interest in the property is being valued. Is it the freehold interest, or a leasehold interest with a specific unexpired term?
*   **Value of Different Estates:** The value of a freehold estate will typically be different from the value of a leasehold estate, even for the same physical property. A leasehold interest is subject to the terms of the lease, including rent obligations and potential unexpired lease term.
*   **Market Value of Freehold vs. Leasehold:** The market value of a property will vary depending on whether it's sold as freehold or subject to a lease.

    *   *Example:* A property might be valued as a vacant freehold for sale. However, if it's subject to a long lease with a substantial ground rent, the valuation of the *freehold interest* will be reduced by the value of the leasehold rights and the income stream from the ground rent. Conversely, the value of the *leasehold interest* will be the market value of the property less the present value of the ground rent.

**Important Point to Remember:** The "doctrine of estate" is crucial because it defines the **rights and duration of ownership** being valued, which directly impacts the value.

## 1.3 Different Forms of Value

"Value" itself is not a single, fixed concept. Different purposes require different **standards of value**. These standards define the basis upon which the valuation is made.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Standard of Value:** The definition of value to be adopted for a specific valuation purpose.
*   **Assumptions and Hypothetical Conditions:** Standards of value often include specific assumptions about the property, its marketing, and the nature of the transaction.

**Common Forms/Standards of Value:**

1.  **Market Value:**
    *   **Definition (common international standard):** The estimated amount for which a property should exchange on the valuation date between a willing buyer and a willing seller in an arm's-length transaction after proper marketing, wherein the parties had each acted knowledgeably, prudently, and without compulsion.
    *   **Key Elements:**
        *   **Willing Buyer & Willing Seller:** Neither is under duress.
        *   **Arm's-Length Transaction:** Parties are unrelated and acting in their own best interests.
        *   **Proper Marketing:** The property has been exposed to the market for a sufficient period.
        *   **Knowledgeable & Prudent Parties:** Both parties understand the property and the market.
        *   **No Compulsion:** Neither party is forced to buy or sell.
    *   **Purpose:** Sale and purchase, mortgage lending, company accounts.
    *   *Example:* The most common value sought by most people when buying or selling a property.

2.  **Investment Value:**
    *   **Definition:** The value of an investment to a particular investor or group of investors based on their specific investment requirements or objectives.
    *   **Key Elements:**
        *   **Specific Investor Focus:** Tailored to the needs of a particular buyer.
        *   **May Include Synergies:** Value can be higher for an investor who can achieve operational efficiencies or utilize the property for their own business.
        *   **Not Necessarily Market Value:** An investor might pay more than market value if the property perfectly fits their strategic goals.
    *   **Purpose:** Investment analysis, acquisition by owner-occupiers or strategic buyers.
    *   *Example:* A company looking to acquire a factory next to its existing operations might pay a premium over market value because of the operational synergies it can achieve.

3.  **Liquidation Value:**
    *   **Definition:** The net amount that may be realized from the disposal of property under either of the following circumstances:
        *   (a) Orderly Liquidation: assumes the property is marketed for sale over a specified period to achieve the best price from a willing buyer.
        *   (b) Forced Liquidation: assumes the property is sold under duress (e.g., bankruptcy sale) in a short period.
    *   **Key Elements:**
        *   **Speed of Sale:** The primary driver; shorter periods lead to lower values.
        *   **Reduced Marketing:** Limited exposure to potential buyers.
    *   **Purpose:** Bankruptcy, distressed sales, wind-ups.
    *   *Example:* A company going bankrupt might need to sell its assets quickly, resulting in a liquidation value that is lower than market value due to the urgency.

4.  **Mortgage Lending Value (or Security Value):**
    *   **Definition:** The value of the property as collateral for a mortgage loan, often reflecting a more conservative estimate than market value, considering potential future saleability and loan-to-value ratios.
    *   **Key Elements:**
        *   **Lender's Perspective:** Focus on capital preservation.
        *   **Liquidity:** Consideration of how quickly the property can be sold in the event of default.
        *   **Loan-to-Value (LTV):** Often a percentage of market value.
    *   **Purpose:** Mortgage lending.
    *   *Example:* A bank might lend 80% of the market value of a property, meaning the mortgage lending value is effectively 80% of market value for their purposes.

5.  **Salvage Value:**
    *   **Definition:** The estimated value of a property that has reached the end of its economic life or is no longer fit for its original purpose, in its existing state.
    *   **Key Elements:**
        *   **As-is condition:** Value of the materials or components, not the functioning property.
    *   **Purpose:** End-of-life asset disposal.
    *   *Example:* The value of old building materials for recycling after demolition.

6.  **Development Value:**
    *   **Definition:** The value of land or property with the potential for redevelopment, often calculated on a "hope value" basis or by assessing the profit a developer could make.
    *   **Key Elements:**
        *   **Development Potential:** Based on zoning, planning permissions, and market demand for new developments.
        *   **Profit Margin:** Developer's expected profit is factored in.
    *   **Purpose:** Development feasibility studies, valuing land for sale to developers.
    *   *Example:* Land zoned for residential development might be worth more to a developer than to an owner-occupier if it has the potential for multiple units.

**Important Point to Remember:** Always clarify the **standard of value** being used in any valuation report, as it significantly impacts the resulting figure.

## 1.4 Factors Affecting Valuation

Numerous factors influence the value of a property. These can be broadly categorized.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Highest and Best Use:** The most probable use of a property that is physically possible, legally permissible, and financially feasible, which results in the highest value.
*   **Supply and Demand:** Fundamental economic principles that drive property prices.

**Categories of Factors:**

1.  **Physical Factors:**
    *   **Location:** Proximity to amenities, transport, schools, employment centres, views, environmental factors (noise, pollution).
        *   *Example:* A property in a prime city location with excellent transport links will generally be worth more than a similar property in a remote rural area.
    *   **Size and Shape of Land:** Usable area, frontage, topography, soil conditions.
        *   *Example:* Flat, regular-shaped land is generally more valuable for development than steep or irregularly shaped land.
    *   **Size and Quality of Buildings:** Gross internal area (GIA), net internal area (NIA), number of rooms, layout, age, construction materials, condition, architectural style, energy efficiency.
        *   *Example:* A modern, well-maintained building with energy-efficient features will command a higher value than an older, dilapidated one.
    *   **Amenities:** Parking, gardens, security, communal facilities, views.
        *   *Example:* A property with ample secure parking will be more attractive in a city centre than one without.
    *   **Condition:** State of repair, need for renovations, structural integrity.
        *   *Example:* A property requiring significant repairs will be valued lower than a similar property in good condition.

2.  **Economic Factors:**
    *   **Market Conditions:** Supply and demand for properties, interest rates, inflation, employment levels, consumer confidence, economic growth.
        *   *Example:* In a recession with high unemployment, property values may fall due to reduced demand and lower affordability.
    *   **Rental Income:** Current and potential rental income, lease terms, tenant covenants, vacancy rates.
        *   *Example:* A commercial property generating strong rental income from a reputable tenant will be valued higher than an vacant one.
    *   **Investment Returns:** Yields in alternative investments, capital growth expectations.
        *   *Example:* If interest rates rise significantly, investors might demand higher yields from property, leading to lower capital values.
    *   **Local Economic Base:** Diversity and strength of the local economy, presence of major employers.
        *   *Example:* A town heavily reliant on a single industry is more vulnerable to economic downturns, potentially impacting property values.

3.  **Legal and Political Factors:**
    *   **Zoning and Land Use Regulations:** Permitted uses, building height restrictions, density controls.
        *   *Example:* Land zoned for high-density residential development will be worth more than land zoned for single-family homes if market demand supports it.
    *   **Planning Permissions:** Existing or potential for obtaining planning permission for development or alterations.
        *   *Example:* A property with recent planning approval for an extension will likely have a higher value than a similar property without it.
    *   **Easements and Covenants:** Rights of way, restrictions on use.
        *   *Example:* An easement allowing a neighbour to cross a portion of the property might reduce its value.
    *   **Property Taxes and Rates:** The level of local property taxes.
        *   *Example:* Higher property taxes can reduce the attractiveness of a property, potentially lowering its value.
    *   **Government Policies:** Housing policies, infrastructure investment, environmental regulations.
        *   *Example:* Government incentives for green building could increase the value of energy-efficient properties.

4.  **Social Factors:**
    *   **Demographics:** Population growth, age distribution, household composition.
        *   *Example:* An area with a growing young population may see increased demand for starter homes.
    *   **Lifestyle Trends:** Preferences for urban vs. rural living, demand for specific property types (e.g., smart homes, sustainable living).
        *   *Example:* Growing interest in walkable urban environments can increase the value of properties in city centres.
    *   **Community Characteristics:** Quality of schools, crime rates, community facilities, local amenities.
        *   *Example:* A neighbourhood with excellent schools and low crime rates will generally be more desirable and command higher values.

**Important Point to Remember:** A valuer must consider the interplay of all these factors to arrive at a comprehensive valuation.

## 1.5 Aspects of Valuation

"Aspects of Valuation" refers to the different perspectives or elements a valuer considers when undertaking a valuation. It's about the scope and considerations involved in the process.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Valuation Approaches:** The methodologies used to arrive at a value (e.g., Income, Sales Comparison, Cost).
*   **Highest and Best Use Analysis:** A critical early step in most valuations.
*   **Market Analysis:** Understanding the current and future trends of the property market.

**Key Aspects a Valuer Considers:**

1.  **Identification of the Property:**
    *   Accurate legal description (title deeds, plans).
    *   Physical boundaries and extent.
    *   Any associated rights or encumbrances (e.g., easements).
2.  **Identification of the Interest to be Valued:**
    *   Freehold, leasehold (and its terms), etc. (as per Doctrine of Estate).
3.  **Purpose of the Valuation:**
    *   This dictates the standard of value and the methodology.
4.  **Standard of Value:**
    *   Market Value, Investment Value, etc.
5.  **Highest and Best Use:**
    *   Determining the most profitable or advantageous use of the property, assuming it's legally permissible, physically possible, and financially feasible.
    *   *Example:* Is it best used as a single-family home, or could it be redeveloped into multiple units?
6.  **Market Research and Analysis:**
    *   Gathering comparable sales data, rental data, development trends, economic indicators.
    *   Understanding supply and demand dynamics.
7.  **Property Inspection:**
    *   Thorough inspection of the physical condition, features, and layout of the property.
    *   Assessing the quality of construction and any defects.
8.  **Data Analysis and Valuation Methodologies:**
    *   Applying appropriate valuation approaches:
        *   **Sales Comparison Approach:** Comparing the subject property to similar recently sold properties.
        *   **Income Capitalisation Approach:** Converting expected future income into a capital value.
        *   **Cost Approach:** Estimating the cost to replace or reproduce the property, less depreciation.
    *   Reconciling the results from different approaches.
9.  **Valuation Date:**
    *   The specific date on which the value is estimated. Market conditions can change rapidly.
10. **Reporting:**
    *   Clearly and accurately presenting the findings, assumptions, and conclusions in a professional valuation report.
    *   Justifying the valuation opinion.

**Important Point to Remember:** Valuation is a process of informed opinion based on data and analysis, not just a calculation. It involves a systematic approach to considering all relevant factors.

## 1.6 Characteristics of Property Valuations

These are the qualities or attributes that define a well-executed and credible property valuation.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Objectivity:** Aiming for an unbiased opinion.
*   **Rationality:** Based on logical reasoning and evidence.
*   **Accuracy:** Striving for the most precise estimate possible.

**Key Characteristics:**

1.  **Objectivity and Impartiality:**
    *   The valuer must be independent and free from bias, providing an opinion based solely on the merits of the property and market evidence.
    *   *Example:* A valuer should not inflate a valuation to please a client if the market evidence does not support it.
2.  **Competence:**
    *   The valuer must possess the necessary knowledge, skills, and experience to undertake the valuation. This includes understanding property law, economics, valuation techniques, and local market conditions.
3.  **Due Diligence:**
    *   Thorough investigation and verification of all relevant information, including property details, legal rights, planning status, and market data.
4.  **Rationality and Logic:**
    *   The valuation process must be based on logical reasoning, supported by evidence and established valuation principles.
    *   *Example:* The choice of comparable properties and adjustments made must be justifiable.
5.  **Accuracy and Precision:**
    *   While valuation is an opinion, it should be as accurate and precise as possible, based on the available data. Precision is limited by the quality and quantity of data.
6.  **Transparency and Disclosure:**
    *   The valuation report should clearly disclose all assumptions, limitations, and the basis of the valuation.
7.  **Compliance with Standards:**
    *   Adherence to relevant professional standards, ethical codes, and legal requirements (e.g., RICS Red Book, USPAP).
8.  **Market-Based:**
    *   The valuation should reflect prevailing market conditions and be supported by market evidence, wherever possible.
9.  **Timeliness:**
    *   The valuation should be completed within a reasonable timeframe, as market conditions can change.
10. **Purpose-Driven:**
    *   The entire process and final opinion must be directly responsive to the stated purpose of the valuation.

**Important Point to Remember:** The credibility of a valuation hinges on these characteristics, ensuring it is a reliable basis for decision-making.

## Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
A property is being valued for the purpose of obtaining a mortgage. Which standard of value is most likely to be used, and why?
a) Market Value
b) Investment Value
c) Mortgage Lending Value
d) Liquidation Value

**Question 2:**
Explain the difference between a freehold estate and a leasehold estate and how this distinction might affect the valuation of a property.

**Question 3:**
List and briefly explain three physical factors that would influence the valuation of a residential property.

**Question 4:**
You are valuing a retail shop. A potential buyer is a national chain looking to open their first store in this town, and they believe this location offers significant strategic advantages for their brand. Another potential buyer is an investor looking purely at the rental yield.
a) Which buyer's perspective aligns more closely with "Market Value"?
b) Which buyer's perspective aligns more closely with "Investment Value"?

**Question 5:**
What are the key characteristics of a competent property valuation?

## Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
The most likely standard of value to be used is **(c) Mortgage Lending Value**.
*   **Reasoning:** Mortgage lenders are concerned with the security of their loan. While they rely on market value, they often apply a more conservative approach, considering factors like liquidity and potential future market declines, to determine the maximum loan amount they are willing to offer. This often translates to a specific "mortgage lending value" or a loan-to-value ratio based on market value.

**Answer 2:**
*   **Freehold Estate:** Represents absolute ownership of the property indefinitely. It includes the right to occupy, sell, lease, or mortgage the property.
*   **Leasehold Estate:** Represents the right to occupy and use a property for a defined period (the lease term), usually in exchange for rent, granted by the freehold owner (landlord).
*   **Impact on Valuation:**
    *   The **value of the freehold interest** will be affected by the existence and terms of any lease. A property subject to a long lease with a substantial ground rent will have a lower freehold value than if it were vacant.
    *   The **value of a leasehold interest** depends on the unexpired term of the lease, the ground rent payable, and any covenants within the lease. A shorter lease or a high ground rent will reduce the value of the leasehold interest.

**Answer 3:**
Three physical factors affecting the valuation of a residential property:
1.  **Location:** Proximity to amenities (schools, shops, transport), neighbourhood desirability, views, and environmental factors (noise, pollution). A prime location with good amenities will command a higher value.
2.  **Size and Layout:** The overall size of the property (both land and buildings), the number and size of rooms, and the efficiency of the layout. A larger property or one with a more functional layout is generally more valuable.
3.  **Condition and Quality:** The state of repair, age of the building, quality of construction materials, finishings, and features like energy efficiency. A well-maintained, modern property in good condition will be valued higher than an older, dilapidated one.

**Answer 4:**
a) The perspective of the **national chain** looking to open its first store aligns more closely with **Market Value**. They are acting as a potential buyer in the market, and their strategic advantage might be a factor they are willing to pay for, but a valuer's primary task for market value is to assess what the market would pay generally. If their strategic advantage leads to them paying a premium *above* what other buyers would pay, this would be captured in an "Investment Value" or "Special Value" for that specific buyer, rather than pure Market Value.

b) The perspective of the **investor looking purely at rental yield** aligns more closely with **Investment Value**. They are assessing the property based on their specific investment criteria and required returns, rather than a general market participant. Their required yield may be higher or lower than the market average, leading to a different valuation conclusion.

**Answer 5:**
Key characteristics of a competent property valuation include:
*   **Objectivity and Impartiality:** Free from bias.
*   **Competence:** Possessing necessary knowledge and skills.
*   **Due Diligence:** Thorough investigation and verification of information.
*   **Rationality and Logic:** Based on sound reasoning and evidence.
*   **Accuracy and Precision:** As precise as the available data allows.
*   **Transparency and Disclosure:** Clearly stating assumptions and limitations.
*   **Compliance with Standards:** Adhering to professional and legal requirements.
*   **Market-Based:** Reflecting prevailing market conditions and evidence.
*   **Timeliness:** Completed within a reasonable timeframe.
*   **Purpose-Driven:** Directly addressing the specific purpose.

## Important Points to Remember Summary

*   **Purpose is Paramount:** Always start by understanding the specific purpose of the valuation. It guides everything else.
*   **Estate Matters:** Clearly identify the property interest (freehold, leasehold) being valued.
*   **Value is Relative:** Recognize that "value" is not a single number. Different standards of value exist for different purposes.
*   **Holistic Approach:** Consider all influencing factors – physical, economic, legal, and social.
*   **Highest and Best Use:** A fundamental concept in determining potential value.
*   **Credibility is Key:** Valuations must be objective, competent, and transparent.
*   **Market Evidence is Crucial:** Valuations should be supported by the best available market data.
