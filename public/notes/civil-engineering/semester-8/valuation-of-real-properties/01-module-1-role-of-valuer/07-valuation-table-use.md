### 1. Introduction to Valuation Tables

#### 1.1 What are Valuation Tables?

*   **Definition:** Valuation tables are pre-calculated mathematical tables that provide specific financial factors. These factors are derived from mathematical formulas related to the time value of money, annuities, and sinking funds.
*   **Purpose:** They simplify and speed up complex calculations required in property valuation, particularly those involving future income streams or capital recovery over time.
*   **Core Principle:** Valuation tables are based on the fundamental concept of the **time value of money**, which states that a sum of money is worth more now than the same sum will be at a future date due to its potential earning capacity.

#### 1.2 Significance in Property Valuation

*   **Efficiency:** They eliminate the need for repetitive manual calculations, saving valuers significant time.
*   **Accuracy:** When used correctly, they ensure accurate and consistent calculations, reducing the risk of human error.
*   **Standardization:** They promote a standardized approach to calculations within the valuation profession.
*   **Decision Making:** They provide crucial figures that underpin investment decisions, loan assessments, and property transactions.

### 2. Key Concepts Underlying Valuation Tables

#### 2.1 Time Value of Money (TVM)

*   **Concept:** The value of money changes over time due to inflation, opportunity cost, and risk. A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   **Present Value (PV):** The current worth of a future sum of money or stream of cash flows, given a specified rate of return.
    *   **Future Value (FV):** The value of a current asset at a specified date in the future, based on an assumed rate of growth.
    *   **Interest Rate (i or r):** The rate of return or discount rate used in calculations. It reflects the cost of capital or the opportunity cost of investing elsewhere.
    *   **Number of Periods (n):** The total number of periods (e.g., years, months) over which the investment or loan operates.

#### 2.2 Annuities

*   **Definition:** A series of equal payments made at equal intervals for a specified period.
*   **Types of Annuities:**
    *   **Ordinary Annuity:** Payments made at the *end* of each period.
    *   **Annuity Due:** Payments made at the *beginning* of each period.

#### 2.3 Sinking Funds

*   **Definition:** A fund formed by making periodic contributions towards a future debt or capital expenditure. It's the reverse of an annuity.
*   **Purpose:** To accumulate a specific sum of money by a future date through regular deposits.

### 3. Types of Valuation Tables and Their Uses

Valuation tables are categorized based on the type of financial calculation they facilitate.

#### 3.1 Present Value Tables

*   **Purpose:** To determine the current worth of a future lump sum or a series of future cash flows.
*   **Common Tables:**
    *   **Present Value of $1 (PVIF - Present Value Interest Factor):** Used to find the present value of a single future payment.
        *   **Formula Basis:** $PV = FV \times (1 + i)^{-n}$
        *   **Factor:** $(1 + i)^{-n}$
    *   **Present Value of an Annuity of $1 (PVIFA - Present Value Interest Factor of an Annuity):** Used to find the present value of a series of equal payments made at the end of each period (ordinary annuity).
        *   **Formula Basis:** $PV_{annuity} = Payment \times \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}{i}$
        *   **Factor:** $\frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}{i}$

*   **Example (PVIF):** A property is expected to be sold for $100,000 in 5 years. If the appropriate discount rate is 8% per annum, what is the present value of that sale price?
    *   Find the PVIF for 8% and 5 years. Let's assume the table value is 0.6806.
    *   $PV = \$100,000 \times 0.6806 = \$68,060$

*   **Example (PVIFA):** An investor expects to receive $10,000 per year for 10 years, starting at the end of year 1. The discount rate is 7%. What is the present value of this income stream?
    *   Find the PVIFA for 7% and 10 years. Let's assume the table value is 7.0236.
    *   $PV_{annuity} = \$10,000 \times 7.0236 = \$70,236$

#### 3.2 Future Value Tables

*   **Purpose:** To determine the future worth of a current lump sum or a series of future payments.
*   **Common Tables:**
    *   **Future Value of $1 (FVIF - Future Value Interest Factor):** Used to find the future value of a single lump sum invested today.
        *   **Formula Basis:** $FV = PV \times (1 + i)^{n}$
        *   **Factor:** $(1 + i)^{n}$
    *   **Future Value of an Annuity of $1 (FVIFA - Future Value Interest Factor of an Annuity):** Used to find the future value of a series of equal payments made at the end of each period (ordinary annuity).
        *   **Formula Basis:** $FV_{annuity} = Payment \times \frac{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}{i}$
        *   **Factor:** $\frac{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}{i}$

*   **Example (FVIF):** You invest $5,000 today at an annual interest rate of 6% for 15 years. What will be the future value of your investment?
    *   Find the FVIF for 6% and 15 years. Let's assume the table value is 2.3966.
    *   $FV = \$5,000 \times 2.3966 = \$11,983$

*   **Example (FVIFA):** You plan to save $500 at the end of each year for 20 years, earning an annual interest rate of 5%. What will be the total amount saved?
    *   Find the FVIFA for 5% and 20 years. Let's assume the table value is 34.6914.
    *   $FV_{annuity} = \$500 \times 34.6914 = \$17,345.70$

#### 3.3 Sinking Fund Tables

*   **Purpose:** To determine the periodic payment required to accumulate a specific future sum. This is crucial for calculating amounts needed for capital replacement, loan amortization, or development funds.
*   **Common Tables:**
    *   **Sinking Fund Factor (SFF):** Used to determine the periodic payment needed to accumulate a future lump sum. It's the inverse of the Future Value of an Annuity of $1$.
        *   **Formula Basis:** $Payment = FV \times \frac{i}{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}$
        *   **Factor:** $\frac{i}{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}$ (which is $1 / FVIFA$)

*   **Example:** A property owner needs to replace a roof in 12 years, and the estimated cost is $25,000. What amount should be set aside annually (at the end of each year) to fund this, assuming an investment return of 4% per annum?
    *   Find the Sinking Fund Factor for 4% and 12 years. This is the inverse of the FVIFA for 4% and 12 years.
    *   Let's assume the FVIFA for 4% and 12 years is 15.0258.
    *   SFF = $1 / 15.0258 = 0.066557$
    *   $Annual Payment = \$25,000 \times 0.066557 = \$1,663.93$

#### 3.4 Capital Recovery Tables

*   **Purpose:** To determine the periodic payment that will repay both the principal and interest over a specified period. This is fundamental for mortgage calculations and determining economic rent. It is the inverse of the Present Value of an Annuity of $1$.
*   **Common Tables:**
    *   **Capital Recovery Factor (CRF) / Annuity Factor:** Used to determine the annual payment required to repay a loan or capital investment.
        *   **Formula Basis:** $Annual Payment = PV \times \frac{i(1 + i)^{n}}{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}$
        *   **Factor:** $\frac{i(1 + i)^{n}}{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}$ (which is $1 / PVIFA$)

*   **Example:** A developer invests $500,000 in a new property. They want to recover this investment over 25 years with an expected annual return of 8%. What is the required annual return (economic rent)?
    *   Find the Capital Recovery Factor for 8% and 25 years. This is the inverse of the PVIFA for 8% and 25 years.
    *   Let's assume the PVIFA for 8% and 25 years is 10.6748.
    *   CRF = $1 / 10.6748 = 0.093683$
    *   $Annual Return = \$500,000 \times 0.093683 = \$46,841.50$

### 4. How to Use Valuation Tables

1.  **Identify the problem:** Determine whether you need to find a Present Value, Future Value, Periodic Payment for Accumulation (Sinking Fund), or Periodic Payment for Repayment (Capital Recovery).
2.  **Identify the parameters:**
    *   Principal amount (PV or FV)
    *   Periodic payment amount
    *   Interest rate (per period)
    *   Number of periods
3.  **Select the correct table:** Choose the table that matches the type of calculation required (e.g., PVIF, PVIFA, FVIF, FVIFA, SFF, CRF).
4.  **Locate the correct factor:** Find the intersection of the specified interest rate (usually down the columns) and the number of periods (usually across the rows).
5.  **Perform the calculation:** Multiply the relevant monetary amount by the factor found in the table.
6.  **Interpret the result:** State the calculated value clearly in the context of the property valuation.

### 5. Practical Application Examples in Property Valuation

#### 5.1 Investment Analysis (Income Approach)

*   **Scenario:** A property generates a net annual income of $20,000 for the next 15 years. The required rate of return (yield) for this type of investment is 10%.
*   **Valuation Method:** To find the present value of future income streams.
*   **Table Used:** Present Value of an Annuity of $1 (PVIFA)$.
*   **Calculation:**
    *   Find PVIFA for 10% and 15 years (e.g., 7.6061).
    *   Property Value = Net Annual Income × PVIFA (10%, 15 years)
    *   Property Value = $20,000 × 7.6061 = \$152,122$

#### 5.2 Sinking Fund for Capital Expenditure

*   **Scenario:** A building owner anticipates a major refurbishment costing $100,000 in 10 years. They can invest surplus funds at 5% per annum.
*   **Valuation Method:** To determine the annual amount to set aside.
*   **Table Used:** Sinking Fund Factor (SFF).
*   **Calculation:**
    *   Find SFF for 5% and 10 years (e.g., 0.07950).
    *   Annual Contribution = Future Cost × SFF (5%, 10 years)
    *   Annual Contribution = $100,000 × 0.07950 = \$7,950$

#### 5.3 Loan Amortization (Mortgage Calculations)

*   **Scenario:** A buyer takes out a mortgage of $200,000 at an interest rate of 6% per annum, to be repaid over 25 years.
*   **Valuation Method:** To calculate the annual mortgage payment.
*   **Table Used:** Capital Recovery Factor (CRF) / Annuity Factor.
*   **Calculation:**
    *   Find CRF for 6% and 25 years (e.g., 0.0871).
    *   Annual Mortgage Payment = Loan Amount × CRF (6%, 25 years)
    *   Annual Mortgage Payment = $200,000 × 0.0871 = \$17,420$

### 6. Limitations and Assumptions of Valuation Tables

*   **Fixed Interest Rate:** Tables assume a constant interest rate throughout the periods, which may not reflect real-world market fluctuations.
*   **Regular Payments:** They are designed for regular, equal payments. Irregular cash flows require different calculations or breakdown into multiple steps.
*   **Discrete Periods:** Calculations are based on discrete compounding periods (usually annual). For semi-annual or monthly compounding, adjustments or more detailed tables/software are needed.
*   **No Inflation Adjustment:** The factors do not inherently account for inflation unless the interest rate used is a real rate (adjusted for inflation).
*   **Accuracy of Table Factors:** While generally accurate, there might be minor rounding differences between different table sources.
*   **Oversimplification:** Real-world property valuations can involve more complex factors than these tables can directly accommodate (e.g., variable income, sale costs, taxation).

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
An investor wants to buy a property that is expected to yield a net income of $15,000 per annum for the next 12 years. The required rate of return is 9%. What is the estimated present value of this income stream?

**Question 2:**
You are saving for a down payment on a property. You plan to deposit $1,000 at the end of each year for 7 years into an account that earns 5% interest annually. How much will you have accumulated at the end of 7 years?

**Question 3:**
A property owner needs to reserve funds for a major repair estimated to cost $50,000 in 15 years. If the funds can be invested at 4% per annum, how much should be set aside each year (at the end of the year) to meet this future cost?

**Question 4:**
A valuation indicates that a property's capital value is $300,000. If this value represents the capital sum to be recovered over 20 years with a required return of 7%, what is the equivalent annual charge (capital recovery amount)?

---

### Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
*   **Type of calculation:** Present Value of an Annuity.
*   **Table needed:** PVIFA.
*   **Parameters:** Payment = $15,000, n = 12 years, i = 9%.
*   **Factor:** Look up PVIFA for 9% and 12 years. (Let's assume the table value is 7.1386).
*   **Calculation:** $15,000 \times 7.1386 = \$107,079$
*   **Estimated Present Value:** $107,079

**Answer 2:**
*   **Type of calculation:** Future Value of an Annuity.
*   **Table needed:** FVIFA.
*   **Parameters:** Payment = $1,000, n = 7 years, i = 5%.
*   **Factor:** Look up FVIFA for 5% and 7 years. (Let's assume the table value is 8.1424).
*   **Calculation:** $1,000 \times 8.1424 = \$8,142.40$
*   **Accumulated Amount:** $8,142.40

**Answer 3:**
*   **Type of calculation:** Sinking Fund.
*   **Table needed:** Sinking Fund Factor (SFF) or use inverse of FVIFA.
*   **Parameters:** Future Cost (FV) = $50,000, n = 15 years, i = 4%.
*   **Factor:** Look up SFF for 4% and 15 years. (Alternatively, find FVIFA for 4%, 15 years = 21.8251; then SFF = 1/21.8251 = 0.045816).
*   **Calculation:** $50,000 \times 0.045816 = \$2,290.80$
*   **Annual Amount to Set Aside:** $2,290.80

**Answer 4:**
*   **Type of calculation:** Capital Recovery.
*   **Table needed:** Capital Recovery Factor (CRF) or use inverse of PVIFA.
*   **Parameters:** Present Value (PV) = $300,000, n = 20 years, i = 7%.
*   **Factor:** Look up CRF for 7% and 20 years. (Alternatively, find PVIFA for 7%, 20 years = 10.5939; then CRF = 1/10.5939 = 0.09439).
*   **Calculation:** $300,000 \times 0.09439 = \$28,317$
*   **Equivalent Annual Charge:** $28,317

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Interest Rate per Period:** Always ensure the interest rate used matches the period for which the table is designed (usually annual, but check table headings). If the interest is compounded more frequently, you'll need to adjust the rate and number of periods.
*   **Timing of Payments:** Be mindful of whether payments are made at the beginning (annuity due) or end (ordinary annuity) of the period, as this affects the calculation and which factors to use (though most standard tables are for ordinary annuities).
*   **Relationship between Tables:** Recognize that PV tables are the inverse of Sinking Fund factors, and FV tables are the inverse of Capital Recovery factors.
*   **"Factors" are Multipliers:** The numbers in the tables are multipliers to simplify calculations.
*   **Context is Key:** Always apply the calculated factors to the correct monetary amount in the context of the property valuation problem.
