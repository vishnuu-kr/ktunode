# Water and Air Quality Management

## Module 3: Water Resources and Quality Management in India: Water Availability

### Topic: Role of National and International Agencies in Water Health and Sanitation

### 1. Introduction: The Importance of Water Health and Sanitation

*   **Definition: Water Health:** Refers to the condition of water bodies and their ability to support aquatic life and human use. It encompasses physical, chemical, and biological parameters.
*   **Definition: Sanitation:** Refers to the provision and disposal of human and household waste in a way that prevents the transmission of disease and protects public health and the environment.
*   **Interconnectedness:** Access to safe water and proper sanitation is fundamental to public health, economic development, and environmental sustainability. Poor water and sanitation lead to waterborne diseases, impacting productivity and well-being.
*   **Governmental and International Role:** Managing these critical resources and services requires concerted efforts from national governments and international organizations.

### 2. Role of National Agencies in Water Health and Sanitation in India

India faces significant challenges in water availability and sanitation due to its large population, diverse geography, and socio-economic factors. Several national agencies are mandated to address these issues:

#### 2.1. Ministry of Jal Shakti (MoJS)

*   **Apex Body:** The central government ministry responsible for all matters related to water resources, including surface and groundwater. It was formed by merging the erstwhile Ministry of Water Resources, River Development & Ganga Rejuvenation, and the Ministry of Drinking Water and Sanitation.
*   **Key Departments and Their Roles:**

    *   **Department of Water Resources, River Development & Ganga Rejuvenation:**
        *   **Water Resource Management:** Planning, development, and management of national water resources.
        *   **River Basin Management:** Development and conservation of river basins, including inter-state river disputes.
        *   **Flood Control & Drought Management:** Implementing measures for flood forecasting, warning, and drought relief.
        *   **Dam Safety & Maintenance:** Ensuring the safety and efficient operation of large dams.
        *   **Ganga Rejuvenation (Namami Gange Programme):** A flagship program aimed at cleaning and rejuvenating the River Ganga.
        *   **Policy Formulation:** Developing national policies and strategies for water resource management.
        *   **Example:** The **National Water Policy** provides a framework for the development and management of water resources.

    *   **Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation:**
        *   **Drinking Water:** Ensuring access to safe and adequate drinking water for all citizens, particularly in rural areas.
        *   **Sanitation:** Promoting and implementing sanitation programs, especially in rural areas, to achieve open defecation free (ODF) status.
        *   **Key Schemes:**
            *   **Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM):** Aims to provide Functional Household Tap Connections (FHTCs) to every rural household by 2024. Focuses on providing potable water in a sustainable manner.
            *   **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (SBA) - Gramin:** Aims to make rural India Open Defecation Free (ODF) and improve overall sanitation coverage. This includes promotion of toilets, solid and liquid waste management.
        *   **Standard Setting:** Prescribing quality standards for drinking water.
        *   **Example:** The launch of the **Jal Jeevan Mission** has significantly boosted household tap water connections across India, improving water availability and reducing reliance on unsafe sources.

#### 2.2. Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA)

*   **Urban Water and Sanitation:** Primarily responsible for water supply and sanitation services in urban areas.
*   **Key Schemes/Initiatives:**
    *   **Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT):** Focuses on improving basic services in urban areas, including water supply, sewerage, and stormwater drainage.
    *   **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (SBA) - Urban:** Aims to make urban India ODF and improve solid waste management.
    *   **Smart Cities Mission:** Includes components related to water management and sanitation infrastructure within smart cities.
*   **Policy and Funding:** Provides policy guidance and financial assistance to urban local bodies for water and sanitation projects.
*   **Example:** AMRUT's focus on 100% coverage of household water connections and sewerage in selected cities addresses urban water availability and sanitation challenges.

#### 2.3. Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA)

*   **Groundwater Regulation:** A statutory body established under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, to regulate and control the development of groundwater.
*   **Functions:**
    *   **Groundwater Assessment:** Monitoring groundwater levels and quality across the country.
    *   **Regulation of Extraction:** Granting permission for groundwater extraction, especially for large-scale use.
    *   **Policy Advisory:** Advising the central government on groundwater conservation and management.
*   **Example:** CGWA's guidelines on groundwater extraction aim to prevent over-exploitation and ensure sustainable groundwater availability.

#### 2.4. Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)

*   **Water Quality Monitoring:** Responsible for setting and monitoring water quality standards.
*   **Functions:**
    *   **Pollution Assessment:** Identifying sources of water pollution and their impact.
    *   **Effluent Standards:** Prescribing standards for industrial and municipal effluents discharged into water bodies.
    *   **Monitoring Networks:** Operating national water quality monitoring networks.
    *   **Policy Support:** Providing technical inputs for water quality management policies.
*   **Example:** CPCB's water quality index helps assess the health of major rivers and lakes, guiding pollution control measures.

#### 2.5. National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)

*   **Disaster Preparedness:** Focuses on preparedness and mitigation for water-related disasters like floods and droughts.
*   **Role:** Develops guidelines and strategies for water management during and after disasters, ensuring access to safe water and sanitation.
*   **Example:** NDMA's guidelines for flood management include provisions for protecting water sources and sanitation facilities.

### 3. Role of International Agencies in Water Health and Sanitation

International agencies play a crucial role in supporting national efforts through funding, technical expertise, policy advice, and capacity building.

#### 3.1. United Nations (UN) and its Agencies

*   **UN-Water:** The inter-agency coordination mechanism for all UN work related to water and sanitation. It ensures that the UN system addresses water and sanitation in a coordinated and effective manner.
    *   **Focus:** Promoting integrated water resources management and advocating for water and sanitation as human rights.
    *   **Example:** UN-Water's role in advocating for Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) influences global and national policies.

*   **World Health Organization (WHO):**
    *   **Health Aspects:** Focuses on the health implications of water quality and sanitation.
    *   **Norms and Standards:** Develops guidelines for drinking water quality and promotes safe sanitation practices.
    *   **Disease Prevention:** Works to prevent waterborne diseases through improved water safety and hygiene.
    *   **Example:** The **WHO Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality** are globally recognized benchmarks that countries often adopt. WHO also provides technical support to India on public health aspects of water and sanitation.

*   **United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF):**
    *   **Child-Centric Approach:** Works on improving water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) for children, particularly in developing countries.
    *   **Program Implementation:** Supports governments in implementing WASH programs, especially in rural and underserved areas.
    *   **Behavior Change:** Focuses on promoting hygiene practices among communities.
    *   **Example:** UNICEF's significant role in India's Swachh Bharat Abhiyan, particularly in promoting handwashing and toilet usage, has been instrumental in achieving sanitation goals.

*   **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP):**
    *   **Integrated Development:** Integrates water and sanitation into broader development frameworks.
    *   **Capacity Building:** Supports governments in policy development, institutional strengthening, and project management for water resources.
    *   **Example:** UNDP assists countries in developing integrated water resource management plans and strengthening governance structures for the water sector.

*   **United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP):**
    *   **Environmental Focus:** Addresses the environmental aspects of water management, including pollution control and ecosystem protection.
    *   **Example:** UNEP supports projects that aim to protect freshwater ecosystems and manage water resources sustainably.

#### 3.2. World Bank

*   **Financing and Policy Advice:** A major source of funding for water and sanitation infrastructure projects in developing countries, including India.
*   **Project Development:** Assists governments in planning, designing, and implementing large-scale water and sanitation projects.
    *   **Example:** The World Bank has funded numerous projects in India aimed at improving urban water supply, sanitation, and wastewater management.

#### 3.3. Asian Development Bank (ADB)

*   **Regional Development:** Focuses on water and sanitation projects in the Asia-Pacific region.
*   **Investment and Technical Assistance:** Provides loans, grants, and technical assistance to promote sustainable water management and improve access to safe water and sanitation.
    *   **Example:** ADB supports projects for urban water supply improvements and wastewater treatment in various Indian cities.

#### 3.4. Bilateral Agencies and Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs)

*   **Bilateral Agencies:** Government aid agencies of developed countries (e.g., USAID, DFID/FCDO, GIZ) often provide funding and technical support for water and sanitation projects.
    *   **Example:** The UK's Department for International Development (DFID), now part of the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO), has been a significant partner in India's sanitation initiatives.
*   **NGOs:** Play a vital role in community mobilization, awareness campaigns, grassroots implementation, and advocacy.
    *   **Example:** Organizations like WaterAid, Sulabh International, and CSE (Centre for Science and Environment) work extensively on WASH issues in India at the community level.

---

### 4. Interrelationships and Collaborations

*   **Synergy:** National and international agencies often work in synergy. International agencies provide funding and technical expertise, while national agencies implement policies and programs.
*   **Policy Alignment:** International guidelines (e.g., WHO drinking water standards, SDG targets) influence national policies and program design.
*   **Project Implementation:** Many large-scale water and sanitation projects in India are co-financed and technically supported by international financial institutions like the World Bank and ADB.
*   **Capacity Building:** International organizations often support capacity building initiatives for national agencies and local authorities.
*   **Knowledge Sharing:** International platforms facilitate the sharing of best practices and innovations in water and sanitation management.

---

### 5. Impact on Water Availability, Quality, and Sanitation

*   **Improved Access:** National and international efforts have significantly increased access to safe drinking water and improved sanitation facilities, especially in rural and urban areas.
*   **Policy Reforms:** International support has often catalyzed policy reforms and the development of robust legal and institutional frameworks for water management.
*   **Infrastructure Development:** Funding from international agencies has been crucial for building and upgrading water supply, sewerage, and wastewater treatment infrastructure.
*   **Awareness and Behavior Change:** Campaigns supported by various agencies have raised public awareness about the importance of hygiene and sanitation.
*   **Data and Monitoring:** International collaboration strengthens data collection, monitoring, and evaluation systems for water resources and sanitation coverage.
*   **Challenges Remain:** Despite progress, challenges persist in ensuring equitable access, maintaining infrastructure, managing pollution, and addressing water scarcity in many parts of India.

---

### 6. Key Points to Remember

*   **Ministry of Jal Shakti** is the nodal ministry for water resources and drinking water/sanitation in India.
*   **Jal Jeevan Mission** and **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** are flagship national programs.
*   **CGWA** regulates groundwater extraction.
*   **CPCB/SPCBs** are responsible for water quality monitoring and pollution control.
*   **WHO** sets global drinking water standards.
*   **UNICEF** focuses on WASH for children and community-level interventions.
*   **World Bank** and **ADB** are major financiers of water infrastructure.
*   Collaboration between national and international agencies is crucial for achieving water and sanitation goals.
*   **SDG 6** is a key global framework guiding efforts in water and sanitation.

### Practice Questions/Exercises:

**Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs):**

1.  Which ministry in India is responsible for both water resources management and drinking water and sanitation in rural areas?
    a) Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs
    b) Ministry of Jal Shakti
    c) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change
    d) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare

    **Answer:** b) Ministry of Jal Shakti

2.  The flagship program aimed at providing Functional Household Tap Connections (FHTCs) to every rural household in India is:
    a) Swachh Bharat Abhiyan
    b) AMRUT
    c) Jal Jeevan Mission
    d) Namami Gange

    **Answer:** c) Jal Jeevan Mission

3.  Which international organization is primarily responsible for setting global guidelines for drinking water quality?
    a) UNICEF
    b) World Bank
    c) WHO
    d) UNDP

    **Answer:** c) WHO

4.  The mandate of the Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA) includes:
    a) Monitoring air quality
    b) Regulating groundwater extraction
    c) Managing urban solid waste
    d) Rejuvenating rivers

    **Answer:** b) Regulating groundwater extraction

It spearheads major national programs like the Jal Jeevan Mission and the rural component of the Swachh Bharat Abhiyan.
2.  **Contribution of the World Bank:** The World Bank is a major financial institution that provides significant funding for large-scale water and sanitation infrastructure projects in India. It also offers technical assistance, policy advice, and supports project development for improving urban water supply, sewerage, and wastewater management.
3.  **Significance of UN-Water:** UN-Water acts as the inter-agency coordination mechanism for all UN work related to water and sanitation. It ensures a cohesive approach from the UN system, advocates for water as a human right, and plays a crucial role in promoting global goals like SDG 6.
4.  **Two key national programs for sanitation:**
    *   **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (SBA) - Gramin:** Aimed at making rural India Open Defecation Free (ODF) and improving overall sanitation.
