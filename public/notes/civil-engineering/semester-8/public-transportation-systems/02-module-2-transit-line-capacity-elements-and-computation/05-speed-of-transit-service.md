### 1. Significance of Speed in Transit Service

**Key Concept:** Transit speed is a critical performance indicator that directly influences the attractiveness, efficiency, and capacity of a public transportation system.

*   **Passenger Perception & Ridership:**
    *   Faster transit services are generally more appealing to potential passengers, leading to increased ridership.
    *   Speed is often a primary factor when individuals choose between public transport and private vehicles.
    *   Competitive travel times make transit a viable alternative.

*   **Operational Efficiency:**
    *   Higher speeds lead to faster vehicle turnaround times, allowing a single vehicle to complete more trips within a given period.
    *   Reduced travel times can decrease operating costs per passenger-mile.

*   **Line Capacity:**
    *   **Crucial Relationship:** Speed directly impacts the number of vehicles that can operate on a given segment of track or road within a specific time frame. Higher speeds generally enable higher capacity.
    *   The time a vehicle spends between stops (travel time) is a major determinant of the frequency at which vehicles can safely follow each other.

*   **Service Reliability:**
    *   Consistent and predictable speeds contribute to overall service reliability.
    *   Faster services can be more resilient to minor disruptions.

*   **Network Connectivity:**
    *   Faster transit services improve the overall connectivity of the network, reducing transfer times and making longer journeys more feasible.

**Important Point to Remember:** Speed is not just about how fast a vehicle *can* go, but how fast it *consistently* gets passengers from point A to point B, considering all operational factors.

### 2. Factors Affecting Transit Speed

**Key Concept:** Transit speed is a complex outcome influenced by a multitude of interacting factors, broadly categorized into vehicle-specific, infrastructure-specific, and operational/environmental factors.

#### 2.1. Vehicle-Specific Factors

*   **Vehicle Design & Technology:**
    *   **Maximum Speed Capability:** The inherent design of the vehicle (e.g., tram, bus, train) dictates its potential maximum speed.
    *   **Acceleration & Deceleration:** The ability of the vehicle to quickly reach its desired speed and brake efficiently significantly impacts travel time between stops. This is particularly important for frequent-stop services.
    *   **Vehicle Type:** Rail vehicles (trains, trams) generally have higher top speeds and better acceleration/deceleration capabilities than buses due to dedicated guideways and less friction.

*   **Vehicle Maintenance & Condition:**
    *   Poorly maintained vehicles can lead to lower speeds due to mechanical issues or safety limitations.

#### 2.2. Infrastructure-Specific Factors

*   **Dedicated vs. Mixed Traffic:**
    *   **Dedicated Guideways (Rail, Busways):** Unimpeded by other traffic, allowing for higher and more consistent speeds. This is the most significant factor in achieving high transit speeds.
    *   **Mixed Traffic (Roads):** Buses operating in mixed traffic are subject to the same speed limits and congestion as private cars, drastically reducing average speeds.

*   **Speed Limits:**
    *   Legal speed limits on roads and operational speed limits on rail lines directly constrain vehicle speeds.

*   **Track/Roadway Condition:**
    *   Smooth, well-maintained tracks or roads allow for higher speeds. Uneven surfaces or poor condition can necessitate lower speeds for safety and comfort.

*   **Alignment & Curvature:**
    *   Sharp curves on rail lines or tight turns on road networks require vehicles to slow down significantly, impacting average speed.
    *   The quality of track geometry (e.g., banking on curves) also plays a role.

*   **Grade (Steepness):**
    *   Uphill gradients will reduce a vehicle's speed, while downhill gradients can increase it (within safe limits).

*   **Station/Stop Spacing:**
    *   **Closer Spacing:** More stops mean more time spent decelerating, stopping, boarding/alighting, and accelerating. This significantly reduces average speed.
    *   **Wider Spacing:** Fewer stops allow for longer periods of cruising at higher speeds, increasing average speed.

*   **Station/Stop Design:**
    *   **Platform Length & Accessibility:** Efficient platform design and clear boarding/alighting procedures reduce dwell times, indirectly affecting overall travel time and thus average speed.
    *   **Bus Stop Location:** Bus stops located within intersections or immediately after curves can cause delays.

#### 2.3. Operational & Environmental Factors

*   **Traffic Congestion:**
    *   The single biggest factor affecting bus speeds in urban environments. Heavily congested roads severely limit bus performance.

*   **Traffic Signals:**
    *   Encountering red lights causes delays and stops, reducing average speed.
    *   **Transit Signal Priority (TSP):** Systems that give transit vehicles priority at traffic signals can significantly improve bus speeds.

*   **Dwell Time:**
    *   The time a vehicle spends at a stop for passengers to board and alight.
    *   **Factors influencing dwell time:** Number of passengers, fare collection methods, boarding/alighting efficiency, accessibility features.

*   **Schedule Adherence & Headway Management:**
    *   Vehicles attempting to make up lost time might drive faster, but poor scheduling can lead to bunching (vehicles arriving close together) which reduces effective capacity and can lead to slower overall service as vehicles wait for each other.

*   **Weather Conditions:**
    *   Rain, snow, ice, or fog can necessitate reduced speeds for safety.

*   **Special Events & Road Closures:**
    *   Temporary disruptions can significantly impact speeds.

*   **Driver Behavior & Skill:**
    *   Experienced and skilled drivers can optimize acceleration, deceleration, and speed management, contributing to smoother and often faster service.

**Example:** A bus route with frequent stops, operating in mixed traffic with heavy congestion and many traffic signals, will have a much lower average speed than a metro train operating on a dedicated elevated guideway with wide station spacing and signal priority.

### 3. Types of Transit Speed

**Key Concept:** It's essential to distinguish between different measures of speed as they represent different aspects of transit performance.

*   **Maximum Speed:**
    *   **Definition:** The highest speed a transit vehicle is designed or permitted to travel under ideal conditions on a specific segment.
    *   **Example:** A train might have a maximum speed of 70 mph on a straight, well-maintained section of track. A bus might have a maximum speed of 50 mph on a clear highway.

*   **Operating Speed:**
    *   **Definition:** The average speed of a vehicle between two specified points, accounting for stops, acceleration, deceleration, and traffic conditions on that segment. This is essentially the average speed *while the vehicle is in motion*.
    *   **Calculation:** Distance between two points / (Time taken to travel between points - Dwell Time at stops between those points).
    *   **Example:** The average speed of a bus between stop A and stop B, excluding the time it stops at stop A and stop B.

*   **Schedule Speed (Average Speed):**
    *   **Definition:** The total distance of a route divided by the total scheduled time to complete that route. This is the most commonly used measure of overall transit service speed and directly reflects the travel time for passengers. It includes all stops, dwell times, and any delays.
    *   **Calculation:** Total Route Length / Total Scheduled Trip Time (including all stops and dwell times).
    *   **Example:** If a bus route is 10 miles long and scheduled to take 40 minutes, the schedule speed is 10 miles / (40/60) hours = 15 mph.

*   **Running Speed:**
    *   **Definition:** Similar to operating speed, it's the average speed of a vehicle when it's moving between stops. It often excludes scheduled layover times if they are explicitly factored in.
    *   **Calculation:** (Total Route Length - Distance between stops) / (Total Time in motion).
    *   **Usefulness:** Helps in analyzing the efficiency of the "traveling" portion of the journey, separate from stopping.

**Important Point to Remember:** Passengers care most about **schedule speed** because it represents their total travel time. However, understanding **operating speed** and the factors affecting it is crucial for improving schedule speed.

### 4. Methods for Calculating Transit Speed

**Key Concept:** Various methods are used to measure and calculate transit speeds, depending on the data available and the purpose of the analysis.

*   **Manual Tracking/Observation:**
    *   **Method:** Observers record arrival and departure times at key points (stops, signalized intersections) for specific trips.
    *   **Pros:** Can provide detailed insights into specific segments and causes of delay.
    *   **Cons:** Labor-intensive, difficult to scale for large networks, susceptible to observer bias.

*   **Trip Sheets/Driver Logs:**
    *   **Method:** Drivers record arrival and departure times from their logs.
    *   **Pros:** Relatively simple to implement if drivers are diligent.
    *   **Cons:** Can be inaccurate due to driver error or intentional manipulation.

*   **Automatic Vehicle Location (AVL) Systems:**
    *   **Method:** GPS or other location-tracking devices installed on vehicles transmit real-time location data at regular intervals.
    *   **Calculation:**
        *   **Operating Speed:** For any segment between two location pings, calculate distance / time between pings, excluding periods where the vehicle is reported as stopped (based on speed = 0).
        *   **Schedule Speed:** By processing AVL data against scheduled stop times, one can calculate actual trip durations and thus actual schedule speeds.
    *   **Pros:** Provides continuous, objective data for all vehicles in the fleet, enabling detailed analysis of speed variations and delays across the entire network. Highly accurate.
    *   **Cons:** Requires investment in AVL technology and data processing infrastructure.

*   **Ticketing/Smart Card Data:**
    *   **Method:** When passengers tap their cards at entry/exit points, timestamps are recorded. By linking entry and exit times for a journey, the total travel time can be estimated.
    *   **Pros:** Leverages existing technology for fare collection.
    *   **Cons:** Primarily measures passenger travel time, which might differ slightly from vehicle travel time if passengers board/alight at different times within the vehicle's stop. Might not capture all segments accurately if multiple transfers are involved.

*   **Dedicated Transit Management Software:**
    *   **Method:** Integrates AVL, scheduling, and passenger information systems to automatically calculate and report various speed metrics.
    *   **Pros:** Provides a comprehensive overview and automated reporting.

**Formula Review:**

*   **Average Operating Speed (Segment):** Distance / (Time in motion for segment)
*   **Average Schedule Speed (Route):** Total Route Length / Total Scheduled Trip Time

**Example Calculation using AVL Data:**
A bus equipped with AVL reports its location at:
*   Point A: 10:00:00 AM, Speed = 0 mph (at stop)
*   Point B: 10:01:30 AM, Speed = 25 mph (mid-segment)
*   Point C: 10:05:00 AM, Speed = 0 mph (at stop)

Distance between A and C is 1 mile.
Dwell time at stop A = 30 seconds (from 10:00:00 to 10:00:30).
Time from 10:00:30 to 10:05:00 is 4 minutes 30 seconds = 270 seconds.
Total trip time from A to C = 5 minutes = 300 seconds.

*   **Operating Speed between A and C:**
    *   Assume the bus starts moving at 10:00:30.
    *   It reaches stop C at 10:05:00.
    *   Time in motion = 4 minutes 30 seconds = 270 seconds.
    *   Operating Speed = 1 mile / (270/3600) hours = 1 mile / 0.075 hours = 13.33 mph.

*   **Schedule Speed between A and C:**
    *   Assume the scheduled arrival at A was 9:59:45 and scheduled departure from C is 10:06:00.
    *   Scheduled Trip Time = 10:06:00 - 9:59:45 = 6 minutes 15 seconds = 6.25 minutes.
    *   Schedule Speed = 1 mile / (6.25/60) hours = 1 mile / 0.104 hours = 9.6 mph.

### 5. Impact of Speed on Transit Line Capacity and Overall System Performance

**Key Concept:** Transit speed is a foundational element that directly dictates the potential capacity of a transit line and has cascading effects on the entire transportation network.

#### 5.1. Impact on Line Capacity

*   **Headway:**
    *   **Definition:** The time interval between consecutive vehicles on a transit line.
    *   **Relationship with Speed:** Shorter headways are desirable for higher capacity and better service frequency.
    *   **Minimum Headway:** Determined by safety regulations, signal systems, and the ability of vehicles to stop and start without colliding.
    *   **Calculation (Simplified):** Minimum Headway = Minimum Safe Following Distance / Operating Speed.
    *   **Impact:** Higher operating speeds allow for shorter safe following distances (relative to the distance covered per unit of time) or shorter time intervals between vehicles, thus enabling more vehicles to pass a point per hour, increasing capacity.

*   **Vehicles per Hour:**
    *   **Definition:** The number of vehicles that can traverse a specific section of the line in one hour.
    *   **Calculation:** 3600 seconds / Minimum Headway (in seconds).
    *   **Impact:** If a higher operating speed allows for a reduction in headway from 120 seconds to 90 seconds, the number of vehicles per hour increases from 30 (3600/120) to 40 (3600/90), a 33% increase in capacity.

*   **Passenger Capacity:**
    *   **Definition:** The total number of passengers that can be transported by the line in a given period.
    *   **Calculation:** Vehicles per Hour * Passengers per Vehicle.
    *   **Impact:** By increasing the number of vehicles per hour, higher speeds indirectly increase the potential passenger capacity of the line, assuming passenger capacity per vehicle remains constant.

**Example:**
Consider a metro line with a minimum safe headway of 90 seconds.
*   If the average operating speed is 20 mph:
    *   Vehicles per hour = 3600 / 90 = 40 vehicles/hour.
    *   If each train carries 200 passengers, passenger capacity = 40 * 200 = 8,000 passengers/hour.
*   If infrastructure improvements (e.g., better signaling) increase the average operating speed to allow for a minimum headway of 75 seconds:
    *   Vehicles per hour = 3600 / 75 = 48 vehicles/hour.
    *   Passenger capacity = 48 * 200 = 9,600 passengers/hour.
    *   This represents a 20% increase in passenger capacity due to improved speed and reduced headway.

#### 5.2. Impact on Overall System Performance

*   **Travel Time Reduction:**
    *   Faster speeds directly translate to shorter journey times for passengers, enhancing the attractiveness and competitiveness of the transit system.

*   **Increased Service Frequency:**
    *   With higher speeds, a single vehicle can complete a route faster, allowing for more frequent services with the same number of vehicles or by optimizing vehicle deployment.

*   **Reduced Operating Costs:**
    *   Faster turnaround times and potentially fewer vehicles needed to maintain a given service level can lead to lower operational expenses per passenger-mile.

*   **Improved Reliability & Punctuality:**
    *   Higher average speeds can make it easier for operators to maintain schedules and recover from minor delays, leading to a more reliable service.

*   **Enhanced Network Efficiency:**
    *   When transit is faster and more reliable, it can draw more passengers away from private vehicles, reducing road congestion for everyone and improving the overall efficiency of the urban transportation network.

*   **Economic Development:**
    *   Efficient and fast transit systems can support economic development by providing better access to jobs, services, and amenities.

**Important Point to Remember:** Improving transit speed is a multi-faceted strategy that unlocks significant benefits for both the transit agency (capacity, efficiency) and the passengers (travel time, reliability, attractiveness), ultimately contributing to a more sustainable and livable city.

A bus route is 8 miles long. The total scheduled time for the route, including all dwell times at 10 stops, is 45 minutes.
a) Calculate the schedule speed of this bus route in miles per hour (mph).

**Question 5:**
Explain the relationship between operating speed, headway, and line capacity for a rail transit line.

---

### Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
c) Travel time
*   **Explanation:** While other factors are important, travel time is often the primary determinant of whether a potential passenger chooses public transit over other modes.

**Answer 2:**
Three key infrastructure-specific factors that can significantly reduce the average speed of a bus include:
1.  **Mixed Traffic Operation:** Buses sharing the road with general traffic are subject to congestion and traffic signal delays.
2.  **Frequent Stops/Short Stop Spacing:** Each stop requires deceleration, boarding/alighting, and acceleration, all of which consume time and reduce average speed.
3.  **Road Geometry (Curves, Gradients):** Tight curves and steep uphill grades necessitate slower speeds. (Other valid answers include poor road surface condition, traffic signal delays if not mitigated by TSP).

**Answer 3:**
**Schedule Speed** is defined as the total length of a transit route divided by the total scheduled time to complete that route, including all stops and dwell times.
*   **Importance for Passengers:** Schedule speed directly reflects the total **journey time** for a passenger. It's the time they experience from departure at their origin to arrival at their destination. Therefore, it's the most relevant metric for them when comparing transit with other travel options.

**Answer 4:**
a) **Calculation of Schedule Speed:**
    *   Route Length = 8 miles
    *   Scheduled Time = 45 minutes = 45/60 hours = 0.75 hours
    *   Schedule Speed = Route Length / Scheduled Time
    *   Schedule Speed = 8 miles / 0.75 hours = **10.67 mph**

b) **Calculation of New Schedule Speed:**
    *   Route Length = 8 miles
    *   New Scheduled Time = 35 minutes = 35/60 hours = 0.5833 hours
    *   New Schedule Speed = Route Length / New Scheduled Time
    *   New Schedule Speed = 8 miles / 0.5833 hours = **13.71 mph**

**Answer 5:**
The operating speed of a rail transit line directly influences the minimum safe headway required between consecutive vehicles.
*   **Relationship:** A higher operating speed means a vehicle covers more distance in a given time. To maintain a safe following distance (expressed as a distance or time interval), a higher operating speed generally allows for a shorter **headway** (the time or distance between vehicles).
*   **Impact on Capacity:** Line capacity is typically calculated as the number of vehicles that can pass a point per hour. This is determined by dividing 3600 seconds by the minimum headway (in seconds).
    *   **Capacity = 3600 / Minimum Headway**
*   **Conclusion:** Therefore, by increasing operating speed, the minimum headway can be reduced, which directly increases the number of vehicles that can operate on the line per hour, thereby increasing the line's **capacity**. For example, if higher speeds allow headway to decrease from 100 seconds to 80 seconds, capacity increases from 36 vehicles/hour (3600/100) to 45 vehicles/hour (3600/80).
