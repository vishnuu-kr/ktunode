# Public Transportation Systems: Module 2: Transit Line Capacity: Elements and Computation

## Topic: Capacities of Different Modes

This module explores the fundamental concept of transit line capacity – the maximum number of passengers a transit service can move over a given period. Understanding capacity is crucial for designing efficient and effective public transportation systems.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   **Define** the concept of transit line capacity and its importance.
*   **Identify** the key elements that influence the capacity of different transit modes.
*   **Calculate** the theoretical and practical capacity of various transit modes.
*   **Compare and contrast** the capacities of different transit modes.
*   **Explain** the factors that can affect actual passenger throughput.

---

### 1. Understanding Transit Line Capacity

#### 1.1 Definition of Transit Line Capacity

*   **Transit Line Capacity:** The maximum number of passengers that can be transported by a specific transit mode along a particular route or segment within a defined time period (typically an hour).
*   It is usually expressed in **passengers per hour per direction (pax/hr/dir)**.

#### 1.2 Importance of Transit Line Capacity

*   **System Design:** Essential for determining the number of vehicles required, frequency of service, and infrastructure needs (e.g., platform length, number of tracks).
*   **Service Planning:** Helps in matching supply with demand, ensuring comfortable travel, and avoiding overcrowding.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** Aids in optimizing schedules, managing resources, and identifying bottlenecks.
*   **Fare Policy:** Can indirectly influence fare structures based on service levels.
*   **Future Planning:** Crucial for projecting future capacity needs and planning expansions.

#### 1.3 Theoretical vs. Practical Capacity

*   **Theoretical Capacity:** The absolute maximum number of passengers a transit line *could* carry under ideal, uninterrupted conditions. This often assumes full vehicle occupancy and minimal dwell times.
*   **Practical Capacity (or Realistic Capacity):** The maximum number of passengers that can be reasonably carried without compromising service quality, safety, or passenger comfort. This accounts for realistic operating conditions, dwell times, boarding/alighting times, and passenger load factors.

**Key Distinction:** Practical capacity is the more relevant figure for operational planning.

### 2. Elements Influencing Transit Line Capacity

The capacity of any transit mode is a function of several interconnected elements:

#### 2.1 Vehicle Characteristics

*   **Vehicle Size/Length:** Longer vehicles can carry more passengers.
*   **Passenger Capacity per Vehicle:** The seated and standing capacity of each individual vehicle.
*   **Doorway Width and Number of Doors:** Affects boarding and alighting speed, which impacts dwell time.
*   **Vehicle Acceleration/Deceleration:** Influences travel speed and headway (time between vehicles).
*   **Vehicle Design:** The internal layout and space allocation for standing passengers.

#### 2.2 Service Characteristics

*   **Headway (Time Headway):** The time interval between successive vehicles passing a fixed point. Shorter headways generally lead to higher capacity.
*   **Frequency:** The number of vehicles operating on a route per unit of time (often the inverse of headway).
*   **Speed:**
    *   **Cruising Speed:** The speed achieved between stops.
    *   **Average Speed:** The overall speed including stops, dwell times, and acceleration/deceleration. Higher average speeds can increase capacity if headway is maintained.
*   **Route Layout:**
    *   **Number of Stops:** More stops increase dwell time and reduce average speed.
    *   **Distance Between Stops:** Affects acceleration/deceleration time.
    *   **Grade (Slopes):** Can affect acceleration and speed.
    *   **Curves:** Can limit speed.
*   **Dwell Time:** The time a vehicle spends at a stop for passengers to board and alight. This is a critical factor.
    *   **Factors influencing Dwell Time:**
        *   Number of passengers boarding/alighting.
        *   Boarding/alighting mechanisms (e.g., ticket gates, fare collection systems).
        *   Vehicle door configuration.
        *   Passenger behavior.
        *   Accessibility features (e.g., ramps for wheelchairs).

#### 2.3 Operational Factors

*   **Passenger Load Factor:** The ratio of passengers carried to the maximum capacity of a vehicle. Full vehicles (100% load factor) are assumed for theoretical capacity, but practical capacity considers more realistic load factors.
*   **Blocking:** The tendency for vehicles to bunch together, reducing effective headway and capacity.
*   **Traffic Congestion (for road-based modes):** External factors that affect vehicle speed and reliability.
*   **Signal Priority:** Systems that give transit vehicles priority at intersections can reduce delays and improve capacity.
*   **Crew Availability and Shift Limitations:** Practical constraints on service provision.

### 3. Capacities of Different Transit Modes

Different transit modes have distinct characteristics that result in varying capacity ranges.

#### 3.1 Buses (Road-Based Public Transit)

*   **Characteristics:** Operate on roadways, flexible routes, susceptible to traffic congestion.
*   **Key Capacity Influencers:**
    *   Vehicle length and passenger capacity (typical bus: 40-60 passengers, articulated bus: 60-90 passengers).
    *   Number and width of doors.
    *   Dwell time at stops (significant factor).
    *   Headway (limited by traffic signals, bus stops, and overall road capacity).
    *   Speed and travel time.
    *   Fare collection system (on-board payment significantly increases dwell time).
*   **Capacity Ranges (typical, pax/hr/dir):**
    *   **Standard Bus:** 800 - 2,500 pax/hr/dir
    *   **Articulated Bus:** 1,500 - 3,500 pax/hr/dir
    *   **Bus Rapid Transit (BRT - dedicated lanes, off-board fare collection, platform-level boarding):** Can significantly increase capacity, potentially 5,000 - 10,000+ pax/hr/dir, approaching rail capacities.

**Example:** A bus route with standard buses operating with 2-minute headways, an average dwell time of 15 seconds per stop, and 10 stops per mile, might have a practical capacity of around 1,500 pax/hr/dir.

#### 3.2 Light Rail Transit (LRT)

*   **Characteristics:** Operates on dedicated or semi-dedicated rights-of-way, often shares some infrastructure with road traffic, higher passenger capacity than buses.
*   **Key Capacity Influencers:**
    *   Vehicle length and passenger capacity (trains can consist of multiple cars, increasing total capacity).
    *   Dwell time at stops (generally lower than buses due to off-board fare collection and efficient boarding).
    *   Headway (can be shorter than buses due to dedicated tracks and signals).
    *   Speed and acceleration/deceleration.
    *   Track capacity (signalization, interlocking design).
    *   Platform length at stations.
*   **Capacity Ranges (typical, pax/hr/dir):**
    *   **Single Car LRT:** 1,500 - 3,000 pax/hr/dir
    *   **Multiple Car LRT Trains:** 3,000 - 9,000 pax/hr/dir

**Example:** An LRT system operating with 3-car trains at 3-minute headways, with an average dwell time of 20 seconds at stations, could achieve a capacity of around 6,000 pax/hr/dir.

#### 3.3 Metro/Heavy Rail (Subway, Underground)

*   **Characteristics:** Operates on fully dedicated, grade-separated rights-of-way, high speeds, highest passenger capacity among common transit modes.
*   **Key Capacity Influencers:**
    *   Train length and passenger capacity (can be very long trains).
    *   Dwell time at stations (optimized for fast boarding/alighting).
    *   Headway (can be very short, e.g., 90 seconds or less).
    *   Signal system and track design (allowing for very close train spacing).
    *   Platform length.
    *   Acceleration/deceleration.
*   **Capacity Ranges (typical, pax/hr/dir):**
    *   **Typical Metro:** 10,000 - 40,000+ pax/hr/dir
    *   **Highly Optimized Metro (e.g., London Underground, Tokyo Metro):** Can exceed 60,000 pax/hr/dir

**Example:** A metro line with 8-car trains, 100 seconds headway, and 30 seconds dwell time, can achieve a very high capacity of over 30,000 pax/hr/dir.

#### 3.4 Trams/Streetcars

*   **Characteristics:** Operate on street-level tracks, often share right-of-way with traffic, can be affected by congestion.
*   **Key Capacity Influencers:**
    *   Vehicle length and passenger capacity.
    *   Dwell time at stops.
    *   Headway (often limited by traffic signals and street interactions).
    *   Presence of dedicated lanes or signal priority.
*   **Capacity Ranges (typical, pax/hr/dir):**
    *   **Standard Tram:** 1,000 - 3,000 pax/hr/dir
    *   **Modern Articulated Trams:** 2,000 - 5,000 pax/hr/dir

**Example:** A tram line with modern, high-capacity trams operating with 4-minute headways on streets with moderate traffic, could have a capacity of around 2,500 pax/hr/dir.

#### 3.5 Commuter Rail

*   **Characteristics:** Designed for longer distances and higher speeds, often operate with fewer stops than metro systems, typically serve suburban areas.
*   **Key Capacity Influencers:**
    *   Train length and passenger capacity.
    *   Speed and acceleration/deceleration.
    *   Headway (often longer than metro due to longer distances and lower frequencies).
    *   Station dwell times (can be longer due to fewer, more dispersed stops).
    *   Track capacity, often sharing with freight rail.
*   **Capacity Ranges (typical, pax/hr/dir):**
    *   **Commuter Rail:** 1,000 - 5,000 pax/hr/dir (can be higher on core segments with frequent service)

**Example:** A commuter rail line with 6-car trains operating hourly into a city center might have a capacity of 1,500 pax/hr/dir on the core segment during peak times.

#### 3.6 Ferries

*   **Characteristics:** Water-based transit, capacity depends heavily on vessel size and number of trips.
*   **Key Capacity Influencers:**
    *   Vessel size (passenger and vehicle capacity).
    *   Number of decks.
    *   Speed of the vessel.
    *   Loading/unloading time at terminals.
    *   Number of available vessels.
    *   Terminal capacity.
*   **Capacity Ranges (typical, pax/hr/dir):**
    *   **Small Ferry:** 500 - 2,000 pax/hr/dir
    *   **Large Ferry/Catamaran:** 2,000 - 10,000+ pax/hr/dir

**Example:** A ferry service with large vessels capable of carrying 500 passengers per trip, operating every 30 minutes in both directions, could have a capacity of 2,000 pax/hr/dir.

### 4. Comparing Capacities of Different Modes

| Mode           | Typical Capacity (pax/hr/dir) | Key Advantages for Capacity                                        | Key Limitations for Capacity                                        |
| :------------- | :---------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Buses**      | 800 - 3,500                   | Flexibility, lower infrastructure cost                             | Susceptible to traffic, longer dwell times, lower frequency limits  |
| **BRT**        | 5,000 - 10,000+               | Dedicated lanes, platform boarding, off-board payment              | Requires significant infrastructure investment                      |
| **Trams**      | 1,000 - 5,000                 | Higher capacity than buses, better ride quality                    | Shares ROW with traffic (can limit headway), not grade-separated   |
| **LRT**        | 3,000 - 9,000                 | Dedicated ROW, multiple cars, efficient boarding                   | Higher infrastructure cost than buses, can be affected by signals |
| **Metro/Heavy Rail** | 10,000 - 40,000+              | Fully grade-separated, short headways, long trains, high speed       | Very high infrastructure cost, fixed routes                        |
| **Commuter Rail** | 1,000 - 5,000                 | Higher speeds over longer distances                                | Lower frequency, longer headways, fewer stops                     |
| **Ferries**    | 500 - 10,000+                 | Can overcome geographical barriers, scalable by vessel size        | Limited by waterway availability, terminal capacity, vessel speed |

**General Trend:** Capacity increases with the level of infrastructure dedicated to transit, the length of vehicles, and the efficiency of operations (shorter headways, shorter dwell times).

### 5. Factors Affecting Actual Passenger Throughput

While theoretical capacity provides an upper bound, actual throughput is influenced by:

*   **Demand:** The actual number of passengers seeking to travel. If demand is lower than capacity, the line will not operate at its maximum.
*   **Vehicle Load Factor:** Even if a line *could* move 40,000 passengers, if vehicles are only 50% full, the actual throughput will be less.
*   **Reliability and Punctuality:** Delays and service disruptions reduce effective capacity.
*   **Operational Disruptions:** Accidents, breakdowns, track maintenance, etc.
*   **Seasonal Variations in Demand:** Summer vs. winter, weekdays vs. weekends.
*   **Special Events:** Can cause temporary surges in demand.
*   **Passenger Behavior:** Slow boarding/alighting, fare evasion, blocking doors.
*   **Crew Performance:** Efficiency of drivers and station staff.
*   **Weather Conditions:** Can impact vehicle speed and passenger numbers.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Define "transit line capacity" and explain why it's an important metric in public transportation planning.
**Answer:** Transit line capacity is the maximum number of passengers a transit service can carry along a route per hour per direction. It's important for determining vehicle requirements, service frequency, infrastructure design, and managing passenger flow to ensure efficient and comfortable travel.

**Question 2:** List three key elements that influence the capacity of a bus service.
**Answer:**
1.  Vehicle capacity (number of passengers per bus)
2.  Headway (time interval between buses)
3.  Dwell time at stops (time for boarding/alighting)
*(Other valid answers include bus speed, number of doors, traffic conditions.)*

**Question 3:** Which transit mode generally offers the highest passenger carrying capacity and why?
**Answer:** Metro/Heavy Rail generally offers the highest passenger carrying capacity. This is due to its fully dedicated and grade-separated rights-of-way, allowing for very short headways, long trains, high speeds, and efficient station operations.

**Question 4:** A standard bus has a capacity of 50 passengers. If buses run every 4 minutes (240 seconds) and the average dwell time at each of the 10 stops is 15 seconds, what is the theoretical capacity of this bus line in passengers per hour per direction, assuming full load?
**Answer:**
*   Number of buses per hour = 60 minutes / 4 minutes/bus = 15 buses/hour
*   Theoretical Capacity = Number of buses/hour * Passengers per bus
*   Theoretical Capacity = 15 buses/hour * 50 passengers/bus = 750 passengers/hour/direction.

*(Note: This calculation is a simplified theoretical capacity. It doesn't account for all factors like acceleration/deceleration or the impact of dwell time on achievable headway. A more accurate capacity calculation would consider headway directly, e.g., passengers per bus / headway in hours = 50 / (4/60) = 50 * 15 = 750 pax/hr/dir. The dwell time information, while listed as an element, is more crucial for practical capacity calculation or understanding service limitations.)*

**Question 5:** Explain the difference between theoretical and practical capacity.
**Answer:** Theoretical capacity is the absolute maximum passengers that *could* be carried under ideal, uninterrupted conditions, often assuming full vehicle occupancy. Practical capacity is the maximum number of passengers that can be *realistically* carried while maintaining acceptable service levels, accounting for factors like dwell times, passenger behavior, and operational limitations.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Capacity is a function of multiple variables:** No single factor dictates capacity; it's a complex interplay.
*   **Headway and Dwell Time are Critical:** These two operational parameters have a profound impact on capacity for all modes.
*   **Infrastructure Matters:** Dedicated rights-of-way significantly boost capacity compared to modes sharing road space.
*   **BRT Bridges the Gap:** BRT systems can achieve capacities comparable to LRT by adopting rail-like operational features.
*   **Practicality is Key:** Always consider practical capacity for real-world planning and operations.
*   **Demand Drives Actual Throughput:** Capacity is an upper limit; actual passengers carried depend on demand.
