# Module 2: Contract Documentation - Form of Agreement & Hierarchy of Terms and Conditions & Typical Structure of Contract - Preamble

## 1. Form of Agreement

The "form of agreement" refers to the **legal structure and format** in which a contract is documented. It dictates how the parties express their mutual consent and create legally binding obligations. The choice of form significantly impacts the enforceability and clarity of the contract.

### Key Concepts & Definitions:

*   **Express Contract:** An agreement where the terms are explicitly stated, either orally or in writing.
    *   **Written Contract:** Preferred for its clarity, evidence, and enforceability.
    *   **Oral Contract (Parol Contract):** Valid but difficult to prove and enforce, especially for significant transactions. Certain types of contracts (e.g., those involving real estate) *must* be in writing due to statutory requirements (Statute of Frauds).
*   **Implied Contract:** An agreement inferred from the conduct of the parties.
    *   **Implied-in-Fact Contract:** Arises from the actions or conduct of the parties, suggesting they intended to create a contract.
        *   **Example:** Regularly ordering coffee from a shop implies an agreement to pay for each cup.
    *   **Implied-in-Law Contract (Quasi-Contract):** Not a true contract, but a legal obligation imposed by a court to prevent unjust enrichment.
        *   **Example:** A doctor providing emergency medical care to an unconscious patient creates an obligation for the patient to pay for the services, even without explicit agreement.
*   **Formal Contract:** A contract that requires a specific form to be legally valid, such as contracts under seal (historically important but less common now) or negotiable instruments.
*   **Informal Contract (Simple Contract):** A contract that does not require a specific form and can be oral, written, or implied, as long as it has the essential elements of a contract (offer, acceptance, consideration, intention to create legal relations, capacity).

### Importance of Form:

*   **Evidence:** Written contracts provide clear evidence of the agreement's terms.
*   **Enforceability:** Certain contracts *must* be in writing to be enforceable by law.
*   **Clarity & Precision:** Writing helps avoid ambiguity and misunderstandings.
*   **Record Keeping:** Facilitates future reference and audit.

## 2. Hierarchy of Terms and Conditions

In many complex contracts, multiple documents might be referenced or attached. The "hierarchy of terms and conditions" establishes **which document takes precedence** in case of a conflict or inconsistency between them. This prevents disputes and ensures that the parties' intentions are clear.

### Key Concepts & Definitions:

*   **Priority Clause/Supremacy Clause:** A specific clause within the contract that explicitly defines the order of precedence among various contract documents.
*   **Conflicting Terms:** When different provisions in different documents contradict each other.
*   **Governing Document:** The document that is considered the primary source of obligations and rights.

### Typical Hierarchy (Example):

The order can vary depending on the contract's nature and industry, but a common hierarchy might look like this:

1.  **The Agreement/Contract Core:** The main body of the contract signed by the parties. This often contains the most critical, specific, and negotiated terms.
2.  **Amendments/Addenda:** Modifications or additions to the original agreement, usually signed or agreed upon by both parties. These typically supersede earlier provisions they amend.
3.  **Schedules/Appendices/Exhibits:** Documents attached to the contract that provide detailed information, specifications, pricing, or technical requirements.
    *   **Example:** A project plan, detailed scope of work, pricing tables, technical drawings.
4.  **General Terms and Conditions (GTCs) / Standard Terms:** Pre-prepared sets of terms often used by one party.
    *   **Example:** Standard purchase order terms, standard service agreement terms.
5.  **Statements of Work (SOWs):** Detailed descriptions of the specific work to be performed under a contract, often used in service or project-based agreements.
6.  **Policies and Procedures:** Referenced documents outlining company policies or industry standards.
    *   **Example:** A company's data privacy policy, relevant ISO standards.

### How it Works:

*   The priority clause will state that if there is a conflict between, for instance, the "Agreement Core" and an "Exhibit," the "Agreement Core" will prevail.
*   Conversely, if an "Amendment" specifically modifies a term in the "Agreement Core," the "Amendment" will take precedence for that specific term.

### **IMPORTANT POINT TO REMEMBER:**

*   **Clarity is paramount.** A well-drafted priority clause is crucial to avoid disputes.
*   **Each document should be clearly identified and dated** within the main agreement.
*   **Ambiguity in the hierarchy can lead to costly litigation.**

## 3. Typical Structure of a Contract

While contract structures can vary, most contracts follow a logical flow to present information clearly and comprehensively. Understanding this structure helps in drafting, reviewing, and managing contracts effectively.

### Key Sections:

1.  **Title:** Clearly states the type of agreement (e.g., "Service Agreement," "Sales Contract," "Lease Agreement").
2.  **Preamble/Recitals (The "Whereas" Clauses):** Background information setting the context for the agreement.
3.  **Parties:** Identifies the legal names and addresses of the individuals or entities entering into the contract.
4.  **Definitions:** Defines key terms used throughout the contract to ensure consistent interpretation.
5.  **Core Obligations/Agreement:** The heart of the contract, detailing what each party agrees to do or provide.
    *   Scope of Work/Goods/Services
    *   Deliverables
    *   Pricing and Payment Terms
    *   Timelines and Delivery Dates
6.  **Representations and Warranties:** Statements of fact made by each party, which are assumed to be true.
7.  **Covenants:** Promises made by the parties to do or refrain from doing certain things during the contract term.
8.  **Term and Termination:** Specifies the duration of the contract and the conditions under which it can be ended prematurely.
9.  **Confidentiality:** Outlines obligations regarding the protection of sensitive information.
10. **Intellectual Property:** Addresses ownership and usage rights of any intellectual property created or used.
11. **Indemnification:** Provisions where one party agrees to compensate the other for losses or damages.
12. **Limitation of Liability:** Caps or restricts the extent of liability for certain types of damages.
13. **Governing Law and Dispute Resolution:** Specifies which jurisdiction's laws will apply and how disputes will be settled (e.g., arbitration, mediation, litigation).
14. **Miscellaneous/General Provisions:** Includes standard clauses that cover various aspects.
    *   Force Majeure (acts of God)
    *   Notices
    *   Assignment
    *   Entire Agreement Clause (Merger Clause)
    *   Severability
    *   Waiver
    *   Amendments
15. **Signatures:** Spaces for authorized representatives of each party to sign, date, and indicate their title, making the contract legally binding.
16. **Attachments/Exhibits/Schedules:** Referenced documents containing detailed information.

## 4. Preamble (Recitals/Whereas Clauses)

The Preamble, also known as the Recitals or "Whereas" clauses, is the introductory section of a contract. It provides context, background information, and the underlying reasons for entering into the agreement. While traditionally not considered operative parts of the contract, they can be crucial for **interpreting the parties' intentions** when the operative clauses are ambiguous.

### Key Concepts & Definitions:

*   **Recitals:** Non-binding statements that set the stage for the contract.
*   **"Whereas" Clauses:** The typical phrasing used to introduce each recital.
*   **Contextual Information:** Details about the parties, their previous dealings, the purpose of the contract, and the circumstances leading to its creation.
*   **Intent of the Parties:** Recitals can shed light on what the parties intended to achieve with the contract.

### Purpose and Function:

*   **Setting the Background:** Explains who the parties are and why they are entering into the agreement.
*   **Stating the Purpose:** Clearly articulates the objectives and goals of the contract.
*   **Providing Context:** Offers insight into prior negotiations, relevant events, or assumptions made by the parties.
*   **Aiding Interpretation:** Courts may refer to recitals to understand the meaning of ambiguous operative clauses. They can help resolve disputes by clarifying the parties' shared understanding.
*   **Establishing Good Faith:** Demonstrates the parties' genuine intent to enter into a legal relationship.

### Structure and Wording:

*   Typically begins with a statement like: "This Agreement is made and entered into on this [Day] day of [Month], [Year], by and between..."
*   Followed by a series of "WHEREAS" clauses, each starting with "WHEREAS," and providing a factual statement.
*   Each "WHEREAS" clause is usually a separate paragraph.
*   Ends with a transition clause, such as: "NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants and agreements set forth herein, and for other good and valuable consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged, the Parties agree as follows:"

### Example:

```
THIS SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT (the "Agreement") is made and entered into as of this 15th day of October, 2023,

BY AND BETWEEN:

[Company Name A], a corporation organized and existing under the laws of [State/Country], with its principal place of business at [Address] (hereinafter referred to as "Client"),

AND

[Company Name B], a corporation organized and existing under the laws of [State/Country], with its principal place of business at [Address] (hereinafter referred to as "Developer").

WHEREAS, Client requires the development of a custom mobile application for its business operations;

WHEREAS, Developer is in the business of providing custom software development services and possesses the necessary expertise and resources to undertake such a project;

WHEREAS, Client desires to engage Developer to develop the aforementioned mobile application, and Developer is willing to provide such services on the terms and conditions set forth in this Agreement;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual promises and covenants contained herein, and for other good and valuable consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged, the Parties hereto agree as follows:
```

### **IMPORTANT POINT TO REMEMBER:**

*   While recitals are generally not legally binding in themselves, they are crucial for **interpreting the intent of the parties** when the operative clauses are unclear or ambiguous.
*   Ensure recitals accurately reflect the factual basis and purpose of the contract.
*   Avoid introducing operative terms or obligations within the recitals. They should be descriptive and background-oriented.

## 6. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**

b) Oral Contract. Oral contracts are valid but difficult to prove, making them less preferred for significant transactions where clear documentation is essential.

**Answer 2:**

c) Priority Clause. This clause dictates which document governs if there are conflicting terms.

**Answer 3:**

b) The Agreement/Contract Core. The main body of the contract, often containing the most heavily negotiated terms, typically holds a higher position than attached schedules or standard terms.

**Answer 4:**

c) To provide background information and context for the agreement, aiding in interpretation. Recitals set the stage and can help clarify the parties' intentions if operative clauses are ambiguous.

**Answer 5:**

The delivery date specified in the main "Agreement Core" is likely to take precedence due to the priority clause stating that the Agreement prevails over any Exhibit in case of conflict. However, if the milestone delivery date in Exhibit A was crucial to the overall timely delivery, and the "Agreement Core" was silent or vague on this specific milestone, there could still be room for interpretation or dispute. The clearest approach would be to ensure consistency or explicit amendment.

**Answer 6:**

*   **Implied-in-Fact Contract:** Arises from the actions and conduct of the parties, indicating their intention to enter into an agreement.
    *   **Example:** A customer regularly visits a barber shop and gets a haircut. Their conduct implies an agreement to pay for the service, even if they don't explicitly say "I agree to pay" each time.
*   **Implied-in-Law Contract (Quasi-Contract):** A legal obligation imposed by a court to prevent unjust enrichment, rather than being based on the parties' agreement.
    *   **Example:** A person suffers a medical emergency and is taken to a hospital. The hospital provides life-saving treatment. The patient, even though unconscious and unable to agree, has a legal obligation to pay for the services because it would be unjust for them to receive such essential care without compensation.

## Important Points to Remember (Summary):

*   **Form matters:** Always prefer written contracts for clarity and enforceability.
*   **Hierarchy prevents chaos:** Clearly define the order of precedence for all contract documents to avoid disputes.
*   **Structure guides understanding:** Familiarize yourself with the typical structure of a contract to review and manage them effectively.
*   **Preamble provides context:** While not usually binding, recitals are vital for interpreting the parties' intentions.
*   **Clarity in drafting:** Ambiguity in any part of the contract, including hierarchy and preamble, can lead to costly problems.
