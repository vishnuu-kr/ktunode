## CIVIL ENGINEERING MODELLING LAB: Module 2 - Double Storeyed Residential Building with Sloped Roof

### Topic: Prepare a file with hardcopies of drawings ready to submit for approval from the authorities.

---

### **1. Overview**

This module focuses on the practical application of architectural and structural design principles to create a set of official drawings for a double-storeyed residential building. These drawings are intended for submission to local municipal authorities for planning permission and building approval. The process involves translating design requirements into clear, accurate, and standardized graphical representations.

Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   **Understand the purpose and importance of official architectural drawings.**
*   **Interpret and utilize given requirements for a residential building and plot.**

### **3. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Architectural Drawings:** Technical drawings that represent the design of a building or structure. They include plans, sections, elevations, and site plans.
*   **Site Plan:** A bird's-eye view of the property showing the building's location, property lines, setbacks, access roads, utilities, landscaping, and other relevant site features.
*   **Floor Plan:** A scaled diagram representing the layout of a building at a specific level, viewed from above. It shows walls, doors, windows, rooms, stairs, and furniture placement.
*   **Sectional View (Section):** A drawing that cuts through a building to reveal its internal construction and spatial relationships. It shows the arrangement of floors, walls, roofs, and structural elements.
*   **Elevation View:** A drawing that shows the exterior appearance of a building from a specific direction (e.g., North Elevation, South Elevation). It depicts the building's façade, including windows, doors, rooflines, and materials.
*   **Scale:** The ratio between the size of the drawing and the actual size of the object. Common scales for architectural drawings include 1:50, 1:100, 1:200.
*   **Setbacks:** The minimum required distance between a building and the property lines, streets, or other structures, as mandated by local zoning regulations.
*   **Building Bye-Laws/Regulations:** Rules and standards set by local authorities that govern building design, construction, safety, and land use. These are crucial for submission.
*   **Line Weights:** Different thicknesses of lines used in drawings to convey information about the visibility and importance of elements. For example, visible outlines are typically thicker than hidden lines.
*   **Annotation:** Text and labels added to drawings to provide specific information, dimensions, material specifications, and notes.
*   **Title Block:** A designated area on the drawing sheet containing essential information such as the project title, drawing title, scale, date, drawing number, designer's name/firm, and approval status.

### **4. Requirements for a Double Storeyed Residential Building with Sloped Roof**

**General Requirements (Hypothetical Example - Always refer to specific problem statement):**

*   **Plot Size:** 15m x 20m (300 sq. m)
*   **Building Type:** Double-storeyed residential house.
*   **Occupancy:** Family of 4-5 members.
*   **Ground Floor:**
    *   Living Room (approx. 4m x 5m)
    *   Dining Area (integrated with living or separate, approx. 3m x 4m)
    *   Kitchen (approx. 3m x 3.5m)
    *   One Bedroom with attached toilet/bathroom (approx. 3.5m x 4m)
    *   Common Toilet/Bathroom (approx. 2m x 2m)
    *   Staircase (internal, centrally located or adjacent to living/dining)
    *   Verandah/Porch at the front.
*   **First Floor:**
    *   Two Bedrooms with attached toilets/bathrooms (approx. 3.5m x 4m each).
    *   One additional common toilet/bathroom or a family lounge.
    *   Balconies attached to bedrooms or facing the front/rear.
*   **Roof:** Sloped roof with moderate pitch (e.g., 1:2 or 1:3 slope) for rain and heat deflection. Material to be considered (e.g., Mangalore tiles, concrete tiles).
*   **Setbacks:** As per local bye-laws (e.g., Front: 3m, Rear: 3m, Sides: 1.5m each).
*   **Building Bye-laws:** Adherence to local building codes for plinth height, floor area ratio (FAR), ventilation, light, fire safety, etc.
*   **Utilities:** Provision for water supply, drainage, electricity.

### **5. Drawing Preparation and Submission File**

The submission file will typically consist of the following drawings, neatly organized and presented:

#### **5.1. Site Plan**

*   **Purpose:** To show the context of the building within the property and its surroundings.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   **Property Boundaries:** Clearly delineated with dimensions.
    *   **Building Footprint:** Outline of the proposed building on the plot.
    *   **Setback Lines:** Indicating the minimum distances from property boundaries.
    *   **Access:** Driveways, pathways, pedestrian access.
    *   **Open Spaces:** Lawn areas, gardens, paved areas.
    *   **Utilities:** Location of water source, septic tank/sewer connection, electrical meter.
    *   **Existing Features:** Roads, neighbouring buildings, trees (if significant).
    *   **North Arrow:** Essential for orientation.
    *   **Scale:** Clearly indicated (e.g., 1:200 or 1:500).
    *   **Title:** "SITE PLAN" with project name and plot number.
    *   **Datum:** Reference point for levels.
*   **Example Scenario:** Imagine a 15m x 20m plot. If setbacks are 3m front/rear and 1.5m sides, the buildable area will be reduced. The site plan will show the plot edges, the setback lines within, and the positioned building footprint with its access from the road.

#### **5.2. Floor Plans**

*   **Purpose:** To illustrate the layout and dimensions of each floor.
*   **Key Elements (for each floor - Ground Floor, First Floor):**
    *   **Walls:** External and internal walls with their thickness.
    *   **Doors:** Shown with swing direction.
    *   **Windows:** Indicated in walls.
    *   **Rooms:** Labeled with their names (Living, Kitchen, Bedroom, Toilet, etc.) and dimensions.
    *   **Staircase:** Detailed with riser and tread dimensions, direction of travel.
    *   **Fixtures:** Sanitary fittings in toilets, kitchen counters.
    *   **Furniture Layout (Optional but recommended):** To show spatial usability.
    *   **Dimensions:** All critical dimensions (wall lengths, room sizes, door/window locations).
    *   **Levels:** Plinth level, floor levels.
    *   **North Arrow:** Consistent with the site plan.
    *   **Scale:** Clearly indicated (e.g., 1:50 or 1:100).
    *   **Title:** "GROUND FLOOR PLAN", "FIRST FLOOR PLAN".
*   **Example Scenario:** For the Ground Floor, the plan would show the living room, dining area, kitchen, one bedroom with attached toilet, common toilet, and the staircase. Dimensions for each room, wall thicknesses, and door/window openings would be provided.

#### **5.3. Sectional Views (Sections)**

*   **Purpose:** To reveal the internal construction and vertical arrangement of the building.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   **Cutting Plane Line:** Shown on the floor plans, indicating where the section is taken.
    *   **Foundation:** Showing the type of foundation (e.g., isolated footings, raft foundation).
    *   **Plinth Beam:** If provided.
    *   **Floor Slabs/Decks:** Showing their thickness and construction.
    *   **Walls:** Showing their construction material and thickness.
    *   **Roof Structure:** Illustrating the sloped roof framing, purlins, rafters, and roofing material.
    *   **Staircase:** Detailed internal view of the staircase.
    *   **Doors and Windows:** Shown in their openings.
    *   **Levels:** Plinth level, floor levels, ceiling levels, roof levels.
    *   **Materials:** Indication of different construction materials.
    *   **Scale:** Clearly indicated (e.g., 1:50 or 1:100).
    *   **Title:** "SECTION A-A", "SECTION B-B" (corresponding to cutting plane lines).
*   **Example Scenario:** A section cut through the living room and a bedroom would reveal the foundation, plinth beam, ground floor slab, first-floor slab, walls, and the pitched roof structure with its supporting elements.

#### **5.4. Elevation Views**

*   **Purpose:** To show the exterior appearance of the building from different sides.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   **Facades:** Typically North, South, East, and West elevations.
    *   **Architectural Features:** Windows, doors, balconies, parapets, rooflines, decorative elements.
    *   **Materials and Textures:** Representation of external finishes.
    *   **Levels:** Ground level, plinth level, floor levels, roof levels, height of openings.
    *   **Dimensions:** Heights of floors, window sill levels, lintel levels, overall height.
    *   **North Arrow:** Consistent.
    *   **Scale:** Clearly indicated (e.g., 1:50 or 1:100).
    *   **Title:** "NORTH ELEVATION", "SOUTH ELEVATION", etc.
*   **Example Scenario:** The front elevation would show the main entrance, the ground floor windows, first-floor windows, balconies, and the profile of the sloped roof from the front.

#### **5.5. Title Block and General Notes**

*   **Title Block:** Must contain all essential project and drawing information as mentioned earlier.
*   **General Notes:** Important information not explicitly shown in the drawings but crucial for construction and approval, such as:
    *   "All dimensions are in millimeters unless otherwise stated."
    *   "All materials and workmanship shall conform to relevant IS codes and local building bye-laws."
    *   "Plinth level to be maintained at X mm above the average ground level."
    *   "Ventilation and light requirements as per bye-laws are satisfied."
    *   "Waterproofing of toilets and roof to be ensured."

#### **5.6. Assembling the File**

*   **Order of Drawings:** Typically, the Site Plan comes first, followed by Ground Floor Plan, First Floor Plan, Sections, and Elevations.
*   **Sheet Size and Layout:** Standard drawing sheets (e.g., A1, A2, A3) should be used, with consistent margins and title block placement.
*   **Hardcopies:** All drawings must be printed clearly on good quality paper.
*   **Binding:** Drawings should be neatly bound in a professional manner, usually in a folder or file cover.
*   **Labeling:** The file itself should be clearly labeled with the project name, owner's name, and applicant's details.

### **6. Understanding Symbology and Conventions**

*   **Lines:**
    *   **Visible Lines (Thick Continuous):** For edges seen by the observer.
    *   **Hidden Lines (Dashed):** For edges hidden from view.
    *   **Center Lines (Long and Short Dashes):** For axes of symmetry.
    *   **Dimension Lines (Thin Continuous with Arrowheads):** To show sizes.
    *   **Extension Lines (Thin Continuous):** Extend from the object to the dimension line.
    *   **Cutting Plane Lines (Thick with Arrowheads):** Indicate the location of a section.
*   **Doors:** Hinged or sliding, with a sweep arc showing the direction of opening.
*   **Windows:** Represented by lines indicating the frame and glass.
*   **Stairs:** Shown with direction arrows and typically nosing lines.
*   **Materials:** Hatching patterns (e.g., cross-hatching for concrete, diagonal lines for brickwork) can be used, though often specified in notes.
*   **Sanitary Fittings:** Standard symbols for W.C., washbasins, sinks, etc.

### **7. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
You are given a plot of 18m x 25m. The local bye-laws stipulate front setback of 4m, rear setback of 3m, and side setbacks of 2m on both sides. If you plan to construct a double-storeyed residential building with a maximum ground coverage of 40%, what will be the buildable area? Draw a conceptual site plan showing the plot boundaries, setback lines, and the building footprint.

**Answer 1:**
*   Plot Area = 18m x 25m = 450 sq. m
*   Maximum Ground Coverage = 40% of 450 sq. m = 0.40 * 450 = 180 sq. m
*   Buildable Area (considering setbacks):
    *   Usable width = 18m - 2m (left) - 2m (right) = 14m
    *   Usable length = 25m - 4m (front) - 3m (rear) = 18m
    *   Maximum buildable area considering setbacks = 14m x 18m = 252 sq. m
*   Since the maximum ground coverage allowed is 180 sq. m, the building footprint must not exceed this area.
*   **Conceptual Site Plan:** The plan would show the 18x25m outer rectangle representing the plot. Inside this, parallel lines would be drawn at 4m from one 18m side (front), 3m from the opposite 18m side (rear), and 2m from the 25m sides (sides). The building footprint (a rectangle or any other shape) would be placed within these setback lines, not exceeding 180 sq. m in area. An access road from one side and a North arrow would also be shown.

**Question 2:**
For a typical bedroom with dimensions 3.5m x 4m, draw a simplified floor plan representation including:
a) One door (0.9m wide) on one of the longer walls.
b) One window (1.2m wide) on one of the shorter walls.
c) Indicate dimensions and room label.
d) Show a typical wall thickness (e.g., 0.23m for external walls).

**Answer 2:**
*   **Representation:** A rectangle of 3.5m x 4m.
*   **Walls:** Outer walls drawn with a thickness of 0.23m.
*   **Door:** Placed on a 4m wall, showing its position and a 0.9m opening arc.
*   **Window:** Placed on a 3.5m wall, showing its position and width.
*   **Dimensions:** The 3.5m and 4m dimensions would be clearly marked. The position of the door and window along the wall would also be dimensioned.
*   **Label:** "BEDROOM" written inside the rectangle.

**Question 3:**
Describe the essential components that must be present in a title block for an official building plan submission.

**Answer 3:**
The essential components of a title block for official building plan submission include:
*   **Project Name/Title:** e.g., "Proposed Residential Building for Mr. XYZ"
*   **Drawing Title:** e.g., "Ground Floor Plan," "Site Plan," "Section A-A"
*   **Drawing Number:** Unique identifier for the drawing.
*   **Scale:** e.g., 1:100, 1:50.
*   **Date of Drawing:** When the drawing was prepared or last revised.
*   **Designer/Architect/Engineer's Name and Signature:** The professional responsible for the design.
*   **Client's Name/Owner's Name:** The person or entity for whom the building is being designed.
*   **Plot Number/Address:** Location of the proposed project.
*   **Sheet Number:** e.g., Sheet 1 of 5.
*   **Approvals/Revisions Block (Optional but common):** For tracking changes and approvals.
*   **Local Authority Name/Logo:** Indicating the governing body.

### **8. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Accuracy is Paramount:** All dimensions, lines, and symbols must be precise and to scale.
*   **Clarity and Legibility:** Drawings should be easy to read and understand, with clear line weights and legible text.
*   **Consistency:** Maintain consistency in line types, symbols, and text styles across all drawings.
*   **Adherence to Bye-Laws:** Thoroughly understand and comply with all local building bye-laws and regulations. This is the most critical aspect for approval.
*   **Professional Presentation:** Neatness, proper formatting, and organized assembly of drawings in the file are crucial for a positive impression.
*   **North Arrow:** Always include a North arrow on plans and elevations for orientation.
*   **Scales:** Clearly state the scale of each drawing. Ensure the scale is appropriate for the level of detail shown.
*   **Annotation:** Use clear and concise annotations to provide all necessary information.
*   **Title Block Completeness:** Ensure the title block contains all required information accurately.
*   **Review:** Before submission, carefully review all drawings for errors or omissions.
