## Module 4: Planning and Drawing of Public Buildings

### Topic: Plan a public building – office complex, public health centre, commercial, educational, post office, bank and draw the plan, section and elevation: Any two.

---

### 1. Introduction to Public Buildings and their Planning

*   **Definition:** Public buildings are structures that serve a community or a significant portion of the population. They are typically funded by public money and are accessible to the general public.
*   **Importance of Planning:** Effective planning is crucial for public buildings to ensure functionality, safety, accessibility, sustainability, and aesthetic appeal. It involves considering the needs of the users, the surrounding environment, and future expansion.
*   **Key Considerations in Public Building Planning:**
    *   **Purpose and Functionality:** What activities will take place in the building? How can the layout optimize these activities?
    *   **User Needs:** Who will use the building? What are their specific requirements (e.g., accessibility for disabled individuals, age-appropriateness for educational institutions)?
    *   **Site Analysis:** Understanding the topography, soil conditions, climate, access roads, and surrounding infrastructure.
    *   **Building Codes and Regulations:** Adherence to local, regional, and national building codes for safety, fire, ventilation, etc.
    *   **Sustainability:** Incorporating energy efficiency, water conservation, use of sustainable materials, and waste management.
    *   **Aesthetics and Urban Context:** How the building fits into the urban landscape and its visual impact.
    *   **Budgetary Constraints:** Balancing desired features with available financial resources.
    *   **Accessibility:** Designing for people of all abilities, including ramps, wide doorways, accessible restrooms, and clear signage.
    *   **Security:** Implementing appropriate security measures for the building's purpose.

### 2. Types of Public Buildings and their Specific Requirements

This module focuses on planning and drawing **any two** from the following:

*   **Office Complex:**
    *   **Purpose:** Housing multiple businesses or government departments, providing workspace for employees.
    *   **Key Spaces:** Reception area, individual offices, meeting rooms, conference rooms, common areas, restrooms, pantry/kitchenette, server rooms, storage.
    *   **Considerations:** Efficient circulation, natural light, acoustic privacy, flexibility for different office layouts, sufficient parking, potential for future expansion.
*   **Public Health Centre:**
    *   **Purpose:** Providing healthcare services to the community.
    *   **Key Spaces:** Waiting areas, reception/registration, consultation rooms (doctor's chambers), examination rooms, treatment rooms, minor operation theatre, pharmacy, laboratory, staff rooms, restrooms, ambulance bay.
    *   **Considerations:** Strict hygiene and sanitation standards, patient flow, privacy, accessibility for patients with mobility issues, clear signage, adequate ventilation and lighting.
*   **Commercial Building:**
    *   **Purpose:** Retail sales, services, entertainment, and sometimes office space.
    *   **Key Spaces:** Retail shops, showrooms, restaurants, cafes, entertainment zones (cinemas, arcades), food courts, common circulation areas (corridors, escalators, elevators), restrooms, parking.
    *   **Considerations:** High foot traffic, visibility, accessibility, security, ventilation, lighting, signage, customer comfort, emergency exits.
*   **Educational Building (e.g., School, College):**
    *   **Purpose:** Providing a learning environment for students.
    *   **Key Spaces:** Classrooms, lecture halls, laboratories (science, computer), library, administrative offices, staff rooms, assembly hall/auditorium, sports facilities, cafeteria, playgrounds, restrooms.
    *   **Considerations:** Age-appropriateness, safety, natural light and ventilation in classrooms, acoustic considerations, accessibility for students with disabilities, space for extracurricular activities.
*   **Post Office:**
    *   **Purpose:** Providing postal services, mail handling, and financial services (in some cases).
    *   **Key Spaces:** Public counter area, mail sorting and processing area, storage for mail, customer waiting area, administrative offices, restrooms.
    *   **Considerations:** Efficient workflow for mail handling, secure storage, public access and waiting comfort, clear counter service design.
*   **Bank:**
    *   **Purpose:** Providing financial services, including deposits, withdrawals, loans, and advisory services.
    *   **Key Spaces:** Public lobby/waiting area, teller counters, manager's office, private meeting rooms, safe deposit boxes, ATM lobby, back-office operations area, restrooms.
    *   **Considerations:** Security (vault, surveillance), privacy for customer transactions, efficient customer service flow, accessibility, professional ambiance.

### 3. Drawing Techniques for Building Plans, Sections, and Elevations

This section covers the fundamental drawing techniques required for representing a building design.

#### 3.1. Architectural Plans

*   **Definition:** A plan is a 2D drawing representing a horizontal slice through a building at a specific height (usually around 1.0m to 1.5m above the floor level). It shows the layout of walls, doors, windows, rooms, and other architectural elements.
*   **Key Elements in a Plan:**
    *   **Walls:** Shown as thick lines. Different types of walls (load-bearing, partition) might be represented by different line weights or patterns.
    *   **Doors:** Represented by a line indicating the door panel and an arc showing the swing direction.
    *   **Windows:** Shown as openings in walls, often with lines indicating the glass and frame.
    *   **Stairs:** Depicted with steps and a directional arrow indicating the direction of ascent.
    *   **Fixtures:** Sanitary fittings (toilets, sinks), furniture, and equipment are shown symbolically.
    *   **Dimensions:** All important dimensions (wall lengths, room sizes, door/window openings) are indicated.
    *   **Labels:** Rooms are labeled with their names and often their areas.
    *   **North Arrow:** Indicates the orientation of the building on the site.
    *   **Scale:** The drawing is produced to a specific scale (e.g., 1:50, 1:100).
    *   **Grid Lines:** Often included to help locate structural elements and coordinate different parts of the building.
*   **Types of Plans:**
    *   **Floor Plan:** Shows the layout of a specific floor.
    *   **Site Plan:** Shows the building's position on the property, including access roads, landscaping, and adjacent structures.
    *   **Foundation Plan:** Details the foundation system.
    *   **Roof Plan:** Shows the layout of the roof.

#### 3.2. Architectural Sections

*   **Definition:** A section is a 2D drawing representing a vertical slice through a building. It reveals the internal structure, heights of different levels, relationship between floors, and details of construction elements like foundations, floors, walls, and roofs.
*   **Key Elements in a Section:**
    *   **Cutting Plane Line:** Indicated on the plan with arrows showing the direction of view.
    *   **Visible Surfaces:** Surfaces cut by the plane are shown with thick, dark lines.
    *   **Hidden Lines:** Elements beyond the cutting plane are shown with dashed lines.
    *   **Vertical Dimensions:** Heights of rooms, floor slabs, beams, and roof elements.
    *   **Structural Elements:** Foundations, columns, beams, slabs, lintels, sills.
    *   **Fixtures and Finishes:** Details of stairs, sanitary fittings, and wall/floor finishes can be shown.
    *   **Labels:** Levels, room heights, and material indications.

#### 3.3. Architectural Elevations

*   **Definition:** An elevation is a 2D drawing representing the exterior (or sometimes interior) of a building as seen from one side. It shows the form, facade treatment, window and door openings, roofline, and overall appearance.
*   **Key Elements in an Elevation:**
    *   **External Features:** Windows, doors, balconies, decorative elements, roof shape.
    *   **Materials and Textures:** Indication of different finishes (brickwork, plaster, glass).
    *   **Vertical Dimensions:** Heights of floors, window sills, lintels, and overall building height.
    *   **Ground Line:** Indicates the level of the ground.
    *   **Projection of Roofs and Balconies:** How these elements extend outwards.
    *   **Labels:** Materials, levels, and significant architectural features.
*   **Types of Elevations:** Typically, front elevation, rear elevation, and side elevations are drawn.

### 4. Practical Exercise: Planning and Drawing of Two Public Buildings

**Objective:** To apply the learned principles to plan and draw the Plan, Section, and Elevation of any two selected public buildings.

**Steps:**

1.  **Choose Two Building Types:** Select any two from the list: Office Complex, Public Health Centre, Commercial Building, Educational Building, Post Office, Bank.
2.  **Define Building Requirements:**
    *   **Purpose:** Clearly state the primary function of each building.
    *   **Size/Scale:** Determine the approximate size and capacity (e.g., number of offices, number of consultation rooms, retail area).
    *   **Key Spaces:** List all essential rooms and areas required for each building.
    *   **Site Context (Conceptual):** Imagine a basic site context – for example, a flat plot with road access.
3.  **Conceptual Design and Layout:**
    *   Sketch out rough layouts for each building, considering:
        *   Functional zoning (public areas, private areas, service areas).
        *   Circulation paths (access to all areas).
        *   Natural light and ventilation.
        *   Adjacency of related spaces (e.g., waiting area near reception).
        *   Accessibility.
4.  **Develop Detailed Drawings:**
    *   **Plan:** Draw a scaled floor plan for each building, showing walls, doors, windows, internal layout, dimensions, and labels.
    *   **Section:** Select a significant cutting line on the plan and draw a corresponding section, showing vertical relationships and structural elements.
    *   **Elevation:** Draw at least two principal elevations (e.g., front and side) for each building, showing external features and materials.
5.  **Add Annotations and Legends:** Ensure all drawings are properly dimensioned, labeled, and include a title block with the building name, scale, and drawing type. Use a clear legend for any symbols or line types used.

### 5. Example Walkthrough (Hypothetical)

Let's consider planning a **Small Office Complex** and a **Basic Public Health Centre**.

#### Example 1: Small Office Complex (G+1)

*   **Purpose:** To house a few small businesses, each occupying a floor.
*   **Key Spaces (Ground Floor):** Reception, waiting area, two small office units, one large office unit, men's and women's restrooms, small pantry, circulation (corridor).
*   **Key Spaces (First Floor):** Four small office units, men's and women's restrooms, circulation (corridor).
*   **Site:** Rectangular plot with road access on one side.

**Drawing Elements to Focus On:**

*   **Plan:**
    *   Main entrance leading to a reception and waiting area.
    *   Clear demarcation of office units.
    *   Efficient corridor layout for access.
    *   Placement of restrooms.
    *   Staircase location for vertical circulation.
    *   Dimensions of rooms and overall building.
*   **Section:**
    *   Show the ground floor slab, first-floor slab, and roof slab.
    *   Vertical heights of each floor.
    *   Window openings at sill and lintel levels.
    *   Staircase profile.
    *   Foundation depth (simplified).
*   **Elevation:**
    *   Facade treatment, window styles, and placement.
    *   Main entrance design.
    *   Roof overhang.

#### Example 2: Basic Public Health Centre

*   **Purpose:** To provide basic outpatient medical services.
*   **Key Spaces:** Waiting area, reception/registration, doctor's consultation room (2), examination room (1), minor treatment room (1), pharmacy counter, dispensary, public restrooms, staff room.
*   **Site:** Rectangular plot with road access and space for a small waiting yard.

**Drawing Elements to Focus On:**

*   **Plan:**
    *   Entrance leading to a spacious waiting area.
    *   Organized flow from reception to consultation/treatment rooms.
    *   Privacy for consultation rooms.
    *   Placement of pharmacy and dispensary for easy access.
    *   Adequate restroom facilities.
    *   Consideration for ambulance access (if applicable).
*   **Section:**
    *   Floor levels, ceiling heights.
    *   Window details.
    *   Details of medical equipment placement (symbolic).
    *   Roof structure.
*   **Elevation:**
    *   Clean and functional facade.
    *   Clear signage for the health centre.
    *   Window and door arrangements for natural light and ventilation.

### 6. Key Concepts and Definitions Recap

*   **Scale:** The ratio of a distance on a drawing to the corresponding distance on the actual building.
*   **Orthographic Projection:** A method of projection used in technical drawings where parallel lines of sight are projected onto projection planes perpendicular to them. This is the basis for plans, sections, and elevations.
*   **Section Line:** A line drawn on a plan to indicate where a section is taken.
*   **Elevation Line:** A line on a plan showing the direction from which an elevation is viewed.
*   **Building Envelope:** The exterior surfaces of a building that enclose conditioned space.
*   **Circulation:** The movement of people within and around a building.
*   **Zoning:** The division of a building's interior into different functional areas.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Describe the key elements you would include in the **floor plan** of a **bank** to ensure efficient customer service and security.

**Answer 1:**
A bank's floor plan should include:
*   **Public Lobby/Waiting Area:** Spacious, comfortable, and well-organized.
*   **Teller Counters:** Clearly defined, accessible, and providing privacy.
*   **Manager's Office:** Private and accessible for customer consultations.
*   **Private Meeting Rooms:** For sensitive financial discussions.
*   **ATM Lobby:** Accessible from outside and inside, with security features.
*   **Back-Office Operations Area:** Secure and organized space for staff.
*   **Safe Deposit Boxes Area:** Highly secure and controlled access.
*   **Restrooms:** Accessible and clean.
*   **Security Features:** Provision for CCTV cameras, secure access points, and clear sightlines.
*   **Circulation:** Smooth flow between areas, avoiding bottlenecks.

**Question 2:**
When drawing the **section** of an **educational building**, what vertical elements are crucial to depict accurately, and why?

**Answer 2:**
Crucial vertical elements include:
*   **Floor Slabs:** Show the thickness and support system.
*   **Columns and Beams:** Illustrate the structural frame and how it supports the building.
*   **Staircases:** Depict the rise, tread, and flight, indicating the direction of travel, crucial for student movement.
*   **Wall Heights:** Show the clear height of classrooms and other spaces, important for natural light and ventilation.
*   **Window Sill and Lintel Levels:** Indicate the vertical positioning of windows for light and view.
*   **Ceiling Heights:** Define the internal volume of spaces.
*   **Foundation Depth:** Show how the building connects to the ground.
Accuracy ensures structural integrity, functional space, and safety.

**Question 3:**
You are designing an **office complex**. What are the primary considerations for the **elevation** drawings?

**Answer 3:**
For an office complex elevation, primary considerations are:
*   **Aesthetics and Professional Appearance:** The facade should project a professional image.
*   **Window Placement and Size:** Maximizing natural light and views for occupants while considering solar heat gain and glare.
*   **Materiality:** Indicating the types of finishes (glass, metal panels, brickwork, plaster) and their patterns.
*   **Entrance Design:** Creating an inviting and functional main entrance.
*   **Roofline:** The shape and appearance of the roof.
*   **Balconies/Overhangs:** If present, their visual impact and functional purpose.
*   **Branding/Signage:** Placement of the building name or company logos.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Consistency:** Maintain consistent line weights, scales, and labelling conventions across all drawings for a project.
*   **Clarity:** Ensure drawings are clear, easy to understand, and free from clutter.
*   **Accuracy:** All dimensions and relationships must be accurate.
*   **Purpose-Driven Design:** Every design decision should serve the intended function and user needs of the public building.
*   **Building Codes:** Always refer to and adhere to relevant building codes and standards during planning and drawing.
*   **Site Integration:** Consider how the building will sit on its site and interact with its surroundings.
*   **Accessibility:** Universal design principles must be incorporated for all public buildings.
*   **Layering (in CAD):** If using CAD software, utilize layers for different building elements (walls, doors, dimensions, text) for better organization and control.
