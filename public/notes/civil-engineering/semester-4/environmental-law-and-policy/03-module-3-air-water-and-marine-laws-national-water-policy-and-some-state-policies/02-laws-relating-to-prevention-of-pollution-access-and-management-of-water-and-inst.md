# ENVIRONMENTAL LAW AND POLICY: Module 3 - Air, Water and Marine Laws

## Topic: Laws relating to prevention of pollution, access and management of water and institutional mechanism

---

### **1. Introduction to Water Law and Policy**

*   **Importance of Water:**
    *   Essential for all life, ecosystems, agriculture, industry, and human health.
    *   A finite and increasingly scarce resource.
    *   Subject to competing demands and increasing pollution.
*   **Evolution of Water Law:**
    *   Historically, water rights were often based on land ownership (riparian rights).
    *   Modern water law increasingly focuses on sustainable management, pollution control, and public access.
*   **Key Principles in Water Management:**
    *   **Polluter Pays Principle:** Those who cause pollution should bear the costs of prevention and control.
    *   **Precautionary Principle:** Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, lack of full scientific certainty shall not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation.
    *   **Sustainable Development:** Meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs, incorporating economic, social, and environmental considerations.
    *   **Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM):** A process that promotes the coordinated development and management of water, land, and related resources, in order to maximize the resultant economic and social welfare in an equitable manner without compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems.

### **2. Laws Relating to Prevention of Water Pollution**

#### **2.1 The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974**

*   **Objective:** To provide for the prevention and control of water pollution and the maintenance or restoration of the wholesomeness of water for the country.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   **Definition of Pollution:** Act defines pollution as such discharge of any substance into any water that causes or is likely to cause nuisance or renders such water harmful or dangerous to public health or safety, or to life, including human life, or to domestic animals, or livestock, or plants, or to aquatic life.
    *   **Establishment of Pollution Control Boards:**
        *   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):** Apex body for water pollution control at the national level. Responsibilities include:
            *   Advising the Central Government on matters relating to water pollution.
            *   Coordinating the activities of the State Boards.
            *   Laying down standards for water quality.
            *   Collecting and disseminating information on water pollution.
            *   Organizing programs for training personnel.
        *   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):** Established in each state to implement the Act within their respective territories. Responsibilities include:
            *   Enforcing the provisions of the Act.
            *   Laying down standards for sewage and trade effluent.
            *   Granting "Consent to Establish" and "Consent to Operate" for industries discharging effluents.
            *   Monitoring water quality.
            *   Investigating and prosecuting polluters.
    *   **Prohibition of Discharge of Polluting Matter:** Section 24 prohibits knowingly causing or permitting to enter into any stream or sewer any poisonous, noxious, or polluting matter, or any matter the discharge of which causes or is likely to cause pollution.
    *   **Powers of Entry and Inspection:** SPCBs are empowered to enter any premises, take samples of water or effluents, and inspect any records.
    *   **Offences and Penalties:** Imposes penalties for contravening the provisions of the Act, including imprisonment and fines.
    *   **Advisory Boards:** The Act provides for the constitution of advisory boards for considering special problems of water pollution.

#### **2.2 The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977**

*   **Objective:** To levy a cess on water consumed by persons carrying on any industry or by such classes of consumers as may be specified by the Central Government.
*   **Purpose of Cess:**
    *   To provide financial resources for the functioning of the Pollution Control Boards.
    *   To incentivize water conservation and reduce water pollution by making water consumption more expensive.
*   **Key Provisions:**
    *   **Levy of Cess:** Consumers who use water for industrial purposes or other specified purposes are liable to pay a cess.
    *   **Rebate for Pollution Control:** A rebate is provided for industries that install and operate effective pollution control devices. This acts as a carrot to encourage pollution control.
    *   **Administration:** The Act is administered by the SPCBs.

#### **2.3 The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (Relevant to Water Pollution)**

*   **Objective:** To provide for the protection and improvement of the environment and for the prevention of hazards to human beings, other living creatures, plants, and property.
*   **Key Provisions related to Water:**
    *   **Power to Take Measures:** The Central Government can take all such measures as it deems necessary or expedient for the purpose of protecting and improving the quality of the environment and preventing, controlling and abating environmental pollution.
    *   **Rules and Standards:** The Central Government can make rules and issue directions for the prevention, control, and abatement of water pollution. This includes laying down standards for water quality.
    *   **Prohibition of Activities:** The Central Government can prohibit the carrying on of any industry or the carrying on of any process or the disposal of any solid or liquid waste, or substances, in any area or areas.
    *   **Power to Give Directions:** The Central Government can issue directions to any person, officer, or any authority for the closure, prohibition, or regulation of any industry, operation, or process, or for the stoppage or regulation of the supply of electricity, water, or any other service.
    *   **Penalties:** Stringent penalties for contravention of the Act and rules made thereunder.

### **3. Laws Relating to Access and Management of Water**

#### **3.1 The National Water Policy (2012)**

*   **Objective:** To provide a framework for the sustainable management of India's water resources, addressing issues of equity, efficiency, and environmental sustainability. It is a policy document and not a legally binding Act, but it guides legislation and action.
*   **Key Principles and Objectives:**
    *   **Water as a common pool resource:** Recognizing water as a precious natural resource that needs to be managed in a holistic and sustainable manner.
    *   **Water for Life:** Ensuring adequate drinking water supply to all citizens as a fundamental right.
    *   **Equitable Distribution:** Addressing the needs of all stakeholders, including agriculture, industry, and environment, with a focus on fairness and social justice.
    *   **Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM):** Promoting coordinated development and management of surface water and groundwater, considering both quality and quantity.
    *   **Sustainable Use:** Promoting water conservation, efficiency in water use, and rain water harvesting.
    *   **Pricing of Water:** Water should be treated as an economic good, with appropriate pricing mechanisms reflecting the cost of service delivery, while ensuring affordability for the poor.
    *   **Management of Water Resources:** Emphasis on community participation, capacity building, and stakeholder involvement in water management.
    *   **Groundwater Management:** Regulating groundwater extraction to prevent over-exploitation and ensure sustainability.
    *   **Water Quality:** Protecting water sources from pollution and ensuring the maintenance of water quality standards.
    *   **Inter-basin Transfers:** Consideration of inter-basin water transfers for optimal utilization of resources, with due consideration for environmental and social impacts.
    *   **Rainwater Harvesting and Artificial Recharge:** Promoting rainwater harvesting and artificial recharge of groundwater.
    *   **Water Use Efficiency:** Encouraging efficient water use in agriculture, industry, and domestic sectors.

#### **3.2 State Water Policies (Examples)**

*   **Context:** While the National Water Policy provides a broad framework, individual states, with their unique geographical, hydrological, and socio-economic contexts, often have their own specific water policies.
*   **Common Themes in State Water Policies:**
    *   **Augmenting Water Availability:** Focusing on water conservation, rainwater harvesting, watershed management, and efficient irrigation.
    *   **Improving Water Use Efficiency:** Promoting drip irrigation, sprinkler irrigation, and water-saving technologies in agriculture and industries.
    *   **Groundwater Regulation:** States like **Gujarat** have enacted specific legislation for groundwater regulation (e.g., Gujarat Groundwater (Regulation and Management) Act, 2005) to control over-extraction.
    *   **River Rejuvenation and Protection:** Policies often include measures to protect rivers from pollution and restore their ecological health.
    *   **Community Participation:** Encouraging local communities to participate in water resource management and conservation efforts.
    *   **Pricing and Cost Recovery:** Implementing principles of water pricing to promote efficient use and cost recovery for water services.
    *   **Inter-sectoral Water Allocation:** Developing frameworks for allocating water among different sectors based on priorities and sustainability.
*   **Example: Gujarat Groundwater (Regulation and Management) Act, 2005**
    *   **Objective:** To regulate the extraction of groundwater and protect it from depletion and degradation.
    *   **Key Provisions:**
        *   **Declaration of Groundwater Over-exploited Areas:** Empowers the government to declare areas where groundwater extraction is exceeding recharge.
        *   **Permit System for Groundwater Extraction:** Mandates obtaining permits for drilling new wells or modifying existing ones in regulated areas.
        *   **Restrictions on Groundwater Use:** Imposes restrictions on the types of activities that can use groundwater, with a priority for drinking water and agriculture.
        *   **Paltry Penalties:** Imposes penalties for contravention of the Act.

### **4. Institutional Mechanism for Water Management and Pollution Control**

*   **Hierarchical Structure:** A multi-tiered institutional framework exists at national, state, and local levels.
*   **National Level:**
    *   **Ministry of Jal Shakti (formerly Ministry of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation):** The nodal ministry responsible for water resources development and management in India.
    *   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):** As mentioned earlier, plays a crucial role in setting standards, coordinating state efforts, and advising the central government.
    *   **National Water Development Agency (NWDA):** Involved in the planning of inter-basin water transfer projects.
*   **State Level:**
    *   **State Water Resource Departments:** Responsible for the planning, development, and management of water resources within the state.
    *   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):** Responsible for implementing water pollution control laws and regulations.
    *   **State Ground Water Departments:** Focus on groundwater assessment and regulation.
*   **Local Level:**
    *   **Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs):** Increasingly being empowered to manage local water resources and sanitation.
    *   **Water User Associations (WUAs):** Community-based organizations involved in the management of irrigation systems.
*   **Inter-Agency Coordination:** Effective management requires coordination among various departments and agencies dealing with water resources, environment, agriculture, health, and urban development.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Explain the role of the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) in preventing and controlling water pollution in India.

**Answer 1:**
The CPCB is the apex body for water pollution control in India. Its key roles include:
*   Advising the Central Government on water pollution matters.
*   Coordinating the activities of State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs).
*   Laying down national water quality standards.
*   Collecting and disseminating information on water pollution.
*   Organizing training programs for personnel involved in water pollution control.
*   Undertaking and promoting research and development in water pollution control.
*   Implementing provisions of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, and the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, related to water pollution.

**Question 2:**
What is the significance of the "Polluter Pays Principle" in water pollution management? Provide an example.

**Answer 2:**
The Polluter Pays Principle is a fundamental environmental principle that mandates that those who generate pollution should bear the costs of managing it to prevent damage to human health or the environment. This principle aims to internalize the external costs of pollution, encouraging polluters to adopt cleaner technologies and reduce their environmental impact.

**Example:** An industrial unit discharging untreated effluents into a river is found to be causing water pollution. Under the Polluter Pays Principle, this unit would be held responsible for the costs associated with treating the polluted water, cleaning up the river, and compensating for any damages caused to aquatic life or human health. This could involve paying fines, investing in effluent treatment plants, or contributing to environmental remediation efforts.

**Question 3:**
Discuss the key objectives of the National Water Policy (2012) concerning water management.

**Answer 3:**
The National Water Policy (2012) aims to achieve sustainable and equitable water management in India. Its key objectives include:
*   **Water as a common pool resource:** Recognizing water as a valuable resource needing holistic management.
*   **Water for Life:** Ensuring access to safe drinking water for all as a right.
*   **Equitable Distribution:** Balancing the needs of different sectors and stakeholders, prioritizing vulnerable populations.
*   **Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM):** Promoting coordinated management of surface and groundwater, considering quantity and quality.
*   **Sustainable Use:** Encouraging water conservation, efficiency, and rainwater harvesting.
*   **Water Pricing:** Treating water as an economic good with appropriate pricing to promote efficiency, while ensuring affordability.
*   **Community Participation:** Involving local communities and stakeholders in water management decisions.
*   **Groundwater Regulation:** Controlling over-extraction to ensure sustainability.
*   **Water Quality Protection:** Preventing pollution and maintaining water quality standards.

**Question 4:**
Briefly explain the purpose of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977.

**Answer 4:**
The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977, aims to levy a cess on water consumed by industries and certain other consumers. The primary purpose of this cess is to:
*   Provide financial resources for the functioning of the Central and State Pollution Control Boards, enabling them to carry out their responsibilities in water pollution control.
*   Incentivize water conservation and pollution reduction by making water consumption more costly for polluters, particularly those who do not invest in pollution control measures. A rebate is offered to industries that install and operate effective pollution control devices.

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Interconnectedness:** Laws related to water pollution, access, and management are interconnected and often overlap. The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, serves as an umbrella legislation.
*   **Role of Boards:** Pollution Control Boards (CPCB and SPCBs) are critical institutions for the enforcement and implementation of water laws.
*   **"Consent" Mechanism:** The "Consent to Establish" and "Consent to Operate" system managed by SPCBs is a key regulatory tool for industries discharging effluents.
*   **Policy vs. Law:** The National Water Policy is a policy document that guides legislative action and government programs, while the Water Act is a legally binding statute.
*   **Groundwater Importance:** Groundwater is increasingly recognized as a critical resource, leading to state-level regulations for its sustainable management.
*   **Community Participation:** Effective water management increasingly relies on the involvement of local communities and user associations.
*   **The Polluter Pays Principle:** This principle is a cornerstone of environmental law and aims to make polluters financially responsible for their actions.
*   **Water as an Economic Good:** The policy shift towards treating water as an economic good aims to promote efficiency and conservation, balanced with ensuring access for the poor.
