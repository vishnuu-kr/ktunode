# Environmental Law and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water and Marine Laws

## Topic: Legal Framework on Air Pollution: Air Act, 1981

### 1. Introduction to Air Pollution and the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

*   **What is Air Pollution?**
    *   The presence of substances in the atmosphere that are harmful to the health of humans and other living beings, or cause damage to the environment.
    *   Includes: particulate matter, gases (SO2, NOx, CO, O3), volatile organic compounds (VOCs), heavy metals, etc.
    *   Sources: Industrial emissions, vehicular exhaust, agricultural activities, burning of fossil fuels, natural events (volcanic eruptions, dust storms).

*   **The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981**
    *   **Objective:** To provide for the prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution, and for the maintenance of the health and well-being of persons.
    *   **Enactment:** Passed by the Parliament of India.
    *   **Purpose:** To implement the recommendations of the United Nations Conference on the Human Environment held in Stockholm in 1972.
    *   **Key Principle:** "Polluter Pays Principle" - those who cause pollution are responsible for the costs of preventing or controlling it.
    *   **Constitutional Mandate:** Article 21 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Life, which includes the Right to a healthy environment.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions under the Air Act, 1981

*   **Air Pollution:**
    *   The presence in the atmosphere of any air pollutant.
    *   **Air Pollutant:** Any solid, liquid, or gaseous substance, including noise, in such concentration as tends to be injurious to human beings or other living creatures or plants or property or interferes with the comfort of dwellers or is likely to cause nuisance. (Section 2(a))

*   **Pollution:**
    *   Such discharge by any person of any solid, liquid or gaseous substance into the atmosphere as —
        *   (i) causes or is likely to cause air pollution; or
        *   (ii) leads or is likely to lead to a concentration of such substance in the atmosphere as contravenes any standard of air quality established by the Central Board or the State Board, as the case may be, under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974. (Section 2(c))

*   **State Board:**
    *   The State Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution constituted under Section 4 of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974. (Section 2(g))
    *   **Note:** The Act effectively integrates air and water pollution control by utilizing existing State Water Pollution Boards.

*   **Central Board:**
    *   The Central Board for the Prevention and Control of Water Pollution constituted under Section 3 of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974. (Section 2(d))

*   **Industry:**
    *   Includes any operation or process carried out in an establishment which is engaged in manufacturing, processing or processing of any raw material, for conversion into finished goods or in mining, or in any agricultural operation, or in the construction of buildings, roads, dams, bridges, tunnels or in any transport or in any operation which is a service industry, or in the generation of power, or in the exploitation of any mineral or natural resource, or in any industry in which pollutants are emitted into the atmosphere. (Section 2(k))

*   **Fume:**
    *   Any airborne particulate matter, whether solid or liquid, in the form of vapours or fumes or mist. (Section 2(i))

*   **Noise:**
    *   Audible sound that is unwanted or disturbing. (Section 2(a) - definition of air pollutant implicitly includes noise)

### 3. Key Provisions and Objectives of the Air Act, 1981

*   **Prevention, Control, and Abatement of Air Pollution:**
    *   The primary goal of the Act is to address all aspects of air pollution.

*   **Establishment of Authorities:**
    *   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):**
        *   Appointed by the Central Government.
        *   Functions: Advise Central Government on air pollution matters, coordinate State Board activities, lay down standards for air quality, collect and disseminate information.
    *   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):**
        *   Already established under the Water Act, 1974, they are entrusted with the functions of the Air Act.
        *   Functions: Lay down standards for industrial emissions, grant consent for industries to emit pollutants, inspect premises, take legal action against defaulters.

*   **Standards for Air Quality and Emissions:**
    *   **Ambient Air Quality Standards:** The CPCB and SPCBs are empowered to lay down standards for air quality in different areas. These are typically expressed as maximum permissible concentrations of various pollutants (e.g., PM2.5, PM10, SO2, NOx) over a specified period.
    *   **Industrial Emission Standards:** The Act mandates that no person shall, without the previous consent of the State Board, establish any industry, operate any control equipment or process, or discharge any air pollutant in excess of the standards laid down by the State Board. (Section 21)
        *   **Example:** An industry emitting sulfur dioxide must ensure its emissions do not exceed the prescribed limits for SO2 emissions per unit of production.

*   **Powers and Functions of State Boards:**
    *   **Advisory Functions:** Advise State Governments on matters relating to prevention and control of air pollution.
    *   **Standard Setting:** Lay down standards for air quality and industrial emissions.
    *   **Consent Mechanism (Section 21):**
        *   No person operating an industry or engaged in a process likely to emit air pollutants can do so without the "consent" of the State Board.
        *   This consent is granted based on compliance with prescribed emission standards and the use of appropriate pollution control equipment.
        *   **Example:** A new thermal power plant needs consent from the SPCB before commencing operations, demonstrating its plans to control SO2 and particulate matter emissions.
    *   **Inspection and Monitoring:** State Boards have the power to inspect any industrial premises and take samples of air or emissions for analysis.
    *   **Order to Take Measures (Section 22):** If a State Board finds that air pollution is being caused or is likely to be caused by a person, it can direct such person to take necessary measures to prevent or control such pollution.
    *   **Power to Close Down Industries (Section 22A):** In cases of grave and imminent danger to public health or the environment due to air pollution, the State Board or any authorized officer can order the closure of the defaulting industry.

*   **Penalties and Prosecution:**
    *   **Offences:** Violation of any provision of the Act, including failure to obtain consent, exceeding emission standards, or obstructing the Board's officers.
    *   **Punishment:** Imprisonment and/or fine.
        *   **For first conviction:** Imprisonment for a term not less than 1 year and 6 months but which may extend to 6 years and with fine. (Section 37)
        *   **For subsequent conviction:** Imprisonment for a term not less than 2 years but which may extend to 7 years and with fine. (Section 37)
        *   **Continuing offence:** Daily fine of up to Rs. 5,000 for each day the offence continues. (Section 38)

*   **Appeals:**
    *   Any person aggrieved by an order made by a State Board under the Act can appeal to the Central Government within a specified period. (Section 31)

### 4. Notable Features and Limitations of the Air Act, 1981

*   **Integration with Water Act:** The Act leverages the existing infrastructure of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, making it efficient.
*   **Focus on Industrial Pollution:** While it covers all sources, the primary focus and enforcement mechanisms are geared towards industrial emissions.
*   **Role of Consent:** The consent mechanism is a crucial tool for regulating industrial activities.
*   **Inclusion of Noise Pollution:** The definition of air pollutant implicitly includes noise, empowering the Boards to address noise pollution as well.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Enforcement Gaps:** Effective enforcement often depends on the capacity and political will of SPCBs.
    *   **Technological Dependence:** Compliance relies heavily on industries adopting appropriate pollution control technologies.
    *   **Challenges in Controlling Non-Point Sources:** Effectively monitoring and controlling pollution from diffuse sources like vehicles and agriculture remains a challenge.
    *   **Penalties sometimes seen as insufficient:** For large corporations, fines might be considered a cost of doing business rather than a significant deterrent.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary objective of the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981?

**Answer 1:** The primary objective of the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, is to provide for the prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution, and for the maintenance of the health and well-being of persons.

**Question 2:** Explain the significance of the "consent" mechanism under Section 21 of the Air Act, 1981.

**Answer 2:** The consent mechanism under Section 21 of the Air Act, 1981, is crucial as it mandates that no person can establish any industry or operate any process likely to emit air pollutants without the prior approval of the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB). This consent is granted based on the applicant's commitment to adhere to prescribed emission standards and utilize effective pollution control measures. It serves as a pre-emptive regulatory tool to ensure that potential polluters comply with environmental norms before commencing or continuing their operations.

**Question 3:** Identify two key functions of the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) under the Air Act, 1981.

**Answer 3:** Two key functions of the CPCB are:
1.  To advise the Central Government on matters relating to the prevention and control of air pollution.
2.  To coordinate the activities of the State Boards and lay down standards for air quality.

**Question 4:** What are the potential penalties for violating the provisions of the Air Act, 1981?

**Answer 4:** Penalties for violating the Air Act, 1981, include imprisonment (ranging from 1.5 years to 7 years depending on the conviction) and/or fines (up to Rs. 5,000 per day for continuing offenses).

**Question 5:** Discuss one limitation of the Air Act, 1981, in effectively controlling air pollution in India.

**Answer 5:** One significant limitation is the challenge in effectively enforcing the Act's provisions due to capacity constraints and sometimes inadequate political will of the State Pollution Control Boards. This can lead to gaps in monitoring, inspection, and prosecution, weakening the overall impact of the legislation.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **The Air Act, 1981, is India's principal legislation for controlling air pollution.**
*   **It builds upon the framework of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, by empowering existing State Water Pollution Boards to also manage air pollution.**
*   **The Act's core strategy involves setting air quality standards and emission standards for industries.**
*   **The "consent to operate" mechanism is a cornerstone of its regulatory approach.**
*   **Penalties are prescribed for non-compliance, with stricter penalties for repeat offenders.**
*   **While comprehensive, the effectiveness of the Act is heavily reliant on robust enforcement by the pollution control boards.**
*   **Noise pollution is implicitly covered under the definition of "air pollutant."**
*   **The "Polluter Pays Principle" is an underlying principle guiding the Act.**
