# Environmental Law and Policy: Module 2 - Forest, Wildlife and Biodiversity Laws

## Topic: The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (WLPA, 1972)

### 1. Evolution and Jurisprudence of Forest and Wildlife Laws

*   **Historical Context:**
    *   Early laws focused on game preservation and royal hunting rights.
    *   Colonial era saw the introduction of forest conservation measures (e.g., Indian Forest Act, 1865, 1878, 1927) primarily for revenue and strategic purposes.
    *   Post-independence, a shift towards broader conservation of flora and fauna, recognizing their intrinsic value and ecological importance.
    *   The WLPA, 1972, marked a significant departure, providing comprehensive protection to wildlife across the country.

*   **Constitutional Basis:**
    *   **Article 48A (Directive Principles of State Policy):** The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country.
    *   **Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duties):** It shall be the duty of every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures.

*   **Jurisprudence of Wildlife Protection:**
    *   **Intrinsic Value:** Recognition that wildlife has value independent of its utility to humans.
    *   **Ecological Value:** Understanding the crucial role of wildlife in maintaining ecosystem health and balance.
    *   **Intergenerational Equity:** The responsibility to conserve wildlife for future generations.
    *   **Precautionary Principle:** Taking preventative action in the face of uncertainty about potential harm to wildlife.
    *   **Polluter Pays Principle:** Those who cause pollution should bear the costs of preventing or mitigating it.

### 2. The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (WLPA, 1972)

#### 2.1 Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Wildlife:** Encompasses any animal, whether wild or captive, belonging to any of the species specified in the Schedules of the Act. It also includes any part or product of such animal.
*   **Animal:** Includes mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish and invertebrates.
*   **Habitat:** Refers to the area in which any wild animal naturally lives or moves.
*   **Specimen:** Includes any animal, whether living or dead, and includes its egg, and any part of an animal or its egg.
*   **Hunting:** Includes killing, poisoning, trapping, capturing, netting, snaring, or injuring any wild animal, or attempting to do so. It also includes disturbing the habitat of any wild animal.
*   **Protected Area:** Areas declared and managed for the conservation of wildlife, such as National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Conservation Reserves, and Community Reserves.
*   **Cessation of Right:** The Act empowers the government to extinguish rights of people in areas declared as National Parks or Wildlife Sanctuaries.

#### 2.2 Objectives of the WLPA, 1972

*   To provide for the protection of wild animals, birds, and plants.
*   To establish a network of protected areas for the conservation of wildlife.
*   To regulate and control hunting and trade in wildlife products.
*   To provide penalties for offenses committed under the Act.
*   To promote wildlife conservation through various measures.

#### 2.3 Key Provisions of the WLPA, 1972

*   **Schedules of Protected Species:**
    *   **Schedule I & Part II of Schedule II:** Provide absolute protection to species, with the strictest penalties for their violation. Examples: Tiger, Lion, Elephant, Indian Rhino, Snow Leopard, Great Indian Bustard.
    *   **Schedule III & Schedule IV:** Protect species that are not critically endangered but still require protection. Penalties are less severe than for Schedule I species. Examples: Many species of deer, monkeys, birds.
    *   **Schedule V:** Lists vermin (e.g., common crow, fruit bats, rats, mice) that may be hunted.
    *   **Schedule VI:** Prohibits the cultivation, production, possession, sale, and import/export of specified plants. Examples: Pitcher plant, Blue Vanda, Red Vanda.

*   **Prohibition of Hunting (Section 9):**
    *   No person shall hunt any wild animal specified in Schedules I, II, III, and IV.
    *   Exceptions are provided for hunting of vermin or for self-defence.

*   **Grant of Permits and Licenses:**
    *   **Special Permits for Hunting (Section 11):** The Chief Wildlife Warden can grant permission to hunt wild animals in specific circumstances:
        *   If the animal has become dangerous to human life or property.
        *   If the animal has become diseased or disabled beyond recovery.
        *   If it is necessary for the scientific purposes of conservation.
    *   **Licenses for Specified Activities (Chapter III):**
        *   **Prohibition of Sale etc. of Scheduled Animals (Section 39):** Declares that specified animals and their products in Schedules I and II shall be the property of the Central Government and cannot be bought, sold, or traded without a license.
        *   **Declaration of Animals by Central Government (Section 40):** The Central Government can declare any animal to be a specified animal for the purpose of trade.
        *   **Licenses for Sale and Possession (Section 41):** Requires licenses for buying, selling, or possessing specified animals or their products.
        *   **Licenses for Zoo and Captive Animals (Section 42):** Regulates the establishment and operation of zoos and the keeping of captive animals.

*   **Protected Areas:**
    *   **National Parks (Sections 35):** Areas of significant ecological, faunal, floral, geomorphological, or zoological significance, declared by the State Government. All private rights are usually extinguished. No human activity is permitted except for ecotourism and research with proper authorization.
        *   **Example:** Jim Corbett National Park, Kaziranga National Park.
    *   **Wildlife Sanctuaries (Section 26A):** Areas declared by the State Government to be a sanctuary for the purpose of protecting, propagating, or developing wildlife. Certain human activities might be permitted with restrictions.
        *   **Example:** Periyar Wildlife Sanctuary, Ranthambore National Park (though often referred to as a National Park, its declaration often follows sanctuary procedures).
    *   **Conservation Reserves (Section 36A):** Areas declared by the State Government, which are adjacent to National Parks or Sanctuaries, to protect passages of wildlife and corridors.
    *   **Community Reserves (Section 36C):** Areas of private or community land that have been protected for wildlife conservation, declared by the State Government on the recommendation of local communities.

*   **Powers of Officers:**
    *   The Act empowers Wildlife Wardens, Assistant Wildlife Wardens, and other officers to enforce the provisions of the Act.
    *   They have the power to enter and inspect any premises, seize animals or their products, and arrest offenders.

*   **Penalties (Chapter VI):**
    *   **Section 50 & 51:** Define offenses and prescribe penalties, including imprisonment and fines.
    *   Penalties are generally more severe for offenses related to Schedule I and Schedule II species.
    *   The Act also provides for forfeiture of property used in committing offenses.

#### 2.4 Amendments and Challenges

*   **Major Amendments:**
    *   **1982 Amendment:** Introduced provisions for National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Conservation Reserves.
    *   **2002 Amendment:** Strengthened penalties, introduced concepts of protected areas like Conservation Reserves and Community Reserves, and provided for the establishment of National Board for Wildlife.
    *   **2006 Amendment:** Further clarified provisions related to protected areas and penalties.

*   **Challenges:**
    *   **Poaching and Illegal Wildlife Trade:** Despite stringent laws, poaching remains a major threat.
    *   **Habitat Loss and Fragmentation:** Development, infrastructure projects, and human encroachment lead to habitat degradation.
    *   **Human-Wildlife Conflict:** Increasing conflict between humans and wildlife due to shrinking habitats.
    *   **Enforcement Issues:** Lack of adequate resources, trained personnel, and corruption can hinder effective enforcement.
    *   **Community Participation:** Ensuring the involvement and cooperation of local communities in conservation efforts.
    *   **Trade in Non-Scheduled Species:** Illegal trade in species not listed in the Schedules.

#### 2.5 Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022

*   **Key Features:**
    *   **Increased Penalties:** Significantly enhanced penalties for various offenses to deter poaching and illegal trade.
    *   **New Schedules:** Revised schedules to align with CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora).
    *   **Regulation of Trade in Animal Articles:** Stricter regulations for the possession, sale, and transfer of animal articles.
    *   **Confiscation of Property:** Empowering authorities to confiscate property derived from wildlife crimes.
    *   **Alien Species Management:** Provisions for managing invasive alien species that threaten native wildlife.
    *   **Increased Representation in NBWL:** Enhanced representation of non-governmental organizations and experts in the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL).

### 3. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** What are the primary objectives of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972?
**Answer:** The primary objectives are to provide protection to wild animals, birds, and plants, establish a network of protected areas, regulate hunting and trade in wildlife, and promote wildlife conservation.

**Question 2:** Explain the significance of the Schedules in the WLPA, 1972. Provide examples of species protected under Schedule I.
**Answer:** The Schedules categorize wild animals and plants based on their level of threat and assign varying degrees of protection. Schedule I and Part II of Schedule II offer absolute protection with the strictest penalties. Examples include the Tiger, Lion, Elephant, and Indian Rhinoceros.

**Question 3:** Differentiate between a National Park and a Wildlife Sanctuary as defined under the WLPA, 1972.
**Answer:**
*   **National Park:** Declared for significant ecological, faunal, floral, geomorphological, or zoological importance. All private rights are usually extinguished, and human activities are highly restricted.
*   **Wildlife Sanctuary:** Declared to protect, propagate, or develop wildlife. Certain human activities may be permitted with restrictions under the supervision of the Chief Wildlife Warden.

**Question 4:** What powers does the WLPA, 1972 grant to wildlife protection officers?
**Answer:** Wildlife protection officers have the power to enter and inspect any premises, search for and seize wild animals, animal articles, trophies, or any other property used in connection with wildlife offenses, and arrest offenders.

**Question 5:** Discuss the impact of the 2022 Amendment to the WLPA, 1972.
**Answer:** The 2022 Amendment significantly increased penalties for wildlife crimes, revised schedules to align with CITES, strengthened regulations for trade in animal articles, and introduced provisions for managing alien species and enhancing representation in the NBWL.

**Question 6 (True/False):** Hunting of any wild animal is prohibited under the WLPA, 1972.
**Answer:** False. While hunting of scheduled species is prohibited, exceptions exist for vermin and in cases of self-defense.

**Question 7 (Short Answer):** What is the role of the Chief Wildlife Warden under the WLPA, 1972?
**Answer:** The Chief Wildlife Warden is responsible for the administration and management of wildlife and protected areas within a state. They have powers to grant special permits for hunting under specific conditions, manage sanctuaries, and enforce the provisions of the Act.

### 4. Important Points to Remember

*   The WLPA, 1972, is a cornerstone legislation for wildlife conservation in India.
*   It provides a hierarchical system of protection through its Schedules.
*   The Act establishes different categories of protected areas: National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Conservation Reserves, and Community Reserves.
*   Penalties for offenses are severe and have been further strengthened by the 2022 Amendment.
*   The Act aims to strike a balance between conservation and the rights of local communities, although this remains a significant challenge.
*   Understanding the constitutional provisions (Article 48A and 51A(g)) provides the framework for wildlife protection in India.
*   The Act is continuously evolving to address new challenges and international commitments (like CITES).
*   Enforcement and community participation are crucial for the effective implementation of the WLPA, 1972.
