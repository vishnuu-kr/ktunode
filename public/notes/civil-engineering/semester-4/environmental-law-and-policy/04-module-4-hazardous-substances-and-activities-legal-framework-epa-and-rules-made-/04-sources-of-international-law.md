# Environmental Law and Policy: Module 4 - Hazardous Substances and Activities

## Topic: Sources of International Law in the Context of Hazardous Substances and Activities

This module focuses on the legal framework governing hazardous substances and activities, particularly through the lens of the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and its rules. However, to fully understand this domestic framework, it's crucial to recognize the influence and role of international law. This section explores the foundational sources of international law that inform and shape how nations, including the United States, manage hazardous substances and activities.

---

### **Learning Outcomes:**

Upon completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   Identify and explain the primary sources of international law.
*   Understand how these sources contribute to the development of international environmental law related to hazardous substances and activities.
*   Recognize the interplay between customary international law and treaty law in this domain.
*   Appreciate the role of general principles of law and judicial decisions in shaping international environmental norms.
*   Analyze how soft law instruments can influence national policies and regulations concerning hazardous substances.

---

### **1. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **International Law:** A body of rules and principles that govern the relations between sovereign states and other subjects of international law, such as international organizations.
*   **Sources of International Law:** The origins and foundations from which international legal rules and principles are derived. These are typically identified in Article 38(1) of the Statute of the International Court of Justice (ICJ).
*   **Treaty:** A formal, written agreement between sovereign states, governed by international law. Treaties are binding on the states that are parties to them.
*   **Customary International Law:** Unwritten rules of international law that arise from the consistent and general practice of states, accepted by them as law (opinio juris).
*   **Opinio Juris (Sive Necessitatis):** The belief by states that a particular practice is legally obligatory, not just a matter of comity or habit.
*   **General Principles of Law:** Fundamental legal principles that are common to the major legal systems of the world.
*   **Judicial Decisions and Scholarly Writings:** While not binding themselves, these can serve as subsidiary means for determining rules of law.
*   **Soft Law:** Instruments that are not legally binding in themselves but can have significant influence in shaping state behavior and the development of hard law. Examples include resolutions of international organizations, declarations, and guidelines.

### **2. Primary Sources of International Law (Article 38(1) of the ICJ Statute)**

Article 38(1) of the Statute of the International Court of Justice is widely recognized as the authoritative statement on the sources of international law. It lists the following:

#### **2.1. International Conventions (Treaties)**

*   **Definition:** Legally binding agreements between states that create rights and obligations for the parties involved.
*   **Relevance to Hazardous Substances and Activities:**
    *   **Basel Convention on the Control of Transboundary Movements of Hazardous Wastes and Their Disposal:** Regulates the international movement of hazardous wastes and ensures they are managed in an environmentally sound manner.
    *   **Rotterdam Convention on the Prior Informed Consent Procedure for Certain Hazardous Chemicals and Pesticides in International Trade:** Facilitates information exchange about hazardous chemicals and aims to promote shared responsibility and cooperative efforts among parties in the international trade of certain hazardous chemicals.
    *   **Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs):** Aims to protect human health and the environment from POPs, which are chemicals that remain intact in the environment for long periods, become widely distributed geographically, accumulate in the fatty tissue of living organisms and are toxic to humans and wildlife.
    *   **Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer:** Controls the production and consumption of ozone-depleting substances, a prime example of international cooperation on a hazardous chemical.
    *   **Minamata Convention on Mercury:** Addresses mercury pollution, a global environmental problem resulting from human activities.
*   **Key Concept:** Treaties are binding only on states that have expressed their consent to be bound (e.g., through ratification or accession).

#### **2.2. International Custom**

*   **Definition:** State practice followed by states because they are motivated by a sense of legal obligation (opinio juris).
*   **Elements:**
    *   **State Practice:** Consistent and widespread behavior of states. This can include diplomatic acts, official statements, legislative and administrative actions, and judicial decisions.
    *   **Opinio Juris:** The belief that the practice is legally required.
*   **Relevance to Hazardous Substances and Activities:**
    *   **Prohibition of Transboundary Harm:** The principle that states have a responsibility to ensure that activities within their jurisdiction or control do not cause damage to the environment of other states or to areas beyond the limits of national jurisdiction. This principle has deep roots in customary international law, exemplified by cases like the *Trail Smelter Arbitration*.
    *   **Polluter Pays Principle:** While often codified in treaties, the underlying idea that those who cause pollution should bear the costs of prevention and control is increasingly recognized as a customary principle in international environmental law.
    *   **Precautionary Principle/Approach:** The idea that where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, lack of full scientific certainty shall not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation. While its status as strict customary law is debated, its influence is undeniable.
*   **Example:** The widespread adoption and adherence to the principle of not dumping hazardous wastes in the marine environment, even before specific treaty prohibitions, can be seen as developing customary international law.

#### **2.3. General Principles of Law Recognized by Civilized Nations**

*   **Definition:** Fundamental legal principles common to the major legal systems of the world, used to fill gaps in treaty or customary law.
*   **Relevance to Hazardous Substances and Activities:**
    *   **Good Faith:** States are expected to act in good faith in their treaty relations and in their interactions concerning environmental matters.
    *   **Reparation for Harm:** The principle that a state responsible for causing harm to another state or its environment must make reparation for that harm.
    *   **Sovereign Immunity:** While states have sovereignty over their territory, this does not grant them an absolute right to cause transboundary harm.
    *   **Estoppel:** A legal principle preventing a party from asserting something contrary to what it has previously implied by its conduct or statement.
*   **Example:** The principle of *res judicata* (a matter already judged) ensures finality in international dispute resolution related to environmental damage.

#### **2.4. Subsidiary Means for the Determination of Rules of Law**

*   **Definition:** These are not primary sources of law but aid in identifying and interpreting the law.
*   **Includes:**
    *   **Judicial Decisions:** Decisions of international courts and tribunals (e.g., ICJ, International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea) and, in some cases, national courts dealing with international law issues.
        *   **Example:** The ICJ's advisory opinion in the *Legality of the Threat or Use of Nuclear Weapons* case discussed environmental concerns. The *Gabcikovo-Nagymaros Project* case involved environmental considerations within a treaty dispute.
    *   **Writings of Publicists (Scholarly Writings):** Works of esteemed legal scholars, which can help clarify and interpret existing law or highlight emerging principles.
        *   **Example:** Writings by scholars on the precautionary principle have been instrumental in its recognition and application in international environmental law.

### **3. The Role of Soft Law in Environmental Governance**

While Article 38(1) focuses on "hard" law, "soft" law plays a crucial role in the development and implementation of international environmental law, particularly concerning hazardous substances and activities.

*   **Definition:** Non-binding instruments that articulate goals, principles, and standards, often serving as precursors to treaties or influencing state practice.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **UN Environment Programme (UNEP) Resolutions and Declarations:** UNEP often adopts resolutions or declarations on issues like hazardous waste management or chemical safety, setting agendas and encouraging state action.
    *   **Recommendations of Expert Groups:** Reports and recommendations from scientific or technical bodies can influence policy.
    *   **Codes of Conduct and Guidelines:** For example, guidelines for the safe handling of specific chemicals.
*   **How it Influences:**
    *   **Building Consensus:** Soft law helps build consensus among states on complex environmental issues.
    *   **Shaping State Practice:** States may voluntarily adopt the principles and standards found in soft law instruments, contributing to the formation of customary international law.
    *   **Guiding Treaty Negotiation:** Soft law can provide a foundation and framework for negotiating new treaties.
    *   **Influencing National Legislation:** National governments may look to soft law as guidance when developing their own environmental regulations.
*   **Example:** The Rio Declaration on Environment and Development (1992), containing principles like the Precautionary Principle and the Polluter Pays Principle, was a soft law instrument that has profoundly influenced subsequent hard law and national policies.

---

### **4. Interplay Between Sources**

*   **Treaties and Custom:**
    *   **Codification:** Treaties can codify existing customary international law.
    *   **Crystallization:** Treaties can help "crystallize" emerging customary law by providing a clear statement of principles that states then begin to follow.
    *   **New Law:** Treaties can create entirely new legal obligations that, over time, may become part of customary international law if adopted by non-signatory states.
*   **Soft Law and Hard Law:** Soft law often paves the way for hard law by creating norms, raising awareness, and facilitating agreement among states.

---

### **5. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Article 38(1) ICJ Statute:** The definitive list of primary sources of international law.
*   **Hierarchy:** Generally, there is no strict hierarchy between treaties and custom, though specific treaty provisions may override customary law between parties. *Jus cogens* (peremptory norms of international law) are superior to all other sources.
*   **Binding Nature:** Treaties are binding on parties; customary law is binding on all states unless a persistent objector can be proven.
*   **Soft Law's Influence:** Don't underestimate the persuasive power and role of soft law in shaping international environmental policy and law.
*   **Relevance to EPA:** Understanding these international sources helps explain *why* the EPA implements certain regulations – they often reflect international commitments or widely accepted principles for managing hazardous substances and activities.

### **6. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Identify and briefly explain the two primary sources of international law that are most directly binding on states regarding the management of hazardous substances in international trade.

**Answer 1:**
The two primary sources are **International Conventions (Treaties)** and **International Custom**.
*   **Treaties** are legally binding agreements between states. Conventions like the Rotterdam Convention directly regulate the international trade of certain hazardous chemicals by establishing a Prior Informed Consent procedure.
*   **International Custom** comprises state practice accepted as law. For instance, the customary norm against causing significant transboundary environmental harm influences how states manage hazardous substances within their borders to prevent harm to neighboring countries.

**Question 2:**
The "Polluter Pays Principle" is often mentioned in international environmental discussions. Is this principle a treaty, a rule of customary international law, or a general principle of law? Discuss its status and how it might influence national legislation.

**Answer 2:**
The Polluter Pays Principle's status is evolving.
*   **Treaty Law:** It is explicitly codified in various international treaties (e.g., the Convention on Biological Diversity, the EU's environmental directives).
*   **Customary International Law:** While its precise definition and scope are debated, it is increasingly recognized as a principle of customary international law due to widespread state practice, the inclusion in numerous treaties, and its articulation in international declarations.
*   **General Principle:** Some scholars consider it a general principle of law, derived from the common understanding that polluters should bear the costs associated with their actions.

**Influence on National Legislation:** Even if not strictly codified in a binding treaty for all states, the principle encourages states to enact legislation that internalizes environmental costs. This could include:
*   Imposing taxes or fees on polluting activities.
*   Requiring companies to undertake remediation for pollution they cause.
*   Establishing liability regimes for environmental damage.

**Question 3:**
Explain the role of "soft law" instruments in the development of international environmental law, particularly concerning the regulation of hazardous waste. Provide an example.

**Answer 3:**
Soft law instruments, such as resolutions, declarations, and guidelines, are non-binding but play a crucial role in international environmental law by:
*   **Building consensus:** They help states agree on common goals and approaches to complex issues like hazardous waste management.
*   **Shaping norms:** They articulate principles that can influence state behavior and contribute to the formation of customary international law.
*   **Guiding policy and legislation:** They provide frameworks and recommendations that national governments can adopt into their domestic laws.

**Example:** The **Agenda 21** adopted at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, while a non-binding declaration, contained comprehensive recommendations for managing wastes, including hazardous wastes, and promoted principles of sustainable development. This influenced many national waste management policies and contributed to the impetus for later binding treaties like the Basel Convention.
