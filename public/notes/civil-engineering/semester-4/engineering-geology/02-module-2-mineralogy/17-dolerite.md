# Engineering Geology: Module 2 - Mineralogy: Dolerite

## 1. Introduction to Dolerite

Dolerite, also known as diabase or basaltic dolerite, is a significant mafic intrusive igneous rock. It plays a crucial role in engineering geology due to its widespread occurrence and often favorable physical properties.

*   **Classification:** Dolerite belongs to the **intermediate** group of igneous rocks in terms of silica content, falling between basalt (extrusive) and gabbro (intrusive). It is characterized by its medium-grained texture.
*   **Occurrence:** Dolerites commonly occur as **intrusions** such as sills, dykes, laccoliths, and small stocks.
*   **Importance in Engineering Geology:**
    *   Foundation materials for structures.
    *   Aggregate for concrete and road construction.
    *   Source of geothermal energy (in some regions).
    *   Understanding its weathering characteristics is vital for slope stability and construction.

## 2. Mineralogical Composition of Dolerite

Dolerite is primarily composed of **plagioclase feldspar** and **clinopyroxene**, with smaller amounts of other mafic minerals.

### 2.1 Major Minerals

*   **Plagioclase Feldspar:**
    *   **Composition:** A solid solution series between sodium-rich albite ($\text{NaAlSi}_3\text{O}_8$) and calcium-rich anorthite ($\text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8$).
    *   **In Dolerite:** Typically intermediate compositions like **andesine** or **labradorite**.
    *   **Optical Properties (Key for Identification):**
        *   **Cleavage:** Two prominent cleavages at nearly 90 degrees.
        *   **Twinning:** Exhibits **Albite twinning** (striations parallel to the cleavage) which is characteristic. Can also show Carlsbad and Baveno twins.
        *   **Color:** White to grey in hand specimen.
        *   **Intergrowth:** Often found as **laths** or **stubby prismatic** crystals.

*   **Clinopyroxene:**
    *   **Composition:** Silicate minerals belonging to the pyroxene group, characterized by a single chain silicate structure.
    *   **In Dolerite:** Most commonly **augite**, a calcium-rich clinopyroxene.
    *   **Optical Properties (Key for Identification):**
        *   **Cleavage:** Two cleavages intersecting at approximately 70° and 110°.
        *   **Color:** Dark green to brown or black in hand specimen and under the microscope.
        *   **Pleochroism:** Weak to moderate pleochroism (color change with rotation) is often observed.
        *   **Crystal Form:** Prismatic to subhedral.

### 2.2 Minor/Accessory Minerals

These minerals are present in smaller quantities but can influence the rock's properties and origin.

*   **Olivine:**
    *   **Composition:** $(\text{Mg,Fe})_2\text{SiO}_4$.
    *   **Occurrence in Dolerite:** Can be present, especially in fresher dolerites. If altered, it forms **serpentine** or **iddingsite**.
    *   **Optical Properties:** Characteristically **conchoidal fracture**, high birefringence, and often shows **pinpoint or sector twinning**.

*   **Opaques:**
    *   **Composition:** Primarily **magnetite** ($\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$) and **ilmenite** ($\text{FeTiO}_3$).
    *   **Occurrence:** As fine grains or skeletal crystals, often concentrated along grain boundaries.
    *   **Significance:** Contribute to the magnetic properties of the rock.

*   **Secondary Minerals (Alteration Products):**
    *   **Chlorite:** Greenish, often forms fibrous aggregates.
    *   **Epidote:** Greenish, often crystalline.
    *   **Serpentine:** Green, often fibrous or platy, forms from olivine alteration.
    *   **Carbonates (Calcite, Dolomite):** Form as veins or fill cavities.
    *   **Zeolites:** Hydrated aluminosilicates, can fill vesicles.

## 3. Textural Characteristics of Dolerite

The texture of a rock describes the size, shape, and arrangement of its constituent mineral grains. Dolerite exhibits characteristic textures related to its intrusive origin and cooling rate.

### 3.1 Key Textural Features

*   **Medium-grained:** Individual mineral grains are visible to the naked eye but not coarse enough to be easily separated.
*   **Sub-ophitic Texture:** A defining textural characteristic of dolerite.
    *   **Definition:** Where plagioclase laths are partially enclosed by larger pyroxene crystals. The pyroxene crystals tend to be slightly irregular in shape, molding around the plagioclase.
    *   **Significance:** Indicates a relatively rapid cooling rate, allowing plagioclase to crystallize first, but not so rapid that only very fine grains form.

*   **Hypidiomorphic Granular:** A general term indicating that the rock is composed of a mixture of euhedral (well-formed) and subhedral (partially formed) crystals.

*   **Vesicles:** Small cavities formed by escaping gas bubbles during the initial cooling.
    *   **Infilling:** Vesicles can be empty or infilled with secondary minerals like **quartz, calcite, zeolites, or chlorite** (forming amygdaloidal dolerite).

## 4. Engineering Properties of Dolerite

The mineralogy and texture of dolerite directly influence its engineering behavior.

### 4.1 Physical and Mechanical Properties

*   **Strength:** Generally exhibits **high compressive strength** due to its interlocking crystalline structure and dense nature.
*   **Hardness:** High hardness due to the presence of feldspar and pyroxene.
*   **Durability:** Good durability and resistance to weathering, especially in fresh, unweathered states.
*   **Porosity and Permeability:** Typically **low porosity and permeability**, making it a relatively impermeable rock mass.
*   **Abrasiveness:** Can be abrasive to excavation equipment due to its hardness.
*   **Thermal Properties:** Good thermal conductivity, which can be relevant in geothermal applications.

### 4.2 Weathering Behavior

*   **Factors:** Weathering primarily attacks the plagioclase feldspar and mafic minerals (pyroxene, olivine).
*   **Products:**
    *   Plagioclase weathers to **clay minerals (kaolinite, smectite)**.
    *   Olivine weathers to **serpentine, chlorite, and iron oxides**.
    *   Pyroxene weathers to **chlorite, epidote, and clay minerals**.
*   **Effects:** Weathering leads to a reduction in strength, increase in porosity and permeability, and the formation of residual soils. This can impact slope stability and the integrity of foundations.
*   **Disintegration:** In some cases, especially with rapid cooling and the presence of minor constituents, dolerite can exhibit **graben or box jointing**, which can lead to rapid disintegration upon exposure.

## 5. Identification of Dolerite in Engineering Geology

Proper identification is crucial for accurate engineering design.

### 5.1 Field Identification

*   **Color:** Typically dark grey to black.
*   **Texture:** Medium-grained, often with visible laths of plagioclase and darker pyroxene crystals.
*   **Feel:** Hard and dense.
*   **Magnetism:** Often exhibits noticeable magnetism due to magnetite content.
*   **Presence of Dykes/Sills:** Recognition of intrusive geological structures.

### 5.2 Laboratory Identification (Petrography)

*   **Thin Section Analysis:** The definitive method.
    *   **Key Minerals:** Identification of plagioclase (twinning, composition) and pyroxene (cleavage angle, color, pleochroism).
    *   **Texture:** Recognition of sub-ophitic or ophitic texture.
    *   **Accessory Minerals:** Identification of olivine, opaques, and secondary alteration products.

## 7. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
The primary mineralogical components of dolerite are **plagioclase feldspar** and **clinopyroxene**, usually augite.
*   **Plagioclase Feldspar:** Appears as white to grey laths or stubby prisms. Under the microscope, it is identified by its two cleavages at nearly 90 degrees and characteristic **Albite twinning** (striations).
*   **Clinopyroxene (Augite):** Appears as dark green to brown or black crystals. Under the microscope, it is identified by its two cleavages intersecting at approximately 70° and 110°, and often weak pleochroism.

**Answer 2:**
The characteristic texture of dolerite is **sub-ophitic** (or sometimes ophitic). This texture occurs when plagioclase feldspar laths are partially or almost completely enclosed by larger pyroxene crystals. It signifies that the rock cooled relatively quickly, allowing plagioclase to crystallize first, followed by pyroxene which grew around the existing plagioclase crystals.

**Answer 3:**
Dolerite's **high compressive strength** and **good durability** make it an excellent material for various engineering applications.
*   **Strength:** Its interlocking crystalline structure provides excellent resistance to crushing, making it suitable for foundations and load-bearing elements.
*   **Durability:** Its resistance to weathering and abrasion makes it a good aggregate for concrete and road construction, ensuring long-term performance and resistance to wear and tear.

**Answer 4:**
The weathering of dolerite primarily attacks the feldspar and mafic minerals.
*   **Plagioclase** weathers to **clay minerals (e.g., kaolinite)**.
*   **Olivine** (if present) weathers to **serpentine** and **iddingsite**.
*   **Pyroxene** weathers to **chlorite** and **epidote**.
The breakdown of these minerals reduces the rock's overall strength and increases its porosity and permeability, potentially leading to reduced shear strength and increased susceptibility to freeze-thaw damage in the weathered rock mass.

**Answer 5:**
Based on the description (dark grey, medium-grained, laths of plagioclase partially enclosed by green clinopyroxene), the rock sample is most likely **dolerite**. The texture described is characteristic of sub-ophitic texture.

## 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Dolerite = Medium-grained mafic intrusive igneous rock.**
*   **Key Minerals:** Plagioclase Feldspar (andesine/labradorite) and Clinopyroxene (augite).
*   **Defining Texture:** **Sub-ophitic** texture (plagioclase laths enclosed by pyroxene).
*   **Common Intrusions:** Sills and dykes.
*   **Engineering Significance:** High strength, durability, good aggregate, low permeability.
*   **Weathering:** Primarily affects plagioclase and pyroxene, leading to clay minerals, chlorite, and epidote.
*   **Field Clues:** Dark color, medium grain size, magnetism.
*   **Microscopic Clues:** Albite twinning in plagioclase, cleavage angles in pyroxene, sub-ophitic texture.
