# Engineering Geology: Module 2 - Mineralogy - Augite

This module delves into the fascinating world of minerals, with a specific focus on Augite. Understanding Augite is crucial in Engineering Geology as it is a common constituent of many igneous rocks and can influence the engineering properties of the materials derived from them.

## Learning Outcomes:

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

*   **Identify** the chemical formula and classification of Augite.
*   **Describe** the physical properties of Augite, including its crystal habit, cleavage, fracture, hardness, specific gravity, and color.
*   **Explain** the optical properties of Augite, particularly its interference colors and extinction angle.
*   **Discuss** the occurrence and formation of Augite in igneous and metamorphic rocks.
*   **Relate** the properties of Augite to its behavior in geological environments and its impact on engineering applications.

## 1. Introduction to Augite

Augite is a crucial mineral in the study of igneous and metamorphic rocks. Its presence and abundance can significantly affect the strength, stability, and durability of rock masses used in engineering projects.

### 1.1. Mineral Classification

*   **Group:** Silicates
*   **Subgroup:** Inosilicates (specifically, Chain Silicates)
*   **Class:** Pyroxenes (specifically, Clinopyroxenes)

**Key Concept:** Silicates are the most abundant mineral group in the Earth's crust, characterized by the presence of the silicon-oxygen tetrahedron ($SiO_4$). Inosilicates are silicates where these tetrahedra are linked together to form chains. Pyroxenes are a major group of inosilicates characterized by single silica chains.

### 1.2. Chemical Formula

*   **General Formula:** $(Ca,Na)(Mg,Fe^{2+},Fe^{3+},Al)_{2}(Si,Al)_{2}O_{6}$
*   **Simplified Common Formula:** $(Ca,Na) (Mg,Fe) Si_{2}O_{6}$

**Explanation of Variables:**

*   **Ca:** Calcium
*   **Na:** Sodium
*   **Mg:** Magnesium
*   **Fe$^{2+}$:** Ferrous Iron
*   **Fe$^{3+}$:** Ferric Iron
*   **Al:** Aluminum
*   **Si:** Silicon
*   **O:** Oxygen

**Key Concept:** Augite is a solid solution series, meaning its chemical composition can vary within certain limits due to the substitution of ions of similar size and charge. The presence of iron and magnesium is particularly important as it influences Augite's color and magnetic properties.

## 2. Physical Properties of Augite

These properties are vital for identifying Augite in hand samples and thin sections.

### 2.1. Crystal Habit

*   **Description:** Typically found as short, prismatic crystals, often with a roughly rectangular or square cross-section.
*   **Euhedral to Subhedral:** Crystals can be well-formed (euhedral) or partially formed (subhedral) depending on the cooling rate of the magma.
*   **Occurrence:** Can be found as individual crystals or as aggregates within igneous rocks.

**Example:** In basalt or gabbro, Augite crystals might appear as dark, stubby prisms.

### 2.2. Cleavage

*   **Description:** Exhibits two distinct cleavage directions that intersect at an angle of approximately **86° and 94°**. This is a characteristic feature of pyroxenes.
*   **Macle:** The intersection of the cleavage planes results in a cross-section that is almost rectangular, but with slightly angled corners.

**Key Concept:** Cleavage is the tendency of a mineral to break along planes of weakness. The characteristic cleavage angle of pyroxenes distinguishes them from amphiboles, which have cleavage angles of approximately 56° and 124°.

### 2.3. Fracture

*   **Description:** Typically **conchoidal to uneven**.
*   **Conchoidal Fracture:** Produces smooth, curved surfaces, resembling the inside of a seashell.
*   **Uneven Fracture:** Produces rough and irregular surfaces.

### 2.4. Hardness

*   **Mohs Hardness Scale:** **5.5 to 6.5**
*   **Description:** Can be scratched by a steel knife or glass, but will scratch glass.

**Key Concept:** Hardness is a measure of a mineral's resistance to scratching. This property is useful for distinguishing Augite from softer or harder minerals.

### 2.5. Specific Gravity

*   **Range:** **3.2 to 3.5**
*   **Description:** Augite is relatively dense, which is characteristic of minerals containing iron and calcium.

**Key Concept:** Specific gravity is the ratio of the density of a mineral to the density of water. Higher specific gravity generally indicates the presence of heavier elements like iron.

### 2.6. Color

*   **Typical Colors:** **Dark green to black**
*   **Variations:** Can sometimes appear brownish or purplish due to variations in chemical composition, particularly the iron content.

**Key Concept:** The color of Augite is primarily due to the presence of iron ($Fe^{2+}$ and $Fe^{3+}$) in its crystal structure.

### 2.7. Streak

*   **Description:** **White to greenish-gray**

**Key Concept:** Streak is the color of a mineral in its powdered form, which is a more reliable indicator of composition than the specimen's natural color.

## 3. Optical Properties of Augite (Under Polarized Light)

These properties are observed using a petrographic microscope and are crucial for thin-section identification.

### 3.1. Birefringence and Interference Colors

*   **Description:** Augite is a **birefringent** mineral, meaning it splits a beam of light into two rays that vibrate at right angles to each other. This results in interference colors when viewed under crossed polarizers.
*   **Interference Colors:** Typically **first-order grays to bright second-order yellows and greens**. The exact color depends on the mineral's thickness and composition.

**Key Concept:** Interference colors are produced by the difference in the speed of light rays as they pass through the mineral. They are classified into orders (first, second, third, etc.) based on their brightness and hue.

### 3.2. Extinction Angle

*   **Description:** The angle between the crystallographic c-axis (elongation of the crystal) and the extinction position (when the mineral appears dark between crossed polarizers).
*   **Augite Extinction Angle:** **40° to 50°**.
*   **Parallel vs. Inclined Extinction:** Augite exhibits **inclined extinction**.

**Key Concept:** The extinction angle is a critical diagnostic property for distinguishing between different silicate mineral groups. Clinopyroxenes, like Augite, have inclined extinction, while orthopyroxenes have parallel extinction.

### 3.3. Pleochroism

*   **Description:** The property of a mineral appearing to have different colors when viewed from different directions under polarized light.
*   **Augite Pleochroism:** **Weak to moderate**, with color changes from greenish-yellow to green or brown.

**Key Concept:** Pleochroism arises from variations in the absorption of light by the mineral's electronic structure along different crystallographic directions.

### 3.4. Relief

*   **Description:** The apparent difference in height between the mineral and the surrounding mounting medium (e.g., Canada balsam) when viewed under a microscope.
*   **Augite Relief:** **Moderate to high**. This is due to its higher refractive indices compared to many common rock-forming minerals and the mounting medium.

**Key Concept:** Relief is related to the difference in refractive indices between the mineral and the mounting medium. Higher relief means the mineral appears more prominent and "stands out" from the background.

## 4. Occurrence and Formation of Augite

Augite is a common mineral found in a variety of igneous and metamorphic environments.

### 4.1. Igneous Rocks

*   **Common Occurrences:**
    *   **Mafic and Ultramafic Igneous Rocks:** Basalt, gabbro, dolerite, andesite.
    *   **Intermediary Rocks:** Diorite.
    *   **Volcanic Rocks:** Frequently found as phenocrysts (larger crystals) in volcanic rocks like basalt.
*   **Formation:** Forms during the cooling and crystallization of magma. As magma cools, minerals with higher melting points crystallize first. Augite typically crystallizes at intermediate temperatures within the mafic to intermediate range.

**Key Concept:** The crystallization sequence of minerals in igneous rocks is governed by their melting points and the composition of the magma. Augite is a characteristic mineral of rocks that are rich in magnesium and iron.

### 4.2. Metamorphic Rocks

*   **Common Occurrences:**
    *   **Contact Metamorphism:** Forms in limestones and dolostones that are metamorphosed by heat from nearby magma intrusions.
    *   **Regional Metamorphism:** Found in some metamorphosed mafic igneous rocks and sediments.
*   **Formation:** Augite can form and recrystallize during metamorphism through chemical reactions involving existing minerals.

**Key Concept:** Metamorphism involves the transformation of existing rocks into new rocks through heat, pressure, and chemical reactions.

## 5. Engineering Significance of Augite

The presence and properties of Augite can have significant implications for civil engineering projects.

### 5.1. Durability and Weathering

*   **Weathering:** Augite, particularly those with higher iron content, can be susceptible to **chemical weathering**. Oxidation of iron can lead to the formation of iron oxides (like limonite), causing the rock to weaken and discolor.
*   **Physical Weathering:** The cleavage planes can act as planes of weakness, facilitating **physical weathering** processes such as frost wedging.

**Example:** Rocks with abundant Augite in areas with high moisture and fluctuating temperatures might exhibit accelerated weathering, leading to a reduction in the strength of building materials or road subgrades.

### 5.2. Strength and Stability of Rock Masses

*   **Rock Strength:** The interlocking nature of Augite crystals contributes to the strength of igneous rocks. However, the presence of cleavage and potential for alteration can reduce this strength.
*   **Geotechnical Properties:** The presence of Augite can influence the shear strength, compressibility, and permeability of rock masses. For instance, rocks with abundant, altered Augite might have lower shear strength and be more prone to deformation under load.

**Example:** In tunnel construction, a rock mass rich in Augite that is undergoing alteration might require more extensive ground support compared to a fresh, unaltered rock mass.

### 5.3. Aggregate Properties

*   **Crushing Resistance:** The hardness of Augite contributes to the crushing resistance of aggregates used in concrete and asphalt.
*   **Polishing Resistance:** Its surface texture and hardness can affect the skid resistance of road surfaces.
*   **Alkali-Silica Reaction (ASR):** While less common for Augite itself, if Augite is associated with reactive silica phases, it could potentially contribute to ASR in concrete, leading to deleterious expansion and cracking.

**Example:** Aggregates derived from rocks with a high proportion of fresh Augite would generally be considered durable and suitable for use in high-performance concrete.

## Important Points to Remember:

*   **Pyroxene Group:** Augite is a member of the clinopyroxene group, characterized by its cleavage angle of ~86°/94°.
*   **Chemical Formula:** The variable composition $(Ca,Na)(Mg,Fe^{2+},Fe^{3+},Al)_{2}(Si,Al)_{2}O_{6}$ is important to recognize.
*   **Color:** Dark green to black is the typical color.
*   **Habit:** Short, prismatic crystals.
*   **Extinction:** Inclined extinction (40-50°) is a key optical property.
*   **Occurrence:** Common in mafic and intermediate igneous rocks, and also found in some metamorphic rocks.
*   **Engineering Impact:** Durability, rock mass strength, and aggregate properties are all influenced by Augite.
