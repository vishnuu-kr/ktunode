# ARCHITECTURAL ENGINEERING - Module 4: Architecture Design Aspect - Basic Anthropometrics

## Topic: Dimensional System in Vastuvidya – Concept of Selection of Perimeter - Proportions

This module explores the fundamental principles of Vastuvidya, an ancient Indian system of architecture and design, with a specific focus on its dimensional systems, the concept of perimeter selection, and the importance of proportions. These principles are deeply intertwined with basic anthropometrics, aiming to create harmonious and functional living spaces that resonate with human well-being.

---

### 1. Introduction to Vastuvidya and its Dimensional System

Vastuvidya (also known as Vastu Shastra) is a holistic science that guides the design and construction of buildings to harmonize with nature and human life. It emphasizes the interconnectedness of the built environment, the cosmos, and the human body. A core element of Vastuvidya is its sophisticated dimensional system, which is rooted in ancient measurements and principles.

*   **Core Philosophy:** Vastuvidya aims to create spaces that promote health, prosperity, and spiritual well-being for the inhabitants.
*   **Cosmic Connection:** The principles are believed to be derived from observations of celestial bodies, natural forces (like solar and magnetic energies), and the human form.
*   **Dimensional System Basis:** Unlike modern metric or imperial systems, Vastuvidya's dimensional system is often based on natural units and human proportions, ensuring a natural fit with the human experience.

---

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

Understanding the core terminology is crucial for grasping Vastuvidya's dimensional system.

*   **Vastu Purusha Mandala:** The cosmic diagram or blueprint that forms the foundation of Vastu design. It represents the cosmic man (Vastu Purusha) and is divided into grids, with each grid having specific energies and deities associated with it.
    *   **Example:** A common Vastu Purusha Mandala is a 9x9 grid (81 squares), but other variations exist.
*   **Hasta (Hand Span):** A fundamental unit of measurement in Vastuvidya. It is generally considered to be the distance from the tip of the thumb to the tip of the little finger when the hand is extended, or a cubit (forearm length).
    *   **Significance:** This unit directly links the design to the human scale, reflecting anthropometric principles.
*   **Maan (Measure):** The principle of measurement and proportion in Vastuvidya. It dictates the ideal dimensions of various architectural elements.
*   **Pramana (Proportion):** The harmonious relationship between different parts of a building and the whole. Proportions are crucial for aesthetic appeal, functionality, and energetic balance.
*   **Parivara (Perimeter/Enclosure):** The boundary or outer dimension of a plot or building. The selection and proportioning of the perimeter are critical in Vastu.

---

### 3. Concept of Selection of Perimeter in Vastuvidya

The perimeter of a plot or building is the first and most crucial dimension to be determined in Vastu planning. It sets the stage for all subsequent design decisions.

*   **Importance of Perimeter:**
    *   **Energetic Containment:** The perimeter defines the energetic field of the space. A well-proportioned perimeter helps in containing and channeling positive energies while mitigating negative ones.
    *   **Boundary Definition:** It clearly demarcates the sacred space from the external environment.
    *   **Foundation for Proportions:** The perimeter often serves as the base unit from which other dimensions and proportions are derived.

*   **Principles for Perimeter Selection:**
    *   **Shape:** Rectangular and square plots are generally considered auspicious. Irregular shapes are often discouraged as they can lead to imbalances in energy flow.
        *   **Ideal:** A square is considered the most perfect and balanced shape.
        *   **Next Best:** A rectangle with a length-to-width ratio close to the Golden Ratio (approximately 1.618:1) is also favorable.
    *   **Orientation:** The orientation of the plot and its perimeter relative to cardinal directions is paramount.
        *   **North-South Axis:** Plots with a longer side along the North-South axis are often preferred for better solar exposure and magnetic alignment.
    *   **Dimensional Harmony (Maan):** The actual dimensions of the perimeter should be derived from Vastu-prescribed units and formulas.
        *   **Calculation:** Often, calculations involve specific multiples of "Hasta" or derived units that are deemed auspicious based on astronomical and numerological principles.
        *   **Avoidance of Certain Dimensions:** Vastuvidya texts often specify certain dimensions or ratios that are considered inauspicious and should be avoided.

---

### 4. Proportions in Vastuvidya: The Link to Anthropometrics

Vastuvidya's emphasis on proportion is deeply rooted in anthropometrics, as it seeks to create environments that are in resonance with the human form and its energies.

*   **Human Scale:** The dimensions and proportions used in Vastu are often derived from or related to the human body. This ensures that the built environment feels natural, comfortable, and supportive to its inhabitants.
    *   **Example:** The height of a doorway might be related to the height of a person, or the width of a room might be proportioned to allow for comfortable movement.
*   **Golden Ratio (Phi - φ):** While not explicitly named as such, many proportions in Vastuvidya align with the Golden Ratio, which is found throughout nature and is often associated with beauty and harmony.
    *   **Application:** The ratio of the length to width of rooms, the proportion of walls to openings, and the height of ceilings can all be influenced by principles that approximate the Golden Ratio.
*   **Modular Systems:** Vastuvidya often employs modular systems based on "Hasta" units, creating a consistent and harmonious relationship between different elements of the building.
    *   **Grid System:** The Vastu Purusha Mandala itself is a grid-based system, which facilitates the application of modular dimensions.
*   **Energetic Flow and Balance:** Proportions are not just about aesthetics; they are believed to influence the flow of subtle energies (prana) within the space.
    *   **Well-proportioned spaces:** facilitate smooth and beneficial energy circulation.
    *   **Poorly proportioned spaces:** can create blockages or turbulence in energy flow, leading to discomfort or ill effects.

### 5. Practical Application and Examples

Let's consider a simplified example to illustrate these concepts.

**Scenario:** Designing a simple rectangular residential room.

1.  **Determine the Perimeter:**
    *   Assume we have a plot of a certain size. We need to decide on the room's external dimensions.
    *   **Shape:** Let's aim for a shape that is close to a rectangle.
    *   **Dimensions:** Instead of arbitrary numbers, we would use Vastu-prescribed units. Let's say a "Hasta" is approximately 18 inches.
    *   **Example Dimensions:** We might choose dimensions like 12 Hastas by 16 Hastas (e.g., 18 ft x 24 ft). The ratio here is 1.33:1, which is reasonably close to the Golden Ratio and considered favorable.

2.  **Apply Proportions:**
    *   **Doorway:** The height of the doorway might be 7 Hastas (approx. 10.5 ft), and the width 3 Hastas (approx. 4.5 ft). The ratio of height to width (7:3 ≈ 2.33:1) is a specific Vastu proportion for doorways.
    *   **Window:** The size and placement of windows would also follow Vastu principles, ensuring proper light and ventilation without compromising the energetic integrity of the walls.
    *   **Ceiling Height:** The ceiling height might be proportional to the room's width or length, e.g., 10 Hastas (15 ft).

**Important Note:** The actual calculations in Vastuvidya are much more complex, involving specific charts, tables, and formulas based on astronomical calculations and traditional knowledge. This is a simplified illustration.
