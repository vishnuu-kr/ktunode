# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 2: Cost Concepts

## Topic: Short-Run Cost Curves

---

### **1. Introduction to Short-Run Production and Costs**

*   **Definition: Short Run:** A period during which at least one factor of production is fixed, while others can be varied. In the context of firms, this typically means the firm can change its output level by altering its variable inputs (like labor and raw materials), but its fixed inputs (like plant size and machinery) remain constant.
*   **Definition: Long Run:** A period long enough for all factors of production to be varied. Firms can enter or exit the industry, and existing firms can adjust their plant size.
*   **Key Insight:** In the short run, a firm's ability to increase output is limited by its fixed inputs. This leads to distinct cost behaviors.

---

### **2. Total Cost (TC) in the Short Run**

*   **Definition: Total Cost (TC):** The sum of all costs incurred in producing a given level of output.
*   **Components of Total Cost:**
    *   **Total Fixed Cost (TFC):** Costs that do not change with the level of output. These are associated with the fixed factors of production.
        *   *Examples:* Rent on a factory building, salaries of administrative staff, depreciation of machinery.
    *   **Total Variable Cost (TVC):** Costs that change directly with the level of output. These are associated with the variable factors of production.
        *   *Examples:* Wages of production workers, cost of raw materials, electricity consumed by machinery.
*   **Formula:** $TC = TFC + TVC$

*   **Characteristics of TFC:**
    *   Remains constant regardless of output (as long as the firm operates in the short run).
    *   Graphs as a horizontal line on a Total Cost vs. Output graph.

*   **Characteristics of TVC:**
    *   Is zero when output is zero.
    *   Increases as output increases.
    *   The rate of increase in TVC eventually rises due to diminishing marginal returns.

*   **Example:**
    A bakery hires 5 bakers (fixed input) and can produce cakes.
    *   Monthly Rent (TFC) = $2,000
    *   Cost of Ingredients & Wages of Bakers (TVC)
        *   0 cakes: $0
        *   100 cakes: $500
        *   200 cakes: $900
        *   300 cakes: $1,500
    *   Total Cost (TC)
        *   0 cakes: $2,000 ($2,000 + $0)
        *   100 cakes: $2,500 ($2,000 + $500)
        *   200 cakes: $2,900 ($2,000 + $900)
        *   300 cakes: $3,500 ($2,000 + $1,500)

### **3. Average Cost (AC) Concepts in the Short Run**

*   **Definition: Average Total Cost (ATC):** The total cost per unit of output.
    *   **Formula:** $ATC = TC / Q$ (where Q is the quantity of output)
    *   **Also:** $ATC = AFC + AVC$
*   **Definition: Average Fixed Cost (AFC):** The total fixed cost per unit of output.
    *   **Formula:** $AFC = TFC / Q$
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Decreases continuously as output increases (since TFC is spread over more units).
        *   Graphs as a rectangular hyperbola.
*   **Definition: Average Variable Cost (AVC):** The total variable cost per unit of output.
    *   **Formula:** $AVC = TVC / Q$
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Initially decreases as output increases due to specialization and efficient use of variable inputs.
        *   Eventually increases as output increases due to diminishing marginal returns.
        *   Graphs as a U-shaped curve.

*   **Relationship between AFC, AVC, and ATC:**
    *   ATC is the sum of AFC and AVC.
    *   The vertical distance between ATC and AVC is AFC.
    *   As output increases, AFC gets smaller, causing ATC to get closer to AVC.
    *   The minimum point of the ATC curve occurs at a higher output level than the minimum point of the AVC curve.

*   **Example (Continuing from previous):**
    | Output (Q) | TFC     | TVC     | TC      | AFC ($TFC/Q$) | AVC ($TVC/Q$) | ATC ($TC/Q$) |
    | :--------- | :------ | :------ | :------ | :------------ | :------------ | :------------ |
    | 0          | $2,000 | $0      | $2,000 | N/A           | N/A           | N/A           |
    | 100        | $2,000 | $500    | $2,500 | $20           | $5            | $25           |
    | 200        | $2,000 | $900    | $2,900 | $10           | $4.50         | $14.50        |
    | 300        | $2,000 | $1,500  | $3,500 | $6.67         | $5            | $11.67        |
    | 400        | $2,000 | $2,300  | $4,300 | $5            | $5.75         | $10.75        |
    | 500        | $2,000 | $3,500  | $5,500 | $4            | $7            | $11           |

### **4. Marginal Cost (MC) in the Short Run**

*   **Definition: Marginal Cost (MC):** The additional cost incurred from producing one more unit of output.
    *   **Formula:** $MC = \Delta TC / \Delta Q$
    *   Since TFC does not change with output, $MC = \Delta TVC / \Delta Q$.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Initially falls as output increases because the additional output from using more variable inputs is initially large.
    *   Eventually rises as output increases due to diminishing marginal returns. When the marginal product of the variable input starts to fall, the marginal cost of producing an additional unit of output begins to rise.
    *   Graphs as a U-shaped curve.

*   **Relationship between MC, AVC, and ATC:**
    *   **MC intersects AVC at the minimum point of AVC.** When the cost of producing the last unit is lower than the average variable cost, it pulls the AVC down. When it's higher, it pulls AVC up.
    *   **MC intersects ATC at the minimum point of ATC.** Similarly, when the cost of producing the last unit is lower than the average total cost, it pulls ATC down. When it's higher, it pulls ATC up.
    *   When $MC < AVC$, AVC is falling.
    *   When $MC > AVC$, AVC is rising.
    *   When $MC < ATC$, ATC is falling.
    *   When $MC > ATC$, ATC is rising.

*   **Example (Continuing from previous):**
    | Output (Q) | TVC     | $\Delta TVC$ | $\Delta Q$ | MC ($\Delta TVC / \Delta Q$) | AVC ($TVC/Q$) | ATC ($TC/Q$) |
    | :--------- | :------ | :----------- | :--------- | :--------------------------- | :------------ | :------------ |
    | 0          | $0      | N/A          | N/A        | N/A                          | N/A           | N/A           |
    | 100        | $500    | $500         | 100        | $5                           | $5            | $25           |
    | 200        | $900    | $400         | 100        | $4                           | $4.50         | $14.50        |
    | 300        | $1,500  | $600         | 100        | $6                           | $5            | $11.67        |
    | 400        | $2,300  | $800         | 100        | $8                           | $5.75         | $10.75        |
    | 500        | $3,500  | $1,200       | 100        | $12                          | $7            | $11           |

    *Notice how MC falls and then rises, and it intersects AVC and ATC at their lowest points (or just before the next output level where they would be lowest).*

### **5. Graphical Representation of Short-Run Cost Curves**

*   **Layout:** Typically, Total Cost (TC), Total Fixed Cost (TFC), and Total Variable Cost (TVC) are plotted on one graph against Quantity (Q).
*   Average Cost (AFC, AVC, ATC) and Marginal Cost (MC) are plotted on a separate graph, also against Quantity (Q).
*   **Key Features of the Average/Marginal Cost Graph:**
    *   AFC is downward sloping.
    *   AVC is U-shaped.
    *   MC is U-shaped and intersects AVC and ATC at their minimum points.
    *   The vertical distance between ATC and AVC is AFC.
    *   The MC curve lies below AVC and ATC when they are falling, and above them when they are rising.

---

### **6. The Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns and its Impact on Costs**

*   **Definition: Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns:** As more and more units of a variable input are added to a fixed input, eventually the marginal product of the variable input will decrease.
*   **Impact on Costs:**
    *   Initially, as variable inputs are added and marginal product is increasing, TVC and MC tend to fall (or rise at a decreasing rate).
    *   As marginal product starts to fall, a greater amount of variable input is needed to produce each additional unit of output. This causes TVC to rise at an increasing rate, and consequently, MC rises.
    *   The U-shape of AVC and MC curves is a direct consequence of the law of diminishing marginal returns.

### **7. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
A firm's fixed costs are $100. Its total variable costs at different output levels are as follows:
| Output (Q) | TVC ($) |
| :--------- | :------ |
| 0          | 0       |
| 10         | 50      |
| 20         | 90      |
| 30         | 150     |
| 40         | 240     |

Calculate the following for each output level:
a) Total Cost (TC)
b) Average Fixed Cost (AFC)
c) Average Variable Cost (AVC)
d) Average Total Cost (ATC)
e) Marginal Cost (MC)

**Answer 1:**

| Q  | TFC ($) | TVC ($) | TC ($) | AFC ($) | AVC ($) | ATC ($) | MC ($) |
| :-- | :------ | :------ | :----- | :------ | :------ | :------ | :----- |
| 0  | 100     | 0       | 100    | N/A     | N/A     | N/A     | N/A    |
| 10 | 100     | 50      | 150    | 10      | 5       | 15      | 5      |
| 20 | 100     | 90      | 190    | 5       | 4.5     | 9.5     | 4      |
| 30 | 100     | 150     | 250    | 3.33    | 5       | 8.33    | 6      |
| 40 | 100     | 240     | 340    | 2.5     | 6       | 8.5     | 9      |

*   **Calculations:**
    *   TC = TFC + TVC
    *   AFC = TFC / Q
    *   AVC = TVC / Q
    *   ATC = TC / Q (or AFC + AVC)
    *   MC = $\Delta TC / \Delta Q$ (or $\Delta TVC / \Delta Q$)

**Question 2:**
Which of the following statements is TRUE regarding short-run cost curves?
a) The MC curve intersects the AVC curve at the AVC curve's minimum point.
b) The AFC curve is U-shaped.
c) The ATC curve is always below the AVC curve.
d) The MC curve is always downward sloping.

**Answer 2:**
a) The MC curve intersects the AVC curve at the AVC curve's minimum point.

### **8. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Short Run vs. Long Run:** The distinction is crucial. Fixed vs. variable costs only apply in the short run.
*   **Diminishing Marginal Returns:** The fundamental driver behind the shape of the MC and AVC curves.
*   **U-Shaped Curves:** AVC, ATC, and MC are typically U-shaped in the short run.
*   **MC and AVC/ATC Intersection:** MC intersects both AVC and ATC at their respective minimum points.
*   **AFC:** Always declines and never reaches zero.
*   **Relationship Check:** Always ensure $TC = TFC + TVC$ and $ATC = AFC + AVC$.
*   **Engineering Application:** Understanding these cost curves helps engineers make decisions about production levels, resource allocation, and process improvements that minimize costs for a given output, considering capacity constraints. For example, choosing the optimal batch size or staffing level for a particular machine.
