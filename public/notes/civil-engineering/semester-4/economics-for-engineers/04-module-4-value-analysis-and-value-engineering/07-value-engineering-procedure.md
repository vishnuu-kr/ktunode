### **Introduction to Value Engineering (VE)**

Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic, organized approach to providing the necessary functions in a project at the lowest possible cost without sacrificing quality, performance, reliability, or safety. It is a creative problem-solving technique focused on identifying and eliminating unnecessary costs while enhancing product or service functionality.

**Key Concept:** The core of VE is the concept of **Value**, which is defined as:

**Value (V) = Function (F) / Cost (C)**

*   **Function:** What the product/service *does*. It's the purpose, the performance, the utility it provides.
*   **Cost:** The resources (money, time, materials, labor) required to achieve the function.

The goal of VE is to **increase value** by either:
*   Improving the Function (F) while keeping Cost (C) the same or increasing it minimally.
*   Reducing the Cost (C) while keeping Function (F) the same or decreasing it minimally.
*   Improving Function (F) and reducing Cost (C) simultaneously.

### **The Value Engineering Procedure (The VE Job Plan)**

The VE procedure, often referred to as the **VE Job Plan**, is a structured, step-by-step methodology divided into several distinct phases. While variations exist, the most common and comprehensive plan includes five core phases and a final management/follow-up phase.

#### **Phase 1: Information Phase (Orientation)**

**Purpose:** To gather all relevant information about the product, project, or service to fully understand its current state, functions, costs, and constraints. This phase is crucial for establishing a solid foundation for subsequent analysis.

**Key Activities & Techniques:**

*   **Define the Scope:** Clearly establish what is being analyzed.
*   **Gather Data:** Collect all pertinent information, including:
    *   **Drawings and specifications:** Technical details, blueprints.
    *   **Cost data:** Material costs, labor costs, overhead, manufacturing costs, lifecycle costs.
    *   **Performance data:** How it functions, operational requirements, reliability metrics.
    *   **Market data:** Customer needs, competitor products, industry standards.
    *   **Operational procedures:** How it's used or maintained.
    *   **Customer feedback/complaints:** Pain points and desired improvements.
*   **Define Functions:** This is the *most critical* activity of the Information Phase.
    *   **Basic Functions:** What the product/service *must* do. Essential for its purpose.
    *   **Secondary Functions:** Functions that support the basic functions or are desirable but not essential.
    *   **Techniques for Function Definition:**
        *   **Function Analysis System Technique (FAST Diagramming):** A graphical method to visually map how functions are related, showing "How" and "Why" relationships. This helps in identifying essential functions and opportunities for improvement.
        *   **Verb-Noun Technique:** Describing each function using a verb and a noun (e.g., "Transmit Force," "Contain Liquid," "Support Load").
*   **Identify Cost Drivers:** Determine which components or processes contribute most significantly to the total cost.
*   **Understand Constraints:** Recognize limitations such as regulations, standards, material availability, existing tooling, or customer preferences.

**Example:** For a simple office chair:
*   **Basic Function:** "Support User."
*   **Secondary Functions:** "Allow Reclining," "Provide Arm Support," "Allow Height Adjustment."
*   **Cost Drivers:** High-density foam, adjustable gas lift mechanism, complex frame.
*   **Constraints:** Ergonomic standards, fire safety regulations, aesthetic requirements.

#### **Phase 2: Creative Phase (Speculation/Brainstorming)**

**Purpose:** To generate a wide range of creative ideas and alternatives for improving the value of the product or service. The emphasis is on quantity and originality, with judgment deferred.

**Key Activities & Techniques:**

*   **Brainstorming:** A group activity where participants freely suggest ideas without criticism.
    *   **Rules:**
        *   Defer judgment.
        *   Encourage wild ideas.
        *   Build on the ideas of others.
        *   Go for quantity.
        *   Stay focused on the topic.
*   **Other Idea Generation Techniques:**
    *   **Attribute Listing:** Breaking down the product into its components or attributes and then brainstorming ways to improve each attribute.
    *   **Checklists:** Using pre-defined lists of questions or prompts to stimulate thinking.
    *   **Mind Mapping:** Visually organizing ideas around a central theme.
    *   **SCAMPER Technique:** A mnemonic for brainstorming related to Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse.
*   **Focus on Functions:** Brainstorming should primarily focus on alternative ways to achieve the identified basic functions.

**Example:** Continuing with the office chair, for the "Support User" function, ideas could include:
*   Different seating materials (mesh, recycled plastic).
*   Simpler base design.
*   Eliminating armrests.
*   Using a static seat rather than adjustable height.
*   Contracting out manufacturing of specific components.

#### **Phase 3: Analytical Phase (Evaluation)**

**Purpose:** To evaluate the generated ideas, select the most promising alternatives, and develop them into feasible solutions. This phase involves critical assessment and refinement.

**Key Activities & Techniques:**

*   **Screening Ideas:** Categorize ideas based on feasibility, potential cost savings, and functional impact.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** For promising ideas, estimate the potential cost savings or value increase versus the implementation cost.
*   **Technical Feasibility:** Assess if the proposed solutions are technically viable with current technology or within reasonable development.
*   **Functional Evaluation:** How well do the proposed alternatives perform the required functions?
*   **Risk Assessment:** Identify potential risks associated with implementing each alternative.
*   **Pareto Analysis (80/20 rule):** Focus on the few ideas that will yield the greatest results.
*   **Decision Making:** Select the best alternatives based on the analysis.

**Example:** For the office chair ideas:
*   **Idea:** "Use recycled plastic for seat instead of foam and fabric."
    *   **Evaluation:**
        *   **Cost:** Likely lower material cost.
        *   **Functionality:** Might reduce comfort, may have durability issues if not specified correctly.
        *   **Feasibility:** High, if material properties are suitable.
        *   **Risk:** Customer acceptance, potential cracking over time.
*   **Idea:** "Simplify the base design to a fixed three-leg base."
    *   **Evaluation:**
        *   **Cost:** Significant cost reduction in materials and manufacturing.
        *   **Functionality:** May reduce stability for some users or situations.
        *   **Feasibility:** High.
        *   **Risk:** Stability concerns, aesthetic appeal might be lower.

#### **Phase 4: Development Phase (Proposal)**

**Purpose:** To develop the selected alternatives into detailed, actionable proposals. This involves documenting the proposed solution, its benefits, and the plan for implementation.

**Key Activities & Techniques:**

*   **Detailed Design/Modification:** Create or modify drawings, specifications, and procedures for the proposed changes.
*   **Cost Estimation:** Provide accurate cost estimates for the proposed change, including development, production, and any new tooling.
*   **Benefit Quantification:** Clearly articulate the expected cost savings, performance improvements, or other value enhancements.
*   **Risk Mitigation Plan:** Develop strategies to address identified risks.
*   **Prepare Proposal Document:** Compile all information into a formal proposal that can be presented to management. This often includes:
    *   Executive Summary
    *   Current Situation
    *   Problem Statement/Opportunity
    *   Proposed Solution
    *   Functional Analysis of Proposal
    *   Cost Analysis (Current vs. Proposed)
    *   Savings/Benefits Calculation
    *   Technical Feasibility
    *   Implementation Plan
    *   Risks and Mitigation
    *   Appendices (drawings, data)

**Example:** The proposal for the simplified office chair base might include:
*   New CAD drawings of the three-leg base.
*   Estimated cost savings of $15 per chair.
*   Analysis showing it meets stability standards.
*   A plan to test prototypes and get user feedback.

#### **Phase 5: Implementation Phase (Action)**

**Purpose:** To put the approved VE proposal into action. This is where the actual changes are made to the product, process, or service.

**Key Activities & Techniques:**

*   **Obtain Approval:** Present the proposal to decision-makers for final approval.
*   **Procurement:** Order new materials or components.
*   **Manufacturing/Process Changes:** Modify production lines, update tooling, or retrain personnel.
*   **Testing and Validation:** Ensure the implemented changes meet all functional and quality requirements.
*   **Documentation Updates:** Update all relevant manuals, drawings, and specifications.
*   **Communication:** Keep all stakeholders informed about the progress and changes.

**Example:** Implementing the chair base change:
*   Manufacturing engineering designs new molds for the three-leg base.
*   Purchasing sources cheaper, durable plastic.
*   Production line is reconfigured.
*   New chairs are tested for stability and durability.

#### **Phase 6: Management / Follow-up Phase**

**Purpose:** To monitor the implemented changes, confirm that the expected savings and benefits are realized, and capture lessons learned. This phase ensures accountability and continuous improvement.

**Key Activities & Techniques:**

*   **Performance Monitoring:** Track actual costs, performance metrics, and customer satisfaction after the change.
*   **Benefit Verification:** Quantify the actual savings achieved and compare them to the projected savings.
*   **Feedback Collection:** Gather feedback from users, operators, and customers on the implemented changes.
*   **Lessons Learned:** Document what worked well and what could be improved for future VE projects.
*   **Recognition:** Acknowledge the contributions of the VE team.

**Example:** Tracking the chair base change:
*   Monitor manufacturing costs and compare them to the $15/chair saving projection.
*   Track customer complaints related to chair stability or comfort.
*   Hold a debrief meeting to discuss challenges encountered during implementation.

### **VE Team Roles and Responsibilities**

A successful VE effort often involves a multidisciplinary team. Key roles include:

*   **VE Facilitator/Manager:** Guides the team through the VE process, ensures adherence to the job plan, and facilitates discussions.
*   **Team Members:** Individuals with expertise relevant to the project being analyzed (e.g., design engineers, manufacturing engineers, cost estimators, marketing specialists, purchasing agents, quality control).
*   **Project Manager/Sponsor:** Provides authority, resources, and final approval for VE proposals.
*   **Customers/End Users:** Provide input on functional requirements and satisfaction.

---

### **Key Roles and Responsibilities in VE**

*   **VE Team:**
    *   Actively participate in all phases.
    *   Contribute expertise and ideas.
    *   Commit to achieving the project objectives.
*   **VE Facilitator:**
    *   Lead the team through the VE Job Plan.
    *   Ensure all phases are thoroughly completed.
    *   Manage team dynamics and encourage participation.
    *   Maintain project focus and timelines.
*   **Management/Sponsor:**
    *   Approve the VE project scope and resources.
    *   Provide necessary support and remove roadblocks.
    *   Review and approve VE proposals.
    *   Champion the VE initiative within the organization.

### **How VE Contributes to Cost Reduction and Functional Improvement**

*   **Focus on Function:** By dissecting products/services into their essential functions, VE ensures that only necessary activities and components are retained, eliminating "cost of confusion" or "cost of tradition."
*   **Challenge Assumptions:** The systematic process encourages questioning established norms and designs, leading to innovative and often simpler solutions.
*   **Creativity and Innovation:** The dedicated brainstorming phases foster a culture of creative problem-solving, leading to breakthrough ideas.
*   **Cross-Functional Collaboration:** Bringing together diverse perspectives helps identify solutions that might be missed by individuals working in isolation.
*   **Lifecycle Cost Perspective:** While often focused on initial cost reduction, VE can also address lifecycle costs by improving reliability, maintainability, and energy efficiency.
*   **Competitive Advantage:** By lowering costs without sacrificing quality, organizations can gain a competitive edge in the market.

**Question 6:**
Imagine you are part of a VE team analyzing a simple screwdriver.
*   Identify one **basic function** of a screwdriver.

*   Brainstorm **two creative ideas** to reduce the cost of a screwdriver without compromising its basic function.

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

**Answer 1:**
c) Value = Function / Cost

**Answer 2:**
c) Information Phase

**Answer 3:**
b) Information Phase

**Answer 4:**
c) To generate a large quantity of diverse ideas.

**Answer 5:**
The purpose of the Analytical Phase is to critically evaluate the ideas generated during the Creative Phase, screen them for feasibility and potential value improvement, and select the most promising alternatives for further development.

**Answer 6:**
*   **Basic Function:** "Transmit Torque" or "Drive Fastener."
*   **Secondary Function:** "Provide Grip" or "Insulate Handle" (for electrical screwdrivers).
*   **Creative Ideas to reduce cost:**
    1.  **Use a simpler handle material:** Instead of a soft-grip ergonomic handle, use a standard, molded plastic handle with less complex tooling.
    2.  **Eliminate the magnetic tip:** For basic screwdrivers where magnetic tips are not essential, removing this feature can save on material and manufacturing steps.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **VE is about Functions, not just Costs:** Always tie cost reduction or improvement back to the functions the product or service provides.
*   **Multidisciplinary Teams are Key:** Diverse perspectives lead to better solutions.
*   **Creative Freedom is Essential:** Don't stifle creativity in the early stages.
*   **Systematic Approach:** The VE Job Plan provides a roadmap to ensure all aspects are considered.
*   **Value is Dynamic:** What is considered "value" can change with technology, market demands, and economic conditions.
*   **VE is Proactive:** It's about improvement *before* problems arise, not just fixing things.
*   **Focus on "Why":** Constantly ask "Why is this done this way?" to uncover opportunities.
