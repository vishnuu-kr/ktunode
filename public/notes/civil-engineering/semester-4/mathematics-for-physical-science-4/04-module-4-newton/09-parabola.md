### 1. Definition of a Parabola

A **parabola** is defined geometrically as the set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed point (the **focus**) and a fixed line (the **directrix**).

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Focus (F):** A fixed point.
*   **Directrix (L):** A fixed line.
*   **Any point P on the parabola:** The distance from P to F is equal to the distance from P to L.

---

### 2. Key Components of a Parabola

Let's consider a parabola with its focus at $(p, 0)$ and its directrix as the line $x = -p$.

*   **Focus (F):** $(p, 0)$
*   **Directrix (L):** $x = -p$
*   **Vertex (V):** The midpoint between the focus and the directrix, lying on the axis of symmetry. For the above setup, the vertex is at the origin $(0, 0)$.
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** The line passing through the focus and perpendicular to the directrix. In this case, it's the x-axis (y=0).
*   **Latus Rectum:** The chord of the parabola that passes through the focus, is perpendicular to the axis of symmetry, and has endpoints on the parabola. Its length is $|4p|$. For the parabola $y^2 = 4px$, the latus rectum is the line segment from $(-p, 2p)$ to $(-p, -2p)$.

---

### 3. Standard Equations of Parabolas

We will consider parabolas with vertices at the origin $(0,0)$ and later at a general point $(h,k)$.

#### 3.1. Parabolas with Vertex at the Origin (0, 0)

**a) Parabola Opening Right:**

*   **Equation:** $y^2 = 4px$
*   **Focus:** $(p, 0)$
*   **Directrix:** $x = -p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = 0$ (x-axis)
*   **Opening Direction:** Towards the positive x-axis if $p > 0$, towards the negative x-axis if $p < 0$.
*   **Latus Rectum Endpoints:** $(-p, 2p)$ and $(-p, -2p)$ (if $p > 0$)

**Example 1:** Find the focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $y^2 = 12x$.

*   Comparing with $y^2 = 4px$, we have $4p = 12$, so $p = 3$.
*   **Focus:** $(3, 0)$
*   **Directrix:** $x = -3$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = 0$

**b) Parabola Opening Left:**

*   **Equation:** $y^2 = -4px$ (where $p > 0$)
*   **Focus:** $(-p, 0)$
*   **Directrix:** $x = p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = 0$ (x-axis)
*   **Opening Direction:** Towards the negative x-axis.

**Example 2:** Find the focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $y^2 = -8x$.

*   Comparing with $y^2 = -4px$, we have $4p = 8$, so $p = 2$.
*   **Focus:** $(-2, 0)$
*   **Directrix:** $x = 2$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = 0$

**c) Parabola Opening Up:**

*   **Equation:** $x^2 = 4py$
*   **Focus:** $(0, p)$
*   **Directrix:** $y = -p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = 0$ (y-axis)
*   **Opening Direction:** Towards the positive y-axis if $p > 0$, towards the negative y-axis if $p < 0$.
*   **Latus Rectum Endpoints:** $(2p, p)$ and $(-2p, p)$ (if $p > 0$)

**Example 3:** Find the focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $x^2 = 16y$.

*   Comparing with $x^2 = 4py$, we have $4p = 16$, so $p = 4$.
*   **Focus:** $(0, 4)$
*   **Directrix:** $y = -4$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = 0$

**d) Parabola Opening Down:**

*   **Equation:** $x^2 = -4py$ (where $p > 0$)
*   **Focus:** $(0, -p)$
*   **Directrix:** $y = p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = 0$ (y-axis)
*   **Opening Direction:** Towards the negative y-axis.

**Example 4:** Find the focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $x^2 = -20y$.

*   Comparing with $x^2 = -4py$, we have $4p = 20$, so $p = 5$.
*   **Focus:** $(0, -5)$
*   **Directrix:** $y = 5$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = 0$

---

#### 3.2. Parabolas with Vertex at $(h, k)$

When the vertex is shifted to $(h, k)$, we replace $x$ with $(x-h)$ and $y$ with $(y-k)$ in the standard equations with vertex at the origin.

**a) Parabola Opening Right:**

*   **Equation:** $(y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h)$
*   **Vertex:** $(h, k)$
*   **Focus:** $(h+p, k)$
*   **Directrix:** $x = h-p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = k$

**b) Parabola Opening Left:**

*   **Equation:** $(y-k)^2 = -4p(x-h)$ (where $p > 0$)
*   **Vertex:** $(h, k)$
*   **Focus:** $(h-p, k)$
*   **Directrix:** $x = h+p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = k$

**c) Parabola Opening Up:**

*   **Equation:** $(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k)$
*   **Vertex:** $(h, k)$
*   **Focus:** $(h, k+p)$
*   **Directrix:** $y = k-p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = h$

**d) Parabola Opening Down:**

*   **Equation:** $(x-h)^2 = -4p(y-k)$ (where $p > 0$)
*   **Vertex:** $(h, k)$
*   **Focus:** $(h, k-p)$
*   **Directrix:** $y = k+p$
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = h$

**Example 5:** Find the vertex, focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $(y-2)^2 = 8(x+1)$.

*   Comparing with $(y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h)$:
    *   $h = -1$, $k = 2$. So, **Vertex:** $(-1, 2)$.
    *   $4p = 8$, so $p = 2$.
*   Since it's of the form $(y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h)$ and $p > 0$, it opens to the right.
*   **Focus:** $(h+p, k) = (-1+2, 2) = (1, 2)$.
*   **Directrix:** $x = h-p = -1-2 = -3$.
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = k$, which is $y = 2$.

**Example 6:** Find the vertex, focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $(x+3)^2 = -12(y-1)$.

*   Comparing with $(x-h)^2 = -4p(y-k)$:
    *   $h = -3$, $k = 1$. So, **Vertex:** $(-3, 1)$.
    *   $-4p = -12$, so $4p = 12$, and $p = 3$.
*   Since it's of the form $(x-h)^2 = -4p(y-k)$ and $p > 0$, it opens downwards.
*   **Focus:** $(h, k-p) = (-3, 1-3) = (-3, -2)$.
*   **Directrix:** $y = k+p = 1+3 = 4$.
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = h$, which is $x = -3$.

### 4. Determining Properties from the Equation

To find the properties of a parabola from its general equation, we need to convert it into the standard form. This often involves completing the square.

**Example 7:** Find the vertex, focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $y^2 - 4y - 8x + 12 = 0$.

1.  **Isolate the squared term:**
    $y^2 - 4y = 8x - 12$

2.  **Complete the square for the y terms:**
    To complete the square for $y^2 - 4y$, we add $(\frac{-4}{2})^2 = (-2)^2 = 4$ to both sides.
    $y^2 - 4y + 4 = 8x - 12 + 4$
    $(y-2)^2 = 8x - 8$

3.  **Factor out the coefficient of x:**
    $(y-2)^2 = 8(x-1)$

4.  **Identify properties by comparing with the standard form:**
    *   This is of the form $(y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h)$, so it opens to the right.
    *   $h = 1$, $k = 2$. **Vertex:** $(1, 2)$.
    *   $4p = 8$, so $p = 2$.
    *   **Focus:** $(h+p, k) = (1+2, 2) = (3, 2)$.
    *   **Directrix:** $x = h-p = 1-2 = -1$.
    *   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = k$, which is $y = 2$.

**Example 8:** Find the vertex, focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $x^2 + 6x + 12y - 3 = 0$.

1.  **Isolate the squared term:**
    $x^2 + 6x = -12y + 3$

2.  **Complete the square for the x terms:**
    To complete the square for $x^2 + 6x$, we add $(\frac{6}{2})^2 = (3)^2 = 9$ to both sides.
    $x^2 + 6x + 9 = -12y + 3 + 9$
    $(x+3)^2 = -12y + 12$

3.  **Factor out the coefficient of y:**
    $(x+3)^2 = -12(y-1)$

4.  **Identify properties by comparing with the standard form:**
    *   This is of the form $(x-h)^2 = -4p(y-k)$, so it opens downwards.
    *   $h = -3$, $k = 1$. **Vertex:** $(-3, 1)$.
    *   $-4p = -12$, so $4p = 12$, and $p = 3$.
    *   **Focus:** $(h, k-p) = (-3, 1-3) = (-3, -2)$.
    *   **Directrix:** $y = k+p = 1+3 = 4$.
    *   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = h$, which is $x = -3$.

### 5. Sketching the Graph of a Parabola

To sketch the graph, follow these steps:

1.  **Determine the vertex:** Identify $(h, k)$.
2.  **Determine the direction of opening:**
    *   $(y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h)$: Opens right if $p>0$, left if $p<0$.
    *   $(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k)$: Opens up if $p>0$, down if $p<0$.
3.  **Find the focus and directrix:** Use the values of $h, k,$ and $p$.
4.  **Find the endpoints of the latus rectum:** These points are $|2p|$ units away from the focus along the axis of symmetry, and perpendicular to it.
5.  **Plot the vertex, focus, and sketch the directrix.**
6.  **Draw the parabola** passing through the vertex and the endpoints of the latus rectum, opening in the determined direction.

**Example 9:** Sketch the graph of the parabola $y^2 = 4x$.

*   **Vertex:** $(0, 0)$.
*   **Equation:** $y^2 = 4px$, so $4p = 4$, $p=1$.
*   **Direction:** Opens to the right (since $p>0$).
*   **Focus:** $(1, 0)$.
*   **Directrix:** $x = -1$.
*   **Latus Rectum Endpoints:** $(-p, \pm 2p) = (-1, \pm 2)$.
*   **Plot:** Vertex (0,0), Focus (1,0). Draw directrix $x=-1$. Plot points (-1, 2) and (-1, -2).
*   **Sketch:** Draw a smooth curve passing through these points.

**Example 10:** Sketch the graph of the parabola $(x-2)^2 = -8(y+1)$.

*   **Vertex:** $(2, -1)$. (Since $h=2, k=-1$)
*   **Equation:** $(x-h)^2 = -4p(y-k)$, so $-4p = -8$, $p=2$.
*   **Direction:** Opens downwards (since $-4p$ is negative).
*   **Focus:** $(h, k-p) = (2, -1-2) = (2, -3)$.
*   **Directrix:** $y = k+p = -1+2 = 1$.
*   **Latus Rectum Endpoints:** The axis of symmetry is $x=2$. The latus rectum is at $y = k-p = -3$. The x-coordinates are $h \pm 2p$. So, $2 \pm 2(2) = 2 \pm 4$. Endpoints are $(6, -3)$ and $(-2, -3)$.
*   **Plot:** Vertex (2,-1), Focus (2,-3). Draw directrix $y=1$. Plot points (6, -3) and (-2, -3).
*   **Sketch:** Draw a smooth curve passing through these points.

### 6. Applications in Physical Science

Parabolas have numerous applications in physical science:

*   **Projectile Motion:** The trajectory of a projectile under the influence of gravity (neglecting air resistance) is a parabola. The vertex represents the highest point of the trajectory.
    *   If a projectile is launched with initial velocity $v_0$ at an angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, its position $(x, y)$ at time $t$ is given by:
        *   $x(t) = (v_0 \cos \theta) t$
        *   $y(t) = (v_0 \sin \theta) t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
        *   Eliminating $t$ results in a parabolic equation.
*   **Telescope and Satellite Dishes:** The reflective property of a parabola is used to focus parallel rays of light or radio waves to a single point (the focus). This is crucial for collecting faint signals.
*   **Headlights:** The reflector in a headlight is parabolic, ensuring that light from the bulb at the focus is reflected into a parallel beam.
*   **Catenary (Approximation):** For certain suspension bridges under uniform load, the shape can be approximated by a parabola. (The true shape is a catenary, $y = a \cosh(x/a)$).

**Physical Science Example:** A projectile is launched with an initial velocity of 20 m/s at an angle of 30 degrees to the horizontal. The trajectory of the projectile can be modeled by a parabola. Let's find the equation of this parabola.

*   Initial velocity $v_0 = 20$ m/s.
*   Launch angle $\theta = 30^\circ$.
*   Acceleration due to gravity $g \approx 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

The horizontal component of velocity is $v_{0x} = v_0 \cos \theta = 20 \cos 30^\circ = 20 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 10\sqrt{3}$ m/s.
The vertical component of velocity is $v_{0y} = v_0 \sin \theta = 20 \sin 30^\circ = 20 \times \frac{1}{2} = 10$ m/s.

The position equations are:
$x(t) = (10\sqrt{3})t$  => $t = \frac{x}{10\sqrt{3}}$
$y(t) = 10t - \frac{1}{2}(9.8)t^2 = 10t - 4.9t^2$

Substitute $t$:
$y = 10\left(\frac{x}{10\sqrt{3}}\right) - 4.9\left(\frac{x}{10\sqrt{3}}\right)^2$
$y = \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}} - 4.9\left(\frac{x^2}{100 \times 3}\right)$
$y = \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{4.9x^2}{300}$
$y = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}x - \frac{4.9}{300}x^2$

This is the equation of the parabolic trajectory. We can also rearrange this into a more standard form if needed, though it's not as straightforward as the geometric definitions.

To find the vertex (maximum height):
The vertex occurs when the vertical velocity is zero: $v_y(t) = v_{0y} - gt = 0$.
$10 - 9.8t = 0 \implies t = \frac{10}{9.8} \approx 1.02$ s.
The time to reach the vertex is $t_{vertex} = \frac{v_0 \sin \theta}{g} = \frac{10}{9.8}$.
The x-coordinate of the vertex is $x_{vertex} = (v_0 \cos \theta) t_{vertex} = (10\sqrt{3}) \times \frac{10}{9.8} = \frac{100\sqrt{3}}{9.8} \approx 17.67$ m.
The y-coordinate of the vertex (maximum height) is $y_{vertex} = (v_0 \sin \theta) t_{vertex} - \frac{1}{2}gt_{vertex}^2 = 10 \times \frac{10}{9.8} - 4.9 \times (\frac{10}{9.8})^2 = \frac{100}{9.8} - 4.9 \times \frac{100}{96.04} = \frac{100}{9.8} - \frac{490}{96.04} \approx 10.20 - 5.10 = 5.10$ m.
So the vertex is approximately $(17.67, 5.10)$.

### 7. Practice Questions

**Question 1:** Find the focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $y^2 = -16x$.

**Question 2:** Find the vertex, focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry of the parabola $(x+1)^2 = 12(y-3)$.

**Question 3:** Determine the equation of the parabola with focus $(4, 0)$ and directrix $x = -4$.

**Question 4:** Convert the equation $x^2 - 6x - 8y + 17 = 0$ into standard form and find its vertex, focus, directrix, and axis of symmetry.

**Question 5:** A satellite dish has a parabolic cross-section. The receiver is placed at the focus of the parabola. If the dish is 1 meter wide and 0.25 meters deep at its deepest point, find the equation of the parabola and the distance from the vertex to the receiver. (Assume the vertex is at the origin and the dish opens upwards).

### 8. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
*   Compare $y^2 = -16x$ with $y^2 = -4px$.
*   $4p = 16 \implies p = 4$.
*   **Focus:** $(-p, 0) = (-4, 0)$.
*   **Directrix:** $x = p = 4$.
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $y = 0$ (the x-axis).

**Answer 2:**
*   Compare $(x+1)^2 = 12(y-3)$ with $(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k)$.
*   $h = -1$, $k = 3$. **Vertex:** $(-1, 3)$.
*   $4p = 12 \implies p = 3$.
*   Since $4p > 0$ and it's an $(x-h)^2$ form, it opens upwards.
*   **Focus:** $(h, k+p) = (-1, 3+3) = (-1, 6)$.
*   **Directrix:** $y = k-p = 3-3 = 0$.
*   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = h \implies x = -1$.

**Answer 3:**
*   The focus $(4, 0)$ lies on the x-axis, and the directrix $x=-4$ is a vertical line. This implies the axis of symmetry is the x-axis ($y=0$).
*   The vertex is the midpoint between the focus $(4,0)$ and the directrix $x=-4$, which is $(0,0)$.
*   The distance from the vertex to the focus is $p = 4$.
*   Since the focus is to the right of the vertex, the parabola opens to the right.
*   The standard equation for a parabola opening right with vertex at the origin is $y^2 = 4px$.
*   Substituting $p=4$: $y^2 = 4(4)x \implies \boxed{y^2 = 16x}$.

**Answer 4:**
*   Start with $x^2 - 6x - 8y + 17 = 0$.
*   Isolate x terms: $x^2 - 6x = 8y - 17$.
*   Complete the square for x: $x^2 - 6x + 9 = 8y - 17 + 9$.
*   $(x-3)^2 = 8y - 8$.
*   Factor out the coefficient of y: $(x-3)^2 = 8(y-1)$.
*   **Standard Form:** $(x-3)^2 = 8(y-1)$.
*   Compare with $(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k)$:
    *   $h = 3$, $k = 1$. **Vertex:** $(3, 1)$.
    *   $4p = 8 \implies p = 2$.
    *   Since $4p > 0$ and it's an $(x-h)^2$ form, it opens upwards.
    *   **Focus:** $(h, k+p) = (3, 1+2) = (3, 3)$.
    *   **Directrix:** $y = k-p = 1-2 = -1$.
    *   **Axis of Symmetry:** $x = h \implies x = 3$.

**Answer 5:**
*   Vertex at $(0, 0)$, opening upwards. Standard form: $x^2 = 4py$.
*   The dish is 1 meter wide, so its endpoints are at $x = \pm 0.5$.
*   It is 0.25 meters deep at its deepest point, which is the vertex. However, this means the points on the rim are at a y-coordinate of 0.25.
*   Let's use a point on the rim. For example, $(0.5, 0.25)$.
*   Substitute into the equation: $(0.5)^2 = 4p(0.25)$.
*   $0.25 = 4p(0.25)$.
*   $1 = 4p$.
*   $p = 1/4 = 0.25$.
*   **Equation of the parabola:** $x^2 = 4(0.25)y \implies \boxed{x^2 = y}$.
*   **Distance from the vertex to the receiver (focus):** $p = 0.25$ meters.

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   A parabola is the locus of points equidistant from a focus and a directrix.
*   The vertex is the midpoint between the focus and the directrix.
*   The axis of symmetry passes through the focus and is perpendicular to the directrix.
*   Master the standard forms of the parabola equations for vertex at origin and at $(h, k)$.
*   Completing the square is a crucial technique for converting general quadratic equations into standard parabolic forms.
*   The sign of $p$ determines the direction of opening:
    *   $y^2 = 4px$: $p>0$ opens right, $p<0$ opens left.
    *   $x^2 = 4py$: $p>0$ opens up, $p<0$ opens down.
*   The length of the latus rectum is $|4p|$.
*   Parabolic shapes are fundamental in understanding projectile motion, optics, and signal reception.
