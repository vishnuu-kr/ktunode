# DESIGN OF CONCRETE STRUCTURES - Module 3: Limit State of Serviceability

## Topic: Analysis of Short Axially Loaded Compression Members under Axial Load with Uniaxial Bending & Biaxial Bending - Numerical Problems

This module delves into the behavior of concrete compression members subjected to combined axial load and bending. We will focus on **short** members, meaning buckling effects are considered negligible and the primary failure mode is crushing of concrete and yielding of steel under the applied loads. The analysis will be performed using the **Limit State Design** philosophy.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Compression Member:** A structural element primarily subjected to axial compressive force. Examples include columns and posts.
*   **Short Compression Member:** A compression member whose slenderness ratio (effective length divided by least lateral dimension) is sufficiently small to prevent buckling as the primary failure mode. Typically, a slenderness ratio below a certain threshold (often around 10-15, depending on the code) is considered short.
*   **Axial Load:** A force acting along the longitudinal axis of the member.
*   **Bending Moment:** A moment causing the member to bend.
*   **Uniaxial Bending:** Bending about one principal axis of the cross-section.
*   **Biaxial Bending:** Bending about both principal axes of the cross-section simultaneously.
*   **Limit State of Serviceability:** The state beyond which the structure or a part of it ceases to perform its intended function under normal service conditions. For compression members, this relates to preventing excessive stress or deformation that could lead to cracking or spalling of concrete.
*   **Interaction Diagram:** A graphical representation showing the relationship between axial load capacity and bending moment capacity for a given cross-section. It defines the combinations of axial load and bending moment that the member can resist.
*   **Balanced Failure:** A failure mode where the concrete crushes and the tension steel yields simultaneously. This point is critical on the interaction diagram.
*   **Pure Compression:** When the axial load is the only load acting on the member.
*   **Pure Bending:** When the bending moment is the only load acting on the member.
*   **Concrete Stress Block:** A simplified representation of the distribution of compressive stresses in the concrete at the ultimate limit state. Common models include the rectangular stress block (Whitney stress block).
*   **Strain Compatibility:** The principle that all materials within a cross-section deform by the same amount at the same location under load.
*   **Equilibrium Equations:** Summation of forces and moments in the vertical and horizontal directions, and about any point, must be zero for the section to be in equilibrium.

### 3. Analysis of Short Axially Loaded Compression Members under Axial Load with Uniaxial Bending

When a compression member is subjected to both axial load ($P$) and a bending moment ($M$), the stresses developed are a combination of axial stress and bending stress. The design checks ensure that the combined effect does not exceed the capacity of the member.

#### 3.1 Key Principles

*   **Superposition of Stresses:** In the serviceability limit state, the stresses due to axial load and bending moment are additive.
*   **Cross-section analysis:** The capacity of the cross-section is determined by considering the stress distribution in concrete and steel.
*   **Design Codes:** Codes like IS 456:2000 or Eurocode 2 provide methods for calculating the capacity and checking the design.

#### 3.2 Interaction Diagrams

Interaction diagrams are fundamental tools for analyzing and designing members under combined axial load and bending.

*   **Construction:** These diagrams are typically generated by calculating the moment capacity for various levels of axial load (or vice-versa) for a specific cross-section and reinforcement details.
*   **Key Points:**
    *   **Maximum Axial Load (Pure Compression):** Represents the capacity when the bending moment is zero.
    *   **Maximum Moment Capacity (Pure Bending):** Represents the capacity when the axial load is zero (though compression members are rarely designed for pure bending).
    *   **Balanced Point:** The point where concrete crushing and tension steel yielding occur simultaneously.
    *   **Various Load Levels:** Points on the curve represent combinations of axial load and bending moment that the section can resist.

#### 3.3 Design Check Procedure (Simplified)

For a given axial load ($P_{Ed}$) and bending moment ($M_{Ed}$), the design check typically involves:

1.  **Determine the Cross-section Properties:** Dimensions, concrete grade, steel grade, and reinforcement details.
2.  **Calculate the Factored Loads:** Apply load factors as per the relevant code.
3.  **Consult or Generate Interaction Diagram:** Obtain the interaction diagram for the given cross-section and reinforcement.
4.  **Locate the Design Point:** Plot the factored axial load ($P_{Ed}$) and factored bending moment ($M_{Ed}$) on the diagram.
5.  **Check for Adequacy:** If the point ($P_{Ed}, M_{Ed}$) falls within or on the boundary of the interaction diagram, the member is considered adequate.

#### 3.4 Numerical Problem Example (Uniaxial Bending)

**Problem:** A short rectangular reinforced concrete column has a cross-section of 300 mm x 400 mm. It is reinforced with 6 bars of 20 mm diameter (3000 mm² total area). The column is subjected to a factored axial load of $P_{Ed} = 1500$ kN and a factored bending moment of $M_{Ed} = 120$ kNm about the axis parallel to the 400 mm side. The concrete grade is M25 and steel grade is Fe415. Check the adequacy of the column.

**Solution Approach (Conceptual - actual calculation involves detailed code procedures or software):**

1.  **Effective Depth ($d$):** Assume a cover of 40 mm. For bending about the 400 mm side, the effective depth will be around $d = 400 - 40 - 8 = 352$ mm (assuming 8 mm stirrups).
2.  **Lever Arm:** For a given axial load, the lever arm to the resultant force in the concrete stress block can be calculated.
3.  **Moment Capacity Calculation:** Using concrete stress block parameters (e.g., $0.36 f_{ck}$ and $0.42 x_{u,max}$ for IS 456:2000) and steel stresses (typically $0.87 f_y$ for tension steel at ultimate limit state), calculate the moment capacity for the given axial load. This involves solving for the neutral axis depth based on force equilibrium and strain compatibility.
4.  **Interaction Diagram:** For a realistic design check, one would consult a pre-computed interaction diagram or use software to generate it. Let's assume, for illustration, that for $P_{Ed} = 1500$ kN, the moment capacity ($M_{Rd}$) is found to be 130 kNm.
5.  **Check:** Since $M_{Ed} = 120$ kNm is less than $M_{Rd} = 130$ kNm, the column is adequate for this load combination.

**Important Note:** Performing the exact calculation of moment capacity for a given axial load involves iterative solutions or specialized charts/software. The above is a simplified illustration of the check.

### 4. Analysis of Short Axially Loaded Compression Members under Axial Load with Biaxial Bending

Biaxial bending in compression members is more complex as it involves bending about both principal axes ($x-x$ and $y-y$).

#### 4.1 Key Concepts

*   **Principal Axes:** The axes of the cross-section about which the moment of inertia is maximum and minimum.
*   **Principal Axes of Bending:** Bending occurs about these axes.
*   **Combined Bending Moments:** The axial load is accompanied by moments $M_{ux}$ and $M_{uy}$ about the $x-x$ and $y-y$ axes, respectively.

#### 4.2 Interaction Diagrams for Biaxial Bending

*   **More Complex:** Interaction diagrams for biaxial bending are 3D surfaces or contours. For a given axial load, the diagram represents the allowable combinations of $M_{ux}$ and $M_{uy}$.
*   **General Form:** Often represented as a surface in $P_u - M_{ux} - M_{uy}$ space.
*   **Design Codes:** Codes provide approximate methods and interaction surfaces/formulas to simplify the analysis.

#### 4.3 Methods for Biaxial Bending Analysis

1.  **Load Contour Method (Simplified):** This method reduces the biaxial bending problem to an equivalent uniaxial bending problem.
    *   **Concept:** The biaxial bending is represented by a single equivalent bending moment ($M_u$) acting about an axis that is inclined at an angle $\theta$ to the $x-x$ axis.
    *   **Formula (IS 456:2000, Clause 32.3.3):**
        $$ \frac{M_{ux}}{M_{ux1}} + \frac{M_{uy}}{M_{uy1}} \le 1.0 $$
        Where:
        *   $M_{ux}$ is the factored moment about the $x-x$ axis.
        *   $M_{uy}$ is the factored moment about the $y-y$ axis.
        *   $M_{ux1}$ is the ultimate moment capacity of the column resisting $M_{ux}$ *only* (i.e., $M_{uy}=0$).
        *   $M_{uy1}$ is the ultimate moment capacity of the column resisting $M_{uy}$ *only* (i.e., $M_{ux}=0$).
    *   **Assumptions:** This equation is an approximation and is generally valid for members with relatively symmetrical reinforcement.
    *   **Consideration of Axial Load:** This equation is applied at a specific axial load level ($P_u$). The values of $M_{ux1}$ and $M_{uy1}$ are read from the uniaxial interaction diagrams for that axial load level.

2.  **Load Contour Charts/Software:** More accurate methods involve using specialized charts or finite element software that can directly handle biaxial bending.

#### 4.4 Numerical Problem Example (Biaxial Bending)

**Problem:** A short square reinforced concrete column has a cross-section of 350 mm x 350 mm. It is reinforced with 8 bars of 20 mm diameter (2512 mm² total area). The column is subjected to a factored axial load of $P_{Ed} = 1200$ kN. It also experiences factored moments: $M_{ux} = 80$ kNm (about the axis parallel to one side) and $M_{uy} = 60$ kNm (about the axis parallel to the other side). The concrete grade is M20 and steel grade is Fe415. Check the adequacy of the column using the load contour method.

**Solution Approach:**

1.  **Determine Uniaxial Capacities:**
    *   **For Uniaxial Bending about $x-x$ (or $y-y$):** Assume a similar effective depth ($d$ or $d'$). Since it's a square section, $M_{ux1}$ and $M_{uy1}$ will be similar if the reinforcement distribution is symmetrical. For illustration, let's assume:
        *   For $P_{Ed} = 1200$ kN, the uniaxial moment capacity $M_{ux1} = M_{uy1} = 110$ kNm (this value would be obtained from uniaxial interaction diagrams for the specific section).
2.  **Apply Load Contour Method:**
    *   Substitute the values into the equation:
        $$ \frac{M_{ux}}{M_{ux1}} + \frac{M_{uy}}{M_{uy1}} $$
        $$ \frac{80 \text{ kNm}}{110 \text{ kNm}} + \frac{60 \text{ kNm}}{110 \text{ kNm}} $$
        $$ 0.727 + 0.545 = 1.272 $$
3.  **Check for Adequacy:**
    *   Since $1.272 > 1.0$, the condition is **not satisfied**. The column is **inadequate** to resist the combined biaxial bending moments at the given axial load.

**What if the column was adequate?**

If the sum was $\le 1.0$, the column would be considered adequate. For example, if $M_{ux} = 50$ kNm and $M_{uy} = 50$ kNm:

$$ \frac{50}{110} + \frac{50}{110} = 0.455 + 0.455 = 0.910 $$
Since $0.910 \le 1.0$, the column would be adequate in this case.

### 5. Practice Questions/Exercises

**Question 1:**
A short circular reinforced concrete column of diameter 400 mm is reinforced with 8 bars of 25 mm diameter. It is subjected to a factored axial load of 1400 kN and a factored uniaxial bending moment of 100 kNm about an axis passing through the center.
*   **Given:** Concrete grade M20, Steel grade Fe415.
*   **Task:** Briefly describe the steps to check the adequacy of this column. If the uniaxial interaction diagram for this section at the given axial load shows a moment capacity of 115 kNm, is the column adequate?

**Answer 1:**

*   **Steps:**
    1.  Determine the cross-section properties (diameter, area of concrete, area of steel).
    2.  Consider the factored axial load ($P_{Ed} = 1400$ kN) and the factored bending moment ($M_{Ed} = 100$ kNm).
    3.  Refer to the uniaxial interaction diagram for the given cross-section at the axial load of 1400 kN to find the moment capacity ($M_{Rd}$).
    4.  Compare the applied moment ($M_{Ed}$) with the moment capacity ($M_{Rd}$). If $M_{Ed} \le M_{Rd}$, the column is adequate.
*   **Adequacy Check:** Since $M_{Ed} = 100$ kNm and $M_{Rd} = 115$ kNm, and $100 \text{ kNm} \le 115 \text{ kNm}$, the column is **adequate**.

**Question 2:**
A short square reinforced concrete column of size 300 mm x 300 mm is reinforced with 6 bars of 16 mm diameter. It is subjected to a factored axial load of $P_{Ed} = 1000$ kN. It also experiences factored moments $M_{ux} = 50$ kNm and $M_{uy} = 30$ kNm. Concrete grade M25, Steel grade Fe415.
Assume for this section, at $P_{Ed} = 1000$ kN:
*   Uniaxial moment capacity about $x-x$ axis, $M_{ux1} = 90$ kNm.
*   Uniaxial moment capacity about $y-y$ axis, $M_{uy1} = 75$ kNm.
Check the adequacy of the column using the load contour method.

**Answer 2:**

*   **Load Contour Method:**
    $$ \frac{M_{ux}}{M_{ux1}} + \frac{M_{uy}}{M_{uy1}} \le 1.0 $$
    Substitute the given values:
    $$ \frac{50 \text{ kNm}}{90 \text{ kNm}} + \frac{30 \text{ kNm}}{75 \text{ kNm}} $$
    $$ 0.556 + 0.400 = 0.956 $$
*   **Adequacy Check:** Since $0.956 \le 1.0$, the column is **adequate** to resist the combined biaxial bending moments at the given axial load.

**Question 3:**
A short rectangular column 250 mm x 500 mm is reinforced with 4 bars of 20 mm diameter. It is subjected to a factored axial load of 1200 kN. The factored moments are $M_{ux} = 70$ kNm and $M_{uy} = 40$ kNm. (Concrete M20, Steel Fe415).
If the uniaxial moment capacity for this section at 1200 kN axial load is $M_{ux1} = 100$ kNm and $M_{uy1} = 80$ kNm, is the column adequate?

**Answer 3:**

*   **Load Contour Method:**
    $$ \frac{M_{ux}}{M_{ux1}} + \frac{M_{uy}}{M_{uy1}} \le 1.0 $$
    Substitute the given values:
    $$ \frac{70 \text{ kNm}}{100 \text{ kNm}} + \frac{40 \text{ kNm}}{80 \text{ kNm}} $$
    $$ 0.700 + 0.500 = 1.200 $$
*   **Adequacy Check:** Since $1.200 > 1.0$, the column is **inadequate**.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Short Columns:** Buckling is not the primary concern; focus on material strengths and geometric capacity.
*   **Interaction Diagrams:** Crucial for understanding the capacity of members under combined loading. They represent a surface of safe load combinations.
*   **Uniaxial Bending:** Simpler analysis, often visualized on a 2D interaction diagram.
*   **Biaxial Bending:** More complex, requiring 3D interaction surfaces or approximate methods like the load contour method.
*   **Load Contour Method:** An approximation that converts biaxial bending into an equivalent uniaxial bending check. Always verify its applicability based on reinforcement symmetry and code provisions.
*   **Factored Loads:** Always use factored loads for ultimate limit state checks.
*   **Equilibrium and Compatibility:** The underlying principles for calculating moment capacities are force equilibrium and strain compatibility across the cross-section.
*   **Code Provisions:** Always refer to the relevant design codes (e.g., IS 456:2000, Eurocode 2) for specific design procedures, stress block parameters, and safety factors.
*   **Symmetrical Reinforcement:** The load contour method is generally more accurate for sections with symmetrical reinforcement. For asymmetrical reinforcement, more rigorous methods or software may be necessary.
