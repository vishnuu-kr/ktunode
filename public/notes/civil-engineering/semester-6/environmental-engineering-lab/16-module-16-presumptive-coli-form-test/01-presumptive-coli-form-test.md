# Environmental Engineering Lab: Module 16 - Presumptive Coliform Test

## Introduction

This module introduces the Presumptive Coliform Test, a fundamental method used in environmental engineering to assess the bacteriological quality of water. It is a crucial step in identifying the potential presence of fecal contamination, which can pose significant health risks.

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   **Understand the significance of coliform bacteria in water quality assessment.**
*   **Explain the principles behind the Presumptive Coliform Test.**
*   **Identify the different methods used for the Presumptive Coliform Test (e.g., Multiple Tube Fermentation, Membrane Filtration).**

## 1. Significance of Coliform Bacteria in Water Quality Assessment

### Key Concepts:

*   **Indicator Organisms:** Microorganisms used to indicate the potential presence of pathogens in water. They are chosen because they are consistently present in fecal matter, easily detectable, and survive in water as long as pathogens do (or longer), but are generally less resistant to disinfection.
*   **Coliform Bacteria:** A group of Gram-negative, non-spore-forming, rod-shaped bacteria that ferment lactose with the production of acid and gas at 37°C. They are commonly found in the intestines of warm-blooded animals, including humans, as well as in soil and surface water.
*   **Fecal Coliforms:** A subgroup of coliform bacteria that are specifically found in the feces of warm-blooded animals. They are more reliable indicators of fecal pollution than total coliforms.
*   **E. coli (Escherichia coli):** A specific species within the fecal coliform group that is considered the most reliable indicator of recent fecal contamination.
*   **Pathogens:** Disease-causing microorganisms. The presence of coliforms, particularly fecal coliforms and *E. coli*, suggests a higher probability of the presence of pathogenic bacteria, viruses, and protozoa.

### Why are Coliforms Used as Indicators?

*   **Ubiquitous in Feces:** Coliform bacteria are naturally present in large numbers in the intestinal tracts of humans and animals.
*   **Easy Detection:** Methods for detecting coliforms are relatively simple and well-established.
*   **Survival:** They survive in water and wastewater for a period, indicating potential contamination that may still be a health concern.
*   **Less Resistance to Disinfection:** Compared to many enteric pathogens, coliforms are generally less resistant to disinfection processes (e.g., chlorination), meaning if coliforms are present after disinfection, pathogens might also be present.
*   **Lack of Pathogenicity (mostly):** While most coliforms are not directly pathogenic, their presence signals potential fecal contamination that *does* contain pathogens.

## 2. Principles Behind the Presumptive Coliform Test

The Presumptive Coliform Test relies on the characteristic ability of coliform bacteria to:

1.  **Ferment Lactose:** Coliforms possess enzymes that break down the sugar lactose.
2.  **Produce Acid and Gas:** This fermentation process results in the production of both acid and gas. The presence of gas is a key indicator.

The test aims to detect the presence of these metabolic activities in a water sample.

## 3. Methods Used for the Presumptive Coliform Test

Two primary methods are commonly employed for the Presumptive Coliform Test:

### 3.1. Multiple Tube Fermentation (MTF) Method

**Princ:**
This method involves inoculating a series of fermentation tubes containing a selective liquid growth medium. The medium supports the growth of coliforms while inhibiting the growth of other bacteria. The production of gas in the inverted Durham tube within the fermentation tube, after incubation at a specific temperature, is considered a positive presumptive result.

**Procedure Overview:**

1.  **Inoculation:** Water samples are inoculated into three sets of fermentation tubes containing different volumes of lauryl tryptose broth (LTB). A typical setup uses five tubes of 10 mL, five tubes of 1 mL, and five tubes of 0.1 mL per sample dilution.
2.  **Incubation:** The tubes are incubated at 35°C (for total coliforms) or 44.5°C (for fecal coliforms) for 24-48 hours.
3.  **Observation:** Tubes are examined for the presence of gas bubbles trapped in the Durham tube, indicating lactose fermentation.
4.  **Interpretation (Presumptive):**
    *   **Positive Presumptive Result:** Gas production in any tube after incubation is considered a presumptive positive result.
    *   **Negative Presumptive Result:** No gas production in any tube.

**Advantages:**
*   Quantifies the presence of coliforms (using Most Probable Number - MPN tables).
*   Can detect a wide range of bacterial concentrations.

**Disadvantages:**
*   Labor-intensive.
*   Requires significant bench space and glassware.
*   Takes longer to get results (presumptive, then confirmed).

### 3.2. Membrane Filtration (MF) Method

**Princ:**
This method involves passing a known volume of water through a sterile membrane filter with pores small enough to retain bacteria but large enough to allow the surrounding liquid to pass through. The filter is then placed on a solid growth medium that supports coliform growth and differentiates them based on their ability to ferment lactose.

**Procedure Overview:**

1.  **Filtration:** A measured volume of water is filtered through a sterile 0.45 µm membrane filter.
2.  **Incubation:** The filter is placed on a selective and differential agar medium (e.g., m-Endo agar, m-ColiBlue24 agar).
3.  **Incubation Conditions:** Incubated at 35°C for 18-24 hours.
4.  **Observation:**
    *   **m-Endo Agar:** Coliform colonies appear as pink to red colonies with a metallic sheen. The sheen is due to the reduction of sulfite and the production of a colorless aldehyde, which then reacts with basic fuchsin in the medium to form a red color. The metallic sheen is thought to be due to the production of acetaldehyde from lactose fermentation.
    *   **m-ColiBlue24 Agar:** *E. coli* colonies appear blue, while other coliforms appear pink or red.
5.  **Interpretation (Presumptive):** The presence of colonies with the characteristic appearance (e.g., pink with metallic sheen on m-Endo) indicates a presumptive positive result.

**Advantages:**
*   Faster results than MTF.
*   Less labor-intensive.
*   Requires less glassware.
*   Concentrates bacteria from large sample volumes.

**Disadvantages:**
*   May be affected by turbid water.
*   Requires sterile filters and proper aseptic technique.
*   May not be suitable for very high bacterial loads without dilution.

## 4. Media Used in the Presumptive Coliform Test

The choice of media is crucial for selectively isolating and identifying coliform bacteria.

### 4.1. Multiple Tube Fermentation (MTF) Media

*   **Lauryl Tryptose Broth (LTB):**
    *   **Purpose:** A rich, selective liquid medium that supports the growth of coliforms.
    *   **Components:**
        *   **Tryptose:** Provides essential nutrients (amino acids and nitrogen) for bacterial growth.
        *   **Lactose:** The carbohydrate substrate that coliforms ferment.
        *   **Sodium Lauryl Sulfate:** Acts as a selective agent, inhibiting the growth of non-coliform bacteria, particularly Gram-positive bacteria, due to its surface-active properties.
        *   **Inorganic salts:** Maintain osmotic balance and provide necessary ions.
    *   **Durham Tube:** An inverted small glass tube placed within the fermentation tube. It is used to collect any gas produced during lactose fermentation.

*   **Brilliant Green Lactose Bile (BGLB) Broth (for confirmed test):** Used in the confirmed phase to further differentiate coliforms. The bile salts in this medium provide stronger inhibition against non-coliforms.

### 4.2. Membrane Filtration (MF) Media

*   **m-Endo Agar:**
    *   **Purpose:** A selective and differential solid medium for the presumptive identification of coliforms.
    *   **Components:**
        *   **Yeast Extract & Tryptose:** Provide nutrients.
        *   **Lactose:** Fermentable carbohydrate.
        *   **Sodium Lauryl Sulfate:** Selective agent.
        *   **Sodium Benzoate & Sodium Acid Citrate:** Further inhibit non-coliform growth.
        *   **Basic fuchsin (sulfite-formaldehyde reagent):** A pH indicator and chromogenic agent. When coliforms ferment lactose, they produce acetaldehyde, which reacts with sodium sulfite to form a colorless compound. Upon exposure to the air and the fuchsin-sulfite reagent, this compound is oxidized to a red colored product, and the metallic sheen is produced by the adsorption of this dye onto the bacterial cells.

*   **m-ColiBlue24 Agar:**
    *   **Purpose:** A chromogenic medium that allows for the simultaneous detection of coliforms and *E. coli*.
    *   **Mechanism:** Contains specific chromogenic enzyme substrates. *E. coli* possesses the enzyme β-glucuronidase, which cleaves a specific substrate, producing a blue color. Other coliforms lack this enzyme and will appear as pink or red colonies.

## 5. Performing the Presumptive Coliform Test and Interpreting Results

### 5.1. Multiple Tube Fermentation (MTF) - Detailed Steps

**Materials:**
*   Water samples
*   Sterile fermentation tubes containing lauryl tryptose broth (LTB) with Durham tubes
*   Incubator set at 35°C (or 44.5°C for fecal coliforms)
*   Pipettes (sterile)

**Procedure:**

1.  **Prepare Tubes:** Ensure you have the correct number of tubes for each sample dilution (e.g., 5 tubes for 10 mL, 5 for 1 mL, 5 for 0.1 mL).
2.  **Inoculate:**
    *   Using a sterile pipette, carefully inoculate 10 mL of water into each of the five 10 mL tubes.
    *   Using a new sterile pipette, inoculate 1 mL of water into each of the five 1 mL tubes.
    *   Using a new sterile pipette, inoculate 0.1 mL of water into each of the five 0.1 mL tubes (often achieved by inoculating 1 mL of a 1:10 dilution into each tube).
3.  **Incubate:** Place the inoculated tubes in the incubator at the specified temperature (35°C or 44.5°C).
4.  **Observe:** After 24 hours, examine all tubes for gas production (visible bubbles in the Durham tube).
5.  **Record:** Record positive (gas present) and negative (no gas) results for each tube.
6.  **Re-incubate:** Return all tubes to the incubator for an additional 24 hours (up to 48 hours total).
7.  **Observe (48 hours):** Re-examine all tubes for gas production and record the results.
8.  **Presumptive Result:** Any tube showing gas production at either 24 or 48 hours is considered a presumptive positive.

**Interpretation (Quantification):**

The number of positive tubes in each dilution set is used to estimate the number of coliforms present using the **Most Probable Number (MPN) table**.

**Example MPN Calculation (for 5 tubes per dilution):**

| \multicolumn{3}{c}{Number of Positive Tubes} | \multicolumn{2}{c}{MPN/100 mL} |
| :------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| 10 mL Tubes | 1 mL Tubes | 0.1 mL Tubes | At 35°C | At 44.5°C |
| 5 | 4 | 1 | 92 | 16 |
| 5 | 5 | 0 | >1100 | 43 |
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 0.7 | 0.2 |

**Important:** You need to consult the appropriate MPN tables specific to the number of tubes and dilutions used in your experiment.

### 5.2. Membrane Filtration (MF) - Detailed Steps

**Materials:**
*   Water samples
*   Sterile membrane filters (0.45 µm pore size)
*   Filter funnels and vacuum apparatus
*   Sterile forceps
*   Incubator set at 35°C
*   Sterile Petri dishes containing m-Endo agar (or m-ColiBlue24)
*   Sterile rinse water (e.g., peptone water or sterile buffered water)

**Procedure:**

1.  **Set up Filtration Apparatus:** Assemble a sterile filtration apparatus under aseptic conditions.
2.  **Wet Filter:** Place a sterile membrane filter onto the filter base.
3.  **Inoculate Filter:**
    *   Rinse the filter funnel with sterile rinse water.
    *   Place the filter funnel onto the filter base.
    *   Measure a known volume of water sample and pour it into the funnel.
    *   Apply vacuum to draw the water through the filter.
4.  **Rinse Filter:** Rinse the filter funnel with sterile rinse water (e.g., 2 x 20 mL) to wash any remaining bacteria onto the filter.
5.  **Remove Filter:** Aseptically remove the filter from the funnel using sterile forceps.
6.  **Transfer to Medium:** Place the filter, grid-side up, onto the surface of the m-Endo agar (or m-ColiBlue24) in a sterile Petri dish. Ensure good contact between the filter and the agar.
7.  **Incubate:** Invert the Petri dish and incubate at 35°C for 18-24 hours.
8.  **Observe and Count:**
    *   **m-Endo Agar:** Count colonies exhibiting a pink to red color with a characteristic metallic sheen.
    *   **m-ColiBlue24 Agar:** Count blue colonies (for *E. coli*) and pink/red colonies (for other coliforms).

**Calculation of Coliform Density:**

Coliforms per 100 mL = (Number of Coliform Colonies / Volume of Sample Filtered in mL) x 100

**Example Calculation:**

If you filtered 50 mL of water and counted 30 coliform colonies (pink with metallic sheen) on m-Endo agar:

Coliforms per 100 mL = (30 colonies / 50 mL) x 100 = 60 coliforms/100 mL

**Important:** For m-ColiBlue24, you would perform separate counts for blue colonies (*E. coli*) and pink/red colonies (other coliforms).

## 6. Limitations of the Presumptive Coliform Test

The Presumptive Coliform Test is a valuable screening tool, but it has limitations:

*   **Presumptive Only:** It does not definitively confirm the presence of coliforms. Further confirmatory tests (e.g., using BGLB broth in MTF or colony characteristics in MF) are required for confirmation.
*   **False Positives:** Other bacteria that can ferment lactose but are not coliforms might produce gas in the presumptive test, leading to a false positive.
*   **False Negatives:**
    *   **Injured Bacteria:** Bacteria that have been stressed by disinfection or environmental conditions may be unable to ferment lactose in the test, even if they are still present.
    *   **Low Bacterial Numbers:** If the bacterial concentration is very low, it might be missed by the chosen sample volume.
    *   **Inhibitory Substances:** Presence of other substances in the water can inhibit coliform growth or metabolism.
*   **Temperature Sensitivity:** The incubation temperature is critical. Deviations can lead to inaccurate results.
*   **MF Method Sensitivity:** Very turbid water can clog the membrane filter, making it difficult to process larger sample volumes.
*   **Non-Fecal Coliforms:** The presence of total coliforms does not always indicate fecal contamination, as they can originate from soil and decaying vegetation. This is why the fecal coliform test (incubated at 44.5°C) or the *E. coli* test is preferred for indicating fecal pollution.

## 7. Confirmatory and Completed Tests

While this module focuses on the presumptive test, it's important to know that it's part of a larger process:

*   **Confirmed Test:** Positive presumptive tubes (or filters) are transferred to a more selective medium (e.g., Brilliant Green Lactose Bile - BGLB broth for MTF) to confirm the presence of coliforms by observing gas production. For MF, colonies are typically transferred to a secondary medium or their characteristics are further evaluated.
*   **Completed Test:** A confirmed positive culture is then used to inoculate a non-selective medium (e.g., nutrient agar slant) and a lactose-containing broth. Gram staining of the colonies from the agar slant is also performed. If the Gram stain reveals Gram-negative rods and gas is produced in the lactose broth, the test is considered completed and confirms the presence of coliforms.

## Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:** The primary characteristic is the **fermentation of lactose with the production of acid and gas**.

**Answer 2:**
*   **MTF:** Relies on inoculating liquid media in fermentation tubes and observing gas production in Durham tubes after incubation. It quantifies bacterial numbers using the MPN index.
*   **MF:** Involves filtering water through a membrane filter and then placing the filter on a solid selective agar medium to observe characteristic colony formation. It allows for direct counting of colonies.

**Answer 3:** LTB is selective because it contains **sodium lauryl sulfate**, which inhibits the growth of many non-coliform bacteria, particularly Gram-positive organisms, while allowing coliforms to grow and ferment lactose.

**Answer 4:** Presumptive coliform colonies on m-Endo agar appear as **pink to red colonies with a characteristic metallic sheen**.

**Answer 5:** With 4 positive tubes in the 10 mL dilution, 2 positive tubes in the 1 mL dilution, and 0 positive tubes in the 0.1 mL dilution, the MPN index is **16**. (This requires consulting a specific MPN table for 5 tubes per dilution).

**Answer 6:** The presence of total coliforms does not always indicate fecal contamination, as they can also be found in non-fecal environments such as **soil and decaying vegetation**. Therefore, other indicators like fecal coliforms or *E. coli* are better indicators of fecal pollution.

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Coliforms are indicators, not necessarily pathogens themselves.** Their presence signifies a potential risk.
*   **The Presumptive Test is the first step.** It needs to be followed by confirmatory and completed tests for definitive identification.
*   **Temperature is critical.** 35°C is for total coliforms, while 44.5°C is for fecal coliforms.
*   **Aseptic technique is paramount** in both MTF and MF methods to prevent contamination.
*   **Proper interpretation of MPN tables** is essential for quantitative results in MTF.
*   **The metallic sheen on m-Endo agar is a key characteristic** for presumptive coliform identification.
*   **Fecal coliforms and *E. coli* are more specific indicators of fecal contamination** than total coliforms.
