# Environmental Engineering Lab - Module 5: Determination and Estimation of Total, Organic, and Inorganic Solids

## Topic: Determination and Estimation of Total, Organic, and Inorganic Solids

### 1. Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this module, you should be able to:

*   Understand the significance of solids in water and wastewater.
*   Differentiate between total solids, dissolved solids, suspended solids, volatile solids (organic solids), and fixed solids (inorganic solids).
*   Perform the laboratory procedures for determining total solids, total suspended solids, and total dissolved solids.
*   Estimate or determine the amount of organic and inorganic solids present in a water or wastewater sample.
*   Interpret the results of solids analysis and their implications for water and wastewater treatment.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

**What are Solids in Water/Wastewater?**

Solids refer to all matter that is suspended or dissolved in water or wastewater. The presence and concentration of solids are crucial parameters in environmental engineering as they can:

*   Affect water clarity and aesthetic quality.
*   Serve as a medium for the growth of microorganisms.
*   Contribute to the BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) and COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand) of wastewater.
*   Cause fouling and abrasion in water distribution systems.
*   Impact the efficiency of treatment processes.
*   Influence the disposal of sludge.

**Types of Solids:**

*   **Total Solids (TS):** The residue remaining after evaporation of water and drying of the residue at a specific temperature (typically 103-105°C). It represents the sum of all dissolved and suspended matter.

    *   **Equation:** TS = TDS + TSS

*   **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS):** The residue remaining after filtration of a water sample, followed by evaporation of the filtrate and drying of the residue at a specific temperature (typically 103-105°C). TDS are the solids that pass through a filter with a pore size of approximately 1.5 micrometers (µm).

*   **Total Suspended Solids (TSS):** The residue retained on a filter with a pore size of approximately 1.5 µm after filtering a water sample. TSS are the solids that are physically retained by the filter.

*   **Volatile Solids (VS) / Organic Solids:** The residue remaining after igniting the dried solids at a specific temperature (typically 550 ± 50°C). This ignition process burns off the organic matter, leaving behind the inorganic residue. Therefore, VS are primarily considered organic solids.

*   **Fixed Solids (FS) / Inorganic Solids:** The residue remaining after igniting the dried solids at a specific temperature (typically 550 ± 50°C). The mass remaining after ignition represents the inorganic, or fixed, fraction of the solids.

    *   **Relationship:**
        *   TS = VS + FS
        *   TDS = Dissolved Volatile Solids (DVS) + Dissolved Fixed Solids (DFS)
        *   TSS = Suspended Volatile Solids (SVS) + Suspended Fixed Solids (SFS)

### 3. Laboratory Determination Procedures

**General Principles:**

The determination of solids involves:

1.  **Evaporation:** Removing the liquid phase (water).
2.  **Drying:** Removing residual moisture at a controlled temperature (103-105°C).
3.  **Ignition (for VS/FS):** Burning off organic matter at a higher temperature (550 ± 50°C).

**Equipment Required:**

*   Analytical balance (accurate to 0.1 mg)
*   Drying oven (thermostatically controlled at 103-105°C and 550 ± 50°C)
*   Muffle furnace (for ignition at 550 ± 50°C)
*   Crucibles or dishes (porcelain or silica, pre-ignited and weighed)
*   Filtration apparatus (Buchner funnel, filter flask, vacuum source)
*   Filter paper (glass fiber filters, typically 1.5 µm pore size)
*   Graduated cylinders, beakers, stirring rods
*   Wash bottle with distilled water

**A. Determination of Total Solids (TS)**

*   **Purpose:** To measure the combined amount of dissolved and suspended solids.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Accurately weigh a clean, dry crucible or dish. Record this as $W_1$.
    2.  Transfer a known volume (e.g., 50-100 mL) of the well-mixed water or wastewater sample into the pre-weighed crucible. Record the sample volume as $V_s$.
    3.  Place the crucible in a drying oven at 103-105°C until a constant weight is achieved (typically 1-2 hours).
    4.  Cool the crucible in a desiccator and then weigh it accurately. Record this as $W_2$.
    5.  Calculate the mass of total solids: Mass of TS = $W_2 - W_1$.
    6.  Calculate the concentration of total solids in mg/L:
        $$ \text{TS (mg/L)} = \frac{W_2 - W_1 (\text{mg})}{V_s (\text{L})} $$

**B. Determination of Total Suspended Solids (TSS)**

*   **Purpose:** To measure the mass of solids that are retained on a filter.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Accurately weigh a clean, dry glass fiber filter. Record this as $W_f$.
    2.  Assemble the filtration apparatus and wet the filter with a small amount of distilled water. Apply vacuum to seat the filter.
    3.  Transfer a known, well-mixed volume (e.g., 50-100 mL) of the water or wastewater sample into the filtration apparatus. Record the sample volume as $V_s$.
    4.  Apply vacuum and filter the entire sample.
    5.  Carefully remove the filter paper with the retained solids and place it in the pre-weighed crucible or dish.
    6.  Place the crucible/dish containing the filter and solids in a drying oven at 103-105°C until a constant weight is achieved (typically 1-2 hours).
    7.  Cool the crucible/dish in a desiccator and then weigh it accurately. Record this as $W_{f+TSS}$.
    8.  Calculate the mass of suspended solids: Mass of TSS = $W_{f+TSS} - W_f$.
    9.  Calculate the concentration of suspended solids in mg/L:
        $$ \text{TSS (mg/L)} = \frac{W_{f+TSS} - W_f (\text{mg})}{V_s (\text{L})} $$

**C. Determination of Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)**

*   **Method 1: Calculated from TS and TSS**
    *   This is the most common and often the most practical method if TS and TSS have already been determined.
    *   **Equation:** TDS = TS - TSS
    *   **Note:** This assumes that all solids not retained by the filter are dissolved.

*   **Method 2: Direct Determination (Filtration followed by Evaporation)**
    *   **Purpose:** To directly measure the mass of solids that pass through a filter.
    *   **Procedure:**
        1.  Accurately weigh a clean, dry crucible or dish. Record this as $W_1$.
        2.  Filter a known volume (e.g., 50-100 mL) of the water or wastewater sample using a glass fiber filter. Collect the filtrate (the liquid that passes through). Record the sample volume as $V_s$.
        3.  Transfer a known volume of the filtrate (e.g., the entire filtrate or a portion of it) into the pre-weighed crucible.
        4.  Place the crucible in a drying oven at 103-105°C until a constant weight is achieved.
        5.  Cool the crucible in a desiccator and then weigh it accurately. Record this as $W_3$.
        6.  Calculate the mass of dissolved solids: Mass of TDS = $W_3 - W_1$.
        7.  Calculate the concentration of dissolved solids in mg/L:
            $$ \text{TDS (mg/L)} = \frac{W_3 - W_1 (\text{mg})}{V_s (\text{L})} $$

**D. Determination of Volatile Solids (VS) and Fixed Solids (FS)**

*   **Purpose:** To estimate the organic and inorganic content of the solids.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  **From Dried Residue:** This procedure continues from the determination of TS or TSS. Take the crucible containing the dried residue (from TS or TSS determination) that has achieved constant weight at 103-105°C.
    2.  Place the crucible in a muffle furnace at 550 ± 50°C for at least 1 hour, or until a constant weight is achieved. This process will combust the organic matter.
    3.  Cool the crucible in a desiccator and then weigh it accurately. Record this as $W_4$.
    4.  **Calculation:**
        *   Mass of Volatile Solids (Organic Solids) = Mass of dried solids - Mass of ignited solids
            $$ \text{Mass of VS} = (W_2 - W_1) - (W_4 - W_1) = W_2 - W_4 \quad (\text{from TS}) $$
            $$ \text{Mass of VS} = (W_{f+TSS} - W_f) - (W_4 - W_f) = W_{f+TSS} - W_4 \quad (\text{from TSS}) $$
        *   Mass of Fixed Solids (Inorganic Solids) = Mass of ignited solids
            $$ \text{Mass of FS} = W_4 - W_1 \quad (\text{from TS}) $$
            $$ \text{Mass of FS} = W_4 - W_f \quad (\text{from TSS}) $$
    5.  **Calculate Concentrations:** Divide the masses of VS and FS by the initial sample volume ($V_s$) to get concentrations in mg/L.
        $$ \text{VS (mg/L)} = \frac{\text{Mass of VS (mg)}}{V_s (\text{L})} $$
        $$ \text{FS (mg/L)} = \frac{\text{Mass of FS (mg)}}{V_s (\text{L})} $$

### 4. Interpretation of Results

*   **High TS:** Indicates a significant amount of matter in the water/wastewater. In drinking water, this can affect taste and appearance. In wastewater, it contributes to pollution load.
*   **High TSS:** Suggests a large amount of particulate matter, which can cause turbidity, clog pipes, and harbor pathogens. TSS is a key parameter for assessing the effectiveness of sedimentation and filtration processes.
*   **High TDS:** Can impact water quality for drinking, agriculture, and industry. In wastewater, high TDS can be indicative of dissolved salts or pollutants.
*   **High VS (Organic Solids):** Indicates a high organic content, which contributes to BOD and can deplete dissolved oxygen in receiving waters if not treated. It also relates to the sludge production in biological treatment processes.
*   **High FS (Inorganic Solids):** Typically consists of minerals, sand, silt, and other non-combustible materials. High FS in wastewater might come from industrial discharges or grit.

**Example Application:**

Consider a wastewater sample with the following results:
*   TS = 500 mg/L
*   TSS = 300 mg/L
*   VS (from TSS) = 200 mg/L

**Calculations:**

*   TDS = TS - TSS = 500 mg/L - 300 mg/L = 200 mg/L
*   SVS = VS (from TSS) = 200 mg/L (Suspended Volatile Solids)
*   SFS = TSS - SVS = 300 mg/L - 200 mg/L = 100 mg/L (Suspended Fixed Solids)

**Interpretation:**

The wastewater sample contains a significant amount of both suspended and dissolved solids. The suspended solids constitute a larger portion of the total solids. Out of the suspended solids, 200 mg/L is organic (likely contributing to BOD) and 100 mg/L is inorganic. The dissolved solids are 200 mg/L.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**

A laboratory analyst collected a 100 mL sample of river water. After filtering the sample and drying the filter paper with retained solids at 105°C to a constant weight, the filter paper weighed 0.5500 g initially and 0.5650 g after drying. The crucible used for evaporation and drying of the filtrate weighed 25.0000 g, and after evaporating the filtrate and drying to a constant weight at 105°C, it weighed 25.0210 g.

a)  Calculate the concentration of Total Suspended Solids (TSS) in mg/L.
b)  Calculate the concentration of Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) in mg/L.
c)  Calculate the concentration of Total Solids (TS) in mg/L.
d)  If the dried residue from the TSS determination was ignited at 550°C and the remaining weight of the filter paper and ignited solids was 0.5580 g, calculate the concentration of Volatile Suspended Solids (VSS) and Fixed Suspended Solids (FSS) in mg/L.

**Answer 1:**

*   Sample volume ($V_s$) = 100 mL = 0.1 L
*   Initial filter paper weight ($W_f$) = 0.5500 g = 550.0 mg
*   Filter paper + TSS weight ($W_{f+TSS}$) = 0.5650 g = 565.0 mg
*   Initial crucible weight ($W_1$) = 25.0000 g
*   Crucible + TDS weight ($W_3$) = 25.0210 g
*   Crucible + ignited TSS weight ($W_4$) = 0.5580 g = 558.0 mg (Note: This weight should be added to the initial filter paper weight for calculation of ignited solids from the filter.)

a)  **TSS:**
    Mass of TSS = $W_{f+TSS} - W_f = 565.0 \text{ mg} - 550.0 \text{ mg} = 15.0 \text{ mg}$
    TSS (mg/L) = $\frac{15.0 \text{ mg}}{0.1 \text{ L}} = 150 \text{ mg/L}$

b)  **TDS:**
    Mass of TDS = $W_3 - W_1 = 25.0210 \text{ g} - 25.0000 \text{ g} = 0.0210 \text{ g} = 21.0 \text{ mg}$
    TDS (mg/L) = $\frac{21.0 \text{ mg}}{0.1 \text{ L}} = 210 \text{ mg/L}$

c)  **TS:**
    TS (mg/L) = TSS (mg/L) + TDS (mg/L) = 150 mg/L + 210 mg/L = 360 mg/L

d)  **VSS and FSS:**
    First, we need the total mass of dried TSS from part (a): 15.0 mg.
    The weight of the filter paper and ignited solids ($W_4$) is 0.5580 g, which means the weight of the ignited solids *on the filter* is $0.5580 \text{ g} - 0.5500 \text{ g} = 0.0080 \text{ g} = 8.0 \text{ mg}$.
    This 8.0 mg represents the Fixed Suspended Solids (FSS).

    Mass of Fixed Suspended Solids (FSS) = $W_4 \text{ (corrected)} - W_f = (0.5580 \text{ g}) - (0.5500 \text{ g}) = 0.0080 \text{ g} = 8.0 \text{ mg}$
    FSS (mg/L) = $\frac{8.0 \text{ mg}}{0.1 \text{ L}} = 80 \text{ mg/L}$

    Mass of Volatile Suspended Solids (VSS) = Mass of TSS - Mass of FSS = 15.0 mg - 8.0 mg = 7.0 mg
    VSS (mg/L) = $\frac{7.0 \text{ mg}}{0.1 \text{ L}} = 70 \text{ mg/L}$

    **Check:** VSS + FSS = 70 mg/L + 80 mg/L = 150 mg/L (which is the TSS, as expected).

**Question 2:**

Explain the difference between Volatile Solids and Fixed Solids. What kind of substances contribute to each?

**Answer 2:**

*   **Volatile Solids (VS):** These are the components of solids that are driven off as gas when the dried residue is heated to a high temperature (typically 550°C). VS are primarily **organic matter**. Examples include:
    *   Biomass (bacteria, algae, protozoa)
    *   Decomposed organic debris (food waste, plant material)
    *   Fats, oils, and greases
    *   Some complex organic compounds.
    The proportion of volatile solids in wastewater is often an indicator of the organic pollution load.

*   **Fixed Solids (FS):** These are the components of solids that remain as residue after ignition at a high temperature. FS are primarily **inorganic matter**. Examples include:
    *   Minerals
    *   Silts and sands
    *   Salts (e.g., NaCl, sulfates)
    *   Metal oxides and hydroxides
    *   Dissolved inorganic salts that precipitate out during drying or ignition.
    High concentrations of fixed solids can indicate the presence of inert materials or dissolved inorganic pollutants.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Sample Homogeneity:** Always ensure the sample is well-mixed before taking a subsample for analysis, as solids can settle.
*   **Constant Weight:** The key to accurate solids determination is ensuring the residue has reached a "constant weight" at both drying and ignition temperatures. This means repeated weighing shows no significant change (typically ± 0.5 mg).
*   **Desiccator:** Always cool crucibles containing dried or ignited solids in a desiccator to prevent them from absorbing atmospheric moisture before weighing.
*   **Filter Paper:** Use appropriate glass fiber filters (typically 1.5 µm pore size) for TSS analysis to ensure consistent retention of suspended particles.
*   **Temperature Control:** Maintain precise control of oven and muffle furnace temperatures. Deviations can lead to incomplete drying or loss of inorganic matter.
*   **Units:** Be consistent with units (mg, g, mL, L) throughout your calculations.
*   **Inter-relationships:** Understand the relationships between TS, TDS, TSS, VS, and FS. They are all interconnected and can be calculated from each other under certain conditions.
*   **Wastewater vs. Drinking Water:** Solids analysis is critical for both, but the interpretation and acceptable limits differ significantly. Wastewater analysis focuses on pollution load and treatment efficiency, while drinking water analysis focuses on aesthetic quality and potential health impacts.
