# Environmental Engineering Lab: Module 3 - Determination of Alkalinity & Acidity

## Topic: Determination of Alkalinity & Acidity

### **1. Introduction to Alkalinity and Acidity**

*   **What are Alkalinity and Acidity?**
    *   **Alkalinity:** The capacity of a water sample to neutralize acids. It is primarily due to the presence of conjugate bases of weak acids, such as carbonate (CO₃²⁻), bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻), and hydroxide (OH⁻) ions. It can also be contributed by other weak bases like ammonia (NH₃).
    *   **Acidity:** The capacity of a water sample to neutralize bases. It is primarily due to the presence of acids, such as carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄), and hydrochloric acid (HCl).

*   **Why are Alkalinity and Acidity Important in Environmental Engineering?**
    *   **Water Treatment:**
        *   **Alkalinity:** Essential for effective coagulation and flocculation (e.g., adding alum requires sufficient alkalinity for the reaction). It also plays a role in pH buffering, maintaining stable pH during chemical addition.
        *   **Acidity:** High acidity can lead to corrosion of pipes and infrastructure.
    *   **Wastewater Treatment:**
        *   **Alkalinity:** Crucial for biological processes, particularly nitrification, which produces acidity and requires alkalinity for buffering. Insufficient alkalinity can lead to process failure.
        *   **Acidity:** Can be generated by industrial discharges and some biological processes.
    *   **Natural Waters:**
        *   **Alkalinity:** Buffers against rapid pH changes, protecting aquatic life from acid rain and other acidic inputs.
        *   **Acidity:** Can indicate pollution from industrial sources or acidic precipitation.
    *   **Aquatic Life:** Most aquatic organisms thrive within a specific pH range. Alkalinity and acidity directly influence the pH of water bodies.

### **2. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Titration:** A quantitative chemical analysis technique used to determine the concentration of a substance (analyte) by reacting it with a solution of known concentration (titrant) until the reaction is complete.
*   **Titrant:** The solution of known concentration added during titration.
*   **Analyte:** The substance being analyzed for its concentration.
*   **Indicator:** A substance that changes color at or near the equivalence point of a titration, signaling the completion of the reaction.
*   **Equivalence Point:** The point in a titration where the amount of titrant added is stoichiometrically equivalent to the amount of analyte present.
*   **End Point:** The point in a titration where a visible change (usually color change of an indicator) occurs, approximating the equivalence point.
*   **Buffer Solution:** A solution that resists changes in pH when a small amount of acid or base is added. Alkalinity contributes to the buffering capacity of water.
*   **Milliequivalent (meq):** A unit of chemical equivalence. In water chemistry, it represents the amount of a substance that will react with one millimole of a univalent ion or half a millimole of a bivalent ion.
*   **mg/L as CaCO₃:** Alkalinity and acidity are typically expressed in milligrams per liter (mg/L) of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). This is a common convention for standardization.

### **3. Determination of Alkalinity**

#### **3.1 Principle of Alkalinity Determination**

Alkalinity is determined by titrating a known volume of water sample with a standardized strong acid (usually sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, or hydrochloric acid, HCl). The acid neutralizes the alkaline components in the water. The titration is carried out to specific pH endpoints, which correspond to the neutralization of different alkaline species.

#### **3.2 Types of Alkalinity**

The type of alkalinity is determined by the pH at which the titration is performed, indicating the neutralization of specific species:

*   **Carbonate Alkalinity (or P-Alkalinity):** This is the alkalinity measured when the titrant is added to a pH where all hydroxide ions (OH⁻) and half of the bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻) are neutralized. The pH endpoint is typically around **8.3**.
    *   Reactions:
        *   OH⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O
        *   HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → H₂CO₃ (This reaction is not fully completed at pH 8.3, only about half is neutralized)

*   **Bicarbonate Alkalinity (or M-Alkalinity):** This is the total alkalinity measured when the titrant is added to a pH where all bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻) are neutralized. The pH endpoint is typically around **4.5**. This is the most common measure of total alkalinity.
    *   Reactions:
        *   OH⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O
        *   HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → H₂CO₃

*   **Total Alkalinity:** The sum of carbonate and bicarbonate alkalinity. It is the alkalinity measured at the point where all acidic species have been neutralized, usually at a pH of 4.5.
    *   The titration to pH 4.5 accounts for the neutralization of all OH⁻, HCO₃⁻, and CO₃²⁻.

#### **3.3 Titration Procedure and Indicators**

The determination of total alkalinity (to pH 4.5) is the most common laboratory procedure.

**Materials:**

*   Water sample
*   Standardized sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) or hydrochloric acid (HCl) titrant (e.g., 0.02 N)
*   Phenolphthalein indicator
*   Methyl orange indicator or Bromocresol green-Methyl red indicator (mixed indicator)
*   Buret
*   Pipette
*   Erlenmeyer flask
*   pH meter (optional, for precise endpoint determination)

**Procedure:**

1.  **Pipette a known volume (e.g., 50 mL or 100 mL) of the water sample into an Erlenmeyer flask.** Record the exact volume.
2.  **Add 1-2 drops of phenolphthalein indicator.**
3.  **Titrate with the standard acid (titrant) until the pink color just disappears.** This point indicates the presence of carbonate alkalinity (neutralization of OH⁻ and half of HCO₃⁻). Record the volume of titrant used (V₁). *Note: If the solution remains colorless, there is no significant OH⁻ alkalinity.*
4.  **Add 1-2 drops of methyl orange indicator (or the mixed indicator).** The solution will turn yellowish-orange to red.
5.  **Continue titrating with the standard acid until the color changes from yellowish-orange to a distinct pale pink or grayish-blue (depending on the indicator).** This point indicates the neutralization of the remaining bicarbonate alkalinity (reaching the M-alkalinity endpoint at pH 4.5). Record the total volume of titrant used (V₂).

**Important Note:** If the phenolphthalein endpoint (V₁) is reached and the solution remains colorless, it means there is no significant OH⁻ alkalinity. In this case, the total alkalinity (to pH 4.5) is determined solely by the volume of titrant added from the start of the titration to the methyl orange endpoint.

#### **3.4 Calculations for Alkalinity**

The alkalinity is calculated using the following formula:

**Alkalinity (as mg/L CaCO₃) = (V × N × 50,000) / Vm**

Where:
*   **V** = Volume of standard acid titrant used (mL).
*   **N** = Normality of the standard acid titrant (eq/L or N).
*   **Vm** = Volume of water sample titrated (mL).
*   **50,000** = Equivalent weight of CaCO₃ (50,000 mg/eq) multiplied by 1000 (to convert mL to L).

**Interpreting Titration Volumes:**

*   **V₁:** Volume of acid to reach phenolphthalein endpoint (pH ~8.3). This primarily neutralizes OH⁻ and some HCO₃⁻.
*   **V₂:** Total volume of acid to reach methyl orange endpoint (pH ~4.5). This neutralizes all OH⁻, HCO₃⁻, and CO₃²⁻.

**Types of Alkalinity Calculations:**

*   **Carbonate Alkalinity (or P-Alkalinity):**
    *   If V₁ > 0 and V₂ > V₁: Carbonate Alkalinity (as mg/L CaCO₃) = (V₁ × N × 50,000) / Vm
*   **Bicarbonate Alkalinity:**
    *   If V₂ > V₁: Bicarbonate Alkalinity (as mg/L CaCO₃) = ((V₂ - V₁) × N × 50,000) / Vm
*   **Total Alkalinity (most commonly measured):**
    *   Total Alkalinity (as mg/L CaCO₃) = (V₂ × N × 50,000) / Vm

**Relationship between P and M Alkalinity:**

*   **If Phenolphthalein endpoint is reached (V₁ > 0):**
    *   If V₂ > V₁: Sample contains OH⁻ and HCO₃⁻ (and possibly CO₃²⁻ if V₁ is interpreted strictly as neutralizing OH⁻ and half HCO₃⁻). The alkalinity species are OH⁻ and HCO₃⁻.
    *   If V₂ ≈ V₁: Sample contains OH⁻ and CO₃²⁻. The alkalinity species are OH⁻ and CO₃²⁻.
    *   If V₁ > V₂: This scenario is not physically possible with standard titration.

*   **If Phenolphthalein endpoint is not reached (V₁ = 0 or near zero):**
    *   If V₂ > 0: Sample contains primarily HCO₃⁻. The alkalinity species is HCO₃⁻.

**Example Calculation:**

A 100 mL water sample is titrated with 0.02 N H₂SO₄.
*   Phenolphthalein endpoint (V₁) = 5 mL
*   Methyl orange endpoint (V₂) = 25 mL

**Calculations:**

*   **Total Alkalinity (as mg/L CaCO₃):**
    *   (25 mL × 0.02 N × 50,000) / 100 mL = **250 mg/L CaCO₃**

*   **Carbonate Alkalinity (as mg/L CaCO₃):**
    *   (5 mL × 0.02 N × 50,000) / 100 mL = **50 mg/L CaCO₃**

*   **Bicarbonate Alkalinity (as mg/L CaCO₃):**
    *   ((25 mL - 5 mL) × 0.02 N × 50,000) / 100 mL = **200 mg/L CaCO₃**

**Interpretation:**
The sample contains both carbonate and bicarbonate alkalinity. The total alkalinity is 250 mg/L CaCO₃.

### **4. Determination of Acidity**

#### **4.1 Principle of Acidity Determination**

Acidity is determined by titrating a known volume of water sample with a standardized strong base (usually sodium hydroxide, NaOH) to a specific pH endpoint, typically around **8.3**. The base neutralizes the acidic components in the water.

#### **4.2 Types of Acidity**

Similar to alkalinity, acidity can be categorized based on the species neutralized:

*   **Strong Acid Acidity (or P-Acidity):** This corresponds to the neutralization of strong acids like HCl and H₂SO₄. The endpoint is usually around **pH 3.7-4.0**.
*   **Weak Acid Acidity (or M-Acidity):** This corresponds to the neutralization of weak acids, such as carbonic acid (H₂CO₃). The endpoint is usually around **pH 8.3**.

In practice, the most common measure of acidity is the total acidity, which is determined by titration to pH 8.3.

#### **4.3 Titration Procedure**

**Materials:**

*   Water sample
*   Standardized sodium hydroxide (NaOH) titrant (e.g., 0.02 N)
*   Phenolphthalein indicator
*   Buret
*   Pipette
*   Erlenmeyer flask
*   pH meter (optional)

**Procedure:**

1.  **Pipette a known volume (e.g., 50 mL or 100 mL) of the water sample into an Erlenmeyer flask.** Record the exact volume.
2.  **Add 1-2 drops of phenolphthalein indicator.**
3.  **Titrate with the standard base (titrant) until the first permanent faint pink color appears and persists for at least 30 seconds.** This endpoint typically corresponds to pH 8.3 and neutralizes all acidic species. Record the volume of titrant used (V).

#### **4.4 Calculations for Acidity**

The acidity is calculated using the following formula:

**Acidity (as mg/L CaCO₃) = (V × N × 50,000) / Vm**

Where:
*   **V** = Volume of standard base titrant used (mL).
*   **N** = Normality of the standard base titrant (eq/L or N).
*   **Vm** = Volume of water sample titrated (mL).
*   **50,000** = Equivalent weight of CaCO₃ (50,000 mg/eq) multiplied by 1000 (to convert mL to L).

**Example Calculation:**

A 50 mL water sample is titrated with 0.02 N NaOH.
*   Phenolphthalein endpoint (V) = 8 mL

**Calculation:**

*   **Total Acidity (as mg/L CaCO₃):**
    *   (8 mL × 0.02 N × 50,000) / 50 mL = **160 mg/L CaCO₃**

**Interpretation:**
The sample has a total acidity of 160 mg/L CaCO₃.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
A 100 mL sample of river water was titrated with 0.02 N H₂SO₄. The phenolphthalein endpoint was reached with 10 mL of acid, and the methyl orange endpoint was reached with a total of 35 mL of acid. Calculate the:
a) Carbonate alkalinity
b) Bicarbonate alkalinity
c) Total alkalinity
Express all results in mg/L as CaCO₃.

**Answer 1:**
*   **a) Carbonate Alkalinity:**
    *   V₁ = 10 mL
    *   Carbonate Alkalinity = (10 mL × 0.02 N × 50,000) / 100 mL = **100 mg/L CaCO₃**
*   **b) Bicarbonate Alkalinity:**
    *   V₂ = 35 mL
    *   Bicarbonate Alkalinity = ((35 mL - 10 mL) × 0.02 N × 50,000) / 100 mL = **250 mg/L CaCO₃**
*   **c) Total Alkalinity:**
    *   Total Alkalinity = (35 mL × 0.02 N × 50,000) / 100 mL = **350 mg/L CaCO₃**

**Question 2:**
A 50 mL sample of industrial wastewater was titrated with 0.01 N NaOH to a phenolphthalein endpoint. The volume of NaOH used was 15 mL. Calculate the total acidity of the wastewater in mg/L as CaCO₃.

**Answer 2:**
*   V = 15 mL
*   N = 0.01 N
*   Vm = 50 mL
*   Total Acidity = (15 mL × 0.01 N × 50,000) / 50 mL = **150 mg/L CaCO₃**

**Question 3:**
In the titration of alkalinity, what is the purpose of using phenolphthalein and methyl orange indicators?

**Answer 3:**
*   **Phenolphthalein indicator:** Used to identify the endpoint where hydroxide (OH⁻) and carbonate (CO₃²⁻) ions are neutralized, reaching approximately pH 8.3. This helps in determining carbonate alkalinity.
*   **Methyl orange indicator:** Used to identify the endpoint where all bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) ions are neutralized, reaching approximately pH 4.5. This endpoint signifies the total alkalinity.

**Question 4:**
Explain why alkalinity is important in biological wastewater treatment.

**Answer 4:**
Alkalinity is crucial for biological wastewater treatment, especially nitrification. Nitrification is a two-step biological process where ammonia is converted to nitrate. Both steps consume alkalinity (release H⁺ ions). Without sufficient alkalinity, the pH of the wastewater can drop significantly, inhibiting the activity of nitrifying bacteria and potentially leading to process failure. Alkalinity acts as a buffer to maintain a stable pH for optimal biological activity.

### **6. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Units:** Always express alkalinity and acidity in **mg/L as CaCO₃**.
*   **Titrant Strength:** Ensure the normality (N) of your titrant (acid or base) is accurately known.
*   **Sample Volume:** Use a precise and recorded volume of the water sample for titration.
*   **Endpoint Determination:** Accurately observe the color change of the indicator for the correct endpoint. Using a pH meter can improve accuracy.
*   **Interpreting Alkalinity Titrations:** The relationship between the volume of titrant used at the phenolphthalein endpoint (V₁) and the methyl orange endpoint (V₂) provides information about the types of alkalinity present (OH⁻, CO₃²⁻, HCO₃⁻).
*   **Acidity Titration Endpoint:** The endpoint for acidity titration is typically around pH 8.3, indicated by the first permanent faint pink color with phenolphthalein.
*   **Corrosion:** High acidity can lead to corrosion of metal pipes and structures.
*   **pH Buffering:** Alkalinity is a measure of the water's ability to resist pH changes.
