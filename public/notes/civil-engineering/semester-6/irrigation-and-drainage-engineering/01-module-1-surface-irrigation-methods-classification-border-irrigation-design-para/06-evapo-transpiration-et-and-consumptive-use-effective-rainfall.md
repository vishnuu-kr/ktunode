### 1. Evapotranspiration (ET) and Consumptive Use (CU)

#### 1.1 Introduction to ET and CU

*   **Definition:** Evapotranspiration (ET) is the combined process of water loss from the Earth's surface to the atmosphere through **evaporation** from soil surfaces, water bodies, and plant foliage, and **transpiration** from plants.
*   **Significance:** ET represents the largest component of water loss in most agricultural systems and is a critical factor in determining crop water requirements and irrigation scheduling.
*   **Consumptive Use (CU):** Consumptive Use (CU) is often used interchangeably with Evapotranspiration (ET) in irrigation engineering. It specifically refers to the **total volume of water used by a crop** for its growth and development, which is primarily transpired by the plant and evaporated from the soil surface within the cropped area.

#### 1.2 Components of Evapotranspiration

*   **Evaporation (E):** The process by which liquid water is converted into water vapor and rises into the atmosphere.
    *   Sources of evaporation:
        *   **Soil surface:** Water directly exposed to the atmosphere.
        *   **Water bodies:** Lakes, ponds, reservoirs within the irrigation command area.
        *   **Interception:** Water intercepted by plant canopies and evaporated before reaching the soil.
*   **Transpiration (T):** The process by which plants absorb water through their roots and then give off water vapor through pores (stomata) in their leaves.
    *   **Biological necessity:** Transpiration is essential for plant growth as it helps in:
        *   **Nutrient transport:** Dissolving and transporting mineral nutrients from the soil to the plant.
        *   **Cooling:** Evaporative cooling of the plant surface.
        *   **Turgor pressure:** Maintaining cell rigidity.

#### 1.3 Factors Affecting ET and CU

*   **Climatic Factors:**
    *   **Solar Radiation:** Primary energy source for evaporation and transpiration. Higher radiation leads to higher ET.
    *   **Temperature:** Higher temperatures increase the vapor pressure deficit and thus ET.
    *   **Humidity:** Lower humidity (higher vapor pressure deficit) leads to higher ET.
    *   **Wind Speed:** Increases the rate of vapor removal from the evaporating/transpiring surfaces, thus increasing ET.
    *   **Precipitation:** While supplying water, it also affects humidity and surface wetness.
*   **Crop Factors:**
    *   **Crop Type:** Different crops have different physiological characteristics and growth habits, leading to variations in ET.
    *   **Crop Growth Stage:** ET rates change significantly throughout the crop's life cycle, from germination to maturity. Peak ET occurs during the flowering and grain-filling stages.
    *   **Crop Cover:** A fully developed canopy shades the soil and reduces evaporation, while increasing transpiration.
    *   **Crop Height and Leaf Area Index (LAI):** Taller crops and those with higher LAI generally transpire more water.
*   **Soil Factors:**
    *   **Soil Moisture:** Available soil moisture significantly impacts ET. As soil dries, ET rates decrease.
    *   **Soil Type:** Affects water holding capacity and infiltration rates, indirectly influencing available moisture for ET.

#### 1.4 Measurement and Estimation of ET and CU

*   **Direct Measurement (Lysimeters):** Large containers filled with soil and a crop, where water balance is meticulously maintained. While accurate, they are expensive and limited in scope.
*   **Empirical Formulas:** Based on observational data and correlations with various meteorological parameters.
    *   **Blaney-Criddle Method:** Relates ET to temperature and daylight hours. (Older method, less accurate).
    *   **Penman-Monteith Equation:** Considered the most accurate and widely accepted method. It combines energy balance and aerodynamic principles to estimate ET.
        *   **$ET_0 = \frac{0.409 \Delta (R_n - G) + \gamma \frac{900}{T + 273} u_2 (e_s - e_a)}{\Delta + \gamma (1 + 0.34 u_2)}$**
            *   $ET_0$: Reference evapotranspiration (mm/day)
            *   $\Delta$: Slope of the saturation vapor pressure curve (kPa/°C)
            *   $R_n$: Net radiation at the crop surface (MJ/m²/day)
            *   $G$: Soil heat flux density (MJ/m²/day)
            *   $\gamma$: Psychrometric constant (kPa/°C)
            *   $T$: Mean daily air temperature at 2 m height (°C)
            *   $u_2$: Wind speed at 2 m height (m/s)
            *   $e_s$: Saturation vapor pressure (kPa)
            *   $e_a$: Actual vapor pressure (kPa)
*   **Crop Coefficients ($K_c$):**
    *   **Definition:** $K_c$ is a dimensionless coefficient that relates the ET of a specific crop ($ET_c$) to the reference ET ($ET_0$) under specific growing conditions.
    *   **Formula:** $ET_c = K_c \times ET_0$
    *   **Usage:** $K_c$ values vary based on the crop type, growth stage, and management practices. They are usually determined through field experiments or obtained from agricultural research organizations.
    *   **Example:** For wheat, $K_c$ might be 0.3 during early growth, 1.1 during peak growth, and 0.5 during maturity.

#### 1.5 Crop Water Requirement (CWR)

*   **Definition:** CWR is the total amount of water needed by a crop for its normal growth and development over a specific period, encompassing ET and any water incorporated into plant tissues.
*   **Formula:** CWR is essentially the consumptive use of the crop.
*   **Importance:** CWR is a fundamental parameter for designing irrigation systems and scheduling irrigation.

### 2. Effective Rainfall (Pe)

#### 2.1 Definition of Rainfall

*   **Rainfall:** Precipitation in the form of water droplets falling from the atmosphere.

#### 2.2 Definition of Effective Rainfall (Pe)

*   **Definition:** Effective Rainfall (Pe) is the portion of total rainfall that is **beneficially used by the crop** and is available in the root zone to meet its water requirements. It is the rainfall that contributes to satisfying the crop's water demand.
*   **Significance:** It reduces the amount of irrigation water needed, leading to cost savings and more efficient water use.

#### 2.3 Factors Affecting Effective Rainfall

*   **Total Rainfall:** The amount of rain that falls.
*   **Rainfall Intensity:** High intensity rainfall can lead to increased runoff, reducing the amount of water infiltrated.
*   **Soil Characteristics:**
    *   **Infiltration Rate:** The rate at which water enters the soil. Higher infiltration rates mean more water can be stored in the root zone.
    *   **Soil Moisture Content:** If the soil is already saturated, rainfall will result in runoff.
    *   **Soil Texture and Structure:** Affect water holding capacity and drainage.
*   **Topography:**
    *   **Slope:** Steeper slopes increase runoff.
    *   **Land Use:** Vegetation cover reduces runoff.
*   **Crop Characteristics:**
    *   **Stage of Growth:** Bare soil will have higher evaporation losses than a fully covered crop.
    *   **Canopy Interception:** Rainfall intercepted by the canopy can evaporate, reducing the amount reaching the soil.
*   **Evapotranspiration Rate:** High ET rates will quickly deplete soil moisture, making it available for rainfall.

#### 2.4 Estimation of Effective Rainfall (Pe)

Several methods are used to estimate Effective Rainfall, often based on empirical relationships.

*   **Empirical Formulas:**
    *   **USDA SCS Method (Soil Conservation Service):**
        *   **$P_e = P \times (1 - e^{-I/P})$** where $I$ is the "water loss" (initial abstraction). This formula is more complex and requires defining initial abstraction.
        *   A simpler approximation often used is: **$P_e = 0.7 \times P - 10$** (This is a very rough approximation and should be used with caution, as it's often cited for specific conditions).
    *   **Gregg and Yevjevich Method:**
        *   **$P_e = P - L$** where $L$ is the losses (runoff and deep percolation). $L$ is a function of rainfall, soil properties, and antecedent moisture.
    *   **Continuity Equation Approach (Water Balance):**
        *   This is the most fundamental approach.
        *   **$P_e = P - R - E_{soil}$**
            *   $P$: Total Rainfall
            *   $R$: Surface Runoff
            *   $E_{soil}$: Evaporation from the soil surface during the rainfall event.
        *   This requires detailed data on runoff generation and soil moisture.

*   **General Guidelines:**
    *   Effective rainfall is typically less than the total rainfall.
    *   In areas with low rainfall, a higher percentage of rainfall might be effective if it falls at a low intensity and the soil is dry.
    *   In high rainfall intensity events, a significant portion will be lost as runoff.

#### 2.5 Importance of Effective Rainfall

*   **Irrigation Planning:** Effective rainfall is deducted from the crop water requirement to determine the net irrigation requirement.
    *   **Net Irrigation Requirement = Crop Water Requirement - Effective Rainfall**
*   **Water Resource Management:** Understanding effective rainfall helps in managing available water resources efficiently and in predicting the frequency and amount of irrigation needed.
*   **Economic Viability:** Reduces the cost of irrigation by minimizing the need for supplemental watering.

### 3. Relationship between ET, CU, and Effective Rainfall

*   **Irrigation Water Requirement (IWR):** The total amount of water that needs to be applied to the field to meet the crop's water demand, considering losses in the irrigation system.
*   **Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR):** The total amount of water that needs to be delivered to the field, accounting for system inefficiencies.
*   **Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR):** The actual amount of water that needs to be supplied to the root zone of the crop.

The fundamental water balance in an irrigated field can be represented as:

**Crop Water Requirement (ETc) = Effective Rainfall (Pe) + Irrigation Supplied (Net)**

Therefore, the **Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR)** is calculated as:

**NIR = ETc - Pe**

The **Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR)** is then calculated by accounting for irrigation system efficiency ($E_a$):

**GIR = NIR / $E_a$**

**$E_a$** is the application efficiency, which represents the ratio of water beneficially used by the crop to the total water applied.

**Example:**

A crop has a Crop Water Requirement (ETc) of 150 mm over a season. During the season, the effective rainfall (Pe) received is 50 mm. The irrigation system's application efficiency ($E_a$) is 75% (0.75).

1.  **Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR):**
    NIR = ETc - Pe
    NIR = 150 mm - 50 mm
    NIR = 100 mm

2.  **Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR):**
    GIR = NIR / $E_a$
    GIR = 100 mm / 0.75
    GIR = 133.33 mm

This means that 100 mm of water needs to be delivered to the root zone, and to achieve this, approximately 133.33 mm of water must be applied at the field inlet.

### Key Points to Remember:

*   **ET is the primary driver of crop water demand.**
*   **Consumptive Use (CU) is often synonymous with ET in irrigation contexts.**
*   **Effective Rainfall (Pe) is the portion of rainfall that is *useful* to the crop.**
*   **$ET_0$ (Reference ET) is a standardized ET value for a reference crop (e.g., grass) used as a baseline.**
*   **$ET_c = K_c \times ET_0$ relates crop ET to reference ET using crop coefficients.**
*   **NIR = ETc - Pe is the fundamental equation for determining the irrigation water needed.**
*   **GIR = NIR / $E_a$ accounts for system inefficiencies.**
*   **Accurate estimation of ET and Pe is crucial for efficient irrigation design and water management.**

### Practice Questions and Exercises:

**Question 1:**
Define Evapotranspiration (ET) and list the main factors influencing it.

**Answer 1:**
Evapotranspiration (ET) is the combined loss of water to the atmosphere through evaporation from soil and water surfaces and transpiration from plants.
Main influencing factors include:
*   **Climatic:** Solar radiation, temperature, humidity, wind speed.
*   **Crop:** Type, growth stage, canopy cover, LAI.
*   **Soil:** Moisture content, type.

**Question 2:**
What is Effective Rainfall (Pe) and why is it important in irrigation?

**Answer 2:**
Effective Rainfall (Pe) is the portion of total rainfall that is beneficially used by the crop to meet its water requirements. It is important because it directly reduces the amount of irrigation water needed, saving resources and costs.

**Question 3:**
A farmer is growing maize. The total crop water requirement (ETc) for the season is estimated to be 200 mm. During the monsoon season, the effective rainfall received is 80 mm. If the irrigation system used has an application efficiency of 80%, calculate:
a) The Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR)
b) The Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR)

**Answer 3:**
Given:
*   ETc = 200 mm
*   Pe = 80 mm
*   Application Efficiency ($E_a$) = 80% = 0.80

a) Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR):
NIR = ETc - Pe
NIR = 200 mm - 80 mm
**NIR = 120 mm**

b) Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR):
GIR = NIR / $E_a$
GIR = 120 mm / 0.80
**GIR = 150 mm**

**Question 4:**
Briefly explain the difference between Reference Evapotranspiration ($ET_0$) and Crop Evapotranspiration ($ET_c$).

**Answer 4:**
Reference Evapotranspiration ($ET_0$) is the ET from a standardized reference crop (e.g., well-watered grass or alfalfa) under specific climatic conditions. Crop Evapotranspiration ($ET_c$) is the ET for a specific crop under prevailing field conditions. $ET_c$ is calculated by multiplying $ET_0$ by a crop coefficient ($K_c$) that accounts for the specific crop's characteristics and growth stage: $ET_c = K_c \times ET_0$.
