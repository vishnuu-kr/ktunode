# Irrigation and Drainage Engineering: Module 1 - Surface Irrigation Methods

## Topic: Irrigation Water Distribution: Canal Network and Canal Regulation

This module focuses on the fundamental aspects of delivering irrigation water to the field, specifically through canal networks and the regulation of flow within these networks. This is a crucial step in ensuring efficient and equitable distribution for surface irrigation methods like border irrigation.

### 1. Introduction to Irrigation Water Distribution

Irrigation water distribution is the process of transporting water from its source (like a river or reservoir) to the individual fields requiring irrigation. This is primarily achieved through a network of canals. The efficient design and operation of this network are critical for maximizing crop yields and minimizing water losses.

#### **Key Concepts:**

*   **Irrigation System:** The entire infrastructure designed to supply water for irrigation, including the source, conveyance structures, distribution network, and field application systems.
*   **Canal Irrigation:** A system where water is conveyed and distributed through a network of open channels (canals).
*   **Water Course:** The final channel from which water is drawn into the fields.

---

### 2. Canal Network Components

A typical canal irrigation network consists of a hierarchy of canals, designed to efficiently transport and distribute water.

#### **Types of Canals:**

*   **Main Canal:**
    *   Carries water from the source (e.g., barrage, diversion dam) to the main off-taking points of branch canals.
    *   Usually the largest in size and carries the highest discharge.
    *   Designed to operate continuously or with planned shutdowns.
*   **Branch Canal:**
    *   Takes off from the main canal and distributes water to a specific area or command.
    *   Smaller than the main canal but larger than distributaries.
    *   Often serves as a header for multiple distributaries.
*   **Distributary:**
    *   Takes off from a branch canal or main canal.
    *   Feeds water to a group of outlets that supply watercourses.
    *   Designed to supply water to a specific command area.
*   **Minor:**
    *   Takes off from a distributary.
    *   Supplies water to a smaller area and is generally smaller in section.
*   **Water Course (or Outlet Channel):**
    *   The final channel taking off from a distributary or minor.
    *   Designed to deliver water to a specific number of fields or to a water management body.
    *   Typically managed by the farmers themselves.

#### **Example of a Canal Hierarchy:**

River Barrage -> Main Canal -> Branch Canal -> Distributary -> Water Course -> Field

### 3. Canal Regulation

Canal regulation refers to the **controlled management of water flow** within the canal network to ensure that water is delivered to the intended areas at the required time, in the correct quantity, and with equitable distribution. It involves the operation of various control structures.

#### **Importance of Canal Regulation:**

*   **Equitable Water Distribution:** Ensures fair sharing of water among users, especially during periods of scarcity.
*   **Meeting Crop Demands:** Delivers water according to the irrigation needs of crops in different command areas.
*   **Minimizing Water Losses:** Prevents over-irrigation and reduces seepage and evaporation losses.
*   **Preventing Waterlogging and Salinity:** Avoids excessive wetting of the soil that can lead to drainage problems.
*   **Operational Flexibility:** Allows for adjustments in water supply based on rainfall, crop stages, and system conditions.
*   **Maintaining Canal Stability:** Prevents scouring or silting of the canal bed and banks.

---

### 4. Canal Regulation Structures

These are engineered structures built within the canal system to control and regulate water flow.

#### **Key Structures and Their Functions:**

*   **Head Regulator:**
    *   Located at the off-taking point of a canal from a larger canal or source (e.g., main canal off-taking from a barrage).
    *   **Function:** Controls the entry of water into the canal and regulates the discharge. Often has gates to adjust flow.
    *   **Example:** The head regulator of a branch canal taking off from a main canal.
*   **Cross Regulator:**
    *   Located at the tail end of a canal or at the junction of two canals.
    *   **Function:** Maintains the full supply level (FSL) in the upstream canal by impounding water, thereby ensuring adequate head for off-taking channels. It can also be used to divert flow.
    *   **Example:** A cross regulator at the tail of a distributary to ensure adequate supply to its outlets.
*   **Regulator (General Term):**
    *   Includes gates or other devices to control the flow. Can be at off-takes (like under-sluices) or within the canal to manage levels.
*   **Under-sluice (or Under-gate):**
    *   A regulator built across the parent canal, usually just downstream of an off-taking canal.
    *   **Function:** Used to pass excess water, silt, or debris downstream, or to control the water level in the parent canal for regulating supply to off-taking channels.
    *   **Example:** Passing heavy silt load during monsoon flow before drawing water for irrigation.
*   **Fall (or Drop):**
    *   Built where there is a significant difference in the levels of two connected canals or sections of the same canal.
    *   **Function:** Safely drops the water level, dissipating energy to prevent scouring.
    *   **Types:** Straight glacis fall, Ogee fall, notched fall.
*   **Regulator on Off-take:**
    *   A structure with gates at the entry of a branch canal, distributary, or water course from a parent canal.
    *   **Function:** Regulates the discharge into the off-taking channel.
*   **Tail Regulator:**
    *   Located at the tail end of a canal.
    *   **Function:** Controls the outflow from the canal, allowing it to be used as a storage reservoir or to regulate flow into another channel.
*   **Escape:**
    *   A channel or structure to discharge excess water from the canal system safely into a river, drain, or sea.
    *   **Function:** Prevents overtopping of canal banks and controls water levels during emergencies or planned releases.
    *   **Example:** Escape channel to drain off surplus monsoon flow.
*   **Super Passage:**
    *   An aqueduct or a bridge for carrying a canal over a natural drain or a railway line.
    *   **Function:** Prevents the mixing of canal water with drainage water or obstruction by the natural feature.
*   **Aqueduct:**
    *   A structure carrying a canal over a natural stream or drainage channel.
    *   **Function:** Ensures that the canal flow is not interrupted by cross-drainage.
*   **Level Crossing:**
    *   A structure where a canal crosses a drainage channel at the same level.
    *   **Function:** Water from both the canal and the drain is allowed to mix, and then separated by a regulator. This is less common due to potential for mixing and increased siltation.

### 5. Principles of Equitable Water Distribution

Ensuring fairness in water allocation is paramount. This is achieved through various distribution methods and policies.

#### **Methods of Water Distribution:**

*   **Fixed Demand (or Rationing):**
    *   A predetermined amount of water is allocated to each outlet for a specific period.
    *   **Pros:** Simpler to manage.
    *   **Cons:** May not meet actual crop needs if demand fluctuates.
*   **Turnover (or Rotational):**
    *   Water is supplied to outlets in rotation for a fixed duration, ensuring that each outlet receives its allocated water over a period.
    *   **Pros:** Allows for flexibility in responding to actual water availability and demand.
    *   **Cons:** Requires careful scheduling and communication.
*   **Common Outlet System:**
    *   Multiple water courses draw water from a single outlet, and farmers at the outlet share the water among themselves.
    *   **Pros:** Can be efficient if farmers cooperate.
    *   **Cons:** Prone to disputes and inequity if not managed well.
*   **Warabandi System (common in India):**
    *   A system where farmers are assigned specific days and times for receiving water at their outlet, based on the irrigation duty and the area served by the outlet.
    *   **Pros:** Ensures equitable distribution based on fixed turns.
    *   **Cons:** Can be rigid and might not align perfectly with actual crop water needs if irrigation timings are strictly enforced.

#### **Factors Influencing Equitable Distribution:**

*   **Irrigation Duty:** The area that can be irrigated with a unit volume of water.
*   **Water Requirements of Crops:** Different crops have varying water needs.
*   **Availability of Water:** Seasonal variations in rainfall and river flow.
*   **Topography of the Command Area:** Affects water flow and the need for different irrigation intensities.
*   **Timeliness of Water Delivery:** Delivering water when crops need it is crucial.

### 6. Water Delivery and Control in Canal Networks

This involves the practical aspects of operating the canal system.

#### **Key Considerations:**

*   **Water Measurement:** Accurate measurement of discharge at various points (e.g., using flow meters, gates settings) is essential for monitoring and control.
*   **Gate Operations:** Skilled operators are required to adjust gates of regulators and off-takes to maintain desired flow rates and levels.
*   **Forecasting:** Predicting water availability and demand helps in planning water distribution.
*   **Communication:** Effective communication channels between canal operators, water users, and managing bodies are vital.
*   **Maintenance:** Regular maintenance of canals and structures ensures efficient operation and prevents failures.
*   **Performance Monitoring:** Evaluating the efficiency of water delivery and identifying areas for improvement.

#### **Ideal Wetting Pattern (Relating to Border Irrigation):**

While this topic focuses on distribution, it's important to link it to the field application. For border irrigation, the ideal wetting pattern is **uniform wetting across the width of the border and sufficient depth of wetting to meet crop root zone requirements.** The canal network's role is to deliver the water to the head of the border at the correct flow rate and pressure to achieve this uniform application. Poor regulation in the canal system can lead to:

*   **Under-watering at the head of the border:** Due to insufficient flow or pressure from the upstream canal.
*   **Over-watering at the tail of the border:** Due to excessive flow or pressure, or if the canal supplies too much water to the inlet.
*   **Non-uniform wetting across the width:** If the inlet to the border is not designed correctly or if the flow from the canal is turbulent.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

1.  **Question:** What is the primary function of a cross-regulator in an irrigation canal network?
    **Answer:** To maintain the full supply level (FSL) in the upstream canal by impounding water, thereby ensuring adequate head for off-taking channels.

2.  **Question:** List the main components of a canal irrigation network in order of their typical hierarchy.
    **Answer:** Main Canal, Branch Canal, Distributary, Minor, Water Course.

3.  **Question:** Explain why equitable water distribution is important in canal irrigation.
    **Answer:** Equitable distribution ensures fair sharing of water among users, meeting crop demands, minimizing water losses, and preventing disputes, especially during periods of water scarcity.

4.  **Question:** If the canal network upstream of a distributary is not properly regulated, what potential problems could arise at the field level for border irrigation?
    **Answer:** The distributary might receive too much or too little water, leading to either over-watering or under-watering of the borders. This can result in non-uniform wetting, waterlogging, or water stress for the crops.

5.  **Question:** Differentiate between a head regulator and an escape.
    **Answer:** A head regulator controls the entry of water into a canal from a larger source, regulating its discharge. An escape, on the other hand, is designed to discharge excess water from the canal system safely to prevent overtopping or flooding.

6.  **Exercise:** Imagine a distributary canal serving 10 water courses, each irrigating 10 hectares of land. If the total irrigation requirement for this distributary is 0.5 m³/s, and water is supplied rotationally, how would you ensure equitable distribution among the water courses? (Assume all water courses irrigate similar crops and land types).
    **Answer:** The total flow of 0.5 m³/s needs to be divided among 10 water courses. Ideally, each water course should receive 0.05 m³/s. In a rotational system, this flow would be maintained for specific durations for each water course in turn. For example, if each water course needs water for 24 hours per week, the 0.5 m³/s would be directed to water course 1 for its allocated time, then to water course 2, and so on. The canal operator would need to manage the off-take regulators for each water course accordingly.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Hierarchy is key:** Understand the flow of water from larger to smaller canals.
*   **Regulation is control:** Canal regulation is about managing water flow for efficiency and fairness.
*   **Structures serve specific purposes:** Each regulation structure has a defined role in managing water.
*   **Equity is a core principle:** Fair distribution is essential for the success of any irrigation system.
*   **Link to field application:** The efficiency of the canal network directly impacts the effectiveness of field irrigation methods like border irrigation.
