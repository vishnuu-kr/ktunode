# REPAIR AND REHABILITATION OF STRUCTURES

## Module 1: Introduction – Maintenance, importance of maintenance, routine and preventive maintenance, rehabilitation, repair, retrofit and strengthening, need for rehabilitation of structures.

### Topic: Damages to Masonry Structures – Various Damages and Causes

---

### **1. Introduction to Damages in Masonry Structures**

Masonry structures, built from materials like brick, stone, mortar, and concrete blocks, are known for their durability. However, they are not immune to damage over time due to various environmental, structural, and human-induced factors. Understanding these damages is crucial for effective maintenance, repair, and rehabilitation.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Masonry:** A structure built from individual units laid in and bound together by mortar.
*   **Durability:** The ability of a material or structure to resist weathering and other environmental effects.
*   **Deterioration:** The process of gradual degradation or decay of a material or structure.

---

### **2. Various Damages to Masonry Structures and Their Causes**

Damages to masonry structures can manifest in numerous ways, affecting their structural integrity, aesthetic appeal, and functionality. They can be broadly categorized based on their origin.

#### **2.1. Physical and Mechanical Damages**

These are damages resulting from direct physical forces or mechanical actions.

*   **Cracking:**
    *   **Description:** Fractures or fissures appearing in masonry units, mortar joints, or the entire wall. Cracks can vary in width, depth, and pattern.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Differential Settlement:** Uneven settling of the foundation due to soil instability or poor compaction.
        *   **Thermal Expansion and Contraction:** Temperature fluctuations causing materials to expand and contract at different rates.
        *   **Moisture Expansion:** Absorption and subsequent expansion of certain masonry materials (e.g., some clay bricks).
        *   **Structural Overloading:** Loads exceeding the designed capacity of the masonry.
        *   **Vibrations:** From nearby construction, traffic, or earthquakes.
        *   **Frost Action:** Water freezing and expanding within pores and cracks, exerting pressure.
        *   **Shrinkage:** Drying shrinkage of mortar or building materials.
        *   **Poor Construction Practices:** Inadequate jointing, improper curing of mortar.
        *   **Concentrated Loads:** Localized stress points due to structural design flaws or alterations.
        *   **Example:** A wall cracking diagonally from a window corner to the floor due to uneven foundation settlement.

*   **Spalling:**
    *   **Description:** The chipping or flaking of the surface layer of masonry units or mortar.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Frost Action:** Water seeping into the surface, freezing, and expanding, causing the outer layer to break away.
        *   **Salt Crystallization:** Soluble salts migrating to the surface and crystallizing, exerting outward pressure.
        *   **Chemical Attack:** Aggressive chemicals in the environment or from nearby substances reacting with the masonry.
        *   **Deterioration of Mortar:** Weakened or deteriorated mortar is more susceptible to spalling.
        *   **High Thermal Stress:** Rapid temperature changes.
        *   **Example:** Brick faces detaching from a wall exposed to freezing temperatures and de-icing salts.

*   **Erosion/Abrasion:**
    *   **Description:** Wearing away of the surface of masonry units and mortar due to friction or abrasive forces.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Wind-blown Sand and Dust:** Particularly in exposed or arid regions.
        *   **Water Flow:** Rainwater runoff carrying abrasive particles.
        *   **Mechanical Scrubbing:** Aggressive cleaning methods.
        *   **Traffic Vibration:** Causing materials to rub against each other.
        *   **Example:** The exposed lower sections of a brick wall showing wear from frequent rain and wind.

*   **Efflorescence:**
    *   **Description:** White, powdery deposits appearing on the surface of masonry, caused by soluble salts crystallizing after moisture evaporates.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Presence of Soluble Salts:** In masonry units (bricks, stone), mortar, or underlying soil.
        *   **Moisture Movement:** Water migrating through the masonry and carrying salts to the surface.
        *   **Poor Waterproofing:** Allowing excessive moisture ingress.
        *   **Example:** White streaks or patches on a brick facade after a period of rain followed by dry weather.

*   **Subflorescence:**
    *   **Description:** Similar to efflorescence, but salts crystallize beneath the surface, causing blistering or delamination of the surface layer.
    *   **Causes:** Similar to efflorescence, but the crystallization occurs within the pores, leading to internal pressure.
    *   **Example:** The paint or render layer on a masonry wall lifting and bubbling due to subflorescence.

*   **Structural Movement Damages:**
    *   **Description:** Damages caused by overall structural instability or excessive movement.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Foundation Issues:** Settlement, uplift, or lateral movement.
        *   **Lateral Loads:** Wind, seismic forces, or impact loads.
        *   **Creep:** Long-term deformation under sustained load.
        *   **Lack of Restraint:** Walls not adequately tied to floors or roofs.
        *   **Example:** Walls bulging outwards due to insufficient lateral support.

#### **2.2. Environmental and Chemical Damages**

These damages are caused by exposure to environmental elements and chemical reactions.

*   **Moisture-Related Damages:**
    *   **Description:** A broad category encompassing issues arising from excess water.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Rain Penetration:** Poor roof drainage, damaged gutters, uncapped or poorly pointed walls.
        *   **Rising Damp:** Groundwater rising through capillary action in porous masonry.
        *   **Condensation:** Moisture from internal sources accumulating on cold surfaces.
        *   **Poor Drainage:** Water pooling around foundations.
        *   **Example:** Mould growth on interior walls, damp patches, and crumbling plaster due to persistent rain penetration.

*   **Chemical Attack on Mortar:**
    *   **Description:** Deterioration of the mortar binder due to chemical reactions.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Sulphate Attack:** Sulphate ions (often from soil or industrial pollution) reacting with cementitious mortar, causing expansion and disintegration.
        *   **Acid Attack:** Exposure to acidic environments (e.g., industrial pollution, acid rain) can dissolve lime-based mortars.
        *   **Alkali-Aggregate Reaction (AAR):** In concrete blocks or concrete components, a reaction between alkalis in cement and certain aggregates can cause expansion and cracking.
        *   **Example:** Mortar joints becoming soft and chalky due to sulphate attack from contaminated soil.

*   **Frost Damage:**
    *   **Description:** Physical damage caused by the expansion of water as it freezes within the pores of masonry.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **High Porosity:** Materials with high water absorption capacity.
        *   **Repeated Freeze-Thaw Cycles:** Periods of freezing and thawing.
        *   **Saturated Conditions:** Masonry that remains wet for extended periods.
        *   **Example:** Brickwork showing surface delamination and cracking in regions with harsh winters.

*   **Biological Growth:**
    *   **Description:** Growth of plants, algae, moss, and fungi on masonry surfaces.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Dampness and Shade:** Providing favorable conditions for growth.
        *   **Nutrients:** Available from porous surfaces or airborne particles.
        *   **Root Action:** Plant roots can penetrate cracks and exert pressure, widening them.
        *   **Example:** Ivy growing on a brick wall, its roots causing damage to mortar joints and potentially the bricks themselves.

#### **2.3. Construction and Design Related Damages**

These are damages that originate from how the structure was conceived and built.

*   **Defective Mortar:**
    *   **Description:** Weak, porous, or improperly mixed mortar.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Incorrect Mix Proportions:** Too much sand, insufficient binder.
        *   **Poor Quality Materials:** Contaminated aggregates or binder.
        *   **Inadequate Mixing:** Non-uniform distribution of components.
        *   **Rapid Drying:** Insufficient curing time for the mortar to harden.
        *   **Example:** Mortar crumbling easily to the touch, indicating it was poorly mixed or cured.

*   **Inadequate Flashing and DPC (Damp Proof Course):**
    *   **Description:** Absence or failure of protective layers designed to prevent water ingress.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Omission during Construction:** Flashing or DPC not installed as per design.
        *   **Deterioration:** Materials degrading over time.
        *   **Incorrect Installation:** Improperly sealed or positioned.
        *   **Example:** Water rising up a wall from the ground due to a missing or failed DPC, leading to dampness and salt attack.

*   **Lack of Wall Ties:**
    *   **Description:** Insufficient or corroded metal ties connecting different leaves of a cavity wall or bonding separate masonry elements.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Omission during construction.**
        *   **Corrosion of metal ties:** Especially in damp environments or with dissimilar metals.
        *   **Example:** A cavity wall showing signs of separation or bulging due to missing or failed wall ties.

*   **Improper Detailing:**
    *   **Description:** Poor design or execution of junctions, openings, or other critical areas.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Inadequate lintels over openings:** Leading to cracking above windows and doors.
        *   **Poorly designed junctions:** Between walls and floors/roofs, leading to water ingress.
        *   **Lack of expansion joints:** In long masonry walls susceptible to thermal movement.
        *   **Example:** Cracks forming above windows and doors where the masonry is not adequately supported by a lintel.

*   **Use of Incompatible Materials:**
    *   **Description:** Using materials with different properties (e.g., porosity, expansion characteristics) that lead to stress and damage.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Repairs with overly strong or impermeable materials:** Trapping moisture in softer original masonry.
        *   **Mixing of different brick types or mortars without consideration for their properties.**
        *   **Example:** Applying a hard, impermeable render to a soft historic brick wall, causing moisture to become trapped and lead to spalling of the brick.

#### **2.4. Human-Induced Damages**

These are damages caused by human activities.

*   **Impact Damage:**
    *   **Description:** Physical damage caused by direct impact.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Vehicular Collisions.**
        *   **Falling Debris.**
        *   **Vandalism.**
        *   **Example:** A corner of a brick building being knocked off by a vehicle.

*   **Alterations and Modifications:**
    *   **Description:** Changes made to the structure that compromise its original design intent.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Removal of load-bearing walls.**
        *   **Cutting of chases for services without proper reinforcement.**
        *   **Installation of new openings without adequate structural support.**
        *   **Example:** Cracking developing in a wall after a new, unsupported opening was cut for a doorway.

*   **Vibration from Adjacent Activities:**
    *   **Description:** Damage caused by vibrations from construction, traffic, or industrial processes.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   **Piling operations.**
        *   **Heavy traffic.**
        *   **Machinery operation.**
        *   **Example:** Loosening of mortar joints or cracking in masonry from prolonged exposure to heavy traffic vibrations.

### **3. Importance of Identifying and Understanding Damages**

*   **Safety:** Unaddressed damages can lead to structural failure, posing a risk to occupants and the public.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Early detection and intervention are generally less expensive than major repairs or complete reconstruction.
*   **Preservation:** Understanding the causes allows for appropriate repair methods that preserve the historical and aesthetic integrity of the structure.
*   **Functionality:** Damages can impair the building's ability to provide shelter, comfort, and its intended use.
*   **Legal and Regulatory Compliance:** Building codes and heritage regulations often mandate the maintenance and repair of structures.

### **6. Answers to Practice Questions**

1.  **Cracking:** Can be caused by differential settlement, thermal movement, or overloading.
    **Spalling:** Typically caused by frost action or salt crystallization, leading to surface flaking.
    **Erosion:** Wearing away of surfaces by wind-blown particles or water, especially in exposed areas.

2.  Efflorescence is the formation of white, powdery salt deposits on the masonry surface. It occurs when soluble salts present in the masonry materials or soil are dissolved by moisture, transported to the surface by capillary action, and then crystallize as the moisture evaporates.

3.  Frost action damages masonry when water enters the pores of the material. When the temperature drops below freezing, this water turns to ice, which expands. This expansion exerts pressure within the pores and on the surrounding material. Repeated cycles of freezing and thawing can cause the surface layers of masonry units to chip, flake (spall), and crack.

4.  An example of inadequate detailing leading to damage is the omission of a proper lintel (a structural beam) over a window or door opening. Without adequate support, the masonry above the opening can crack under its own weight, leading to diagonal cracks radiating from the corners of the opening.

5.  Identifying the cause of damage is crucial because the correct repair method depends entirely on the underlying problem. For instance, addressing a damp patch requires different interventions (e.g., DPC repair, drainage improvement) than addressing a crack caused by structural overload (e.g., reinforcement, load redistribution). Treating only the symptom (e.g., repointing a crack without fixing the cause of settlement) will likely result in the damage recurring, leading to further costs and potential structural compromise.

---

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Moisture is a primary enemy of masonry.** It can cause physical damage (frost), chemical attack (sulphates), and promote biological growth.
*   **Differential movement** (settlement, thermal) is a significant cause of cracking.
*   **Mortar quality is critical.** Weak or incompatible mortar compromises the entire wall.
*   **Always look for the cause, not just the symptom.** Proper diagnosis is the first step to effective repair.
*   **Good design and construction practices** prevent many common damages from occurring in the first place.
