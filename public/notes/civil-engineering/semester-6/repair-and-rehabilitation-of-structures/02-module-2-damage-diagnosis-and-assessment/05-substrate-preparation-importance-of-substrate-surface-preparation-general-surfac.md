# REPAIR AND REHABILITATION OF STRUCTURES - Module 2: Damage Diagnosis and Assessment

## Topic: Substrate Preparation

### 1. Importance of Substrate/Surface Preparation

Effective substrate preparation is the **cornerstone of successful repair and rehabilitation**. Without it, even the most advanced repair materials and techniques will fail prematurely, leading to wasted time, money, and continued structural degradation.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Adhesion:** The ability of a new repair material to bond effectively to the existing substrate. Poor preparation leads to weak or non-existent adhesion.
*   **Durability:** The long-term performance and resistance of the repaired structure to environmental factors and loads. Proper preparation significantly enhances durability.
*   **Integrity:** The structural soundness and wholeness of the repaired element.
*   **Compatibility:** Ensuring the prepared surface is suitable for the chosen repair material.

**Why is Substrate Preparation Crucial?**

*   **Ensures Proper Adhesion:**
    *   Removes weak, loose, or contaminated layers that prevent the new material from bonding to the sound substrate.
    *   Creates a surface profile (roughness) that increases the surface area for mechanical interlocking.
*   **Improves Durability of Repairs:**
    *   Removes delaminating concrete, rust, and other unsound materials that can lead to future spalling or cracking.
    *   Protects reinforcing steel from further corrosion by removing rust and chlorides.
    *   Provides a clean surface for the application of protective coatings or membranes.
*   **Enhances Aesthetics:**
    *   Removes unsightly stains, discoloration, and damaged material, leading to a visually pleasing repair.
*   **Facilitates Material Performance:**
    *   Allows repair materials to cure and achieve their intended strength and properties without interference from contaminants.
*   **Prevents Future Deterioration:**
    *   Addresses the root causes of damage, such as ingress of aggressive substances, by creating a barrier or removing contaminated material.

**Examples:**

*   **Bond failure:** Applying a repair mortar over chalky or dusty concrete will result in the mortar delaminating.
*   **Corrosion recurrence:** If rust is not removed from reinforcing steel before applying a repair mortar, the rust will continue to expand and crack the repair.
*   **Coating delamination:** Painting over a poorly prepared, oily concrete surface will cause the paint to peel.

### 2. General Surface Preparation Methods and Procedure

The choice of surface preparation method depends on the type of substrate, the nature and extent of damage, and the repair material to be used. The general procedure typically involves a sequence of steps.

**General Procedure:**

1.  **Inspection and Diagnosis:** Thoroughly inspect the area to be repaired to understand the type and extent of damage.
2.  **Define the Repair Area:** Clearly delineate the boundaries of the damaged zone requiring preparation.
3.  **Select Appropriate Method(s):** Based on inspection and the repair material specifications, choose the most suitable preparation techniques.
4.  **Execute Surface Preparation:** Carry out the selected methods to achieve the desired surface condition.
5.  **Clean the Prepared Surface:** Remove all dust, debris, and loose particles.
6.  **Inspect the Prepared Surface:** Verify that the surface meets the required standards before applying the repair material.

**Common Surface Preparation Methods:**

*   **A. Mechanical Methods:**

    *   **Scabbling/Bush Hammering:**
        *   **Description:** Uses pneumatic tools with multiple chisels to chip away at the concrete surface.
        *   **Purpose:** Removes damaged, delaminated, or weak concrete and creates a rough surface profile.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   Identify and mark the areas to be scabbed.
            *   Use scabbling tools to remove concrete to a consistent depth, exposing sound material.
            *   Avoid over-scabbling, which can further damage the substrate.
            *   Carefully remove all debris generated.
        *   **Key Points:** Can be labor-intensive, generates significant dust and vibration. Good for thicker delamination.
        *   **Surface Profile Achieved:** Typically ICRI CSP 3-4 (Concrete Surface Profile).

    *   **Grit Blasting/Sandblasting (Abrasive Blasting):**
        *   **Description:** Propels abrasive media (e.g., sand, grit, slag) at high velocity against the surface using compressed air.
        *   **Purpose:** Removes rust, coatings, laitance, and creates a clean, textured surface. Highly effective for achieving good adhesion.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   Protect surrounding areas and personnel from abrasive material.
            *   Apply blasting at appropriate pressure and from a suitable distance.
            *   Achieve a uniform blasting pattern.
            *   Remove all abrasive residue and dust after blasting.
        *   **Key Points:** Highly effective, can be controlled to achieve different surface profiles. Requires specialized equipment and safety precautions (respiratory protection, eye protection). Can be wet or dry blasting.
        *   **Surface Profile Achieved:** ICRI CSP 3-7, depending on the abrasive and pressure.

    *   **High-Pressure Water Jetting:**
        *   **Description:** Uses highly pressurized water (typically 10,000 - 40,000 psi) to clean and remove unsound material.
        *   **Purpose:** Removes weak concrete, laitance, coatings, and efflorescence. Minimally damaging to sound concrete.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   Ensure adequate water supply and pressure.
            *   Operate the jetting equipment at a safe distance, ensuring uniform coverage.
            *   The debris and water mixture needs containment and disposal.
        *   **Key Points:** Environmentally friendly, reduces dust. Can be very effective. Can cause "frazzled" surface texture if not controlled, which might require a bonding agent.
        *   **Surface Profile Achieved:** ICRI CSP 2-4.

    *   **Chipping (Hammer and Chisel):**
        *   **Description:** Manual removal of unsound concrete using hammers and chisels.
        *   **Purpose:** Localized removal of small areas of spalled or cracked concrete.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   Identify and mark the perimeter of the unsound material.
            *   Chisel away material perpendicular to the surface.
            *   Create a "dove-tail" or undercut at the edges to improve mechanical keying.
            *   Remove all debris.
        *   **Key Points:** Suitable for small, localized repairs. Labor-intensive and can be time-consuming.
        *   **Surface Profile Achieved:** ICRI CSP 2-3.

    *   **Grinding:**
        *   **Description:** Uses rotating abrasive discs or diamond tooling to smooth or roughen the surface.
        *   **Purpose:** Removing thin layers of paint, coatings, laitance, or creating a smooth finish for certain applications. Can also be used to create a slight texture.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   Use appropriate grinding discs for the task.
            *   Maintain a consistent angle and pressure.
            *   Ensure dust extraction.
        *   **Key Points:** Effective for surface smoothing and removal of light contaminants. Less aggressive than blasting.
        *   **Surface Profile Achieved:** ICRI CSP 1-3.

*   **B. Chemical Methods:**

    *   **Acid Etching:**
        *   **Description:** Using dilute acids (e.g., hydrochloric or phosphoric acid) to react with and remove the surface layer of cement paste.
        *   **Purpose:** To open the pores and create a better bonding surface for coatings or thin overlays, especially on new concrete.
        *   **Procedure:**
            *   Protect surrounding areas and personnel from acid.
            *   Apply acid evenly.
            *   Allow sufficient reaction time.
            *   Thoroughly neutralize the acid and rinse the surface multiple times with clean water.
            *   Allow the surface to dry completely.
        *   **Key Points:** Generates hazardous waste, requires thorough rinsing and neutralization. Less common for structural repairs. Can cause micro-cracking if not controlled.
        *   **Surface Profile Achieved:** Typically a CSP 1-2.

**ICRI Concrete Surface Profile (CSP) Classification:**

The International Concrete Repair Institute (ICRI) has developed a widely used standard for classifying surface profiles, crucial for selecting the appropriate repair material and preparation method.

| CSP No. | Description                  | Approximate Roughness (mm / mils) | Typical Preparation Method(s)             | Suitable for:                                                              |
| :------ | :--------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1       | Very Rough                   | 0.25 / 10                         | Grinding, Light Acid Etching              | Thin overlays, sealers, coatings with good adhesion                          |
| 2       | Rough                        | 0.50 / 20                         | Heavy Grinding, Water Jetting, Acid Etching | Coatings, membranes, polymer overlays                                      |
| 3       | Medium                     | 1.0 / 40                          | Light Sandblasting, Water Jetting, Chipping | Cementitious overlays, epoxy mortars, patching materials                   |
| 4       | Medium-Coarse                | 1.5 / 60                          | Sandblasting, Scabbling                   | Structural mortars, polymer concrete, self-leveling underlayments            |
| 5       | Coarse                       | 2.0 / 80                          | Aggressive Sandblasting, Scabbling          | Heavy patching, structural overlays, shotcrete                             |
| 6       | Very Coarse                  | 2.5 / 100                         | High-Pressure Water Jetting, Shotblasting | Shotcrete, repair mortars requiring significant mechanical keying            |
| 7       | Extremely Coarse             | 3.0+ / 120+                       | Scarifying, Heavy Scabbling, Blasting     | Heavy structural repairs, shotcrete, polymer overlays requiring deep embedment |

**Important Considerations for General Preparation:**

*   **Depth of Removal:** Remove concrete to a depth that ensures all defective material is gone and provides adequate embedment for the repair material. Typically, at least 10-20 mm (3/8 - 3/4 inch) beyond the visible defect.
*   **Edge Treatment:** Edges of the prepared area should be squared or slightly undercut (dove-tailed) to provide a mechanical key and prevent feather-edging, which is prone to cracking.
*   **Dust Control:** Implement effective dust suppression and collection systems (e.g., HEPA vacuums) to protect workers and the environment, especially when using dry methods.
*   **Water/Wet Methods:** If using water jetting, manage the wastewater and runoff. For some repair materials, a saturated surface dry (SSD) condition might be required before application.

### 3. Reinforcing Steel Cleaning

Reinforcing steel (rebar) is critical for the tensile strength of concrete structures. Its condition, particularly the presence of rust, significantly impacts the long-term performance of repairs.

**Why is Reinforcing Steel Cleaning Crucial?**

*   **Prevents Further Corrosion:** Rust is porous and retains moisture and chlorides, accelerating corrosion. Removing rust is essential to stop this process.
*   **Ensures Bond:** Rust, mill scale, and loosely adhering deposits on the rebar prevent the repair material from bonding effectively to the steel.
*   **Restores Steel Cross-Section:** Severe corrosion can reduce the diameter of the rebar, diminishing its load-bearing capacity. Cleaning reveals the extent of this loss.
*   **Improves Durability of Repair:** A clean, sound rebar surface is crucial for the integrity and longevity of the repaired concrete.

**Methods for Reinforcing Steel Cleaning:**

*   **A. Mechanical Methods:**

    *   **Wire Brushing:**
        *   **Description:** Using hand-held or power-driven wire brushes to scrub the rebar.
        *   **Purpose:** Removes loose rust, dirt, and scale.
        *   **Procedure:** Thoroughly brush all exposed surfaces of the rebar.
        *   **Key Points:** Suitable for light rust. May not remove all adherent rust or contamination.

    *   **Chipping:**
        *   **Description:** Using small hammers and chisels to break off rust and scale.
        *   **Purpose:** Removes more adherent rust than wire brushing.
        *   **Procedure:** Carefully chip away rust and scale from the rebar.
        *   **Key Points:** Can be labor-intensive. Care must be taken not to gouge or damage the steel.

    *   **Abrasive Blasting (Sandblasting, Grit Blasting):**
        *   **Description:** Similar to surface preparation, but targeted at the rebar.
        *   **Purpose:** The most effective method for removing all rust, scale, and contamination, achieving a bright, shiny surface (near white metal finish).
        *   **Procedure:** Blast all exposed rebar surfaces until all rust and scale are removed.
        *   **Key Points:** Highly effective. Requires careful control to avoid over-blasting and reducing the rebar diameter. The cleaned steel should be primed or covered with the repair material immediately to prevent flash rusting.

    *   **Needle Scaling:**
        *   **Description:** Using a tool with multiple vibrating needles that impact the surface.
        *   **Purpose:** Effective for removing rust, scale, and old coatings from rebar and other metallic surfaces.
        *   **Procedure:** Pass the needle scaler over the rebar until all contaminants are removed.
        *   **Key Points:** Good for getting into irregular shapes and corners of rebar.

*   **B. Chemical Methods:**

    *   **Rust Removers/Converters:**
        *   **Description:** Chemical solutions applied to the rebar. Rust removers dissolve rust, while rust converters chemically change rust into a stable, inert coating (often black).
        *   **Purpose:** Removes or stabilizes rust.
        *   **Procedure:** Apply according to manufacturer's instructions. Often requires subsequent rinsing or neutralization.
        *   **Key Points:** Can be effective but require careful handling and disposal. Rust converters are usually not sufficient for structural repairs; a mechanical cleaning step is still often recommended.

**Standards for Rebar Cleaning:**

*   **SSPC-SP 10 / NACE No. 2 (Near White Metal Blast Cleaning):** This is a common standard for rebar cleaning. It requires blasting to remove all oil, grease, dirt, dust, mill scale, and rust, leaving only a very light rust-staining or tight mill scale that cannot be removed by lifting with a dull knife.
*   **SSPC-SP 11 (Power Tool Cleaning to Bare Metal):** Requires removal of all rust, scale, and tightly adhering contaminants by power tools, resulting in a bare metal surface.

**Procedure Summary for Rebar Cleaning:**

1.  **Expose Rebar:** Ensure adequate exposure of the reinforcing steel within the repair area.
2.  **Initial Cleaning:** Remove loose rust and debris with a wire brush or chipping hammer.
3.  **Thorough Cleaning:** Employ abrasive blasting (SSPC-SP 10 or SP 11) or needle scaling to achieve a clean, bare metal surface.
4.  **Inspection:** Visually inspect the rebar for any remaining rust, pitting, or significant reduction in diameter.
5.  **Protection:** Apply a suitable anti-corrosion primer (compatible with the repair material) to the cleaned rebar immediately to prevent flash rusting. Alternatively, proceed with the repair material application if it contains integral corrosion inhibitors and the substrate is prepared to the correct moisture content.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Never apply repair materials over contaminated or inadequately prepared surfaces.**
*   **The goal is to achieve good mechanical and chemical bond.**
*   **Surface profile (CSP) is critical and must be matched to the repair material's requirements.**
*   **Thoroughly clean all dust and debris after preparation.**
*   **Protect cleaned reinforcing steel from re-contamination and immediate flash rusting.**
*   **Always follow the manufacturer's recommendations for both surface preparation and the repair material.**

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**

You are tasked with repairing a spalled concrete beam. The spalled area has delaminated concrete down to the reinforcing steel, which shows moderate rust. The repair material specified is a high-strength cementitious mortar.

a) What are the primary reasons why proper substrate preparation is crucial in this scenario?
b) Describe the steps you would take to prepare the concrete substrate in the spalled area. What ICRI CSP would you aim to achieve?
c) How would you clean the exposed reinforcing steel, and what standard should you aim for? What is the critical step after cleaning the rebar?

**Answer 1:**

a) **Importance of Substrate Preparation:**
    *   **Adhesion:** To ensure the new mortar bonds strongly to the sound concrete substrate, preventing future delamination.
    *   **Durability:** To remove the weak, damaged concrete and prevent further deterioration, ensuring the longevity of the repair.
    *   **Integrity:** To restore the structural integrity of the beam by providing a sound base for the repair.
    *   **Reinforcement Protection:** To remove rust from the rebar, stopping corrosion and ensuring effective load transfer.

b) **Concrete Substrate Preparation:**
    *   **Define Repair Area:** Mark the perimeter of the spalled area, extending at least 10-20 mm beyond the visible damage into sound concrete.
    *   **Remove Delaminated Concrete:** Use scabbling or chipping to remove all loose, cracked, and delaminated concrete until sound, hard concrete is exposed.
    *   **Edge Treatment:** Square off the edges of the prepared area or create a slight undercut (dove-tail) to provide mechanical keying.
    *   **Surface Profiling:** The overall surface within the repair area should be prepared to create a suitable profile for the cementitious mortar. **Abrasive blasting (e.g., sandblasting) or high-pressure water jetting would be suitable.** Aim for an **ICRI CSP of 3-5** for good mechanical keying and bond with a cementitious mortar.
    *   **Clean Surface:** After mechanical preparation, thoroughly clean the area using a HEPA vacuum or air blast to remove all dust and debris.

c) **Reinforcing Steel Cleaning:**
    *   **Method:** The most effective method for moderate rust is **abrasive blasting (sandblasting or grit blasting)** to achieve a **Near White Metal finish (SSPC-SP 10 / NACE No. 2)**. Alternatively, needle scaling can be used. Wire brushing might be sufficient for very light, superficial rust, but for moderate rust, it's insufficient.
    *   **Critical Step After Cleaning:** **Immediately apply a compatible anti-corrosion primer to the cleaned reinforcing steel** to prevent flash rusting. If the repair material itself contains corrosion inhibitors, ensure the steel is clean and the substrate is at the correct moisture content (e.g., SSD if required) before application.

**Question 2:**

Which surface preparation method is generally considered the most effective for removing old paint, heavy rust, and mill scale from steel structures before recoating, while also providing an excellent profile for adhesion?

**Answer 2:**

**Abrasive blasting (sandblasting, grit blasting, or shotblasting)** is generally considered the most effective method for removing old paint, heavy rust, and mill scale from steel structures while providing an excellent profile for adhesion.

---

**Question 3:**

True or False: Acid etching is a common and preferred method for preparing concrete substrates for structural concrete repairs due to its effectiveness and ease of use.

**Answer 3:**

**False.** Acid etching is less common for structural concrete repairs. While it can open pores for coatings, it generates hazardous waste, requires thorough neutralization and rinsing, and is less effective at creating the deeper surface profiles needed for structural mortars compared to mechanical methods like blasting or scabbling. It's more suited for light preparation of new concrete for coatings.
