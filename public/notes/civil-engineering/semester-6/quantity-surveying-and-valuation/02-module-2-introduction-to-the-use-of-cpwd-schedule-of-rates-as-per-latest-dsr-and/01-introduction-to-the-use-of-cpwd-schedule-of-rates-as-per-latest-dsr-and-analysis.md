# Quantity Surveying and Valuation: Module 2 - CPWD Schedule of Rates & Rate Analysis

## Topic: Introduction to the use of CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR) and Analysis of Rates (DAR)

This module introduces the fundamental tools used in India for estimating construction costs: the Central Public Works Department (CPWD) Schedule of Rates (DSR) and the CPWD Schedule of Rates for Analysis of Rates (DAR). Understanding these documents is crucial for accurate cost estimation, tendering, and project management.

---

### **Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   Understanding the purpose and structure of the CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR).
*   Identifying the key components and information contained within the DSR.
*   Explaining the concept and importance of Rate Analysis.
*   Understanding the methodology of preparing Rate Analysis as per the latest DAR.
*   Applying the principles of DSR and DAR in practical quantity surveying scenarios.

### **1. Introduction to CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR)**

#### **1.1 What is the CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR)?**

*   **Definition:** The CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR) is a comprehensive document published by the Central Public Works Department (CPWD) that provides standard rates for various building and civil engineering items of work.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   To standardize rates for common construction activities across different projects undertaken by the CPWD.
    *   To provide a benchmark for estimating costs in government and public sector projects.
    *   To ensure fairness and transparency in tendering and contract administration.
    *   To serve as a basis for contract negotiations and variations.
*   **Frequency of Updates:** The DSR is revised periodically (typically every few years) to reflect changes in material costs, labor wages, and technological advancements. The "latest DSR" refers to the most recently published edition.

#### **1.2 Structure and Components of the DSR:**

The DSR is typically organized into various volumes or sections, each covering specific types of construction. The general structure includes:

*   **Introduction/Preamble:** Explains the purpose, scope, and general conditions for using the DSR.
*   **General Specifications:** Outlines the quality standards, materials, workmanship, and testing requirements for various items. This is a crucial companion document to the rates.
*   **Item-wise Rates:** This is the core of the DSR, listing thousands of individual construction items. Each item typically includes:
    *   **Item No.:** A unique numerical code for identification.
    *   **Item Description:** A detailed description of the work to be performed, including dimensions, materials, and quality standards.
    *   **Unit of Measurement:** The unit in which the item is measured (e.g., sqm, cum, mtr, kg, No.).
    *   **Rate:** The standard rate for the item, usually broken down into components:
        *   **Cost of Materials:** Cost of all materials required per unit of work.
        *   **Cost of Labour:** Cost of labor required per unit of work.
        *   **Cost of Labour Welfare/Contingencies:** Provision for labor welfare, tools, plants, etc.
        *   **Total Rate:** The sum of the above components.
*   **Appendices:** May include additional information such as standard specifications, material lists, conversion factors, etc.
*   **Index:** A comprehensive index for easy navigation of items.

#### **1.3 Key Concepts and Information within the DSR:**

*   **Item Description:** Precision in understanding the item description is vital. It defines the scope and quality, which directly impacts the rate.
*   **Unit of Measurement:** Correctly identifying the unit of measurement is essential for accurate billing and measurement.
*   **Rates (Material, Labour, Total):** The DSR provides a composite rate, but the breakdown into material and labour costs is important for analysis and adjustments.
*   **Specifications:** The DSR rates are applicable only when the work is carried out as per the specified standards. Any deviation may require a rate analysis.
*   **Zone/Location Factors:** In some cases, the DSR might provide different rates for different geographical zones or locations due to variations in material availability and transportation costs. (Less common in the main DSR itself, more often factored in during tendering).
*   **Market Rates:** The DSR rates are generally based on prevailing market conditions at the time of its compilation. They are not necessarily the *current* market rates.

#### **1.4 How to Use the DSR:**

1.  **Identify the Item:** Accurately identify the construction item from the Bill of Quantities (BOQ) or project drawings.
2.  **Locate the Item in DSR:** Use the item number or description to find the corresponding entry in the DSR.
3.  **Understand the Description and Specifications:** Carefully read the item description and refer to the relevant general and specific specifications.
4.  **Note the Unit of Measurement:** Ensure consistency with the BOQ.
5.  **Extract the Rate:** Record the rate for the identified item.
6.  **Adjust for Variations (if any):** If the item is not exactly as per the DSR specification, or if market rates have significantly changed, rate analysis might be required.

### **2. Introduction to Analysis of Rates (DAR)**

#### **2.1 What is Analysis of Rates (Rate Analysis)?**

*   **Definition:** Rate analysis is the process of determining the fair and reasonable rate for a specific item of work by breaking it down into its constituent inputs (materials, labor, machinery, etc.) and calculating the cost of each input per unit of the finished item.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   To establish rates for items *not* included in the DSR.
    *   To determine rates for items where specifications differ from those in the DSR.
    *   To justify rates for items in the BOQ when tendering.
    *   To settle claims for variations or extra items of work.
    *   To facilitate costing and budgeting for unique or specialized construction activities.

#### **2.2 The CPWD Schedule of Rates for Analysis of Rates (DAR):**

The CPWD also publishes a separate document, often referred to as the DAR, which complements the DSR. The DAR provides:

*   **Standard Labour Output:** Norms for the quantum of work a specific category of labour can perform in a day. This is crucial for calculating labour costs.
*   **Standard Machinery Output:** Norms for the output of various construction machinery.
*   **Material Requirements:** Detailed schedules of materials required per unit of various items (often cross-referenced with DSR items).
*   **Methodology for Rate Analysis:** Guidelines and formats for preparing rate analyses.
*   **Classification of Labour:** Definitions of different categories of labour (e.g., skilled, semi-skilled, unskilled).

#### **2.3 Key Concepts and Components of Rate Analysis:**

The rate analysis process involves determining the cost per unit of work based on:

*   **Materials:**
    *   **Item-wise Material Consumption:** Quantities of each material required per unit of work (e.g., cement, sand, aggregate, steel, bricks).
    *   **Issue Rate of Materials:** Cost of materials at the site of work, including carriage. This can be taken from the DSR's material rates or from current market rates if significantly different.
*   **Labour:**
    *   **Labour Productivity Norms (from DAR):** The output of different types of labour per day for the specific item.
    *   **Labour Requirement per Unit Work:** Calculated by dividing the standard unit of work by the labour productivity norm.
    *   **Wages of Labour:** Daily wages for different categories of labour (skilled, semi-skilled, unskilled), including any statutory benefits.
*   **Machinery:**
    *   **Machinery Requirement:** The quantum of machine usage per unit of work.
    *   **Hiring/Operating Charges of Machinery:** Cost of running the machinery, including fuel, operator, maintenance, depreciation, etc.
*   **Tools and Plants (T&P):**
    *   A percentage of the total material and labour cost, or a specific lump sum, to account for the cost of tools, plants, and equipment used. This is often a standard percentage specified in the DSR/DAR.
*   **Water Charges:**
    *   Cost of water required for the work.
*   **Contingencies/Overheads:**
    *   A provision for unforeseen expenses, site supervision, establishment charges, etc. This is often included in the rate or as a separate percentage.

#### **2.4 Methodology of Preparing Rate Analysis (as per latest DAR):**

The DAR provides a standardized format and methodology. The general steps are:

1.  **Identify the Item and its Unit:** Clearly define the item for which the rate is to be analyzed and its unit of measurement.
2.  **Determine the Work Content:** Break down the item into its basic operations and identify the materials, labour, and machinery required for each operation.
3.  **Quantify Material Requirements:**
    *   Calculate the quantity of each material needed per unit of work. This often involves considering wastage.
    *   Refer to the DAR's material schedules or standard engineering practices.
4.  **Calculate Labour Requirements:**
    *   Determine the number and type of labourers required per unit of work.
    *   Use DAR labour productivity norms. If the norm is 'X' units per day for 'Y' labourers, then for 1 unit of work, you need (Y/X) labourers.
    *   Determine the daily wages for each type of labour.
5.  **Quantify Machinery Requirements (if applicable):**
    *   Determine the time/output of machinery required per unit of work.
    *   Calculate the hourly/daily charges for the machinery.
6.  **Calculate Material Costs:**
    *   Multiply the quantity of each material per unit of work by its issue rate.
    *   Sum up the costs of all materials.
7.  **Calculate Labour Costs:**
    *   Multiply the labour requirement per unit of work (in man-days) by the daily wages of each labour category.
    *   Sum up the costs for all labour categories.
8.  **Calculate Machinery Costs:**
    *   Multiply the machine usage per unit of work by its operating charges.
9.  **Add T&P, Water, Contingencies:**
    *   Add the provision for T&P, water charges, and other overheads as per norms or project requirements.
10. **Summation:** Add all calculated costs (materials, labour, machinery, T&P, etc.) to arrive at the total rate per unit of work.

### **3. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**

What is the primary purpose of the CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR)?
a) To provide a list of contractors
b) To standardize rates for construction items and serve as a benchmark for estimation
c) To detail construction methodologies only
d) To outline safety regulations

**Answer 1:** b) To standardize rates for construction items and serve as a benchmark for estimation

**Question 2:**

List any three key components found within a DSR item entry.

**Answer 2:**
*   Item Number
*   Item Description
*   Unit of Measurement
*   Rate (Material, Labour, Total)
*   Specifications

**Question 3:**

When is Rate Analysis typically required? Provide two scenarios.

**Answer 3:**
*   For items not included in the DSR.
*   For items where specifications differ from the DSR.
*   To justify rates during tendering for non-DSR items.
*   To settle claims for extra items of work.

**Question 4:**

If a labour norm states that 10 unskilled labourers can complete 20 sqm of plastering in a day, how many man-days of unskilled labour are required to plaster 1 sqm?

**Answer 4:**
Productivity = 20 sqm / day for 10 labourers
Man-days per sqm = (Total Labourers) / (Productivity)
Man-days per sqm = 10 labourers / 20 sqm/day = 0.5 man-days/sqm

**Question 5:**

Briefly explain the role of DAR in the rate analysis process.

**Answer 5:** The DAR provides the necessary data and norms for rate analysis, including standard labour productivity, material consumption details, machinery output norms, and methodologies for calculating rates for items not covered by the DSR or with different specifications.

---

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Always use the LATEST DSR and DAR:** Ensure you are working with the most current versions published by CPWD.
*   **Specifications are KEY:** The rates in the DSR are directly linked to the specified quality and workmanship. Always refer to the general and specific specifications.
*   **Accuracy in Measurement:** Correctly measuring quantities and identifying units of measurement is fundamental for applying DSR rates.
*   **Understanding the Breakdown:** While the DSR provides a composite rate, understanding the material and labour components is essential for adjustments and rate analysis.
*   **DAR is the Backbone of Rate Analysis:** The DAR provides the essential productivity norms and guidelines for constructing realistic rates for unlisted or modified items.
*   **Site Conditions Matter:** DSR rates are generally standard. Actual site conditions, market fluctuations, and specific project requirements might necessitate adjustments or rate analyses.
*   **Documentation:** Maintain clear and well-documented rate analyses for justification and record-keeping.
