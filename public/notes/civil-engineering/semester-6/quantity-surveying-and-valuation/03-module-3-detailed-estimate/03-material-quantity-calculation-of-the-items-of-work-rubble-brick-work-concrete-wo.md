# Quantity Surveying and Valuation: Module 3 - Detailed Estimate

## Topic: Material Quantity Calculation for Building Work

This module focuses on the meticulous process of calculating the quantities of materials required for various building works, forming the backbone of a detailed estimate. We will explore the methods for calculating quantities of rubble work, brickwork, concrete work, and plastering, utilizing data typically found in a Data Analysis and Reporting (DAR) document.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon completion of this study of this topic, you will be able to:

*   Understand the principles of material quantity calculation for building items.
*   Calculate the quantities of rubble work based on provided dimensions.
*   Determine the quantities of brickwork, accounting for mortar and wastage.
*   Calculate the quantities of concrete work, including various mix ratios and reinforcement.
*   Estimate the quantities of plastering, considering surface area and plaster thickness.
*   Apply DAR data (e.g., unit quantities for bricks, cement, sand, aggregate) in calculations.
*   Prepare accurate material quantity statements for a detailed estimate.

### 1. Introduction to Detailed Estimate and Material Quantity Calculation

*   **Detailed Estimate:** A comprehensive estimate that breaks down the cost of a project into individual items of work, specifying the quantities of materials and labor required for each.
*   **Purpose of Material Quantity Calculation:**
    *   To procure the correct amount of materials, minimizing wastage.
    *   To accurately estimate the cost of materials, a significant component of the total project cost.
    *   To facilitate efficient project planning and scheduling of material deliveries.
    *   To serve as a basis for comparison with actual material consumption during execution.
*   **Data Analysis and Reporting (DAR):** A crucial document in quantity surveying, often containing:
    *   **Bill of Quantities (BOQ):** A list of all the items of work to be executed, with their descriptions and units.
    *   **Specifications:** Details on the quality of materials, workmanship, and construction methods.
    *   **Drawings:** Architectural, structural, and other technical drawings that provide the dimensions and layout of the building.
    *   **Unit Quantities/Rates (from Schedules of Rates or DAR):** Information on the standard quantities of materials (e.g., number of bricks per cubic meter of brickwork) or labor units per unit of work. This is vital for this module.

### 2. Material Quantity Calculation for Rubble Work

*   **Definition:** Rubble work is masonry construction using undressed or roughly dressed stones (rubble) laid in mortar. It's typically used for foundations, retaining walls, and boundary walls.
*   **Units of Measurement:** Cubic Meters ($m^3$)
*   **Calculation Method:**
    1.  **Identify the item of work:** e.g., Rubble masonry in foundation.
    2.  **Obtain dimensions from drawings:** Length, width (thickness), and height (depth) of the rubble masonry element.
    3.  **Calculate the gross volume:** Volume = Length × Width × Height
    4.  **Deduct for openings:** If any, calculate the volume of openings and subtract from the gross volume.
    5.  **Consider mortar and voids:** Rubble masonry generally has a significant proportion of mortar and voids between stones. A typical reduction factor is applied to the gross volume to arrive at the net volume of stone.
        *   **Common DAR data/Specification:** Rubble masonry is often estimated assuming a certain percentage of mortar and voids (e.g., 20-30%). This means the *net volume of stone* is calculated by reducing the gross volume.
        *   **Alternative approach (more detailed):** Some specifications might provide a specific mortar quantity per cubic meter of rubble masonry. In such cases, you would calculate the gross volume and then calculate the mortar required separately.
    6.  **Calculate quantities of materials:**
        *   **Stone:** Net volume of stone.
        *   **Mortar:** Volume of mortar = Gross Volume × (Mortar percentage/100). Then, convert to cement and sand quantities based on the mix ratio and a bulking factor for cement and sand (often provided in DAR or specifications).

*   **Example:**
    Calculate the quantity of rubble masonry for a foundation trench of dimensions 10m (Length) × 1m (Width) × 1.2m (Depth), assuming 25% mortar and voids.

    *   **Gross Volume:** $10m \times 1m \times 1.2m = 12 m^3$
    *   **Net Volume of Stone:** $12 m^3 \times (100\% - 25\%) = 12 m^3 \times 0.75 = 9 m^3$
    *   **Volume of Mortar:** $12 m^3 \times 25\% = 3 m^3$

    If the DAR specifies a mortar mix of 1:6 (Cement:Sand) and provides unit quantities:
    *   Cement: 0.15 $m^3$ per $m^3$ of mortar
    *   Sand: 0.15 $m^3$ per $m^3$ of mortar (before bulking)

    *   **Cement Required:** $3 m^3 \times 0.15 m^3/m^3 = 0.45 m^3$
    *   **Sand Required:** $3 m^3 \times 0.15 m^3/m^3 = 0.45 m^3$ (Note: This is usually expressed as dry volume. Specifications might provide adjustment factors for bulking if working with wet sand quantities).

### 3. Material Quantity Calculation for Brickwork

*   **Definition:** Brickwork is a construction using bricks laid in mortar. It's used for walls, columns, arches, etc.
*   **Units of Measurement:** Cubic Meters ($m^3$)
*   **Calculation Method:**
    1.  **Identify the item of work:** e.g., 230mm thick brick wall in cement mortar 1:6.
    2.  **Obtain dimensions from drawings:** Length, height, and thickness of the wall.
    3.  **Calculate the gross volume:** Volume = Length × Height × Thickness.
        *   **Important:** Ensure consistent units. For a 230mm wall, use 0.23m for thickness.
    4.  **Deduct for openings:**
        *   Calculate the volume of doors, windows, vents, etc.
        *   **Wastage on openings:** A percentage (e.g., 0.5% to 1%) is typically added to the volume of openings to account for the extra cutting and wastage of bricks around openings.
        *   Net Volume of Brickwork = Gross Volume - (Volume of Openings + Wastage on Openings)
    5.  **Calculate quantities of materials:**
        *   **Bricks:**
            *   **DAR Data:** DAR will provide the number of bricks required per cubic meter of brickwork for a given thickness and mortar mix. For example, for a 230mm wall with 10mm mortar joints, it might be around 480-500 bricks per $m^3$.
            *   **Calculation:** Total Bricks = Net Volume of Brickwork × (Number of Bricks per $m^3$)
            *   **Wastage on Bricks:** Add a percentage for general wastage (e.g., 5%). Total Bricks with Wastage = Total Bricks × (1 + Wastage Percentage/100).
        *   **Mortar:**
            *   **Mortar Quantity per $m^3$ of Brickwork (from DAR):** DAR will specify the volume of mortar required per cubic meter of brickwork for a given thickness and mortar mix (e.g., 0.3 $m^3$ of mortar for 1 $m^3$ of 230mm brickwork).
            *   **Calculation:** Total Mortar Volume = Net Volume of Brickwork × (Mortar Volume per $m^3$)
            *   **Cement and Sand:** Convert the total mortar volume into quantities of cement and sand based on the specified mix ratio (e.g., 1:6) and DAR data for unit quantities of cement and sand per cubic meter of mortar (considering bulking for sand).

*   **Example:**
    Calculate the quantities of bricks and mortar for a 230mm thick brick wall, 10m long and 3m high, with a door opening of 1m × 2m. Assume DAR data: 490 bricks/ $m^3$ of 230mm brickwork, 0.3 $m^3$ of mortar/ $m^3$ of 230mm brickwork, mortar mix 1:6, and 5% wastage for bricks.

    *   **Gross Volume:** $10m \times 3m \times 0.23m = 6.9 m^3$
    *   **Volume of Door Opening:** $1m \times 2m \times 0.23m = 0.46 m^3$
    *   **Wastage on Opening (1%):** $0.46 m^3 \times 1\% = 0.0046 m^3$
    *   **Net Volume of Brickwork:** $6.9 m^3 - (0.46 m^3 + 0.0046 m^3) = 6.4354 m^3$

    *   **Total Bricks (excluding wastage):** $6.4354 m^3 \times 490 \text{ bricks/m}^3 = 3153.35 \text{ bricks}$
    *   **Total Bricks (including 5% wastage):** $3153.35 \text{ bricks} \times 1.05 = 3311 \text{ bricks}$ (round up)

    *   **Total Mortar Volume:** $6.4354 m^3 \times 0.3 m^3/\text{m}^3 = 1.9306 m^3$

    If DAR data for 1:6 mortar is: Cement: 0.15 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of mortar, Sand: 0.15 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of mortar (dry volume).

    *   **Cement Required:** $1.9306 m^3 \times 0.15 m^3/m^3 = 0.2896 m^3$
    *   **Sand Required:** $1.9306 m^3 \times 0.15 m^3/m^3 = 0.2896 m^3$

### 4. Material Quantity Calculation for Concrete Work

*   **Definition:** Concrete is a composite material composed of aggregate (gravel and sand), cement, and water, which hardens over time. Used for foundations, slabs, beams, columns, etc.
*   **Units of Measurement:** Cubic Meters ($m^3$)
*   **Calculation Method:**
    1.  **Identify the item of work:** e.g., PCC (Plain Cement Concrete) for foundation, RCC (Reinforced Cement Concrete) for slab.
    2.  **Obtain dimensions from drawings:** Length, width, and depth (or height) of the concrete element.
    3.  **Calculate the gross volume:** Volume = Length × Width × Depth.
    4.  **Deduct for embedded items:**
        *   For RCC, deduct the volume of embedded steel reinforcement bars. This is usually calculated separately and then a wastage factor is applied.
        *   Deduct for any sleeves, pipes, or other embedded items if significant.
    5.  **Convert to wet volume for material calculation:**
        *   **Bulking Factor:** Due to the voids filled by cement paste, the volume of dry concrete materials (cement, sand, aggregate) is less than the volume of wet concrete. A bulking factor (e.g., 1.54 for M20 grade concrete) is applied.
        *   **Wet Volume = Dry Volume × Bulking Factor** (Or, more commonly, you calculate the dry volume of materials needed for a unit volume of wet concrete).
    6.  **Calculate quantities of materials (based on mix ratio and DAR data):**
        *   **DAR Data:** DAR will provide the proportion of cement, sand, and aggregate by volume for a specific concrete mix (e.g., M20 is 1:1.5:3 cement:sand:aggregate). It will also provide the *dry volume of materials required per cubic meter of wet concrete*. For example, for M20, it might be:
            *   Cement: 7.5 bags/ $m^3$ of wet concrete (or its equivalent in $m^3$)
            *   Sand: 0.45 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of wet concrete
            *   Aggregate: 0.90 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of wet concrete
        *   **Cement:**
            *   Calculate the total wet volume of concrete.
            *   Total Cement = Total Wet Volume × (Cement quantity per $m^3$ of wet concrete). Convert bags to $m^3$ if needed (e.g., 1 bag cement = 0.0347 $m^3$ or 50 $kg/0.0347 m^3$ for 50kg bag). Add wastage (e.g., 2-5%).
        *   **Sand:**
            *   Total Sand = Total Wet Volume × (Sand quantity per $m^3$ of wet concrete). Add wastage (e.g., 2-5%).
        *   **Aggregate:**
            *   Total Aggregate = Total Wet Volume × (Aggregate quantity per $m^3$ of wet concrete). Add wastage (e.g., 2-5%).
        *   **Water:** Water required is usually specified per bag of cement or as a ratio (water-cement ratio).

*   **Example:**
    Calculate the quantities of cement, sand, and aggregate for an RCC slab of 8m × 6m × 0.15m (thickness), using M20 grade concrete. Assume DAR data for M20:
    *   Cement: 7.5 bags/ $m^3$ of wet concrete
    *   Sand: 0.45 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of wet concrete
    *   Aggregate: 0.90 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of wet concrete
    *   Wastage for cement, sand, aggregate: 5%

    *   **Wet Volume of Concrete:** $8m \times 6m \times 0.15m = 7.2 m^3$

    *   **Cement Required (excluding wastage):** $7.2 m^3 \times 7.5 \text{ bags/m}^3 = 54 \text{ bags}$
    *   **Cement Required (including 5% wastage):** $54 \text{ bags} \times 1.05 = 56.7 \text{ bags}$ (round up to 57 bags)

    *   **Sand Required (excluding wastage):** $7.2 m^3 \times 0.45 m^3/m^3 = 3.24 m^3$
    *   **Sand Required (including 5% wastage):** $3.24 m^3 \times 1.05 = 3.402 m^3$

    *   **Aggregate Required (excluding wastage):** $7.2 m^3 \times 0.90 m^3/m^3 = 6.48 m^3$
    *   **Aggregate Required (including 5% wastage):** $6.48 m^3 \times 1.05 = 6.804 m^3$

*   **Steel Reinforcement:**
    *   Calculate the total length of steel bars of different diameters required from structural drawings.
    *   Convert lengths to weights using the formula: Weight (kg) = (Diameter (mm) / 1000)$^2$ × Length (m) × 7850 (density of steel).
    *   Add wastage (e.g., 5-10%) for cutting and bending.

### 5. Material Quantity Calculation for Plastering

*   **Definition:** Plastering is the application of a layer of mortar to surfaces like walls and ceilings to provide a smooth finish, protection, and decoration.
*   **Units of Measurement:** Square Meters ($m^2$)
*   **Calculation Method:**
    1.  **Identify the item of work:** e.g., 12mm cement plaster in 1:4 mortar for internal walls.
    2.  **Calculate the surface area of walls and ceilings:**
        *   **Walls:** Calculate the net area of walls (Gross Wall Area - Area of Openings).
            *   Gross Wall Area = (Length of walls + Length of corners) × Height of walls.
            *   Area of Openings = Sum of areas of doors, windows, etc.
        *   **Ceilings:** Area of ceilings = Length × Width of the room.
        *   **Floors:** For floor finishes (not typically plastering, but similar area calculation).
    3.  **Deduct for openings:** For plastered surfaces (like walls), openings (doors, windows) are generally **not deducted** because plaster is usually applied to the reveals and soffits of these openings, and the slight reduction in wall area is compensated by this. However, some specifications might require deduction.
    4.  **Calculate quantities of materials:**
        *   **Plaster Thickness:** This is crucial for determining the mortar volume.
        *   **Mortar Volume:** Mortar Volume = Total Plastered Area × Plaster Thickness.
        *   **Cement and Sand:** Convert the mortar volume into quantities of cement and sand based on the specified mix ratio and DAR data for unit quantities of cement and sand per cubic meter of mortar (considering bulking).
        *   **Wastage:** Add a percentage for wastage (e.g., 5-10%).

*   **Example:**
    Calculate the quantity of 12mm cement plaster in 1:5 mortar for the internal walls of a room with dimensions 4m (Length) × 3m (Width) × 3m (Height). It has one door opening of 1m × 2m and two window openings of 1.5m × 1m each. Assume DAR data for 1:5 mortar: Cement: 0.11 $m^3$/ $m^2$ of plaster, Sand: 0.11 $m^3$/ $m^2$ of plaster for 12mm thickness. Wastage: 5%.

    *   **Perimeter of the room:** $2 \times (4m + 3m) = 14m$
    *   **Gross Area of Walls:** $14m \times 3m = 42 m^2$
    *   **Area of Door Opening:** $1m \times 2m = 2 m^2$
    *   **Area of Window Openings:** $2 \times (1.5m \times 1m) = 3 m^2$
    *   **Total Area of Openings:** $2 m^2 + 3 m^2 = 5 m^2$

    *   **Net Plastered Area of Walls (if deductions are made - generally not for plaster):** $42 m^2 - 5 m^2 = 37 m^2$.
        *   **However, typically for plastering, we calculate the gross area and then add for reveals/soffits, or simply use the gross area assuming the additions balance deductions.** Let's use the gross area for simplicity as is common practice unless specified otherwise.
        *   **Plastered Area:** $42 m^2$

    *   **Mortar Volume:** $42 m^2 \times 0.012m \text{ (12mm)} = 0.504 m^3$

    *   **Cement Required (excluding wastage):** $0.504 m^3 \times (0.11 m^3 \text{ cement} / m^2 \text{ plaster}) \times (1/0.012m \text{ (for mortar conversion)}) \approx 4.58 m^3$? **This approach is incorrect.** The DAR data should be for mortar volume per unit area or directly for cement/sand per unit area for a given thickness.

    *   **Let's use DAR data that directly relates to area and thickness:**
        *   DAR data for 12mm plaster in 1:5 mortar:
            *   Cement: 4.6 kg/ $m^2$
            *   Sand: 0.014 $m^3$/ $m^2$
            *   Wastage: 5%

    *   **Cement Required (excluding wastage):** $42 m^2 \times 4.6 \text{ kg/m}^2 = 193.2 \text{ kg}$
    *   **Cement Required (including 5% wastage):** $193.2 \text{ kg} \times 1.05 = 202.86 \text{ kg}$

    *   **Sand Required (excluding wastage):** $42 m^2 \times 0.014 m^3/m^2 = 0.588 m^3$
    *   **Sand Required (including 5% wastage):** $0.588 m^3 \times 1.05 = 0.6174 m^3$

### 6. Practice Questions

**Question 1:**
Calculate the quantity of rubble masonry required for a retaining wall 8m long, 1.5m high, and 0.5m thick. Assume 30% mortar and voids. If DAR specifies 0.2 $m^3$ of mortar per $m^3$ of rubble masonry (gross volume), and the mortar mix is 1:5, calculate the cement and sand needed.

**Answer 1:**
*   Gross Volume = $8m \times 1.5m \times 0.5m = 6 m^3$
*   Net Volume of Stone = $6 m^3 \times (100\% - 30\%) = 6 m^3 \times 0.70 = 4.2 m^3$
*   Volume of Mortar = $6 m^3 \times 30\% = 1.8 m^3$

If DAR data for 1:5 mortar is: Cement: 0.18 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of mortar, Sand: 0.18 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of mortar (dry volume).
*   Cement Required = $1.8 m^3 \times 0.18 m^3/m^3 = 0.324 m^3$
*   Sand Required = $1.8 m^3 \times 0.18 m^3/m^3 = 0.324 m^3$

**Question 2:**
A 230mm thick brick wall is 15m long and 4m high. It has two window openings of 1.2m × 1.5m each. Calculate the quantity of bricks and mortar (1:6 mix) required. Assume DAR data: 485 bricks/ $m^3$ of 230mm brickwork, 0.32 $m^3$ of mortar/ $m^3$ of 230mm brickwork, and 5% wastage for bricks.

**Answer 2:**
*   Gross Volume = $15m \times 4m \times 0.23m = 13.8 m^3$
*   Area of one window = $1.2m \times 1.5m = 1.8 m^2$
*   Volume of one window opening (230mm thick wall) = $1.8 m^2 \times 0.23m = 0.414 m^3$
*   Total Volume of Openings = $2 \times 0.414 m^3 = 0.828 m^3$
*   Wastage on Openings (0.5%) = $0.828 m^3 \times 0.005 = 0.00414 m^3$
*   Net Volume of Brickwork = $13.8 m^3 - (0.828 m^3 + 0.00414 m^3) = 12.96786 m^3$

*   Total Bricks (excluding wastage) = $12.96786 m^3 \times 485 \text{ bricks/m}^3 = 6290.7 \text{ bricks}$
*   Total Bricks (including 5% wastage) = $6290.7 \text{ bricks} \times 1.05 = 6605.2 \text{ bricks}$ (round up to 6606 bricks)

*   Total Mortar Volume = $12.96786 m^3 \times 0.32 m^3/m^3 = 4.15 m^3$

If DAR data for 1:6 mortar is: Cement: 0.15 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of mortar, Sand: 0.15 $m^3$/ $m^3$ of mortar (dry volume).
*   Cement Required = $4.15 m^3 \times 0.15 m^3/m^3 = 0.6225 m^3$
*   Sand Required = $4.15 m^3 \times 0.15 m^3/m^3 = 0.6225 m^3$

**Question 3:**
Calculate the quantities of cement, sand, and aggregate for an RCC footing (PCC base of footing) of size 2m × 1.5m × 0.1m, using M15 grade concrete. Assume DAR data for M15: Cement: 6.5 bags/ $m^3$, Sand: 0.5 $m^3$/ $m^3$, Aggregate: 1.0 $m^3$/ $m^3$. Wastage: 5% for all materials.

**Answer 3:**
*   Wet Volume of Concrete = $2m \times 1.5m \times 0.1m = 0.3 m^3$

*   Cement Required (excluding wastage) = $0.3 m^3 \times 6.5 \text{ bags/m}^3 = 1.95 \text{ bags}$
*   Cement Required (including 5% wastage) = $1.95 \text{ bags} \times 1.05 = 2.0475 \text{ bags}$ (round up to 3 bags, as typically purchased in full bags)

*   Sand Required (excluding wastage) = $0.3 m^3 \times 0.5 m^3/m^3 = 0.15 m^3$
*   Sand Required (including 5% wastage) = $0.15 m^3 \times 1.05 = 0.1575 m^3$

*   Aggregate Required (excluding wastage) = $0.3 m^3 \times 1.0 m^3/m^3 = 0.3 m^3$
*   Aggregate Required (including 5% wastage) = $0.3 m^3 \times 1.05 = 0.315 m^3$

**Question 4:**
Calculate the quantity of 15mm thick cement plaster in 1:4 mortar for the ceiling of a room measuring 5m × 4m. Assume DAR data for 15mm plaster in 1:4 mortar: Cement: 6.0 kg/ $m^2$, Sand: 0.017 $m^3$/ $m^2$. Wastage: 5%.

**Answer 4:**
*   Area of Ceiling = $5m \times 4m = 20 m^2$

*   Cement Required (excluding wastage) = $20 m^2 \times 6.0 \text{ kg/m}^2 = 120 \text{ kg}$
*   Cement Required (including 5% wastage) = $120 \text{ kg} \times 1.05 = 126 \text{ kg}$

*   Sand Required (excluding wastage) = $20 m^2 \times 0.017 m^3/m^2 = 0.34 m^3$
*   Sand Required (including 5% wastage) = $0.34 m^3 \times 1.05 = 0.357 m^3$

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Consistency in Units:** Always ensure all dimensions are in the same units (meters for length, width, depth; square meters for area; cubic meters for volume) before calculation.
*   **DAR is Key:** The accuracy of your calculations heavily depends on the specific data provided in the DAR (or relevant schedules of rates and specifications). Pay close attention to the unit quantities and wastage factors.
*   **Wastage Factors:** Understand the purpose of wastage factors and apply them as specified. Different materials have different wastage percentages.
*   **Deductions for Openings:** Be mindful of whether to deduct openings and any specific instructions regarding wastage on openings for different items of work (e.g., generally no deduction for plastering, but for brickwork, yes).
*   **Bulking of Materials:** Remember that cement and sand bulks when mixed with water. DAR data usually accounts for this by providing quantities of dry materials needed for a unit volume of wet concrete or mortar.
*   **Accuracy:** Double-check your calculations to avoid errors, as these can lead to significant cost overruns or shortages of materials.
*   **Rounding:** Round up quantities to practical purchasing units (e.g., bags of cement, numbers of bricks).
