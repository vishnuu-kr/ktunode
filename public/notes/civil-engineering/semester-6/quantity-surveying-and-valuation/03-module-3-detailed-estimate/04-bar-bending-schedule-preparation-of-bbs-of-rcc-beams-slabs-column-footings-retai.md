## Topic: Bar Bending Schedule (BBS)

### 1. Introduction to Bar Bending Schedule (BBS)

#### 1.1. What is a Bar Bending Schedule?

*   A Bar Bending Schedule (BBS) is a detailed list of all reinforcement bars required for a structural element.
*   It specifies the **diameter**, **shape**, **length**, **number of pieces**, and **weight** of each type of reinforcement bar.
*   It serves as a crucial document for ordering, cutting, bending, and fixing reinforcement steel in concrete structures.
*   It ensures that the correct amount and type of steel are used, minimizing wastage and ensuring structural integrity.

#### 1.2. Importance of BBS in Quantity Surveying

*   **Accurate Material Estimation:** Provides a precise quantity of steel required, forming the basis for procurement.
*   **Cost Control:** Helps in controlling the cost of steel by minimizing wastage during cutting and bending.
*   **Efficient Labour Deployment:** Guides the bar benders and fixers, improving efficiency and reducing errors.
*   **Quality Assurance:** Ensures that the specified reinforcement is used as per the structural drawings.
*   **Billing and Payment:** Forms a basis for interim and final billing for reinforcement work.
*   **Reduced Wastage:** Proper planning in BBS minimizes off-cuts and scrap material.

#### 1.3. Key Information from Structural Drawings for BBS

To prepare a BBS, you need to thoroughly understand the structural drawings, which typically include:

*   **Reinforcement Drawings:** These are the primary source, showing the placement, size, spacing, and cover of all reinforcement bars.
*   **Sectional Views:** Provide detailed information about bar arrangements within the structural element.
*   **Schedules of Reinforcement:** Sometimes provided by the designer, listing bar sizes, lengths, and number of pieces.
*   **Concrete Cover:** The minimum distance between the reinforcement and the outer surface of the concrete.
*   **Lap Lengths:** The required length for overlapping reinforcing bars to ensure continuity of stress.
*   **Development Lengths:** The length of bar required to ensure proper bond between concrete and steel.
*   **Hook Lengths:** Standard lengths for hooks at the ends of bars for anchorage.
*   **Cutting Lengths:** The total length of steel bar required for a particular shape, considering bends.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions Related to BBS

*   **Reinforcement Bar (Rebar):** Steel bars used to strengthen concrete structures.
*   **Diameter of Bar:** The thickness of the rebar (e.g., 8mm, 12mm, 16mm).
*   **Shape of Bar:** The configuration of the rebar (e.g., straight, bent-up, L-shaped, stirrup).
*   **Overall Length:** The total length of a piece of rebar before any bending or cutting.
*   **Cutting Length:** The length of rebar required to be cut from a standard stock length to form a specific shape, considering bends.
*   **Number of Pieces:** The quantity of bars of a specific shape and diameter.
*   **Total Length:** The sum of the cutting lengths of all pieces of a particular bar type (Number of Pieces x Cutting Length).
*   **Weight of Bar:** The weight of steel per unit length, calculated based on its diameter and density of steel (typically 7850 kg/m³).
*   **Total Weight:** The total weight of steel for a particular bar type (Total Length x Weight per meter).
*   **Concrete Cover:** The protective layer of concrete around the reinforcement, crucial for durability and fire resistance.
*   **Bends:** Deviations in the straightness of a bar, often for load transfer and anchorage.
*   **Hooks:** Shaped ends of bars used for anchorage and to prevent slippage. Standard hooks are typically 90°, 135°, or 180°.
*   **Lap:** The length over which two reinforcing bars are overlapped to maintain continuity of stress.
*   **Development Length:** The length of embedment required to develop the full tensile strength of a bar in concrete.
*   **Standard Stock Length:** The length in which reinforcing bars are manufactured and supplied (commonly 12 meters).
*   **Wastage:** The amount of steel lost during cutting and bending.

### 3. Preparation of BBS for Various RCC Elements

The core of BBS preparation involves calculating the cutting length for each bar based on its shape and the dimensions provided in the drawings. We'll use standard formulas and considerations for bends.

**General Formula for Cutting Length:**

**Cutting Length = Straight Length + Length of Bends**

**Considerations for Bends:**

*   **90° Bend:** Add **2 times the radius of bend** to the straight length. The radius of bend is typically 4 times the diameter of the bar for bars up to 20mm, and 6 times for larger diameters.
*   **135° Bend:** Add **3 times the radius of bend** to the straight length.
*   **180° Bend (U-shape):** Add **4 times the radius of bend** to the straight length.
*   **Hooks:** Add the length of the hook as per IS code standards.

**Standard Hook Lengths (as per IS 2502):**

*   **90° Hook:** 8 times the diameter of the bar.
*   **135° Hook:** 12 times the diameter of the bar.

**Note on Bending Allowances:** While IS 2502 provides guidance, in practice, it's common to use the following simplified allowances for bends:

*   **90° Bend:** Add **8 times the diameter of the bar** to the straight length.
*   **135° Bend:** Add **12 times the diameter of the bar** to the straight length.
*   **180° Bend:** Add **16 times the diameter of the bar** to the straight length.

**We will use these simplified allowances for the examples below as they are commonly adopted in the industry.**

#### 3.1. Bar Bending Schedule for RCC Beams

**Assumptions:**

*   Beam dimensions are provided.
*   Reinforcement details (main bars, stirrups, bent-up bars) are shown in the drawings.
*   Concrete cover is specified.
*   Standard lap and development lengths are to be considered if applicable.

**Example: A Simply Supported Beam**

**Beam Details:**

*   Span (L): 4 meters
*   Width (b): 250 mm
*   Depth (D): 400 mm
*   Concrete Cover: 25 mm
*   Main Reinforcement: 3 bars of 16mm diameter (top and bottom)
*   Stirrups: 8mm diameter, at 150 mm c/c spacing.
*   Bent-up bars: 2 bars of 16mm diameter at 45° at the supports.

**Calculations for Main Bars (Bottom):**

*   **Number of bars:** 3
*   **Diameter:** 16mm
*   **Clear Span:** 4000 mm
*   **Add bearing at both ends:** Let's assume 200 mm at each end.
*   **Development Length (LD):** For 16mm bar in M25 concrete, approx. 40 * 16 = 640 mm (refer to IS 456 for accurate calculation).
*   **Total length per bar:** Clear Span + 2 * Bearing + 2 * Development Length = 4000 + 2 * 200 + 2 * 640 = 4000 + 400 + 1280 = 5680 mm = 5.68 meters.
*   **Weight per meter for 16mm bar:** (16²/162) kg/m = 1.58 kg/m (approx.)
*   **Total weight:** 3 bars * 5.68 m/bar * 1.58 kg/m = 26.88 kg.

**Calculations for Main Bars (Top - for crack control/anchorage):**

*   **Number of bars:** 3
*   **Diameter:** 16mm
*   **Assume they extend for development length at supports.**
*   **Total length per bar:** 2 * Development Length = 2 * 640 = 1280 mm = 1.28 meters.
*   **Weight per meter:** 1.58 kg/m
*   **Total weight:** 3 bars * 1.28 m/bar * 1.58 kg/m = 6.05 kg.

**Calculations for Bent-up Bars:**

*   **Number of bars:** 2
*   **Diameter:** 16mm
*   **Length calculation:** The bent-up bars are typically bent upwards from the bottom reinforcement to resist shear. They extend for the development length at one end and are bent at 45°.
*   **Straight portion (bottom):** Assume they extend for development length. So, 640 mm.
*   **Bent portion:** The length of the bent portion at 45° can be approximated. Let's say the bend starts at a distance 'x' from the support.
    *   The diagonal length of the bent portion is typically 1.414 * (length of the bent segment).
    *   A common way to estimate is to consider the beam depth and the position of bending.
    *   **Simplified calculation:** Consider the straight portion and the additional length due to bend and hook.
        *   Let's assume the bar is bent at 45° and extends to the top for development.
        *   Straight bottom length: Let's say 1 meter from the support.
        *   Bent portion: The bar is bent upwards. The increase in length due to a 45° bend is approximately 1.414 * (some length). A more practical way is to estimate the total length.
        *   **Alternative approach for bent-up bars:** Consider the length of the bottom bar segment, the angled segment, and the top segment for development.
            *   Assume the bend starts 0.25 * L from the support = 0.25 * 4000 = 1000 mm.
            *   The bar is bent up. Let's assume it goes up to the top for anchorage.
            *   Bottom straight length: 1000 mm.
            *   Angled portion: This depends on the height it goes up. If it goes up to a height 'h', the length is sqrt(h² + h²)=1.414h.
            *   **A common way to calculate:**
                *   Length of the bottom segment before bend: e.g., (Clear Span/4) - Cover - Stirrup Dia.
                *   Length of the bent segment: e.g., (Beam Depth - Top Cover - Bottom Cover - Stirrup Dia) / sin(45°).
                *   Length of the top segment for development.
            *   **Let's use a simpler, common approximation for practice:** Assume the bent-up bar runs the span, is bent at 45 degrees, and continues for development.
                *   Straight length at bottom: Let's say the bar starts at the support and bends at x from the support.
                *   The total length will be approximately the span + addition for bend and development.
                *   **Common practice for bent-up bars:** The length is usually calculated as Span + 2 * (approx. 0.4 * Depth) + Development Length.
                *   Let's assume the bent-up bars are needed to resist shear. They typically extend from the bottom at the support to the top at a distance from the support.
                *   **Let's consider a simplified bent-up bar:** It starts from one end, goes up at 45 degrees, and continues to the other end.
                *   Bottom straight length: Let's say 500 mm from the support.
                *   Angled length: Assume it goes up from a depth of 400-25=375mm to the top. The horizontal projection is say, 500 mm. The vertical rise is 375 mm. The angled length = sqrt(500² + 375²) = 625 mm.
                *   Top straight length for development: 640 mm.
                *   Total length per bent-up bar: 500 + 625 + 640 = 1765 mm.
                *   **This is complex and highly dependent on drawing details.**
                *   **For exam purposes, if not explicitly detailed, assume:** Span length + addition for 45° bend + development length. The addition for a 45° bend can be approximated.
                *   **Practical approach for bent-up bars:** Consider the total length as approximately the span length plus an allowance for the bend and anchorage. For simplicity, if the drawing doesn't specify, we might estimate it based on common practice.
                *   Let's assume the bent-up bars extend across the entire span, with bends at both ends.
                *   Length = Span + 2 * (135° hook allowance) + 2 * (45° bend allowance). This is not accurate.
                *   **Let's use a common simplified method:** Straight length + 1.414 * (depth of bend) for the angled part.
                *   A simpler approach often used in practice for bent-up bars is to take the full span length and add allowances for the bends.
                *   **Let's re-calculate assuming the bent-up bars are placed as per typical shear reinforcement:** They originate from the bottom reinforcement, bend upwards, and anchor into the top.
                *   Length of bottom segment: Assume it runs from the support center to the point of bend. Let's say 1 meter.
                *   Length of angled segment: Assume it goes up to the top of the beam. Vertical rise = 400 - 25 (bottom cover) - 25 (top cover) = 350 mm. If the horizontal projection is also 350 mm, the angled length = sqrt(350² + 350²) = 495 mm.
                *   Length of top segment for development: 640 mm.
                *   Total length per bent-up bar = 1000 + 495 + 640 = 2135 mm.
                *   **This is still an approximation.** The exact drawing is crucial.
                *   **Let's try another common practice for bent-up bars:** Length = (Clear Span/2) + (Depth - Covers) / sin(45°) + Development Length.
                    *   Clear Span/2 = 4000/2 = 2000 mm.
                    *   (Depth - Covers) = 400 - 25 - 25 = 350 mm.
                    *   (Depth - Covers) / sin(45°) = 350 / 0.707 = 495 mm.
                    *   Development Length = 640 mm.
                    *   Total length per bent-up bar = 2000 + 495 + 640 = 3135 mm.
                *   **This seems more plausible for bars spanning half the beam and bending up.**
                *   Let's assume 2 bent-up bars are placed at each end. So, 4 bent-up bars in total for the beam.
                *   Length per bent-up bar = 3135 mm = 3.135 m.
                *   Weight per meter (16mm): 1.58 kg/m.
                *   Total weight: 4 bars * 3.135 m/bar * 1.58 kg/m = 19.8 kg.

**Calculations for Stirrups:**

*   **Number of Stirrups:**
    *   Spacing (s): 150 mm c/c
    *   Number of stirrups = (Total Length / Spacing) + 1 = (4000 / 150) + 1 = 26.67 + 1 ≈ 28 stirrups.
    *   **However, stirrups are usually provided in the end zones more closely.** Let's assume for simplicity they are uniformly spaced.
    *   A more accurate count would be to determine the length requiring closely spaced stirrups.
    *   **Standard practice:** Uniform spacing over the clear span, or closer spacing at ends and wider spacing in the middle. Let's assume uniform spacing for this example.
    *   Number of stirrups = (Clear Span / Spacing) + 1 = (4000 / 150) + 1 = 27.67 + 1 = 28.67. Round up to 29 stirrups.
*   **Diameter:** 8mm
*   **Weight per meter for 8mm bar:** (8²/162) kg/m = 0.395 kg/m (approx.)
*   **Cutting length per stirrup:**
    *   Beam width = 250 mm, Beam depth = 400 mm.
    *   Consider concrete cover = 25 mm.
    *   Inner dimensions = (250 - 2*25) = 200 mm (width) and (400 - 2*25) = 350 mm (depth).
    *   Stirrup shape: A rectangle with hooks at the ends.
    *   Perimeter = 2 * (Inner Width + Inner Depth) + 2 * (135° bend allowance).
    *   Inner width = 250 - 2 * 25 = 200 mm.
    *   Inner depth = 400 - 2 * 25 = 350 mm.
    *   The stirrup hooks are usually 135°.
    *   Cutting Length = 2 * (200) + 2 * (350) + 2 * (135° bend allowance).
    *   Using the simplified allowance: 2 * (200) + 2 * (350) + 2 * (12 * 8mm) = 400 + 700 + 2 * 96 = 1100 + 192 = 1292 mm = 1.292 m.
    *   **Let's recheck the bend allowance for stirrups.** Typically, stirrups have a rectangular shape with hooks at the ends.
    *   **Correct calculation for stirrup cutting length:**
        *   Outer dimensions: 250 mm x 400 mm.
        *   Concrete cover: 25 mm.
        *   Inner dimensions: (250 - 2*25) = 200 mm; (400 - 2*25) = 350 mm.
        *   The length of the stirrup is the perimeter of the inner rectangle plus the length of the hooks.
        *   Length = 2 * (inner width) + 2 * (inner depth) + 2 * (hook length).
        *   Inner width = 200 mm. Inner depth = 350 mm.
        *   The stirrup is bent at the corners. These are 90° bends if considering the inner perimeter.
        *   Let's re-evaluate the cutting length based on outer dimensions and allowances.
        *   Length = 2 * (Outer Width - 2 * Cover - 2 * Bend Allowance) + 2 * (Outer Depth - 2 * Cover - 2 * Bend Allowance) + 2 * Hook Length. This is confusing.
        *   **Correct approach for stirrups:** Consider the outer dimensions and subtract cover. Or, consider inner dimensions and add hook lengths.
        *   **Let's use the inner dimensions:**
            *   Inner width = 250 - 2*25 = 200 mm
            *   Inner depth = 400 - 2*25 = 350 mm
            *   The length of the shape is the perimeter. The bends are at the corners.
            *   Length of one side along inner width = 200 mm.
            *   Length of one side along inner depth = 350 mm.
            *   At each corner, there's a 90° bend. Using the simplified allowance of 8 * diameter:
            *   Cutting Length = 2 * (Inner Width) + 2 * (Inner Depth) + 2 * (90° Bend Allowance) + 2 * (Hook Length)
            *   The hooks are usually at the ends of the stirrup. So, one hook at each end of the long side.
            *   Let's visualize: The stirrup forms a rectangle with two U-shaped ends (hooks).
            *   Length = (Inner Width) + (Inner Depth) + (Inner Width) + (Inner Depth) + Hooks.
            *   Consider one side: 200 mm (straight) + 350 mm (straight) + 200 mm (straight) + 350 mm (straight).
            *   The corners are bent. If we take the full inner perimeter, we are not accounting for the bends properly.
            *   **Standard calculation for stirrups:**
                *   Length = 2 * (Beam Width - 2*Cover) + 2 * (Beam Depth - 2*Cover) + 2 * (Hook Length) - 8 * Diameter (for 90° bends).
                *   This is also confusing. Let's use the IS code method.
                *   **From IS 2502:** For a rectangular stirrup, the cutting length = 2 * (Length of stirrup) + 2 * (Width of stirrup) + 2 * (Hook Length).
                *   Length of stirrup = Outer length - 2*Cover - 2*Bend allowance (or hook length).
                *   Let's use the **simplest and most common approach for stirrups:**
                    *   Outer dimensions: 250 mm x 400 mm.
                    *   Length of bar to form the rectangle = 2 * (250) + 2 * (400) = 500 + 800 = 1300 mm.
                    *   Now, consider the cover. The bars are placed inside.
                    *   Length = 2 * (Outer Width) + 2 * (Outer Depth) - 8 * Diameter (for 4 corners) + 2 * Hook Length.
                    *   Let's consider the overall shape and the bends.
                    *   **A common way to calculate stirrup length:**
                        *   Length = Perimeter of the center line of the bar.
                        *   Center line dimensions = (250 - 2*25 - 2*4) = 214 mm and (400 - 2*25 - 2*4) = 354 mm.
                        *   Perimeter = 2 * (214 + 354) = 2 * 568 = 1136 mm. This doesn't account for hooks.
                    *   **Let's use the practical approach:**
                        *   Length = 2 * (Outer Width) + 2 * (Outer Depth) - 2 * (Bend Deduction) + 2 * Hook Length.
                        *   Length = 2 * (250) + 2 * (400) - 2 * (8*Diameter) + 2 * (8*Diameter) = 2 * 250 + 2 * 400 = 1300 mm. This is incorrect as it ignores the bend.
                        *   **Correct stirrup cutting length calculation:**
                            *   Length = (2 * (Width - 2*Cover)) + (2 * (Depth - 2*Cover)) + 2 * (Hook Length) + 2 * (90° bend allowance).
                            *   Inner width = 250 - 2*25 = 200 mm.
                            *   Inner depth = 400 - 2*25 = 350 mm.
                            *   Let's assume the hooks are at the ends of the depth dimension.
                            *   Length = 2 * (Inner Width) + 2 * (Inner Depth) + 2 * (Hook Length).
                            *   Inner Width = 200 mm. Inner Depth = 350 mm.
                            *   Hooks are 135° bends at the ends. Let's use the allowance of 12*Diameter for each hook.
                            *   Cutting Length = 2 * (200) + 2 * (350) + 2 * (12 * 8mm) = 400 + 700 + 2 * 96 = 1100 + 192 = 1292 mm.
                            *   This assumes the bends are taken into account by the hook length.
                            *   **Let's use the IS 2502 rule for bends within the perimeter:**
                                *   Length = 2 * (Width - 2*Cover) + 2 * (Depth - 2*Cover) + 8 * Diameter (for 4 bends).
                                *   Let's reconsider the stirrup shape. It's a rectangle with 135° hooks at the ends.
                                *   Cutting Length = (Outer Width) + (Outer Depth) + (Outer Width) + (Outer Depth) - 8 * Diameter (for 4 x 90° bends) + 2 * Hook Length.
                                *   **Correct Simplified Calculation:**
                                    *   Length = 2 * (Beam Width - 2*Cover) + 2 * (Beam Depth - 2*Cover) + 2 * (Hook Length).
                                    *   Length = 2 * (250 - 2*25) + 2 * (400 - 2*25) + 2 * (12 * 8).
                                    *   Length = 2 * (200) + 2 * (350) + 2 * (96) = 400 + 700 + 192 = 1292 mm.
                                    *   This is the correct calculation for a stirrup with 135° hooks.
                                *   Cutting Length per stirrup = 1.292 m.
*   **Total weight of stirrups:** 29 stirrups * 1.292 m/stirrup * 0.395 kg/m = 14.66 kg.

#### 3.2. Bar Bending Schedule for RCC Slabs

**Assumptions:**

*   Slab dimensions, thickness, and reinforcement details (main bars, distribution bars) are provided.
*   Concrete cover is specified.
*   Development lengths are considered for bars at supports.

**Example: A Simply Supported Slab**

**Slab Details:**

*   Span (Lx): 3 meters (main reinforcement direction)
*   Span (Ly): 4 meters (distribution reinforcement direction)
*   Thickness (D): 150 mm
*   Concrete Cover: 20 mm
*   Main Reinforcement (along Ly): 10mm diameter @ 150 mm c/c
*   Distribution Reinforcement (along Lx): 10mm diameter @ 200 mm c/c

**Calculations for Main Reinforcement (along Ly):**

*   **Diameter:** 10mm
*   **Weight per meter for 10mm bar:** (10²/162) kg/m = 0.617 kg/m (approx.)
*   **Spacing:** 150 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** The main reinforcement runs along the shorter span (Ly = 4 m).
    *   Clear span = 4000 mm.
    *   Development length (LD) for 10mm bar in M20 concrete ≈ 32 * 10 = 320 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Clear Span + 2 * Development Length = 4000 + 2 * 320 = 4000 + 640 = 4640 mm = 4.64 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the Lx span (3 m).
    *   Number of bars = (Lx / Spacing) + 1 = (3000 / 150) + 1 = 20 + 1 = 21 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 21 bars * 4.64 m/bar = 97.44 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 97.44 m * 0.617 kg/m = 60.12 kg.

**Calculations for Distribution Reinforcement (along Lx):**

*   **Diameter:** 10mm
*   **Weight per meter:** 0.617 kg/m
*   **Spacing:** 200 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** The distribution reinforcement runs along the longer span (Lx = 3 m).
    *   Clear span = 3000 mm.
    *   Development length (LD) for 10mm bar ≈ 320 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Clear Span + 2 * Development Length = 3000 + 2 * 320 = 3000 + 640 = 3640 mm = 3.64 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the Ly span (4 m).
    *   Number of bars = (Ly / Spacing) + 1 = (4000 / 200) + 1 = 20 + 1 = 21 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 21 bars * 3.64 m/bar = 76.44 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 76.44 m * 0.617 kg/m = 47.17 kg.

#### 3.3. Bar Bending Schedule for RCC Column Footings

**Assumptions:**

*   Footing dimensions, thickness, and reinforcement details (top and bottom steel) are provided.
*   Concrete cover is specified.
*   Development lengths are critical for anchoring bars into the footing and column.

**Example: A Pad Footing**

**Footing Details:**

*   Footing Size: 1.8m x 1.8m
*   Footing Thickness: 300 mm
*   Concrete Cover: 50 mm (to the bottom and sides), 40 mm (to the top)
*   Bottom Reinforcement: 12mm diameter @ 150 mm c/c in both directions.
*   Top Reinforcement (for crack control or if doweled): 10mm diameter @ 200 mm c/c in both directions.

**Calculations for Bottom Reinforcement (Direction 1):**

*   **Diameter:** 12mm
*   **Weight per meter for 12mm bar:** (12²/162) kg/m = 0.889 kg/m (approx.)
*   **Spacing:** 150 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** The bars run across the footing width.
    *   Clear footing width = 1800 mm.
    *   Development length (LD) for 12mm bar in M20 concrete ≈ 32 * 12 = 384 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Clear Footing Width + 2 * Development Length = 1800 + 2 * 384 = 1800 + 768 = 2568 mm = 2.568 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the footing length.
    *   Number of bars = (Footing Length / Spacing) + 1 = (1800 / 150) + 1 = 12 + 1 = 13 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 13 bars * 2.568 m/bar = 33.384 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 33.384 m * 0.889 kg/m = 29.68 kg.

**Calculations for Bottom Reinforcement (Direction 2):**

*   These are identical to Direction 1 due to the square footing.
*   **Total Length:** 33.384 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 29.68 kg.

**Calculations for Top Reinforcement (Direction 1):**

*   **Diameter:** 10mm
*   **Weight per meter for 10mm bar:** 0.617 kg/m
*   **Spacing:** 200 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** The bars run across the footing width.
    *   Clear footing width = 1800 mm.
    *   Development length (LD) for 10mm bar ≈ 320 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Clear Footing Width + 2 * Development Length = 1800 + 2 * 320 = 1800 + 640 = 2440 mm = 2.44 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the footing length.
    *   Number of bars = (Footing Length / Spacing) + 1 = (1800 / 200) + 1 = 9 + 1 = 10 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 10 bars * 2.44 m/bar = 24.4 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 24.4 m * 0.617 kg/m = 15.07 kg.

**Calculations for Top Reinforcement (Direction 2):**

*   These are identical to Direction 1 due to the square footing.
*   **Total Length:** 24.4 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 15.07 kg.

#### 3.4. Bar Bending Schedule for Retaining Walls

**Assumptions:**

*   Retaining wall dimensions, height, base width, and soil pressure details are provided.
*   Reinforcement details for the wall face, heel, toe, and counterforts (if any) are shown.
*   Concrete cover is specified.

**Example: A Cantilever Retaining Wall**

**Retaining Wall Details:**

*   Height of wall above ground (H): 3 meters
*   Base Slab Width (B): 1.8 meters
*   Base Slab Thickness: 200 mm
*   Wall Thickness at Base: 250 mm
*   Wall Thickness at Top: 150 mm
*   Concrete Cover: 30 mm
*   **Vertical Reinforcement (Inner Face - along height):** 12mm diameter @ 200 mm c/c
*   **Horizontal Reinforcement (Inner Face - along length):** 10mm diameter @ 180 mm c/c
*   **Heel Reinforcement (Top - along length):** 12mm diameter @ 180 mm c/c
*   **Heel Reinforcement (Bottom - along width):** 10mm diameter @ 200 mm c/c
*   **Toe Reinforcement (Top - along length):** 12mm diameter @ 180 mm c/c
*   **Toe Reinforcement (Bottom - along width):** 10mm diameter @ 200 mm c/c

**Calculations for Vertical Reinforcement (Inner Face):**

*   **Diameter:** 12mm
*   **Weight per meter:** 0.889 kg/m
*   **Spacing:** 200 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** The bars run the full height of the wall, with development lengths at the base and top.
    *   Height of wall = 3000 mm.
    *   Development length (LD) for 12mm bar ≈ 384 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Wall Height + 2 * Development Length = 3000 + 2 * 384 = 3000 + 768 = 3768 mm = 3.768 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the length of the wall. Assume a 1-meter length of wall for estimation.
    *   Number of bars per meter length = (1000 mm / Spacing) + 1 = (1000 / 200) + 1 = 5 + 1 = 6 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 6 bars * 3.768 m/bar = 22.608 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 22.608 m * 0.889 kg/m = 20.10 kg per meter length of wall.

**Calculations for Horizontal Reinforcement (Inner Face):**

*   **Diameter:** 10mm
*   **Weight per meter:** 0.617 kg/m
*   **Spacing:** 180 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** These bars run across the wall length. Assume a 1-meter length of wall.
    *   Clear length of bar = 1000 mm.
    *   Development length (LD) for 10mm bar ≈ 320 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Clear Length + 2 * Development Length = 1000 + 2 * 320 = 1000 + 640 = 1640 mm = 1.64 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the height of the wall.
    *   Number of bars = (Wall Height / Spacing) + 1 = (3000 / 180) + 1 = 16.67 + 1 ≈ 18 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 18 bars * 1.64 m/bar = 29.52 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 29.52 m * 0.617 kg/m = 18.21 kg per meter length of wall.

**Calculations for Heel Reinforcement (Top - along length):**

*   **Diameter:** 12mm
*   **Weight per meter:** 0.889 kg/m
*   **Spacing:** 180 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** The bars run across the heel width.
    *   Heel width = 1.8 m.
    *   Development length for 12mm bar ≈ 384 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Heel Width + 2 * Development Length = 1800 + 2 * 384 = 2568 mm = 2.568 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the length of the wall.
    *   Number of bars per meter length = (1000 mm / Spacing) + 1 = (1000 / 180) + 1 = 5.56 + 1 ≈ 7 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 7 bars * 2.568 m/bar = 17.976 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 17.976 m * 0.889 kg/m = 16.00 kg per meter length of wall.

**Calculations for Heel Reinforcement (Bottom - along width):**

*   **Diameter:** 10mm
*   **Weight per meter:** 0.617 kg/m
*   **Spacing:** 200 mm c/c
*   **Length of bar:** These bars run across the heel length.
    *   Heel length = 1.8 m.
    *   Development length for 10mm bar ≈ 320 mm.
    *   Length per bar = Heel Length + 2 * Development Length = 1800 + 2 * 320 = 2440 mm = 2.44 m.
*   **Number of bars:** These bars are spaced along the width of the wall.
    *   Number of bars per meter length = (1000 mm / Spacing) + 1 = (1000 / 200) + 1 = 5 + 1 = 6 bars.
*   **Total Length:** 6 bars * 2.44 m/bar = 14.64 m.
*   **Total Weight:** 14.64 m * 0.617 kg/m = 9.03 kg per meter length of wall.

### 4. Standard BBS Format

A typical BBS format includes the following columns:

*   **S.No.:** Serial Number.
*   **Item No.:** Corresponding item number from the Bill of Quantities (BOQ).
*   **Location:** Name of the structural element (e.g., Beam B1, Slab S2, Column C3).
*   **Bar Mark / Designation:** A reference from the structural drawing for a specific bar.
*   **Bar Diameter (mm):** The diameter of the bar.
*   **Shape of Bar:** A sketch or code representing the shape (e.g., Straight, L-shape, U-shape, Stirrup).
*   **No. of Pieces:** The number of bars of that specific shape and diameter.
*   **Length per Piece (m):** The cutting length of a single bar.
*   **Total Length (m):** The sum of lengths for all pieces of that bar (No. of Pieces x Length per Piece).
*   **Weight per Meter (kg/m):** Calculated based on bar diameter (d²/162).
*   **Total Weight (kg):** Total length multiplied by weight per meter.
*   **Remarks:** Any specific instructions or notes.

---

### 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Accuracy is Paramount:** Even small errors in BBS can lead to significant wastage or shortage of steel.
*   **Refer to Drawings Carefully:** Always refer to the structural drawings for detailed reinforcement information.
*   **Understand Bar Shapes:** Familiarize yourself with standard rebar shapes and how to calculate their cutting lengths.
*   **Factor in Concrete Cover:** Always account for the specified concrete cover when calculating effective lengths.
*   **Development and Lap Lengths:** These are crucial for structural integrity and must be calculated as per IS codes.
*   **Wastage Allowance:** While BBS aims to minimize wastage, a small allowance might be added for practical cutting and bending operations. This is usually a percentage of the total weight.
*   **Units Consistency:** Ensure all units (mm, m, kg) are consistent throughout the calculations.
*   **Standardisation:** Follow a consistent method for calculating bend allowances to avoid errors.
*   **Cross-Checking:** It's good practice to cross-check your BBS with the design calculations or a colleague.
*   **Software Usage:** Many software packages are available for generating BBS, which can improve accuracy and speed.

### 6. Practice Questions

1.  Calculate the cutting length of a 12mm diameter stirrup for a rectangular column measuring 400mm x 600mm, with a concrete cover of 30mm. The stirrup has 135° hooks.
2.  A simply supported beam has a clear span of 5 meters. It requires 3 bars of 16mm diameter at the bottom and 2 bars of 16mm diameter as bent-up bars at each end. The development length for 16mm bars is 600mm. Calculate the total weight of steel required for the bottom and bent-up bars, assuming the bent-up bars extend for the full development length at both ends and are bent at 45°.
3.  A slab is 5 meters long and 3 meters wide, with a thickness of 125mm. The main reinforcement (10mm diameter) is provided along the 3-meter span at 180mm c/c, and the distribution reinforcement (8mm diameter) is provided along the 5-meter span at 250mm c/c. Calculate the total weight of steel required for both main and distribution reinforcement, considering a development length of 40 times the bar diameter.
4.  Explain the importance of a Bar Bending Schedule in quantity surveying.
5.  Describe the key information required from structural drawings to prepare a BBS.

### Answers to Practice Questions

1.  **Stirrup Cutting Length:**
    *   Inner Width = 400mm - 2 * 30mm = 340mm
    *   Inner Depth = 600mm - 2 * 30mm = 540mm
    *   Hook Length (135°) = 12 * Diameter = 12 * 12mm = 144mm
    *   Cutting Length = 2 * (Inner Width) + 2 * (Inner Depth) + 2 * (Hook Length)
    *   Cutting Length = 2 * (340mm) + 2 * (540mm) + 2 * (144mm)
    *   Cutting Length = 680mm + 1080mm + 288mm = 2048mm = **2.048 meters**

2.  **Beam Reinforcement Weight:**
    *   **Bottom Bars:**
        *   Diameter: 16mm. Weight per meter = 1.58 kg/m.
        *   Number of bars: 3.
        *   Length per bar = Clear Span + 2 * Development Length = 5000mm + 2 * 600mm = 6200mm = 6.2m.
        *   Total Length = 3 * 6.2m = 18.6m.
        *   Total Weight = 18.6m * 1.58 kg/m = **29.39 kg**.
    *   **Bent-up Bars:**
        *   Diameter: 16mm. Weight per meter = 1.58 kg/m.
        *   Number of bars: 2 at each end = 4 bars.
        *   Length per bar: Assume they run half the span and bend up, anchoring at the top. A simplified estimate for bent-up bars extending full span with bends: Span + 2*(addition for 45° bend) + 2*Development Length.
        *   A more common approximation for shear bars is (Clear Span/2) + (Depth - Covers)/sin(45°) + Development Length. Let's assume the beam depth is 500mm and covers are 30mm.
        *   Approximate Length per bar = (5000/2) + (500 - 2*30)/sin(45°) + 600 = 2500 + (440/0.707) + 600 = 2500 + 622 + 600 = 3722mm = 3.722m.
        *   *If the problem implies the bent-up bars simply replace the straight bars for development at supports, then length = 2 * Development Length = 2 * 600 = 1200mm. This is unlikely for shear. Let's stick with the previous estimation.*
        *   Total Length = 4 * 3.722m = 14.888m.
        *   Total Weight = 14.888m * 1.58 kg/m = **23.53 kg**.
    *   **Total Weight:** 29.39 kg + 23.53 kg = **52.92 kg**.

3.  **Slab Reinforcement Weight:**
    *   **Main Reinforcement (10mm @ 180 c/c along 3m span):**
        *   Diameter: 10mm. Weight per meter = 0.617 kg/m.
        *   Length of bar (along 5m span) = Clear Span (5m) + 2 * Development Length.
        *   Development Length (10mm) = 40 * 10mm = 400mm.
        *   Length per bar = 5000mm + 2 * 400mm = 5800mm = 5.8m.
        *   Number of bars (along 3m span) = (3000mm / 180mm) + 1 = 16.67 + 1 ≈ 18 bars.
        *   Total Length = 18 * 5.8m = 104.4m.
        *   Total Weight = 104.4m * 0.617 kg/m = **64.42 kg**.
    *   **Distribution Reinforcement (8mm @ 250 c/c along 5m span):**
        *   Diameter: 8mm. Weight per meter = 0.395 kg/m.
        *   Length of bar (along 3m span) = Clear Span (3m) + 2 * Development Length.
        *   Development Length (8mm) = 40 * 8mm = 320mm.
        *   Length per bar = 3000mm + 2 * 320mm = 3640mm = 3.64m.
        *   Number of bars (along 5m span) = (5000mm / 250mm) + 1 = 20 + 1 = 21 bars.
        *   Total Length = 21 * 3.64m = 76.44m.
        *   Total Weight = 76.44m * 0.395 kg/m = **30.20 kg**.
    *   **Total Weight:** 64.42 kg + 30.20 kg = **94.62 kg**.

4.  **Importance of BBS:**
    *   **Accurate Material Procurement:** Ensures the right quantity of steel is ordered, preventing shortages or excess.
    *   **Cost Control:** Minimizes wastage during cutting and bending, directly impacting project costs.
    *   **Efficient Labour Management:** Guides bar fixers, improving productivity and reducing errors.
    *   **Quality Assurance:** Verifies that the specified reinforcement is used as per design, essential for structural integrity.
    *   **Basis for Billing:** Used for calculating and verifying the quantity of reinforcement work for payment.

5.  **Key Information from Drawings:**
    *   **Dimensions of structural elements:** Length, width, depth/thickness.
    *   **Reinforcement details:** Bar diameters, spacing, and positions (top, bottom, sides).
    *   **Bar shapes and bending details:** Including hooks, bends, and cuts.
    *   **Concrete cover:** The required distance from the bar to the concrete surface.
    *   **Development lengths and lap lengths:** As specified by the designer or as per relevant codes.
    *   **Sectional views and details:** Provide crucial information on reinforcement arrangement.
    *   **Bar marks or designations:** Unique identifiers for different types of bars.
