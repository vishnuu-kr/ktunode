# Quantity Surveying and Valuation: Module 1 - Introduction

## Topic: Bill of Quantities (BOQ) - Typical Format and Use

This module introduces you to the fundamental document in construction cost management: the Bill of Quantities (BOQ). We will explore its structure, purpose, and how it is used throughout the construction lifecycle.

---

### **1. Introduction to the Bill of Quantities (BOQ)**

**1.1 What is a Bill of Quantities?**

*   A detailed list of all the materials, labor, and plant required for a construction project, broken down into specific work items.
*   It quantifies these requirements, providing a basis for pricing by contractors.
*   Essentially, it's a document that translates the design intent into measurable quantities for costing.

**1.2 Purpose and Objectives of a BOQ**

*   **Tender Evaluation:** Provides a standardized basis for contractors to submit their prices, allowing for fair comparison of tenders.
*   **Cost Control:** Acts as a benchmark for monitoring and controlling project costs during construction.
*   **Valuation of Work Done:** Facilitates the valuation of work completed for interim payments to the contractor.
*   **Variation Management:** Helps in assessing the cost implications of any changes or variations to the original scope of work.
*   **Contractual Basis:** Forms a crucial part of the construction contract, defining the scope and pricing mechanism.
*   **Procurement:** Essential for obtaining competitive bids from contractors.
*   **Cost Planning and Estimating:** Aids in the initial cost planning and estimating phases of a project.

**1.3 Key Principles of BOQ Preparation**

*   **Standardization:** Adherence to standard methods of measurement (SMM) is crucial for consistency and comparability. (e.g., SMM7 in the UK, NRM in the UK, or other local standards).
*   **Accuracy and Completeness:** Quantities must be accurate and all measurable work items should be included.
*   **Clarity and Unambiguity:** Work descriptions should be clear, concise, and unambiguous to avoid misinterpretation.
*   **Fairness:** Quantities should be measured in a fair and unbiased manner.
*   **Consistency:** Consistent application of measurement rules throughout the document.

### **2. Typical Format of a Bill of Quantities**

The format of a BOQ can vary slightly depending on the project and the prevailing SMM, but generally includes the following columns:

**2.1 Essential Columns in a BOQ**

| Column Number | Column Name        | Description                                                                                                                                                           |
| :------------ | :----------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1             | **Item Number**    | A unique sequential number for each work item, often organized by trade or section.                                                                                    |
| 2             | **Description**    | A detailed and specific description of the work item, including materials, dimensions, quality, and any specific requirements as per the design drawings and specifications. |
| 3             | **Unit**           | The unit of measurement for the work item (e.g., m³ for cubic meters, m² for square meters, kg for kilograms, No. for number, L.s. for lump sum).                         |
| 4             | **Quantity**       | The calculated quantity of the work item based on the design drawings and specifications.                                                                             |
| 5             | **Rate**           | The price per unit of the work item (to be filled by the contractor). This covers labor, materials, plant, and profit.                                                |
| 6             | **Amount**         | The total cost of the work item (Quantity x Rate) (to be filled by the contractor).                                                                                    |
| 7 (Optional)  | **Provisional Sum**| An estimated amount of money included in the contract for work that cannot be precisely defined at the time of tender (e.g., attending to unforeseen conditions).       |
| 8 (Optional)  | **Prime Cost Sum** | An amount of money included in the contract to cover the cost of specific materials, goods, or work to be supplied or carried out by a nominated subcontractor.         |

**2.2 Standard Structure of a BOQ**

BOQs are typically structured by trades or work sections to facilitate pricing and management. Common sections include:

*   **Preliminaries:** General requirements, site establishment, temporary works, insurances, etc.
*   **General Items:** Work of a general nature not specific to a trade.
*   **Excavator and Earthworks:** Excavation, filling, compaction, site clearance.
*   **Concrete Work:** Foundations, columns, beams, slabs, formwork, reinforcement.
*   **Masonry Work:** Brickwork, blockwork, stone masonry, mortar.
*   **Structural Steelwork:** Fabrication and erection of steel members.
*   **Carpentry and Joinery:** Timber framing, doors, windows, finishes.
*   **Roofing:** Waterproofing, roof covering, insulation.
*   **Plastering and Rendering:** Wall and ceiling finishes.
*   **Painting and Decorating:** Surface preparation and application of paints.
*   **Flooring:** Tiling, screeds, vinyl, carpet.
*   **Plumbing and Drainage:** Water supply, sanitary fittings, drainage systems.
*   **Electrical Installations:** Wiring, fixtures, conduits.
*   **Mechanical Services:** HVAC systems, elevators.
*   **External Works and Landscaping:** Paving, planting, fencing.

**2.3 Example of a BOQ Item**

Let's consider an example for concrete work:

### **3. Use of the Bill of Quantities**

**3.1 Pre-Construction Phase**

*   **Tender Documentation:** The BOQ is a critical document issued to prospective contractors for bidding.
*   **Tender Analysis:** Quantity Surveyors use the BOQ to compare the rates and total prices submitted by different contractors, identifying potential discrepancies and ensuring fairness.
*   **Negotiation:** The BOQ can be used as a basis for negotiation with the preferred bidder.

**3.2 During Construction Phase**

*   **Valuation of Work Done:** The BOQ's quantities are used to measure the actual work executed on-site. The contractor then prices these measured quantities at their submitted rates for interim payment certificates.
    *   **Example:** If 8.00 m³ of reinforced concrete in columns (Item 3.2) has been completed, and the contractor's rate is $500/m³, the interim payment for this item would be 8.00 m³ * $500/m³ = $4000.
*   **Variation Orders:** When changes occur, the BOQ items are used as a basis for valuing the additional or omitted work. If a new item is introduced, it needs to be measured and priced by the contractor, often referring to existing rates for similar work.
*   **Cost Monitoring and Control:** By comparing the actual cost of work against the priced BOQ, Quantity Surveyors can track project expenditure and identify any cost overruns.
*   **Resource Planning:** The quantities in the BOQ can inform the planning and procurement of materials, labor, and plant.

**3.3 Post-Construction Phase**

*   **Final Account:** The BOQ is essential for preparing the final account, which details all payments made to the contractor, including adjustments for variations.
*   **Defect Liability Period:** The BOQ can be referenced when assessing the cost of rectifying defects.

### **4. Advantages and Disadvantages of using a BOQ**

**4.1 Advantages**

*   **Fair and Equitable Tendering:** Promotes competitive pricing by providing a common basis for all bidders.
*   **Cost Predictability:** Helps in forecasting project costs more accurately.
*   **Simplified Cost Control:** Facilitates easier monitoring and control of project expenditure.
*   **Efficient Valuation:** Streamlines the process of valuing work for payments.
*   **Clear Scope Definition:** Clearly outlines the scope of work to be undertaken.
*   **Reduced Disputes:** Minimizes disputes by providing a clear basis for pricing and measurement.

**4.2 Disadvantages**

*   **Time and Cost of Preparation:** Preparing a detailed and accurate BOQ can be time-consuming and expensive.
*   **Risk of Omissions/Errors:** Any errors or omissions in the BOQ can lead to disputes or significant cost implications.
*   **Design Certainty Required:** Requires a relatively well-developed design to quantify accurately.
*   **Potential for Contractor "Gaming":** Contractors may inflate rates for items they anticipate will be reduced or omitted.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**

What are the primary purposes of a Bill of Quantities in a construction project? (List at least three).

**Answer 1:**

The primary purposes of a BOQ are:
*   To provide a standardized basis for tender evaluation and comparison.
*   To facilitate cost control and monitoring during construction.
*   To enable the valuation of work done for interim payments.
*   To assist in the management of variation orders.

**Question 2:**

Identify the typical columns found in a Bill of Quantities and briefly explain the function of each.

**Answer 2:**

*   **Item Number:** Unique identifier for each work item.
*   **Description:** Detailed explanation of the work to be performed.
*   **Unit:** The unit of measurement for the item (e.g., m³, m², kg).
*   **Quantity:** The calculated amount of the item required.
*   **Rate:** The price per unit (filled by the contractor).
*   **Amount:** Total cost of the item (Quantity x Rate, filled by the contractor).

**Question 3:**

Imagine you are a Quantity Surveyor and have measured 200 m² of plastering work. If the contractor's submitted rate for plastering (item XYZ) is $15 per m², calculate the amount payable for this completed work.

**Answer 3:**

Amount = Quantity × Rate
Amount = 200 m² × $15/m² = $3000

**Question 4:**

Explain the difference between a Provisional Sum and a Prime Cost Sum in a BOQ.

**Answer 4:**

*   **Provisional Sum:** An estimated amount included for work that cannot be precisely defined at the time of tender. It is typically used for unforeseen conditions or items where the exact scope is yet to be determined.
*   **Prime Cost Sum:** An amount included to cover the cost of specific materials, goods, or work to be supplied or carried out by a nominated subcontractor or supplier. The contractor adds their own mark-up for attending to these items.

### **6. Important Points to Remember**

*   **BOQ is a Contractual Document:** Treat it with the utmost care and attention to detail.
*   **Standard Methods of Measurement (SMM):** Always refer to the relevant SMM for accurate measurement rules.
*   **Clarity is Key:** Ambiguous descriptions lead to disputes and cost overruns.
*   **BOQ is a Living Document:** It is used and referenced throughout the entire project lifecycle.
*   **Accuracy in Measurement:** The integrity of the BOQ relies on accurate quantification.
*   **Contractor's Role:** Contractors price the BOQ and their rates form the basis of the contract sum.
