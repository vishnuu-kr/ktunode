## Topic: Items of Work and Units of Measurement (Focus: RCC Residential Building)

### 1. Introduction to Quantity Surveying and Valuation

**Quantity Surveying** is the practice of managing and controlling construction costs. A Quantity Surveyor (QS) is responsible for calculating the quantities of materials and labor required for a construction project, preparing cost estimates, and managing the financial aspects of the project from inception to completion.

**Valuation** in construction refers to the process of determining the monetary worth of a property or a specific construction element at a particular point in time. This can be for various purposes such as sale, purchase, insurance, mortgage, or disputes.

**Importance:** Accurate quantity surveying and valuation are crucial for:
*   Budgeting and financial planning.
*   Tendering and contract administration.
*   Cost control and monitoring.
*   Informed decision-making.
*   Ensuring project profitability.

### 2. Identifying Items of Work from Drawings

Construction projects are executed based on detailed **drawings** and **specifications**. Quantity Surveyors must be able to meticulously extract information from these documents to identify individual items of work.

**Key Drawings for Identification:**

*   **Architectural Drawings:** These depict the aesthetic and functional aspects of the building. They include:
    *   **Site Plan:** Shows the building's location on the site, boundaries, access roads, and surrounding features.
    *   **Floor Plans:** Horizontal sections showing the layout of rooms, walls, doors, windows, and stairs on each floor.
    *   **Elevations:** External views of the building from different sides.
    *   **Sections:** Vertical cuts through the building, revealing internal construction and relationships between different levels.
    *   **Door & Window Schedules:** Lists of all doors and windows with their types, sizes, materials, and specifications.
*   **Structural Drawings:** These show the load-bearing elements of the building. They include:
    *   **Foundation Plans:** Detail the type, size, and reinforcement of foundations.
    *   **Column Layouts:** Location, size, and reinforcement details of columns.
    *   **Beam Layouts:** Location, size, and reinforcement details of beams.
    *   **Slab Layouts:** Thickness, reinforcement details, and any openings in floor slabs and roof slabs.
    *   **Staircase Details:** Dimensions, reinforcement, and construction of staircases.

**Process of Identifying Items of Work:**

1.  **Understand the Project Scope:** Familiarize yourself with the overall building type and its intended use.
2.  **Systematic Review:** Examine each drawing category by category (e.g., start with foundations, then move to walls, slabs, etc.).
3.  **Read Specifications:** Refer to the project specifications for detailed information on materials, quality, workmanship, and performance requirements for each item.
4.  **Cross-Referencing:** Compare information across different drawings (e.g., a wall shown on the floor plan needs to be checked in section drawings for height and in structural drawings for its composition).
5.  **Break Down Complex Elements:** Decompose complex elements (like a reinforced concrete beam) into their constituent materials (concrete, steel reinforcement, formwork).

**Example Items of Work in an RCC Residential Building:**

Based on the drawings and specifications, a QS will identify numerous items of work. Here's a breakdown by building element:

**A. Substructure (Foundations):**

*   **Excavation:**
    *   Earthwork in excavation for foundations (e.g., for footing, plinth beam).
    *   Disposal of excavated earth.
*   **Concrete:**
    *   Plain Cement Concrete (PCC) for foundation beds.
    *   Reinforced Cement Concrete (RCC) for footings.
    *   RCC for plinth beams.
*   **Reinforcement:**
    *   Mild Steel (MS) reinforcement bars for footings and plinth beams.
*   **Formwork:**
    *   Formwork for footings.
    *   Formwork for plinth beams.
*   **Backfilling:**
    *   Filling with sand/earth for damp proofing.
    *   Compacting earth filling around foundations.

**B. Superstructure (Above Ground):**

*   **Framed Structure (Columns & Beams):**
    *   RCC Columns (including concrete, reinforcement, and formwork).
    *   RCC Beams (including concrete, reinforcement, and formwork).
*   **Floors & Roofs:**
    *   RCC Slabs (floor slabs, roof slabs - including concrete, reinforcement, and formwork).
    *   Chajjas (slabs projecting from walls for sunshading - including concrete, reinforcement, and formwork).
*   **Staircases:**
    *   RCC Staircases (including concrete, reinforcement, and formwork for treads, risers, landings, and beams).
*   **Walls:**
    *   Brickwork in cement mortar (e.g., for partition walls, external walls if not RCC).
    *   Blockwork in cement mortar.
    *   RCC walls (if any).
*   **Plastering:**
    *   Cement plastering (e.g., internal walls, external walls, ceilings).
*   **Doors & Windows:**
    *   Supply and fixing of door frames (e.g., RCC, wood, metal).
    *   Supply and fixing of doors (e.g., flush doors, panelled doors).
    *   Supply and fixing of window frames.
    *   Supply and fixing of glazing (glass panes).
    *   Supply and fixing of window shutters.
*   **Finishes:**
    *   Flooring (e.g., tiles, marble, concrete hardeners).
    *   Skirting (e.g., tile, stone).
    *   Painting (e.g., primer, distemper, emulsion, enamel).
    *   Waterproofing for roofs and bathrooms.
*   **Miscellaneous:**
    *   RCC parapet walls.
    *   RCC sunshades/louvers.
    *   Pre-cast elements (if any).

### 3. Units of Measurement of Various Materials and Works

Each item of work, once identified, is measured in specific units. These units are standardized by national bodies (e.g., IS Codes in India, SMM in the UK).

**Key Principles of Measurement:**

*   **Consistency:** Use the same unit for similar items throughout the project.
*   **Clarity:** Units should be unambiguous.
*   **Practicality:** Units should be practical for site measurement and billing.
*   **Dimensional Accuracy:** Measurements should be taken to the required degree of accuracy (e.g., to the nearest cm or 0.01 m for lengths, to the nearest sq m for areas, and to the nearest 0.01 cum for volumes).

**Units of Measurement for Common Items in RCC Residential Buildings:**

| Item of Work                                     | Unit of Measurement | Notes                                                                                                                             |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Excavation:**                                  |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Earthwork in excavation for foundations          | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Measured by the volume of earth removed. Usually calculated as length x width x depth. Depth is typically measured from ground level or formation level. |
| Disposal of excavated earth                      | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Measured by the volume of earth transported away from the site.                                                                   |
| **Concrete:**                                    |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Plain Cement Concrete (PCC)                      | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Measured by the volume of concrete placed.                                                                                        |
| Reinforced Cement Concrete (RCC) – General       | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Measured by the volume of concrete placed, excluding the volume of steel reinforcement and formwork.                              |
| **Reinforcement:**                               |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Steel Reinforcement Bars (Fe 415, Fe 500 etc.)   | Kilogram (Kg)       | Measured by the total weight of steel bars used. Weight is calculated from the length and the bar diameter (using standard weight per meter). |
| **Formwork:**                                    |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Formwork for RCC Members (Columns, Beams, Slabs) | Square Meter (Sq m) | Measured by the surface area of the formwork in contact with the concrete. Calculated for sides, soffits, and ends.               |
| **Walls:**                                       |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Brickwork in Cement Mortar                       | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Measured by the volume of brickwork. Deductions for openings (doors, windows) larger than a specified size (e.g., 0.5 sq m) are made. |
| Blockwork in Cement Mortar                       | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Similar to brickwork, measured by volume.                                                                                         |
| **Plastering:**                                  |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Cement Plastering (Internal/External)            | Square Meter (Sq m) | Measured by the surface area of walls/ceilings plastered. Deductions for openings are typically made.                               |
| **Flooring:**                                    |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Tile/Marble/Stone Flooring                       | Square Meter (Sq m) | Measured by the area covered.                                                                                                     |
| **Doors & Windows:**                             |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Door/Window Frames                               | Numbers (No.)       | Measured as individual units.                                                                                                     |
| Doors (Flush/Panelled Shutters)                  | Square Meter (Sq m) | Measured by the area of the shutter.                                                                                              |
| Windows (Shutters with Glazing)                  | Square Meter (Sq m) | Measured by the area of the shutter including the glass.                                                                          |
| **Painting:**                                    |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Painting (2 Coats, 3 Coats etc.)                 | Square Meter (Sq m) | Measured by the surface area painted. Deductions for openings may or may not be made depending on the specification (often not for small openings). |
| **Staircases:**                                  |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| RCC Staircase (overall)                          | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Can be measured as a combined volume of concrete, steel, and formwork for simplification if not itemized separately.              |
| Staircase Risers and Treads                      | Square Meter (Sq m) | Sometimes measured separately as finished surfaces.                                                                               |
| **Miscellaneous:**                               |                     |                                                                                                                                   |
| Parapet Wall                                     | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Measured by volume.                                                                                                               |
| Sunshades/Chajjas                              | Cubic Meter (Cum)   | Measured by volume.                                                                                                               |
| Waterproofing                                    | Square Meter (Sq m) | Measured by the area treated.                                                                                                     |

**Important Considerations for Units:**

*   **Deductions:** For areas like openings (doors, windows) in walls, deductions are made for items like brickwork/blockwork and plastering if the opening exceeds a certain minimum size (commonly 0.5 sq m). However, for items like painting, deductions are often not made for smaller openings as the additional labor to cut and finish around them can offset the savings.
*   **Additions:** Sometimes, additional items like sills, rebates, or cornices are measured separately or included within the main item based on the measurement standards.
*   **Thickness:** For items measured in square meters (e.g., plastering, flooring), the thickness is assumed to be standard unless otherwise specified. For items measured in cubic meters (e.g., brickwork), the thickness is an integral part of the calculation.
*   **Long and Short Wall Method:** This is a common method for measuring brickwork and plastering, which helps in minimizing errors by systematically calculating wall lengths.
    *   **Long Wall:** The center-to-center length of the wall is taken.
    *   **Short Wall:** The center-to-center length of the wall minus twice the thickness of the wall is taken.

### 4. Linking Items of Work to Units of Measurement

The core task of a quantity surveyor is to accurately measure the quantities of each identified item of work using the correct units.

**Example Scenario:**

Consider a simple RCC beam as shown in structural drawings.

*   **Identify Items:**
    *   RCC (concrete)
    *   Steel Reinforcement (main bars, stirrups)
    *   Formwork (soffit, two sides)

*   **Measure Dimensions from Drawings:**
    *   Length of the beam (e.g., L = 4 meters)
    *   Width of the beam (e.g., b = 0.3 meters)
    *   Overall depth of the beam (e.g., D = 0.5 meters)
    *   Quantity of steel bars (length and diameter from reinforcement schedule)
    *   Dimensions for formwork (soffit area, side areas)

*   **Calculate Quantities using Correct Units:**
    *   **RCC Volume:** Volume = Length x Width x Depth = 4 m x 0.3 m x 0.5 m = 0.60 Cum
    *   **Steel Reinforcement Weight:** Sum of lengths of all bars (main, stirrups) x weight per meter for each bar diameter = Total weight in Kg.
    *   **Formwork Area:**
        *   Soffit Area = Length x Width = 4 m x 0.3 m = 1.20 Sq m
        *   Side Area (2 sides) = 2 x (Length x Depth) = 2 x (4 m x 0.5 m) = 4.00 Sq m
        *   Total Formwork Area = 1.20 Sq m + 4.00 Sq m = 5.20 Sq m

**Crucial Connection:** An error in identifying an item or an error in applying the correct unit of measurement can lead to significant discrepancies in the overall cost estimate.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
From an architectural floor plan, you identify a partition wall made of brickwork. The wall is 10 meters long, 0.15 meters thick, and 3 meters high. What is the unit of measurement for this brickwork, and what is its quantity?

**Answer 1:**
*   **Unit of Measurement:** Cubic Meter (Cum)
*   **Quantity:** Length x Thickness x Height = 10 m x 0.15 m x 3 m = **4.50 Cum**

**Question 2:**
A structural drawing shows an RCC roof slab that is 8 meters long, 6 meters wide, and 0.15 meters thick. Identify the items of work involved in this slab and their units of measurement.

**Answer 2:**
The main items of work for the RCC roof slab are:
*   **RCC (Concrete):** Measured in **Cubic Meter (Cum)**.
    *   Quantity = 8 m x 6 m x 0.15 m = 7.20 Cum
*   **Steel Reinforcement:** Measured in **Kilogram (Kg)**. (Specific quantity would require a reinforcement schedule).
*   **Formwork:** Measured in **Square Meter (Sq m)**.
    *   Soffit Area = 8 m x 6 m = 48.00 Sq m. (Side formwork is usually negligible for slabs unless they are very deep or have beams integrated).

**Question 3:**
You are measuring the painting of an external wall that is 5 meters long and 3 meters high. There is a window opening on this wall that is 1.5 meters wide and 1.2 meters high. If the specification states that deductions for openings are made for all openings larger than 0.5 sq m, what is the area to be painted?

**Answer 3:**
*   **Total Wall Area:** 5 m x 3 m = 15.00 Sq m
*   **Window Opening Area:** 1.5 m x 1.2 m = 1.80 Sq m
*   **Deduction Applicability:** The window opening area (1.80 sq m) is greater than 0.5 sq m, so a deduction will be made.
*   **Area to be Painted:** Total Wall Area - Window Opening Area = 15.00 Sq m - 1.80 Sq m = **13.20 Sq m**

**Question 4:**
List at least five different items of work typically found in the superstructure of an RCC residential building and their respective units of measurement.

**Answer 4:**
1.  **RCC Columns:** Cubic Meter (Cum)
2.  **RCC Beams:** Cubic Meter (Cum)
3.  **RCC Slabs:** Cubic Meter (Cum)
4.  **Steel Reinforcement:** Kilogram (Kg)
5.  **Formwork for Beams:** Square Meter (Sq m)
6.  **Brickwork:** Cubic Meter (Cum)
7.  **Plastering:** Square Meter (Sq m)
8.  **Door Frames:** Numbers (No.)
9.  **Door Shutters:** Square Meter (Sq m)
10. **Painting:** Square Meter (Sq m)

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Drawings are Primary Source:** Always start with architectural and structural drawings.
*   **Specifications are Crucial:** They provide details on materials, quality, and workmanship that are not always evident from drawings alone.
*   **Standard Units are Mandatory:** Adhere to the accepted units of measurement to ensure accuracy and comparability.
*   **Accuracy in Measurement:** Precision in taking dimensions from drawings is paramount.
*   **Understand Measurement Rules:** Be aware of how deductions, additions, and specific methods (like long/short wall) apply.
*   **Consistency is Key:** Maintain a consistent approach throughout the measurement process.
*   **Cross-Verification:** Check your measurements against different drawings and specifications.
*   **Digital Tools:** While manual measurement is fundamental, modern QS practice involves using software for take-off, which still relies on understanding the fundamental principles.
