# Environmental Laws and Policy: Module 2 - Forest, Wildlife, and Biodiversity Related Laws

## 1. Introduction to Forest, Wildlife, and Biodiversity Laws

This module explores the legal framework governing forests, wildlife, and biodiversity in India. These natural resources are crucial for ecological balance, economic development, and the well-being of communities. Understanding these laws is vital for their conservation and sustainable management.

### 1.1. Why are these laws important?

*   **Ecological Balance:** Forests and wildlife play a critical role in maintaining ecosystems, regulating climate, and ensuring water security.
*   **Economic Value:** They provide resources for timber, non-timber forest products, tourism, and livelihoods for many people.
*   **Biodiversity Conservation:** India is a megadiverse country, and protecting its rich biodiversity is a global responsibility.
*   **Cultural and Aesthetic Value:** Forests and wildlife hold cultural significance and contribute to the natural beauty of the landscape.

### 1.2. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Forest:** Legally defined under the **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980**. It includes any area recorded as forest in government records. The Supreme Court, in the **Godavarman judgment (1996)**, expanded the definition to include any area which is forest in de facto sense, irrespective of whether it is notified under the Forest Act or not.
*   **Wildlife:** All animals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, insects, and other living organisms found in their natural habitat.
*   **Biodiversity:** The variety of life on Earth at all its levels, from genes to ecosystems, and the ecological and evolutionary processes that sustain it.
*   **Habitat:** The natural home or environment of an animal, plant, or other organism.
*   **Protected Area:** Areas designated for conservation, such as National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Conservation Reserves.
*   **Sustainable Use:** Using biological resources in a way and at a rate that does not lead to the long-term decline of biodiversity.

## 2. The Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980

This act is the cornerstone of forest protection in India, aimed at preventing the diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes without prior approval from the Central Government.

### 2.1. Objectives of the Act

*   To curb deforestation and degradation of forest land.
*   To regulate the diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes.
*   To ensure that any diversion is done with minimal environmental impact and appropriate compensatory measures.

### 2.2. Key Provisions

*   **Section 3:** The Central Government is empowered to make rules and regulations for the conservation of forests and for matters connected therewith.
*   **Section 2:** Prohibits the denotification of reserved forests or the clearing of forests on any land under the management of the government for non-forest purposes without the prior approval of the Central Government.
*   **Non-Forest Purpose:** Defined to include cultivation of any agricultural crop, planting of any species of trees, rehabilitation of any forest land that is degraded, or any other purpose that the Central Government may notify.
*   **Compensatory Afforestation:** A key requirement for any diversion of forest land. This involves planting trees on equivalent or twice the area of forest land diverted, usually on non-forest land.
*   **Net Present Value (NPV):** An economic valuation of the ecosystem services lost due to forest diversion, which is to be paid by the user agency.
*   **Forest Rights Act, 2006 (Scheduled Tribes and Other Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006):** While the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980, focuses on government-owned forests, the Forest Rights Act recognizes the rights of forest-dwelling scheduled tribes and other forest dwellers over forest lands. It mandates Gram Sabha consent for diversion of forest land.

### 2.3. Amendments and Important Judgments

*   **Forest (Conservation) Amendment Act, 2023:** Introduced significant changes, including exemption for certain projects from the Act's purview, such as linear projects (roads, railways) passing through forest land and projects on land within 100 km of the Line of Control. It also aims to streamline the approval process.
*   **Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (1996):** This landmark Supreme Court judgment clarified the definition of "forest" and stated that any area that is forest in *de facto* sense, irrespective of its legal status or record, would be covered under the Act. It also mandated the creation of Special Investigation Teams (SITs) to monitor forest encroachments and illegal felling.

### 2.4. Examples

*   A proposal for constructing a highway through a forest area requires prior approval from the Central Government under the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980. The project proponent will have to provide compensatory afforestation and pay NPV.
*   The expansion of a mining project into a forest land necessitates obtaining clearance under this Act.

## 3. The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972

This is the primary legislation for the protection of wildlife and its habitats in India. It provides for the establishment of protected areas, the regulation of trade in wild animals and their products, and the prevention of cruelty to animals.

### 3.1. Objectives of the Act

*   To protect wild animals, birds, and plants.
*   To safeguard their habitats.
*   To regulate and control activities that threaten wildlife.
*   To prevent the illegal trade in wildlife and wildlife products.

### 3.2. Key Provisions

*   **Hunting of Wild Animals (Chapter III):** Prohibits the hunting of scheduled wild animals. Hunting is defined broadly to include killing, poisoning, trapping, injuring, or disturbing in any way the wild animal.
    *   **Schedules I, II, III, IV, V:** These schedules list various wild animals and plants with varying degrees of protection. Animals in Schedule I and Part II of Schedule II receive the highest protection.
*   **Protected Areas (Chapter IV):**
    *   **National Parks (Section 35):** Areas of ecological importance declared by the Central Government for the purpose of protecting, propagating, and developing wildlife and its environment. Activities like grazing, collection of forest produce, and private rights are prohibited.
    *   **Wildlife Sanctuaries (Section 18):** Areas declared by the State Government for the purpose of protecting, propagating, and developing wildlife or its environment. Limited restrictions may be imposed on activities to facilitate conservation.
    *   **Conservation Reserves (Section 36-C):** Areas declared by the State Government to protect flora, fauna, and other natural features. These are buffer zones around National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries.
    *   **Community Reserves (Section 36-C):** Areas of private or community land that have been voluntarily protected by the community for conservation.
*   **Penalties (Chapter VI):** Stringent penalties, including imprisonment and fines, are prescribed for violations of the Act.
*   **Regulation of Trade in Wildlife and Wildlife Products (Chapter V):**
    *   **Prohibition of Trade:** Prohibits the buying, selling, or possessing of scheduled animals or their products without a license.
    *   **Sanctuaries and National Parks Boards:** Advisory bodies are constituted to assist in the management of these areas.
*   **Central Zoo Authority:** Constituted for the proper management of zoos and to ensure that zoos function in accordance with the Act.

### 3.3. Amendments and Important Judgments

*   **Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act, 2002:** Introduced significant changes, including:
    *   Inclusion of Conservation Reserves and Community Reserves.
    *   Strengthening of penalties and provisions against poaching and illegal trade.
    *   Establishment of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL).
*   **National Board for Wildlife (NBWL):** A statutory body that advises the Central Government on policy and measures for wildlife conservation. It also approves projects that may impact protected areas.
*   **Supreme Court's directives:** The Supreme Court has consistently emphasized the importance of protecting wildlife and has issued directives for the implementation of the Act, including setting up eco-sensitive zones around protected areas.

### 3.4. Examples

*   Poaching a tiger, which is listed in Schedule I of the Act, is a severe offense with significant penalties.
*   Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand is a National Park established under this Act.
*   The establishment of an eco-sensitive zone around the Periyar National Park in Kerala is aimed at regulating activities that could impact the park's wildlife.

## 4. The Biological Diversity Act, 2002

This Act aims to conserve and promote the sustainable use of biological resources and the equitable sharing of benefits arising from their use. It is India's response to the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD).

### 4.1. Objectives of the Act

*   To conserve and sustainably use biological diversity.
*   To ensure the fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources, including genetic resources, traditional knowledge, and intellectual property rights.
*   To recognize the rights of local communities and indigenous people over their biodiversity and traditional knowledge.

### 4.2. Key Provisions

*   **Definitions (Section 2):**
    *   **Biological Diversity:** Refers to the variability among living organisms from all sources including, inter alia, terrestrial, marine and other aquatic ecosystems and the ecological complexes of which they are part; this includes diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems.
    *   **Biological Resources:** Includes plants, animals, micro-organisms and their parts, and products thereof, and includes by any way of survey, collection, bio-prospecting, research, training, exchange or any other means.
    *   **Genetic Resources:** Means material of plant, animal, microbial or other origin containing functional units of heredity.
    *   **Traditional Knowledge:** Refers to knowledge, innovations and practices of indigenous and local communities embodying traditional lifestyles or having a cultural significance, which are relevant to the conservation and sustainable use of biological diversity.
*   **Key Institutions:**
    *   **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA):** A statutory body established to implement the provisions of the Act, approve proposals for obtaining biological resources or associated traditional knowledge for commercial use, and advise the Central Government on biodiversity-related matters.
    *   **State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs):** Established in each state to implement the Act at the state level.
    *   **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs):** Local level committees at the Panchayats, Municipalities, or equivalent level to document and protect biodiversity and associated knowledge.
*   **Regulation of Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS):**
    *   **Prior Informed Consent (PIC):** Foreign nationals and non-Indian companies need permission from the NBA to obtain biological resources for commercial purposes.
    *   **Approval of NBA:** Any person, except a non-Indian citizen, seeking to obtain biological resources for commercial utilization needs to apply to the State Government for approval.
    *   **Benefit Sharing:** A key principle of the Act, ensuring that benefits arising from the use of biological resources and traditional knowledge are shared with the communities who have conserved and used them. This can be in the form of knowledge, technological, or financial benefits.
*   **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR):** The Act prevents the grant of IPRs for inventions based on Indian biological resources or traditional knowledge without proper authorization and benefit sharing.
*   **Declaration of Threatened Species (Section 38):** The Central Government can declare species that are found to be in danger of extinction as threatened species.

### 4.3. Examples

*   A pharmaceutical company wanting to extract medicinal plants from a forest area for drug development would need to obtain prior approval from the NBA and ensure benefit sharing with the local community and the State Government.
*   The registration of traditional knowledge about the use of specific herbs by an indigenous community by the respective BMC is a step towards protecting that knowledge.

## 5. Other Important Related Laws and Policies

While the above three are the primary laws, several other policies and laws have implications for forest, wildlife, and biodiversity conservation.

### 5.1. Forest Policy and National Policies

*   **National Forest Policy, 1988:** A key policy document that laid down broad objectives for forest conservation and management, including:
    *   Maintaining ecological balance.
    *   Safeguarding the environment and preventing soil erosion and degradation.
    *   Meeting the basic needs of the people, especially the rural and tribal populations, for fuelwood, fodder, small timber, and other forest produce.
    *   Promoting afforestation and social forestry.
*   **National Wildlife Action Plan (NWAP):** A plan that outlines strategies and programs for wildlife conservation, habitat management, and research.
*   **National Biodiversity Action Plan (NBAP):** A plan that outlines India's strategies for implementing the Convention on Biological Diversity.

### 5.2. Other Relevant Legislations

*   **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** A comprehensive umbrella act that provides for the protection and improvement of the environment. It can be used to regulate activities that impact forests and wildlife.
*   **The Scheduled Tribes and Other Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006:** (As mentioned earlier) Crucial for recognizing and vesting forest rights in forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and Other Forest Dwellers, and it emphasizes the role of Gram Sabhas in decision-making regarding forest diversion.
*   **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 & Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981:** These acts, while primarily for water and air quality, can indirectly impact forest and wildlife by controlling industrial pollution.

## 6. Challenges and Emerging Issues

*   **Encroachment and Illegal Activities:** Persistent problem affecting forest areas.
*   **Human-Wildlife Conflict:** Increasing conflict due to habitat degradation and human population growth.
*   **Climate Change:** Impacting biodiversity and forest ecosystems.
*   **Infrastructure Development vs. Conservation:** Balancing the need for development with the imperative of protecting natural resources.
*   **Effective Implementation and Enforcement:** Ensuring that laws are effectively implemented and enforced at the ground level.
*   **Community Participation:** Engaging local communities in conservation efforts.
*   **Implementation of the Forest Rights Act:** Ensuring its effective implementation for the benefit of forest dwellers.

Benefit Sharing under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, is a core principle that ensures the equitable distribution of benefits arising from the utilization of India's biological resources and associated traditional knowledge. This means that when a biological resource or traditional knowledge is used for commercial purposes, the communities who have conserved, nurtured, and developed this knowledge and resource are entitled to a share of the benefits. ## Important Points to Remember

*   The **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980**, is primarily about preventing the diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes.
*   The **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**, focuses on the protection of wild animals, birds, plants, and their habitats, and regulates trade in wildlife products.
*   The **Biological Diversity Act, 2002**, is about the conservation of biodiversity, the sustainable use of biological resources, and the equitable sharing of benefits.
*   The **Godavarman judgment** significantly broadened the interpretation of "forest" in India.
*   **Compensatory Afforestation** and **Net Present Value (NPV)** are key financial mechanisms under the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980, for mitigating forest diversion.
*   **National Parks** have stricter regulations on human activities compared to **Wildlife Sanctuaries**.
*   **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)** are crucial local institutions under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, for documenting and protecting local biodiversity.
*   The **Forest Rights Act, 2006**, plays a vital role in recognizing and vesting rights of forest-dwelling communities.
*   There is often a **tension between development projects and conservation**, and these laws aim to strike a balance.
