# Environmental Laws and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water, and Marine Laws

## Topic: Pollution Control Boards, Groundwater and Law, Judicial Remedies and Procedures, Marine Laws of India

---

### **I. Pollution Control Boards (PCBs)**

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**
*   Understanding the role and functions of Pollution Control Boards in India.
*   Knowledge of the legal framework establishing and empowering PCBs.
*   Familiarity with the common powers and responsibilities of PCBs.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Pollution Control Board (PCB):** Statutory bodies established under environmental legislation to prevent, control, and abate environmental pollution. In India, these are primarily the **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)** and **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)**.
*   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):** Apex body responsible for coordinating the activities of SPCBs, laying down standards, and carrying out national-level research and monitoring.
*   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):** State-level bodies responsible for implementing environmental laws and regulations within their respective states.

**Legal Framework:**

*   **The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:**
    *   Established the **Central Water Pollution Board** (later renamed CPCB) and **State Water Pollution Boards** (later expanded to SPCBs).
    *   Mandated SPCBs to prevent and control water pollution.
*   **The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981:**
    *   Empowered SPCBs to prevent and control air pollution.
    *   Expanded the scope of SPCBs to include air quality management.
*   **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:**
    *   Provided a broader framework for environmental protection and gave the central government powers to establish authorities, including PCBs, for this purpose.
    *   PCBs derive significant powers and responsibilities under this Act.

**Functions and Responsibilities of PCBs:**

*   **Standard Setting:**
    *   CPCB sets national ambient air quality standards (NAAQS) and national water quality standards.
    *   SPCBs adopt and enforce these standards at the state level.
*   **Monitoring and Surveillance:**
    *   Regular monitoring of ambient air and water quality across various locations.
    *   Establishing monitoring stations and collecting data.
*   **Granting Consents/Permissions:**
    *   Issuing **Consent to Establish (CTE)** and **Consent to Operate (CTO)** to industries and other establishments to regulate their pollution discharge.
    *   This is a crucial mechanism for controlling point-source pollution.
*   **Enforcement and Prosecution:**
    *   Taking legal action against polluters who violate environmental norms.
    *   Issuing directions, notices, and closure orders.
*   **Information and Public Awareness:**
    *   Collecting and disseminating environmental data.
    *   Conducting public awareness campaigns.
*   **Research and Development:**
    *   Promoting research on pollution control and abatement techniques.
*   **Policy Formulation:**
    *   Advising the government on environmental policies and legislation.

**Example:** An industry intending to set up a new factory needs to obtain a "Consent to Establish" from the State Pollution Control Board before commencing construction. This consent is granted after assessing the proposed factory's environmental impact and ensuring compliance with pollution control norms.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   PCBs are crucial for the operationalization of environmental laws.
*   The CPCB sets national standards, while SPCBs implement and enforce them.
*   The "Consent to Establish" and "Consent to Operate" system is a primary tool for regulating industrial pollution.

### **II. Groundwater and Law**

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**
*   Understanding the legal status of groundwater in India.
*   Identifying sources of groundwater pollution.
*   Knowledge of legal mechanisms for groundwater protection and management.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Groundwater:** Water held underground in the soil or in pores and crevices in rock.
*   **Riparian Rights:** Historically, rights associated with ownership of land bordering a watercourse, often extending to the use and flow of water. However, these are less directly applicable to groundwater in India.
*   **Public Trust Doctrine:** A legal principle that suggests certain natural resources (like air, water, forests) are held in trust by the government for the benefit of the public. This doctrine has been increasingly invoked to protect groundwater.
*   **Over-extraction:** Pumping out groundwater at a rate faster than its natural replenishment, leading to depletion.

**Legal Status of Groundwater in India:**

*   **No specific comprehensive legislation** solely dedicated to groundwater management and ownership.
*   Historically, groundwater ownership was tied to **land ownership**, leading to the "doctrine of absolute ownership" – landowners could extract as much groundwater as they wished.
*   However, judicial pronouncements and evolving legal understanding have moved towards recognizing groundwater as a **common resource**.
*   The **Right to Life and Personal Liberty (Article 21 of the Constitution)** has been interpreted to include the right to clean drinking water, which inherently requires protection of groundwater.

**Sources of Groundwater Pollution:**

*   **Industrial Effluents:** Discharge of untreated or inadequately treated wastewater from industries.
*   **Agricultural Runoff:** Pesticides, fertilizers, and other chemicals leaching into the groundwater from agricultural fields.
*   **Domestic Sewage:** Improper disposal of sewage and septic tank leachates.
*   **Leachates from Landfills:** Toxic substances from waste dumps seeping into the groundwater.
*   **Mining Activities:** Contamination from mining operations.
*   **Salinity Intrusion:** In coastal areas, excessive pumping can lead to saltwater entering freshwater aquifers.

**Legal Mechanisms for Groundwater Protection and Management:**

*   **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:**
    *   Empowers the government to take measures to protect and improve the environment, including groundwater.
    *   Allows for the notification of ecologically sensitive areas where extraction or pollution might be prohibited.
*   **The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:**
    *   Prohibits the discharge of pollutants into water bodies, which includes groundwater if it's connected to surface water.
    *   PCBs play a role in regulating industrial discharges that could affect groundwater.
*   **Groundwater Regulation Rules (State-level):**
    *   Some states have enacted specific rules for groundwater extraction, often requiring permits or licenses for commercial or industrial use, and stipulating extraction limits.
    *   **Example:** Delhi Groundwater Authority regulates groundwater extraction in the National Capital Territory.
*   **Judicial Interventions:**
    *   Courts have played a significant role in recognizing groundwater as a common resource and directing action against pollution and over-extraction.
    *   The **Public Trust Doctrine** has been applied to groundwater protection.

**Example:** In drought-prone areas, excessive agricultural use of groundwater can lead to its depletion. State governments might impose restrictions on groundwater extraction for irrigation based on studies of aquifer levels.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Groundwater is increasingly viewed as a common resource, not solely tied to land ownership.
*   The lack of a single, comprehensive national law for groundwater is a challenge.
*   The Environment (Protection) Act and judicial pronouncements are key drivers for groundwater protection.
*   Groundwater pollution is a serious threat to public health and ecosystems.

### **III. Judicial Remedies and Procedures**

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**
*   Understanding the various legal avenues available for environmental protection in India.
*   Familiarity with the role of courts and tribunals in enforcing environmental laws.
*   Knowledge of key legal procedures and remedies for environmental grievances.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** A legal action undertaken to bring about a change in law or to remedy a public wrong or to enforce a public duty. It allows any citizen or organization to approach the courts on behalf of those who are unable to do so themselves due to poverty, disability, or social disadvantage.
*   **Writs:** Extraordinary remedies issued by higher courts to lower courts or government authorities. Key writs relevant to environmental law include:
    *   **Mandamus:** An order commanding a public official to perform a statutory duty.
    *   **Prohibition:** An order prohibiting a lower court or authority from exceeding its jurisdiction.
    *   **Certiorari:** An order quashing a decision of a lower court or authority on grounds of illegality or error.
    *   **Habeas Corpus:** An order to produce a person detained illegally before the court.
    *   **Quo Warranto:** An order questioning the authority by which a public office is held.
*   **Civil Suits:** Legal actions brought by an individual or entity seeking compensation or injunctions for harm caused by pollution.
*   **Criminal Proceedings:** Actions taken by the state against individuals or entities for violating environmental laws, which can lead to fines, imprisonment, or both.
*   **National Green Tribunal (NGT):** A specialized statutory body established under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010, to handle environmental disputes and enforce environmental laws.

**Legal Avenues and Procedures:**

1.  **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):**
    *   **Purpose:** To address widespread environmental degradation or injustice where affected parties cannot easily access the courts.
    *   **Procedure:** Any person or organization can file a PIL in the High Courts or the Supreme Court. The court then issues notices to the concerned parties (government, polluters) and hears the case.
    *   **Example:** PILs have been filed against air pollution in Delhi, illegal mining, and deforestation, leading to significant court interventions and policy changes.

2.  **Writ Jurisdiction (Articles 226 & 32 of the Constitution):**
    *   **Article 226:** Power of High Courts to issue writs for enforcement of fundamental rights and for other purposes.
    *   **Article 32:** Power of the Supreme Court to issue writs for enforcement of fundamental rights.
    *   **Application:** Used to direct government authorities (like PCBs) to perform their statutory duties (e.g., inspect a polluting unit, enforce a standard) or to stop illegal actions by polluters.

3.  **Civil Remedies:**
    *   **Injunctions:** A court order restraining a party from doing something or compelling them to do something (e.g., an injunction to stop a polluting factory from operating).
    *   **Damages:** Monetary compensation for losses suffered due to pollution (e.g., crop damage, health costs).
    *   **Procedure:** A civil suit is filed in a civil court.

4.  **Criminal Proceedings:**
    *   **Purpose:** To penalize polluters and deter future violations.
    *   **Laws:** Violations of Acts like the Water Act, Air Act, Environment (Protection) Act can lead to criminal prosecution.
    *   **Penalties:** Fines, imprisonment, or both.
    *   **Procedure:** Filed by the state or investigating agencies.

5.  **National Green Tribunal (NGT):**
    *   **Establishment:** Under the NGT Act, 2010.
    *   **Jurisdiction:** Handles all civil cases relating to the environment and its protection, including:
        *   Enforcement of any legal right relating to the environment.
        *   Relief and compensation for damages to persons and property and the environment.
        *   Prevention of accidents which may cause environmental harm.
    *   **Powers:** Similar to a civil court for summoning witnesses, production of documents, etc. Can grant interim relief, award compensation, and pass orders for restoration of the environment.
    *   **Advantage:** Provides a specialized forum for speedy disposal of environmental cases.

**Example:** If a factory is illegally discharging pollutants into a river, affected villagers can approach the NGT through a petition, seeking an injunction to stop the discharge and compensation for the damage caused to their livelihood and health.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   PIL and Writ jurisdiction are powerful tools for judicial intervention in environmental matters.
*   The NGT is a specialized body designed for efficient handling of environmental disputes.
*   Environmental laws provide for both civil and criminal remedies for pollution.

### **IV. Marine Laws of India**

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**
*   Understanding the legislative framework governing marine pollution and resource management in India.
*   Knowledge of key Indian statutes related to the marine environment.
*   Familiarity with the sources of marine pollution and measures for their control.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Maritime Zones:** India's jurisdiction extends to various zones in the sea:
    *   **Territorial Waters:** Up to 12 nautical miles (nm) from the baseline, where India has full sovereignty.
    *   **Contiguous Zone:** Up to 24 nm from the baseline, where India can enforce laws relating to customs, fiscal, immigration, or sanitary matters.
    *   **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** Up to 200 nm from the baseline, where India has sovereign rights for exploration, exploitation, conservation, and management of natural resources (living and non-living).
    *   **Continental Shelf:** The seabed and subsoil of the submarine areas extending beyond the territorial sea throughout the natural prolongation of the land territory.
*   **Marine Pollution:** The introduction of substances or energy into the marine environment which results or is likely to result in such deleterious effects as harm to marine resources and human health, hindrance to marine activities, including fishing and other legitimate uses of the sea, impairment of quality for use of sea and alteration of marine environment.
*   **MARPOL:** The International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships. India is a signatory and implements its provisions.

**Legislative Framework for Marine Laws in India:**

1.  **The Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, Exclusive Economic Zone and other Maritime Zones Act, 1976:**
    *   Defines India's maritime zones and asserts its rights and jurisdiction within these zones.
    *   Provides the legal basis for applying Indian laws to activities in these areas.

2.  **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:**
    *   This is the umbrella legislation that also covers marine pollution.
    *   The Central Government is empowered to take measures to prevent, control, and abate marine pollution.
    *   Rules like the **Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification** are issued under this Act.

3.  **Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification:**
    *   Issued under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
    *   Classifies coastal areas into different zones (CRZ-I, II, III, IV) with specific regulations for development and permissible activities to protect coastal ecosystems.
    *   Aims to conserve and protect ecologically sensitive coastal areas.

4.  **The Merchant Shipping Act, 1958:**
    *   Deals with shipping and navigation, including provisions for preventing pollution from ships.
    *   Implements international conventions like MARPOL.

5.  **The Indian Penal Code, 1860:**
    *   Sections like 277 (Fouling water of public spring or reservoir) and 283 (Danger or obstruction in public way or line of navigation) can be applied to marine pollution offenses.

6.  **The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:**
    *   While primarily for inland waters, its principles extend to preventing pollution of marine waters connected to land.

7.  **The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972:**
    *   Protects marine flora and fauna, including endangered species found in marine habitats.

**Sources of Marine Pollution and Control Measures:**

*   **Pollution from Ships:**
    *   **Sources:** Oil spills, discharge of bilge water, sewage, garbage, and noxious liquid substances.
    *   **Control:** Implementation of MARPOL Annexes, mandatory use of oil record books, reception facilities at ports, ballast water management.
*   **Land-Based Sources:**
    *   **Sources:** Industrial effluents, municipal sewage, agricultural runoff, solid waste.
    *   **Control:** CRZ regulations, setting discharge standards, treatment of effluents before release, waste management practices.
*   **Offshore Sources:**
    *   **Sources:** Oil and gas exploration activities, platform discharges.
    *   **Control:** Environmental impact assessments, strict operational guidelines for offshore installations, oil spill contingency plans.
*   **Fishing Gear and Debris:**
    *   **Sources:** Lost or discarded fishing nets, plastic debris.
    *   **Control:** Public awareness, promoting responsible fishing practices, cleanup campaigns.

**Example:** The Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) norms prohibit certain construction activities within specific distances from the high tide line to protect ecologically sensitive coastal areas like mangroves and coral reefs. Any violation can lead to penalties under the Environment (Protection) Act.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   India asserts jurisdiction over its maritime zones, including the EEZ.
*   The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, and the CRZ Notification are crucial for managing coastal and marine environments.
*   India implements international conventions like MARPOL to control pollution from ships.
*   Marine pollution is a complex issue requiring a multi-faceted legal and regulatory approach.

### **Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** What is the primary role of the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) in India?
a) Granting forest permits
b) Setting national ambient air and water quality standards
c) Regulating international shipping
d) Managing wildlife sanctuaries

**Answer 1:** b) Setting national ambient air and water quality standards

**Question 2:** The "Consent to Establish" and "Consent to Operate" are mechanisms primarily associated with which environmental regulatory body?
a) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change
b) National Green Tribunal
c) Pollution Control Boards
d) Forest Survey of India

**Answer 2:** c) Pollution Control Boards

**Question 3:** Historically, groundwater ownership in India was often linked to **______________,** but now it is increasingly recognized as a **______________.**

**Answer 3:** land ownership, common resource

**Question 4:** Which legal tool allows any citizen or organization to approach the High Courts or Supreme Court to address a widespread environmental injustice?
a) Civil Suit
b) Criminal Complaint
c) Public Interest Litigation (PIL)
d) Appeal to the NGT

**Answer 4:** c) Public Interest Litigation (PIL)

**Question 5:** The National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established under which Act?
a) The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
b) The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974
c) The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981
d) The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010

**Answer 5:** d) The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010

**Question 6:** India's jurisdiction over the seabed and subsoil of the submarine areas extending beyond its territorial sea is known as the:
a) Contiguous Zone
b) Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)
c) Continental Shelf
d) Territorial Waters

**Answer 6:** c) Continental Shelf

**Question 7:** Explain the significance of the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification in India.

**Answer 7:** The CRZ Notification, issued under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, classifies coastal areas into different zones (CRZ-I, II, III, IV) with specific regulations to protect ecologically sensitive coastal ecosystems like mangroves, coral reefs, and beaches. It aims to conserve these areas by regulating permissible activities, including construction and development, within specified distances from the high tide line.

**Question 8:** Briefly describe two sources of marine pollution and the corresponding control measures.

**Answer 8:**
*   **Source 1: Pollution from Ships (e.g., oil spills, discharge of waste)**
    *   **Control Measure:** India implements international conventions like MARPOL, which mandates standards for ship design, operation, and waste management. This includes the use of oil record books, proper disposal of garbage, and treatment of bilge water.
*   **Source 2: Land-based Sources (e.g., industrial effluents, sewage)**
    *   **Control Measure:** Strict enforcement of discharge standards for industries, proper treatment of municipal sewage before release into the sea, and waste management practices in coastal areas. CRZ regulations also play a role in limiting polluting activities near the coast.
