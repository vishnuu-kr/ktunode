# ENVIRONMENTAL LAWS AND POLICY - Module 3: Air, Water and Marine Laws

## Topic: The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

---

### **1. Introduction and Objectives**

The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, is a landmark legislation in India aimed at protecting and improving the quality of air. It was enacted to:

*   **Prevent and Control Air Pollution:** To address the growing problem of air pollution and its detrimental effects on human health and the environment.
*   **Establish Authorities:** To set up Central and State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) with powers to implement air quality standards and regulations.
*   **Promote Environmental Quality:** To ensure the maintenance of a healthy atmosphere for present and future generations.

**Historical Context:**
The Act was enacted following India's participation in the United Nations Conference on the Human Environment held in Stockholm in 1972. It aligns with international efforts to combat pollution.

---

### **2. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Air Pollution:**
    *   The presence in the atmosphere of any air pollutant.
    *   **Air Pollutant:** Any solid, liquid, or gaseous substance (including noise) which is discharged into the atmosphere by any industrial, trade, or other process, and which is likely to cause harm to human beings or other living creatures or plants or property or the environment. (Note: Noise pollution was included later through amendments).

*   **Atmosphere:**
    *   Includes atmosphere of any building or structure which is within the vicinity of any industrial plant or activity.

*   **State Board:**
    *   The State Pollution Control Board constituted under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974. If no such Board exists, the State Government can establish one under this Act.

*   **Central Board:**
    *   The Central Pollution Control Board constituted under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974.

*   **Occupier:**
    *   In relation to any factory or premises, means the person who has the ultimate control over the affairs of the factory or premises.

*   **Industry:**
    *   Includes any industrial plant or process.

*   **Pollution Control Equipment:**
    *   Any equipment, device, or appliance used for the prevention, control, or abatement of air pollution.

---

### **3. Authorities and Their Functions**

**3.1 Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)**

*   **Composition:** Constituted under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, and functions under the Air Act as well.
*   **Key Functions:**
    *   **Advise Central Government:** On matters related to the prevention and control of air pollution.
    *   **Coordinate SPCBs:** Lay down standards for the quality of air and facilitate coordination between State Boards.
    *   **Collect and Disseminate Information:** On air pollution and its control.
    *   **Undertake and Sponsor Investigations:** Research and development activities.
    *   **Develop and Promote:** Techniques for pollution prevention and control.
    *   **Lay Down Standards:** For quality of air, and for emissions from various sources.
    *   **Provide Technical Assistance:** To SPCBs and State Governments.
    *   **Organize Programs:** For training personnel in air pollution control.

**3.2 State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)**

*   **Composition:** Constituted under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, or established afresh under the Air Act.
*   **Key Functions:**
    *   **Lay Down Standards:** For the quality of air for the whole State or any part thereof.
    *   **Specify Air Pollutants:** And their sources.
    *   **Furnish Information:** To the Central Board and to the State Government.
    *   **Collect and Disseminate Information:** On air pollution, its prevention and control.
    *   **Inspect Air Pollution Control Equipment:** And to give directions for its proper working.
    *   **Establish and Maintain:** Laboratories for monitoring air quality.
    *   **Grant Consents:** To operate industrial units based on their pollution control measures.
    *   **Take Legal Action:** Against defaulting units.

---

### **4. Powers and Functions of the State Government**

*   **Declaration of Air Pollution Control Areas:** The State Government can declare specific areas as "Air Pollution Control Areas" to ensure stricter monitoring and control.
*   **Restriction of Industrial Operations:** Within these areas, the State Government can prohibit or restrict the type of industries or industrial operations permitted, or specify the pollution control equipment that must be installed.
*   **Appointing State Boards:** If no State Board exists under the Water Act, the State Government can constitute one under this Act.

---

### **5. Powers of Entry, Inspection, and Sampling**

*   **Right of Entry:** Any person empowered by the State Board can enter any premises or place at any reasonable time, with prior notice, for the purpose of:
    *   Ascertaining the quality of air.
    *   Examining records, books, or other documents.
    *   Collecting samples of air or emissions.
    *   Inspecting pollution control equipment.

*   **Notice for Entry:** Generally, reasonable notice is required, except in cases where there are grounds to believe that a violation is occurring and immediate inspection is necessary.

*   **Sampling of Emissions:**
    *   The person taking the sample must inform the occupier of their intention to take a sample.
    *   The sample is to be divided into two equal parts, sealed, and signed by both the inspector and the occupier.
    *   One part is given to the occupier, and the other is sent to an approved laboratory for analysis.
    *   The analysis report is crucial evidence in legal proceedings.

---

### **6. Control Measures and Enforcement**

*   **Consent to Operate:** Industrial units require consent from the State Board to operate and discharge pollutants into the atmosphere. This consent is subject to specific conditions regarding emission standards and pollution control measures.

*   **Emission Standards:** The Act empowers the Central and State Boards to lay down standards for emissions from various sources, including industrial plants, vehicles, and power plants.

*   **Prohibition of Discharge:** No person shall, without the previous consent of the State Board, establish any industry, operate any control equipment, or discharge any air pollutant in excess of the standards laid down by the Board.

*   **Appeals:** An appeal against the order of the State Board lies with the Central Government or any other authority designated by it.

---

### **7. Penalties and Legal Proceedings**

*   **Offences:**
    *   Contravention of the provisions of the Act or rules/orders made thereunder.
    *   Failure to comply with directions issued by the Boards.
    *   Discharging pollutants in excess of prescribed standards.
    *   Making false statements or furnishing false information.

*   **Penalties:**
    *   **First Conviction:** Imprisonment for a term not less than one year and six months, which may extend to six years, and with a fine.
    *   **Subsequent Conviction:** Imprisonment for a term not less than two years, which may extend to seven years, and with a fine.
    *   **Continuing Offence:** An additional fine for each day the contravention continues.

*   **Cognizance of Offences:** No court can take cognizance of an offence under this Act except on a complaint made by the State Board or any person authorized by it.

---

### **8. Key Provisions and Amendments**

*   **Inclusion of Noise Pollution:** Amendments have been made to include noise as an air pollutant.
*   **Power to Issue Directions:** The Boards have the power to issue directions, including closure of polluting units, in case of non-compliance.
*   **Establishment of Laboratories:** The Act mandates the establishment of laboratories for effective monitoring.
*   **Public Awareness:** The Act indirectly promotes public awareness through its enforcement mechanisms.

---

### **9. Interplay with Other Environmental Laws**

*   **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:** The Air Act utilizes the same Boards constituted under the Water Act, facilitating a consolidated approach to pollution control.
*   **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** The Environment (Protection) Act is a broader umbrella legislation. The Air Act is a specific law enacted to implement certain provisions of the Environment (Protection) Act related to air quality.

### **10. Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** What are the primary objectives of the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981?

**Answer:** The primary objectives are to prevent and control air pollution and to establish authorities (Central and State Pollution Control Boards) to implement air quality standards and regulations, thereby promoting environmental quality.

**Question 2:** Who are the key authorities responsible for implementing the Air Act, and what are their main functions?

**Answer:** The key authorities are the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and the State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs).
*   **CPCB:** Advises the Central Government, coordinates SPCBs, lays down standards, and undertakes research.
*   **SPCBs:** Lay down State-level standards, specify pollutants, collect information, inspect equipment, establish laboratories, and grant consents to operate.

**Question 3:** What is the significance of "Consent to Operate" under the Air Act?

**Answer:** "Consent to Operate" is a crucial requirement where industrial units must obtain permission from the State Board to operate. This consent is granted based on the unit's adherence to prescribed emission standards and the implementation of adequate pollution control measures. It serves as a primary enforcement tool.

**Question 4:** What powers do the Boards have regarding inspection and sampling of emissions?

**Answer:** The Boards have the power to enter any premises, examine records, and collect samples of air or emissions. The sampling process involves dividing the sample into two parts, sealing them, and providing one to the occupier, with the other sent for laboratory analysis. This ensures transparency and provides evidence for legal action.

**Question 5:** What are the penalties for contravening the provisions of the Air Act, 1981?

**Answer:** Penalties for contravention include imprisonment ranging from a minimum of one year and six months (for first conviction) to two years (for subsequent convictions), and fines. In cases of continuing contravention, additional daily fines are imposed.

---

### **11. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Dual Role of Boards:** The CPCB and SPCBs function under both the Water Act and the Air Act, signifying an integrated approach to pollution control.
*   **"Consent to Operate" is Mandatory:** Industrial units must obtain this consent before commencing or continuing operations.
*   **Emission Standards are Crucial:** Compliance with the standards laid down by the Boards is a key requirement.
*   **Right to Inspection and Sampling:** This is a powerful tool for enforcing the Act.
*   **Penalties are Severe:** The Act prescribes significant penalties to deter non-compliance.
*   **Noise as a Pollutant:** Remember that noise pollution is also covered under the Act.
*   **The Act is Preventive and Control-Oriented:** It focuses on both preventing new pollution and controlling existing pollution.
