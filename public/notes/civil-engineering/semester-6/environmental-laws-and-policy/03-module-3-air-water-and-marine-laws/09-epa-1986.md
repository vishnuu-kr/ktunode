# Environmental Laws and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water and Marine Laws

## Topic: EPA, 1986 (Environmental Protection Act, 1986)

---

### **Introduction**

The Environmental Protection Act (EPA), 1986, is a landmark legislation in India that provides for the protection and improvement of the environment. It is an umbrella legislation that covers a wide range of environmental issues, including the prevention and control of pollution. This Act was enacted in the wake of the Bhopal Gas Tragedy and aims to provide for the creation of an authority or authorities for the protection and improvement of the environment, prevention of environmental hazards, and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

### **1. Constitutional Basis and Objectives of EPA, 1986**

*   **Constitutional Mandate:**
    *   **Article 48A (Directive Principles of State Policy):** The State shall endeavor to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country.
    *   **Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duties):** It shall be the duty of every citizen of India to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures.
*   **Objectives of the EPA, 1986:**
    *   To provide for the protection and improvement of the environment.
    *   To prevent environmental hazards.
    *   To provide for the creation of an authority or authorities for the protection and improvement of the environment.
    *   To lay down standards for the quality of the environment and for emissions or discharge of environmental pollutants.
    *   To prohibit the location of any industry in any environmental sensitive zone.
    *   To provide for the prevention, control and abatement of environmental pollution.
    *   To provide for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

---

### **2. Powers and Functions of the Central Government**

The Central Government is vested with wide-ranging powers under the EPA, 1986 to implement its objectives.

*   **Powers to Take Measures:**
    *   **Section 3:** The Central Government may take all such measures as it deems necessary or expedient for the purpose of protecting and improving the quality of the environment and preventing, controlling and abating environmental pollution.
    *   **Specific Measures Include:**
        *   Laying down standards for the quality of the environment and for emissions or discharge of environmental pollutants.
        *   Restricting the areas in which any industries, operations or processes or the handling of hazardous substances may be carried out or permitted.
        *   Prohibiting the location of any industry, operation or process or the carrying on of any treatment, storage or disposal of any hazardous substance in any environment sensitive zone.
        *   Laying down procedures and safeguards for the prevention of accidents which may cause environmental pollution and for the remediation of areas affected by such accidents.
        *   Laying down procedures and safeguards for the handling of hazardous substances.
        *   Prohibiting the manufacture, storage and import of such hazardous substances as the Central Government may specify by notification in the Official Gazette.
        *   Providing for the prevention and control of pollution of water and the laying down of standards for water quality.
        *   Providing for the prevention and control of pollution of air and the laying down of standards for air quality.
        *   Providing for the prevention, control and abatement of noise pollution.
        *   Nucleus for co-ordinating environmental protection activities.
*   **Delegation of Powers:**
    *   **Section 3(2):** The Central Government may, by order, delegate to any person or any authority the powers conferred upon it by virtue of Section 3(1), subject to such conditions and restrictions as may be specified in the order. This is crucial for the operationalization of the Act.

### **3. The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)**

The CPCB is the principal regulatory body for pollution control in India.

*   **Establishment and Composition:**
    *   **Section 4:** Constituted under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, but its powers and functions are expanded and codified under the EPA, 1986.
    *   Composed of a Chairman and other members appointed by the Central Government.
*   **Powers and Functions:**
    *   **Section 5 & 6:** The CPCB advises the Central Government on matters relating to the improvement of the quality of the environment and the prevention, control and abatement of environmental pollution.
    *   **Advisory Role:** Advise the Central Government in the formulation of policies and programs for effective prevention, control and abatement of environmental pollution.
    *   **Technical Assistance:** Provide technical assistance and guidance to the SPCBs.
    *   **Co-ordination:** Co-ordinate the activities of the SPCBs.
    *   **Research:** Promote research and development in environmental protection and pollution control.
    *   **Data Collection & Dissemination:** Collect, compile and disseminate environmental data.
    *   **Standards Setting:** Lay down, modify or annul standards for the quality of the environment or for emissions or discharge of environmental pollutants from such sources as the Central Government may specify.
    *   **Planning and Execution:** Plan and cause to be executed a nationwide programme for the prevention, control and abatement of environmental pollution.
    *   **Assessment and Evaluation:** Assess and evaluate the environmental impact of any activity or process or proposed activity or process.

---

### **4. State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) and Pollution Control Committees (PCCs)**

SPCBs are responsible for implementing environmental laws at the state level, while PCCs function in Union Territories without a legislature.

*   **Establishment:**
    *   **Section 4(1) & 4(2):** Established by the State Governments under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, and their powers are extended under EPA, 1986.
    *   In Union Territories, the Central Government establishes Pollution Control Committees (PCCs).
*   **Powers and Functions:**
    *   **Section 17:** SPCBs/PCCs exercise powers and perform functions similar to CPCB within their respective territories, subject to the overall superintendence of CPCB and Central Government.
    *   **Prevention and Control:** Prevent, control and abate environmental pollution.
    *   **Standards Enforcement:** Lay down standards for the quality of the environment and for emissions or discharge of environmental pollutants, subject to standards laid down by CPCB.
    *   **Granting Consents:** Grant consents to industries for discharging effluents and emissions and lay down conditions for the same.
    *   **Inspection and Monitoring:** Inspect industries and other facilities to monitor compliance with environmental regulations.
    *   **Legal Action:** Take legal action against defaulting industries or individuals.
    *   **Awareness Generation:** Promote environmental awareness and education.

### **6. Strict Liability and Absolute Liability**

These legal principles are crucial in environmental law for holding polluters accountable.

*   **Strict Liability:**
    *   **Origin:** English common law principle developed in the case of *Rylands v Fletcher* (1868).
    *   **Principle:** A person who brings onto his land and collects and keeps there anything likely to do mischief if it escapes, and he does so at his own peril, is answerable for all the damage which is the natural consequence of its escape.
    *   **Defense:** The defendant could escape liability by proving "Act of God" or "Act of a stranger."
*   **Absolute Liability:**
    *   **Indian Development:** Developed by the Supreme Court of India in the context of hazardous industries, particularly after the Bhopal Gas Tragedy.
    *   **Case:** *M.C. Mehta v. Union of India* (Oleum Gas Leak Case, 1987).
    *   **Principle:** Where an enterprise is engaged in a hazardous or inherently dangerous activity and harm results therefrom, the enterprise is **absolutely liable** to compensate for the harm, and made no exceptions. The enterprise cannot escape liability by showing that it had taken all reasonable care.
    *   **Key Distinction from Strict Liability:** Absolute liability does not allow for any defenses, making the liability of the polluter more stringent.
*   **Relevance under EPA, 1986:**
    *   While the EPA, 1986 itself doesn't explicitly use the terms "strict liability" or "absolute liability" in its definitions, its penalty provisions and the overall framework for compensation for environmental damage are in line with these principles. The Act empowers authorities to hold polluters liable for damages caused by pollution, regardless of intent or negligence, reflecting the spirit of absolute liability.

### **7. Penalties and Compensation for Environmental Damage**

The Act provides for severe penalties for non-compliance.

*   **Offences and Penalties (Section 14-16):**
    *   **Section 15:**
        *   Whoever fails to comply with or contravenes any of the provisions of this Act or the rules made thereunder, shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years or with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees, or with both.
        *   If failure or contravention continues, imprisonment may extend to seven years.
    *   **Section 16:**
        *   Offences by Companies: If an offence under this Act is committed by a company, every person who, at the time of the offence, was in charge of, and was responsible to, the company for the conduct of the business of the company, as well as the company, shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly.
        *   Liability of Directors, etc.: If an offence is committed by a company and it is proved that it was committed with the consent or connivance of, or was attributable to any negligence on the part of, any director, manager, secretary or other officer of the company, such director, manager, secretary or other officer shall also be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly.
*   **Compensation for Damage to Environment (Section 17):**
    *   The SPCBs/PCCs can claim compensation from any person who causes or is liable for environmental pollution.
    *   This compensation is to be used for the purpose of restoring the environment or for the purpose of remedial measures for environmental protection.
    *   **Case:** *Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India* (1996) - Supreme Court affirmed the principle of absolute liability and ordered the polluter to reimburse the cost of remediation.
*   **Environment Relief Fund:**
    *   The Act also provides for the establishment of an Environment Relief Fund to provide relief to persons affected by accidents caused by hazardous substances.

### **8. Powers of Entry, Inspection, and Sampling of Environmental Pollutants**

These powers are crucial for enforcement.

*   **Powers of Entry and Inspection (Section 19):**
    *   Any person empowered by the Central Government or SPCBs/PCCs has the power to:
        *   Enter any place which is reasonably believed to be in, or to relate to, any premises or any place in which an industry, operation or process or any site for the treatment, storage or disposal of hazardous waste is carried on, or is believed to be carried on.
        *   Inspect any such place.
        *   Inspect any works, machinery, apparatus, documents, records, registers or other things.
        *   Inspect any raw material or substance which is brought into, or is stored in, such place for use in any industry, operation or process or treatment, storage or disposal of hazardous waste.
        *   Inspect any waste or pollutant.
*   **Powers of Sampling (Section 19(2)):**
    *   If such empowered person has reason to believe that any material, substance or any part of any system, process, equipment, or structure in such place is of such a nature as to cause or likely to cause environmental pollution, he may take samples of such material, substance or part or of any emission or discharge from such place.
    *   **Procedure:** The samples must be taken in accordance with the prescribed procedure and the analysis is done by the prescribed laboratory.
    *   **Admissibility:** The report of the analysis is admissible in evidence.

### **9. Public Liability Insurance**

This provision aims to provide immediate relief to victims of environmental accidents.

*   **Mandatory Insurance (Section 16A):**
    *   Every owner (handling hazardous substance) must take out an insurance policy before starting or restarting any industry, operation or process.
    *   The insurance policy must cover liability for damages arising from an accident in the course of operation.
*   **Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991:** The EPA, 1986, paved the way for the enactment of the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991, which specifically deals with mandatory insurance for industries handling hazardous substances.
*   **Objective:** To provide for immediate relief to victims of accidents arising from the handling of hazardous substances, without recourse to lengthy legal battles.
*   **Key Features:**
    *   **No-fault Liability:** The owner is liable irrespective of fault.
    *   **Statutory Minimum Amount:** The insurance policy must be for a minimum statutory amount.
    *   **Environment Relief Fund:** The Act also establishes an Environment Relief Fund, funded by industries, to supplement the insurance amount for victims.

### **10. Role of Citizens and Legal Remedies**

The EPA, 1986, also empowers citizens to participate in environmental protection.

*   **Right to Information:** Citizens have the right to access environmental information related to pollution control.
*   **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** The judiciary has played a significant role in environmental protection through PILs, allowing any public-spirited citizen to approach the courts for redressal of environmental grievances.
*   **Notice for Offences (Section 19(3)):** Before taking legal proceedings against any person for contravention of any provision of this Act, the empowered officer shall give such person a notice in writing of such contravention.
*   **Citizen Suit Provisions (Implied):** While not explicitly detailed as "citizen suit" provisions like in some other countries' environmental laws, the spirit of citizen participation is embedded through the ability to report violations and seek redressal through legal channels, including PILs.

---

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Umbrella Legislation:** EPA, 1986, is a comprehensive and overarching law for environmental protection in India.
*   **Constitutional Backing:** Directly derived from Article 48A and Article 51A(g) of the Constitution.
*   **Central Government's Role:** Broad powers to take measures to protect and improve the environment.
*   **CPCB & SPCBs:** Key regulatory bodies for pollution control and standard enforcement.
*   **Absolute Liability:** The principle of absolute liability for hazardous activities is central to environmental jurisprudence in India, stemming from Supreme Court judgments and supported by the EPA's framework.
*   **Strict Prohibition:** The Act strictly prohibits exceeding prescribed environmental quality standards.
*   **Penalties:** Substantial penalties, including imprisonment, are prescribed for violations.
*   **Public Liability Insurance:** Mandatory insurance for industries handling hazardous substances for immediate victim relief.
*   **Citizen Empowerment:** Citizens play a vital role through information access and legal recourse (PILs).

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

**A1.** The constitutional basis for environmental protection in India is found in Article 48A (Directive Principles of State Policy), which mandates the State to protect and improve the environment, and Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duties), which makes it the duty of every citizen to protect the natural environment. The EPA, 1986, is an umbrella legislation that translates these constitutional mandates into actionable legal provisions, establishing authorities, laying down standards, and prescribing penalties for environmental protection and pollution control.

**A2.** Under EPA, 1986, the Central Government has broad powers to take all necessary measures for protecting and improving the environment and preventing pollution. This includes laying down standards for environmental quality, restricting industries in sensitive zones, prohibiting hazardous substances, providing procedures for accident prevention and remediation, and coordinating environmental activities. The government can also delegate these powers to other authorities.

**A3.** The CPCB is the apex body responsible for advising the Central Government, coordinating with SPCBs, promoting research, collecting data, and setting national standards for pollution. SPCBs are established at the state level (and PCCs in UTs) to implement the Act's provisions within their respective jurisdictions. Their functions include preventing, controlling, and abating pollution, enforcing standards, granting consents to industries, and taking legal action against violators.

**A4.**
*   **Strict Liability (Rylands v Fletcher):** Liability for escape of dangerous things from one's land, with defenses like "Act of God" or "Act of a stranger."
*   **Absolute Liability (M.C. Mehta v. Union of India):** Developed in India for hazardous activities, it imposes liability on the enterprise for harm caused by such activities **without** allowing any exceptions or defenses.
*   **Relevance to EPA, 1986:** The EPA, 1986, through its penalty and compensation provisions, reflects the spirit of absolute liability by holding polluters accountable for environmental damage, often without requiring proof of negligence.

**A5.** Section 15 of EPA, 1986, prescribes imprisonment up to five years or a fine up to ₹1 lakh, or both, for failing to comply with the Act. If the contravention continues, the imprisonment can extend to seven years. Section 16 addresses offences by companies, making the company and responsible individuals liable. Directors or officers can also be held liable if the offence was committed with their consent, connivance, or negligence.

**A6.** The mandatory Public Liability Insurance, as outlined in Section 16A, requires owners handling hazardous substances to obtain an insurance policy. Its purpose is to provide immediate relief and compensation to victims of accidents arising from the handling of hazardous substances, aiming to bypass lengthy legal procedures and ensure prompt succour. This provision was further strengthened by the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991.

**A7.** Empowered officers have the authority to enter and inspect any premises or place where industries, operations, processes, or hazardous waste management occurs. They can examine works, machinery, documents, and raw materials. If they suspect pollution, they can take samples of emissions, discharges, or materials, which are then analyzed and their reports are admissible as evidence in legal proceedings.

**A8.** The EPA, 1986, empowers citizens by providing them with the right to access environmental information. Furthermore, the judicial system, through the mechanism of Public Interest Litigation (PIL), allows any public-spirited citizen to approach the courts to seek redressal for environmental grievances, holding polluters and authorities accountable. The provision of a notice before initiating proceedings also gives an opportunity for rectification.
