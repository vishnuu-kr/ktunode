# Environmental Laws and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water and Marine Laws

## Topic: Water Cess Act, 1977 & Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

This module delves into two pivotal pieces of Indian environmental legislation: the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977, and the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. Understanding these acts is crucial for comprehending India's approach to water pollution control and broader environmental management.

---

### I. The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977

**Objective:** To provide for the imposition and collection of a cess on water consumed by persons carrying on any industry and by local authorities with the primary objective of increasing the resources of the Central Government and the State Governments to promote the activities of preventing and controlling water pollution.

---

#### **A. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Cess:** A tax levied by the government for a specific purpose. In this context, it's a tax on water consumption.
*   **Water Resource:** Refers to water supplied by a State Government or any other local authority, or water supplied by any other person which is supplied to the consumer from any source, whether private or public.
*   **Consuming:** Includes supplying water, or using water, or both.
*   **Schedule I:** Lists the industries to which the Act applies and the respective rates of cess.
*   **Schedule II:** Lists the local authorities to which the Act applies and the respective rates of cess.
*   **Industrial Consumer:** Any person carrying on any industry specified in Schedule I and consuming water.
*   **Local Authority:** Any municipality, town area committee, port trust or any other authority constituted under any law for the time being in force for the conservation, improvement and regulation of water supply.
*   **Pollution:** As defined under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974. (It's important to note that this Act draws its definition from the parent Act for water pollution.)

---

#### **B. Salient Features and Provisions**

1.  **Levy of Cess:**
    *   A cess is levied on water consumed by:
        *   **Industrial Consumers:** Based on the type of industry and the quantity of water consumed.
        *   **Local Authorities:** Based on the population of the area they serve and the quantity of water supplied.

2.  **Rates of Cess:**
    *   The rates are specified in the Schedules and are subject to revision by the Central Government.
    *   **Industrial Consumers (Schedule I):**
        *   **General Rate:** Initially, a general rate of 2 paise per thousand litres.
        *   **Higher Rates:** For industries that consume water and discharge effluents which do not conform to the prescribed standards, the cess can be increased. This acts as a disincentive for pollution.
        *   **Rebate:** A rebate of cess is provided if the industrial consumer effectively treats the sewage or trade effluent discharged from their premises to conform to the prescribed standards. The rebate is a significant incentive for pollution control.
    *   **Local Authorities (Schedule II):**
        *   Rates are generally lower than for industrial consumers and are linked to the scale of their operations.

3.  **Collection of Cess:**
    *   The State Governments are responsible for assessing and collecting the cess.
    *   A portion of the collected cess can be retained by the State Governments for their expenses related to water pollution control activities. The remainder is to be paid to the Central Government.

4.  **Purpose of Cess Collection:**
    *   To augment the financial resources of the Central and State Governments for activities related to the prevention and control of water pollution.
    *   To fund activities such as setting up and operating Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs), Effluent Treatment Plants (ETPs), monitoring water quality, and research.

5.  **Penalties:**
    *   Failure to pay the cess on time can lead to penalties, including interest and fines.

---

#### **C. Importance and Impact**

*   **Financial Mechanism:** Provides a dedicated financial resource for water pollution control initiatives.
*   **Polluter Pays Principle:** Implicitly promotes the polluter pays principle by charging for water consumption and offering incentives (rebates) for pollution control.
*   **Behavioral Change:** Encourages industries to conserve water and invest in wastewater treatment by making untreated discharge more expensive.

---

#### **D. Examples**

*   **Scenario 1: Textile Industry:** A textile factory consumes 50,000 litres of water per day. If the prescribed cess rate for textile industries is 5 paise per thousand litres, the daily cess would be 50,000/1000 * 5 = 250 paise or ₹2.50. If the factory's effluent meets prescribed standards after treatment, it may be eligible for a rebate.
*   **Scenario 2: Municipal Corporation:** A municipal corporation supplies water to a city with a population of 5 lakhs. The cess levied on the corporation would be based on the volume of water supplied and the prescribed rate for local authorities. This collected cess would then contribute to funds for maintaining water supply and sanitation infrastructure.

---

#### **E. Important Points to Remember**

*   The Water Cess Act, 1977, is primarily a **fiscal measure** to finance water pollution control.
*   It is **complementary** to the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, which lays down the regulatory framework.
*   The **rebate provision** is a key incentive for industries to adopt pollution control measures.
*   The Act applies to **specified industries** and **local authorities**.

### II. The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (EPA, 1986)

**Objective:** To provide for the protection and improvement of the environment and for the prevention of hazards to human beings, other living creatures, plants, and property. It is an **umbrella legislation** that provides a framework for the Central Government to coordinate environmental protection activities.

---

#### **A. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Environment:** Includes water, air and land and the inter-relationship which exists between water, air and land and human beings, other living creatures, plants, micro-organism and property. (This is a broad and inclusive definition.)
*   **Environmental Pollutant:** Any solid, liquid or gaseous substance with such characteristics and in such concentration as tends to or is likely to cause pollution.
*   **Pollution:** The presence in the environment of any environmental pollutant.
*   **Hazardous Substance:** Any substance which, by reason of being explosive, flammable, poisonous, corrosive, readily combustible, reactive or toxic, or by reason of any other characteristic, is likely to cause harm to human beings, other living creatures, plants, property or the environment.
*   **Occupier:** In relation to any factory or premises, means the person who has control over the affairs of the factory or premises.
*   **Forest:** Includes any area declared as forest by the Central Government or State Government or any recognised forest under any law for the time being in force.
*   **Animal:** Means any animal, bird or insect, and includes any aquatic or land animal, whether wild or tame.
*   **Plant:** Means any species of plant or tree, and includes any produce of that species.

---

#### **B. Salient Features and Provisions**

1.  **Broad Objectives:** To protect and improve the environment and prevent environmental hazards.

2.  **Powers of the Central Government:** This is the cornerstone of the Act. It grants extensive powers to the Central Government to:
    *   **Lay down standards:** For the quality of the environment and for the prevention, control and abatement of environmental pollution. These standards can be for different areas, different sources of pollution, and different types of pollutants.
    *   **Regulate industrial activities:** Specify location of industries, restrict industrial activities in certain areas, and prohibit certain activities.
    *   **Lay down procedures and safeguards:** For the prevention of accidents which may cause environmental pollution and for remedial measures in respect of such accidents.
    *   **Lay down procedures and safeguards:** For the handling of hazardous substances.
    *   **Carry out and sponsor investigations and research:** Relating to problems of environmental pollution.
    *   **Collect and publish information:** Relating to environmental quality, pollution and its control.
    *   **Make rules:** For carrying out the purposes of the Act.
    *   **Establish authorities:** For the purpose of preventing, controlling and abating environmental pollution.

3.  **Environmental Protection Authorities:**
    *   The Central Government can appoint authorities, either by notification in the Official Gazette, for a specified area or for specified purposes.
    *   These authorities have powers to:
        *   Take measures to protect and improve the environment.
        *   Prevent and control environmental pollution.
        *   Prevent and control environmental hazards.
        *   Issue directions to any person or officer to take such measures or to prevent or control environmental pollution.
        *   Enter any premises, inspect any plant, machinery, etc., for the purpose of discharging their functions.

4.  **Prevention, Control and Abatement of Pollution:**
    *   The Central Government can issue directions to any person, officer, or authority to take measures for preventing, controlling, or abating environmental pollution.
    *   These directions can include:
        *   Closing, prohibiting, or regulating any industry or operation.
        *   Regulating the supply of electricity, water, or any other utility to any industry or operation.
        *   Prohibiting or regulating the discharge of any environmental pollutant.

5.  **Handling of Hazardous Substances:**
    *   The Act provides for the regulation of the manufacturing, storage, and handling of hazardous substances.
    *   Specific rules have been framed under the EPA, 1986, for hazardous waste management, chemical accidents, etc.

6.  **Penalties:**
    *   **General Penalty:** Any person who fails to comply with or contravenes any provisions of the Act, or any rules, orders, or directions issued thereunder, shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years or with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees, or with both.
    *   **Continuing Offence:** If the failure or contravention continues, an additional fine of up to five thousand rupees for every day during which such failure or contravention continues may be imposed.
    *   **Corporate Liability:** If the contravention is done by a company, the company itself and every person in charge of and responsible to the company for the conduct of its business at the time of the contravention shall be liable.

7.  **Power to enter and inspect:**
    *   Any person empowered in this behalf by the Central Government or any authority appointed under the Act has the power to enter any premises, inspect any plant, machinery, equipment, manufacturing process, record, document, or any substance for the purpose of performing their functions.

---

#### **C. Importance and Impact**

*   **Umbrella Legislation:** Provides a broad framework for environmental protection, allowing the Central Government to address various environmental issues by framing specific rules and regulations.
*   **Centralized Authority:** Empowers the Central Government to take decisive action on environmental matters, overriding state-level inaction or conflicting policies.
*   **Wide Scope:** Covers a vast range of environmental issues, including pollution, hazardous substances, and eco-sensitive zones.
*   **Enforcement Mechanism:** Provides for penalties and legal recourse for environmental violations.
*   **Foundation for Rules:** Many important environmental rules, such as the Hazardous Waste Management Rules, EIA Notification, Plastic Waste Management Rules, etc., are framed under the EPA, 1986.

---

#### **D. Examples**

*   **Scenario 1: EIA Notification (2006, amended):** Under the EPA, 1986, the Environment Impact Assessment (EIA) Notification was issued, making it mandatory for certain developmental projects to obtain prior environmental clearance from the government based on an assessment of their potential environmental impact. This is a direct application of the Act's power to regulate industrial activities and lay down procedures.
*   **Scenario 2: Hazardous Waste Management Rules:** The Hazardous Wastes (Management, Handling and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016 (framed under EPA, 1986) provide detailed guidelines for the environmentally sound management of hazardous waste generated by industries, from its generation to its disposal.
*   **Scenario 3: Ganga Action Plan/National River Conservation Plan:** While specific Acts exist for river pollution, the overarching framework for cleaning rivers and lakes, setting discharge standards for industries, and monitoring water quality often finds its legal basis under the EPA, 1986, which empowers the government to take such measures.
*   **Scenario 4: Eco-Sensitive Zones:** The EPA, 1986, has been used to declare Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZs) around protected areas like National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries to regulate certain activities that could harm the environment.

---

#### **E. Important Points to Remember**

*   The EPA, 1986, is a **comprehensive and enabling legislation**.
*   It grants **extensive powers** to the Central Government to protect and improve the environment.
*   It acts as an **umbrella legislation**, providing the framework for issuing numerous specific rules and notifications.
*   The Act emphasizes **prevention, control, and abatement of pollution** and **safe handling of hazardous substances**.
*   **Penalties are stringent**, including imprisonment and fines.
*   The Act allows for the establishment of **environmental protection authorities**.
