# Environmental Laws and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water and Marine Laws

## Topic: Water Act, 1974

---

### **1. Introduction to the Water Act, 1974**

*   **Purpose:** The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, is a landmark legislation enacted in India to prevent and control water pollution and to maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water for domestic, industrial, and other legitimate uses.
*   **Historical Context:** Before this Act, there was no comprehensive legislation specifically dealing with water pollution. This Act was introduced to address the growing problem of water contamination due to industrialization and urbanization.
*   **Scope:** It extends to the entire country, including the State of Jammu and Kashmir.
*   **Key Objective:** To establish a framework for the prevention, control, and abatement of water pollution.

---

### **2. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Water:** Includes drinking water, lake, river, stream, well, pond, channel, sea, inland water or any other liquid, whether in its natural or artificial form.
*   **Pollution:** Means such discharge of any **sewage** or **trade effluent** or of any other liquid, gaseous or solid substance into any **stream** or **well** or **sewer** or on land as may or is likely to create a **nuisance** to the public or render such **stream** or **well** or **sewer** or land **unwholesome** to the public for their use or deprive any of the inhabitants of such water or land of any public right thereto.
    *   **Sewage:** Means any substance, liquid or solid, which consists wholly or in part of or is contaminated by, human excreta or discharge of water from any dwelling, building, farm, stable or premise in which human beings or animals are housed or kept or in which any industrial or trade process is carried on.
    *   **Trade Effluent:** Means any discharged substance, liquid or solid, which is discharged from any premises used for carrying on any **industry** or **trade** and includes any such substance which is discharged from the premises where the **process of crushing, grinding or any other processing of minerals** or any **alloy** thereof is carried on.
    *   **Stream:** Includes –
        *   A river, whether flowing or not.
        *   A creek.
        *   A canal.
        *   A lake or other natural collection of water, whether flowing or not.
        *   A subterranean stream.
        *   A sea, tidal waters, estuaries, sea-bed, and sub-soil of such waters, or the bed and sub-soil below the high water mark.
    *   **Well:** Includes any natural or artificial source of water, conduit to or from any spring, or any excavation made or being made to obtain or use underground water.
    *   **Industry:** Means any operation or process for manufacturing, producing, treating, processing, or developing any article or substance or part thereof or any assembly of parts, and includes mining, quarrying, fishing, agriculture, aquaculture, and any other operation or process declared by the State Government, by notification in the Official Gazette, to be an industry for the purposes of this Act.
    *   **Nuisance:** An inconvenience or annoyance that interferes with the rights of others.
    *   **Unwholesome:** Unfit for human consumption or use.
*   **Board:** Refers to the Central Board (CPCB) or the State Board (SPCB) as the case may be.

---

### **3. Objectives and Salient Features of the Water Act, 1974**

*   **Prevention and Control of Water Pollution:** The primary objective.
*   **Establishment of Boards:**
    *   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):** Constituted under the Act for coordinating State Boards, advising the Central Government, laying down standards, and carrying out other functions.
    *   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):** Constituted in each State for the prevention and control of water pollution in that State.
*   **Powers and Functions of Boards:**
    *   **Advisory:** Advise the State Government on matters related to water pollution.
    *   **Standards Setting:** Lay down, modify or annul water quality standards.
    *   **Facilitating and Securing:** Obtain information, conduct investigations, provide advice, collect and disseminate information on water pollution.
    *   **Assistance:** Assist State Governments, local authorities, and other persons in their efforts to prevent and control water pollution.
    *   **Training and Education:** Organize training programmes for people working in water pollution control.
    *   **Research:** Sponsor or undertake studies and investigations on problems connected with water pollution.
*   **Prevention and Control Measures:**
    *   **Prohibition of Discharge of Polluting Matter:** Section 24 prohibits the discharge of polluting matter into streams or wells.
        *   *Example:* A factory cannot discharge untreated industrial waste into a river.
    *   **Restriction on Use of Streams for Disposal of Polluting Matter:** Section 25 restricts the use of streams for discharge of polluting matter **without the previous consent** of the State Board.
        *   *Requirements for Consent:* Applications for consent must be submitted to the SPCB with necessary details. Consent can be granted subject to certain conditions.
    *   **Appeals:** An aggrieved person can appeal to the Appellate Authority against the decision of the State Board.
*   **Reconstitution of Existing Boards:** The Act provides for the reconstitution of existing water pollution prevention boards established under state legislation.
*   **Joint Boards:** Provision for establishing joint boards for two or more States for efficient water pollution control.
*   **Offences and Penalties:**
    *   **Section 41:** Penalties for contravention of provisions like discharging polluting matter without consent.
        *   *First Conviction:* Imprisonment for a term not less than one year and six months which may extend to six years and with fine.
        *   *Subsequent Conviction:* Imprisonment for a term not less than two years which may extend to seven years and with fine.
    *   **Section 42:** Penalty for failure to furnish information.
    *   **Section 43:** Penalty for obstruction.
*   **Procedure for Complaint:** Any person can file a complaint of pollution to the State Board or the Central Government, and if no action is taken, they can file a complaint to a Magistrate after giving notice.
*   **Power to Obtain Information:** The State Board has the power to obtain necessary information regarding discharge of pollutants.
*   **Power of Entry and Inspection:** Authorized officers can enter any place for inspection and sampling.
*   **Water Quality Standards:** The Act enables the establishment of water quality standards for different uses of water bodies.

---

### **4. Structure of the Water Act, 1974**

The Act is divided into several Chapters and Sections:

*   **Chapter I: Preliminary** (Sections 1-2) - Deals with short title, extent, commencement, and definitions.
*   **Chapter II: Establishment of Boards and their Powers and Functions** (Sections 3-16) - Covers the constitution of CPCB and SPCBs, their powers and functions.
*   **Chapter III: Joint Boards** (Sections 17-19) - Provisions for joint boards.
*   **Chapter IV: Powers to Obtain Information, etc.** (Sections 20-23) - Powers of the Board to gather information, enter premises, and take samples.
*   **Chapter V: Prevention and Control of Water Pollution** (Sections 24-32) - Core provisions on prohibiting discharge of polluting matter, obtaining consent for discharge, and setting standards.
*   **Chapter VI: Financial Provisions** (Sections 33-37) - Deals with budgets, accounts, and audit of the Boards.
*   **Chapter VII: Penalties and Procedure** (Sections 38-49) - Outlines offences, penalties, prosecution procedures, and powers of courts.
*   **Chapter VIII: Miscellaneous** (Sections 50-64) - Covers various miscellaneous provisions like protection of action taken, power to make rules, etc.

### **5. Role and Functions of CPCB and SPCBs**

**Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):**

*   **National Level Apex Body:** Responsible for the overall environmental management in India.
*   **Coordination:** Coordinates the activities of SPCBs and resolves disputes between them.
*   **Advisory:** Advises the Central Government on matters concerning prevention and control of water pollution.
*   **Standards:** Lays down, modifies, or annul water quality standards.
*   **Research:** Sponsors and conducts research on water pollution.
*   **Information Dissemination:** Collects and disseminates information on water pollution and its prevention.
*   **Training:** Organizes training programs.
*   **National Water Quality Monitoring:** Manages the National Water Quality Monitoring Programme.

**State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):**

*   **State Level Implementation Agencies:** Responsible for implementing the Act in their respective states.
*   **Consent Management:** Grants or refuses consent for discharge of sewage or trade effluent into water bodies.
*   **Monitoring:** Monitors water quality and the performance of pollution control measures.
*   **Enforcement:** Enforces the provisions of the Act and takes action against violators.
*   **Advisory:** Advises the State Government on matters relating to water pollution.
*   **Public Awareness:** Undertakes public awareness programs.
*   **Investigation:** Investigates complaints regarding water pollution.

---

### **6. Compliance and Enforcement**

*   **Consent Mechanism:** Industries and local authorities must obtain consent from the SPCB before discharging any sewage or trade effluent.
*   **Monitoring and Inspection:** SPCBs conduct regular monitoring and inspections to ensure compliance.
*   **Penalties:** Stringent penalties are prescribed for violations, including imprisonment and fines.
*   **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** The Act's provisions can be invoked through PILs to address water pollution issues.
*   **Citizen Suits:** Section 49 allows any person to file a complaint with a Magistrate after giving 60 days' notice to the Board and the alleged offender, if the Board fails to take action.

---

### **7. Amendments and Developments**

*   The Water Act, 1974, has been amended from time to time to strengthen its provisions and address emerging challenges.
*   The **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**, also plays a crucial role in environmental management, and its provisions complement the Water Act.
*   Introduction of concepts like **Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD)** for highly polluting industries.

---

### **8. Important Points to Remember**

*   **"Pollution" definition is broad:** It covers any discharge that creates a nuisance or renders water unwholesome.
*   **Consent is mandatory:** No discharge of sewage or trade effluent into water bodies is allowed without prior consent from the SPCB.
*   **Boards are key:** CPCB and SPCBs are the central authorities for prevention and control.
*   **Strict penalties:** Violations of the Act lead to severe legal consequences.
*   **Citizen participation:** The Act empowers citizens to report pollution and seek redressal.
*   **Holistic approach:** The Act aims to protect water bodies for various public uses.

### **10. Answers to Practice Questions**

1.  **Pollution:** Defined as any discharge of sewage or trade effluent or any other liquid, gaseous or solid substance into any stream or well or sewer or on land that may or is likely to create a nuisance to the public or render such stream or well or sewer or land unwholesome to the public for their use or deprive any of the inhabitants of such water or land of any public right thereto.
    **Trade Effluent:** Defined as any discharged substance, liquid or solid, which is discharged from any premises used for carrying on any industry or trade and includes any such substance which is discharged from the premises where the process of crushing, grinding or any other processing of minerals or any alloy thereof is carried on.
2.  The primary objectives of the Water Act, 1974, are to prevent and control water pollution and to maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water for domestic, industrial, and other legitimate uses.
3.  **CPCB:** Acts as the apex body for coordinating SPCBs, advising the Central Government, setting national water quality standards, and conducting research.
    **SPCB:** Is the state-level authority responsible for implementing the Act, granting consents for discharge, monitoring water quality, enforcing compliance, and advising the State Government.
4.  A factory can discharge trade effluent into a river only after obtaining the **previous consent** of the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB). The SPCB will grant consent subject to specific conditions, which may include pre-treatment of the effluent to meet prescribed standards before discharge.
5.  Section 24 prohibits the discharge of any polluting matter into streams or wells, making it illegal to pollute water bodies directly. This section forms the backbone of the Act's preventive measures.
6.  Contravention of provisions like discharging polluting matter without consent (Section 25) can lead to imprisonment for a term not less than one year and six months which may extend to six years and with fine for the first conviction. Subsequent convictions attract a term not less than two years and may extend to seven years with fine. Other sections also prescribe penalties for specific failures or obstructions.
7.  Under Section 49 of the Water Act, 1974, if the SPCB fails to take action within 60 days of receiving a complaint about pollution, any person can file a complaint before a Magistrate. This provision empowers citizens to act as watchdogs and ensures accountability.
