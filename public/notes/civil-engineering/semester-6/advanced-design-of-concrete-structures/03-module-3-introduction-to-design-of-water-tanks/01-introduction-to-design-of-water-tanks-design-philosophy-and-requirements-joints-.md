# Advanced Design of Concrete Structures: Module 3 - Introduction to Design of Water Tanks

This module provides a comprehensive introduction to the design of water tanks, focusing on the underlying principles, essential requirements, common joints, relevant IS code recommendations, and the practical application of IS 3370-2009 for designing both rectangular and circular water tanks.

---

## Learning Outcomes:

Upon completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   Understand the fundamental design philosophy for water tanks.
*   Identify and explain the essential design requirements for water tanks.
*   Recognize and describe common types of joints used in water tanks and their design considerations.
*   Recall and apply relevant IS code recommendations for water tank design.
*   Perform the design of rectangular and circular water tanks using the coefficients provided in IS 3370-2009.

## 1. Design Philosophy for Water Tanks

The design of water tanks differs significantly from ordinary building structures due to the presence of water, which exerts hydrostatic pressure and requires watertightness.

### Key Concepts:

*   **Watertightness:** This is the primary objective. The structure must prevent leakage of stored water and ingress of external water.
*   **Durability:** Water tanks are subjected to continuous exposure to water, often with dissolved chemicals. This necessitates careful material selection and crack prevention.
*   **Structural Integrity:** The tank must safely withstand all applied loads, including the weight of water, the tank itself, soil pressure (if underground), wind, seismic forces, and temperature variations.
*   **Serviceability:** The tank should remain functional throughout its lifespan, minimizing deformations and cracking that could compromise its watertightness or structural performance.

### Design Approaches:

*   **Design as a Rigid Structure:** This approach considers the overall rigidity of the tank and its components, assuming they behave elastically.
*   **Design as a Flexible Structure:** This approach acknowledges the flexibility of certain tank components and allows for controlled cracking. The focus is on ensuring that cracks are fine enough to maintain watertightness.

**IS 3370-2009 generally adopts a semi-rigid or flexible design approach, allowing for controlled cracking.** This is often more economical and practical for large tanks.

## 2. Design Requirements for Water Tanks

Water tanks must meet specific functional and structural requirements.

### Key Requirements:

*   **Watertightness:**
    *   Achieved through proper concrete mix design, reinforcement detailing, and effective joint construction.
    *   Minimizing cracks is crucial. Cracks up to a certain width are permitted if they are properly controlled.
*   **Strength:**
    *   The structure must be able to resist all anticipated loads with adequate safety factors.
    *   Loads include:
        *   **Self-weight of the tank:** Concrete, reinforcement, finishes.
        *   **Hydrostatic Pressure:** Acts outwards on walls and upwards on the base. This is the dominant load.
        *   **Superimposed Loads:** Live load on the roof, equipment, personnel.
        *   **External Loads:** Soil pressure, surcharge, wind pressure, seismic forces.
        *   **Temperature Variations:** Can induce stresses and deformations.
*   **Stability:**
    *   The tank must be stable against overturning and sliding, especially for elevated tanks.
    *   Foundation design is critical to ensure stability against bearing capacity failure and differential settlement.
*   **Durability:**
    *   Use of durable concrete (low water-cement ratio, appropriate aggregates).
    *   Adequate concrete cover to reinforcement to protect against corrosion.
    *   Consideration of water aggressiveness (e.g., presence of sulphates, chlorides).
*   **Crack Control:**
    *   Limiting crack widths is paramount for watertightness.
    *   Reinforcement detailing plays a vital role in crack distribution and width control.
    *   IS 3370-2009 specifies maximum allowable crack widths.

### IS Code Recommendations (General):

*   **IS 3370 (Parts 1-4):** This is the primary Indian Standard for Concrete Structures for Containing Liquids.
    *   **Part 1:** General clauses and classification.
    *   **Part 2:** Methods of design and construction for reinforced concrete structures.
    *   **Part 3:** Construction of plain concrete and reinforced concrete structures.
    *   **Part 4:** Design and construction of water tanks on foundation. (Note: While this part exists, the design of tanks on foundations is often integrated with the general reinforced concrete design principles, and IS 3370-2009 (Parts 1, 2, and 4 are key for comprehensive understanding).
*   **Concrete Mix:**
    *   Minimum grade of concrete is generally M20 for direct contact with water and M15 for others (as per IS 3370-2009). Higher grades might be required based on durability and strength requirements.
    *   Low water-cement ratio (0.45 to 0.50) is recommended for watertightness.
    *   Admixtures might be used to improve workability and watertightness.
*   **Reinforcement:**
    *   Mild steel or high-yield strength deformed bars can be used.
    *   Minimum steel percentage is specified to control cracking.
    *   Adequate cover to reinforcement is essential.

## 3. Joints in Water Tanks

Joints are critical elements in water tank construction, as they are potential leakage points. Proper design and execution are vital.

### Types of Joints:

*   **Construction Joints:**
    *   Formed when concreting is stopped and then resumed.
    *   Typically occur between vertical walls and the base slab, or between different lifts of walls.
    *   **Design Considerations:**
        *   **Keying:** Provision of a groove (key) to ensure shear transfer and prevent differential movement.
        *   **Waterstops:** Embedded flexible materials (rubber, PVC, bentonite) that span the joint and create a barrier against water leakage.
        *   **Reinforcement Continuity:** Ensuring reinforcement passes through the joint to maintain structural integrity.
*   **Movement Joints (Expansion/Contraction Joints):**
    *   Used to accommodate thermal expansion and contraction, and shrinkage of concrete.
    *   Allow for controlled movement without inducing excessive stresses.
    *   **Design Considerations:**
        *   **Sealants:** Flexible, watertight materials (e.g., polysulphide sealants) are used to seal the gap.
        *   **Waterstops:** Essential for watertightness across movement joints. Often a combination of a continuous waterstop and a joint filler is used.
        *   **Arrangement:** Placed at regular intervals or at points where significant movement is anticipated.
*   **Control Joints (Contraction Joints):**
    *   Intentionally created cracks in concrete to control the location of natural shrinkage cracks.
    *   Not typically used for primary watertightness in water tanks unless combined with other sealing measures.

### IS Code Recommendations for Joints:

*   **IS 3370-2009:**
    *   **Section 4:** Deals with Construction and Joints.
    *   **Watertightness:** Emphasizes the need for effective joint treatment.
    *   **Waterstops:** Recommends the use of appropriate waterstops made of rubber or PVC. The type and profile of the waterstop depend on the joint type and expected movement.
    *   **Joint Sealants:** Recommends the use of durable, flexible, and water-resistant sealants for movement joints.
    *   **Construction Joints:** Shall be adequately bonded and, where necessary, provided with keys and waterstops.
    *   **Movement Joints:** Shall be designed to accommodate the expected movement and sealed with suitable waterstops and sealants.

## 4. IS Code Recommendations (IS 3370-2009) - Specifics

IS 3370-2009 provides detailed guidelines for the design and construction of water retaining structures.

### Key Provisions:

*   **Permissible Stresses:** Specifies allowable stresses for concrete and steel, which are generally lower than for ordinary building structures to account for the continuous exposure to water and the need for crack control.
*   **Reinforcement Requirements:**
    *   **Minimum Reinforcement:** Minimum percentage of reinforcement is specified for each face of a member to control cracking.
    *   **Distribution Reinforcement:** Ensures uniform distribution of stresses and cracks.
    *   **Reinforcement Spacing:** Limits on the maximum spacing of reinforcement bars to effectively control crack widths.
*   **Crack Width Limits:**
    *   **Part 2 of IS 3370-2009** specifies maximum allowable crack widths for different exposure conditions and structural components. For direct contact with water, crack widths are generally limited to 0.1 mm.
*   **Design of Walls:**
    *   Walls are typically designed as cantilever or R.C. frame members, depending on their support conditions.
    *   **Free Edge Condition:** For walls supported on a base slab but free at the top, they act as cantilevers.
    *   **Supported Edges:** For tanks with rigid roofs, the walls might be continuous over the roof.
    *   **Hydrostatic Pressure:** Uniformly distributed pressure varying with depth.
*   **Design of Slabs (Base and Roof):**
    *   **Base Slabs:** Can be designed as one-way or two-way slabs, depending on the support conditions and dimensions. They resist upward hydrostatic pressure (if below ground), downward loads, and sometimes also internal hydrostatic pressure.
    *   **Roof Slabs:** Designed to resist superimposed loads and potentially wind/seismic forces. They also contribute to the structural integrity of the tank.
*   **Coefficients for Design:**
    *   IS 3370-2009 provides tabulated coefficients that simplify the calculation of bending moments, shear forces, and other design parameters for common tank geometries and support conditions. These coefficients are derived from structural analysis.

## 5. Design of Rectangular Water Tanks using IS Code Coefficients

Rectangular tanks are commonly used for smaller capacities or where space constraints dictate their shape.

### Design Steps (General):

1.  **Determine Tank Dimensions and Capacity:** Based on requirements.
2.  **Classify the Tank:** Based on its location (e.g., on ground, elevated, underground) and exposure conditions as per IS 3370.
3.  **Determine Loads:** Hydrostatic pressure, self-weight, earth pressure, surcharge, wind, seismic loads.
4.  **Design of Walls:**
    *   **Cantilever Walls:** Walls supported at the base slab and free at the top.
    *   **Supported Walls:** Walls supported at the base and the roof slab.
    *   Calculate maximum bending moments and shear forces using IS code coefficients for different support conditions.
    *   Determine the required reinforcement (tension and shear) based on these forces and the permissible stresses.
    *   Check for crack control by ensuring adequate distribution reinforcement.
5.  **Design of Base Slab:**
    *   **For Tanks on Ground:** The base slab resists the outward pressure of water and the inward pressure of soil (if underground). It is usually designed as a slab resting on the ground, or as a rigid base if it needs to bridge over soft patches.
    *   **For Tanks on Columns (Elevated):** The base slab acts as a foundation and needs to transfer loads to the supporting columns.
    *   Calculate bending moments and shear forces based on the hydrostatic pressure and support conditions. Use IS code coefficients for slab design.
    *   Provide reinforcement accordingly.
6.  **Design of Roof Slab:**
    *   Designed as a slab, typically one-way or two-way, depending on its support and spans.
    *   Resists superimposed loads and can act as a bracing element for the walls.
7.  **Joint Design:** Design joints (construction and movement) with appropriate waterstops and sealants.
8.  **Detailing:** Provide proper reinforcement detailing, cover, and laps.

### IS Code Coefficients for Rectangular Walls:

IS 3370-2009 (Part 2) provides coefficients to determine the bending moments in the walls of rectangular tanks. These coefficients depend on the aspect ratio of the wall (length of the supported edge to the height) and the support conditions.

**Example Scenario:** A rectangular tank with walls supported at the base and the roof.

*   **Load:** Hydrostatic pressure ($p = wh$, where $w$ is the unit weight of water and $h$ is the depth).
*   **Wall Analysis:** The wall can be analyzed as a continuous slab with fixed supports at the base and top, subjected to hydrostatic pressure.
*   **Coefficients:** The code provides coefficients ($k_1, k_2, k_3$) for maximum moments at the top, middle, and base of the wall, and for shear forces. These coefficients are typically tabulated based on the ratio of the horizontal span to the vertical height.

**Simplified Illustration (Conceptual):**

Imagine a wall of height $H$ and horizontal span $L$. The hydrostatic pressure increases with depth.

*   **Maximum Moment:** Will generally occur near the supports (base and top) and at mid-height, depending on the relative stiffness and span.
*   **Shear Force:** Maximum shear will occur at the base and top supports.

The coefficients in IS 3370-2009 are derived from elastic analysis and are used to quickly calculate these critical design forces. For instance, the maximum bending moment ($M$) might be calculated as $M = k \cdot p \cdot H^2$, where $p$ is the maximum hydrostatic pressure and $k$ is a coefficient from the code.

## 6. Design of Circular Water Tanks using IS Code Coefficients

Circular tanks are generally more economical for larger capacities due to better distribution of stresses.

### Design Steps (General):

1.  **Determine Tank Dimensions and Capacity:** Diameter and height of the tank.
2.  **Classify the Tank:** Similar to rectangular tanks.
3.  **Determine Loads:** Hydrostatic pressure, self-weight, etc.
4.  **Design of Walls:**
    *   **Hoop Tension:** The primary stress in the cylindrical wall due to internal hydrostatic pressure is hoop tension. This is calculated using the formula $T = \frac{pD}{2}$, where $p$ is the hydrostatic pressure at a given depth and $D$ is the diameter.
    *   **Bending Moments:** While hoop tension is dominant, bending moments can also exist, especially at the base and top of the wall due to:
        *   Support conditions at the base (fixed or hinged).
        *   Connection to the roof slab.
        *   Temperature effects.
    *   **IS Code Coefficients:** IS 3370-2009 provides coefficients for calculating bending moments and shear forces at the junction of the wall with the base and roof, and also for the hoop tension. These coefficients are derived from the analysis of cylindrical shells.
    *   **Reinforcement:** Design for hoop tension (circumferential reinforcement) and vertical reinforcement (to resist bending, temperature effects, and provide shrinkage reinforcement).
5.  **Design of Base Slab:**
    *   **Doughnut Type Base:** A common type where the circular wall is supported by a ring beam, and the inner portion of the base is a slab.
    *   **Flat Base Slab:** The entire base is a slab.
    *   The base slab resists the hydrostatic pressure of the contained water and any external loads. It is designed as a circular slab, often subjected to uniform upward pressure.
    *   Calculate bending moments and shear forces using IS code coefficients for circular slabs.
    *   Provide reinforcement in two directions (radial and circumferential).
6.  **Design of Roof Slab:**
    *   Often designed as a flat slab, dome, or conical slab, depending on the span and loading.
    *   Supported by the walls or columns.
7.  **Joint Design:** Design joints (e.g., at the base-wall junction) with appropriate waterstops.
8.  **Detailing:** Proper reinforcement detailing for circular members, including bending and hoop reinforcement.

### IS Code Coefficients for Circular Walls:

IS 3370-2009 (Part 2) provides coefficients for circular tanks, particularly for the junction of the wall with the base and roof. These coefficients are crucial for calculating bending moments and shear forces arising from the continuity of the structure.

**Example Scenario:** A circular tank with a flat base and supported at the top.

*   **Wall Analysis:** The wall is subjected to hoop tension due to hydrostatic pressure.
*   **Bending Moments:** At the base junction, if it's considered a continuous connection (e.g., monolithic with the base), bending moments will exist. Similarly, at the roof junction, continuity can induce bending.
*   **Coefficients:** The code provides coefficients ($m_1, m_2, m_3$ for moments, and $s_1, s_2$ for shears) at the base and top of the wall. These coefficients are functions of the diameter-to-height ratio and the relative stiffness of the wall and base/roof.
*   **Hoop Tension Calculation:**
    *   Hoop tension at any depth $h$ is $T = \frac{w h D}{2}$.
    *   The required area of circumferential reinforcement is $A_{sc} = \frac{T}{0.87 f_y}$.

**Using Coefficients for Bending:**

The bending moment ($M$) at the base of the wall might be calculated as:

$M_{base} = \frac{p_{base} D^2}{k}$, where $p_{base}$ is the hydrostatic pressure at the base and $k$ is a coefficient from IS 3370-2009. This moment, along with the hoop tension, dictates the reinforcement required at the base.

## 7. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary function of a waterstop in a construction joint of a water tank?

**Answer:** The primary function of a waterstop is to prevent leakage of water through the joint by forming a continuous barrier.

**Question 2:** According to IS 3370-2009, what is the minimum grade of concrete recommended for concrete in direct contact with water?

**Answer:** Minimum grade M20.

**Question 3:** Explain the concept of hoop tension in a circular water tank.

**Answer:** Hoop tension is the tensile stress developed in the circumferential direction of a cylindrical tank wall due to the internal hydrostatic pressure acting outwards. This stress tries to split the cylinder into two halves.

**Question 4:** For a rectangular tank wall that is cantilevered from the base and free at the top, where would you expect the maximum bending moment to occur?

**Answer:** The maximum bending moment would occur at the base support.

**Question 5:** A circular tank has a diameter of 10m and a water depth of 5m. Calculate the hoop tension at the base of the wall. Assume the unit weight of water is $10 \, \text{kN/m}^3$.

**Solution:**
*   Hydrostatic pressure at the base, $p = w \times h = 10 \, \text{kN/m}^3 \times 5 \, \text{m} = 50 \, \text{kN/m}^2$.
*   Diameter, $D = 10 \, \text{m}$.
*   Hoop tension, $T = \frac{pD}{2} = \frac{50 \, \text{kN/m}^2 \times 10 \, \text{m}}{2} = 250 \, \text{kN/m}$.
    This is the tension per unit length of the circumference.

## 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Watertightness is Paramount:** Design and construction must prioritize preventing leakage.
*   **Crack Control:** Limiting crack widths is essential. Adequate reinforcement distribution is key.
*   **IS 3370-2009 is the Guiding Standard:** Familiarize yourself with its provisions, especially Part 2 for design.
*   **Hydrostatic Pressure:** This is the most critical load and varies linearly with depth.
*   **Joints are Vulnerable:** Proper selection and installation of waterstops and sealants are vital.
*   **Circular tanks are more efficient for larger capacities** due to stress distribution.
*   **Hoop tension is the primary consideration for circular tank walls.**
*   **Rectangular tank walls can be designed as cantilevers or frames,** with bending moments being a significant factor.
*   **Always consider durability and environmental exposure** when selecting concrete mixes and detailing reinforcement.
