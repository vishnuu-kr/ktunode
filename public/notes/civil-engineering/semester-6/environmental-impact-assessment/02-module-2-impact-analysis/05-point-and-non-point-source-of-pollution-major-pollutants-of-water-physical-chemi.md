# Environmental Impact Assessment - Module 2: Impact Analysis

## Topic: Pollution Sources, Water Quality & Standards

This module delves into the crucial aspect of identifying and analyzing pollution sources, understanding the characteristics of water, and setting standards for its quality, all vital for assessing environmental impacts.

---

### 1. Point and Non-point Sources of Pollution

Understanding the origin of pollution is fundamental to assessing its impact and devising mitigation strategies.

#### 1.1. Point Source Pollution

*   **Definition:** Pollution that comes from a single, identifiable source. These sources are typically discrete, and their discharge points can be readily located.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Easily identifiable and traceable.
    *   Discharge often occurs through pipes, ditches, or channels.
    *   Regulation and management are generally more straightforward.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Industrial discharge pipes:** Factories releasing wastewater directly into rivers or oceans.
    *   **Sewage treatment plants:** Effluent from municipal wastewater treatment facilities.
    *   **Mines:** Discharge of acidic or metal-laden water from mining operations.
    *   **Oil spills from tankers:** A single, localized event causing significant pollution.
    *   **Animal feedlots:** Concentrated animal waste disposal.

#### 1.2. Non-point Source Pollution (Diffuse Pollution)

*   **Definition:** Pollution that comes from a diffuse, widespread area and cannot be traced to a single discharge point. It originates from the land surface, often due to rainfall or snowmelt washing over these areas.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Difficult to identify and trace to a specific origin.
    *   Often results from land use practices.
    *   Management and control are more complex and require a landscape-level approach.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Agricultural runoff:** Fertilizers, pesticides, and animal waste from farms being washed into water bodies by rain.
    *   **Urban runoff:** Stormwater carrying oil, grease, chemicals, and litter from streets, parking lots, and construction sites.
    *   **Atmospheric deposition:** Air pollutants settling onto land and water surfaces.
    *   **Acid rain:** Resulting from emissions of sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
    *   **Soil erosion:** Sediment being carried into water bodies.

### 2. Major Pollutants of Water

Water can be polluted by a wide range of substances, broadly categorized by their origin and nature.

#### 2.1. Organic Pollutants

*   **Definition:** Substances derived from living organisms, including sewage, animal waste, agricultural waste, and industrial organic matter.
*   **Impacts:**
    *   **Oxygen Depletion (Biochemical Oxygen Demand - BOD & Chemical Oxygen Demand - COD):** Microorganisms decompose organic matter, consuming dissolved oxygen, which can harm aquatic life.
    *   **Nutrient Enrichment (Eutrophication):** Organic matter often contains nitrogen and phosphorus, leading to excessive algal growth.
    *   **Disease Transmission:** Pathogenic bacteria, viruses, and protozoa can be present in untreated sewage.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Human and animal excreta.
    *   Food processing waste.
    *   Dead organic matter.

#### 2.2. Inorganic Pollutants

*   **Definition:** Non-living chemical substances, often originating from industrial processes, mining, and natural geological formations.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Acids and Alkalis:** From industrial processes (e.g., pickling, metal plating), lowering or raising pH.
    *   **Heavy Metals:** Such as lead (Pb), mercury (Hg), cadmium (Cd), arsenic (As), chromium (Cr). Toxic, bioaccumulative, and can cause neurological damage, kidney damage, and cancer.
        *   *Source:* Mining, industrial discharge, batteries, paints.
    *   **Salts:** High concentrations of sodium chloride (NaCl) or other salts can harm freshwater ecosystems and make water unsuitable for drinking or irrigation.
        *   *Source:* Road de-icing, agricultural runoff, industrial processes.
    *   **Nutrients (Nitrogen and Phosphorus):** Primarily from fertilizers and sewage. Can cause eutrophication.
        *   *Source:* Agriculture, sewage, detergents.

#### 2.3. Suspended Solids (Turbidity)

*   **Definition:** Fine particles of soil, silt, clay, organic matter, or other debris suspended in water.
*   **Impacts:**
    *   **Reduced Light Penetration:** Hinders photosynthesis by aquatic plants.
    *   **Siltation:** Can smother aquatic habitats and clog fish gills.
    *   **Carrier of Other Pollutants:** Can absorb and transport heavy metals and organic pollutants.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Soil erosion from construction sites and agricultural fields.
    *   Industrial waste.
    *   Sewage discharge.

#### 2.4. Thermal Pollution

*   **Definition:** The discharge of heated water into a water body, usually from industrial cooling processes or power plants.
*   **Impacts:**
    *   **Decreased Dissolved Oxygen:** Warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen.
    *   **Increased Metabolic Rates:** Aquatic organisms may experience stress due to higher temperatures.
    *   **Altered Reproduction and Growth:** Can disrupt life cycles and species composition.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Cooling water discharge from power plants.
    *   Wastewater from certain industrial processes.

#### 2.5. Biological Pollutants (Pathogens)

*   **Definition:** Microorganisms (bacteria, viruses, protozoa, helminths) that can cause disease in humans and animals.
*   **Impacts:**
    *   **Waterborne Diseases:** Consumption of contaminated water leads to various illnesses.
*   **Examples:**
    *   *E. coli*
    *   *Salmonella*
    *   Hepatitis A virus
    *   *Giardia lamblia*
    *   *Cryptosporidium*

#### 2.6. Oil Pollution

*   **Definition:** Contamination of water bodies by petroleum products.
*   **Impacts:**
    *   **Harm to Aquatic Life:** Coats feathers of birds, interferes with respiration and feeding.
    *   **Toxicity:** Components of oil are toxic to marine organisms.
    *   **Degradation of Aesthetics and Recreation:** Fouls beaches and water surfaces.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Oil spills from tankers, offshore drilling rigs.
    *   Runoff from roads and industrial areas containing oil and grease.

#### 2.7. Radioactivity

*   **Definition:** Contamination of water by radioactive substances.
*   **Impacts:**
    *   **Health Risks:** Can cause cancer and genetic mutations.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Discharge from nuclear power plants.
    *   Mining of radioactive ores.

### 3. Physical, Chemical, and Biological Characteristics of Water

Assessing water quality involves measuring its physical, chemical, and biological properties.

#### 3.1. Physical Characteristics

*   **Temperature:**
    *   **Measurement:** Thermometers, probes.
    *   **Significance:** Affects dissolved oxygen levels, metabolic rates of aquatic organisms, and chemical reaction rates.
*   **Turbidity:**
    *   **Measurement:** Turbidity meter (Nephelometric Turbidity Units - NTU).
    *   **Significance:** Measures the cloudiness or haziness of water due to suspended particles. Impacts light penetration, can clog gills, and absorb pollutants.
*   **Color:**
    *   **Measurement:** Color comparators, spectrophotometers.
    *   **Significance:** Can indicate the presence of dissolved organic matter, industrial waste, or minerals. Aesthetic quality and can indicate potential health hazards.
*   **Odor:**
    *   **Measurement:** Olfactory assessment.
    *   **Significance:** Can indicate the presence of dissolved organic matter, gases (e.g., hydrogen sulfide), or industrial chemicals.
*   **Total Suspended Solids (TSS):**
    *   **Measurement:** Filtration and drying of water samples.
    *   **Significance:** Directly quantifies the amount of suspended matter.

#### 3.2. Chemical Characteristics

*   **pH:**
    *   **Measurement:** pH meter, pH strips.
    *   **Significance:** Measures the acidity or alkalinity of water (scale 0-14). Crucial for aquatic life and chemical reactions. Most aquatic life thrives in a pH range of 6.5-8.5.
*   **Dissolved Oxygen (DO):**
    *   **Measurement:** DO meter, Winkler titration.
    *   **Significance:** Essential for the survival of aerobic aquatic organisms. Low DO indicates organic pollution. Healthy levels are typically above 5 mg/L.
*   **Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD):**
    *   **Measurement:** Laboratory incubation of water samples to measure oxygen consumption by microorganisms over a set period (usually 5 days at 20°C - BOD5).
    *   **Significance:** Indicates the amount of biodegradable organic matter in water. High BOD means high organic pollution and low DO.
*   **Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD):**
    *   **Measurement:** Laboratory process using strong chemical oxidizers to measure the total amount of oxidizable organic matter, both biodegradable and non-biodegradable.
    *   **Significance:** Provides a measure of the total organic pollution. COD is generally higher than BOD for the same sample.
*   **Nutrients (Nitrogen and Phosphorus):**
    *   **Measurement:** Spectrophotometers, specific ion probes.
    *   **Significance:** Essential for plant growth. Excess levels (from fertilizers, sewage) cause eutrophication, leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion. Measured as nitrates (NO3-), nitrites (NO2-), ammonia (NH3), and phosphates (PO43-).
*   **Heavy Metals:**
    *   **Measurement:** Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometry (AAS), Inductively Coupled Plasma (ICP) spectroscopy.
    *   **Significance:** Toxic even at low concentrations. Includes lead, mercury, cadmium, arsenic, chromium, etc. Can cause severe health problems.
*   **Dissolved Solids (TDS):**
    *   **Measurement:** TDS meter, gravimetric analysis.
    *   **Significance:** Measures the total concentration of all dissolved inorganic and organic substances in water. High TDS can affect taste, cause scaling, and harm aquatic life.
*   **Hardness:**
    *   **Measurement:** Titration methods (EDTA).
    *   **Significance:** Primarily due to dissolved calcium (Ca2+) and magnesium (Mg2+) ions. Affects soap lathering and can cause scaling in pipes. Generally not a health concern but impacts water usability.
*   **Chloride:**
    *   **Measurement:** Titration (Mohr method), ion-selective electrodes.
    *   **Significance:** High levels can affect taste and be indicative of pollution from sewage or industrial waste. Can corrode pipes.
*   **Fluoride:**
    *   **Measurement:** Ion-selective electrodes.
    *   **Significance:** Beneficial for dental health in controlled amounts, but excess can cause fluorosis (mottling of teeth and skeletal damage).
*   **Pesticides and Herbicides:**
    *   **Measurement:** Gas Chromatography (GC), High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC).
    *   **Significance:** Synthetic organic compounds used in agriculture. Can be toxic to aquatic life and humans.

#### 3.3. Biological Characteristics

*   **Presence of Pathogens:**
    *   **Measurement:** Microbial culturing techniques (e.g., fecal coliform count, *E. coli* count).
    *   **Significance:** Indicates fecal contamination and the potential presence of disease-causing microorganisms.
*   **Algal Blooms:**
    *   **Measurement:** Microscopic examination, chlorophyll-a concentration.
    *   **Significance:** Excessive growth of algae, often due to nutrient enrichment. Can lead to oxygen depletion when they decompose.
*   **Aquatic Macrophytes:**
    *   **Measurement:** Visual surveys, biomass estimation.
    *   **Significance:** The types and abundance of aquatic plants can indicate water quality conditions. Some species thrive in polluted waters.
*   **Macroinvertebrates:**
    *   **Measurement:** Sampling and identification of aquatic insects, crustaceans, mollusks, etc.
    *   **Significance:** Different species have varying tolerances to pollution. Presence or absence of certain groups can be indicators of water quality (e.g., mayflies and stoneflies are sensitive to pollution).

### 4. Waterborne Diseases

Waterborne diseases are illnesses caused by consuming or coming into contact with water contaminated with pathogenic microorganisms or toxic substances.

*   **Transmission:** Occurs when sewage or other human/animal waste contaminates drinking water sources, recreational waters, or food irrigated with contaminated water.
*   **Major Types and Causative Agents:**
    *   **Bacterial Diseases:**
        *   **Cholera:** *Vibrio cholerae*
        *   **Typhoid Fever:** *Salmonella Typhi*
        *   **Dysentery (Bacillary):** *Shigella* species
        *   **Gastroenteritis:** *Escherichia coli* (certain strains), *Salmonella* species, *Campylobacter jejuni*
    *   **Viral Diseases:**
        *   **Hepatitis A:** Hepatitis A virus
        *   **Poliomyelitis:** Poliovirus
        *   **Gastroenteritis:** Rotavirus, Norovirus
    *   **Protozoan Diseases:**
        *   **Giardiasis:** *Giardia lamblia*
        *   **Amebiasis (Amebic Dysentery):** *Entamoeba histolytica*
        *   **Cryptosporidiosis:** *Cryptosporidium parvum*
    *   **Helminthic Diseases (Worms):**
        *   **Ascariasis:** *Ascaris lumbricoides*
        *   **Schistosomiasis (Bilharzia):** Blood flukes (*Schistosoma* species) – transmitted via freshwater snails.

### 5. Water Quality Standards (IS 10500-2012)

Water quality standards provide guidelines for the acceptable levels of various parameters in water, ensuring it is safe for its intended use (e.g., drinking, irrigation, industrial use). Indian Standard (IS) 10500-2012 specifies acceptable limits for drinking water in India.

**Key Aspects of IS 10500-2012:**

*   **Purpose:** To specify the limits for physical, chemical, biological, and radiological parameters for drinking water.
*   **Two Categories of Limits:**
    *   **Acceptable Limit:** The desirable concentration of a parameter.
    *   **Permissible Limit in the Absence of Alternate Source:** The maximum concentration allowed if no other suitable source is available, often with associated health advisories or treatment requirements.
*   **Key Parameters and Their Limits (Examples):**

| Parameter                      | Unit     | Acceptable Limit | Permissible Limit (Absence of Alternate Source) |
| :----------------------------- | :------- | :--------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| **Physical**                   |          |                  |                                                 |
| Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)   | mg/L     | 500              | 2000                                            |
| Turbidity                      | NTU      | 1                | 5                                               |
| Colour                         | Hazen    | 5                | 15                                              |
| Odour                          | -        | Agreeable        | Agreeable                                       |
| **Chemical**                   |          |                  |                                                 |
| pH                             | -        | 6.5 - 8.5        | 6.0 - 9.0                                       |
| Dissolved Oxygen (DO)          | mg/L     | 6 (Min)          | 4 (Min)                                         |
| Hardness (as CaCO3)            | mg/L     | 200              | 600                                             |
| Chloride (as Cl)               | mg/L     | 250              | 1000                                            |
| Sulphate (as SO4)              | mg/L     | 200              | 400                                             |
| Nitrate (as NO3)               | mg/L     | 45               | 45 (Note: 10 as NO3-N)                          |
| Fluoride (as F)                | mg/L     | 1.0              | 1.5                                             |
| Iron (as Fe)                   | mg/L     | 0.3              | 1.0                                             |

| Chromium (Total, as Cr)        | mg/L     | 0.05             | 0.05                                            |
| Lead (as Pb)                   | mg/L     | 0.01             | 0.01                                            |

*   **Important Note on Permissible Limits:** When the permissible limit is exceeded, the water is generally considered unfit for drinking, and alternative sources must be sought. If no alternative exists, stringent treatment and monitoring are required.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**1. Distinguish between Point Source and Non-point Source pollution with examples.**

**Answer:**
*   **Point Source:** Pollution from a single, identifiable source. Example: Industrial discharge pipe releasing treated wastewater into a river.
*   **Non-point Source:** Pollution from diffuse, widespread areas, often due to land runoff. Example: Agricultural fields where fertilizers and pesticides are washed into nearby streams by rain.

**2. Explain the significance of Dissolved Oxygen (DO) and Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) in water quality assessment.**

**Answer:**
*   **DO:** Essential for aquatic life. Low DO indicates organic pollution as microorganisms consume oxygen during decomposition.
*   **BOD:** Measures the amount of biodegradable organic matter. High BOD indicates a high level of organic pollution, leading to low DO.

**3. List three major categories of water pollutants and provide one example for each.**

**Answer:**
*   **Organic Pollutants:** Sewage, animal waste.
*   **Inorganic Pollutants:** Heavy metals (e.g., lead, mercury), excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus).
*   **Biological Pollutants:** Pathogens (e.g., *E. coli*, viruses).

**4. What are the primary health impacts of consuming water contaminated with *E. coli*?**

**Answer:**
Consuming water contaminated with *E. coli* can cause severe gastrointestinal illnesses, including diarrhea (often bloody), abdominal cramps, nausea, and vomiting. In severe cases, it can lead to kidney failure (hemolytic uremic syndrome).

**5. According to IS 10500-2012, what is the acceptable limit for Turbidity in drinking water, and what is the permissible limit in the absence of an alternate source?**

**Answer:**
*   Acceptable Limit for Turbidity: 1 NTU
*   Permissible Limit (in absence of alternate source): 5 NTU

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Point sources** are easier to regulate than **non-point sources**.
*   **Eutrophication** is primarily caused by excess **nitrogen and phosphorus**.
*   **High BOD** is an indicator of **organic pollution** and **low DO**.
*   **Heavy metals** are persistent, toxic, and **bioaccumulative**.
*   **Turbidity** reduces light penetration and can carry other pollutants.
*   **Pathogens** in water are the primary cause of **waterborne diseases**.
*   **IS 10500-2012** provides essential standards for **drinking water quality in India**.
*   Understanding water **physical, chemical, and biological characteristics** is crucial for impact assessment.
*   Always consider the **intended use of water** when evaluating quality standards.
