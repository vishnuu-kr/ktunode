# Environmental Impact Assessment: Module 3 - Solid Waste

## Topic: Solid Waste Management Rules 2016

---

### **Introduction to Solid Waste Management Rules 2016**

The **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 (SWM Rules, 2016)** are a significant legislative framework in India aimed at improving the collection, segregation, treatment, and disposal of solid waste. These rules were notified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC) and superseded the earlier Municipal Solid Waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 2000. The primary objective is to promote sustainable solid waste management practices and reduce the environmental and health impacts associated with unscientific waste disposal.

---

### **Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Solid Waste:** Includes combustible and non-combustible waste, household waste, garden rubbish, yard waste, commercial waste, institutional waste, construction and demolition waste, treated biomedical waste, **[Note: Biomedical waste is also governed by separate rules, but SWM Rules 2016 provide a context for its generation and initial management from the municipal waste perspective.]** treated infectious or pathological waste, treated chemical waste, treated hazardous waste, and **[Note: Hazardous waste has its own dedicated rules, but SWM Rules 2016 address the incidental generation and management of certain types within municipal solid waste.]** radioactive waste as may be specified by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) from time to time.

*   **Segregation of Waste at Source:** The process of separating different types of waste (e.g., wet, dry, hazardous) at the point of generation (households, institutions, etc.). This is a cornerstone of the SWM Rules, 2016.

*   **Wet Waste:** Waste that is organic and biodegradable, such as kitchen waste, food scraps, fruits and vegetables.

*   **Dry Waste:** Waste that is non-biodegradable, such as paper, plastic, metal, glass, rubber, leather.

*   **Domestic Hazardous Waste:** Waste generated at household level, which is hazardous in nature, such as batteries, paint, pesticides, CFL bulbs, mercury-containing items, etc.

*   **Construction and Demolition (C&D) Waste:** Waste generated from building and civil construction activities, road construction, and demolition of buildings and structures.

*   **Sanitary Landfill:** An engineered disposal site designed and operated to minimize environmental pollution, typically involving liners, leachate collection systems, and cover materials.

*   **Waste to Energy:** Processes that convert waste into usable forms of energy, such as incineration with energy recovery or anaerobic digestion.

*   **Composting:** The biological decomposition of organic matter into a nutrient-rich soil amendment.

*   **Waste Picker:** An informal sector worker who collects, segregates, and recycles waste materials. The rules aim to integrate and protect them.

*   **Authorized.-** The rules define "authorized" as any agency, company, or organization that has been granted a license or permit by the appropriate authority to carry out specific waste management activities.

*   **Appropriate Authority:** In relation to a city or town, it refers to the Municipal Corporation or Municipal Council, or any other body responsible for providing municipal services. For rural areas, it refers to the Panchayat or District Administration.

### **Learning Outcomes Covered:**

#### **1. Overview and Key Provisions of the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016**

*   **Shift from "Collection and Disposal" to "Management":** The rules emphasize a holistic approach to waste management, encompassing generation, segregation, collection, transportation, processing, and disposal.
*   **Mandatory Segregation at Source:** This is the most critical provision.
    *   **Households:** Must segregate waste into three streams:
        1.  **Biodegradable (Wet) Waste:** For composting or biogas.
        2.  **Non-Biodegradable (Dry) Waste:** For recycling.
        3.  **Domestic Hazardous Waste:** For safe disposal.
    *   **Institutions (Hotels, Restaurants, Markets, Offices, etc.):** Must also segregate waste into the same three streams and ensure proper treatment or disposal. They are required to set up composting or biodigester facilities for their biodegradable waste, or hand it over to authorized waste collectors.
    *   **Street Vendors:** To keep their waste in a container such that no waste spills into the street.
    *   **Mandatory Segregation for Bulk Waste Generators:** Large generators of waste (e.g., housing societies with more than 5,000 sq. m. area, hotels and restaurants with more than 500 sq. m. area, commercial establishments generating more than 10 kg per day) must segregate waste at source and manage their biodegradable waste.
*   **Responsibilities of Various Stakeholders:** The rules clearly define the roles and responsibilities of:
    *   **Central Government:** Policy formulation, monitoring, and capacity building.
    *   **State Governments/Union Territories:** Implementation of rules, setting up of State Monitoring Committees.
    *   **Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)/Gram Panchayats:** Primary responsibility for planning, implementation, and management of solid waste within their jurisdiction. This includes ensuring door-to-door collection, segregation, transportation, processing, and scientific disposal.
    *   **Waste Generators:** Segregation, reducing waste generation, and assisting ULBs.
    *   **Manufacturers/Brand Owners:** Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for waste management of their products.
    *   **Waste Processors:** Operating facilities according to specified standards.
    *   **Waste Pickers:** Recognition and integration into the formal waste management system.

*   **Phased Implementation:** The rules provided a timeline for implementation, encouraging states and ULBs to adopt the provisions gradually.
