# GROUND IMPROVEMENT TECHNIQUES

## Module 4: Grouting And Injection

### Topic: Types of Grouting

---

### 1. Introduction to Grouting

**Definition:** Grouting is a ground improvement technique that involves injecting fluid materials (grouts) into the pores, fissures, or voids of soil or rock masses to improve their engineering properties.

**Purpose of Grouting:**

*   **Reduce permeability:** To create impermeable barriers for water control (e.g., dams, tunnels).
*   **Increase strength and stiffness:** To improve the bearing capacity and reduce settlement of soils and rocks.
*   **Stabilize structures:** To fill voids and support existing structures, preventing collapse.
*   **Control groundwater:** To reduce groundwater inflow into excavations.
*   **Consolidate loose materials:** To fill voids and bind loose particles together.

**Key Components of Grouting:**

*   **Grout Material:** The fluid injected into the ground (cementitious, chemical, asphaltic).
*   **Grout Injection:** The process of delivering the grout into the ground (pressure, gravity).
*   **Ground Formation:** The soil or rock mass being treated.

### 2. Classification of Grouting Based on Grout Material

This is a primary way to categorize grouting. The choice of grout material depends on the soil/rock type, the desired improvement, and environmental considerations.

#### 2.1. Cementitious Grouting

*   **Description:** Involves the injection of cement-based slurries. Cement is mixed with water, and sometimes additives, to form a pumpable suspension.
*   **Grout Composition:** Portland cement, water, and various admixtures (plasticizers, accelerators, retarders, fillers).
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Relatively inexpensive.
    *   High strength and durability.
    *   Environmentally friendly.
    *   Can be used in a wide range of soil and rock conditions.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Lower penetration into fine-grained soils (low permeability).
    *   Can be prone to shrinkage.
    *   Requires careful control of mixing and injection.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Consolidation of gravels and sands.
    *   Sealing fissures in rock.
    *   Dam foundations.
    *   Tunnel lining.
    *   Stabilizing shallow foundations.
*   **Key Concept:** Particle size of cement limits penetration into fine pores.

#### 2.2. Chemical Grouting

*   **Description:** Involves the injection of chemical solutions that react and solidify within the ground formation.
*   **Types of Chemical Grouts:**
    *   **Silicate Grouts:**
        *   **Description:** Sodium silicate reacts with a hardening agent (e.g., sodium aluminate, calcium chloride, organic compounds) to form silica gel.
        *   **Properties:** Can achieve very low permeability, can solidify quickly or slowly depending on the system.
        *   **Advantages:** Excellent for fine sands and silts, can achieve very low permeability.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Potential environmental concerns (alkalinity), can be expensive, strength may be lower than cement.
        *   **Examples:** Sodium silicate + calcium chloride, sodium silicate + organic hardener.
    *   **Acrylamide Grouts:**
        *   **Description:** Monomers of acrylamide and methylenebisacrylamide are injected and polymerized to form a gel.
        *   **Properties:** Forms a stable, impermeable gel with good strength.
        *   **Advantages:** Can penetrate very fine sands and silts, predictable gel time, good chemical resistance.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Toxic monomers require careful handling, potential for environmental contamination if not managed properly.
        *   **Examples:** Acrylamide + methylenebisacrylamide + catalyst.
    *   **Polyurethane Grouts:**
        *   **Description:** Two-component systems that react upon mixing to form a solid or gel.
        *   **Properties:** Can be hydrophobic or hydrophilic, flexible or rigid.
        *   **Advantages:** Can seal active water leaks effectively (hydrophobic), good adhesion, relatively quick setting.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Can be expensive, potential for toxicity.
        *   **Examples:** Isocyanate + polyol.
    *   **Urea-Formaldehyde Grouts:**
        *   **Description:** Two-component system that reacts to form a rigid plastic.
        *   **Properties:** High strength, low viscosity.
        *   **Advantages:** Good penetration into fine soils, high strength.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Formaldehyde is a carcinogen, can be brittle.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Waterproofing of tunnels and basements.
    *   Stabilizing loose sands.
    *   Sealing leaks in dams and reservoirs.
    *   Consolidation of fine-grained soils.
*   **Key Concept:** Chemical reaction leads to solidification; viscosity and gel time are crucial parameters.

#### 2.3. Asphaltic Grouting (Bituminous Grouting)

*   **Description:** Involves injecting hot, fluid asphaltic binders into the ground.
*   **Grout Composition:** Bitumen (asphalt) mixed with fine aggregates or fillers.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Excellent waterproofing properties.
    *   Good binding capacity.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires high temperatures for injection, posing safety risks.
    *   Limited penetration into fine-grained soils due to viscosity.
    *   Can be environmentally problematic if not handled carefully.
    *   Less common in modern practice compared to cementitious and chemical grouting.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Waterproofing of dams and reservoirs.
    *   Consolidation of coarser granular materials.
    *   Filling voids in mines.
*   **Key Concept:** Hot application and viscosity are major considerations.

### 3. Classification of Grouting Based on Injection Method and Purpose

This classification focuses on *how* and *why* the grout is being injected.

#### 3.1. Permeation Grouting

*   **Description:** The grout is injected under pressure into the soil or rock pores. The grout fills the voids without displacing the soil particles. This is possible when the void spaces are larger than the grout particles.
*   **Mechanism:** Grout flows through the interconnected pore spaces.
*   **Grout Suitability:**
    *   **Cementitious grouts:** For coarse sands and gravels.
    *   **Chemical grouts:** For fine sands, silts, and even some clays.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Preserves the in-situ soil structure.
    *   Can significantly reduce permeability.
    *   Can improve strength and stiffness.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires sufficient void size for grout to flow.
    *   Effective only when the soil/rock is permeable enough.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Reducing permeability of dam foundations.
    *   Stabilizing excavation sites.
    *   Waterproofing tunnels.
*   **Key Concept:** Grout flows through existing voids without disturbing the soil fabric.

#### 3.2. Compaction Grouting

*   **Description:** A stiff, low-slump grout (typically cementitious) is injected under pressure. The grout displaces and compacts the surrounding soil particles, densifying the soil mass. The grout forms grout columns that stiffen the soil.
*   **Mechanism:** The injected grout is stiffer than the soil, causing the soil to bulge and consolidate around the grout.
*   **Grout Suitability:**
    *   Stiff cementitious grout (low water-cement ratio).
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Increases soil density and bearing capacity.
    *   Can be very effective in reducing settlement.
    *   Less likely to cause excessive fracturing in rock.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Can cause heave if not controlled properly.
    *   May not significantly reduce permeability unless filling large voids.
    *   Requires careful control of grout consistency and injection pressure.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Foundation underpinning.
    *   Lifting settled structures.
    *   Stabilizing sinkhole-prone areas.
    *   Consolidating loose sands and collapsing soils.
*   **Key Concept:** Grout acts as a displacement medium to densify the soil.

#### 3.3. Jet Grouting

*   **Description:** A high-pressure stream of grout (often cement-based) is used to erode and mix with the soil, forming a solidified soil-cement column or block. The drilling rods rotate and are withdrawn while the jet cuts the soil and mixes it with the grout.
*   **Mechanism:** Erosion by high-pressure fluid (grout, water, or air) followed by mixing and solidification.
*   **Grout Suitability:**
    *   Cementitious grouts, often with admixtures.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Can treat a wide range of soil types, including soft clays and loose sands.
    *   Forms continuous treated columns or panels.
    *   High strength and durability of the treated soil.
    *   Can be used for deep soil improvement.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires specialized equipment.
    *   Control of treatment diameter can be challenging.
    *   Can generate significant spoil and waste.
    *   Can be expensive.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Deep mixing for foundation support.
    *   Excavation support (cut-off walls).
    *   Stabilization of slopes.
    *   Water barrier construction.
*   **Key Concept:** Simultaneous erosion and grouting to create soil-cement composite.

#### 3.4. Fracturing Grouting (Shear Grouting)

*   **Description:** The grout is injected at pressures high enough to fracture the soil or rock, creating channels for grout penetration. This is typically used in less permeable materials where permeation grouting would not be effective.
*   **Mechanism:** Grout pressure exceeds the tensile or shear strength of the soil/rock, creating new flow paths.
*   **Grout Suitability:**
    *   Low viscosity chemical grouts or very fluid cementitious grouts.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Can treat materials with low natural permeability.
    *   Can create interconnected grout bodies.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Can lead to uncontrolled ground loosening and fracturing.
    *   Difficult to control the extent and pattern of fractures.
    *   Can be wasteful if grout escapes.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Treatment of fractured rock masses.
    *   Consolidation of some medium to fine sands.
*   **Key Concept:** Deliberate fracturing to improve grout accessibility. (Less commonly used due to control issues).

#### 3.5. Slurry Grouting (or Slurry Injection)

*   **Description:** Involves injecting a suspension of fine solid particles (e.g., bentonite, cement, fly ash) in water, often at lower pressures than permeation grouting. The primary mechanism is filling voids rather than permeating through pores.
*   **Mechanism:** The slurry fills larger voids, fissures, and gaps. As water drains, the solid particles deposit, forming a solidified mass.
*   **Grout Suitability:**
    *   Bentonite-cement slurries.
    *   Cement slurries with fine fillers.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Can be effective in filling larger voids and fissures.
    *   Can create effective water barriers.
    *   Bentonite provides plasticity and reduces bleeding.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Limited penetration into fine pores.
    *   Strength development can be lower than dense cement grouts.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Sealing joints in precast concrete segments (e.g., tunnels).
    *   Filling voids behind tunnel linings.
    *   Creating impermeable barriers in granular soils.
*   **Key Concept:** Filling of larger voids and fissures by a particle suspension.

#### 3.6. Back Grouting (or Void Grouting)

*   **Description:** Grout is injected into existing voids or cavities, such as those behind tunnel linings, in abandoned mines, or under structures.
*   **Mechanism:** Filling of pre-existing open spaces.
*   **Grout Suitability:**
    *   Cementitious grouts are common.
    *   Properties adjusted for viscosity and setting time.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Stabilizes structures by filling voids.
    *   Prevents water ingress into voids.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires access to the void.
    *   Effectiveness depends on the ability to fill the entire void.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Grouting behind tunnel segments.
    *   Grouting under bridge abutments.
    *   Filling voids in abandoned mine workings.
*   **Key Concept:** Filling of known, open cavities.

### 4. Other Important Grouting Concepts

#### 4.1. Grouting Equipment

*   **Grout Mixer:** To prepare the grout slurry.
*   **Grout Pump:** To deliver the grout under pressure (piston pumps, diaphragm pumps, centrifugal pumps).
*   **Grout Lines/Hoses:** To transport the grout to the injection points.
*   **Injection Packers/Nipples:** To seal the injection points in the ground.
*   **Drilling Equipment:** For creating boreholes or injection holes.

#### 4.2. Grout Properties

*   **Viscosity:** Resistance to flow. Affects penetration capability and pumpability.
*   **Fluidity/Workability:** Ease of mixing and pumping.
*   **Setting Time/Gel Time:** Time until the grout loses its fluidity and solidifies.
*   **Bleeding:** Separation of water from the grout.
*   **Strength:** Compressive strength of the solidified grout.
*   **Permeability:** Permeability of the solidified grout.
*   **Shrinkage:** Volume reduction upon setting.

#### 4.3. Grouting Design Considerations

*   **Soil/Rock Characteristics:** Particle size distribution, permeability, void ratio, fissures.
*   **Grout Type Selection:** Based on soil type, desired improvement, and environmental factors.
*   **Injection Pressure:** Must be sufficient for penetration but not so high as to cause damage.
*   **Grout Hole Pattern and Spacing:** To achieve the desired coverage and uniformity.
*   **Grout Mix Design:** To achieve required viscosity, setting time, and strength.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Which type of grouting is most suitable for reducing the permeability of a clean gravel deposit?
a) Compaction Grouting
b) Chemical Grouting
c) Permeation Grouting with cementitious grout
d) Jet Grouting

**Question 2:**
A stiff, low-slump cementitious grout injected to densify a loose sand layer and reduce settlement is an example of:
a) Permeation Grouting
b) Jet Grouting
c) Compaction Grouting
d) Slurry Grouting

**Question 3:**
Which chemical grout system involves the reaction of monomers to form a stable gel?
a) Silicate Grouting
b) Acrylamide Grouting
c) Polyurethane Grouting
d) Asphaltic Grouting

**Question 4:**
Jet grouting is characterized by:
a) Slow injection of fluid chemical solutions.
b) High-pressure streams of grout that erode and mix with the soil.
c) Injection of a stiff grout that displaces soil particles.
d) Filling of pre-existing large voids.

**Question 5:**
Describe the primary difference in mechanism between Permeation Grouting and Compaction Grouting.

---

### 7. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
c) Permeation Grouting with cementitious grout
*   **Reasoning:** Clean gravel has large pores that cementitious grout can permeate through to fill. Compaction grouting is for densification, chemical grouting might be overkill, and jet grouting is more aggressive than needed for simple permeability reduction in clean gravel.

**Answer 2:**
c) Compaction Grouting
*   **Reasoning:** The description perfectly matches the purpose and mechanism of compaction grouting – densifying soil with a stiff grout.

**Answer 3:**
b) Acrylamide Grouting
*   **Reasoning:** Acrylamide grouts work by polymerizing monomers to form a gel, which is their characteristic mechanism.

**Answer 4:**
b) High-pressure streams of grout that erode and mix with the soil.
*   **Reasoning:** This describes the fundamental principle of jet grouting, which uses high-pressure jets for soil erosion and mixing.

**Answer 5:**
*   **Permeation Grouting:** The grout flows through the existing pore spaces of the soil or rock without significantly disturbing the soil fabric. It fills the voids, reducing permeability and sometimes improving strength.
*   **Compaction Grouting:** A stiff grout is injected, which displaces and compacts the surrounding soil particles. The grout itself forms columns, and the soil is densified by the displacement action, leading to increased strength and reduced settlement. The primary goal is densification, not just filling voids.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Grouting is a versatile technique** with applications ranging from water control to structural support.
*   **The choice of grout material is critical** and depends heavily on the ground conditions and the desired outcome.
*   **Permeation grouting** fills existing voids, while **compaction grouting** densifies the soil by displacement.
*   **Jet grouting** uses high-pressure jets to create soil-cement composites.
*   **Chemical grouts** are often used for finer soils where cementitious grouts cannot penetrate.
*   **Understanding grout properties** (viscosity, setting time) is essential for successful grouting operations.
*   **Safety and environmental considerations** are paramount, especially with chemical grouts.
*   **The success of grouting depends on careful design, proper mix proportions, and controlled injection.**
