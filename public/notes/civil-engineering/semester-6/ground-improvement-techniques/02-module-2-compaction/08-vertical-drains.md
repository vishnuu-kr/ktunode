# Ground Improvement Techniques: Module 2 - Compaction

## Topic: Vertical Drains

---

### 1. Introduction to Vertical Drains

**Key Concept:** Vertical drains are a widely used ground improvement technique to accelerate consolidation and improve the strength and stability of soft, saturated fine-grained soils. They work by providing a shorter drainage path for pore water to escape, thereby reducing excess pore water pressure and increasing effective stress.

**Why are Vertical Drains needed?**
*   Soft, saturated fine-grained soils (clays, silts) have low permeability.
*   When subjected to a load (e.g., embankment, surcharge), these soils generate significant excess pore water pressure.
*   The slow dissipation of this excess pore water pressure leads to:
    *   Slow consolidation settlement.
    *   Low shear strength, leading to instability and potential bearing capacity failures.
    *   Reduced stiffness.
*   Traditional surface compaction or preloading alone may be insufficient or take too long to achieve the desired strength and settlement.

**How Vertical Drains Work:**
*   They are installed vertically into the soft soil layer.
*   These drains create a more permeable pathway within the low-permeability soil mass.
*   As pore water drains towards the vertical drains, it travels a shorter distance than it would in an undrained soil.
*   This accelerated drainage leads to:
    *   Faster dissipation of excess pore water pressure.
    *   Increased effective stress.
    *   Accelerated consolidation settlement.
    *   Improved soil strength and stiffness.

### 2. Types of Vertical Drains

**Key Concept:** Vertical drains can be categorized based on their material and installation method. The choice of drain type depends on factors such as soil conditions, project requirements, and cost.

#### 2.1. Sand Drains (Granular Drains)

*   **Description:** Consist of a vertical column of sand or gravel installed into the soft soil.
*   **Installation:**
    *   Typically installed by drilling a borehole and then backfilling it with sand.
    *   A casing may be used to prevent borehole collapse, which is then withdrawn.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Relatively simple and well-established technology.
    *   Good drainage capacity.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires significant excavation and backfilling.
    *   Can cause soil disturbance during installation.
    *   Susceptible to clogging if surrounding soil is very fine or contains organic matter.
    *   Can be labor-intensive and time-consuming.
*   **Example:** Historically, sand drains were a primary method before the advent of prefabricated vertical drains. They were used in projects like the Transistor Highway embankment over soft clay.

#### 2.2. Prefabricated Vertical Drains (PVDs) or Geodrains

*   **Description:** Consist of a strip of permeable geotextile or geogrid with a corrugated core, enclosed in a permeable filter sheath. They are delivered in rolls and are much thinner than sand drains.
*   **Installation:**
    *   Installed using a specialized drilling rig and a "mandrel" system.
    *   The mandrel pushes the PVD vertically into the soil, minimizing disturbance compared to sand drains.
    *   Once installed, the mandrel is withdrawn, leaving the PVD in place.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Much faster and more efficient installation than sand drains.
    *   Less soil disturbance during installation.
    *   Can be installed in a wider range of soil conditions.
    *   More consistent drainage performance due to engineered design.
    *   Lighter and easier to transport.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Higher initial material cost compared to sand.
    *   Requires specialized installation equipment.
    *   Can be susceptible to damage during installation in very dense or gravelly soils.
    *   Long-term durability in aggressive chemical environments needs consideration.
*   **Example:** Widely used in modern infrastructure projects like airport runways, port facilities, and highway embankments over soft ground. Examples include the construction of bridges over soft coastal plains.

### 3. Design Considerations for Vertical Drains

**Key Concepts:** Effective design of vertical drain systems involves considering the soil properties, the drainage boundary conditions, and the required consolidation time.

#### 3.1. Soil Properties

*   **Permeability (k):** Crucial for determining drainage rate. Horizontal permeability ($k_h$) is generally more important for vertical drains as water flows horizontally towards the drains.
*   **Consolidation Characteristics:**
    *   **Coefficient of Consolidation ($c_v$):** Represents the rate of consolidation. Lower $c_v$ values indicate slower consolidation, making vertical drains more beneficial.
    *   **Compression Index ($C_c$):** Influences the total consolidation settlement.
    *   **Coefficient of Secondary Compression ($C_\alpha$):** Affects long-term settlements.
*   **Soil Stratigraphy:** The thickness and layering of soft soils will dictate the required depth and spacing of drains.
*   **Presence of Sand/Silt Layers:** These can act as natural drainage layers, influencing the design.

#### 3.2. Drain Spacing and Pattern

*   **Spacing:** The distance between vertical drains significantly affects the drainage path length and thus the consolidation time. Closer spacing leads to faster consolidation.
*   **Pattern:** Drains are typically arranged in a triangular (equilateral) or square grid pattern.
    *   **Triangular Pattern:** Provides more uniform coverage and slightly faster consolidation for the same spacing compared to a square pattern.
    *   **Square Pattern:** Simpler to lay out.
*   **Drainage Radius ($r_e$):** The effective radius of influence for a vertical drain.
    *   For a **square pattern**, $r_e = s / \sqrt{2}$, where $s$ is the spacing.
    *   For a **triangular pattern**, $r_e = s / \sqrt{3}$, where $s$ is the spacing between drains.
    *   The zone of influence for a drain is considered a cylinder of radius $r_e$.

#### 3.3. Depth of Drains

*   Drains should extend through the entire thickness of the compressible soil layer to be consolidated.
*   In some cases, drains may extend into a firmer underlying stratum to ensure full drainage.

#### 3.4. Permeability of Drains

*   **Sand Drains:** The permeability of the sand backfill needs to be sufficient.
*   **PVDs:** The "equivalent sand drain diameter" is often used to represent the drainage capacity of a PVD. This value is determined by laboratory testing or manufacturer specifications. The filter permeability of the sheath is also important to prevent clogging.

#### 3.5. Required Consolidation Time

*   The target percentage of consolidation (e.g., 90%) and the desired time frame for achieving it are critical design inputs.
*   This dictates the required spacing of the drains.

#### 3.6. Installation Factors

*   **Soil Disturbance:** Minimizing disturbance during installation is crucial to avoid damaging the drain's permeability or the surrounding soil.
*   **Clogging:** The risk of the drain becoming clogged by fine particles from the surrounding soil.

### 4. Design Calculations and Theory

**Key Concept:** The design of vertical drains relies on consolidation theory, particularly Terzaghi's one-dimensional consolidation theory, adapted for radial drainage.

#### 4.1. Hansbo's Radial Drainage Theory

*   **Concept:** Hansbo (1960) extended Terzaghi's theory to account for radial drainage towards vertical drains. He introduced the concept of an "equivalent sand drain diameter" for PVDs.
*   **Governing Equation:** The degree of consolidation ($U_r$) in radial flow towards a vertical drain is typically expressed in a form similar to Terzaghi's equation but involves additional parameters related to radial flow. A simplified form for a single drain influencing a cylindrical zone of radius $r_e$ is:

    $U_r = 1 - e^{-\frac{8 c_h t}{D_e^2}} \quad \text{for } U_r \le 0.6$
    $U_r = 1 - \frac{4}{\pi U_r} e^{-\frac{\pi^2 c_h t}{4 r_e^2}} \quad \text{for } U_r > 0.6$

    Where:
    *   $U_r$ = Degree of consolidation due to radial drainage
    *   $c_h$ = Coefficient of consolidation in the horizontal direction (often assumed to be similar to $c_v$ for practical purposes, but horizontal permeability is the key driver)
    *   $t$ = Time
    *   $D_e$ = Equivalent sand drain diameter (for PVDs) or the diameter of the sand drain.
    *   $r_e$ = Effective radius of influence of the drain (e.g., $s/\sqrt{\pi}$ for circular area, $s/\sqrt{2}$ for square, $s/\sqrt{3}$ for triangular)

*   **Key Takeaway:** The consolidation time is proportional to the square of the drainage path length (effectively the spacing squared, $r_e^2$). This means doubling the spacing increases the consolidation time by a factor of four.

#### 4.2. Calculating Drain Spacing for a Desired Consolidation Time

*   The design process usually involves:
    1.  Determining the required degree of consolidation ($U_r$) and the target time ($t$).
    2.  Obtaining the relevant soil properties ($c_h$).
    3.  Choosing a drain pattern (triangular or square).
    4.  Using the radial consolidation equation to solve for the effective radius ($r_e$) or equivalent sand drain diameter ($D_e$).
    5.  Calculating the required drain spacing ($s$) from $r_e$ based on the chosen pattern.

*   **Example Calculation:**
    *   **Problem:** A 5m thick layer of soft clay needs 90% consolidation in 1 year. The horizontal coefficient of consolidation ($c_h$) is $0.5 \, m^2/year$. Drains are to be installed in a triangular pattern. Assume the PVD has an equivalent sand drain diameter ($D_e$) of 0.1m.
    *   **Solution:**
        *   We need to find $r_e$ for $U_r = 0.90$.
        *   Using $U_r = 1 - \frac{4}{\pi U_r} e^{-\frac{\pi^2 c_h t}{4 r_e^2}}$ for $U_r > 0.6$.
        *   $0.90 = 1 - \frac{4}{0.90 \pi} e^{-\frac{\pi^2 (0.5)(1)}{4 r_e^2}}$
        *   $0.10 = \frac{4}{0.90 \pi} e^{-\frac{2.467}{r_e^2}}$
        *   $0.0225 \pi = e^{-\frac{2.467}{r_e^2}}$
        *   $0.0707 = e^{-\frac{2.467}{r_e^2}}$
        *   $\ln(0.0707) = -\frac{2.467}{r_e^2}$
        *   $-2.650 = -\frac{2.467}{r_e^2}$
        *   $r_e^2 = \frac{2.467}{2.650} \approx 0.931 \, m^2$
        *   $r_e \approx 0.965 \, m$
        *   For a triangular pattern, $r_e = s / \sqrt{3}$.
        *   $s = r_e \sqrt{3} = 0.965 \times 1.732 \approx 1.67 \, m$.
        *   **Answer:** Drains should be spaced approximately 1.7m apart in a triangular pattern.

#### 4.3. Design of the Drainage Blanket

*   After the drains reach the surface, the pore water needs to be collected and discharged away from the site.
*   A permeable drainage blanket (gravel or sand layer) is typically placed on top of the soft soil layer before the fill or surcharge load is applied.
*   This blanket collects water from the vertical drains and directs it to a collection system (e.g., a toe drain or outlet pipes).
*   The thickness and permeability of the drainage blanket must be sufficient to handle the flow of water from the drains without becoming saturated or clogged.

### 5. Installation Methods and Equipment

**Key Concepts:** The effectiveness of vertical drains is highly dependent on their proper installation.

#### 5.1. Sand Drains Installation

*   **Drilling:** A borehole is typically drilled to the required depth.
*   **Casing:** A casing is often used to support the borehole walls, especially in unstable soils, and prevent collapse.
*   **Backfilling:** Clean sand is then carefully placed into the borehole to fill it.
*   **Withdrawal:** The casing is slowly withdrawn as the sand is placed, ensuring the sand column remains continuous and stable.
*   **Equipment:** Rotary drilling rigs, casing vibrators, and sand pumping systems.

#### 5.2. PVDs Installation

*   **Mandrel System:** A rigid metal "mandrel" is attached to the top of the PVD.
*   **Driving:** The mandrel, with the PVD attached, is driven into the ground using a vibratory hammer or a static driving system attached to an excavator or crane.
*   **Soil Displacement:** The mandrel displaces the soil as it penetrates, minimizing smearing of the soil along the drain.
*   **Withdrawal:** Once the desired depth is reached, the mandrel is withdrawn, leaving the PVD in place. The PVD is then cut off at ground level.
*   **Equipment:** Excavators, cranes, vibratory hammers, specialized PVD driving rigs, PVD rolls, cutting tools.

#### 5.3. Key Considerations during Installation

*   **Maintaining Verticality:** Drains must be installed as vertically as possible.
*   **Minimizing Smearing:** Soil smearing on the drain surface can reduce its permeability.
*   **Preventing Clogging:** Ensuring clean materials are used (for sand drains) and the filter sheath remains intact (for PVDs).
*   **Achieving Target Depth:** Drains must reach the designed depth through the compressible layer.
*   **Continuity of the Drain:** The drain material should be continuous and free from breaks.
*   **Top and Bottom Conditions:** Ensuring proper connection to the drainage blanket at the top and a clear exit for water at the bottom if applicable.

### 6. Applications of Vertical Drains

**Key Concepts:** Vertical drains are applied in various civil engineering projects where soft ground conditions are encountered.

*   **Embankments and Roadways:** To increase the rate of settlement and improve the stability of embankments built on soft clays or silts.
*   **Airport Runways and Taxiways:** To reduce settlement and increase bearing capacity.
*   **Port Facilities and Waterfront Structures:** To support quay walls, loading platforms, and reclamation areas.
*   **Building Foundations:** To accelerate consolidation under raft foundations or other structures on soft ground.
*   **Land Reclamation:** To consolidate reclaimed land and improve its load-bearing capacity.
*   **Dike and Levee Construction:** To improve stability and reduce settlement.
*   **Surcharge Preloading:** Vertical drains are often used in conjunction with surcharge preloading to accelerate the consolidation process and achieve the required settlement before construction.

---

### 7. Advantages and Disadvantages of Vertical Drains

#### 7.1. Advantages

*   **Accelerated Consolidation:** Significantly reduces the time required for consolidation settlement.
*   **Increased Strength and Stability:** Leads to faster gain in shear strength, improving the stability of the ground.
*   **Reduced Excess Pore Water Pressure:** Helps prevent pore pressure build-up, reducing the risk of instability.
*   **Reduced Settlement Time:** Allows for faster project completion.
*   **Cost-Effective for Certain Projects:** Can be more economical than other methods (like deep foundations) for large areas of soft ground.
*   **Environmentally Friendly:** Reduces the need for extensive excavation and replacement of soil.

#### 7.2. Disadvantages

*   **Installation Cost:** Can involve significant upfront costs for materials and specialized equipment.
*   **Soil Disturbance:** Although minimized with PVDs, some disturbance is inherent.
*   **Clogging Risk:** Drains can be susceptible to clogging in very fine soils or if installation is not done properly.
*   **Requires Skilled Labor and Equipment:** Specialized knowledge and equipment are needed for effective installation.
*   **Limited Effectiveness in Highly Permeable Soils:** Not necessary or effective in granular soils.
*   **Potential for Damage:** PVDs can be damaged during installation in certain conditions.

### 9. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
The primary purpose of vertical drains is to accelerate the process of consolidation settlement in soft, saturated fine-grained soils. They achieve this by providing a shorter drainage path for pore water to escape, thereby reducing excess pore water pressure and allowing the soil to gain strength and stiffness more rapidly.

**Answer 2:**
*   **Sand Drains:**
    *   **Material:** Consist of granular material (sand or gravel).
    *   **Installation:** Boreholes are drilled and filled with sand; often requires casing.
    *   **Advantages:** Well-established, good drainage capacity.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Labor-intensive, high soil disturbance, slow installation, potential clogging.
*   **Prefabricated Vertical Drains (PVDs):**
    *   **Material:** Synthetic filter material with a corrugated core.
    *   **Installation:** Driven into the ground using a mandrel.
    *   **Advantages:** Fast and efficient installation, less soil disturbance, consistent performance, lighter.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher material cost, requires specialized equipment, potential for damage during installation.

**Answer 3:**
*   **Given:**
    *   $U_r = 0.90$
    *   $t = 1.5 \, years$
    *   $c_h = 0.4 \, m^2/year$
    *   $D_e = 0.12 \, m$
    *   Pattern: Square
    *   Equation for $U_r > 0.6$: $U_r = 1 - \frac{4}{\pi U_r} e^{-\frac{\pi^2 c_h t}{4 r_e^2}}$

*   **Calculation:**
    *   $0.90 = 1 - \frac{4}{0.90 \pi} e^{-\frac{\pi^2 (0.4)(1.5)}{4 r_e^2}}$
    *   $0.10 = \frac{4}{0.90 \pi} e^{-\frac{1.853}{r_e^2}}$
    *   $0.0225 \pi = e^{-\frac{1.853}{r_e^2}}$
    *   $0.0707 = e^{-\frac{1.853}{r_e^2}}$
    *   $\ln(0.0707) = -\frac{1.853}{r_e^2}$
    *   $-2.650 = -\frac{1.853}{r_e^2}$
    *   $r_e^2 = \frac{1.853}{2.650} \approx 0.699 \, m^2$
    *   $r_e \approx 0.836 \, m$

*   **Spacing (Square Pattern):**
    *   $r_e = s / \sqrt{2}$
    *   $s = r_e \sqrt{2} = 0.836 \times 1.414 \approx 1.18 \, m$

*   **Answer:** The maximum spacing for the drains is approximately **1.18 meters**.

**Answer 4:**
The drainage blanket is a permeable layer (usually sand or gravel) placed on top of the soft soil layer, over which the fill or surcharge is placed. Its role is to collect the pore water that drains from the vertical drains and convey it to an outlet or collection system, preventing the drains from becoming submerged and ensuring effective discharge of water.

**Answer 5:**
Three common applications of vertical drains are:
1.  Construction of highways and embankments on soft ground.
2.  Development of airport runways and taxiways.
3.  Construction of port facilities and reclamation of land for waterfront development.
4.  Support for building foundations on compressible soils.

### 10. Important Points to Remember

*   **Purpose:** Accelerate consolidation, increase strength, reduce settlement time.
*   **Mechanism:** Shorten drainage path for pore water.
*   **Key Soil Properties:** Horizontal permeability ($k_h$), coefficient of consolidation ($c_h$).
*   **Drain Spacing:** Critically affects consolidation time (time $\propto$ spacing$^2$). Closer spacing = faster consolidation.
*   **Drain Pattern:** Triangular and square grids are common.
*   **PVDs vs. Sand Drains:** PVDs are generally preferred in modern practice due to faster installation and less disturbance.
*   **Hansbo's Theory:** The basis for radial drainage calculations.
*   **Drainage Blanket:** Essential for collecting and discharging water from the drains.
*   **Installation Quality:** Crucial for effectiveness; minimize disturbance and clogging.
