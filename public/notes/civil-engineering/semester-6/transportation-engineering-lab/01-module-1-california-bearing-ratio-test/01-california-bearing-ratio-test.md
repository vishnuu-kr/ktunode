### 1. Introduction and Objectives

This module introduces the California Bearing Ratio (CBR) test, a fundamental laboratory test used in pavement design. It aims to determine the strength of subgrade soils and base/subbase materials, which are critical for the structural integrity of flexible pavements.

**Learning Outcomes:**

*   **Understand the principle of the CBR test:** Comprehend what the CBR value represents and how it's derived.
*   **Identify the equipment used in the CBR test:** Be familiar with the apparatus required to conduct the test.
*   **Learn the procedure for conducting the CBR test:** Follow the step-by-step process for sample preparation and testing.
*   **Calculate the CBR value:** Be able to perform the necessary calculations to obtain the CBR percentage.
*   **Interpret the results of the CBR test:** Understand how the CBR value is used in pavement design.
*   **Identify factors affecting the CBR value:** Recognize variables that can influence the test results.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

#### 2.1. California Bearing Ratio (CBR)

*   **Definition:** The CBR is a measure of the strength of a soil or unbound aggregate relative to a standard crushed stone. It represents the penetration load required to cause a standard plunger to penetrate a specified depth into the soil sample, expressed as a percentage of the load required for the same penetration into a standard material.
*   **Principle:** The test is essentially a **penetration test**. It measures the resistance of a soil to penetration by a standard plunger under a controlled loading rate.
*   **Standard Material:** The reference material for the CBR test is a standard crushed limestone, which is assigned a CBR value of 100%.

#### 2.2. Soaked vs. Unsoaked CBR

*   **Soaked CBR:** This is the most commonly used value. The soil sample is saturated with water for a specified period (typically 96 hours) before testing. This simulates the worst-case scenario for subgrade strength, where the soil can become saturated due to groundwater or rainfall.
*   **Unsoaked CBR:** The test is conducted on a dry or compacted sample without soaking. This is used for materials that are not expected to become saturated or for initial assessment of material strength.

#### 2.3. Design CBR

*   **Definition:** The design CBR is the minimum CBR value expected for a particular layer of the pavement structure, considering variations in material quality and environmental conditions. It is typically the average of several tested values, with a certain percentage of individual values allowed to be below the average.

### 3. Equipment Used in the CBR Test

The following equipment is essential for conducting the CBR test:

*   **CBR Mould:** A cylindrical mould, typically 150 mm in diameter and 175 mm in height, with a perforated base.
*   **Rammer:** A standard rammer (either 4.5 kg or 2.5 kg) with a drop height of 450 mm for compaction.
*   **Tamping Foot:** A circular foot attached to the rammer for uniform compaction.
*   **Surcharge Weights:** Metal discs of specific weights (e.g., 2.04 kg, 4.08 kg, 4.5 kg) to simulate the load of overlying pavement layers.
*   **Penetration Plunger:** A cylindrical plunger with a specific diameter (e.g., 50 mm for coarse-grained soils, 25 mm for fine-grained soils).
*   **Loading Machine:** A universal testing machine (UTM) or a dedicated CBR loading machine capable of applying load at a controlled rate.
*   **Dial Gauges:** Two dial gauges to measure the penetration depth of the plunger.
*   **Balance:** For weighing the soil samples.
*   **Water Tank:** For soaking the samples.
*   **Percolation disc:** To allow water to flow into the sample from the bottom.
*   **Filter paper:** To prevent fine particles from entering the percolation disc.
*   **Oven:** For drying samples to determine moisture content.

### 4. Procedure for Conducting the CBR Test

The procedure can be broadly divided into sample preparation and testing.

#### 4.1. Sample Preparation

1.  **Sample Selection:** Obtain a representative sample of the soil or aggregate to be tested.
2.  **Moisture Content Adjustment:** The sample is usually prepared at a specific moisture content, often close to its optimum moisture content (OMC) determined from a Standard or Modified Proctor test.
3.  **Compaction:** The soil is compacted in the CBR mould in layers (typically 5 layers) using a standard rammer. The number of blows per layer is specified to achieve a certain density.
    *   **For soaked CBR:** Compact the soil to a predetermined density and moisture content, then soak the sample.
    *   **For unsoaked CBR:** Compact the soil to a predetermined density and moisture content, and test it immediately without soaking.

#### 4.2. Soaking Procedure (for Soaked CBR)

1.  **Place Surging Weights:** After compaction, place surcharge weights on top of the compacted soil. A typical surcharge loading is around 4.5 kg to simulate the overburden pressure. If testing at a higher OMC, a higher surcharge might be used.
2.  **Place Percolation Disc and Filter Paper:** A perforated disc and filter paper are placed at the bottom of the mould to facilitate water ingress.
3.  **Submerge in Water:** The mould is then submerged in a water tank for a specified period, typically 96 hours. The water level is maintained above the top of the mould.

#### 4.3. Testing Procedure

1.  **Mount the Mould:** Place the soaked (or unsoaked) CBR mould on the loading machine.
2.  **Apply Surcharge Weights:** Add the required surcharge weights on top of the sample to simulate the pavement structure.
3.  **Position Dial Gauges:** Place one dial gauge to measure the penetration of the plunger and another, typically around the mould, to measure any vertical movement of the sample.
4.  **Apply Load:** Start applying the load using the penetration plunger at a constant rate.
    *   **Standard loading rate:** Typically 1.25 mm/min for the 50 mm plunger and 1.0 mm/min for the 25 mm plunger.
5.  **Record Penetration Readings:** Record the load applied at various penetration depths. Readings are typically taken at penetrations of 2.5 mm, 5.0 mm, 7.5 mm, 10.0 mm, 12.5 mm, etc.

---

### 5. Calculation of CBR Value

The CBR value is calculated using the following formula:

$$ \text{CBR} (\%) = \frac{\text{Load at a given penetration}}{\text{Standard load at the same penetration}} \times 100 $$

**Steps for Calculation:**

1.  **Plot Load-Penetration Curve:** Plot the applied load against the penetration depth.
2.  **Determine Corrected Penetration:** The load-penetration curve may not start from zero due to unevenness of the surface or initial seating of the plunger. A tangent to the steepest portion of the curve is drawn. The load corresponding to zero penetration is taken as the load at the origin (0,0) of the corrected curve.
3.  **Obtain Loads at Standard Penetrations:** From the corrected load-penetration curve, find the loads corresponding to standard penetration depths:
    *   **2.5 mm penetration**
    *   **5.0 mm penetration**
4.  **Calculate CBR at 2.5 mm and 5.0 mm:**
    *   **CBR at 2.5 mm:** $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} = \frac{\text{Load at 2.5 mm}}{2065 \text{ kg}} \times 100 $
    *   **CBR at 5.0 mm:** $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} = \frac{\text{Load at 5.0 mm}}{2450 \text{ kg}} \times 100 $
    *   *(Standard loads are for the 50 mm diameter plunger. Different standard loads apply for other plunger sizes.)*
5.  **Select the Design CBR:**
    *   If $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} $ is greater than $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} $, the $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} $ value is taken as the CBR value.
    *   If $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} $ is greater than $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} $, a correction is applied. The $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} $ is recalculated by finding the load at 7.5 mm penetration and using the ratio of load at 7.5 mm to the standard load at 5.0 mm. If this recalculated $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} $ is still less than $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} $, then the $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} $ value is reported as the CBR. However, it is more common to report the *lower* of the two values unless there's a significant difference, indicating a potential issue with the test or sample. **Typically, the *lower* value of CBR at 2.5 mm or 5.0 mm is reported, unless $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} $ is significantly higher than $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} $ which suggests an unusual curve.**

**Example Calculation:**

Suppose the corrected loads obtained from the test are:
*   Load at 2.5 mm penetration = 1000 kg
*   Load at 5.0 mm penetration = 1200 kg

Calculate CBR:
*   $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} = \frac{1000 \text{ kg}}{2065 \text{ kg}} \times 100 \approx 48.4\% $
*   $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} = \frac{1200 \text{ kg}}{2450 \text{ kg}} \times 100 \approx 49.0\% $

Since $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} > \text{CBR}_{2.5} $, the design CBR is taken as $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} $ which is **48.4%**.

### 6. Interpretation of CBR Results in Pavement Design

The CBR value is a crucial parameter in flexible pavement design. It directly influences the thickness of the pavement layers.

*   **Higher CBR:** Indicates a stronger subgrade/material, requiring thinner pavement layers.
*   **Lower CBR:** Indicates a weaker subgrade/material, requiring thicker pavement layers to distribute the traffic loads effectively and prevent excessive deformation.

**Commonly Used CBR Values:**

*   **Subgrade:** Typically ranges from 2% to 10%.
*   **Subbase:** Typically ranges from 20% to 50%.
*   **Base Course:** Typically ranges from 70% to 100% or more.

**Pavement Design Methods:** Pavement design guides (like the AASHTO method or the IRC method) use the CBR value of the subgrade and the design CBR of the overlying materials to determine the required thickness of each pavement layer (e.g., asphalt concrete, base course, subbase course).

---

### 7. Factors Affecting CBR Value

Several factors can significantly influence the CBR value obtained in the laboratory:

*   **Moisture Content of the Sample:** Higher moisture content generally leads to lower CBR values due to reduced soil strength.
*   **Dry Density of Compaction:** Higher dry density leads to higher CBR values as the soil particles are more tightly packed.
*   **Compaction Effort:** The method and energy applied during compaction (Proctor compaction) affect the final density and thus the CBR.
*   **Surcharge Weight:** Simulates the load of overlying layers. An insufficient or excessive surcharge can affect the test results.
*   **Soaking Period:** The duration of soaking influences the moisture content and swell, impacting the CBR.
*   **Penetration Rate:** The rate at which the plunger is pushed into the sample can affect the measured load.
*   **Plunger Diameter:** Different plungers (50 mm, 25 mm) are used for different soil types, and their respective standard loads are specific.
*   **Sample Preparation Method:** Hand compaction vs. mechanical compaction can lead to variations.
*   **Presence of Coarse Aggregates:** Larger aggregates can influence the contact pressure and load distribution.

### 8. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of the California Bearing Ratio (CBR) test in Transportation Engineering?

**Answer:** The primary purpose of the CBR test is to determine the relative strength of subgrade soils and pavement materials (base and subbase) for the design of flexible pavements.

**Question 2:** Explain the difference between Soaked CBR and Unsoaked CBR. Which one is generally considered more critical for pavement design and why?

**Answer:**
*   **Soaked CBR:** The soil sample is saturated with water for 96 hours before testing. It represents the worst-case scenario for subgrade strength.
*   **Unsoaked CBR:** The test is conducted on a dry or compacted sample without soaking.
The **Soaked CBR** is generally considered more critical for pavement design because it simulates the conditions where the subgrade might become saturated due to environmental factors (rainfall, high water table), leading to a significant reduction in its strength.

**Question 3:** If the corrected load at 2.5 mm penetration is 900 kg and the corrected load at 5.0 mm penetration is 1050 kg, calculate the CBR values at both penetrations and determine the design CBR. (Assume the standard loads for a 50 mm plunger).

**Answer:**
*   Standard load at 2.5 mm = 2065 kg
*   Standard load at 5.0 mm = 2450 kg

*   $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} = \frac{900 \text{ kg}}{2065 \text{ kg}} \times 100 \approx 43.6\% $
*   $ \text{CBR}_{5.0} = \frac{1050 \text{ kg}}{2450 \text{ kg}} \times 100 \approx 42.9\% $

Since $ \text{CBR}_{2.5} > \text{CBR}_{5.0} $, the design CBR is **42.9%**. (In this case, the lower value is reported).

**Question 4:** List any three factors that can affect the CBR value obtained from a test.

**Answer:** Any three of the following:
*   Moisture content of the sample
*   Dry density of compaction
*   Compaction effort
*   Surcharge weight
*   Soaking period
*   Penetration rate
*   Plunger diameter

---

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   The CBR test is a **penetration resistance test**.
*   The **soaked CBR value** is crucial for designing flexible pavements to account for worst-case moisture conditions.
*   Always plot the **load-penetration curve** and obtain **corrected loads** for accurate calculations.
*   The **design CBR is generally the lower of the CBR values at 2.5 mm and 5.0 mm penetration**, unless a specific correction rule is applied.
*   Consistency in **sample preparation (moisture content, density, compaction)** is vital for reliable results.
*   The CBR value directly influences the **thickness of pavement layers** in design.
*   Ensure the **loading rate and plunger size** are appropriate for the material being tested and the relevant standards.
