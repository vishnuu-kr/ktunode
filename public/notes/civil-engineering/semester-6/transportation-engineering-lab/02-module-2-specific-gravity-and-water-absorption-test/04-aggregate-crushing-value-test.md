# Transportation Engineering Lab: Module 2 - Aggregate Crushing Value Test

## 1. Introduction to Aggregate Crushing Value Test

This test is crucial in assessing the strength and durability of aggregates used in road construction. It determines the aggregate's resistance to crushing under a gradual compressive load. A lower Aggregate Crushing Value (ACV) indicates a stronger, more wear-resistant aggregate.

## 2. Learning Outcomes Covered

*   **Understanding the Purpose:** To determine the resistance of aggregate to crushing under a gradually applied compressive load.
*   **Familiarization with the Principle:** The test involves subjecting a representative sample of aggregate to a specific load in a cylindrical mould and measuring the proportion of material that passes a 2.36 mm sieve.
*   **Knowledge of Apparatus:** Understanding the function and use of the various equipment required for the test.
*   **Mastery of the Procedure:** Detailing the step-by-step process for conducting the test accurately.
*   **Calculation and Interpretation:** Calculating the ACV and understanding what the resulting value signifies in terms of aggregate quality.
*   **Significance and Applications:** Recognizing the importance of ACV in pavement design and material selection.

## 3. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Aggregate:** Inert granular material such as sand, gravel, crushed stone, or slag used in combination with a cementing agent to form hydraulic cement concrete, asphalt concrete, or other composite materials.
*   **Crushing Value:** A measure of the resistance of aggregate to crushing under a gradually applied compressive load. It is expressed as a percentage of the weight of the fine material produced when a sample is subjected to a specific loading regime.
*   **Specific Gravity:** The ratio of the density of a substance to the density of a given reference substance at a specified temperature. For aggregates, it relates to their density and voids. (Though not directly measured in this test, it's a related property of aggregates).
*   **Water Absorption:** The amount of water absorbed by the aggregate, usually expressed as a percentage of the dry weight. (Also a related property).
*   **Fine Aggregate:** Aggregate passing the 4.75 mm sieve.
*   **Coarse Aggregate:** Aggregate retained on the 4.75 mm sieve.
*   **Standard Crushing Value:** The value obtained when the test is conducted on aggregate passing a 10 mm sieve and retained on a 20 mm sieve, with a load of 40 tonnes (400 kN) applied for 10 minutes.
*   **ACV (Aggregate Crushing Value):** The percentage by weight of the material passing a 2.36 mm sieve after crushing.

## 4. Principle of the Test

The test is based on subjecting a compacted sample of aggregate to a gradually increasing load in a cylindrical steel mould. The load is applied using a compression testing machine. The percentage by weight of the crushed material passing a 2.36 mm sieve is determined. This percentage is the Aggregate Crushing Value.

**Formula:**

$ACV = (Weight\ of\ crushed\ material\ passing\ 2.36\ mm\ sieve / Weight\ of\ dry\ sample) \times 100$

## 5. Apparatus Required

*   **Aggregate Crushing Value Testing Machine:** A compression testing machine capable of applying a load of 40 tonnes (400 kN) at a controlled rate.
*   **Cylindrical Steel Mould:** A hollow steel cylinder with a precisely machined internal diameter and height. Standard dimensions are:
    *   Inner Diameter: 100 mm
    *   Height: 50 mm (or 100 mm for specific standards)
*   **Plunger:** A steel piston that fits snugly into the mould and transmits the load to the aggregate sample.
*   **Base Plate:** A sturdy steel plate on which the mould is placed.
*   **Tamping Rod:** A steel rod used for compacting the aggregate in the mould.
*   **Sieves:**
    *   10 mm sieve
    *   2.36 mm sieve
*   **Balance:** Accurate to 0.1 g.
*   **Oven:** For drying the aggregate sample.
*   **Steel Balls (for the modified test):** Used to ensure uniform load distribution.

## 6. Procedure for Conducting the Test

### 6.1 Sample Preparation

1.  **Sieve Analysis:** Perform a sieve analysis on the aggregate to obtain a representative sample passing a 10 mm sieve and retained on a 2.36 mm sieve.
2.  **Grading:** For the standard test, the aggregate sample should conform to the following grading:
    *   Aggregate passing 10 mm sieve and retained on 7.5 mm sieve: 25% by weight.
    *   Aggregate passing 7.5 mm sieve and retained on 5.0 mm sieve: 50% by weight.
    *   Aggregate passing 5.0 mm sieve and retained on 2.36 mm sieve: 25% by weight.
3.  **Drying:** Dry the prepared sample in an oven at a temperature of 100-110°C until it reaches a constant weight.
4.  **Cooling:** Allow the sample to cool to room temperature.

### 6.2 Performing the Test (Standard Procedure)

1.  **Weighing:** Weigh the dry, cooled, and sieved sample. Let this weight be 'W1'.
2.  **Compaction:** Place the mould on the base plate. Fill the mould in three layers, each layer being approximately one-third of the mould's volume. Compact each layer by 25 strokes of the tamping rod, distributed evenly over the surface.
3.  **Placing the Plunger:** Place the plunger on top of the compacted aggregate in the mould.
4.  **Applying Load:** Place the mould with the plunger in the compression testing machine. Apply the load gradually and uniformly at a rate of 40 tonnes (400 kN) in 10 minutes (or as specified by the relevant standard).
5.  **Removing the Plunger:** After the load application, remove the mould from the machine. Carefully remove the plunger.
6.  **Crushing the Sample:** Place the base plate back on the mould, and then place the plunger on the base plate. Invert the mould and place it back in the compression testing machine. Apply a load of 40 tonnes (400 kN) in 10 minutes. This process crushes the aggregate.
7.  **Collecting Crushed Material:** Remove the mould from the machine. Carefully remove the plunger. The crushed aggregate is now in the mould.
8.  **Separating Fine Material:** Carefully remove the crushed aggregate from the mould. Pass it through the 2.36 mm sieve.
9.  **Weighing Fine Material:** Weigh the material that passes the 2.36 mm sieve. Let this weight be 'W2'.

### 6.3 Modified Procedure (for soft aggregates)

For aggregates that are relatively soft or when a more consistent result is desired, a layer of steel balls (typically 25 mm diameter) is placed on top of the aggregate sample before inserting the plunger. This ensures a more uniform distribution of the applied load.

## 7. Calculations and Interpretation

**Aggregate Crushing Value (ACV) = (W2 / W1) x 100**

Where:
*   W2 = Weight of crushed material passing the 2.36 mm sieve.
*   W1 = Weight of the dry sample (initially placed in the mould).

**Interpretation of Results:**

*   **Low ACV:** Indicates a strong aggregate that is resistant to crushing. Suitable for high-traffic roads and surface courses.
*   **High ACV:** Indicates a weaker aggregate that crushes easily. May be suitable for lower traffic roads or as a base course material.

**Typical ACV Requirements (as per Indian Standards/IRC):**

| Road Layer       | Maximum Permissible ACV |
| :--------------- | :----------------------- |
| Surface Course   | 30%                      |
| Binder Course    | 35%                      |
| Base Course      | 40%                      |
| Sub-base Course  | 45%                      |

**Important Note:** These values can vary based on specific project requirements and local standards. Always refer to the project specifications.

## 8. Significance and Applications

*   **Pavement Design:** The ACV is a critical parameter in selecting aggregates for different layers of a pavement structure.
*   **Durability:** It provides an indication of the aggregate's ability to withstand the stresses and strains encountered during construction and service life, particularly from traffic loads.
*   **Wear Resistance:** Aggregates with low ACV are more resistant to wear and disintegration, contributing to a longer-lasting pavement.
*   **Material Selection:** Helps engineers choose appropriate aggregate sources for specific road construction projects.
*   **Quality Control:** Used as a quality control measure to ensure that aggregates meet specified strength requirements.

## 9. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
An aggregate sample weighing 500 g was subjected to the Aggregate Crushing Value test. After crushing and sieving through a 2.36 mm sieve, the material passing the sieve weighed 75 g. Calculate the Aggregate Crushing Value.

**Answer 1:**
W1 = 500 g
W2 = 75 g
ACV = (75 g / 500 g) x 100 = 15%

**Question 2:**
What is the main purpose of the Aggregate Crushing Value test?
a) To determine the density of aggregate.
b) To assess the resistance of aggregate to crushing under impact.
c) To assess the resistance of aggregate to crushing under a gradually applied compressive load.
d) To determine the water absorption of aggregate.

**Answer 2:**
c) To assess the resistance of aggregate to crushing under a gradually applied compressive load.

**Question 3:**
If an aggregate has a high Aggregate Crushing Value, what does it imply about its suitability for the surface course of a highway?

**Answer 3:**
A high Aggregate Crushing Value implies that the aggregate is weak and crushes easily. This makes it unsuitable for the surface course of a highway, which experiences the highest stresses from traffic and requires strong, durable aggregates.

**Question 4:**
List the main apparatus required for the Aggregate Crushing Value test.

**Answer 4:**
*   Aggregate Crushing Value Testing Machine
*   Cylindrical Steel Mould
*   Plunger
*   Base Plate
*   Tamping Rod
*   Sieves (10 mm, 2.36 mm)
*   Balance
*   Oven

**Question 5:**
What is the standard load applied in the ACV test, and for how long is it typically applied?

**Answer 5:**
The standard load applied is 40 tonnes (400 kN) and it is typically applied for 10 minutes.

## 10. Important Points to Remember

*   **Accurate Sieving:** Ensure the aggregate sample is accurately sieved to the specified grading before the test.
*   **Proper Compaction:** Consistent compaction of the aggregate within the mould is crucial for reproducible results.
*   **Controlled Loading Rate:** The load should be applied gradually and at the specified rate.
*   **Dry Sample:** The test must be conducted on a dry aggregate sample.
*   **Sieve Size:** The critical sieve size for determining the crushed material is 2.36 mm.
*   **Interpretation is Key:** Understand that a lower ACV signifies a stronger aggregate suitable for demanding applications.
*   **Refer to Standards:** Always adhere to the specific standards (e.g., IS, BS, ASTM) for precise apparatus dimensions, procedure details, and acceptable ACV limits.
*   **Modified Test:** Be aware of the modified test procedure for softer aggregates.
