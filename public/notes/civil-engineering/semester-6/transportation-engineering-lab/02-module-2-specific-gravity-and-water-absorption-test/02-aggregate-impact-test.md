# Transportation Engineering Lab: Module 2 - Aggregate Impact Test

## 1. Introduction

This module focuses on fundamental tests performed on aggregates, which are essential components of road construction. While Specific Gravity and Water Absorption are crucial for mix design, understanding the **Aggregate Impact Test** is vital for assessing the resistance of aggregates to **sudden shock or impact**.

## 3. Key Concepts and Definitions

### 3.1. Aggregate Impact Value (AIV)

*   **Definition:** The AIV is a measure of the **resistance of an aggregate to sudden shock or impact**. It quantifies the percentage by weight of the aggregate that is broken when subjected to a specific impact force.
*   **Significance:** Aggregates used in road construction are subjected to repeated stresses, including impact from moving traffic. A higher AIV indicates a weaker aggregate, making it more prone to crushing and disintegration under impact. This can lead to premature pavement distress.

### 3.2. Toughness

*   **Definition:** Toughness is the property of a material to resist fracture under sudden loads or impacts.
*   **Relation to AIV:** The Aggregate Impact Test directly measures the toughness of aggregates.

### 3.3. Brittle vs. Ductile Materials

*   **Brittle Materials:** Fracture or break with little deformation (e.g., glass, cast iron).
*   **Ductile Materials:** Can undergo significant plastic deformation before fracturing (e.g., steel, copper).
*   **Aggregate Behavior:** Aggregates are generally brittle materials. The AIV test helps to differentiate between aggregates with varying degrees of brittleness.

## 4. Equipment Required

*   **Impact Testing Machine:**
    *   A heavy base or foundation.
    *   A vertical guide rod.
    *   A cylindrical metal cup (mould) of specific dimensions to hold the aggregate sample.
    *   A hammer or ram of a specific weight, which falls from a fixed height.
    *   A mechanism to lift and release the hammer.
*   **Standard Sieves:**
    *   10 mm sieve (for retaining fine aggregate passing the 10 mm sieve).
    *   2.36 mm sieve (for separating the crushed material from the uncrushed).
*   **Balance:** Accurate to 0.1 g.
*   **Tamping Rod:** For compacting the aggregate in the mould.
*   **Measuring Cylinder:**
*   **Oven:** For drying the samples.
*   **Gloves and Safety Goggles:** For personal protection.

## 5. Procedure for Conducting the Aggregate Impact Test

The test is typically conducted on aggregate passing a 10 mm sieve and retained on a 4.75 mm sieve (or a similar size range as specified by standards). The procedure generally involves the following steps:

### 5.1. Sample Preparation

1.  **Obtain a representative sample** of the aggregate to be tested.
2.  **Dry the sample in an oven** at a temperature of 100-110°C until a constant weight is achieved.
3.  **Sieve the dried sample** over a 10 mm sieve.
4.  **Further sieve the material passing the 10 mm sieve** over a 4.75 mm sieve.
5.  **The aggregate retained on the 4.75 mm sieve** is used for the test.
6.  **Prepare two test specimens** of the aggregate.

### 5.2. Filling the Mould

1.  **Fill the cylindrical metal cup (mould) in three layers**, each layer being approximately one-third of the depth of the mould.
2.  **Compact each layer** by giving 25 strokes with the tamping rod. The strokes should be distributed evenly over the surface of the layer.
3.  **Ensure the aggregate fills the mould** and the surface is leveled.

### 5.3. Impact Application

1.  **Place the filled mould in the testing machine**.
2.  **Place the hammer** on the surface of the aggregate in the mould.
3.  **Raise the hammer** to the specified height (typically 380 mm from the top of the aggregate surface).
4.  **Release the hammer** freely to strike the aggregate.
5.  **Apply a total of 15 blows** to the aggregate sample in the mould. For the first blow, the hammer is dropped from the specified height. For subsequent blows, the hammer is lifted to the same height after each blow.
6.  **Note:** The hammer should be released without any interference or guidance.

### 5.4. Sieving and Weighing

1.  **Remove the moulded aggregate from the cup.**
2.  **Sieve the crushed aggregate** using a 2.36 mm sieve.
3.  **Weigh the portion of the aggregate that passes** the 2.36 mm sieve. Let this weight be 'W1'.
4.  **Weigh the original sample** used for this test. Let this weight be 'W'.

### 5.5. Repeat for Second Specimen

Repeat steps 5.1 to 5.4 for the second test specimen.

## 6. Calculation of Aggregate Impact Value (AIV)

The Aggregate Impact Value (AIV) is calculated as the percentage by weight of the material passing the 2.36 mm sieve after impact.

**Formula:**

$AIV = (W1 / W) * 100$

Where:
*   $W1$ = Weight of the fraction passing the 2.36 mm sieve (grams)
*   $W$ = Total weight of the sample before impact (grams)

**Example Calculation:**

Let's assume:
*   Weight of the original sample ($W$) = 200 grams
*   Weight of the material passing the 2.36 mm sieve after impact ($W1$) = 50 grams

$AIV = (50 / 200) * 100 = 25\%$

**Important Note:** The average of the results from the two specimens should be reported as the AIV. If the difference between the results of the two specimens is more than 5%, a third test should be conducted, and the average of the two closer results should be reported.

## 7. Interpretation of Results and Suitability of Aggregates

The AIV provides an indication of the aggregate's resistance to impact. Generally, lower AIV values indicate stronger and more durable aggregates.

| Pavement Layer            | Maximum Permissible AIV (%) |
| :------------------------ | :-------------------------- |
| Surface Course            | 30%                         |
| Base Course               | 35%                         |
| Sub-base Course           | 40%                         |

**Explanation:**

*   **Surface Course:** This layer is directly subjected to traffic loads and impact. Therefore, it requires aggregates with high resistance to impact (low AIV).
*   **Base Course:** This layer also experiences significant stress but is protected to some extent by the surface course. A slightly higher AIV is acceptable.
*   **Sub-base Course:** This layer is further protected and subjected to less direct impact. Aggregates with higher AIV can be used here.

**Example Scenario:**

If an aggregate is intended for use in the surface course of a highway and its AIV is found to be 35%, it would generally be considered unsuitable for this application based on the typical specifications.

## 8. Limitations and Factors Influencing Test Results

*   **Aggregate Size and Shape:** The test is sensitive to the size and shape of the aggregate particles. Using a consistent size range is crucial for comparable results.
*   **Moisture Content:** While the sample is dried before the test, residual moisture can affect the results.
*   **Compaction Effort:** Variations in the compaction of the aggregate in the mould can influence the distribution of impact.
*   **Hammer Drop Height and Weight:** Strict adherence to the specified height and weight of the hammer is essential.
*   **Sieve Analysis Accuracy:** The accuracy of the sieving process directly impacts the calculated AIV.
*   **Type of Aggregate:** Different rock types will naturally have varying impact resistance.
*   **Quality of the Testing Machine:** The calibration and condition of the impact testing machine are critical.

## 9. Practice Questions and Exercises

1.  **Define Aggregate Impact Value (AIV). What does a low AIV indicate?**
    *   **Answer:** The Aggregate Impact Value (AIV) is the percentage by weight of aggregate that breaks when subjected to a standard impact test. A low AIV indicates that the aggregate is resistant to sudden shock or impact, suggesting it is strong and durable.

2.  **List the key equipment required for conducting the Aggregate Impact Test.**
    *   **Answer:** Impact testing machine (with mould and hammer), standard sieves (10 mm, 2.36 mm), balance, tamping rod, oven, measuring cylinder, gloves, and safety goggles.

3.  **If a 200g sample of aggregate retained on a 4.75mm sieve was tested and the weight of material passing a 2.36mm sieve after impact was found to be 44g, calculate the AIV.**
    *   **Answer:** $AIV = (44g / 200g) * 100 = 22\%$.

4.  **An aggregate sample yields AIV values of 28% and 34% for the two specimens. What should be the reported AIV?**
    *   **Answer:** The difference between the results is $34\% - 28\% = 6\%$. Since this is greater than 5%, a third test should be conducted, and the average of the two closer results should be reported.

5.  **What is the maximum permissible AIV for aggregates used in the surface course of a pavement?**
    *   **Answer:** The maximum permissible AIV for aggregates used in the surface course is typically 30%.

## 10. Important Points to Remember

*   The Aggregate Impact Test is a measure of **toughness** and resistance to **shock/impact**.
*   The test is performed on aggregate passing a **10 mm sieve and retained on a 4.75 mm sieve**.
*   The hammer is dropped from a **fixed height (380 mm)**.
*   The result is expressed as the **percentage of material passing a 2.36 mm sieve**.
*   **Lower AIV values are desirable** for road construction, especially for the surface course.
*   Always follow the **standard procedure carefully** to ensure accurate and comparable results.
*   **Safety precautions**, including wearing gloves and safety goggles, are essential during the test.
