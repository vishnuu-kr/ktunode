# Transportation Engineering Lab: Module 2 - Specific Gravity and Water Absorption Test

## Topic: Stripping Value of Road Aggregates

### 1. Introduction and Importance

*   **What is Stripping?**
    *   Stripping refers to the **dislodgement or separation of the bitumen binder from the aggregate surface** in a bituminous mixture.
    *   This phenomenon occurs due to the presence of water, which can displace the bitumen film.
*   **Why is Stripping Important?**
    *   Stripping is a major cause of **premature failure** in flexible pavements.
    *   It leads to a loss of cohesion and strength in the bituminous layer, resulting in **raveling, potholes, and disintegration** of the road surface.
    *   The **adhesion** between bitumen and aggregate is crucial for the durability and performance of asphaltic mixes.

### 2. Factors Affecting Stripping

Several factors influence the susceptibility of aggregates to stripping:

*   **Aggregate Properties:**
    *   **Surface Texture:** Rougher surfaces generally provide better adhesion.
    *   **Mineralogy:**
        *   **Acidic/Siliceous aggregates** (e.g., granite, quartz) tend to be more susceptible to stripping due to their negatively charged surfaces, which have a weaker attraction to the polar components of bitumen.
        *   **Basic/Calcareous aggregates** (e.g., limestone, dolomite) generally exhibit better adhesion due to their positively charged surfaces, leading to stronger chemical attraction.
    *   **Surface Area:** Higher surface area can mean more sites for bitumen adhesion, but also more sites for water ingress.
*   **Bitumen Properties:**
    *   **Polarity:** Bitumen contains polar compounds that are responsible for adhesion to the aggregate surface.
    *   **Bitumen Content:** An adequate bitumen film thickness is necessary for good adhesion and resistance to stripping.
*   **Water Properties:**
    *   **Presence of Water:** Water is the primary agent that causes stripping by displacing the bitumen film.
    *   **pH of Water:** Acidic water can exacerbate stripping.
*   **Mixing and Compaction:**
    *   **Inadequate Mixing:** Poor bitumen coating can leave aggregate particles exposed to water.
    *   **Overheating:** Can alter bitumen properties and reduce adhesion.
    *   **Insufficient Compaction:** Creates voids where water can enter and initiate stripping.
*   **Environmental Conditions:**
    *   **Rainfall and High Humidity:** Increase the likelihood of water contact with the pavement.

### 3. Tests for Stripping Value of Road Aggregates

The "stripping value" is not a directly measured property in most common tests. Instead, we assess the **resistance to stripping** of bitumen-aggregate mixtures. The most common method is the **Visual Retained Coating Test**, often referred to as the "Stripping Test."

#### 3.1. Visual Retained Coating Test

This test aims to visually assess the percentage of aggregate surface still coated with bitumen after immersion in water.

*   **Objective:** To evaluate the ability of the bitumen binder to adhere to the aggregate surface in the presence of water.
*   **Principle:** A sample of bituminous mix is immersed in water, and the percentage of retained bitumen coating on the aggregate particles is visually estimated.

*   **Apparatus:**
    *   Bituminous mixture sample (prepared with a specific aggregate and bitumen)
    *   Beakers or containers for water immersion
    *   Hot plate or water bath for heating bitumen
    *   Sieve (e.g., 4.75 mm) for separating aggregate
    *   Drying oven
    *   Forceps or tongs for handling samples
    *   White light source for visual inspection

*   **Procedure (Typical steps, may vary slightly based on specific standards like IS 2386 Part II or ASTM D1664):**

    1.  **Preparation of Test Samples:**
        *   Prepare a batch of bituminous mixture using the aggregate under investigation and a specific type of bitumen at the optimum binder content or a specified content.
        *   The mixing temperature and procedure should follow standard practices.
        *   The aggregate should be heated to the appropriate mixing temperature before being mixed with the heated bitumen.

    2.  **Coating the Aggregate:**
        *   Ensure all aggregate particles are well coated with bitumen.

    3.  **Water Immersion:**
        *   Place a known quantity of the coated aggregate in a beaker.
        *   Cover the aggregate with distilled water (or tap water as specified).
        *   The water should be at room temperature or slightly elevated as per the standard.

    4.  **Soaking Period:**
        *   Allow the sample to soak for a specified period (e.g., 24 hours). Some standards may involve a heating period after immersion.

    5.  **Visual Examination:**
        *   After the soaking period, carefully decant the water.
        *   Gently agitate the aggregate sample to dislodge any loosely adhering bitumen.
        *   Visually inspect the aggregate particles under good lighting.
        *   Estimate the percentage of aggregate surface still covered with a coherent film of bitumen.

    6.  **Record Keeping:**
        *   Record the estimated percentage of retained bitumen coating.

*   **Interpretation of Results:**

    *   A higher percentage of retained bitumen coating indicates better resistance to stripping.
    *   **Acceptance Criteria:** Minimum retained coating percentages are specified by various highway authorities and standards. For example, a common requirement might be **95% retained coating for acceptable performance.**
    *   **Example Interpretation:** If, after immersion, 90% of the aggregate surface is still coated with bitumen, the retained coating is 90%. If only 60% is coated, the retained coating is 60%.

#### 3.2. Chemical Admixtures (Anti-stripping Agents)

*   **Purpose:** To improve the adhesion between bitumen and aggregate, thereby enhancing resistance to stripping.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Lime (Hydrated Lime):** A very common and effective anti-stripping agent. It reacts with the acidic components of bitumen and the aggregate surface, creating a stronger bond and making the film more resistant to water displacement.
    *   **Certain amines and other chemical compounds:** These can also be used, often as liquid additives.
*   **Mechanism:** They modify the surface chemistry of the aggregate and/or the bitumen to promote better adhesion and water resistance.

#### 3.3. Other Tests (Less Common for Direct Stripping Value)

While not directly measuring "stripping value," tests that assess adhesion properties indirectly include:

*   **Coating and Compaction Test (Marshall Test related):** While primarily for mix design, the visual inspection of coated aggregate during preparation and after compaction can give an indication.
*   **Immersion Compression Test (ASTM D1075 / IS 624):** This test measures the retained strength of a compacted bituminous mix after immersion in water compared to its strength in dry conditions. A higher retained strength suggests better resistance to moisture damage, which includes stripping.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Define stripping in the context of road aggregates and bituminous mixes.

**Answer:**
Stripping is the dislodgement or separation of the bitumen binder from the aggregate surface in a bituminous mixture, typically caused by the presence of water.

**Question 2:**
List three factors that can increase the susceptibility of aggregates to stripping.

**Answer:**
1.  Acidic/Siliceous mineralogy of the aggregate.
2.  Rough surface texture (can be positive or negative depending on other factors).
3.  Presence of water.
4.  Poor bitumen coating.

**Question 3:**
What is the main principle behind the Visual Retained Coating Test?

**Answer:**
The main principle is to visually estimate the percentage of aggregate surface that remains coated with bitumen after the bituminous mixture sample has been immersed in water for a specified period.

**Question 4:**
If a bituminous mix sample shows that 85% of the aggregate surface is still coated with bitumen after the stripping test, what is the retained coating value? Is this generally considered acceptable?

**Answer:**
The retained coating value is 85%. Generally, a minimum retained coating of 95% is considered acceptable for good performance. Therefore, 85% might be considered borderline or unacceptable depending on the specific project specifications.

**Question 5:**
Name one common anti-stripping agent used in road construction and briefly explain its role.

**Answer:**
Hydrated lime is a common anti-stripping agent. It improves adhesion between bitumen and aggregate, making the bitumen film more resistant to displacement by water, thereby reducing stripping.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Stripping is a critical failure mechanism** in flexible pavements.
*   **Water is the primary antagonist**, displacing the bitumen binder.
*   **Aggregate mineralogy plays a significant role:** Acidic aggregates are generally more prone to stripping than basic aggregates.
*   The **Visual Retained Coating Test** is a common method to assess resistance to stripping.
*   **High retained bitumen coating (e.g., ≥ 95%)** is desirable for durable pavement construction.
*   **Anti-stripping agents (like hydrated lime)** are often added to improve the bitumen-aggregate adhesion and mitigate stripping.
*   Proper **mixing temperature, bitumen content, and compaction** are essential for good adhesion and resistance to stripping.
