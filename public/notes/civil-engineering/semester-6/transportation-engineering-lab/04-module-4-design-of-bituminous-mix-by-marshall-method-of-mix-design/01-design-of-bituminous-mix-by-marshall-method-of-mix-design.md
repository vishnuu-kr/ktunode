# Transportation Engineering Lab: Module 4 - Design of Bituminous Mix by Marshall Method

This module introduces the **Marshall Method of Mix Design**, a widely used empirical method for determining the optimal combination of aggregate, asphalt binder, and additives to produce a durable and stable bituminous pavement mix.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

*   Understand the principles and objectives of bituminous mix design.
*   Identify the steps involved in the Marshall method of mix design.
*   Perform laboratory tests to evaluate the properties of bituminous mixes according to the Marshall method.
*   Analyze the results of Marshall tests to determine the optimal asphalt binder content.
*   Evaluate the suitability of a designed mix for specific pavement applications.

## 1. Introduction to Bituminous Mix Design

### 1.1. What is Bituminous Mix Design?

Bituminous mix design is the process of selecting and proportioning appropriate aggregates and asphalt binder to produce a pavement mixture that meets specific performance requirements, such as:

*   **Stability:** Resistance to deformation under load.
*   **Durability:** Resistance to weathering, aging, and the effects of traffic.
*   **Workability:** Ease of mixing, placing, and compaction.
*   **Economy:** Cost-effectiveness while meeting performance criteria.

### 1.2. Objectives of Bituminous Mix Design

The primary objectives are to achieve:

*   **Sufficient Strength:** To withstand traffic loads without excessive rutting or cracking.
*   **Adequate Durability:** To resist aging, moisture damage, and oxidation.
*   **Good Workability:** To ensure proper placement and compaction.
*   **Desirable Surface Characteristics:** Good skid resistance and smoothness.
*   **Economic Viability:** To produce a mix at a reasonable cost.

### 1.3. Factors Affecting Bituminous Mix Design

Several factors influence the choice of mix design:

*   **Traffic Volume and Axle Loads:** Higher traffic demands require stronger mixes.
*   **Climatic Conditions:** Extreme temperatures and moisture can affect mix performance.
*   **Aggregate Properties:** Gradation, shape, surface texture, and strength of aggregates are crucial.
*   **Asphalt Binder Properties:** Viscosity, penetration, and grade of asphalt binder are important.
*   **Environmental Considerations:** Availability of materials and pollution control.

## 2. The Marshall Method of Mix Design

The Marshall method is an empirical approach developed by Bruce Marshall in the 1930s. It focuses on evaluating the stability and flow properties of a compacted bituminous specimen under load.

### 2.1. Principles of the Marshall Method

*   **Compaction:** Specimens are compacted using a standard Marshall hammer.
*   **Stability:** The maximum load a specimen can withstand before failure.
*   **Flow:** The deformation of the specimen under the maximum load.
*   **Void Analysis:** Determination of air voids, voids in mineral aggregate (VMA), and voids filled with asphalt (VFA).

### 2.2. Key Components of the Marshall Method

*   **Asphalt Binder:** The adhesive material that coats and binds the aggregates.
*   **Aggregates:** Coarse aggregate, fine aggregate, and mineral filler.
*   **Additives (Optional):** Materials like anti-stripping agents or polymers to enhance performance.

### 2.3. Properties Evaluated

The Marshall method evaluates the following properties:

*   **Marshall Stability:** The peak load reached during the test.
*   **Marshall Flow:** The total deformation at the peak load, measured in units of 0.25 mm (or 1/100th inch).
*   **Voids in Total Mix (VTM):** The percentage of air voids in the compacted mix.
*   **Voids in Mineral Aggregate (VMA):** The volume of inter-granular void space between aggregate particles in a compacted mix.
*   **Voids Filled with Asphalt (VFA):** The percentage of VMA that is filled with asphalt binder.
*   **Unit Weight/Density:** The weight per unit volume of the compacted mix.
*   **Coefficients of Retained Stability:** Measures resistance to moisture damage.

## 3. Laboratory Procedure for Marshall Mix Design

This section outlines the step-by-step process for conducting Marshall tests.

### 3.1. Materials

*   **Asphalt Binder:** The asphalt cement to be used in the mix.
*   **Aggregates:**
    *   Coarse Aggregate: Typically retained on a 4.75 mm sieve.
    *   Fine Aggregate: Passing a 4.75 mm sieve.
    *   Mineral Filler: Very fine material, usually passing a 0.075 mm sieve (e.g., stone dust, hydrated lime).
*   **Equipment:**
    *   Heating Kettle/Oven
    *   Mixing Bowl
    *   Balance
    *   Sieves (various sizes)
    *   Graduated Cylinders
    *   Marshall Compactor (hammer and base plate)
    *   Marshall Press (for stability and flow testing)
    *   Marshall test head (for holding specimens)
    *   Thermometer
    *   Water bath (for temperature conditioning)
    *   Calipers
    *   Volume-measuring device (e.g., vacuum sealing apparatus or specific gravity jig)

### 3.2. Specimen Preparation

1.  **Aggregate Preparation:**
    *   Aggregates are dried, sieved into different size fractions, and stored separately.
    *   A trial aggregate blending is performed to achieve the desired gradation.
    *   The blending is repeated for each proposed asphalt binder content.

2.  **Mixing:**
    *   For each trial asphalt binder content (typically ranging from 4% to 6% by weight of aggregate, in 0.5% increments), the aggregates are heated to the mixing temperature recommended for the asphalt binder.
    *   The asphalt binder is heated to the mixing temperature.
    *   The heated aggregates and asphalt binder are thoroughly mixed by hand or using a mechanical mixer until a uniform coating is achieved.

3.  **Compaction:**
    *   The mixed material is placed in the Marshall mold.
    *   The mold is placed on the base plate of the Marshall compactor.
    *   The mixture is compacted with a standard Marshall hammer, which has a specific weight and drop height.
    *   Each side of the specimen is given **35 blows** (for standard traffic). Higher traffic may require 50 or 75 blows.
    *   The compacted specimen is then removed from the mold.

### 3.3. Testing of Compacted Specimens

For each trial asphalt binder content, at least three specimens are prepared.

1.  **Temperature Conditioning:**
    *   Compacted specimens are cooled to room temperature.
    *   They are then conditioned in a water bath at **93°C ± 0.5°C** for **30-35 minutes** (for asphalt binder grades typically used in India) or **40-45 minutes** (for lighter grades) before testing. The conditioning temperature can vary depending on the binder grade and climate.

2.  **Marshall Stability and Flow Test:**
    *   The conditioned specimen is placed in the Marshall test head.
    *   The specimen is subjected to a constant rate of loading (typically **50.8 mm/min** or **2 inches/min**) using the Marshall press.
    *   The maximum load (stability) and the corresponding deformation (flow) are recorded.

3.  **Density and Void Analysis:**
    *   **Bulk Specific Gravity ($G_m$):** The weight of the specimen in air and in water is measured to determine its bulk specific gravity.
    *   **Maximum Theoretical Specific Gravity ($G_{sb}$):** This is determined by compacting a loose mixture of the same composition to the maximum density without any air voids. The specific gravity of the asphalt binder ($G_b$) and aggregates ($G_{sa}$, $G_{sf}$) are also required.
    *   **Air Voids (VTM):** Calculated using the bulk specific gravity of the specimen and the maximum theoretical specific gravity.
    *   **Voids in Mineral Aggregate (VMA):** Calculated using the bulk specific gravity and the asphalt binder content.
    *   **Voids Filled with Asphalt (VFA):** Calculated using VTM and VMA.

### 3.4. Calculations and Analysis

**Key Formulas:**

*   **Bulk Specific Gravity ($G_m$):**
    $G_m = \frac{\text{Weight of specimen in air}}{(\text{Weight of specimen in air}) - (\text{Weight of specimen in water})}$

*   **Maximum Theoretical Specific Gravity ($G_{sb}$):**
    $G_{sb} = \frac{100}{\frac{P_a}{G_a} + \frac{P_b}{G_b} + \frac{P_f}{G_f}}$
    where:
    *   $P_a$ = Percentage by weight of coarse aggregate
    *   $G_a$ = Specific gravity of coarse aggregate
    *   $P_b$ = Percentage by weight of fine aggregate
    *   $G_b$ = Specific gravity of fine aggregate
    *   $P_f$ = Percentage by weight of filler
    *   $G_f$ = Specific gravity of filler (assumed to be same as fine aggregate if not specified)

*   **Unit Weight ($\gamma$):**
    $\gamma = G_m \times \rho_w$ (where $\rho_w$ is the unit weight of water, typically 1000 kg/m³ or 62.4 lb/ft³)

*   **Voids in Total Mix (VTM):**
    $VTM = (1 - \frac{G_m}{G_{sb}}) \times 100$

*   **Voids in Mineral Aggregate (VMA):**
    $VMA = (1 - \frac{G_m \times P_a}{G_{sa} \times P_{total}})$
    where:
    *   $P_{total}$ = Percentage of total aggregate by weight of mix
    *   $G_{sa}$ = Bulk specific gravity of the aggregate blend

*   **Voids Filled with Asphalt (VFA):**
    $VFA = (\frac{VMA - VTM}{VMA}) \times 100$

### 3.5. Determination of Optimal Asphalt Binder Content

1.  **Plotting:** For each asphalt binder content, the average values of stability, flow, VTM, VMA, and VFA are plotted against the percentage of asphalt binder.
2.  **Selection of Optimal Content:** The optimal asphalt binder content is determined as the average of the asphalt binder contents at which the following criteria are met:
    *   **Maximum Stability:** The peak of the stability curve.
    *   **Satisfactory Flow:** Within the specified range (e.g., 8-16 units of 0.25 mm for standard traffic).
    *   **Target VTM:** Within the specified range (e.g., 3-5% for asphalt concrete).
    *   **Target VFA:** Within the specified range (e.g., 65-75%).
3.  **Refinement:** If the initially selected optimal content doesn't satisfy all criteria, the aggregate gradation or binder content range might need to be adjusted, and the process repeated.

## 5. Examples and Practice Questions

### Example Problem:

Given the following test results for a bituminous mix:

| Asphalt Binder Content (%) | Stability (kN) | Flow (mm of 0.25) | Bulk SG ($G_m$) | Max Theoretical SG ($G_{sb}$) | Unit Weight (kg/m³) |
| :------------------------- | :------------- | :---------------- | :------------ | :---------------------------- | :------------------ |
| 4.0                        | 12.0           | 10                | 2.350         | 2.550                         | 2350                |
| 4.5                        | 14.5           | 12                | 2.375         | 2.545                         | 2375                |
| 5.0                        | 16.0           | 14                | 2.390         | 2.540                         | 2390                |
| 5.5                        | 15.5           | 16                | 2.405         | 2.535                         | 2405                |
| 6.0                        | 14.0           | 18                | 2.415         | 2.530                         | 2415                |

*Assume the aggregate blend has a specific gravity of 2.65 and the asphalt binder has a specific gravity of 1.03.*

**Calculate:**

a)  VTM for each asphalt binder content.
b)  VMA for each asphalt binder content (assume total aggregate is 95% and asphalt is 5% by weight of mix for the 5% binder content calculation). *Note: For accurate VMA, you'd need the aggregate gradations and their individual specific gravities.* For this simplified example, we'll make a reasonable assumption for VMA at 5% binder content.
c)  VFA for each asphalt binder content at 5% binder content.
d)  Determine the optimal asphalt binder content based on typical criteria (Stability > 9 kN, Flow = 8-16, VTM = 3-5%).

**Solutions:**

**a) VTM Calculation:**

$VTM = (1 - \frac{G_m}{G_{sb}}) \times 100$

*   **4.0%:** $VTM = (1 - \frac{2.350}{2.550}) \times 100 = (1 - 0.9215) \times 100 = 7.85\%$
*   **4.5%:** $VTM = (1 - \frac{2.375}{2.545}) \times 100 = (1 - 0.9332) \times 100 = 6.68\%$
*   **5.0%:** $VTM = (1 - \frac{2.390}{2.540}) \times 100 = (1 - 0.9409) \times 100 = 5.91\%$
*   **5.5%:** $VTM = (1 - \frac{2.405}{2.535}) \times 100 = (1 - 0.9487) \times 100 = 5.13\%$
*   **6.0%:** $VTM = (1 - \frac{2.415}{2.530}) \times 100 = (1 - 0.9545) \times 100 = 4.55\%$

**b) VMA Calculation (for 5.0% binder content as an example):**

To calculate VMA accurately, we need the specific gravities of the aggregate components and their proportions. Let's assume for the sake of this example that at 5% binder content, the VMA is found to be 17.0%.

**c) VFA Calculation (for 5.0% binder content):**

$VFA = (\frac{VMA - VTM}{VMA}) \times 100$
$VFA = (\frac{17.0 - 5.91}{17.0}) \times 100 = (\frac{11.09}{17.0}) \times 100 = 65.24\%$

**d) Optimal Asphalt Binder Content:**

| Asphalt Binder Content (%) | Stability (kN) | Flow (mm of 0.25) | VTM (%) | Meets Criteria? |
| :------------------------- | :------------- | :---------------- | :------ | :-------------- |
| 4.0                        | 12.0           | 10                | 7.85    | No (VTM too high) |
| 4.5                        | 14.5           | 12                | 6.68    | No (VTM too high) |
| 5.0                        | 16.0           | 14                | 5.91    | No (VTM slightly high) |
| 5.5                        | 15.5           | 16                | 5.13    | Yes             |
| 6.0                        | 14.0           | 18                | 4.55    | Yes (Flow slightly high) |

Based on the typical criteria (Stability > 9 kN, Flow = 8-16, VTM = 3-5%):

*   At 5.5% binder content, Stability is 15.5 kN, Flow is 16 mm, and VTM is 5.13%. This is very close to meeting all criteria.
*   At 6.0% binder content, Stability is 14.0 kN, Flow is 18 mm (exceeds max), and VTM is 4.55%.

**Therefore, based on these limited results, 5.5% asphalt binder content appears to be the most suitable, as it closely meets all criteria. A finer adjustment around this value might be considered in a real-world scenario.**

### Practice Question 4:

If a Marshall specimen has a bulk specific gravity ($G_m$) of 2.380 and a maximum theoretical specific gravity ($G_{sb}$) of 2.560, what is the percentage of Voids in Total Mix (VTM)?

**Answer:**
$VTM = (1 - \frac{G_m}{G_{sb}}) \times 100$
$VTM = (1 - \frac{2.380}{2.560}) \times 100$
$VTM = (1 - 0.9297) \times 100$
$VTM = 7.03\%$

## 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Aggregate Gradation is Key:** The gradation of aggregates significantly influences the properties of the mix.
*   **Binder Content:** The amount of asphalt binder is critical for both performance and durability. Too little binder leads to ravelling, while too much leads to rutting.
*   **Temperature Control:** Maintaining correct mixing and compaction temperatures is vital for proper coating and compaction.
*   **Compaction Effort:** The number of blows from the Marshall hammer directly affects the density and void content of the specimen.
*   **Specimen Consistency:** Prepare multiple specimens for each binder content to ensure reliable results.
*   **Project Specifications:** Always adhere to the specific design criteria provided by the project engineer or relevant transportation authority.
*   **Environmental Factors:** Consider how climate and moisture will affect the chosen mix design.
*   **Quality Control:** The Marshall method is a primary tool for quality control during asphalt paving operations.
