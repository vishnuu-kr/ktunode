# TRAFFIC ENGINEERING AND MANAGEMENT - Module 2: Scope of Traffic Management Measures - Part 1

## Introduction

This module delves into specific traffic management measures that aim to improve traffic flow, safety, and efficiency. We will explore how restricting turning movements, implementing one-way streets, utilizing tidal flow operations, closing side streets, and establishing exclusive bus lanes can significantly impact urban transportation systems.

---

## 1. Restrictions to Turning Movements

**Key Concept:** Limiting or prohibiting specific turning movements at intersections can significantly improve traffic flow and reduce conflicts.

**Definition:** Turning movement restrictions involve designating specific lanes for particular turns (e.g., mandatory left turn lanes) or completely prohibiting certain turns (e.g., no left turn during peak hours).

**Objectives:**

*   **Reduce conflicts:** Minimize interactions between opposing traffic streams, pedestrians, and turning vehicles.
*   **Increase intersection capacity:** Allow through traffic to proceed more freely by eliminating or delaying turning vehicles.
*   **Improve safety:** Reduce the likelihood of collisions, especially those involving turning vehicles.
*   **Simplify traffic control:** Make signal phasing and operation more straightforward.

**Types of Restrictions:**

*   **No Left Turn/No Right Turn:** Prohibits a specific turning movement at an intersection.
    *   **Example:** A "No Left Turn" sign during peak hours on a busy arterial road to allow uninterrupted flow of through traffic.
*   **Mandatory Turn Lanes:** Designates a lane for a specific turn (e.g., "Left Turn Only," "Right Turn Only").
    *   **Example:** At a large intersection, a lane designated "Left Turn Only" ensures vehicles intending to turn left do not block through traffic.
*   **Protected/Permitted Turns (Phased Turns):** Signalization that allows turns only during specific phases (protected) or during the general traffic phase (permitted).
    *   **Example:** A protected left turn phase where a green arrow allows left turns without conflicting with oncoming traffic.
*   **Prohibition of U-Turns:** Prevents vehicles from making a U-turn at specific locations.
    *   **Example:** Prohibiting U-turns on highways or at busy intersections to avoid creating severe conflicts.

**Implementation Considerations:**

*   **Traffic Volume:** High volumes of specific turning movements might necessitate restrictions.
*   **Intersection Geometry:** The layout of the intersection plays a crucial role in determining the feasibility and effectiveness of restrictions.
*   **Pedestrian Activity:** High pedestrian volumes may require stricter turning restrictions to enhance pedestrian safety.
*   **Alternative Routes:** Ensuring that prohibited turns have viable alternative routes is essential.

## 2. One-Way Streets

**Key Concept:** Designating streets where traffic can only travel in one direction.

**Definition:** A one-way street is a road or highway that is designated to carry traffic in only one direction.

**Objectives:**

*   **Improve Traffic Flow:** Reduce the number of conflict points (e.g., opposing turns, head-on collisions) and simplify traffic movements.
*   **Increase Road Capacity:** Allow for more efficient use of road space, often enabling narrower lanes or additional lanes in the single direction.
*   **Enhance Safety:** Eliminate or significantly reduce head-on collisions and improve visibility for drivers.
*   **Simplify Traffic Control:** Easier to manage signal timing and reduce the complexity of intersection control.
*   **Facilitate Pedestrian and Cyclist Movement:** Can create safer environments for non-motorized users by reducing the number of approaching vehicles.

**Implementation Considerations:**

*   **Street Network Design:** Often implemented in grids or coordinated systems to create efficient circulation patterns.
*   **Direction of Travel:** Consideration of prevailing traffic patterns and origins/destinations.
*   **Access to Properties:** Ensuring businesses and residences still have adequate access.
*   **Traffic Signal Coordination:** Can facilitate smoother progression of traffic along the street.
*   **Public Awareness:** Clear signage and public education are vital for driver compliance.

**Examples:**

*   **Downtown Business Districts:** Many city centers implement one-way streets to manage high traffic volumes and improve circulation.
*   **One-Way Pairs:** Two parallel streets designated as one-way in opposite directions can effectively handle bidirectional traffic flow.

## 3. Tidal Flow Operations

**Key Concept:** Reversing the direction of traffic on a street or bridge to match peak travel demands in different directions.

**Definition:** Tidal flow operations involve dynamically changing the direction of traffic on a roadway based on the time of day or other demand patterns.

**Objectives:**

*   **Maximize Road Capacity:** Utilize the full width of the roadway to serve the dominant flow direction during peak periods.
*   **Improve Peak Hour Performance:** Significantly reduce congestion during morning and evening commutes.
*   **Efficient Use of Infrastructure:** Optimize the use of existing road space without requiring major construction.

**Methods of Implementation:**

*   **Lane Reversible:** Changing the direction of traffic on one or more lanes.
*   **Barrier Reversible:** Moving physical barriers (e.g., concrete barriers) to redefine lane directions.
*   **Signal Reversible:** Using traffic signals and lane control signals to indicate the direction of travel.

**Key Features:**

*   **Lane Control Signals:** Overhead signals (e.g., green arrows, red crosses) are crucial for indicating which lanes are open and in which direction.
*   **Physical Barriers:** Often used to clearly delineate traffic lanes and prevent head-on collisions.
*   **Timed Operations:** Typically implemented during specific hours to coincide with peak travel demands.

**Examples:**

*   **Bridges and Tunnels:** Tidal flow is commonly used on bridges and tunnels with multiple lanes to accommodate the higher demand in one direction during morning commutes and the opposite direction during evening commutes.
*   **Urban Arterial Streets:** Some major arterial roads in dense urban areas may employ tidal flow operations.

**Important Considerations:**

*   **Safety:** Strict adherence to lane control signals and physical barriers is paramount to prevent head-on collisions.
*   **Complexity:** Requires sophisticated traffic control systems and careful planning.
*   **Public Education:** Drivers need to be well-informed about the operation and adhere to the signals.

## 4. Closing Side Streets

**Key Concept:** Temporarily or permanently closing intersecting side streets to improve flow and safety on a main arterial.

**Definition:** This involves preventing vehicles from entering or exiting a main street from specific side streets.

**Objectives:**

*   **Improve Flow on Main Arterial:** Eliminate or reduce the number of conflict points and delays caused by traffic entering or exiting side streets.
*   **Enhance Safety:** Reduce the number of potential conflict points, especially at high-speed arterials or where visibility is limited.
*   **Reduce Driver Confusion:** Simplify decision-making for drivers on the main arterial.

**Methods of Implementation:**

*   **Permanent Closures:** Physically closing off the intersection of a side street with a main road.
*   **Temporary Closures:** Using barriers or temporary signage to close side streets during specific periods (e.g., peak hours, special events).
*   **Partial Closures:** Prohibiting certain movements from side streets (e.g., closing a side street to left turns onto the arterial).

**Examples:**

*   **Rural Intersections:** Closing minor side roads at a major rural highway to improve safety and throughput.
*   **Urban Arterial with High Traffic Volume:** Temporarily closing some side streets during peak hours to maintain smooth flow on the main road.

**Implementation Considerations:**

*   **Impact on Local Access:** Need to ensure continued access to properties on the closed side streets via alternative routes.
*   **Traffic Diversion:** Understanding where traffic from the closed side streets will be diverted.
*   **Community Impact:** Consulting with residents and businesses affected by the closure.

## 5. Exclusive Bus Lanes

**Key Concept:** Dedicated lanes on a roadway specifically for the use of buses.

**Definition:** Bus lanes are marked and often signal-prioritized lanes designated for public transit buses.

**Objectives:**

*   **Improve Bus Speed and Reliability:** Allow buses to bypass general traffic congestion, leading to faster and more predictable journey times.
*   **Increase Transit Ridership:** Make public transit a more attractive option by improving its efficiency.
*   **Reduce Overall Traffic Congestion:** By encouraging modal shift to public transit.
*   **Improve Air Quality:** By promoting the use of more fuel-efficient public transport.

**Types of Bus Lanes:**

*   **Peak Period Bus Lanes:** Designated for buses only during specific peak hours.
*   **24-Hour Bus Lanes:** Permanently reserved for buses.
*   **Queue Jump Lanes:** Short bus lanes at intersections that allow buses to bypass queues of general traffic.
*   **Bus-Only Streets:** Entire streets dedicated to bus traffic.

**Implementation Considerations:**

*   **Bus Route Network:** Strategic placement along high-ridership bus routes.
*   **Roadway Capacity:** Balancing the needs of buses with other road users.
*   **Enforcement:** Effective enforcement is crucial to ensure compliance.
*   **Integration with Traffic Signals:** Bus priority at signals can further enhance performance.

**Examples:**

*   **Curbside Bus Lanes:** Typically located at the edge of the roadway.
*   **Center Median Bus Lanes:** Located in the median of wide arterials, often with physical separation.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Exclusive bus lanes require clear signage, pavement markings, and enforcement to be effective.
*   They are a key component of Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) systems.

## Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What are the primary objectives of restricting turning movements at intersections?
**Answer:** The primary objectives are to reduce conflicts, increase intersection capacity, improve safety, and simplify traffic control.

**Question 2:** Explain the concept of "tidal flow operations" and provide an example of where it is commonly implemented.
**Answer:** Tidal flow operations involve reversing the direction of traffic on a roadway to match peak travel demands. It is commonly implemented on bridges and tunnels with multiple lanes to accommodate higher demand in one direction during morning commutes and the opposite direction during evening commutes.

**Question 3:** What are the advantages of implementing one-way streets?
**Answer:** Advantages include improved traffic flow, increased road capacity, enhanced safety by reducing head-on collisions, and simplified traffic control.

**Question 4:** What is a key consideration when implementing the closure of side streets to improve flow on a main arterial?
**Answer:** A key consideration is the impact on local access and ensuring that properties on the closed side streets still have adequate access via alternative routes.

**Question 5:** Why are exclusive bus lanes important for urban transportation systems?
**Answer:** Exclusive bus lanes improve bus speed and reliability, increase transit ridership, and can help reduce overall traffic congestion by encouraging a shift to public transport.

## Summary of Key Points

*   **Turning Movement Restrictions:** Essential for improving safety and flow by managing conflicts at intersections.
*   **One-Way Streets:** A fundamental tool for organizing traffic flow, enhancing safety, and increasing road capacity.
*   **Tidal Flow Operations:** A dynamic strategy to maximize road capacity during peak periods by reversing traffic direction. Requires careful implementation and strict adherence to safety protocols.
*   **Closing Side Streets:** A measure to improve the performance of main arterials by reducing interference from intersecting traffic.
*   **Exclusive Bus Lanes:** Crucial for improving the efficiency and attractiveness of public transit, contributing to better urban mobility and reduced congestion.
