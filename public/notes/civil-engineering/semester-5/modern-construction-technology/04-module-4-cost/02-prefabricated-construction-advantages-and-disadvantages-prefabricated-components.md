## 1. Introduction to Prefabricated Construction

### **1.1 Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Prefabricated Construction (Off-site Construction/Industrialized Building):** A construction method where building components are manufactured in a factory or controlled off-site environment and then transported to the construction site for assembly.
*   **Modular Construction:** A type of prefabricated construction where entire rooms or sections of a building are pre-built as modules in a factory and then assembled on-site.
*   **Pre-engineered Building (PEB):** A building where the structural framework is fabricated in a factory and then assembled on-site. Often used for industrial and commercial buildings.
*   **Standardization:** The use of common sizes, designs, and specifications for building components to facilitate mass production and interchangeability.

### **1.2 Principles of Prefabricated Construction:**

*   **Factory Production:** Manufacturing components in a controlled factory setting ensures higher quality, accuracy, and efficiency compared to on-site work.
*   **Transportation:** Components are designed for efficient and safe transportation from the factory to the construction site.
*   **On-site Assembly:** Components are delivered to the site and assembled by skilled workers using specialized equipment.
*   **Integration:** A high degree of coordination is required between the factory production and on-site assembly stages.

---

## 2. Advantages of Prefabricated Construction

### **2.1 Reduced Construction Time:**

*   **Simultaneous Activities:** Factory production of components can happen concurrently with site preparation (e.g., foundation laying), significantly shortening the overall project timeline.
*   **Weather Independence:** Manufacturing in a factory is not affected by weather conditions, leading to consistent progress.
*   **Faster Assembly:** On-site assembly of pre-made components is typically much faster than traditional on-site construction.
