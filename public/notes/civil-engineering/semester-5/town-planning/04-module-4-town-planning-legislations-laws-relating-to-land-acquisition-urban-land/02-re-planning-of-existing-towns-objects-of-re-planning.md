# Town Planning: Module 4 - Town Planning Legislations

## Topic: Re-planning of Existing Towns: Objects of Re-planning

### 1. Introduction to Re-planning of Existing Towns

Re-planning of existing towns, often referred to as **urban renewal**, **urban redevelopment**, or **urban regeneration**, is a critical aspect of town planning. It involves the process of improving and revitalizing existing urban areas that have become obsolete, degraded, or are no longer functioning efficiently according to contemporary needs and standards.

**Key Concept:** **Urban Renewal** - The process of improving and revitalizing existing urban areas that have become obsolete, degraded, or are no longer functioning efficiently.

**Why is Re-planning Necessary?**

*   **Aging Infrastructure:** Most existing towns have infrastructure that is old and needs upgrading (e.g., water supply, sewerage, roads, power).
*   **Obsolete Land Uses:** Land uses might have changed over time, leading to conflicts (e.g., residential areas encroaching on industrial zones).
*   **Slums and Blighted Areas:** Deterioration of housing, unsanitary conditions, and lack of basic amenities lead to the formation of slums and blighted areas.
*   **Traffic Congestion:** Inefficient road networks and growing vehicle ownership result in severe traffic congestion.
*   **Environmental Degradation:** Pollution, lack of green spaces, and inadequate waste management negatively impact the quality of life.
*   **Economic Decline:** Outdated infrastructure and a poor urban environment can hinder economic growth and investment.
*   **Social Segregation:** Spatial planning can inadvertently lead to social segregation, which re-planning can address.
*   **Disaster Vulnerability:** Existing towns may not be designed to withstand natural or man-made disasters.

### 2. Objects of Re-planning of Existing Towns

The primary objective of re-planning is to **improve the quality of life for its residents** and to **enhance the functionality, sustainability, and resilience of the urban environment.** This overarching goal is achieved through several specific objectives:

#### 2.1. Improvement of Living Conditions and Public Health

*   **Slum Upgradation/Clearance:** Removing or upgrading substandard housing and providing access to basic amenities like clean water, sanitation, and electricity.
    *   **Example:** Bombay Slum Areas (Improvement, Clearance and Redevelopment) Act, 1974, facilitates the improvement and redevelopment of slum areas in Mumbai.
*   **Provision of Basic Services:** Ensuring adequate provision and upgrading of water supply, sewerage, solid waste management, and drainage systems.
*   **Enhancing Public Health:** Creating healthier living environments by reducing overcrowding, improving sanitation, and increasing access to green spaces.

#### 2.2. Modernization of Infrastructure and Services

*   **Upgrading Transportation Networks:** Improving road networks, introducing public transport systems, creating pedestrian walkways, and managing traffic flow to reduce congestion.
    *   **Example:** Redevelopment of Ring Road in Delhi aims to improve traffic circulation and reduce congestion.
*   **Modernizing Utilities:** Upgrading water supply, sewerage, stormwater drainage, and electricity distribution networks to meet current demands and improve efficiency.
*   **Improving Communication Infrastructure:** Facilitating the integration of modern communication technologies.

#### 2.3. Optimization of Land Use and Density

*   **Rationalizing Land Use:** Resolving conflicts between different land uses and ensuring that land is used in a more efficient and beneficial manner.
    *   **Example:** Converting old industrial areas that are no longer economically viable into mixed-use developments with residential, commercial, and recreational spaces.
*   **Controlling Urban Sprawl:** Encouraging more compact development within existing urban boundaries to preserve surrounding natural or agricultural land.
*   **Achieving Optimal Density:** Re-planning can involve increasing density in suitable areas to support public transport and provide more housing, or decreasing density in congested areas to improve living conditions.

#### 2.4. Economic Revitalization and Development

*   **Attracting Investment:** Creating a more attractive urban environment can encourage new businesses and investments.
*   **Job Creation:** Redevelopment projects often create employment opportunities during the construction phase and through new businesses.
*   **Enhancing Property Values:** Improving infrastructure and amenities can lead to increased property values.
*   **Promoting Mixed-Use Development:** Creating vibrant neighborhoods with a mix of residential, commercial, and recreational activities can stimulate local economies.

#### 2.5. Environmental Improvement and Sustainability

*   **Increasing Green Spaces:** Creating and enhancing parks, gardens, and urban forests to improve air quality, reduce the urban heat island effect, and provide recreational opportunities.
*   **Pollution Control:** Implementing measures to reduce air, water, and noise pollution, and improving waste management systems.
    *   **Example:** The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, and the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, provide the framework for environmental protection.
*   **Promoting Sustainable Practices:** Encouraging energy efficiency, water conservation, and the use of sustainable building materials.
*   **Disaster Mitigation and Resilience:** Incorporating disaster-resistant design and planning for new infrastructure and buildings to reduce vulnerability to natural hazards.
    *   **Example:** Building codes that mandate earthquake-resistant construction in seismic zones.

#### 2.6. Social Equity and Community Improvement

*   **Promoting Social Integration:** Designing urban spaces that encourage interaction among different social groups and reduce social segregation.
*   **Improving Accessibility:** Ensuring that urban areas are accessible to all, including people with disabilities and the elderly.
*   **Preserving Heritage:** Identifying and preserving historical buildings and areas that contribute to the character and identity of the town.
*   **Community Participation:** Involving residents in the planning and decision-making processes to ensure that re-planning meets their needs and aspirations.

### 3. Interrelation with Legislations and Guidelines (Module 4 Context)

The objects of re-planning are intrinsically linked to the legislations and guidelines mentioned in Module 4.

*   **Laws relating to Land Acquisition:** Re-planning often necessitates the acquisition of land to assemble sites for redevelopment or to create new infrastructure. The **Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013** (LARR Act, 2013) governs this process.
*   **Urban Land Ceiling:** Acts like the **Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act, 1976** (though repealed in most states) aimed to prevent the concentration of urban land in the hands of a few and make surplus land available for public purposes, including redevelopment.
*   **UDPFI Guidelines (Urban Development Plans Formulation and Implementation Guidelines):** These national guidelines provide a framework for preparing integrated urban development plans, which can include strategies for re-planning existing urban areas to promote sustainable and equitable development.
*   **Disaster Mitigation Management:** The **Disaster Management Act, 2005** and associated policies emphasize the need to build resilient infrastructure and communities. Re-planning efforts must incorporate disaster risk reduction measures.
*   **Environmental and Pollution Control Acts:** The **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**, along with the Water and Air Acts, mandates pollution control and environmental impact assessment, which are crucial considerations in any re-planning project to ensure environmental sustainability.

### 4. Key Points to Remember

*   Re-planning of existing towns is about **improving quality of life and urban functionality.**
*   It addresses issues stemming from **aging infrastructure, obsolete land uses, and environmental degradation.**
*   The core objects include **improving living conditions, modernizing infrastructure, optimizing land use, economic revitalization, environmental sustainability, and social equity.**
*   Re-planning efforts are guided and facilitated by various **legislations and national guidelines.**
*   **Community participation and heritage preservation** are increasingly important aspects of successful re-planning.
