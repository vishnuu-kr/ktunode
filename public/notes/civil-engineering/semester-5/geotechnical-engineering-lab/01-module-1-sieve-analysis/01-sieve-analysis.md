# Geotechnical Engineering Lab: Module 1 - Sieve Analysis

## Topic: Sieve Analysis

### 1. Introduction to Sieve Analysis

Sieve analysis is a fundamental laboratory test used in geotechnical engineering to determine the particle-size distribution of soil samples. This distribution, often referred to as the **gradation** or **texture** of the soil, significantly influences its engineering properties, such as:

*   **Permeability:** The ability of water to flow through the soil.
*   **Compressibility:** How much the soil deforms under load.
*   **Shear Strength:** The soil's resistance to sliding.
*   **Workability:** Ease of manipulation during construction.

**Key Concept:** Sieve analysis is primarily used for soils containing **coarser particles** (gravel and sand). For finer soils (silt and clay), **hydrometer analysis** is employed.

### 3. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Sieve:** A wire mesh or perforated plate with square openings of a specific size.
*   **Sieve Series:** A standard set of sieves with progressively smaller opening sizes, arranged in a geometric progression.
*   **Retained Soil:** Soil particles that are too large to pass through a specific sieve opening.
*   **Passing Soil:** Soil particles that are small enough to pass through a specific sieve opening.
*   **Sieve Number:** A designation given to a sieve based on the number of openings per inch (e.g., a No. 4 sieve has 4 openings per inch).
*   **Sieve Opening:** The clear space between the wires of a sieve mesh.
*   **Dry Weight of Sample:** The weight of the soil sample after it has been completely dried in an oven.
*   **Weight Retained on Sieve:** The weight of soil particles that did not pass through a particular sieve.
*   **Cumulative Weight Retained:** The sum of the weights retained on a sieve and all sieves above it in the series.
*   **Percentage Retained:** The ratio of the weight retained on a sieve to the total dry weight of the sample, expressed as a percentage.
*   **Cumulative Percentage Retained:** The ratio of the cumulative weight retained on a sieve to the total dry weight of the sample, expressed as a percentage.
*   **Cumulative Percentage Passing:** The percentage of the total dry weight of the sample that has passed through a particular sieve. It is calculated as 100% - Cumulative Percentage Retained.
*   **Particle-Size Distribution Curve (Gradation Curve):** A semi-logarithmic plot showing the cumulative percentage of soil passing versus the sieve opening size (usually plotted on a log scale).
*   **Effective Size (D10):** The sieve opening size through which 10% of the soil by weight passes.
*   **Uniformity Coefficient (Cu):** A measure of the range of particle sizes in a soil. Calculated as $C_u = D_{60} / D_{10}$.
*   **Coefficient of Curvature (Cc):** A measure of the shape of the gradation curve. Calculated as $C_c = (D_{30})^2 / (D_{10} \times D_{60})$.

### 4. Equipment and Materials

*   **Soil Sample:** A representative portion of the soil to be tested.
*   **Sieve Shaker:** A mechanical device that vibrates and tumbles the sieves to separate particles.
*   **Set of Sieves:** A standard set of sieves with progressively smaller openings (e.g., US Standard Sieve Series or ASTM Sieve Series). Common sieves include:
    *   No. 4 (4.75 mm)
    *   No. 10 (2.00 mm)
    *   No. 40 (0.425 mm)
    *   No. 200 (0.075 mm)
*   **Sieve Brushes:** For cleaning sieves.
*   **Bottom Pan:** To collect the finest particles.
*   **Sieve Cover:** To prevent sample loss during shaking.
*   **Balance:** Accurate to at least 0.1 g.
*   **Oven:** For drying the soil sample (typically at 110 ± 5°C).
*   **Sample Splitter (optional):** For obtaining a representative smaller portion of a large sample.
*   **Trowel or Scoop:** For handling the soil.

### 6. Procedure for Sieve Analysis

1.  **Sample Preparation:**
    *   Obtain a representative dry soil sample. If the sample is moist, air-dry it first or oven-dry it at 110 ± 5°C until constant weight is achieved.
    *   If the total dry weight of the sample is too large, use a sample splitter to obtain a smaller, representative portion. The total dry weight of the sample should be sufficient to yield a significant amount of material on the finer sieves (e.g., a minimum of 100g for soils with significant fine content).
    *   **Crucially, ensure that the sample being sieved contains only particles that can be retained on the largest sieve in the set.** If the sample contains particles larger than the largest sieve, these must be separated and weighed first, and then the remaining finer portion is subjected to sieve analysis.

2.  **Assemble the Sieve Stack:**
    *   Arrange the sieves in order from coarsest at the top to finest at the bottom, with the bottom pan below the finest sieve (usually the No. 200 sieve).
    *   Ensure the sieves are clean and undamaged.

3.  **Weigh the Sample:**
    *   Accurately weigh the prepared dry soil sample. This is your **total dry weight ($W_{total}$)**.

4.  **Place the Sample on the Top Sieve:**
    *   Carefully place the weighed dry soil sample onto the uppermost sieve in the stack.
    *   Place the cover on top.

5.  **Sieve the Sample:**
    *   Place the assembled sieve stack into the sieve shaker.
    *   Operate the sieve shaker for a sufficient duration (typically 5-10 minutes) to ensure complete separation of particles. The shaking should impart both vertical and horizontal motion, as well as a slight tilting action.

6.  **Weigh the Soil on Each Sieve:**
    *   After shaking, carefully remove the sieve stack.
    *   Starting from the top sieve, carefully remove each sieve.
    *   Weigh the soil retained on each sieve using the balance. **Use a sieve brush to ensure all particles are removed from the mesh.**
    *   Record the weight of soil retained on each sieve.

7.  **Weigh the Fines Passing the Bottom Sieve:**
    *   The soil collected in the bottom pan is the material that passed the finest sieve (e.g., the No. 200 sieve). Weigh this material.

8.  **Record and Calculate:**
    *   Tabulate the sieve numbers, sieve openings, weight retained on each sieve, and the weight passing the bottom sieve.
    *   Calculate the **weight retained** on each sieve.
    *   Calculate the **cumulative weight retained** on each sieve.
    *   Calculate the **percentage retained** on each sieve:
        $Percentage \, Retained = (Weight \, Retained \, on \, Sieve / W_{total}) \times 100\%$
    *   Calculate the **cumulative percentage retained** on each sieve:
        $Cumulative \, Percentage \, Retained = (Cumulative \, Weight \, Retained / W_{total}) \times 100\%$
    *   Calculate the **cumulative percentage passing** each sieve:
        $Cumulative \, Percentage \, Passing = 100\% - Cumulative \, Percentage \, Retained$

9.  **Check for Accuracy:**
    *   The sum of the weights retained on all sieves plus the weight passing the bottom sieve should equal the initial total dry weight of the sample ($W_{total}$).
    *   The cumulative percentage passing the largest sieve should be 100% (or very close to it, accounting for any larger particles that were removed first).
    *   The cumulative percentage passing the smallest sieve should be close to the weight of the material in the bottom pan expressed as a percentage.

### 9. Determining Gradation Parameters

From the plotted gradation curve:

*   **$D_{10}$:** Find the sieve opening size on the x-axis corresponding to 10% cumulative passing on the y-axis.
*   **$D_{30}$:** Find the sieve opening size on the x-axis corresponding to 30% cumulative passing on the y-axis.
*   **$D_{60}$:** Find the sieve opening size on the x-axis corresponding to 60% cumulative passing on the y-axis.
*   **Uniformity Coefficient ($C_u$):** Calculate $C_u = D_{60} / D_{10}$.
*   **Coefficient of Curvature ($C_c$):** Calculate $C_c = (D_{30})^2 / (D_{10} \times D_{60})$.

**Example Calculation (using data from the table):**

*   From the table:
    *   10% passing is between 0.425 mm (12.0%) and 0.075 mm (1.8%). Interpolating for 10%:
        $D_{10} \approx 0.40 \, mm$ (or from the graph if precisely plotted)
    *   30% passing is between 2.00 mm (29.6%) and 4.75 mm (60.7%). Interpolating for 30%:
        $D_{30} \approx 2.30 \, mm$
    *   60% passing is between 2.00 mm (29.6%) and 4.75 mm (60.7%). Interpolating for 60%:
        $D_{60} \approx 4.50 \, mm$

*   Calculations:
    *   $C_u = D_{60} / D_{10} = 4.50 \, mm / 0.40 \, mm \approx 11.25$
    *   $C_c = (D_{30})^2 / (D_{10} \times D_{60}) = (2.30 \, mm)^2 / (0.40 \, mm \times 4.50 \, mm) = 5.29 / 1.80 \approx 2.94$

### 10. Interpretation of Gradation Curve and Parameters

*   **$D_{10}$ (Effective Size):** Indicates the size of the smallest particles that can significantly influence permeability. A smaller $D_{10}$ means finer material is present.

*   **Uniformity Coefficient ($C_u$):**
    *   **$C_u < 2$:** Poorly graded (uniform size distribution).
    *   **$2 \le C_u \le 6$:** Moderately graded.
    *   **$C_u > 6$:** Well-graded (wide range of particle sizes).
    *   In the example above, $C_u = 11.25$, indicating a well-graded soil.

*   **Coefficient of Curvature ($C_c$):**
    *   **$C_c$ between 1 and 3:** Generally considered to indicate a well-graded soil.
    *   **$C_c < 1$ or $C_c > 3$:** May indicate a gap-graded or poorly graded soil.
    *   In the example above, $C_c = 2.94$, which falls within the range for well-graded.

*   **Soil Classification based on Gradation:**
    *   **Well-graded sands and gravels (SW, GW):** $C_u > 6$ and $1 < C_c < 3$. These soils have good engineering properties, including good load-bearing capacity and drainage.
    *   **Poorly graded sands and gravels (SP, GP):** $C_u < 2$ or $C_c < 1$ or $C_c > 3$. These soils tend to be more susceptible to settlement and liquefaction.
    *   **Gap-graded sands and gravels (SW-SM, SW-SC, GW-GM, GW-GC):** Exhibit a deficiency in certain intermediate particle sizes.

**Important Note:** The interpretation of $C_u$ and $C_c$ is often used in conjunction with other classification systems like the Unified Soil Classification System (USCS) and AASHTO classification.

### 11. Soil Classification (USCS - Partial)

The sieve analysis results are crucial for classifying soils using the USCS.

*   **Gravels:** More than 50% of the material by weight is retained on the No. 4 sieve.
    *   **GW:** Well-graded gravels ($C_u > 4$ and $1 < C_c < 3$).
    *   **GP:** Poorly graded gravels (all other cases).
    *   **GM:** Silty gravels (fines are silt).
    *   **GC:** Clayey gravels (fines are clay).

*   **Sands:** More than 50% of the material by weight passes the No. 4 sieve.
    *   **SW:** Well-graded sands ($C_u > 6$ and $1 < C_c < 3$).
    *   **SP:** Poorly graded sands (all other cases).
    *   **SM:** Silty sands (fines are silt).
    *   **SC:** Clayey sands (fines are clay).

**To determine if fines are silt or clay, hydrometer analysis or a plastic limit test is required.** However, for a purely sieve analysis based interpretation, you can make assumptions based on visual inspection if the "fines" (passing No. 200 sieve) content is significant.

**Question 2:**
What is the primary limitation of sieve analysis?

**Answer 2:**
The primary limitation of sieve analysis is that it is only effective for determining the particle-size distribution of **coarser-grained soils** (gravels and sands). It cannot accurately determine the sizes of **finer-grained soils** (silts and clays) due to their small size and tendency to flocculate or cling together. For these finer particles, hydrometer analysis is necessary.

### 13. Important Points to Remember

*   **Representative Sample:** Always ensure the soil sample used for sieve analysis is representative of the larger soil mass.
*   **Dry Weight:** Perform sieve analysis on dry soil. Drying at 110 ± 5°C ensures removal of moisture.
*   **Sieve Condition:** Use clean, undamaged sieves. Check for holes or deformation.
*   **Order of Sieves:** Arrange sieves from coarsest (top) to finest (bottom).
*   **Thorough Sieving:** Ensure adequate shaking time to achieve complete separation of particles.
*   **Accurate Weighing:** Use a calibrated balance for all weight measurements.
*   **Fines Content:** The material passing the No. 200 sieve requires a different testing method (hydrometer analysis) for accurate sizing.
*   **Plotting:** The gradation curve is plotted with cumulative percentage passing on the y-axis (linear) and sieve opening size on the x-axis (logarithmic).
*   **$C_u$ and $C_c$ Interpretation:** These parameters provide valuable insights into the soil's gradation and potential engineering behavior.
