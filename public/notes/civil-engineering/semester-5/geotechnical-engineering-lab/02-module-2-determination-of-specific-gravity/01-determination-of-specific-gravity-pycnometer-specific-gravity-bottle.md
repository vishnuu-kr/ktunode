### 1. Introduction

Specific gravity ($G_s$) is a fundamental property of soil solids that describes the ratio of the unit weight of soil solids to the unit weight of water at a specified temperature. It is a dimensionless quantity and is crucial for various calculations in geotechnical engineering, including:

*   **Density calculations:** Determining the density of soil solids and the soil mass.
*   **Void ratio and porosity:** Relating the volume of solids to the volume of voids.
*   **Effective stress calculations:** Understanding the contribution of soil solids to the overall stress.
*   **Classification of soils:** Certain soil classification systems utilize specific gravity values.

### 3. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Specific Gravity ($G_s$):** The ratio of the unit weight of soil solids to the unit weight of water at a standard temperature (usually 27°C or 4°C).
    $$G_s = \frac{\gamma_s}{\gamma_w}$$
    where:
    *   $\gamma_s$ = unit weight of soil solids
    *   $\gamma_w$ = unit weight of water

    Alternatively, it can be expressed in terms of mass and volume:
    $$G_s = \frac{\rho_s}{\rho_w}$$
    where:
    *   $\rho_s$ = density of soil solids
    *   $\rho_w$ = density of water (approximately 1000 kg/m³ or 1 g/cm³ at 27°C)

*   **Soil Solids:** The mineral particles that make up the soil skeleton.
*   **Water Unit Weight ($\gamma_w$):** The weight of a unit volume of water. This value varies slightly with temperature but is commonly taken as 9.81 kN/m³ or 1 g/cm³.
*   **Temperature Correction:** Specific gravity is temperature-dependent because the density of water changes with temperature. The standard temperature for reporting specific gravity is typically 27°C.

### 4. Methods for Determining Specific Gravity

Two common laboratory methods for determining the specific gravity of soil solids are the **Pycnometer Method** and the **Specific Gravity Bottle (or Flask) Method**. Both methods rely on accurately measuring the mass of soil solids, water, and the combined volume occupied by them.

#### 4.1. Pycnometer Method

**4.1.1. Principle:**

The pycnometer is a glass flask of known volume. The method involves accurately determining the mass of:
1.  The empty pycnometer.
2.  The pycnometer filled with distilled water.
3.  The pycnometer filled with oven-dried soil and then topped up with distilled water.

By measuring these masses, the volume of the pycnometer (which is equivalent to the volume of water displaced by the soil solids) can be determined.

**4.1.2. Equipment:**

*   **Pycnometer:** A glass flask with a ground-glass stopper that has a capillary tube extending through it. The volume of the pycnometer should be suitable for the soil sample size (e.g., 250 mL or 500 mL).
*   **Oven:** For drying the soil sample to a constant weight.
*   **Analytical Balance:** With a precision of at least 0.1 g (preferably 0.01 g).
*   **Distilled Water:** De-aired water is preferred to avoid air bubbles.
*   **Stirring Rod:** For mixing the soil and water.
*   **Thermometer:** To measure the temperature of the water.
*   **Filter Paper or Funnel:** For transferring the soil into the pycnometer.
*   **Beakers:** For handling water and soil.
*   **Sieve (e.g., No. 40 or 0.425 mm):** To remove particles larger than 2 mm for fine-grained soils or up to 4.75 mm for coarse-grained soils, depending on the standard. Typically, the test is performed on the material passing the 4.75 mm sieve.

**4.1.3. Procedure:**

1.  **Preparation of Soil Sample:**
    *   Obtain a representative soil sample.
    *   Dry the soil sample in an oven at 105°C to 110°C until a constant weight is achieved. This ensures all pore water is removed.
    *   Sieve the dried soil to remove particles larger than the specified sieve size (e.g., 4.75 mm for general purposes or 2 mm for fine-grained soils).
    *   Cool the dried soil in a desiccator to prevent moisture absorption from the atmosphere.

2.  **Weighing the Pycnometer:**
    *   Clean and dry the pycnometer.
    *   Weigh the empty pycnometer and record its mass ($M_{pyc}$).

3.  **Determining the Mass of Pycnometer + Water:**
    *   Fill the pycnometer with distilled water, ensuring no air bubbles are trapped.
    *   Insert the stopper. Excess water will escape through the capillary tube.
    *   Wipe the outside of the pycnometer dry.
    *   Weigh the pycnometer filled with water ($M_{pyc+w}$).
    *   Measure and record the temperature of the water.

4.  **Determining the Mass of Pycnometer + Soil + Water:**
    *   Empty the pycnometer and dry it thoroughly.
    *   Place a known mass of oven-dried soil ($M_s$) into the pycnometer. The amount of soil should be approximately 1/3 to 1/2 of the pycnometer's volume.
    *   Add distilled water to cover the soil.
    *   Gently stir the soil and water mixture to disperse the soil particles and remove entrapped air. A vacuum desiccator can be used for more effective de-airing.
    *   Fill the pycnometer with distilled water, leaving space for the stopper.
    *   Insert the stopper, ensuring no air bubbles are trapped.
    *   Wipe the outside of the pycnometer dry.
    *   Weigh the pycnometer filled with soil and water ($M_{pyc+s+w}$).

**4.1.4. Calculations:**

*   **Mass of water in pycnometer:**
    $$M_w = M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}$$

*   **Mass of soil solids:**
    $$M_s = \text{Mass of oven-dried soil added to pycnometer}$$
    *(This is directly measured in step 4 before adding water, or the difference between the total mass in step 4 and the mass of pycnometer filled with water, minus the mass of the displaced water, can be used if the soil mass is not directly recorded)*

*   **Mass of water displaced by soil solids:**
    This is the mass of water that would occupy the same volume as the soil solids.
    $$M_{w(displaced)} = (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc+s}) - (M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc})$$
    Where:
    *   $M_{pyc+s}$ is the mass of the pycnometer plus the dry soil solids. This can be calculated as $M_{pyc+s} = M_{pyc} + M_s$.
    *   So, $M_{w(displaced)} = (M_{pyc+s+w} - (M_{pyc} + M_s)) - (M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc})$

    A simpler way to express the mass of water displaced by the soil solids is:
    $$M_{w(displaced)} = (M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}) - (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{s} - M_{pyc})$$
    $$M_{w(displaced)} = (M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}) - (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s)$$

    Let's reconsider the calculation:
    Mass of pycnometer + soil = $M_{pyc} + M_s$
    Mass of pycnometer + soil + water = $M_{pyc+s+w}$
    Mass of water that fills the remaining volume = $(M_{pyc+s+w} - (M_{pyc} + M_s))$

    The volume of the pycnometer ($V_{pyc}$) is known or can be determined by the mass of water it holds: $V_{pyc} = M_w / \rho_w$.
    The volume of water that fills the pycnometer with soil and water is $V_{water\_added} = (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s) / \rho_w$.
    The volume of soil solids ($V_s$) is the volume of the pycnometer minus the volume of water added:
    $$V_s = V_{pyc} - V_{water\_added}$$
    $$V_s = \frac{M_w}{\rho_w} - \frac{M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s}{\rho_w}$$
    $$V_s = \frac{M_w - (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s)}{\rho_w}$$
    $$V_s = \frac{(M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}) - (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s)}{\rho_w}$$
    $$V_s = \frac{M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s}{\rho_w}$$

    **Specific Gravity ($G_s$):**
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s / V_s}{\rho_w} = \frac{M_s}{\rho_w \cdot V_s}$$
    Substituting $V_s$:
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s}{\rho_w \cdot \frac{M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s}{\rho_w}}$$
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s}{M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s}$$

    *Note: For accuracy, it's crucial to use the density of water ($\rho_w$) at the measured temperature. Values can be found in tables.*

#### 4.2. Specific Gravity Bottle (or Flask) Method

**4.2.1. Principle:**

This method is similar to the pycnometer method but uses a specific type of flask (often called a volumetric flask or specific gravity bottle) with a precisely known volume at a calibrated temperature. The principle remains the same: determining the mass of dry soil solids and the mass of water that has the same volume as the soil solids.

**4.2.2. Equipment:**

*   **Specific Gravity Bottle (Flask):** A glass flask with a stopper that has a capillary tube. It has a known, calibrated volume. Common sizes are 50 mL or 100 mL.
*   **Oven:** For drying the soil sample.
*   **Analytical Balance:** Precision of 0.1 g or better.
*   **Distilled Water:** Preferably de-aired.
*   **Thermometer:** For measuring water temperature.
*   **Sieve (e.g., No. 40 or 0.425 mm):** For sample preparation.
*   **Desiccator:** To cool dried samples.

**4.2.3. Procedure:**

1.  **Preparation of Soil Sample:** Same as step 1 in the pycnometer method.

2.  **Weighing the Empty Specific Gravity Bottle:**
    *   Clean and dry the specific gravity bottle and its stopper.
    *   Weigh the empty bottle and stopper ($M_{bottle}$).

3.  **Determining the Mass of the Bottle + Water:**
    *   Fill the bottle with distilled water, ensuring the water level reaches the top of the capillary tube when the stopper is in place.
    *   Wipe the outside dry.
    *   Weigh the bottle filled with water ($M_{bottle+w}$).
    *   Record the water temperature.

4.  **Determining the Mass of the Bottle + Soil + Water:**
    *   Empty and dry the bottle.
    *   Place a known mass of oven-dried soil ($M_s$) into the bottle (typically about 1/3 to 1/2 of its volume).
    *   Add distilled water to cover the soil.
    *   Agitate the mixture to disperse the soil and remove air bubbles. A vacuum can be applied.
    *   Fill the bottle with distilled water up to the calibration mark (top of the capillary tube) with the stopper in place.
    *   Wipe the outside dry.
    *   Weigh the bottle filled with soil and water ($M_{bottle+s+w}$).

**4.2.4. Calculations:**

*   **Mass of water in the bottle (same volume as the bottle):**
    $$M_w = M_{bottle+w} - M_{bottle}$$

*   **Mass of soil solids:**
    $$M_s = \text{Mass of oven-dried soil added}$$

*   **Mass of water displaced by soil solids:**
    The volume of soil solids ($V_s$) is the total volume of the bottle minus the volume of water that fills the remaining space in the bottle when soil is present.
    Volume of bottle ($V_{bottle}$) = $M_w / \rho_w$.
    Mass of water in the bottle with soil = $(M_{bottle+s+w} - M_{bottle} - M_s)$.
    Volume of water in the bottle with soil = $(M_{bottle+s+w} - M_{bottle} - M_s) / \rho_w$.

    $$V_s = V_{bottle} - \text{Volume of water in the bottle with soil}$$
    $$V_s = \frac{M_w}{\rho_w} - \frac{M_{bottle+s+w} - M_{bottle} - M_s}{\rho_w}$$
    $$V_s = \frac{(M_{bottle+w} - M_{bottle}) - (M_{bottle+s+w} - M_{bottle} - M_s)}{\rho_w}$$
    $$V_s = \frac{M_{bottle+w} - M_{bottle+s+w} + M_s}{\rho_w}$$

    **Specific Gravity ($G_s$):**
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s / V_s}{\rho_w} = \frac{M_s}{\rho_w \cdot V_s}$$
    Substituting $V_s$:
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s}{\rho_w \cdot \frac{M_{bottle+w} - M_{bottle+s+w} + M_s}{\rho_w}}$$
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s}{M_{bottle+w} - M_{bottle+s+w} + M_s}$$

    This formula is identical to the pycnometer method. The key difference lies in the flask itself and its calibrated volume.

### 5. Factors Affecting Accuracy

*   **Entrapped Air:** Air bubbles in the water or within the soil pores will lead to an overestimation of the volume of soil solids and hence an underestimation of specific gravity. Thorough de-airing is crucial.
*   **Temperature:** Fluctuations in temperature affect the density of water, leading to errors if not accounted for. The water temperature must be measured accurately.
*   **Oven-Drying:** Incomplete drying of the soil sample will result in an overestimated mass of solids. Drying to constant weight is essential.
*   **Sample Contamination:** Impurities in the soil sample can affect the measured masses.
*   **Weighing Precision:** The accuracy of the analytical balance directly impacts the precision of the results.
*   **Completeness of Saturation:** For fine-grained soils, it might be difficult to ensure complete saturation and removal of all entrapped air.

---

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Always use oven-dried soil.**
*   **Ensure thorough removal of entrapped air** by agitation or vacuum.
*   **Record the temperature of the water** accurately.
*   **Use distilled or de-ionized water.**
*   **Wipe the pycnometer/bottle dry** on the outside before weighing.
*   **The specific gravity of soil solids is typically between 2.65 and 2.80** for most common mineral soils. Values outside this range may indicate unusual mineralogy or the presence of organic matter.
*   **For coarse-grained soils, the No. 4 (4.75 mm) sieve is commonly used** to prepare the sample. For fine-grained soils, the No. 10 (2.00 mm) sieve is often used. Always refer to the specific test standard.
*   **It is good practice to repeat the test** at least twice for each sample to ensure consistency and accuracy.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
A pycnometer has a mass of 250.0 g. When filled with water at 25°C, its mass is 750.0 g. When 100.0 g of oven-dried soil is placed in the pycnometer and filled with water at 25°C, the total mass is 800.0 g. Calculate the specific gravity of the soil solids.
*Assume the density of water at 25°C is 997 kg/m³ or 0.997 g/cm³.*

**Solution 1:**
*   Mass of empty pycnometer ($M_{pyc}$) = 250.0 g
*   Mass of pycnometer + water ($M_{pyc+w}$) = 750.0 g
*   Mass of oven-dried soil ($M_s$) = 100.0 g
*   Mass of pycnometer + soil + water ($M_{pyc+s+w}$) = 800.0 g
*   Density of water at 25°C ($\rho_w$) = 0.997 g/cm³

*   Mass of water in the pycnometer ($M_w$) = $M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc} = 750.0 - 250.0 = 500.0$ g
*   Mass of soil solids ($M_s$) = 100.0 g
*   Mass of water displaced by soil solids = $(M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}) - (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_s - M_{pyc})$
    *   Mass of water in the pycnometer with soil = $(M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc}) - M_s = (800.0 - 250.0) - 100.0 = 550.0 - 100.0 = 450.0$ g
    *   Mass of water displaced by soil solids = $M_w - (\text{Mass of water in pycnometer with soil})$
        Wait, this is not correct. Let's use the direct formula:
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s}{M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s}$$
    $$G_s = \frac{100.0 \, \text{g}}{750.0 \, \text{g} - 800.0 \, \text{g} + 100.0 \, \text{g}}$$
    $$G_s = \frac{100.0}{50.0}$$
    $$G_s = 2.00$$

    *Let's recheck the calculation of displaced water mass:*
    Mass of water alone in the pycnometer = $M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc} = 750.0 - 250.0 = 500.0$ g
    Mass of water and soil in the pycnometer = $M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} = 800.0 - 250.0 = 550.0$ g
    Mass of water in the pycnometer (when soil is present) = $(M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc}) - M_s = 550.0 - 100.0 = 450.0$ g
    The volume of soil solids is the volume of water that is *not* present in the second case compared to the first case when filled to the brim.
    Volume of water = $500.0$ g / $0.997$ g/cm³ = $501.50$ cm³ (This is the volume of the pycnometer)
    Volume of water + soil = $550.0$ g / $0.997$ g/cm³ = $551.65$ cm³ (This is the volume of the pycnometer + soil, which is incorrect. It's the mass of water + soil, not volume)

    Let's use the formula derived:
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s}{M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s}$$
    This formula is derived from:
    $V_s = V_{pyc} - V_{water\_added}$
    $V_{pyc} = (M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}) / \rho_w$
    $V_{water\_added} = (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s) / \rho_w$
    $V_s = [(M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}) - (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s)] / \rho_w$
    $V_s = [M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s] / \rho_w$

    $G_s = \frac{M_s}{\rho_s} \cdot \frac{\rho_w}{1}$ (where $\rho_s$ is density of soil solids)
    $G_s = \frac{M_s}{V_s \cdot \rho_w}$
    $G_s = \frac{M_s}{[(M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s) / \rho_w] \cdot \rho_w}$
    $G_s = \frac{M_s}{M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s}$

    $G_s = \frac{100.0}{750.0 - 800.0 + 100.0} = \frac{100.0}{50.0} = 2.00$
    This result is unusually low for typical soils. Let's re-read the question and my understanding.

    Ah, the mass of water displaced by soil solids is the difference in mass of water that fills the bottle *alone* versus the mass of water that fills the bottle *with soil*.
    Mass of water filling the pycnometer = $M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc} = 500.0 \, g$
    Mass of soil solids = $M_s = 100.0 \, g$
    Mass of pycnometer + soil = $M_{pyc} + M_s = 250.0 + 100.0 = 350.0 \, g$
    Mass of pycnometer + soil + water = $M_{pyc+s+w} = 800.0 \, g$
    Mass of water that fills the remaining volume = $(M_{pyc+s+w} - (M_{pyc} + M_s)) = 800.0 - 350.0 = 450.0 \, g$

    The mass of water that has the same volume as the soil solids is the difference between the mass of water that fills the pycnometer and the mass of water that fills the remaining volume when soil is present.
    Mass of water displaced by soil solids = $(M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc}) - (M_{pyc+s+w} - M_{pyc} - M_s)$
    Mass of water displaced by soil solids = $500.0 \, g - 450.0 \, g = 50.0 \, g$

    Specific Gravity ($G_s$) = $\frac{\text{Mass of soil solids}}{\text{Mass of water displaced by soil solids}}$
    $$G_s = \frac{M_s}{M_{w(displaced)}} = \frac{100.0 \, g}{50.0 \, g} = 2.00$$

    The formula $G_s = \frac{M_s}{M_{pyc+w} - M_{pyc+s+w} + M_s}$ is indeed correct. The low value might be due to the example values chosen.

**Question 2:**
Explain why de-airing is important in the determination of specific gravity. What would be the likely effect on the calculated specific gravity if air bubbles are not completely removed?

**Answer 2:**
De-airing is crucial because any entrapped air within the soil pores or in the water within the pycnometer/bottle will occupy volume. This added volume will be mistakenly attributed to the volume of soil solids. If the volume of soil solids ($V_s$) is overestimated, and the mass of soil solids ($M_s$) remains the same, the calculated specific gravity ($G_s = M_s / (V_s \cdot \rho_w)$) will be **underestimated**.
