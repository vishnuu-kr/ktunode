# Geotechnical Engineering Lab: Field Density Test - Sand Replacement Method

## Module 7: Field Density Test

### Topic: (ii) Sand Replacement Method

---

### 1. Introduction & Purpose

The Sand Replacement Method is a widely used field technique to determine the in-situ density of compacted soils, particularly in road construction, earth dams, and other earthworks. It's an alternative to the core cutter method when the soil is too coarse-grained (e.g., gravelly soils) or too hard to be excavated with the core cutter.

**Purpose:**

*   To determine the **in-situ dry density** of compacted soil.
*   To assess the **degree of compaction** achieved in the field by comparing the in-situ dry density to the maximum dry density obtained from laboratory compaction tests (e.g., Proctor Test).

---

### 2. Key Concepts & Definitions

*   **In-situ Density:** The density of a soil deposit as it exists in its natural state or after compaction, without disturbing its structure.
*   **Wet Density ($\rho_w$):** The mass of soil per unit volume, including both solids and water.
    $$ \rho_w = \frac{\text{Mass of soil sample}}{\text{Volume of soil sample}} $$
*   **Dry Density ($\rho_d$):** The mass of solid soil particles per unit volume of the soil. This is the most important parameter for assessing compaction.
    $$ \rho_d = \frac{\text{Mass of dry solids}}{\text{Volume of soil sample}} $$
*   **Water Content ($w$):** The ratio of the mass of water to the mass of dry solids in a soil sample, usually expressed as a percentage.
    $$ w = \frac{\text{Mass of water}}{\text{Mass of dry solids}} \times 100\% $$
*   **Relationship between Wet and Dry Density:**
    $$ \rho_d = \frac{\rho_w}{1 + w} $$
*   **Degree of Compaction:** The ratio of the in-situ dry density to the maximum dry density achieved in a laboratory compaction test.
    $$ \text{Degree of Compaction} = \frac{\rho_{d, \text{field}}}{\rho_{d, \text{max, lab}}} \times 100\% $$
*   **Calibrated Sand:** A clean, uniform, dry, and free-flowing granular material (usually sand) whose **density and particle size distribution** are known and consistent. It is often used as a medium to measure the volume of the excavated pit.

---

### 3. Principle of the Sand Replacement Method

The method relies on excavating a known volume of soil from the in-situ deposit and then filling that same volume with a precisely calibrated granular material (sand). By measuring the mass of sand required to fill the pit, and knowing the density of the calibrated sand, the volume of the excavated pit can be accurately determined. The excavated soil is then weighed and its water content determined to calculate its dry density.

**The core steps involve:**

1.  **Excavating a hole** of a known volume in the soil layer to be tested.
2.  **Measuring the wet mass** of the excavated soil.
3.  **Determining the water content** of the excavated soil.
4.  **Filling the excavated hole** with calibrated sand.
5.  **Measuring the mass of sand** used.
6.  **Calculating the volume of the hole** using the known density of the calibrated sand.
7.  **Calculating the in-situ dry density** of the soil.

---

### 4. Apparatus Required

*   **Sand Pouring Device (Sand Cone Apparatus):** This apparatus typically consists of a conical funnel connected to a cylindrical container that holds the calibrated sand. A sliding gate or stopper controls the flow of sand.
*   **Calibrated Sand:** Dry, uniform, free-flowing sand with a known bulk density.
*   **Digging Tools:** Spades, shovels, trowels for excavating the soil.
*   **Metal Tray or Sheet:** To place the excavated soil to prevent loss.
*   **Balance:** Sensitive enough to weigh soil samples and sand accurately (e.g., 0.1 g precision).
*   **Measuring Tape/Ruler:** For measuring dimensions.
*   **Containers:** For collecting soil samples for water content determination.
*   **Oven:** For drying soil samples to determine water content.
*   **Hammer and Chisel (optional):** For loosening hard soils.
*   **Gauge (optional):** To ensure a level surface around the excavation.

---

### 5. Procedure

The procedure can be broken down into the following steps:

**Step 1: Preparation of the Test Area**

*   Select a representative area of the compacted soil layer.
*   Level the surface of the ground where the test will be conducted.

**Step 2: Placement of the Sand Pouring Device**

*   Place the sand pouring device (with the gate closed) firmly onto the prepared ground surface.
*   **Crucially:** Excavate a small, approximately cylindrical hole *through* the base of the cone of the apparatus, ensuring the soil is removed from within the area covered by the apparatus. The excavation should be as deep as the layer being tested.
*   Carefully place the excavated soil into a metal tray or onto a sheet to avoid loss.

**Step 3: Filling the Excavated Hole with Calibrated Sand**

*   Close the gate of the sand pouring device.
*   Fill the container of the sand pouring device with the calibrated sand. Record the initial mass of the sand container (or weigh the container before and after filling).
*   Place the sand pouring device with the filled container back onto the prepared ground surface, aligning it with the previously excavated hole.
*   Open the gate of the sand pouring device and allow the calibrated sand to flow into the excavated hole until it is completely filled. Ensure the sand flows uniformly without bridging.
*   Once the hole is filled, carefully close the gate of the sand pouring device.
*   Weigh the remaining sand in the container to determine the mass of sand used.

**Step 4: Sampling the Excavated Soil**

*   Take the soil collected in the tray.
*   Thoroughly mix the excavated soil to ensure uniformity.
*   Obtain representative samples of the excavated soil for water content determination. Place these samples in airtight containers.

**Step 5: Laboratory Determination of Water Content**

*   In the lab, determine the water content of the collected soil samples using a standard oven-drying method.

**Step 6: Calibration of the Sand (Typically done beforehand)**

*   This is a critical pre-requisite. Before field use, the sand needs to be calibrated to determine its **bulk density**.
*   Fill a container of known volume (e.g., a cylinder of known volume) with the calibrated sand using the same pouring technique as in the field.
*   Weigh the sand used to fill the known volume.
*   Calculate the bulk density of the calibrated sand:
    $$ \rho_{\text{sand, bulk}} = \frac{\text{Mass of sand}}{\text{Volume of container}} $$
*   Alternatively, and more commonly for the field test, the calibration is performed to determine the mass of sand per unit volume. Fill a standard calibrating container (often of a specific volume) with the sand, and record the mass of sand used. This gives a **calibration factor (mass of sand per unit volume)**.

---

### 6. Calculations

**Given:**

*   $M_s$ = Mass of calibrated sand used to fill the hole (kg)
*   $\rho_{\text{sand, bulk}}$ = Bulk density of calibrated sand (kg/m³) - *or* Calibration factor (kg/m³) if volume of container is 1 m³ or a standard volume is used.
*   $M_{\text{wet}}$ = Wet mass of excavated soil sample (kg)
*   $M_{\text{dry}}$ = Mass of dry solids from the excavated soil sample (kg)
*   $w$ = Water content of the excavated soil sample (as a decimal, e.g., 0.20 for 20%)

**Calculations:**

1.  **Volume of the Excavated Hole ($V$):**
    *   If $\rho_{\text{sand, bulk}}$ is known:
        $$ V = \frac{M_s}{\rho_{\text{sand, bulk}}} $$
    *   If a calibration factor ($C_{\text{sand}}$ = mass of sand per unit volume) is used:
        $$ V = \frac{M_s}{C_{\text{sand}}} $$

2.  **Wet Density of In-situ Soil ($\rho_{w, \text{field}}$):**
    $$ \rho_{w, \text{field}} = \frac{M_{\text{wet}}}{V} $$

3.  **Dry Density of In-situ Soil ($\rho_{d, \text{field}}$):**
    *   First, determine the mass of dry solids ($M_{\text{dry}}$) from the excavated soil. This is done by drying a known mass of wet soil. If you sampled a portion of the excavated soil:
        $$ M_{\text{dry}} = \frac{M_{\text{wet\_sample}}}{1 + w} $$
        Where $M_{\text{wet\_sample}}$ is the wet mass of the sample taken for water content determination, and $w$ is its water content. Assuming the water content is representative of the entire excavated soil.
    *   Then, calculate the dry density:
        $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{M_{\text{dry}}}{V} $$
        Alternatively, if you have the total wet mass of excavated soil ($M_{\text{wet}}$) and the water content ($w$) of that same bulk sample:
        $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{M_{\text{wet}}}{V(1+w)} $$

---

### 7. Example Calculation

**Scenario:**

A field density test is performed using the sand replacement method.

*   Mass of calibrated sand used to fill the hole ($M_s$) = 8.50 kg
*   Bulk density of calibrated sand ($\rho_{\text{sand, bulk}}$) = 1500 kg/m³
*   Wet mass of excavated soil ($M_{\text{wet}}$) = 7.20 kg
*   Water content of the excavated soil ($w$) = 15% (or 0.15)

**Calculations:**

1.  **Volume of the Excavated Hole ($V$):**
    $$ V = \frac{M_s}{\rho_{\text{sand, bulk}}} = \frac{8.50 \text{ kg}}{1500 \text{ kg/m³}} = 0.005667 \text{ m³} $$

2.  **Wet Density of In-situ Soil ($\rho_{w, \text{field}}$):**
    $$ \rho_{w, \text{field}} = \frac{M_{\text{wet}}}{V} = \frac{7.20 \text{ kg}}{0.005667 \text{ m³}} = 1270.5 \text{ kg/m³} $$

3.  **Dry Density of In-situ Soil ($\rho_{d, \text{field}}$):**
    $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{M_{\text{wet}}}{V(1+w)} = \frac{7.20 \text{ kg}}{0.005667 \text{ m³}(1 + 0.15)} = \frac{7.20 \text{ kg}}{0.005667 \text{ m³} \times 1.15} $$
    $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{7.20 \text{ kg}}{0.006517 \text{ m³}} \approx 1104.8 \text{ kg/m³} $$

**Result:** The in-situ dry density of the soil is approximately 1104.8 kg/m³.

### 8. Factors Affecting Accuracy & Precautions

*   **Quality of Calibrated Sand:** The sand must be clean, dry, and have a uniform particle size distribution. Any variation in its density will lead to errors in volume calculation.
*   **Calibration Accuracy:** The calibration of the sand must be done meticulously and regularly.
*   **Excavation Technique:** The hole should be excavated with minimal disturbance to the surrounding soil and the bottom of the hole should be made as smooth and level as possible.
*   **Complete Filling of the Hole:** Ensure the calibrated sand completely fills the excavated volume without voids or bridging.
*   **Soil Loss:** Prevent any loss of excavated soil during transfer to the tray.
*   **Water Content Determination:** Accurate measurement of water content is crucial for calculating dry density.
*   **Soil Type:** The method is best suited for soils with particle sizes up to about 10 mm. For soils with larger particles, modifications or alternative methods might be needed.
*   **Surface Preparation:** A level and clean surface is essential for proper seating of the sand pouring device.
*   **Careful Operation:** Avoid spillage of sand during filling and closing the gate.

---

### 9. Advantages of the Sand Replacement Method

*   **Suitable for Coarser Soils:** More suitable than the core cutter method for soils containing gravel and larger particles.
*   **Relatively Simple Procedure:** Once calibrated, the field operation is straightforward.
*   **Reasonably Accurate:** Can provide good results if performed carefully.

---

### 10. Disadvantages of the Sand Replacement Method

*   **Requires Pre-calibration:** The calibrated sand is essential and its properties must be known accurately.
*   **Time-Consuming:** Can be more time-consuming than other methods, especially with the excavation and water content determination.
*   **Sensitivity to Soil Properties:** Less accurate for soils with a wide range of particle sizes or where the soil is very difficult to excavate cleanly.
*   **Potential for Sand Contamination:** If the excavated soil contains fines that can pass through the sieve or mix with the sand, it can affect the accuracy.

### 11. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1:**

During a field density test using the sand replacement method, the following data was collected:

*   Mass of calibrated sand used: 6.8 kg
*   Bulk density of calibrated sand: 1600 kg/m³
*   Wet mass of excavated soil: 5.5 kg
*   Water content of excavated soil: 20%

Calculate the in-situ dry density of the soil.

**Answer 1:**

1.  **Volume of the Excavated Hole ($V$):**
    $$ V = \frac{M_s}{\rho_{\text{sand, bulk}}} = \frac{6.8 \text{ kg}}{1600 \text{ kg/m³}} = 0.00425 \text{ m³} $$
2.  **Dry Density of In-situ Soil ($\rho_{d, \text{field}}$):**
    $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{M_{\text{wet}}}{V(1+w)} = \frac{5.5 \text{ kg}}{0.00425 \text{ m³}(1 + 0.20)} = \frac{5.5 \text{ kg}}{0.00425 \text{ m³} \times 1.20} $$
    $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{5.5 \text{ kg}}{0.0051 \text{ m³}} \approx 1078.4 \text{ kg/m³} $$

**Question 2:**

A contractor needs to check the compaction of a sub-base layer. They use the sand replacement method.

*   A sand cone apparatus was used, and after filling a known volume of 0.003 m³ with calibrated sand, the mass of sand used was 4.65 kg.
*   A hole was excavated, yielding 6.0 kg of wet soil. The water content of this soil was found to be 12%.

Calculate:
a) The calibration factor for the sand (mass of sand per unit volume).
b) The in-situ dry density of the soil.

**Answer 2:**

a) **Calibration Factor ($C_{\text{sand}}$):**
    $$ C_{\text{sand}} = \frac{\text{Mass of sand}}{\text{Volume of container}} = \frac{4.65 \text{ kg}}{0.003 \text{ m³}} = 1550 \text{ kg/m³} $$

b) **In-situ Dry Density ($\rho_{d, \text{field}}$):**
    *   **Volume of the Excavated Hole ($V$):** Using the calibration factor:
        $$ V = \frac{M_s}{C_{\text{sand}}} = \frac{4.65 \text{ kg}}{1550 \text{ kg/m³}} = 0.003 \text{ m³} $$
        *(Note: This matches the known volume used for calibration, as expected.)*
    *   **Dry Density of In-situ Soil ($\rho_{d, \text{field}}$):**
        $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{M_{\text{wet}}}{V(1+w)} = \frac{6.0 \text{ kg}}{0.003 \text{ m³}(1 + 0.12)} = \frac{6.0 \text{ kg}}{0.003 \text{ m³} \times 1.12} $$
        $$ \rho_{d, \text{field}} = \frac{6.0 \text{ kg}}{0.00336 \text{ m³}} \approx 1785.7 \text{ kg/m³} $$

---

### 12. Important Points to Remember

*   The **dry density** is the key parameter for assessing compaction.
*   The **calibrated sand's density** is critical for accurately determining the excavated volume.
*   The sand replacement method is preferred for **gravelly or coarser soils**.
*   **Careful excavation** and **prevention of soil loss** are vital for accuracy.
*   The **water content determination** must be precise.
*   Always compare the calculated **in-situ dry density** with the **laboratory maximum dry density** to determine the degree of compaction.
