### 1. Introduction to Irrigation

**What is Irrigation?**

Irrigation is the artificial application of water to land or soil to supplement natural rainfall, primarily to assist in growing agricultural crops in regions or during periods when rainfall is insufficient to support plant growth.

**Why is Irrigation Necessary?**

*   **Unreliable Rainfall:** Natural rainfall is often erratic, insufficient, or unevenly distributed, leading to crop failures.
*   **Arid and Semi-Arid Regions:** Many agriculturally productive areas receive inadequate rainfall for crop growth.
*   **Multiple Cropping:** To achieve multiple harvests or grow high-value crops, supplemental irrigation is often required.
*   **Water Management:** Irrigation allows for better control over water availability, ensuring crops receive water at critical growth stages.
*   **Increased Food Production:** Irrigation is a cornerstone of modern agriculture, significantly boosting food production to meet growing global demand.

---

### 2. Benefits of Irrigation

*   **Increased Crop Yields:** Consistent and timely water supply leads to healthier plants and significantly higher yields.
*   **Improved Crop Quality:** Proper irrigation can enhance the size, texture, and nutritional value of crops.
*   **Cultivation of a Wider Variety of Crops:** Allows for the growth of crops that require more water or specific moisture regimes.
*   **Protection Against Drought:** Shields crops from the devastating effects of drought conditions.
*   **Extended Growing Season:** Enables year-round cultivation or multiple cropping cycles in regions with distinct dry seasons.
*   **Control over Plant Growth:** Allows farmers to manage plant growth rates and development by controlling water availability.
*   **Frost Protection:** In some cases, irrigating fields during cold nights can protect crops from frost damage due to the latent heat released during water freezing.
*   **Nutrient Transport:** Water acts as a solvent and carrier for essential nutrients in the soil to the plant roots.
*   **Land Reclamation:** Irrigation can make arid or semi-arid lands productive for agriculture.
*   **Economic Development:** Enhanced agricultural productivity through irrigation contributes to rural economic growth and national food security.

---

### 3. Ill Effects of Irrigation

While beneficial, improper irrigation practices can lead to several negative consequences:

*   **Waterlogging:**
    *   **Definition:** Occurs when the water table rises to a level where it saturates the root zone of the soil, impeding aeration and hindering root development.
    *   **Causes:** Over-irrigation, poor drainage, high water table.
    *   **Effects:** Reduced oxygen for roots, increased salinity, spread of root diseases, ultimately leading to crop failure.
*   **Salinization and Alkalization:**
    *   **Definition:**
        *   **Salinization:** Accumulation of soluble salts in the soil.
        *   **Alkalization:** Accumulation of sodium ions in the soil, leading to a high pH and dispersion of soil aggregates.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Irrigation with saline water.
        *   Poor drainage, leading to evaporation from the surface and capillary rise of dissolved salts.
        *   Over-irrigation without adequate leaching.
    *   **Effects:** Toxicity to plants, reduced water uptake by roots, soil structural degradation, decreased fertility.
*   **Soil Erosion:**
    *   **Causes:** Improper application of irrigation water, especially surface irrigation with high flow rates or slopes, can dislodge soil particles.
    *   **Effects:** Loss of fertile topsoil, sedimentation of water bodies, reduced infiltration.
*   **Water-borne Diseases:**
    *   **Causes:** Stagnant water from irrigation can become breeding grounds for mosquitoes and other vectors, spreading diseases like malaria, dengue, etc.
    *   **Effects:** Public health issues.
*   **Water Scarcity in Downstream Areas:**
    *   **Causes:** Inefficient irrigation upstream can deplete water resources, leaving insufficient water for users downstream.
    *   **Effects:** Conflict over water resources, reduced agricultural production in downstream areas.
*   **Changes in Groundwater Quality:**
    *   **Causes:** Seepage from canals and fields can recharge groundwater with surface contaminants or dissolved salts.
    *   **Effects:** Degradation of groundwater quality for drinking and irrigation.

### 4. Irrigation Efficiencies

**What is Irrigation Efficiency?**

Irrigation efficiency is a measure of how effectively the irrigation water applied to a field is used by the crops. It quantizes the losses that occur during the delivery and application of water.

**Why is Efficiency Important?**

*   **Water Conservation:** Maximizing the amount of water used by crops minimizes wastage, crucial in water-scarce regions.
*   **Economic Viability:** Reduced water usage can lower irrigation costs (pumping, energy).
*   **Environmental Sustainability:** Minimizing runoff and deep percolation reduces the risk of soil erosion, salinization, and water pollution.
*   **Increased Productivity:** Efficient water use ensures water is available when and where the crop needs it most, leading to better growth.

**Types of Irrigation Efficiencies:**

Several efficiencies are defined to measure losses at different stages of the irrigation process.

#### 4.1. Conveyance Efficiency ($E_c$)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the water delivered to the field to the water diverted from the source.
*   **Formula:**
    $E_c = \frac{\text{Volume of water delivered to the field}}{\text{Volume of water diverted from the source}} \times 100\%$
*   **Losses:** Seepage, evaporation, and operational losses from canals, pipelines, and watercourses.
*   **Typical Values:** Can range from 50% (unlined canals) to over 95% (pressurized pipelines).

#### 4.2. Application Efficiency ($E_a$)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the volume of water stored in the root zone of the soil that is actually available to the crop to the volume of water delivered to the field.
*   **Formula:**
    $E_a = \frac{\text{Volume of water stored in the root zone available to the crop}}{\text{Volume of water delivered to the field}} \times 100\%$
*   **Losses:** Deep percolation below the root zone, surface runoff from the field, and evaporation from the soil surface during irrigation.
*   **Factors Affecting:** Irrigation method (e.g., furrow, sprinkler, drip), soil type, field slope, irrigation depth, and duration.
*   **Typical Values:** Varies greatly with method; e.g., furrow (50-70%), sprinkler (70-85%), drip (90-95%).

#### 4.3. Water Use Efficiency ($E_u$) or Crop Water Use Efficiency ($E_{cw}$)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the total amount of water consumed by the crop for its physiological processes (transpiration) and building its biomass to the total amount of water delivered to the field.
*   **Formula:**
    $E_u = \frac{\text{Water consumed by crop (transpiration)}}{\text{Volume of water delivered to the field}} \times 100\%$
*   **Losses:** Deep percolation, surface runoff, evaporation from soil surface (evapotranspiration is total water loss, $E_u$ focuses on transpiration). This is often considered a measure of how efficiently the *delivered* water is *used* by the crop.

#### 4.4. Storage Efficiency ($E_s$)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the average depth of water stored in the root zone to the depth of water required to bring the root zone to the desired moisture content (e.g., field capacity).
*   **Formula:**
    $E_s = \frac{\text{Average depth of water stored in the root zone}}{\text{Desired depth of water to be stored}} \times 100\%$
*   **Significance:** Measures how uniformly the applied water is distributed within the root zone. Low $E_s$ indicates some areas are over-irrigated, while others are under-irrigated.

#### 4.5. Distribution Efficiency ($E_d$)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the minimum depth of water stored in the root zone of any part of the field to the average depth of water stored in the root zone of the entire field.
*   **Formula:**
    $E_d = \frac{\text{Minimum depth of water stored in the root zone}}{\text{Average depth of water stored in the root zone}} \times 100\%$
*   **Significance:** Measures the uniformity of water distribution across the field. A high $E_d$ means water is distributed uniformly.

#### 4.6. Overall Irrigation Efficiency ($E_o$)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the water consumed by the crop (transpiration) to the total water diverted from the source.
*   **Formula:**
    $E_o = \frac{\text{Water consumed by crop (transpiration)}}{\text{Volume of water diverted from the source}} \times 100\%$
*   **Relationship with other efficiencies:**
    $E_o = E_c \times E_a \times (\text{some factor for crop water use})$
    A more common and practical definition for overall efficiency links the water effectively used by the crop to the total water diverted:
    $E_o = \frac{\text{Net irrigation requirement}}{\text{Gross irrigation requirement}} \times 100\%$
    Where:
    *   **Net Irrigation Requirement (NIR):** The amount of water needed by the crop that is not met by rainfall or soil moisture.
    *   **Gross Irrigation Requirement (GIR):** The total amount of water that needs to be applied to the field to meet the NIR, accounting for all on-farm losses.
    $E_o = \frac{NIR}{GIR} \times 100\%$
    Also, if we consider $E_a$ as the efficiency of water reaching the root zone and $E_u$ as the efficiency of water used by the plant from the root zone:
    $E_o \approx E_c \times E_a \times E_u$ (This is an approximation as $E_u$ itself can be defined in different ways).
    A more practical definition for overall efficiency is the water actually used by the crop divided by the water diverted from the source:
    $E_o = \frac{\text{Water transpired by crop}}{\text{Water diverted from source}}$

**Example Calculation:**

Let's say:
*   Water diverted from the canal = 1000 m³
*   Water delivered to the field edge = 800 m³
*   Water stored in the root zone available to the crop = 600 m³
*   Water transpired by the crop = 500 m³

Calculate:
1.  Conveyance Efficiency ($E_c$)
2.  Application Efficiency ($E_a$)
3.  Water Use Efficiency ($E_u$)
4.  Overall Irrigation Efficiency ($E_o$) (using the definition: water transpired by crop / water diverted from source)

**Solution:**

1.  $E_c = \frac{800 \text{ m³}}{1000 \text{ m³}} \times 100\% = 80\%$
2.  $E_a = \frac{600 \text{ m³}}{800 \text{ m³}} \times 100\% = 75\%$
3.  $E_u = \frac{500 \text{ m³}}{800 \text{ m³}} \times 100\% = 62.5\%$ (This is the efficiency of water delivered to the field being transpired by the crop)
4.  $E_o = \frac{500 \text{ m³}}{1000 \text{ m³}} \times 100\% = 50\%$

### 5. Factors Affecting Irrigation Efficiency

*   **Irrigation Method:** Drip and sprinkler systems are generally more efficient than surface irrigation methods.
*   **System Design and Maintenance:** Poorly designed or maintained canals, pumps, or application systems lead to higher losses.
*   **Soil Characteristics:** Soil texture, structure, and infiltration rate influence how water is stored and distributed. Sandy soils may have high percolation losses, while clay soils can lead to runoff if water application is too fast.
*   **Field Topography:** Uneven or steep slopes can cause uneven water distribution and increased runoff.
*   **Water Management Practices:**
    *   **Irrigation Schedule:** Applying water at the right time (based on crop water needs and soil moisture) is crucial.
    *   **Irrigation Depth and Duration:** Over-irrigation or irrigating for too long leads to deep percolation and runoff.
    *   **Clogging of Emitters/Sprinklers:** Reduces the effectiveness of the system.
*   **Water Quality:** Saline water can contribute to salinization issues, indirectly affecting efficiency.
*   **Operator Skill and Knowledge:** The farmer's understanding of irrigation principles and practices.
*   **Climate:** High evaporation rates can increase water losses.

---

### 6. Strategies for Improving Irrigation Efficiency

*   **Modernization of Irrigation Systems:**
    *   **Lining of Canals:** Reduces seepage losses.
    *   **Pressurized Pipelines:** Eliminates conveyance losses from seepage and evaporation.
    *   **Adoption of Efficient Application Methods:** Switching from furrow to sprinkler or drip irrigation.
*   **Improved On-Farm Water Management:**
    *   **Proper System Design:** Tailoring the system to soil, crop, and topography.
    *   **Regular Maintenance:** Cleaning filters, repairing leaks, calibrating sprinklers.
    *   **Water Scheduling:** Using soil moisture sensors, weather data, or crop models to determine when and how much to irrigate.
    *   **Optimizing Irrigation Depth and Duration:** Applying just enough water to replenish the root zone.
    *   **Leveling Fields:** Reduces runoff and improves distribution uniformity.
*   **Drainage Systems:** Installing adequate drainage to prevent waterlogging and salinity.
*   **Training and Extension Services:** Educating farmers on efficient water management techniques.
*   **Water Pricing and Policies:** Implementing policies that encourage water conservation.
*   **Use of Treated Wastewater:** Can be a valuable resource if managed properly to avoid health and soil degradation issues.
*   **Mulching:** Reduces soil evaporation.

### Practice Questions/Exercises

**Question 1:**

Define Conveyance Efficiency and explain two major causes of water loss during conveyance.

**Answer 1:**

Conveyance Efficiency ($E_c$) is the ratio of the water delivered to the field to the water diverted from the source.
Major causes of water loss during conveyance are:
1.  **Seepage:** Water leaking out of unlined canals into the surrounding soil.
2.  **Evaporation:** Water lost from the surface of the canal due to atmospheric conditions.
3.  **Operational Losses:** Leakages from structures, unauthorized abstractions, and spillage.

**Question 2:**

A farmer irrigates a field using a sprinkler system. The gross irrigation depth applied is 60 mm. The net irrigation requirement (water actually stored in the root zone and available to the crop) is 45 mm. Calculate the Application Efficiency ($E_a$).

**Answer 2:**

Given:
Gross irrigation depth = 60 mm
Net irrigation requirement = 45 mm

Application Efficiency ($E_a$) is the ratio of the water stored in the root zone available to the crop to the water delivered to the field. In this context, the gross irrigation depth is the water delivered to the field, and the net irrigation requirement is the water stored and available.

$E_a = \frac{\text{Net irrigation requirement}}{\text{Gross irrigation depth}} \times 100\%$
$E_a = \frac{45 \text{ mm}}{60 \text{ mm}} \times 100\% = 0.75 \times 100\% = 75\%$

**Question 3:**

If a canal delivers 5000 m³ of water to a field head, but due to seepage and evaporation, only 4250 m³ reaches the field edge. Out of this 4250 m³, 3500 m³ is effectively stored in the root zone and made available to the crop. Calculate the Conveyance Efficiency and Application Efficiency.

**Answer 3:**

Given:
Water diverted from source (initial delivery to canal) = 5000 m³ (This is the amount diverted for the canal system)
Water delivered to the field edge = 4250 m³
Water stored in root zone and available to crop = 3500 m³

Conveyance Efficiency ($E_c$):
$E_c = \frac{\text{Water delivered to field edge}}{\text{Water diverted from source}} \times 100\%$
$E_c = \frac{4250 \text{ m³}}{5000 \text{ m³}} \times 100\% = 0.85 \times 100\% = 85\%$

Application Efficiency ($E_a$):
$E_a = \frac{\text{Water stored in root zone available to crop}}{\text{Water delivered to field edge}} \times 100\%$
$E_a = \frac{3500 \text{ m³}}{4250 \text{ m³}} \times 100\% \approx 0.8235 \times 100\% \approx 82.35\%$

**Question 4:**

Discuss any two ill effects of improper irrigation practices.

**Answer 4:**

Two ill effects of improper irrigation practices are:
1.  **Waterlogging:** This occurs when the water table rises too high, saturating the soil and reducing oxygen available to plant roots. It can be caused by over-irrigation or poor drainage, leading to stunted growth and reduced yields.
2.  **Salinization:** This is the accumulation of soluble salts in the soil. It often happens in arid and semi-arid regions where irrigation water contains dissolved salts, and poor drainage prevents leaching. As water evaporates from the soil surface, it leaves the salts behind, which can become toxic to plants and degrade soil structure.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   Irrigation is vital for food security, especially in regions with inadequate rainfall.
*   While beneficial, irrigation can cause severe environmental problems like waterlogging and salinization if not managed properly.
*   Irrigation efficiencies measure how effectively water is used at different stages.
*   **Conveyance Efficiency** ($E_c$) relates to water losses in transport.
*   **Application Efficiency** ($E_a$) relates to water losses within the farm (runoff, deep percolation).
*   **Overall Irrigation Efficiency** ($E_o$) is a comprehensive measure of how much water diverted from the source is actually used by the crop.
*   Modern irrigation methods (drip, sprinkler) are generally more efficient than traditional surface methods.
*   Improving irrigation efficiency is crucial for water conservation, economic viability, and environmental sustainability.
*   Factors like system design, soil type, topography, and management practices significantly impact efficiency.
