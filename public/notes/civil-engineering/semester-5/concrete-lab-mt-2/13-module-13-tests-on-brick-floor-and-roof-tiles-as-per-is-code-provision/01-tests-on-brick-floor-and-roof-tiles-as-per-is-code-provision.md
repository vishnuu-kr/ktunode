# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2) - Study Notes

## Module 13: Tests on Brick, Floor and Roof Tiles as per IS Code Provisions

This module focuses on understanding and performing various tests on bricks, floor tiles, and roof tiles as stipulated by Indian Standards (IS) codes. These tests are crucial for ensuring the quality, durability, and suitability of these building materials for their intended applications.

---

### **1. Introduction to Bricks, Floor Tiles, and Roof Tiles**

**1.1. Bricks:**

*   **Definition:** Bricks are rectangular blocks made from clay or shale, fired in a kiln, and used as a primary building material for walls, foundations, and paving.
*   **Classification:** Bricks are classified based on their strength, water absorption, and quality. Common classifications include:
    *   **First Class Bricks:** Used for load-bearing walls, ornamental works, and damp situations.
    *   **Second Class Bricks:** Used for general building construction where dampness is not a major concern.
    *   **Third Class Bricks:** Suitable for temporary structures and foundation work where dampness is a factor.
    *   **Fourth Class Bricks (over-burnt):** Can be used for foundations and floors where strength is a primary requirement.
*   **Importance of Testing:** Testing ensures that bricks meet specified strength, durability, and aesthetic requirements, preventing structural failures and premature deterioration.

**1.2. Floor Tiles:**

*   **Definition:** Floor tiles are thin, hard, and durable units used for covering floors, providing a finished surface that is aesthetically pleasing, easy to clean, and resistant to wear.
*   **Materials:** Commonly made from ceramic, porcelain, vitrified clay, or natural stones.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Ceramic Tiles:** Most common, available in various glazes and finishes.
    *   **Porcelain Tiles:** Denser and less porous than ceramic, suitable for high-traffic areas and outdoor use.
    *   **Vitrified Tiles:** Combine characteristics of ceramic and porcelain, offering good strength and low water absorption.
    *   **Natural Stone Tiles:** Granite, marble, slate, etc., offering unique aesthetics but can be more expensive and require sealing.
*   **Importance of Testing:** Tests on floor tiles ensure they have adequate strength, abrasion resistance, slip resistance, and water resistance for their intended use in different environments (e.g., residential, commercial, wet areas).

**1.3. Roof Tiles:**

*   **Definition:** Roof tiles are units designed to cover a roof structure, providing protection from the elements (rain, sun, wind) and contributing to the building's aesthetics.
*   **Materials:** Primarily clay (fired), concrete, or metal.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Clay Tiles:** Traditional, come in various profiles like pan and cover, interlocking, or flat.
    *   **Concrete Tiles:** More economical than clay, available in a wide range of profiles and colors.
    *   **Metal Tiles:** Lightweight, durable, and resistant to fire and extreme weather.
*   **Importance of Testing:** Tests on roof tiles ensure they possess sufficient strength to withstand wind loads, thermal expansion/contraction, resistance to water penetration, and durability against weathering.

### **3. Tests on Bricks**

**3.1. Absorption Test**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the amount of water a brick can absorb, which is an indicator of its porosity and durability. High absorption generally indicates lower quality.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 1077 (typically).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Select at least five representative bricks from a sample.
    2.  Dry the bricks in an oven at 105-115°C until they reach a constant weight. Record this dry weight ($W_1$).
    3.  Immerse the dried bricks completely in water at room temperature (27 ± 2°C) for 24 hours.
    4.  After 24 hours, remove the bricks, wipe their surfaces dry with a damp cloth, and immediately record their saturated weight ($W_2$).
    5.  Calculate the percentage water absorption using the formula:
        **Water Absorption (%) = $\frac{W_2 - W_1}{W_1} \times 100$**
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Porosity:** The measure of void spaces in the brick.
    *   **Saturation Coefficient:** The ratio of water absorption after 24 hours immersion to water absorption after being kept in boiling water for 5 hours. A lower saturation coefficient indicates better durability.
*   **Acceptable Limits (as per IS 1077 for First Class Bricks):**
    *   **Water absorption:** Not more than 20% by weight of dry brick.
    *   **Saturation Coefficient:** Not more than 0.80.
*   **Example:** If a dry brick weighs 2.5 kg and its saturated weight after 24 hours is 2.8 kg, its water absorption is $\frac{2.8 - 2.5}{2.5} \times 100 = 12\%$.

**3.2. Compressive Strength Test**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the maximum load a brick can withstand before failing, indicating its strength and suitability for load-bearing structures.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 1077 (typically).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Select at least five representative bricks.
    2.  Fill the frogs (indentations on the brick face) with sand or cement mortar as specified by the code to create a uniform bearing surface. Allow mortar to cure if used.
    3.  Place the prepared bricks in a testing machine (compression testing machine).
    4.  Apply a gradual compressive load at a specified rate until the brick fails.
    5.  Record the maximum load ($P$) at which failure occurs.
    6.  Calculate the compressive strength using the formula:
        **Compressive Strength (N/mm²) = $\frac{P}{A}$**
        where $A$ is the net cross-sectional area of the brick in contact with the loading platens.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Failure:** The point at which the brick cracks or disintegrates under load.
    *   **Crushing Strength:** The compressive strength of the brick.
*   **Acceptable Limits (as per IS 1077 for First Class Bricks):** Minimum average compressive strength of 7.5 N/mm². No individual brick should have a strength less than 5.0 N/mm².
*   **Example:** If a brick fails under a load of 150 kN and its cross-sectional area is 200 mm x 100 mm, its compressive strength is $\frac{150 \times 1000}{200 \times 100} = 7.5$ N/mm².

**3.3. Efflorescence Test**

*   **Purpose:** To detect the presence of soluble salts (like sulphates and carbonates of sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium) in the brick, which can cause unsightly staining and deterioration of the surrounding mortar.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 1077 (typically).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Select a representative brick.
    2.  Place the brick in a shallow dish with distilled water to a depth of about 25 mm.
    3.  Keep the dish in a warm, dry place so that the water evaporates gradually.
    4.  When the brick is dry, repeat the process of adding water and allowing it to evaporate for a total of 4 cycles.
    5.  Examine the brick surface for the presence of powdery deposits or crystalline efflorescence.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Efflorescence:** A powdery deposit of soluble salts on the surface of a brick, often caused by capillary action and subsequent evaporation of water.
    *   **Soluble Salts:** Salts that dissolve in water and can be transported to the surface.
*   **Acceptable Limits (as per IS 1077):**
    *   **No visible deposit:** For first-class bricks.
    *   **Slight powdery deposit:** Not exceeding 5% of the total surface area.
    *   **Noticeable powdery deposit:** Not exceeding 10% of the total surface area, and not in streaks.
*   **Example:** If a white powdery substance appears on 8% of the brick surface after the test, it may be acceptable for some classes of bricks but not for first-class bricks.

**3.4. Hardness Test**

*   **Purpose:** To assess the resistance of the brick to scratching, indicating its surface durability.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 1077 (typically).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Scratch the surface of the brick with a sharp fingernail.
    2.  If the fingernail leaves a distinct mark, the brick is considered soft.
    3.  If the brick resists scratching by a fingernail but can be scratched by a hard stone, it is considered of medium hardness.
    4.  If it cannot be scratched even by a hard stone, it is considered hard.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Surface Hardness:** The ability of the brick's surface to resist abrasion or scratching.
*   **Acceptable Requirements:** Bricks should be hard enough not to be easily scratched by a fingernail.

**3.5. Shape and Size Test**

*   **Purpose:** To ensure that bricks conform to the specified dimensions and have sharp edges and square corners, which facilitates proper masonry work.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 1077 (typically).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Select a sample of bricks.
    2.  Measure the length, width, and height of each brick using a measuring tape or calipers.
    3.  Check for the presence of sharp edges and square corners.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Dimensional Accuracy:** The closeness of the brick's dimensions to the specified standard.
    *   **Tolerance:** The permissible variation from the specified dimensions.
*   **Acceptable Requirements:** Bricks should be uniform in size and shape, with sharp edges and square corners. They should conform to the standard dimensions as per IS 1077.

**3.6. Soundness Test (Percussion Test)**

*   **Purpose:** To determine if the brick is well-burnt and free from cracks or flaws that might cause it to break easily when struck.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 1077 (typically).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Take two bricks.
    2.  Strike them against each other.
    3.  Alternatively, suspend a brick by a string and strike it with a metal hammer.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Soundness:** The property of a brick that indicates it is free from internal stresses or defects and will produce a clear ringing sound when struck.
*   **Acceptable Requirements:** The bricks should produce a clear metallic ringing sound. If a dull sound is produced, it indicates that the brick is cracked or over-burnt.

### **4. Tests on Floor Tiles**

**4.1. Water Absorption Test**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the capacity of a tile to absorb water, which is crucial for assessing its frost resistance and suitability for wet areas.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 13712 (Methods of Test), IS 13007 (Specification for Floor Tiles).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Select a representative sample of tiles.
    2.  Dry the tiles in an oven at 105-115°C until they reach a constant weight ($W_1$).
    3.  Immerse the dried tiles in water at room temperature (27 ± 2°C) for 24 hours.
    4.  After 24 hours, remove the tiles and wipe their surfaces dry with a cloth. Record their saturated weight ($W_2$).
    5.  Calculate the percentage water absorption:
        **Water Absorption (%) = $\frac{W_2 - W_1}{W_1} \times 100$**
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Vitrification:** The process of making ceramic materials glassy and non-porous through high-temperature firing. Higher vitrification leads to lower water absorption.
*   **Acceptable Limits (as per IS 13007 for Floor Tiles):**
    *   **Vitrified Tiles:** Not more than 0.5%.
    *   **Glazed Ceramic Tiles:** Typically between 3% and 6%.
    *   **Unglazed Tiles:** Generally between 6% and 10%.

**4.2. Abrasion Resistance Test (PEI Rating)**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the resistance of the tile surface to wear and tear from foot traffic and abrasion. This is often expressed as a PEI (Porcelain Enamel Institute) rating.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 13712 (Methods of Test), IS 13007 (Specification for Floor Tiles).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  A standard abrasion machine is used.
    2.  A specific abrasive material (e.g., sand) is applied to the tile surface.
    3.  A rotating wheel with a specific load is passed over the abrasive material, simulating foot traffic.
    4.  The number of passes required to wear away a certain amount of the glaze or surface is recorded.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Abrasion:** The process of scraping or wearing away.
    *   **PEI Rating:** A scale indicating the durability of a tile's surface under abrasion. Higher PEI ratings mean higher durability.
*   **PEI Scale (General Guideline):**
    *   **PEI 0:** No foot traffic (wall tiles only).
    *   **PEI I:** Very light traffic (e.g., residential bathrooms).
    *   **PEI II:** Light traffic (e.g., residential bedrooms, bathrooms).
    *   **PEI III:** Moderate traffic (e.g., residential floors, small commercial areas).
    *   **PEI IV:** Moderate to heavy traffic (e.g., all residential floors, hotel lobbies, offices).
    *   **PEI V:** Heavy to extra-heavy traffic (e.g., commercial areas with heavy foot traffic, shopping malls, airports).
*   **Example:** A tile with a PEI rating of 4 is suitable for most residential floors and many commercial applications, while a PEI 2 tile would be appropriate for a low-traffic bathroom.

**4.3. Breaking Strength Test**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the load a tile can withstand before breaking, indicating its structural integrity.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 13712 (Methods of Test), IS 13007 (Specification for Floor Tiles).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Support the tile on two parallel knife-edge supports at a specified distance apart.
    2.  Apply a gradually increasing load at the center of the tile.
    3.  Record the load at which the tile breaks.
    4.  Calculate the breaking strength.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Modulus of Rupture:** A measure of the tensile strength of a brittle material.
*   **Acceptable Limits:** Specified in the individual tile standards based on the tile type and intended use.

**4.4. Slip Resistance Test**

*   **Purpose:** To assess how slippery the tile surface is, which is critical for safety, especially in areas prone to moisture.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 13712 (Methods of Test), IS 13007 (Specification for Floor Tiles).
*   **Procedure:** Varies depending on the specific test method referenced in the IS code. It often involves measuring the coefficient of friction between the tile surface and a standardized shoe sole or pendulum slider under various conditions (dry, wet, soapy).
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Coefficient of Friction (COF):** A measure of the resistance to sliding between two surfaces.
    *   **Pendulum Test:** A common method where a pendulum with a standardized slider is allowed to swing across the tile surface.
*   **Acceptable Requirements:** Higher COF values indicate better slip resistance. The required COF will vary based on the intended application (e.g., higher for bathrooms or ramps).

### **5. Tests on Roof Tiles**

**5.1. Water Absorption Test**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the water absorption capacity of roof tiles, which affects their durability, resistance to freeze-thaw cycles, and staining.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 4846 (Concrete roofing tiles), IS 13712 (for ceramic tiles, if applicable).
*   **Procedure:** Similar to the absorption test for bricks and floor tiles.
    1.  Dry the tiles in an oven at 105-115°C until constant weight ($W_1$).
    2.  Immerse in water at 27 ± 2°C for 24 hours.
    3.  Record saturated weight ($W_2$).
    4.  Calculate Water Absorption (%) = $\frac{W_2 - W_1}{W_1} \times 100$.
*   **Acceptable Limits (as per IS 4846 for Concrete Roofing Tiles):** Not more than 10% by weight of dry tile.

**5.2. Transverse Strength Test (Load Bearing Capacity)**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the strength of the roof tile to withstand loads, such as wind pressure or the weight of people walking on the roof during maintenance.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 4846 (Concrete roofing tiles).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Place the roof tile on two supports at a specified span.
    2.  Apply a gradually increasing load at the center of the tile (or at a specified point according to the profile).
    3.  Record the load at which the tile breaks.
    4.  Calculate the transverse strength.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Span:** The distance between the supports.
    *   **Load:** The force applied to the tile.
*   **Acceptable Limits (as per IS 4846 for Concrete Roofing Tiles):** Minimum transverse strength varies based on the type of tile (e.g., single Roman tile, double Roman tile), typically around 600 N to 1200 N depending on the loading condition.

**5.3. Efflorescence Test**

*   **Purpose:** To check for the presence of soluble salts that can cause discoloration and affect the durability of roof tiles, especially in humid climates.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 4846 (Concrete roofing tiles).
*   **Procedure:** Similar to the efflorescence test for bricks.
*   **Acceptable Limits (as per IS 4846):** Not more than 5% of the total surface area should show a powdery deposit.

**5.4. Shape and Size Test**

*   **Purpose:** To ensure that roof tiles are manufactured to the correct dimensions and profiles for proper interlocking and fit, ensuring water-tightness and structural stability of the roof.
*   **IS Code Reference:** IS 4846 (Concrete roofing tiles).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Select a sample of tiles.
    2.  Measure the length, width, and overall dimensions.
    3.  Check the profile and interlocking features for accuracy.
*   **Acceptable Requirements:** Tiles should be uniform in shape and size, with well-defined profiles and no warping or distortion.

### **7. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of the absorption test for bricks?
**Answer:** To determine the amount of water a brick can absorb, which is an indicator of its porosity and durability. High absorption generally indicates lower quality.

**Question 2:** A brick has a dry weight of 2.2 kg and a saturated weight of 2.6 kg after 24 hours immersion. Calculate its water absorption and state if it is suitable for first-class brickwork according to IS 1077.
**Calculation:** Water Absorption = $\frac{2.6 - 2.2}{2.2} \times 100 = \frac{0.4}{2.2} \times 100 \approx 18.18\%$
**Suitability:** For first-class bricks, water absorption should not exceed 20%. So, this brick is suitable based on this parameter.

**Question 3:** Define "efflorescence" and explain its significance in brickwork.
**Answer:** Efflorescence is a powdery deposit of soluble salts on the surface of a brick, caused by capillary action and evaporation of water containing these salts. Its significance lies in its potential to cause unsightly staining and long-term deterioration of the brickwork and surrounding mortar.

**Question 4:** Which test is used to assess the resistance of floor tiles to wear and tear from foot traffic?
**Answer:** The Abrasion Resistance Test (often indicated by the PEI rating).

**Question 5:** Why is the transverse strength test important for roof tiles?
**Answer:** It determines the tile's ability to withstand loads like wind pressure or maintenance foot traffic, ensuring the structural integrity and weather resistance of the roof.

**Question 6:** What is the typical acceptable limit for water absorption for vitrified floor tiles as per IS codes?
**Answer:** Not more than 0.5%.
