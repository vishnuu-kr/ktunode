# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2) - Module 7: Test on Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value

## 1. Introduction and Purpose of the Test

The Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value test is a fundamental laboratory test conducted to assess the **strength of coarse aggregates** under **compressive loads**. It provides an indication of how well the aggregate will resist crushing when subjected to direct compressive forces, such as those experienced in pavement layers or in the production of concrete.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Coarse Aggregate:** Crushed stone, gravel, or slag used as a constituent in concrete.
*   **Crushing:** The process of breaking or fracturing material under pressure.
*   **Compressive Strength:** The ability of a material to withstand being compressed without failing.

**Purpose of the Test:**

*   To determine the **resistance of coarse aggregates to crushing** under gradual applied compressive load.
*   To evaluate the **suitability of aggregates for use in concrete mixes**, especially for pavements and structures subjected to heavy traffic or loads.
*   To ensure that aggregates meet specified strength requirements for various construction applications.

**Learning Outcome Covered:**

*   **Understand the purpose and significance of the Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value test.**

## 2. Principle of the Test

The test involves subjecting a sample of coarse aggregate to a **gradual compressive load** in a **standardized testing machine** (e.g., a compression testing machine). The load is applied to a cylindrical portion of the aggregate sample placed within a **steel cylinder**. The percentage of material that passes a **specified sieve** after the load application is the **Aggregate Crushing Value (ACV)**. A lower ACV indicates a stronger aggregate.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Standardized Testing Machine:** A machine designed and calibrated to apply a consistent and controlled compressive load.
*   **Steel Cylinder:** A cylindrical mold used to contain the aggregate sample during testing.
*   **Gradual Applied Load:** The load is increased slowly and steadily over a specified period to simulate realistic stress conditions.
*   **Specified Sieve:** A sieve with a particular aperture size, typically 2.36 mm, used to measure the fine material produced by crushing.

## 3. Apparatus Required

A successful Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value test requires specific and calibrated equipment.

**Essential Apparatus:**

*   **Cylindrical Steel Cup (Crushing Cylinder):** With a diameter of 100 mm and a height of 115-127 mm. It usually has a hardened steel plunger that fits closely into the cylinder.
*   **Plunger:** A hardened steel disk that fits into the cylinder and transmits the load to the aggregate.
*   **Base Plate:** A hardened steel plate to support the cylinder.
*   **Tamping Rod:** A cylindrical steel rod, approximately 10 mm diameter and 300 mm long, for compacting the aggregate in the cylinder.
*   **Balance:** Accurate to 0.1 g.
*   **Sieves:** Of the specified sizes (typically 10 mm and 12.5 mm, and the 2.36 mm sieve for determining the crushing value).
*   **Compression Testing Machine:** Capable of applying a load at a controlled rate.
*   **Oven:** For drying the aggregate sample.
*   **Steel Disc:** To distribute the load evenly.

## 4. Test Procedure

The test procedure must be followed precisely to ensure accurate and reproducible results.

**Steps:**

1.  **Sample Preparation:**
    *   Obtain a representative sample of the coarse aggregate.
    *   **Sieve the aggregate to the specified size range.** For the standard test, this is typically **10 mm to 12.5 mm**.
    *   **Dry the sample in an oven at a temperature of 100-110°C until a constant weight is achieved.** This removes any moisture that could affect the crushing strength.
    *   **Cool the sample to room temperature.**

2.  **Filling the Cylinder:**
    *   Place the base plate on the cylinder.
    *   Fill the cylinder with the prepared aggregate in **three equal layers**.
    *   **Compact each layer by giving 25 strokes with the tamping rod.** Ensure the tamping rod is dropped vertically and evenly distributed across the surface of each layer.
    *   **Level the surface of the aggregate with the top of the cylinder.**

3.  **Applying the Load:**
    *   Place the steel disc on top of the aggregate in the cylinder.
    *   Place the cylinder with the aggregate and disc in the center of the compression testing machine.
    *   Apply the load **gradually and uniformly at a rate of 40 ± 5 kN/s** (or as specified by the relevant standard, e.g., IS 2386 Part IV).
    *   **Continue applying the load until the cylinder reaches a total load of 1100 kN (for normal-weight aggregates).** The load should be applied for approximately **10 minutes**.
    *   **Record the maximum load applied.**

4.  **Determining the Crushing Value:**
    *   Remove the cylinder from the testing machine.
    *   **Carefully remove the crushed aggregate from the cylinder and sieve it using the 2.36 mm sieve.**
    *   **Weigh the material passing the 2.36 mm sieve.** Let this weight be 'b'.
    *   **Weigh the initial sample of aggregate taken for the test.** Let this weight be 'a'.

5.  **Calculation:**
    *   The **Aggregate Crushing Value (ACV)** is calculated using the following formula:

        $$ \text{ACV} = \frac{b}{a} \times 100 $$

        Where:
        *   'b' = Weight of material passing the 2.36 mm sieve (grams)
        *   'a' = Total weight of the oven-dried sample taken for the test (grams)

## 5. Interpretation of Results

The ACV provides a direct measure of the aggregate's crushing resistance.

**Key Points for Interpretation:**

*   **Lower ACV = Higher Strength:** An aggregate with a lower crushing value is considered stronger and more resistant to crushing.
*   **Higher ACV = Weaker Aggregate:** An aggregate with a higher crushing value is weaker and more prone to crushing.
*   **Specification Limits:** The acceptable ACV varies depending on the intended use of the aggregate. For instance, aggregates used in wearing courses of roads or for concrete pavements generally require a lower ACV compared to aggregates used in mass concrete.

**Typical Specification Limits (Refer to relevant IS codes or standards for precise values):**

*   **Wearing Courses:** Typically less than **30%**.
*   **Other Parts of Roads and Concrete:** Typically less than **40%**.

## 6. Factors Affecting the Crushing Value

Several factors can influence the outcome of the test.

**Factors:**

*   **Aggregate Type and Composition:** Different types of rocks (e.g., granite, basalt, limestone) have inherent differences in strength due to their mineral composition and geological formation.
*   **Aggregate Shape:** Flaky and elongated aggregates tend to have lower crushing strength due to stress concentrations at their sharp edges or points.
*   **Aggregate Size and Gradation:** The test is conducted on a specific size range. Variations in grading can affect the packing density and load distribution within the cylinder.
*   **Moisture Content:** While the sample is dried, any residual moisture or significant variations in drying can impact results.
*   **Loading Rate:** The rate at which the load is applied is critical for consistent results.
*   **Compaction Effort:** The number of tamping strokes directly influences the density of the aggregate sample in the cylinder.

**Learning Outcome Covered:**

## 7. Importance and Applications

The ACV test is crucial for ensuring the durability and performance of concrete and pavement structures.

**Applications:**

*   **Road Construction:** Essential for selecting aggregates for different layers of pavements, especially the wearing course, to withstand traffic loads and abrasion.
*   **Concrete Mix Design:** Helps in selecting suitable aggregates for concrete mixes based on the required strength and durability of the final concrete product.
*   **Structural Concrete:** Important for aggregates used in reinforced concrete structures where they are subjected to significant loads.
*   **Quality Control:** Used as a quality control measure to ensure that aggregates supplied for construction projects meet the required strength specifications.

## 8. Comparison with Impact Value Test

While both tests assess aggregate strength, they do so under different stress conditions.

**Comparison:**

| Feature             | Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value Test                                       | Impact Value Test                                                              |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Type of Stress**  | **Gradual Compressive Load**                                               | **Sudden Shock or Impact Load**                                                |
| **Purpose**         | Measures resistance to **crushing** under compression.                     | Measures resistance to **fracture** or **breakage** under impact.              |
| **Apparatus**       | Compression testing machine, cylindrical steel cup, plunger.               | Impact testing machine, hammer, anvil, measuring cylinder.                     |
| **Result**          | **Aggregate Crushing Value (ACV)** - Percentage passing 2.36 mm sieve.    | **Aggregate Impact Value (AIV)** - Percentage passing 3.35 mm sieve.           |
| **Sensitivity**     | Sensitive to the inherent compressive strength of the aggregate.           | Sensitive to the toughness and resistance to fracture under impact.            |
| **Applications**    | Pavement layers, concrete structures subjected to compressive loads.      | Roads and railways subjected to dynamic and impact loads (e.g., traffic).    |

**Learning Outcome Covered:**

*   **Differentiate the Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value test from the Impact Value test, highlighting their respective purposes and testing conditions.**

Safety is paramount during laboratory testing.

## 10. Practice Questions and Exercises

Let's test your understanding!

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of the Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value test?
    a) To measure the resistance to abrasion.
    b) To measure the resistance to crushing under compressive load.
    c) To measure the resistance to disintegration by weathering.
    d) To measure the specific gravity of aggregates.

**Answer:** b) To measure the resistance to crushing under compressive load.

---

**Question 2:** Which sieve is typically used to determine the fine material produced after crushing in the ACV test?
    a) 4.75 mm sieve
    b) 2.36 mm sieve
    c) 600 micron sieve
    d) 10 mm sieve

**Answer:** b) 2.36 mm sieve

---

**Question 3:** If a sample of coarse aggregate weighing 500 g passes the 2.36 mm sieve after crushing, and the total weight of the initial dry sample was 1500 g, calculate the Aggregate Crushing Value.

**Answer:**
    *   Weight passing 2.36 mm sieve (b) = 500 g
    *   Total weight of initial sample (a) = 1500 g
    *   ACV = (b / a) * 100 = (500 / 1500) * 100 = **33.33%**

---

**Question 4:** Briefly explain why drying the aggregate sample to a constant weight is crucial before conducting the ACV test.

**Answer:** Drying the aggregate sample ensures that the results are not influenced by moisture content, which can affect the aggregate's strength and the load distribution within the cylinder. Testing dry aggregates provides a consistent baseline for comparison.

---

**Question 5:** What does a low Aggregate Crushing Value indicate about the aggregate?

**Answer:** A low Aggregate Crushing Value indicates that the aggregate is strong and has a high resistance to crushing.

---

**Question 6:** What are the typical size ranges for aggregates used in the standard Coarse Aggregate Crushing Value test?

**Answer:** The typical size range for aggregates used in the standard test is **10 mm to 12.5 mm**.
