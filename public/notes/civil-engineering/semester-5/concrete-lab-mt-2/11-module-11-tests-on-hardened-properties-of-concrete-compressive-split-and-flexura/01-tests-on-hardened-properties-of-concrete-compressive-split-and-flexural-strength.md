# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2): Module 11 - Tests on Hardened Properties of Concrete

## Topic: Compressive, Split Tensile, and Flexural Strength Tests

---

## 1. Introduction to Hardened Properties of Concrete

After concrete has set and hardened, its properties are evaluated to ensure it meets design requirements and performance standards. These properties dictate the structural integrity and durability of concrete elements. This module focuses on three key hardened properties:

*   **Compressive Strength:** The ability of concrete to resist crushing forces.
*   **Tensile Strength:** The ability of concrete to resist being pulled apart. This is typically measured indirectly through the **Split Tensile Strength** test.
*   **Flexural Strength (Modulus of Rupture):** The ability of concrete to resist bending stresses.

## 2. Compressive Strength Test

### 2.1. Learning Outcomes Covered:
*   Understand the principles and procedure of the compressive strength test.
*   Identify the equipment used for the compressive strength test.
*   Calculate the compressive strength of concrete.
*   Recognize the significance of the compressive strength test in concrete engineering.

### 2.2. Key Concepts & Definitions:
*   **Compressive Strength:** The maximum compressive stress a concrete specimen can withstand before failure. It is the most important property of hardened concrete, as it dictates the load-bearing capacity of structural members.
*   **Cylinder Test:** The most common method for determining compressive strength. Standard cylinders are cured and then subjected to axial compression.
*   **Cube Test:** An alternative method using cubic specimens. While easier to cast and handle, cylinders are generally preferred for representing the strength of concrete in situ.
*   **Failure Modes:** How the concrete specimen breaks under compression. Common modes include shear cone formation, splitting, and general crushing.
*   **Characteristic Strength:** The strength of concrete below which not more than 5% of the test results are expected to fall.
*   **Design Strength:** The characteristic strength divided by a partial safety factor.

### 2.3. Procedure (Based on IS 516 - Methods of Tests for Strength of Concrete):

1.  **Specimen Preparation:**
    *   Cylindrical specimens (typically 150mm diameter x 300mm height) or Cubical specimens (150mm x 150mm x 150mm) are cast according to specified curing conditions (e.g., standard curing).
    *   The number of specimens typically required for a test at a given age is three.
    *   Ensure specimens are free from surface moisture before testing.

2.  **Testing Machine:**
    *   A calibrated compression testing machine (CTM) with a suitable capacity is used.
    *   The machine should have a spherically-seated loading platform to ensure uniform distribution of load.

3.  **Testing:**
    *   Place the specimen centrally on the lower platen of the CTM.
    *   Ensure the axis of the cylinder is aligned vertically with the center of the thrust of the upper platen.
    *   Apply the load gradually and continuously without shock at a *rate of approximately 14 N/mm² per minute* for cylinders and *20 N/mm² per minute* for cubes.
    *   Record the maximum load at which the specimen fails.

4.  **Calculation:**
    *   **Compressive Strength (MPa) = Maximum Load (N) / Cross-sectional Area of Specimen (mm²)**

    *   **For Cylindrical Specimen (diameter 'd'):**
        Area = $\pi \times (d/2)^2 = (\pi d^2) / 4$
        For d = 150 mm, Area $\approx 17671 mm^2$

    *   **For Cubical Specimen (side 's'):**
        Area = $s \times s = s^2$
        For s = 150 mm, Area = $22500 mm^2$

5.  **Result Reporting:**
    *   Report the average of the three specimens' strengths. If one result deviates significantly (e.g., by more than 15% from the average), it can be discarded.

### 2.4. Significance:
*   **Design Basis:** Compressive strength is the primary parameter for designing concrete mixes and structural elements.
*   **Quality Control:** It verifies that the concrete produced meets the specified strength requirements.
*   **Durability Indicator:** Higher strength often correlates with better durability.

### 2.5. Important Points to Remember:
*   **Curing:** Proper curing is crucial for achieving the intended strength.
*   **Loading Rate:** Adhering to the specified loading rate is vital for accurate results.
*   **Specimen Condition:** Surface smoothness and freedom from moisture are important.
*   **Failure Pattern:** Observe the failure pattern to understand the concrete's behavior.

## 3. Split Tensile Strength Test

### 3.1. Learning Outcomes Covered:
*   Understand the principles and procedure of the split tensile strength test.
*   Identify the equipment used for the split tensile strength test.
*   Calculate the split tensile strength of concrete.
*   Recognize the importance of split tensile strength in assessing concrete's behavior under tensile stress.

### 3.2. Key Concepts & Definitions:
*   **Tensile Strength:** Concrete is weak in tension due to its brittle nature and the presence of microcracks.
*   **Split Tensile Strength:** An indirect method to determine the tensile strength of concrete. A cylindrical specimen is subjected to a diametrical compressive load. The tensile stress is developed across the horizontal plane at the center of the specimen due to this compression.
*   **Tensile Stress Distribution:** When a diametrical compression is applied, a uniform tensile stress is developed across the vertical diametrical plane.

### 3.3. Procedure (Based on IS 5816 - Method of Test for Tensile Strength of Concrete by Split Cylinder):

1.  **Specimen Preparation:**
    *   Cylindrical specimens (typically 150mm diameter x 300mm height) are used.
    *   Ensure the specimens are properly cured and their surfaces are smooth and clean.

2.  **Testing Machine:**
    *   A compression testing machine (CTM) is used.
    *   Cylindrical pads or strips (typically 150mm long, 13mm thick, and 25mm wide) are placed between the specimen and the loading platens to distribute the load. These pads help prevent premature failure due to stress concentrations at the points of contact.

3.  **Testing:**
    *   Place the specimen on its side on the lower platen of the CTM.
    *   Place the bearing strips centrally on the upper and lower surfaces of the specimen, aligned with the diameter along which the load is to be applied.
    *   Ensure the axis of the cylinder is perpendicular to the loading surface.
    *   Apply the load gradually and continuously without shock at a rate of approximately 2.0 N/mm² per minute (of the area of the specimen).
    *   Record the maximum load at which the specimen splits into two halves.

4.  **Calculation:**
    *   **Split Tensile Strength (MPa) = (2 $\times$ Maximum Load (N)) / ($\pi \times$ Length of Specimen (mm) $\times$ Diameter of Specimen (mm))**

    *   For a specimen of diameter 'd' and length 'l':
        Split Tensile Strength = $(2 \times P) / (\pi \times l \times d)$
        Where P is the maximum load.

5.  **Result Reporting:**
    *   Report the average of the three specimens' strengths.

### 3.4. Significance:
*   **Tensile Behavior:** Provides an estimate of concrete's tensile strength, which is important for understanding its behavior in situations like shrinkage, thermal stresses, and the formation of microcracks.
*   **Crack Resistance:** A higher split tensile strength generally indicates better resistance to cracking.
*   **Structural Design:** Used in certain design considerations, especially for elements subjected to tensile stresses or for estimating bond strength.

### 3.5. Important Points to Remember:
*   **Bearing Strips:** The use and proper placement of bearing strips are critical for obtaining valid results.
*   **Loading Rate:** A slower, controlled loading rate is specified for this test.
*   **Specimen Alignment:** Accurate alignment of the specimen and bearing strips is essential.

## 4. Flexural Strength Test (Modulus of Rupture)

### 4.1. Learning Outcomes Covered:
*   Understand the principles and procedure of the flexural strength test.
*   Identify the equipment used for the flexural strength test.
*   Calculate the flexural strength of concrete.
*   Appreciate the relevance of flexural strength in pavement and slab design.

### 4.2. Key Concepts & Definitions:
*   **Flexural Strength (Modulus of Rupture - MOR):** The maximum tensile stress that concrete can withstand before fracturing when subjected to bending. It's an indicator of the concrete's ability to resist bending stresses.
*   **Third-Point Loading (or Four-Point Loading):** A common method for conducting the flexural strength test. The load is applied at two points along the specimen, creating a region of pure bending.
*   **Beam Specimens:** Prismatic specimens (typically 150mm x 150mm x 500mm or 100mm x 100mm x 400mm) are used for this test.
*   **Pure Bending:** A condition where the bending moment is constant along a section of the specimen.

### 4.3. Procedure (Based on IS 516 - Methods of Tests for Strength of Concrete):

1.  **Specimen Preparation:**
    *   Prismatic beam specimens are cast and cured according to standard procedures.
    *   The dimensions of the specimen are measured accurately.

2.  **Testing Machine:**
    *   A flexural testing machine or a universal testing machine with appropriate fixtures.
    *   The specimen is supported on two knife-edge supports at a specific span length (typically 400mm for 150mm specimens, 300mm for 100mm specimens).
    *   The loads are applied through two similar knife-edge loading fixtures spaced at one-third of the span length.

3.  **Testing:**
    *   Place the specimen on the supports with the tension surface facing downwards (the smoother side).
    *   Apply the load gradually and continuously at a rate of loading that produces a rate of increase of the **outer fiber stress** of approximately **0.01 N/mm² per second** for third-point loading.
    *   Record the maximum load at which the specimen fails.

4.  **Calculation:**
    *   **Flexural Strength (Modulus of Rupture) (MPa) = (3 $\times$ Load (N) $\times$ Applied Span (mm)) / (2 $\times$ Width of Specimen (mm) $\times$ Depth of Specimen (mm)²) **

    *   For third-point loading with span 'L' and specimen dimensions 'b' (width) and 'd' (depth):
        MOR = $(3 \times P \times L) / (2 \times b \times d^2)$
        Where P is the maximum load.

    *   **Note:** The applied span is the distance between the two outer supports.

5.  **Result Reporting:**
    *   Report the average of the three specimens' strengths.

### 4.4. Significance:
*   **Pavement Design:** Flexural strength is a critical parameter for the design of rigid pavements (concrete roads), as it determines the ability of the slab to withstand traffic loads without cracking.
*   **Slab and Bridge Decks:** Relevant for the design of concrete slabs, bridge decks, and other elements subjected to bending.
*   **Resistance to Cracking:** Provides an indication of concrete's resistance to cracking under service loads.

### 4.5. Important Points to Remember:
*   **Specimen Dimensions:** Accurate measurement of specimen dimensions is crucial for calculation.
*   **Span and Loading Points:** Correctly set up the supports and loading points according to the standard.
*   **Loading Rate:** The specified rate of increase in outer fiber stress must be maintained.
*   **Tension Surface:** The side of the beam that is expected to be in tension should be placed downwards.

## 5. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1:**
A cylindrical concrete specimen of 150mm diameter and 300mm height was tested for compressive strength. It failed under a maximum load of 450 kN. Calculate the compressive strength of the concrete.

**Answer:**
*   Maximum Load (P) = 450 kN = 450,000 N
*   Diameter (d) = 150 mm
*   Cross-sectional Area = $(\pi \times d^2) / 4 = (\pi \times 150^2) / 4 = 17671.46 mm^2$
*   Compressive Strength = $P / Area = 450,000 N / 17671.46 mm^2 = 25.46 MPa$

**Question 2:**
During a split tensile strength test, a concrete cylinder (150mm diameter, 300mm length) failed under a load of 150 kN. Calculate the split tensile strength.

**Answer:**
*   Maximum Load (P) = 150 kN = 150,000 N
*   Diameter (d) = 150 mm
*   Length (l) = 300 mm
*   Split Tensile Strength = $(2 \times P) / (\pi \times l \times d) = (2 \times 150,000 N) / (\pi \times 300 mm \times 150 mm)$
*   Split Tensile Strength = $300,000 N / 141371.67 mm^2 = 2.12 MPa$

**Question 3:**
A concrete beam specimen of size 150mm (width) x 150mm (depth) x 500mm (length) was tested for flexural strength using third-point loading with a span of 400mm. It failed under a load of 25 kN. Calculate the flexural strength (Modulus of Rupture).

**Answer:**
*   Maximum Load (P) = 25 kN = 25,000 N
*   Width (b) = 150 mm
*   Depth (d) = 150 mm
*   Applied Span (L) = 400 mm
*   Flexural Strength = $(3 \times P \times L) / (2 \times b \times d^2) = (3 \times 25,000 N \times 400 mm) / (2 \times 150 mm \times 150 mm^2)$
*   Flexural Strength = $30,000,000 Nmm / 675,000 mm^3 = 44.44 MPa$

**Exercise 1:**
List three crucial factors that can influence the compressive strength of concrete.

**Answer:**
1.  Water-cement ratio
2.  Curing conditions (temperature and humidity)
3.  Age of concrete
4.  Quality and type of aggregates and cement
5.  Compaction effort

**Exercise 2:**
Why is the split tensile strength test preferred over a direct tensile test for routine concrete testing?

**Answer:**
Direct tensile tests are difficult to perform accurately because it's challenging to grip the specimen without causing stress concentrations at the grips, leading to premature failure in the grip area rather than in the concrete itself. The split tensile strength test, while indirect, provides a more practical and consistent method for estimating tensile strength.

## 6. Summary and Key Takeaways

*   **Compressive Strength** is the most critical property, defining concrete's load-bearing capacity. Tested using cylinders or cubes under axial compression.
*   **Split Tensile Strength** is an indirect measure of tensile strength, important for understanding crack resistance and behavior under tensile stresses. Tested on cylinders loaded diametrically.
*   **Flexural Strength (Modulus of Rupture)** measures resistance to bending and is vital for pavements and slabs. Tested on beam specimens under third-point loading.
*   **Consistent Procedures:** Adhering to specified procedures, loading rates, and specimen preparation is essential for accurate and reliable results.
*   **Quality Control:** These tests are fundamental for quality control in concrete construction, ensuring that the hardened concrete meets design specifications.
