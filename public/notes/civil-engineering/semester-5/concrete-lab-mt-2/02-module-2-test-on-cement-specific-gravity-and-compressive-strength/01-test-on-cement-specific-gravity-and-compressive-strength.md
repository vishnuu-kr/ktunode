## 1. Test for Specific Gravity of Cement

### 1.1. Introduction and Significance

*   **Definition:** Specific gravity of a substance is the ratio of its density to the density of a reference substance (usually water) at a specified temperature.
*   **For Cement:** It is the ratio of the density of cement to the density of water at a standard temperature (typically 27°C).
*   **Significance:**
    *   **Quality Control:** A deviation from the standard specific gravity (usually around 3.15) can indicate adulteration or improper manufacturing of cement.
    *   **Mix Design:** Specific gravity is a crucial parameter in the volumetric proportioning of concrete mixes. It helps in calculating the volume occupied by cement in the mix, which is essential for achieving the desired strength and workability.
    *   **Hydration Studies:** It plays a role in understanding the density changes during the hydration process of cement.

### 1.2. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Density:** Mass per unit volume of a substance.
*   **Specific Gravity (G):** $G = \frac{\text{Density of Cement}}{\text{Density of Water}} = \frac{\text{Weight of Cement / Volume of Cement}}{\text{Weight of Water / Volume of Water}}$
*   **Le Chatelier Flask:** A specially designed glass flask used for this test. It has a narrow graduated neck to accurately measure volume changes.
*   **Naphtha / Kerosene:** Used as a liquid to wet the cement particles. Water is not used directly because it reacts chemically with cement (hydration), leading to inaccurate results. Naphtha or kerosene are inert and fill the voids between cement particles.

### 1.3. Apparatus Required

*   Le Chatelier Flask
*   Balance (accurate to 0.01 g)
*   Thermometer
*   Spatula
*   Oven (for drying cement)
*   Weighing bottle
*   Distilled water
*   Naphtha or Kerosene
*   Sample of cement

### 1.4. Procedure (as per IS 269 / BS EN 196-5)

1.  **Preparation of Cement Sample:**
    *   Ensure the cement sample is dry and free from lumps.
    *   Pass the cement through a sieve of appropriate mesh size (e.g., 90 micron IS sieve) if necessary to break any lumps.

2.  **Filling the Le Chatelier Flask with Liquid:**
    *   Fill the Le Chatelier flask with distilled water up to the zero mark of the graduated scale.
    *   Record the initial temperature of the water.

3.  **Adding Naphtha/Kerosene:**
    *   Add approximately 65-70 grams of dry cement to the Le Chatelier flask containing water.
    *   Carefully add naphtha or kerosene to the flask until the level of the liquid reaches the zero mark of the graduated scale. The naphtha/kerosene should cover the cement completely and fill the space between the cement particles.

4.  **Removing Entrapped Air:**
    *   Gently rotate the flask to dislodge any air bubbles entrapped in the cement or liquid. You can tap the flask gently.
    *   Continue adding naphtha/kerosene until the liquid level again reaches the zero mark.

5.  **Record the Final Reading:**
    *   Allow the flask to stand for a short period to ensure all cement has settled.
    *   Record the final reading on the graduated scale of the Le Chatelier flask. Let this be $V_1$ ml.

6.  **Weighing the Cement:**
    *   Carefully transfer the cement from the Le Chatelier flask into a weighing bottle.
    *   Weigh the cement sample accurately. Let this be $W$ grams.
