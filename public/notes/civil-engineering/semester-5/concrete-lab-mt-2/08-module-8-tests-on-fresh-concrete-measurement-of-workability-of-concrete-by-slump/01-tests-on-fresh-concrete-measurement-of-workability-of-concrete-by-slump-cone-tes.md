# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2) - Module 8: Tests on Fresh Concrete: Workability

## 1. Introduction to Workability of Concrete

Workability refers to the ease with which concrete can be mixed, placed, consolidated, and finished without segregation. It is a crucial property of fresh concrete as it significantly influences the quality and performance of the hardened concrete.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Workability is not a single property:** It encompasses several aspects like consistency, mobility, cohesiveness, and stability.
*   **Importance of Workability:**
    *   Ensures proper placement and compaction of concrete.
    *   Minimizes segregation and bleeding.
    *   Contributes to achieving the desired strength and durability.
    *   Affects the surface finish of the concrete.
*   **Factors Affecting Workability:**
    *   **Water-cement ratio (w/c):** Higher w/c generally leads to higher workability (but can compromise strength).
    *   **Aggregate characteristics:**
        *   Shape and texture (rounded, smooth aggregates are more workable).
        *   Grading (well-graded aggregates improve workability).
        *   Maximum size of aggregates (larger aggregates can increase workability).
    *   **Admixtures:** Plasticizers, superplasticizers increase workability.
    *   **Cement content and type:** Higher cement content can increase workability.
    *   **Temperature:** Higher temperatures decrease workability due to faster evaporation.
    *   **Mixing time:** Over-mixing can reduce workability.

## 2. Slump Cone Test for Workability

The Slump Cone Test is the most commonly used and simple method for measuring the workability of concrete. It measures the consistency of the concrete by observing the amount of subsidence (slump) when a standard truncated cone mold is removed.

**Learning Outcome:** To understand the principle, procedure, and interpretation of the slump cone test.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Slump:** The vertical subsidence of a freshly molded concrete specimen when the mold is removed.
*   **Principle:** Based on the reduction in volume of concrete due to the removal of the mold, the degree of reduction indicating the workability. A more workable concrete will slump more.
*   **Apparatus:**
    *   Slump cone (truncated cone shape, base diameter 200 mm, top diameter 100 mm, height 300 mm, made of metal).
    *   Tamping rod (16 mm diameter, 600 mm long, rounded at the ends).
    *   Measuring scale.
    *   Base plate (steel or concrete, with a ring to hold the cone).
    *   Scoop or shovel.
    *   Brush or damp cloth.

**Procedure:**

1.  **Prepare the Apparatus:** Place the slump cone on a clean, flat, non-absorbent base plate. Lightly moisten the inside of the cone and the base plate.
2.  **Fill the Cone:** Fill the cone with concrete in three layers, each layer approximately one-third of the volume of the cone.
3.  **Compact Each Layer:** Compact each layer by tamping with the tamping rod 25 times evenly distributed over the surface. The rod should penetrate the previous layer to a depth of about 1 cm.
4.  **Level the Top:** Strike off the excess concrete from the top of the cone with a straight edge to ensure it is level.
5.  **Remove the Cone:** Lift the cone vertically upwards smoothly and steadily.
6.  **Measure the Slump:** Measure the vertical distance from the original top surface of the concrete to the highest point of the slumped concrete. This is the slump value.

**Interpretation of Slump Values:**

| Slump Value (mm) | Description of Workability | Typical Use Cases                                                                    |
| :--------------- | :------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0-25             | Very Low                   | Mass concrete, heavily reinforced sections where compaction is difficult.            |
| 25-50            | Low                        | Pavements, heavily reinforced sections, dry mixes.                                   |
| 50-100           | Medium                     | General purpose concrete, beams, columns, slabs.                                     |
| 100-150          | High                       | Pumped concrete, heavily reinforced sections with closely spaced bars, self-compacting concrete. |
| > 150            | Very High                  | Special applications requiring extreme fluidity, highly flowing concrete.            |

**Types of Slump:**

*   **True Slump:** The concrete subsides as a coherent mass, showing a clear vertical drop.
*   **Shear Slump:** One side of the concrete mass subsides more than the other, indicating loss of cohesion. This suggests poor mix design or inadequate compaction.
*   **Collapse Slump:** The concrete collapses completely, indicating a very wet mix or poor aggregate quality.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The test should be performed immediately after mixing.
*   The base plate must be level and rigid.
*   Compaction must be uniform.
*   Lifting the cone should be done vertically and steadily.
*   The slump cone test is not suitable for very dry or very wet mixes where the concrete collapses.

**Example:**

If a concrete sample shows a slump of 75 mm, it indicates medium workability, suitable for general construction purposes like casting beams or columns. If it shows a shear slump, it means the concrete is likely not well-mixed or the aggregate grading is poor.

## 3. Compacting Factor Test for Workability

The Compacting Factor Test measures the workability of concrete by determining the ratio of the mass of fully compacted concrete to the mass of the same concrete in an uncompacted state. It is considered a more accurate measure for a wider range of workability, especially for medium to low workability concrete.

**Learning Outcome:** To understand the principle, procedure, and interpretation of the compacting factor test.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Compacting Factor:** The ratio of the volume occupied by a concrete when compacted by a standard effort to the volume occupied by the same concrete when compacted fully. It is a dimensionless quantity.
*   **Principle:** Based on the degree of compaction achieved by a standard amount of energy applied to the concrete. More workable concrete requires less energy to compact, resulting in a higher compacting factor.
*   **Apparatus:**
    *   **Compacting Factor Apparatus:** Consists of two cylindrical hoppers (top and bottom) and a vibrating table or a drop-weight mechanism.
        *   **Top Hopper:** Open at the bottom, containing a removable trap door.
        *   **Bottom Hopper:** Conical in shape, with a trap door at the bottom.
        *   **Cylindrical Measure:** Open at the top, closed at the bottom, with a known volume (e.g., 12 liters).
    *   **Trowel:** For filling the measure.
    *   **Tamping Rod:** For levelling.
    *   **Weighing balance:** To weigh the concrete.
    *   **Scoop or shovel:** For transferring concrete.

**Procedure:**

1.  **Prepare Apparatus:** Ensure all parts of the compacting factor apparatus are clean and dry.
2.  **Fill Top Hopper:** Place the concrete sample into the top hopper in two layers, each layer being tamped 15 times with the tamping rod.
3.  **Release Concrete:** Open the trap door of the top hopper to allow concrete to fall into the bottom hopper.
4.  **Release Concrete into Measure:** Immediately after the concrete has ceased to be in motion, open the trap door of the bottom hopper, allowing the concrete to fall into the cylindrical measure.
5.  **Level the Measure:** Strike off the excess concrete from the top of the measure using a trowel or other suitable tool.
6.  **Weigh Uncompacted Concrete:** Weigh the measure filled with uncompacted concrete (Mass A).
7.  **Fully Compact Concrete:** Add more concrete to the same measure until it is full. Compact this concrete by applying a standard effort (e.g., by dropping the weight from a specific height repeatedly or by vibrating).
8.  **Level and Weigh Compacted Concrete:** Strike off the excess concrete and weigh the measure filled with fully compacted concrete (Mass B).
9.  **Calculate Compacting Factor:**
    $ \text{Compacting Factor} = \frac{\text{Mass A}}{\text{Mass B}} $

**Interpretation of Compacting Factor Values:**

| Compacting Factor | Description of Workability |                                                                                   |
| :---------------- | :------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0.36 - 0.44       | Very Low                   | Concrete that requires very high effort for compaction.                           |
| 0.44 - 0.50       | Low                        | Concrete that requires substantial effort for compaction.                       |
| 0.50 - 0.57       | Medium                     | Concrete that requires moderate effort for compaction.                            |
| 0.57 - 0.65       | High                       | Concrete that requires little effort for compaction.                            |
| > 0.65            | Very High                  | Concrete that is practically self-compacting, requires no compaction effort. |

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The compacting factor test is suitable for concrete with a wide range of workability, from very lean to fairly rich mixes.
*   It is more sensitive than the slump test for low workability concrete.
*   The procedure requires more elaborate equipment compared to the slump cone test.
*   The efficiency of compaction is directly related to the compacting factor.

**Example:**

If Mass A (uncompacted) is 15 kg and Mass B (fully compacted) is 25 kg, the compacting factor is $15/25 = 0.60$. This indicates high workability, meaning the concrete can be easily compacted with little effort.

## 4. Comparison Between Slump Cone Test and Compacting Factor Test

| Feature              | Slump Cone Test                                      | Compacting Factor Test                                                                    |
| :------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Principle**        | Measurement of subsidence under self-weight.        | Measurement of the degree of compaction achieved by a standard energy.                    |
| **Suitability**      | Medium to high workability concrete.                | Very low to very high workability concrete. More sensitive for low workability.           |
| **Equipment**        | Simple and portable (cone, rod, scale).              | More complex and less portable (hoppers, measure, vibrating table/weight).               |
| **Accuracy**         | Less accurate for low workability; prone to errors (shear/collapse slump). | Generally considered more accurate and reliable, especially for low workability.          |
| **Ease of Operation**| Simple and quick.                                    | More involved and time-consuming.                                                         |
| **Cost**             | Low.                                                 | Higher due to specialized equipment.                                                      |
| **Parameters Measured** | Slump value (mm).                                    | Compacting factor (dimensionless ratio).                                                  |
| **Sensitivity**      | Less sensitive to variations in low workability mixes. | More sensitive to variations in workability, particularly for lean mixes.                 |

**Which test to use?**

*   **Slump Cone Test:** Preferred for routine site control of medium to high workability concrete where speed and simplicity are key.
*   **Compacting Factor Test:** Preferred in laboratories or for situations where a more precise measure of workability is required, especially for low workability concrete or when the mix design is being developed.

## 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Define workability of concrete. What are the factors that affect it?

**Answer:** Workability refers to the ease with which concrete can be mixed, placed, consolidated, and finished without segregation. Factors affecting workability include water-cement ratio, aggregate characteristics (shape, texture, grading, size), admixtures, cement content, type of cement, temperature, and mixing time.

**Question 2:** Describe the procedure for conducting a Slump Cone Test. What are the three types of slump encountered?

**Answer:**
**Procedure:**
1.  Place the slump cone on a level, non-absorbent base.
2.  Fill the cone with concrete in three layers, tamping each layer 25 times.
3.  Strike off the excess concrete.
4.  Lift the cone vertically.
5.  Measure the slump (vertical subsidence).

**Types of Slump:**
*   **True Slump:** Uniform subsidence.
*   **Shear Slump:** One side subsides more than the other.
*   **Collapse Slump:** Complete collapse of the concrete.

**Question 3:** What is the Compacting Factor, and how is it determined?

**Answer:** The Compacting Factor is the ratio of the mass of fully compacted concrete to the mass of the same concrete in an uncompacted state. It is determined by filling a standard cylindrical measure with concrete under two conditions: first, with a standard amount of compaction energy, and second, with full compaction energy. The ratio of the weights of concrete in these two conditions gives the compacting factor.

**Question 4:** A concrete mix produced a slump of 90 mm. What does this indicate about its workability and potential uses?

**Answer:** A slump of 90 mm indicates medium workability. This type of concrete is suitable for general construction purposes such as casting beams, columns, and slabs, where ease of placement and moderate compaction effort are required.

**Question 5:** Which test is generally more accurate for low workability concrete, and why?

**Answer:** The Compacting Factor Test is generally more accurate for low workability concrete because it measures the degree of compaction achieved by a standard effort. Low workability concrete has less tendency to slump, making the slump test less sensitive to variations in its workability. The compacting factor test, by applying a defined energy, provides a more reliable measure of how well the concrete can be consolidated.

**Question 6:** A batch of concrete has a compacting factor of 0.40. What does this suggest about the concrete?

**Answer:** A compacting factor of 0.40 indicates very low workability. This concrete would require a high degree of effort for compaction and is typically used in mass concrete or heavily reinforced sections where placing and consolidation might be challenging.

## 6. Important Points to Remember (Module Summary)

*   **Workability** is essential for achieving durable and strong concrete.
*   The **Slump Cone Test** is a quick and simple method for measuring workability, primarily for medium to high workability mixes.
*   **Types of slump** (true, shear, collapse) provide insights into the concrete's consistency and potential issues.
*   The **Compacting Factor Test** is a more comprehensive method, suitable for a wider range of workability, and is more accurate for low workability concrete.
*   Both tests are valuable for **quality control** of fresh concrete on site and in the laboratory.
*   Understanding the **factors affecting workability** is crucial for designing concrete mixes that meet project requirements.
*   The choice of test depends on the **workability range** and the required **accuracy**.
