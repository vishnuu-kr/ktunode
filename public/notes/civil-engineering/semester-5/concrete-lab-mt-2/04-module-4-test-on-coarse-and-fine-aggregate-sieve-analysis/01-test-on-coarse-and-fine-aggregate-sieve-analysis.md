# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2) - Module 4: Test on Coarse and Fine Aggregate: Sieve Analysis

## **Topic: Test on Coarse and Fine Aggregate: Sieve Analysis**

### **1. Introduction to Aggregates**

*   **Definition:** Aggregates are inert granular materials that constitute the major portion of concrete. They are typically crushed stone, gravel, sand, or artificial aggregates.
*   **Types of Aggregates:**
    *   **Fine Aggregate:** Usually sand, with particle sizes generally passing through a 4.75 mm sieve.
    *   **Coarse Aggregate:** Usually gravel or crushed stone, with particle sizes retained on a 4.75 mm sieve.
*   **Importance of Aggregates:** The properties of aggregates significantly influence the workability, strength, durability, and economy of concrete.

---

### **2. Sieve Analysis: Purpose and Importance**

*   **Definition:** Sieve analysis is a process of physically separating a mixture of particles into different sizes by passing them through a series of sieves with progressively smaller mesh openings.
*   **Purpose of Sieve Analysis for Aggregates:**
    *   **Determine the Particle Size Distribution (Gradation):** This is the primary goal. It tells us the proportion of aggregate particles within different size ranges.
    *   **Assess Suitability for Concrete Mix Design:** Gradation affects the void content, workability, and overall performance of the concrete.
    *   **Identify Fines:** The amount of material passing the finest sieve (e.g., 75-micron sieve) is crucial for understanding the presence of dust and silt.
    *   **Compare with Standards:** Ensure the aggregate meets the specified gradation requirements for different types of concrete.
    *   **Detect Presence of Oversized or Undersized Particles:** Identify any particles that are too large or too fine for the intended application.

---

### **3. Learning Outcome 1: Understanding the Principles of Sieve Analysis**

*   **Princ:** Sieve analysis is based on the principle of **mechanical separation** of particles by size using a series of sieves with standardized mesh openings.
*   **How it Works:**
    1.  A stack of sieves is arranged in descending order of mesh size (largest opening at the top, smallest at the bottom).
    2.  A known weight of aggregate sample is placed on the topmost sieve.
    3.  The sieve stack is then agitated (shaken) mechanically or manually for a specified period.
    4.  Particles larger than the mesh opening of a sieve are retained on that sieve.
    5.  Particles smaller than the mesh opening pass through to the next sieve in the stack.
    6.  After agitation, the weight of aggregate retained on each sieve is measured.

---

### **4. Learning Outcome 2: Identifying and Understanding Sieves and Sieve Sizes**

*   **Sieve:** A wire mesh or perforated plate with a uniform and known size of opening.
*   **Standard Sieves:** Sieves used in sieve analysis conform to international standards (e.g., ASTM, IS, BS) to ensure consistency and comparability of results.
*   **IS Sieve Series (Indian Standards):** Commonly used sieves include:
    *   **Coarse Aggregate:** 20 mm, 16 mm, 12.5 mm, 10 mm, 4.75 mm
    *   **Fine Aggregate:** 4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, 1.18 mm, 600 micron, 300 micron, 150 micron, 75 micron
    *   **Pan:** A receiver placed at the bottom to collect the finest material.
*   **Sieve Sizes:** These are the **clear aperture** or **nominal opening** of the sieve mesh. The standard sieve sizes are fixed and follow a geometric progression for better gradation analysis.

**Important Note:** The nominal size of a sieve refers to the size of the opening. For example, a 4.75 mm sieve has openings of 4.75 mm x 4.75 mm.

### **5. Learning Outcome 3: Performing Sieve Analysis for Fine and Coarse Aggregates**

#### **5.1. Sieve Analysis of Fine Aggregate**

**Objective:** To determine the particle size distribution of fine aggregate (sand).

**Apparatus:**
*   Set of IS sieves (e.g., 4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, 1.18 mm, 600 µm, 300 µm, 150 µm, 75 µm)
*   Sieve shaker (mechanical)
*   Weighing balance (accurate to 0.1 g)
*   Sample of fine aggregate
*   Brush or blower for cleaning sieves

**Procedure:**
1.  **Obtain Sample:** Take a representative sample of fine aggregate. For laboratory testing, the sample mass is typically specified by the relevant standard (e.g., IS 2386 Part 1). For a typical lab exercise, a sample of around 500 g to 1 kg might be used.
2.  **Prepare Sieves:** Clean the sieves and stack them in descending order of sieve aperture size, with the coarsest sieve at the top and the finest sieve at the bottom, followed by the pan.
3.  **Weigh Sample:** Accurately weigh the clean, dry sample of fine aggregate. Record this as the **Total Initial Weight (W)**.
4.  **Sieving:**
    *   Place the weighed sample on the topmost sieve (4.75 mm).
    *   Cover with the lid and subject the sieve stack to agitation using a sieve shaker for a sufficient time (e.g., 10 minutes). The agitation should be vigorous enough to ensure all particles pass through sieves of their smaller aperture size.
    *   After shaking, carefully remove each sieve from the stack.
5.  **Weighing Retained Material:**
    *   Weigh the material retained on each sieve. Record this as **Weight Retained (Wr)** for each sieve.
    *   Also, weigh the material collected in the pan (passing the finest sieve).
6.  **Calculate Total Weight:** Sum up the weights retained on all sieves and the weight in the pan. This should be very close to the initial total weight of the sample. If there's a significant difference (more than 1-2%), the sieving may not have been complete, or there might have been loss of material.
7.  **Calculations:**
    *   **Cumulative Weight Retained:** For each sieve, calculate the cumulative weight retained by adding the weight retained on that sieve to the weights retained on all sieves above it.
    *   **Percentage Cumulative Weight Retained:** Calculate this by dividing the cumulative weight retained on each sieve by the total initial weight of the sample and multiplying by 100.
    *   **Percentage Passing:** Calculate this by subtracting the percentage cumulative weight retained from 100%.

#### **5.2. Sieve Analysis of Coarse Aggregate**

**Objective:** To determine the particle size distribution of coarse aggregate.

**Apparatus:**
*   Set of IS sieves (e.g., 20 mm, 16 mm, 12.5 mm, 10 mm, 4.75 mm)
*   Sieve shaker (mechanical)
*   Weighing balance (accurate to 1 g for coarser sieves and 0.1 g for finer ones)
*   Sample of coarse aggregate
*   Brush or blower for cleaning sieves

**Procedure:**
1.  **Obtain Sample:** Take a representative sample of coarse aggregate. The sample mass is specified by the relevant standard (e.g., IS 2386 Part 1) and is generally larger than for fine aggregate due to the larger particle sizes (e.g., 4 kg for nominal max size up to 10 mm, 10 kg for nominal max size up to 20 mm, 15 kg for nominal max size up to 40 mm).
2.  **Prepare Sieves:** Clean the sieves and stack them in descending order of sieve aperture size, with the coarsest sieve at the top and the finest sieve at the bottom, followed by the pan.
3.  **Weigh Sample:** Accurately weigh the clean, dry sample of coarse aggregate. Record this as the **Total Initial Weight (W)**.
4.  **Sieving:**
    *   Place the weighed sample on the topmost sieve (e.g., 20 mm).
    *   Cover with the lid and subject the sieve stack to agitation using a sieve shaker for a sufficient time (e.g., 10-15 minutes, or until the weight retained on each sieve is constant).
    *   After shaking, carefully remove each sieve from the stack.
5.  **Weighing Retained Material:**
    *   Weigh the material retained on each sieve. Record this as **Weight Retained (Wr)** for each sieve.
    *   Also, weigh the material collected in the pan (passing the 4.75 mm sieve).
6.  **Calculate Total Weight:** Sum up the weights retained on all sieves and the weight in the pan. This should be very close to the initial total weight of the sample.
7.  **Calculations:**
    *   **Cumulative Weight Retained:** For each sieve, calculate the cumulative weight retained by adding the weight retained on that sieve to the weights retained on all sieves above it.
    *   **Percentage Cumulative Weight Retained:** Calculate this by dividing the cumulative weight retained on each sieve by the total initial weight of the sample and multiplying by 100.
    *   **Percentage Passing:** Calculate this by subtracting the percentage cumulative weight retained from 100%.

*   **Gradation Curve:** The sieve analysis results are often plotted on a **gradation curve**, with sieve size on the x-axis (logarithmic scale) and percentage passing on the y-axis.

    *   **Interpreting the Curve:**
        *   A steep curve indicates a gap-graded aggregate (missing some sizes).
        *   A flatter curve indicates a well-graded aggregate (good distribution of sizes).
        *   The curve shows the range of particle sizes present in the aggregate.

*   **Key Parameters Derived from Sieve Analysis:**
    *   **Fineness Modulus (FM):** This is a single-value indicator of the fineness or coarseness of an aggregate.
        *   **Calculation for Fine Aggregate:** FM is calculated by summing the cumulative percentages of material passing the standard sieves (usually starting from 4.75 mm down to 75 µm) and dividing by 100.
            *   Formula: $FM = (\Sigma \% \text{ cumulative passing standard sieves}) / 100$
            *   Standard sieves for FM: 4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, 1.18 mm, 600 µm, 300 µm, 150 µm, 75 µm.
        *   **Typical Values:**
            *   Fine sand: FM < 2.2
            *   Medium sand: FM = 2.2 - 2.9
            *   Coarse sand: FM > 2.9
        *   **Importance:** Helps in selecting appropriate fine aggregate for a concrete mix to achieve desired workability and strength.
    *   **Nominal Maximum Size of Aggregate (NMSA):** The smallest sieve upon which a specified percentage (usually not more than 10%) of the aggregate is retained. This is particularly important for coarse aggregates.
    *   **Aggregate Passing Sieves:** The percentage of aggregate passing a particular sieve size.

### **7. Learning Outcome 5: Understanding the Significance of Fineness Modulus (FM)**

*   **Definition:** Fineness Modulus (FM) is an empirical coefficient derived from the sieve analysis of aggregate. It provides a single number that represents the average size of the particles in the aggregate.
*   **Calculation (as detailed in Section 6):**
    *   Sum of cumulative percentages of aggregate retained on a standard set of sieves (4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, 1.18 mm, 600 µm, 300 µm, 150 µm, 75 µm).
    *   Divide the sum by 100.
*   **Significance in Concrete Mix Design:**
    *   **Fine Aggregate:** FM is crucial for selecting the right sand.
        *   A lower FM indicates finer sand, which might require more cement paste and can affect workability.
        *   A higher FM indicates coarser sand, which generally leads to better workability and reduced cement paste requirement.
        *   For typical concrete, a FM of fine aggregate between 2.3 and 3.1 is generally preferred.
    *   **Coarse Aggregate:** While FM is calculated for coarse aggregate too, it's less commonly used for mix design compared to NMSA and overall gradation. The overall gradation of coarse aggregate is more important.

What is the primary purpose of conducting a sieve analysis on aggregates?

**Answer:** The primary purpose is to determine the particle size distribution (gradation) of the aggregates, which is crucial for concrete mix design and assessing their suitability for various applications.

**Answer:**
*   **(a) Fine Aggregate:** 4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, 1.18 mm, 600 µm, 300 µm, 150 µm, 75 µm.

### **9. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Representative Sampling:** Always ensure the sample used for sieve analysis is representative of the bulk aggregate.
*   **Dry Condition:** Aggregates should generally be tested in a dry condition unless otherwise specified.
*   **Sieve Purity:** Sieves must be clean and free from any obstruction for accurate results.
*   **Sufficient Agitation:** Ensure adequate shaking time for the sieve stack to allow all particles to pass through their respective sieve openings.
*   **Accuracy of Weighing:** Use a weighing balance with appropriate accuracy for the sample size and sieve sizes.
*   **Total Weight Check:** Always verify that the sum of weights retained on all sieves and the pan equals the initial total weight of the sample.
*   **Fineness Modulus (FM):** It's a measure of fineness/coarseness and is particularly important for fine aggregates in mix design.
*   **Gradation Curve:** A visual representation that helps in understanding the particle size distribution and comparing it with desired specifications.
*   **Standard Specifications:** Always refer to relevant national or international standards (e.g., IS codes) for correct sieve sizes, sample masses, and procedures.
