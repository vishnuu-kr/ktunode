# Transportation Engineering: Module 4 - Introduction to Airport Engineering

## Topic: Docks – Functions and Types (Dry Docks, Wet Docks)

**Note:** This topic, while part of Module 4, focuses on docks, which are typically associated with **Port and Harbour Engineering** rather than Airport Engineering. It's important to understand this distinction. However, we will cover the topic as requested.

---

### 1. Introduction to Docks

**Definition:** A dock is a enclosed or controlled area of water, often artificially constructed, that is used for the loading and unloading of cargo and passengers from ships, as well as for the repair and maintenance of vessels.

**Importance in Transportation:** While not directly an airport component, docks play a crucial role in the broader **intermodal transportation system**. They facilitate the movement of goods and people between land and sea, connecting ports to inland transportation networks. This connection is vital for global trade and logistics.

---

### 2. Functions of Docks

Docks serve several critical functions in maritime transportation and commerce:

*   **Cargo Handling:**
    *   **Loading and Unloading:** The primary function is to provide a stable and accessible platform for transferring cargo between ships and land-based transportation (trucks, trains, barges).
    *   **Storage:** Docks often include facilities for temporary storage of goods before or after transit, such as warehouses and open storage yards.
*   **Vessel Accommodation:**
    *   **Berthing:** Providing safe and secure places for ships to tie up alongside piers or quays.
    *   **Moorings:** Offering facilities for vessels to anchor or tie up in controlled areas.
*   **Vessel Maintenance and Repair:**
    *   **Dry Docks:** Essential for exposing the underwater hull of a ship for inspection, cleaning, painting, and repairs.
    *   **Wet Docks:** Can be used for routine maintenance, minor repairs, and docking of vessels that do not require dewatering.
*   **Passenger Transfer:**
    *   **Ferry Terminals:** Dedicated areas for passengers to embark and disembark from ferries and other passenger vessels.
    *   **Cruise Ship Terminals:** Facilities designed for the efficient handling of large numbers of passengers and their luggage.
*   **Logistics and Supply Chain Integration:**
    *   **Intermodal Transfer Points:** Acting as crucial links where goods can be efficiently transferred between different modes of transport.
    *   **Customs and Clearance:** Often co-located with customs and border control facilities.

### 3. Types of Docks

Docks are broadly categorized based on their operational characteristics, primarily related to water level control and their specific purpose.

#### 3.1. Dry Docks

**Definition:** A dry dock is a narrow basin or structure that can be completely emptied of water to allow for the construction, inspection, cleaning, and repair of the underwater parts of a ship or other watercraft.

**Key Features:**

*   **Enclosed Basin:** A watertight structure, typically built of concrete or masonry.
*   **Gate/Caisson:** A watertight barrier at the entrance that can be opened to allow vessels to enter or leave and closed to seal the dock.
*   **Pumping System:** Powerful pumps are used to remove water from the dock once the gate is closed.
*   **Supporting Keel Blocks and Shoring:** Structures placed on the dock floor to support the vessel when it is out of the water.

**Functions:**

*   **Hull Maintenance:** Cleaning, painting, and repairing the external surfaces below the waterline.
*   **Propeller and Rudder Repairs:** Providing access to these critical components.
*   **Hull Inspection:** Thorough examination for damage, corrosion, or fouling.
*   **Construction:** Building new vessels or large sections of vessels.
*   **Major Overhauls:** Performing extensive repairs and upgrades.

**Types of Dry Docks:**

*   **Graving Docks:**
    *   **Description:** These are the most common type of dry dock. They are excavated basins in the ground, with walls and a gate at one end.
    *   **Operation:** Ships enter the dock at high tide, the gate is closed, and the water is pumped out.
    *   **Example:** Many large shipbuilding and repair yards have graving docks.
*   **Floating Dry Docks:**
    *   **Description:** These are essentially large, pontoon-like structures that can be submerged to a level where a ship can enter. Once the ship is inside, the pontoons are pumped out, raising the dock and the vessel within it.
    *   **Operation:** The dock itself floats. It is submerged to allow the ship to enter, then de-ballasted (water pumped out) to lift the ship.
    *   **Advantages:** Can be moved to different locations, suitable for areas where constructing a graving dock is difficult or impossible.
    *   **Example:** Used in naval shipyards and for specialized repair work.
*   **Marine Railways (Slipways):**
    *   **Description:** While not strictly a "dock" in the sense of an enclosed basin, marine railways are a method of hauling vessels out of the water onto land for repair. They consist of a submerged track and a cradle that runs on the track.
    *   **Operation:** A ship is maneuvered over the cradle, and winches pull the cradle and the ship up an inclined track onto land.
    *   **Example:** Often used for smaller vessels like yachts, tugboats, and smaller commercial fishing boats.

#### 3.2. Wet Docks

**Definition:** A wet dock is a basin or area of water that is kept at a constant water level, usually by means of gates or locks, to accommodate ships for loading, unloading, and storage, without the water level fluctuating with the tides.

**Key Features:**

*   **Controlled Water Level:** Maintained by locks or tidal gates.
*   **Berthing Facilities:** Piers, quays, and jetties for ships to tie up.
*   **No Dewatering:** Vessels remain afloat.

**Functions:**

*   **Cargo Transfer:** Loading and unloading of cargo from vessels.
*   **Passenger Transfer:** Embarking and disembarking passengers.
*   **Storage and Lay-up:** Providing safe mooring for ships when they are not in active service or are awaiting cargo.
*   **Routine Maintenance:** Minor repairs, cleaning, and inspections that do not require the vessel to be lifted out of the water.
*   **Navigational Channel:** Acts as a protected waterway within a port.

**Types of Wet Docks:**

*   **Entrances with Locks (Impounded Wet Docks):**
    *   **Description:** These wet docks are connected to the sea or a river via a lock system. The lock gates maintain a constant water level within the dock, independent of the external tide.
    *   **Operation:** Ships enter or leave the dock by passing through a lock, where the water level is adjusted to match the external water level.
    *   **Example:** The London Docklands and many commercial ports in areas with significant tidal ranges utilize lock-controlled wet docks.
*   **Entrances with Tidal Gates:**
    *   **Description:** These docks have gates that are opened during high tide to allow ships to enter or leave, and closed at low tide to maintain a relatively constant water level within the dock. The water level might still fluctuate slightly with the tides, but significantly less than an open harbor.
    *   **Operation:** Gates are opened only when the external tide is high enough to allow passage.
    *   **Example:** Historically used in some older port designs where full lockage was not implemented.
*   **Floating Docks (in a broader sense):**
    *   **Description:** While floating dry docks are for dewatering, the term "floating dock" can also refer to any structure that floats and provides a platform for vessels, such as pontoon docks or pontoon quays, which are often moored within a wet dock area.
    *   **Example:** Floating pontoons used for leisure boat berthing.

### 4. Site Selection for Airports (Briefly touched upon, as per Module Title)

While the topic is Docks, the module title includes "selection of site for airport." This is a crucial aspect of airport engineering. Key factors include:

*   **Availability of Land:** Sufficient area for runways, terminals, aprons, taxiways, and future expansion.
*   **Topography:** Relatively flat terrain to minimize grading and ensure safe approach/departure paths.
*   **Wind Conditions:** Prevailing winds should be favorable for runway orientation (parallel to prevailing winds).
*   **Proximity to Population Centers:** Accessibility for passengers and cargo, balanced with noise pollution considerations.
*   **Obstructions:** Absence of tall buildings, mountains, or other structures that could interfere with flight paths.
*   **Soil Conditions:** Stable ground to support heavy structures and aircraft loads.
*   **Environmental Impact:** Minimizing noise pollution, air pollution, and impact on natural habitats.
*   **Accessibility for Ground Transportation:** Easy connection to road and rail networks.
*   **Airspace Considerations:** Compatibility with existing air traffic routes and procedures.

**Relationship to Docks (Indirect):** In a multimodal transport context, an airport's location might be chosen to facilitate efficient transfer of goods from air cargo to maritime cargo, or vice versa, requiring good connectivity to port facilities.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of a dry dock?
**Answer:** The primary purpose of a dry dock is to empty it of water to allow for the inspection, cleaning, and repair of the underwater parts of a ship.

**Question 2:** Differentiate between a graving dock and a floating dry dock.
**Answer:** A graving dock is a fixed, excavated basin with a gate, while a floating dry dock is a buoyant structure that can be submerged to allow a ship to enter and then de-ballasted to lift the ship out of the water.

**Question 3:** Explain the role of a wet dock in port operations.
**Answer:** A wet dock maintains a constant water level, allowing ships to berth and remain afloat for loading/unloading of cargo, passenger transfer, and routine maintenance without being affected by tidal fluctuations.

**Question 4:** If a port experiences significant tidal variations, which type of dock would be most beneficial for routine cargo handling to ensure consistent operations?
**Answer:** A wet dock with lock entrances would be most beneficial as it maintains a constant water level, unaffected by external tidal changes, ensuring consistent operations.

**Question 5:** Which of the following is NOT a primary function of a dock?
    a) Cargo loading and unloading
    b) Vessel berthing
    c) Aircraft takeoff and landing
    d) Vessel maintenance
**Answer:** c) Aircraft takeoff and landing. This is a function of an airport, not a dock.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Docks are crucial for maritime transportation and intermodal connectivity.**
*   **Dry docks are for dewatering and hull work; wet docks are for maintaining constant water levels for operational purposes.**
*   **Graving docks are fixed basins, while floating dry docks are mobile structures.**
*   **Lock systems are used in wet docks to control water levels, especially in areas with significant tides.**
*   **The distinction between dry and wet docks lies primarily in whether the vessel is dewatered.**
*   **While this topic is in Airport Engineering module, docks are inherently part of port and harbour engineering.**
