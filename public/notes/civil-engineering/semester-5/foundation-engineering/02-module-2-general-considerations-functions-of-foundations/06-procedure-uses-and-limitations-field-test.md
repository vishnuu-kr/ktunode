# FOUNDATION ENGINEERING - Module 2: General Considerations: Functions of Foundations

## Topic: Procedure, Uses, and Limitations - Field Tests

### Learning Outcomes Covered:
*   Understand the purpose and necessity of field tests in foundation engineering.
*   Describe the procedures for conducting common field tests.
*   Identify the uses and applicability of various field tests.
*   Recognize the limitations of different field tests.
*   Interpret basic results from field tests.

---

### 1. Introduction to Field Tests in Foundation Engineering

*   **Purpose:** Field tests are crucial for determining the in-situ engineering properties of soil and rock that influence foundation performance. They provide a direct assessment of subsurface conditions, complementing laboratory tests.
*   **Necessity:**
    *   **Verification of design assumptions:** Confirming the soil properties assumed during the design phase.
    *   **Evaluation of bearing capacity:** Estimating the ultimate and allowable bearing capacity of the soil.
    *   **Assessment of settlement characteristics:** Predicting how the foundation will settle under load.
    *   **Identification of problematic soil layers:** Detecting weak zones, collapsible soils, or expansive soils.
    *   **Quality control:** Ensuring the constructed foundation meets the specified performance requirements.
    *   **Pre-emptive problem identification:** Detecting issues before they lead to costly failures.

### 2. Common Field Tests and Their Procedures

Here, we'll cover some of the most widely used field tests.

#### 2.1. Standard Penetration Test (SPT)

*   **Concept:** A dynamic test that measures the resistance of the soil to penetration by a standard split-spoon sampler driven by a standardized hammer.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  **Borehole Advancement:** A borehole is drilled to the desired depth, typically to the proposed founding level of the foundation.
    2.  **Sampler Assembly:** A standard split-spoon sampler (typically 50 mm OD, 35 mm ID, 650 mm length) is attached to drill rods.
    3.  **Hammer Drop:** A standard split-spoon sampler (typically 50 mm OD, 35 mm ID, 650 mm length) is attached to drill rods. A standard 63.5 kg (140 lb) hammer is allowed to fall from a height of 760 mm (30 inches).
    4.  **Penetration Measurement:** The hammer strikes the anvil at the top of the drill rods, driving the sampler into the soil. The number of blows required to drive the sampler through three successive 150 mm (6-inch) intervals is recorded.
    5.  **N-Value:** The sum of the blows for the last two 150 mm (6-inch) intervals is the **Standard Penetration Resistance (N-value)**.
    6.  **Sampling:** After driving, the sampler is withdrawn, and the soil sample collected is used for visual classification and laboratory testing.
    7.  **Corrections:** The raw N-value may need corrections for factors like overburden pressure, hammer energy, and borehole diameter to obtain a **Corrected N-value (N60)**.
*   **Uses:**
    *   **Soil classification:** Correlating N-values with soil types (sand, gravel, clay).
    *   **Estimating bearing capacity:** Particularly for granular soils.
    *   **Predicting settlement:** For sands and clays.
    *   **Liquefaction potential assessment:** In earthquake-prone areas.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Disturbance:** The sampling process inherently disturbs the soil.
    *   **Variability:** Results can be highly variable due to operator skill, equipment calibration, and soil stratification.
    *   **Less reliable in stiff clays and gravels:** Penetration can be difficult.
    *   **Indirect measurement:** It's an empirical test, and correlations can be approximate.
    *   **Correction factors:** Applying correct factors is crucial for reliable interpretation.

#### 2.2. Cone Penetration Test (CPT) / Static Cone Penetration Test (SCPT)

*   **Concept:** A static, continuous probing test where a cone-shaped penetrometer is pushed into the soil at a constant rate, measuring tip resistance and sleeve friction.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  **Penetrometer Advancement:** A cone penetrometer (typically 35.7 mm OD, 60° apex angle) is pushed into the soil at a controlled rate (e.g., 20 mm/s).
    2.  **Data Acquisition:** Electronic sensors within the cone measure:
        *   **Tip Resistance (qc):** Resistance at the cone tip.
        *   **Sleeve Friction (fs):** Frictional resistance along the cone's sleeve.
        *   **(Optional) Pore Water Pressure (u):** In piezocone tests (CPTU).
    3.  **Continuous Profiling:** The penetrometer is advanced continuously, providing a detailed profile of soil properties with depth.
*   **Uses:**
    *   **Detailed soil stratification:** Excellent for identifying different soil layers and their boundaries.
    *   **Estimating soil parameters:** Friction angle, undrained shear strength, unit weight, and soil behavior type.
    *   **Predicting bearing capacity and settlement:** Especially for sands and normally consolidated clays.
    *   **Liquefaction assessment:** Highly effective for this purpose.
    *   **Clay characterization:** Can distinguish between normally consolidated, overconsolidated, and sensitive clays.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Cannot obtain soil samples:** Direct visual inspection and lab testing of samples are not possible.
    *   **Difficult in gravelly or cemented soils:** High resistance can damage the equipment.
    *   **Requires specialized equipment:** More sophisticated than SPT.
    *   **Interpretation:** Requires expertise and understanding of the correlations.

#### 2.3. Vane Shear Test (VST)

*   **Concept:** A mechanical test to measure the undrained shear strength (cohesion, cu) of saturated, soft to medium-stiff cohesive soils (clays and silts) in situ.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  **Installation:** A four-bladed vane (typical dimensions: 50 mm diameter, 100 mm height) is inserted into the soil, usually by pushing or carefully drilling.
    2.  **Rotation:** The vane is rotated at a constant slow speed (e.g., 6°/min or 12°/min).
    3.  **Torque Measurement:** The torque required to initiate rotation and the maximum torque required to shear the soil are recorded.
    4.  **Shear Strength Calculation:** The shear strength (cu) is calculated from the maximum torque using established formulas, accounting for vane dimensions.
    5.  **(Optional) Remolded Strength:** The vane can be rotated rapidly to remold the soil and then re-tested to measure the remolded shear strength, allowing for sensitivity determination.
*   **Uses:**
    *   **Measuring undrained shear strength (cu):** Crucial for designing foundations on soft clays.
    *   **Assessing soil sensitivity:** Ratio of undisturbed to remolded shear strength.
    *   **Determining liquidity limit and plasticity index:** Through correlations.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Applicable only to cohesive soils:** Not suitable for granular soils.
    *   **Less reliable in stiff or fissured clays:** The vane may not fully engage the soil.
    *   **Disturbance during installation:** Can occur if drilling is not done carefully.
    *   **Sensitivity of results to rotation rate:** Must be controlled.

#### 2.4. Plate Load Test (PLT)

*   **Concept:** A direct test to measure the bearing capacity and settlement of soil under a loaded plate. It simulates the behavior of a foundation directly.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  **Excavation/Borehole:** A test pit is excavated to the proposed founding level, or a borehole is drilled.
    2.  **Plate Placement:** A rigid steel plate (typically 300 mm x 300 mm or 750 mm x 750 mm, with diameter for circular plates) is placed on the prepared soil surface.
    3.  **Loading:** Load is applied to the plate in increments using a hydraulic jack or kentledge (heavy weights).
    4.  **Settlement Measurement:** Settlements are measured using dial gauges or settlement platforms connected to reference beams or stakes beyond the zone of influence of the test.
    5.  **Load-Settlement Curve:** A load-settlement curve is plotted. The ultimate bearing capacity is determined from this curve (e.g., at the point of shear failure or significant increase in settlement rate). Allowable bearing capacity is then determined by applying a factor of safety.
*   **Uses:**
    *   **Direct determination of bearing capacity:** Especially for shallow foundations.
    *   **Measurement of settlement:** Provides direct settlement data.
    *   **Verification of laboratory/empirical methods:** Can validate design assumptions.
    *   **Used for critical structures or complex soil conditions.**
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Scale effect:** Results obtained from a small plate may not directly represent the behavior of a larger foundation. The zone of influence of the plate is limited.
    *   **Expensive and time-consuming:** Requires significant excavation and loading equipment.
    *   **Affected by groundwater table:** Test conditions must be representative of the foundation's conditions.
    *   **Deep soils are not tested:** Only the shallowest layer directly beneath the plate influences the results.

#### 2.5. Pressuremeter Test (PMT)

*   **Concept:** An in-situ test that measures the stress-strain behavior of soil or rock by expanding a cylindrical probe laterally within a borehole.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  **Borehole Preparation:** A borehole is drilled to the desired depth. The borehole should be as clean and undisturbed as possible.
    2.  **Probe Insertion:** A pressuremeter probe, consisting of a cylindrical section with expandable membranes and a control unit, is lowered into the borehole.
    3.  **Expansion:** The probe is inflated by applying increasing gas or liquid pressure.
    4.  **Volume Change Measurement:** The corresponding volume changes of the probe (and thus the borehole) are measured.
    5.  **Pressure-Volume Curve:** A pressure-volume curve is generated, which can be converted to a stress-strain curve for the soil.
*   **Uses:**
    *   **Comprehensive stress-strain data:** Provides both strength and deformation characteristics.
    *   **Determination of limit pressure and limit strain:** Related to bearing capacity and settlement.
    *   **Evaluation of soil modulus:** Can directly determine deformation modulus.
    *   **Applicable to a wide range of soils and rocks:** Including stiff clays and weaker rocks.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Borehole quality is critical:** Undercutting or smearing of the borehole walls can affect results.
    *   **Requires specialized equipment and expertise:** Not as common as SPT or CPT in many regions.
    *   **Can be time-consuming:** Careful calibration and execution are needed.

#### 2.6. Dynamic Cone Penetration Test (DCPT)

*   **Concept:** A simpler, lighter version of the SPT, often used for shallower investigations or in conjunction with other methods. It involves driving a cone with a hammer.
*   **Procedure:** Similar to SPT but uses a lighter hammer (e.g., 8 kg) falling from a shorter height (e.g., 560 mm) and drives a cone-tipped rod directly into the ground. The number of blows per unit depth (e.g., per 100 mm) is recorded.
*   **Uses:**
    *   **Rapid assessment of soil strength:** Particularly in granular soils for pavement subgrades.
    *   **Correlation with SPT N-values:** Can be used to estimate N-values.
    *   **Cost-effective for shallow investigations.**
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Less accurate than SPT or CPT:** Due to less standardization and the disturbance caused by driving a solid rod.
    *   **Limited depth penetration:** Compared to SPT.
    *   **Not suitable for stiff clays or cohesive soils:** Where the cone may not penetrate effectively.

### 3. Uses and Limitations of Field Tests: A Summary

| Test                       | Primary Uses                                                                    | Key Limitations                                                                                                      | Best Suited For                                                                      |
| :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SPT**                    | Soil classification, bearing capacity, settlement estimation, liquefaction.     | Disturbance, variability, less reliable in stiff clays/gravels, empirical.                                           | Sands, silts, soft to medium clays.                                                  |
| **CPT/SCPT**               | Detailed soil profiling, bearing capacity, settlement, liquefaction, clay char. | No samples, difficult in gravel/cemented soils, specialized equipment.                                               | Sands, silts, clays (all types).                                                     |
| **VST**                    | Undrained shear strength (cu), soil sensitivity.                                | Cohesive soils only, less reliable in stiff/fissured clays, installation disturbance.                                | Soft to medium clays.                                                                |
| **PLT**                    | Direct bearing capacity & settlement, validation.                               | Scale effect, expensive, time-consuming, shallow soil influence.                                                     | Shallow foundations, critical structures, verifying design.                          |
| **PMT**                    | Stress-strain behavior, modulus, limit pressure.                                | Borehole quality critical, specialized equipment, time-consuming.                                                    | Wide range of soils and weak rocks.                                                  |
| **DCPT**                   | Rapid strength assessment, pavement subgrades.                                  | Less accurate than SPT/CPT, disturbance, limited depth, not for stiff clays.                                       | Granular soils, shallow investigations.                                              |

### 4. Interpretation of Field Test Results

*   **Correlations:** Many field test results are interpreted using empirical correlations derived from extensive research and case studies. These correlations relate test parameters to soil properties (e.g., N-value to friction angle, qc to cu).
*   **Soil Behavior Type (CPT):** CPT data can be used to classify soil behavior types (e.g., sensitive fine-grained, organic soil, clay, silty clay, sandy silt, sand).
*   **Bearing Capacity Calculation:**
    *   **SPT:** Using correlations between N-value and allowable bearing pressure (e.g., Terzaghi's or Meyerhof's methods).
    *   **CPT:** Using correlations between qc and ultimate bearing capacity, or by estimating soil properties from qc and fs.
    *   **PLT:** Directly from the load-settlement curve by applying a factor of safety to the ultimate bearing capacity.
*   **Settlement Prediction:**
    *   **SPT:** Using correlations between N-value and settlement (e.g., Schmertmann method for sands).
    *   **CPT:** Using correlations between qc or other CPT parameters and settlement.
    *   **PMT:** Directly calculating settlement from the soil modulus obtained.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
Which field test is most commonly used for determining the undrained shear strength of soft clays in situ?
(a) Standard Penetration Test (SPT)
(b) Cone Penetration Test (CPT)
(c) Vane Shear Test (VST)
(d) Plate Load Test (PLT)

**Answer:** (c) Vane Shear Test (VST)

**Question 2:**
What is the primary limitation of the Plate Load Test (PLT) regarding the extrapolation of results to actual foundations?
(a) It cannot determine bearing capacity.
(b) It is too expensive to conduct.
(c) There is a scale effect between the test plate and the foundation.
(d) It is not suitable for cohesive soils.

**Answer:** (c) There is a scale effect between the test plate and the foundation.

**Question 3:**
The Standard Penetration Test (SPT) records the number of blows required to drive a split-spoon sampler a certain distance. What is the standard N-value definition?
(a) Blows for the first 150 mm.
(b) Blows for the last 300 mm of penetration.
(c) Blows for the middle 300 mm of penetration.
(d) Blows for the last 150 mm of penetration.

**Answer:** (b) Blows for the last 300 mm of penetration (sum of blows for the last two 150 mm intervals).

**Question 4:**
Which test provides a continuous and detailed profile of soil stratification and properties?
(a) SPT
(b) VST
(c) PLT
(d) CPT

**Answer:** (d) CPT

**Question 5:**
Briefly explain the primary advantage of the Cone Penetration Test (CPT) over the Standard Penetration Test (SPT).

**Answer:** The CPT provides a continuous, detailed profile of soil stratification and properties with depth, allowing for more precise identification of soil layers and their variations. It is also a static test, generally less affected by operator variability compared to the dynamic SPT.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Field tests are complementary to laboratory tests.** They provide in-situ properties that may differ from disturbed lab samples.
*   **The choice of field test depends on:**
    *   The type of soil being investigated.
    *   The type and size of the foundation.
    *   The required information (strength, settlement, stratification).
    *   Cost and time constraints.
    *   Site accessibility.
*   **Always consider the limitations of each test.** No single test is perfect for all situations.
*   **Proper execution and calibration are critical** for reliable field test results.
*   **Interpretation requires expertise** and an understanding of soil mechanics principles and empirical correlations.
*   **The influence of the groundwater table** must be carefully considered when conducting and interpreting field tests.
