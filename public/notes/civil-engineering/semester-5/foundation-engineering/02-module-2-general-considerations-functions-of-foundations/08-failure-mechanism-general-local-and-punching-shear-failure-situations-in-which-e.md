# FOUNDATION ENGINEERING: Module 2 - General Considerations: Functions of Foundations

## Topic: Failure Mechanisms in Shallow Foundations

### 1. Introduction to Foundation Failure Mechanisms

Foundations are designed to safely transfer the loads from a structure to the underlying soil. However, under certain conditions, the soil beneath a foundation can fail, leading to structural settlement or collapse. Understanding the different failure mechanisms is crucial for selecting appropriate foundation types and ensuring the stability of the structure.

### 2. Types of Failure Mechanisms

Shallow foundations, particularly spread footings and rafts, are susceptible to three primary modes of soil failure:

*   **General Shear Failure**
*   **Local Shear Failure**
*   **Punching Shear Failure**

The occurrence of each failure mode depends on factors such as the soil type, the foundation geometry, and the magnitude of the applied load.

---

### 3. General Shear Failure

#### 3.1. Definition

General shear failure is characterized by a progressive failure that occurs across the entire width of the foundation. It involves the development of a distinct slip surface extending from the edges of the foundation to the ground surface. The failure is sudden and accompanied by a significant settlement of the foundation.

#### 3.2. Characteristics

*   **Failure Surface:** A well-defined, continuous slip surface forms, extending from the foundation edges to the ground surface.
*   **Load-Settlement Curve:** A clear peak load is observed, followed by a sudden drop in load capacity.
*   **Deformation:** Significant tilting and settlement of the foundation.
*   **Soil Movement:** Extensive soil mass underneath the foundation is involved in the failure.

#### 3.3. Situations in Which General Shear Failure Can Be Expected

General shear failure is typically observed in **dense granular soils** (e.g., dense sand, gravelly sand) and **stiff cohesive soils** (e.g., stiff clay). This is because these soils have a high shear strength and can mobilize their strength more effectively before failure.

**Key Factors Favoring General Shear Failure:**

*   **High Soil Density/Stiffness:** Dense sands and stiff clays have high internal friction angles ($\phi$) and/or undrained shear strength ($c_u$).
*   **Shallow Foundations:** Generally more prevalent in shallow foundations where the depth-to-width ratio ($D_f/B$) is small.
*   **Narrow Foundations:** A smaller width ($B$) tends to promote this mode of failure.
*   **Low Confining Pressure:** Less confinement at depth can facilitate the propagation of the failure surface.

**Example:** A relatively narrow footing resting on a deep deposit of dense sand. As the load increases, the sand at the edges of the footing will start to shear, and the failure surface will propagate upwards to the ground surface.

---

### 4. Local Shear Failure

#### 4.1. Definition

Local shear failure occurs when the soil directly beneath the foundation yields, but the failure does not extend to the ground surface. The slip surfaces are confined to the soil immediately below the foundation. The failure is not as sudden as general shear, and the peak load is not as distinct.

#### 4.2. Characteristics

*   **Failure Surface:** Slip surfaces are more bulging and localized, not extending to the ground surface.
*   **Load-Settlement Curve:** A rounded peak load is observed, or the load capacity increases gradually with increasing settlement.
*   **Deformation:** Moderate settlement and bulging of soil at the foundation edges.
*   **Soil Movement:** Primarily confined to the soil in the immediate vicinity of the foundation.

#### 4.3. Situations in Which Local Shear Failure Can Be Expected

Local shear failure is more common in **soils of medium density/stiffness**. This includes:

*   **Medium dense sands:** The soil has enough strength to resist full shear but is not dense enough for a clear slip surface to form.
*   **Soft to medium clays:** Clays with intermediate undrained shear strength.

**Key Factors Favoring Local Shear Failure:**

*   **Medium Soil Density/Stiffness:** Soils with moderate internal friction angles or undrained shear strengths.
*   **Moderate Depth-to-Width Ratio:** As $D_f/B$ increases, the confinement increases, which can lead to local shear.
*   **Wider Foundations:** A larger width can also contribute to more localized failure.
*   **Moderate Confining Pressure:** Some confinement is present but not enough to prevent yielding.

**Example:** A footing on a deposit of medium dense sand. As the load increases, the soil directly under the footing compresses and bulges outwards, causing significant settlement, but the failure zone doesn't reach the ground surface.

---

### 5. Punching Shear Failure

#### 5.1. Definition

Punching shear failure occurs when the foundation essentially "punches" through the soil layer, with minimal shear deformation of the surrounding soil. This is a localized failure where the soil beneath the foundation fails in shear along a relatively narrow zone, and the soil on either side of the foundation remains largely undisturbed.

#### 5.2. Characteristics

*   **Failure Surface:** The failure occurs along a cylindrical or nearly cylindrical surface that surrounds the foundation.
*   **Load-Settlement Curve:** The load-settlement curve continues to rise with increasing settlement, with no distinct peak load. The ultimate bearing capacity is theoretically infinite in this mode, but practical failure is defined by an acceptable settlement.
*   **Deformation:** Significant vertical settlement of the foundation with little to no bulging of soil at the edges.
*   **Soil Movement:** The soil "punches" down, and the surrounding soil is largely unaffected.

#### 5.3. Situations in Which Punching Shear Failure Can Be Expected

Punching shear failure is most commonly associated with **foundations on soft soils with low shear strength**, especially when the soil layer is relatively shallow or underlain by a stronger layer at a shallow depth. It is particularly prevalent in:

*   **Soft clays:** Clays with very low undrained shear strength ($c_u$).
*   **Loose sands:** Sands with very low friction angles ($\phi$).
*   **Foundations with high loads and small footprint:** Such as the columns of a bridge or tall structures.
*   **Foundations resting on a thin crust of strong soil overlying a weak layer:** The strong crust can shear off, allowing the foundation to punch through.

**Key Factors Favoring Punching Shear Failure:**

*   **Low Soil Shear Strength:** Very low $\phi$ or $c_u$.
*   **Shallow Depth-to-Width Ratio ($D_f/B$):** When the foundation is very shallow relative to its width.
*   **Presence of a Weak Layer Beneath a Stronger Crust:** The foundation punches through the crust.
*   **High Concentrated Loads:** Applied over a small area.

**Example:** A column footing resting on a thick layer of very soft clay. As the load is applied, the clay directly beneath the footing yields, and the footing moves downwards, shearing the clay along a cylindrical surface. The surrounding soft clay offers little resistance to this downward movement. Another example is a foundation on a shallow layer of sand that is underlain by a very soft clay layer; the sand layer might shear off, and the footing punches into the soft clay.

### 6. Summary of Failure Modes and Soil Conditions

| Failure Mode              | Soil Type / Condition                                  | Key Characteristics                                                                | Load-Settlement Curve     |
| :------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------ |
| **General Shear Failure** | Dense granular soils, Stiff cohesive soils             | Well-defined slip surfaces to ground surface; significant tilting & settlement      | Clear peak load, sudden drop |
| **Local Shear Failure**   | Medium dense sands, Soft to medium clays               | Localized bulging slip surfaces; moderate settlement                              | Rounded peak load         |
| **Punching Shear Failure**| Soft clays, Loose sands, Thin crust over weak layer | Cylindrical shear surface around foundation; significant vertical settlement only | No distinct peak load       |

---

### 7. Factors Influencing the Type of Failure

*   **Soil Properties:** Shear strength ($\phi$, $c_u$), density, stiffness.
*   **Foundation Geometry:** Width ($B$), length ($L$), shape.
*   **Depth of Foundation ($D_f$):** The $D_f/B$ ratio is critical. Higher ratios generally lead to more confinement and favor general or local shear.
*   **Load Characteristics:** Magnitude and type of load (static vs. dynamic).
*   **Soil Stratification:** Presence of different soil layers with varying strengths.

### 8. Practice Questions

1.  **Question:** A footing is to be placed on a deposit of dense sand. Which type of shear failure mechanism is most likely to occur if the footing is relatively wide?
    **Answer:** While dense sand generally favors general shear, a very wide footing on dense sand might exhibit characteristics of local shear due to increased confinement and potential for localized yielding before a full slip surface develops. However, **general shear failure** is the primary concern in dense sands.

2.  **Question:** Describe the key difference between local shear failure and punching shear failure in terms of the soil movement and the load-settlement behavior.
    **Answer:** In **local shear failure**, the soil beneath the foundation yields and bulges laterally, but the failure zone does not reach the ground surface. The load-settlement curve shows a rounded peak. In **punching shear failure**, the foundation essentially "punches" through the soil layer, with failure occurring along a cylindrical surface surrounding the foundation and minimal lateral soil movement. The load-settlement curve continues to rise with settlement without a distinct peak.

3.  **Question:** Under what soil conditions would you expect punching shear failure to be a significant concern for a shallow foundation? Provide an example.
    **Answer:** Punching shear failure is a concern in soils with very low shear strength, such as **soft clays** or **loose sands**. It is also critical when a foundation rests on a thin layer of stronger soil that is immediately underlain by a much weaker layer.
    **Example:** A footing supporting a slender column in a building, placed on a thick layer of highly compressible, soft organic clay. The low shear strength of the clay would not resist the downward movement, leading to punching failure.

4.  **Question:** Differentiate between general shear failure and local shear failure by their characteristic load-settlement curves.
    **Answer:** The load-settlement curve for **general shear failure** exhibits a distinct peak load, after which the load capacity drops significantly with continued settlement. The curve for **local shear failure** is more rounded, showing a gradual increase in load with settlement, with a less defined peak or no distinct peak at all.

---

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   **Soil Properties are Key:** The shear strength characteristics of the soil are the primary determinant of the failure mode.
*   **Geometry Matters:** The width-to-depth ratio ($B/D_f$) and the aspect ratio of the foundation play significant roles.
*   **General Shear = Clean Break:** Think of a clean break in a strong material.
*   **Local Shear = Yielding & Bulging:** Think of a material that deforms before failing.
*   **Punching Shear = Piercing:** Think of a sharp object piercing a soft material.
*   **Design Implications:** The chosen foundation design must account for the potential failure mode to ensure adequate safety factors and limit settlements to acceptable levels. For punching shear, the design often focuses on limiting settlement rather than reaching a peak load.
