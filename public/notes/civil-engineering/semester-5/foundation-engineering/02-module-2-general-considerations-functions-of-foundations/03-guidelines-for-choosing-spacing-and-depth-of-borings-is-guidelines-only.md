# FOUNDATION ENGINEERING: Module 2 - General Considerations

## Topic: Guidelines for Choosing Spacing and Depth of Borings (I.S. Guidelines Only)

---

### 1. Introduction to Subsurface Investigations

**Objective:** To understand the critical importance of subsurface investigations for safe and economical foundation design.

*   **What is Subsurface Investigation?**
    *   A systematic process to determine the soil and rock conditions at a proposed construction site.
    *   It involves collecting information about the subsurface layers, their properties, and their spatial distribution.

*   **Why are Subsurface Investigations Crucial?**
    *   **Foundation Design:** To select the appropriate type of foundation (e.g., shallow vs. deep) and determine its capacity.
    *   **Settlement Analysis:** To predict potential settlements of the structure.
    *   **Stability Analysis:** To assess the bearing capacity and slope stability of the ground.
    *   **Excavation Considerations:** To understand groundwater levels, soil stability during excavation, and dewatering requirements.
    *   **Economic Design:** To avoid over-designing foundations due to insufficient or inaccurate data, leading to cost savings.
    *   **Safety:** To prevent catastrophic failures of structures due to unforeseen ground conditions.

---

### 2. Importance of Borings in Subsurface Investigation

**Objective:** To understand how borings provide essential data for foundation engineering.

*   **Borings:**
    *   The most common method for obtaining subsurface soil and rock samples.
    *   Involves drilling holes into the ground to a specified depth.
    *   Samples are collected at various depths for laboratory testing and in-situ testing is also performed.

*   **Information Obtained from Borings:**
    *   **Stratification:** Identification of different soil/rock layers and their thicknesses.
    *   **Soil/Rock Properties:** Physical and mechanical properties of each layer (e.g., grain size distribution, Atterberg limits, shear strength, compressibility, permeability).
    *   **Groundwater Table:** Location and fluctuation of the groundwater level.
    *   **Presence of Obstructions:** Boulders, old foundations, utility lines, etc.
    *   **Unusual Soil Conditions:** Soft clay layers, organic soils, collapsible soils, expansive soils.

### 3. Indian Standards (I.S.) Guidelines for Borings

**Objective:** To learn the specific recommendations from Indian Standards for the spacing and depth of borings.

**Key Indian Standards:**

*   **IS 1892:1979 (Reaffirmed 2019) - Code of Practice for Subsurface Investigations for Foundations** (This is the primary standard for this topic).
*   **IS 2721:1977 (Reaffirmed 2013) - Code of Practice for Geophysical Prospecting** (While not directly about borings, it complements subsurface investigation).
*   **IS 6926:1973 (Reaffirmed 2013) - Criteria for design and construction of shallow foundations** (Provides context on what information is needed from investigations).

#### 3.1. Guidelines for Spacing of Borings

**Objective:** To understand the factors influencing the decision on how far apart borings should be drilled.

*   **General Principle:** The spacing of borings should be such that the ground conditions between borings can be reliably inferred. This depends heavily on the variability of the subsurface strata.

*   **Factors Influencing Spacing:**
    *   **Uniformity of Strata:**
        *   If the soil layers are uniform and predictable, wider spacing is acceptable.
        *   If the soil layers are highly variable (e.g., presence of lenses, pockets of different soils, irregular layering), closer spacing is required.
    *   **Type of Structure:**
        *   **Critical Structures:** Buildings with high loads, tall buildings, bridges, dams, etc., require more detailed and closer-spaced borings.
        *   **Less Critical Structures:** Warehouses, single-story buildings with light loads, may tolerate wider spacing.
    *   **Soil/Rock Type:**
        *   **Homogeneous Soils:** Wider spacing.
        *   **Stratified Soils:** Moderate spacing.
        *   **Complex Geological Formations:** Closer spacing.
        *   **Presence of Anomalies:** Karstic limestone, filled ground, old quarry sites, etc., necessitate very close spacing.
    *   **Topography:**
        *   Significant variations in ground level might indicate underlying geological changes, requiring closer spacing.
    *   **Local Experience:**
        *   Knowledge of the geological history and past experience in the area can guide spacing decisions.
    *   **Economic Considerations:**
        *   The cost of borings versus the potential cost of foundation failure or over-design.

*   **I.S. 1892:1979 Recommendations (General Guidelines):**
    *   **Grid Pattern:** Borings are often arranged in a grid pattern for systematic coverage.
    *   **Buildings and Structures:**
        *   **General:** Typically, borings are taken at the corners of the building or structure.
        *   **For buildings of significant size or where foundation loads are heavy:** Borings may be placed at intermediate points, such as the midpoint of each side or at regular intervals within the building footprint.
        *   **Spacing Example (General Guidance, not rigid rules):**
            *   Buildings of moderate size: 10-20 meters apart.
            *   Large or critical structures: 5-15 meters apart.
    *   **Bridges:**
        *   **Abutments:** At least one boring at each abutment.
        *   **Piers:** One boring at each pier location.
        *   **Spacing:** Additional borings may be required between abutments and piers depending on the span and foundation type.
    *   **Embankments and Dams:**
        *   Borings are typically located along the centerline of the embankment and at regular intervals transverse to the centerline, especially in areas of potential weakness.
    *   **Linear Structures (Pipelines, Roads):**
        *   Borings may be spaced at regular intervals (e.g., every 50-100 meters) or at locations where geological changes are expected (e.g., changes in topography, crossings of geological features).
    *   **Important Note:** These are general guidelines. The specific spacing should be determined by a qualified geotechnical engineer based on a thorough assessment of the site conditions and structural requirements.

#### 3.2. Guidelines for Depth of Borings

**Objective:** To determine how deep borings should be drilled to obtain relevant information for foundation design.

*   **General Principle:** Borings should extend to a depth where the soil or rock strata are no longer likely to significantly influence the foundation's performance (i.e., they are "out of the influence zone" of the foundation).

*   **Factors Influencing Depth:**
    *   **Type of Foundation:**
        *   **Shallow Foundations:** Borings need to reach a depth where the underlying strata can adequately support the foundation load without excessive settlement or shear failure. This often means reaching a firm stratum or a depth where the stress increase in the soil due to the foundation load becomes negligible.
        *   **Deep Foundations (Piles, Piers):** Borings must extend to a depth where the pile can develop sufficient end bearing capacity and/or skin friction, or through any weak layers that might compromise the pile's performance.
    *   **Magnitude of Foundation Load:**
        *   Heavier loads induce greater stress in the soil, extending the influence zone deeper. Therefore, deeper borings are required for heavier loads.
    *   **Soil Compressibility:**
        *   Highly compressible soils (e.g., soft clays, organic soils) require borings to be extended deeper to assess the total settlement potential.
    *   **Groundwater Table:**
        *   The depth of the groundwater table is a critical factor and borings should extend below it to assess its influence on bearing capacity and settlement.
    *   **Geological Conditions:**
        *   Presence of bedrock, a firm stratum, or layers with significantly different properties will influence the required depth.
    *   **Potential for Scour:**
        *   For bridges and structures in water bodies, borings must extend below the maximum anticipated scour depth.
    *   **Settlement Criteria:**
        *   The allowable settlement for the structure dictates how deep the influence zone is considered.

*   **I.S. 1892:1979 Recommendations (Methods for Determining Depth):**

    *   **Method 1: Depth of Firm Stratum:**
        *   Borings should extend to a depth where a firm stratum (e.g., dense sand, stiff clay, bedrock) capable of supporting the foundation loads is encountered.

    *   **Method 2: Depth of Negligible Stress Increase:**
        *   The depth at which the increase in vertical stress due to the foundation load becomes negligible (typically 10-20% of the applied load or a fixed value like 20 kPa, depending on the soil type and sensitivity of the structure).
        *   The stress distribution in the soil due to a foundation can be estimated using various methods (e.g., Boussinesq's theory for point loads, Westergaard's theory, approximate methods for uniformly loaded areas).
        *   **Rule of Thumb:** Extend borings to a depth of 1.5 to 2 times the width of the footing (B) for shallow foundations if no firm stratum is encountered earlier. For rafts, this may extend to 3B or more.

    *   **Method 3: Depth of Significant Settlement Reduction:**
        *   Continue boring until further increase in depth does not significantly alter the computed settlement. This is particularly important for compressible soils.

    *   **Method 4: Depth to Accommodate Pile Capacity:**
        *   For pile foundations, borings must extend to a depth where the pile can develop the required end bearing and/or skin friction capacity. This often means extending to bedrock or a stratum of sufficient strength.

    *   **Method 5: Depth below Ground Improvement Zone:**
        *   If ground improvement techniques are planned, the borings should extend below the zone of improvement.

    *   **Method 6: Depth to Prevent Scour:**
        *   For structures in proximity to flowing water, borings must extend below the predicted maximum scour depth.

    *   **Specific Depth Recommendations (General Guidance, not rigid rules):**
        *   **Lightly Loaded Structures (e.g., single-story buildings):** Borings may need to extend to a depth of about 3-6 meters or to a firm stratum.
        *   **Moderately Loaded Structures (e.g., multi-story buildings):** Borings typically extend to 10-20 meters or to a depth where the stress influence is negligible, or to a firm stratum.
        *   **Heavily Loaded Structures (e.g., tall buildings, industrial structures):** Borings may need to extend to 20-30 meters or more, depending on the soil profile and load intensity.
        *   **For pile foundations:** Borings should typically penetrate at least 3 times the expected pile diameter below the pile toe, or to bedrock if encountered.

    *   **Crucial Point:** The final depth of borings should be determined by a qualified geotechnical engineer, considering all relevant factors, including the findings from initial borings and the structure's specific requirements.

### 4. Site Specific Considerations and I.S. Guidelines

**Objective:** To understand how site-specific conditions influence the application of I.S. guidelines.

*   **Geological Maps and Reports:**
    *   Consulting existing geological maps and reports for the area can provide initial insights into expected soil conditions, which can guide the planning of borings.
*   **Local Building Codes and Practices:**
    *   Local regulations or standard practices might stipulate specific minimum depths or spacing for borings for certain types of structures.
*   **Type of Soil/Rock Deposit:**
    *   **Alluvial Deposits:** Often exhibit considerable variation in soil types and properties over short distances, requiring closer spacing.
    *   **Hard Rocks:** Fewer borings might be needed, but the depth should ensure penetration into sound rock, avoiding weathered zones.
    *   **Volcanic Soils/Laterites:** Can have variable strength and consistency, influencing spacing and depth.
*   **Variations in Proposed Foundation Level:**
    *   If the foundation level varies significantly across the site (e.g., due to sloping terrain), this might require adjustments in boring locations and depths.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Objective:** To test understanding of the I.S. guidelines for spacing and depth of borings.

**Question 1:**
According to IS 1892:1979, what are the primary factors that influence the **spacing** of borings at a construction site?

**Answer 1:**
The primary factors influencing the spacing of borings include:
*   Uniformity of strata
*   Type of structure
*   Soil/rock type
*   Topography
*   Local experience
*   Economic considerations

**Question 2:**
For a multi-story commercial building, what general spacing of borings might be considered according to IS guidelines, and why?

**Answer 2:**
For a multi-story commercial building (considered moderately to heavily loaded and requiring careful design), borings might be spaced at regular intervals within the building footprint, perhaps every 10-20 meters, or at the corners and intermediate points. This is because such structures impose significant loads, and variations in soil conditions, even over relatively short distances, can significantly impact foundation performance and lead to differential settlements.

**Question 3:**
Explain two methods recommended by IS 1892:1979 for determining the **depth** of borings for shallow foundations.

**Answer 3:**
Two methods recommended by IS 1892:1979 for determining the depth of borings for shallow foundations are:
1.  **Depth of Firm Stratum:** Borings should extend to a depth where a firm stratum (capable of supporting foundation loads without excessive settlement or shear failure) is encountered.
2.  **Depth of Negligible Stress Increase:** Borings should extend to a depth where the increase in vertical stress due to the foundation load becomes negligible (e.g., 10-20% of the applied load or a fixed low value like 20 kPa).

**Question 4:**
For a proposed bridge pier, what is the minimum number and typical location of borings recommended by IS guidelines?

**Answer 4:**
For a bridge pier, IS guidelines recommend at least one boring at the pier location.

**Question 5:**
If you are investigating a site known for highly variable alluvial soil deposits for a critical structure like a hospital, would you opt for wider or closer spacing of borings? Justify your answer.

**Answer 5:**
You would opt for **closer spacing** of borings. This is because highly variable alluvial deposits, especially for a critical structure like a hospital (where settlement control is paramount), can have significant changes in soil properties over short distances. Closer spacing ensures a more reliable understanding of the subsurface conditions, minimizing the risk of foundation failure or excessive differential settlements due to unforeseen variations.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **IS 1892:1979 is the primary code for subsurface investigations in India.**
*   **Spacing and depth of borings are NOT fixed rules but guidelines.** They must be determined by a qualified geotechnical engineer based on site-specific conditions and structural requirements.
*   **Closer spacing is generally required for:** critical structures, variable soil conditions, complex geological formations, and heavier loads.
*   **Deeper borings are generally required for:** heavier loads, compressible soils, deep foundations (piles), groundwater influence, and potential scour.
*   **Understanding the "influence zone" of the foundation is key to determining boring depth.**
*   **Always consult the latest revisions of Indian Standards.**
