# ANTENNA THEORY AND WAVE PROPAGATION

## Module 1: Antenna Theory: Radiation Mechanism from an Antenna

### Topic: Biconical Antenna

---

### 1. Introduction to Biconical Antennas

The biconical antenna is a fundamental and conceptually important antenna structure. It can be considered as an extension of the dipole antenna into three dimensions. Understanding the biconical antenna provides insights into the radiation process and is a stepping stone to more complex antenna designs.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Extension of Dipole:** A biconical antenna is essentially two conical conductors, apex to apex, forming a symmetrical structure. It can be visualized as a thin dipole antenna whose arms are expanded into conical shapes.
*   **Symmetrical Structure:** The symmetrical nature of the biconical antenna simplifies its analysis and leads to desirable impedance characteristics.
*   **Radiation Mechanism:** The radiation occurs due to the oscillating currents flowing along the conical surfaces. The time-varying electric and magnetic fields produced by these currents propagate outwards as electromagnetic waves.

**Reference (Balanis, 4th Ed., Chapter 4):** Balanis extensively discusses the biconical antenna as a foundational element in understanding radiation from wire antennas. He introduces it as a generalization of the thin linear dipole.

---

### 2. Geometry and Structure

A biconical antenna consists of two conical conductors joined at their apexes. The angle of the cone ($\alpha$) and the total length of the antenna (related to the cone slant height) are key parameters.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Conical Conductors:** The radiating elements are conical in shape, providing a continuous change in the radius along the length.
*   **Apex:** The point where the two cones meet. This is typically where the feed point is located.
*   **Semi-vertical Angle ($\alpha$):** The angle between the cone's axis and its slant edge.
*   **Slant Height (L):** The length of the conical surface from the apex to the base.
*   **Cone Half-Angle ($2\alpha$):** The angle between the two cone axes (which are collinear for a standard biconical antenna).

**Example:** Imagine two cones, one pointing upwards and one downwards, joined at their pointy ends. The feed is applied at this junction.

**Reference (Kraus, 3rd Ed., Chapter 7):** Kraus provides a detailed description of the biconical antenna's physical structure and its relationship to the linear dipole.

---

### 3. Radiation Mechanism and Current Distribution

The radiation from a biconical antenna is generated by the oscillating currents flowing along its conical surfaces. The current distribution is different from that of a thin dipole and is influenced by the cone angle.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Surface Current:** The oscillating currents flow on the conductive surfaces of the cones.
*   **Sinusoidal Distribution (Approximation):** For thin cones (small $\alpha$), the current distribution is often approximated as sinusoidal, similar to a thin dipole, but with modifications due to the changing radius.
*   **Non-sinusoidal Distribution (General Case):** For wider cones, the current distribution becomes more complex and non-sinusoidal, depending on the cone angle and frequency.
*   **Charge Distribution:** The movement of charges along the conical surfaces creates oscillating electric and magnetic fields.
*   **Electromagnetic Wave Generation:** These time-varying fields propagate away from the antenna as electromagnetic waves.

**Analysis (Balanis, 4th Ed., Chapter 4):** Balanis presents the mathematical analysis of the biconical antenna, deriving the current distribution and subsequently the radiated fields. He highlights the fact that for a biconical antenna, a *sinusoidal* current distribution can be maintained over a wider range of frequencies compared to a thin dipole of the same length. This is a crucial advantage.

---

### 4. Theoretical Analysis and Electromagnetic Fields

The analysis of the biconical antenna involves solving Maxwell's equations subject to the boundary conditions on the conical surfaces. This leads to expressions for the radiated electric and magnetic fields.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Spherical Coordinates:** The conical geometry makes spherical coordinates a natural choice for analysis.
*   **Vector Potential:** The radiation is typically analyzed using vector potentials (magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}$).
*   **Electric and Magnetic Fields:** The radiated fields are derived from the vector potential.
*   **Far-Field Approximation:** For calculating radiation patterns and power, the far-field approximation is used.
*   **Radiation Pattern:** The spatial distribution of the radiated power. For a biconical antenna, the pattern is generally omnidirectional in the plane perpendicular to the antenna axis and exhibits nulls along the axis.

**Mathematical Formulation (Balanis, 4th Ed., Chapter 4):** Balanis provides the derivation of the radiated fields in spherical coordinates. The electric field component ($E_\theta$) and magnetic field component ($H_\phi$) are the primary components in the far field.

$E_\theta = j \frac{I_0 e^{-j\beta r}}{2\pi r} \left[ \frac{\cos(\beta L \cos\theta) - \cos(\beta L)}{\sin\theta} \right]$

Where:
*   $I_0$ is the current amplitude at the feed point.
*   $\beta$ is the phase constant.
*   $r$ is the distance from the antenna.
*   $\theta$ is the elevation angle.
*   $L$ is the slant height of each cone.

**Important Point:** The term in the bracket represents the contribution from each cone. The term $\frac{\cos(\beta L \cos\theta) - \cos(\beta L)}{\sin\theta}$ is related to the current distribution. For a thin dipole ($\alpha \to 90^\circ$), this term approximates the pattern of a thin dipole.

---

### 5. Radiation Pattern

The radiation pattern describes how the antenna radiates power in different directions.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Omnidirectional in Azimuth:** The biconical antenna typically has a radiation pattern that is nearly omnidirectional in the plane perpendicular to its axis (azimuth plane). This makes it suitable for applications where a wide coverage area is needed.
*   **Nulls on Axis:** The radiation intensity is zero along the axis of the antenna due to the absence of current flow perpendicular to the axis in the far field.
*   **Beamwidth:** The angular width of the main lobe of the radiation pattern.
*   **Effect of Cone Angle:** The cone angle ($\alpha$) influences the shape of the radiation pattern. Wider cones tend to produce a more uniform pattern in the elevation plane.

**Comparison (Collin, 2nd Ed., Chapter 3):** Collin might compare the biconical antenna's pattern to that of a dipole or other basic antennas, highlighting its advantages for certain applications.

---

### 6. Impedance and Bandwidth

The impedance of an antenna determines how well it matches the transmission line, affecting power transfer efficiency. The biconical antenna offers advantages in terms of impedance and bandwidth.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Feed Point Impedance:** The impedance seen at the terminals where the antenna is connected to the transmission line.
*   **Constant Impedance:** A significant characteristic of the biconical antenna is that its input impedance remains relatively constant over a broad range of frequencies. This is because as the frequency increases, the cone angle effectively "widens" from the perspective of the wave, which helps to counteract the impedance changes that would occur with a thin linear antenna.
*   **Bandwidth:** The range of frequencies over which the antenna operates effectively (typically defined by a Voltage Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR) limit). The biconical antenna has a significantly wider bandwidth compared to a thin dipole of similar length.
*   **Theoretical vs. Practical Impedance:** The theoretical impedance of an infinitely long biconical antenna with zero cone angle is 120$\Omega$. For practical biconical antennas, the impedance is typically around 120$\Omega$ to 150$\Omega$, depending on the cone angle and dimensions.

**Reference (Pozar, 4th Ed., Chapter 8):** Pozar's "Microwave Engineering" might discuss antenna impedance matching and bandwidth considerations, where the biconical antenna's desirable characteristics can be highlighted in the context of microwave applications.

**Important Point:** The constant impedance and wide bandwidth are key reasons for the biconical antenna's practical importance.

---

### 7. Applications

The favorable impedance and bandwidth characteristics of the biconical antenna make it suitable for various applications.

**Key Applications:**

*   **Broadband Dipole Replacement:** Used as a broadband alternative to the standard dipole antenna.
*   **Transmitting and Receiving Antennas:** Employed in a wide range of communication systems.
*   **Test Antennas:** Due to its predictable performance and broadband nature, it's used as a standard for antenna testing.
*   **Early Radar Systems:** Historically, biconical antennas were used in early radar applications.

**Example:** A biconical antenna could be used in a point-to-point communication link where a wide range of frequencies needs to be covered with a single antenna.

---

### 8. Advantages and Disadvantages

Like all antennas, the biconical antenna has its pros and cons.

**Advantages:**

*   **Wide Bandwidth:** Significantly wider bandwidth than thin linear antennas.
*   **Constant Input Impedance:** Facilitates easier matching to transmission lines.
*   **Simple Structure:** Relatively straightforward to design and construct.
*   **Omnidirectional Azimuth Pattern:** Useful for omnidirectional coverage.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Larger Size:** Generally larger than a thin dipole for the same operating frequency.
*   **Pattern Distortion at Wide Angles:** The azimuth pattern may not be perfectly omnidirectional at very wide cone angles or for longer antennas.
*   **Low Gain:** Typically has lower gain compared to directional antennas.

### 9. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary advantage of a biconical antenna over a thin linear dipole antenna of the same length?

**Answer:** The primary advantage of a biconical antenna over a thin linear dipole is its significantly wider operational bandwidth and more constant input impedance over that bandwidth.

**Question 2:** Describe how the radiation mechanism in a biconical antenna differs from that of a thin dipole.

**Answer:** While both involve oscillating currents, the biconical antenna's continuous change in radius along its length leads to a current distribution that is less affected by frequency compared to a thin dipole. This allows the biconical antenna to maintain a more consistent radiation pattern and impedance over a wider frequency range.

**Question 3:** What shape is the radiation pattern of a biconical antenna in the azimuth plane (plane perpendicular to the antenna axis)?

**Answer:** The radiation pattern of a biconical antenna in the azimuth plane is nearly omnidirectional.

**Question 4:** If a biconical antenna has a cone semi-vertical angle of $\alpha$, what happens to its impedance characteristics as $\alpha$ increases towards 90 degrees?

**Answer:** As the cone semi-vertical angle $\alpha$ increases towards 90 degrees, the biconical antenna approaches a thin linear dipole. Its impedance characteristics will start to deviate from the wideband behavior and become more frequency-sensitive, similar to a thin dipole. The impedance will also tend to decrease from its typical value of around 120-150$\Omega$.

**Question 5:** (Conceptual) Why is the biconical antenna considered a broadband antenna?

**Answer:** The biconical antenna is considered broadband because the progressive widening of its conical shape from apex to base naturally compensates for the frequency-dependent impedance and radiation characteristics that plague thin linear antennas. As frequency increases, the effective electrical length of the antenna doesn't change as drastically as it would for a thin wire, maintaining a reasonable match and pattern over a larger frequency range.

### 10. Important Points to Remember

*   **Biconical Antenna = 3D Dipole:** Visualize it as a dipole with conical arms.
*   **Wide Bandwidth & Constant Impedance:** Key characteristics that make it practical.
*   **Omnidirectional in Azimuth:** Useful for broad coverage.
*   **Nulls on Axis:** A consequence of the geometry and current distribution.
*   **Cone Angle ($\alpha$) matters:** Influences pattern and impedance.
*   **Sinusoidal Current Approximation:** Valid for thin cones, but more complex for wider ones.
*   **Feed Point Impedance ~ 120-150$\Omega$:** Typically higher than a thin dipole (around 73$\Omega$).
