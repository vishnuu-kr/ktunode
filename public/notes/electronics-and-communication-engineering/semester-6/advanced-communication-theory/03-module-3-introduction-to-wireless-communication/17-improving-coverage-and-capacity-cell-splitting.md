# Advanced Communication Theory: Module 3 - Introduction to Wireless Communication
## Topic: Improving Coverage and Capacity - Cell Splitting

---

### **Introduction to Cellular Systems**

Cellular systems divide a geographical area into smaller regions called "cells." Each cell is served by a base station (BS). This cell-based architecture is fundamental to wireless communication, allowing for efficient reuse of radio frequencies.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Cell:** A geographical area served by a single base station.
*   **Base Station (BS):** The fixed transceiver that serves mobile users within its cell.
*   **Mobile Station (MS):** The wireless device used by the subscriber.
*   **Frequency Reuse:** The concept of using the same set of frequencies in geographically separated cells. This significantly increases the overall capacity of the system.
*   **Cellular Network:** A collection of interconnected cells forming a wireless communication system.

**(Refer to Goldsmith, Chapter 1; Rappaport, Chapter 2; Schiller, Chapter 3)**

---

### **Improving Coverage and Capacity**

As the demand for wireless services grows, the need to expand both the geographical coverage and the number of simultaneous users (capacity) becomes critical. Cell splitting is a primary technique to achieve this.

---

### **1. Improving Coverage**

**1.1 What is Coverage?**

Coverage refers to the geographical area where a mobile user can establish and maintain a reliable wireless connection with the network. Good coverage ensures that users can make and receive calls or data sessions without experiencing dropped connections or poor signal quality.

**1.2 Factors Affecting Coverage:**

*   **Transmit Power:** Higher power generally leads to greater coverage but also increases interference.
*   **Antenna Gain:** Directional antennas can focus the signal, improving coverage in a specific direction.
*   **Antenna Height:** Taller antennas can overcome terrain obstacles and extend coverage.
*   **Receiver Sensitivity:** The ability of the receiver to detect weak signals.
*   **Path Loss:** Signal attenuation as it travels from the transmitter to the receiver due to distance, absorption, and scattering.
*   **Interference:** Signals from other cells or other sources that degrade the desired signal.
*   **Fading:** Fluctuations in signal strength due to multipath propagation and other channel impairments.

**(Refer to Goldsmith, Chapter 2; Rappaport, Chapter 3; Haykin, Chapter 1)**

**1.3 Techniques to Improve Coverage:**

While cell splitting primarily focuses on capacity, it also indirectly improves coverage by reducing the area served by each base station, thus increasing the signal strength within that smaller area. Other techniques include:

*   **Increasing Transmit Power:** Limited by regulations and battery life.
*   **Higher Antenna Height:** Can be costly and limited by infrastructure.
*   **Using Repeaters/Boosters:** Amplify signals to extend range.
*   **Site Diversity:** Using multiple antennas at different locations to combat fading.
*   **Improving Receiver Sensitivity:** Through advanced receiver design.
