### 1. Introduction to Microwave Filters and the Image Parameter Method

**1.1 What are Microwave Filters?**

Microwave filters are passive or active devices used to selectively pass signals within a certain frequency range and attenuate signals outside that range. They are crucial components in almost all microwave systems for:

*   **Frequency Selection:** Isolating desired signals from unwanted ones.
*   **Noise Reduction:** Removing noise frequencies.
*   **Harmonic Suppression:** Eliminating unwanted harmonics generated by nonlinear devices.
*   **Preventing Oscillation:** Stabilizing amplifiers and oscillators.
*   **Interference Mitigation:** Blocking interfering signals.

**1.2 The Image Parameter Method**

The image parameter method is a classical approach to filter design that predates the more modern insertion loss method. It was developed by engineers like George Campbell and Otto Zobel at Bell Telephone Laboratories.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Image Impedance ($Z_{01}$ and $Z_{02}$):** For a two-port network, the image impedances are the impedances that must be connected to the input and output ports, respectively, such that the impedance looking into each port is equal to the impedance connected to that port. In simpler terms, if you terminate one port with its image impedance, the impedance seen at the other port will be its own image impedance.
*   **Propagation Constant ($\gamma$):** This parameter describes how a wave propagates through a section of a transmission line or a circuit. For a homogeneous medium, $\gamma = \alpha + j\beta$, where $\alpha$ is the attenuation constant (in Nepers/unit length) and $\beta$ is the phase constant (in radians/unit length).
*   **Characteristic Impedance ($Z_0$):** The impedance of an infinitely long uniform transmission line.

**The Design Process (General Idea):**

The image parameter method involves designing a filter by cascading symmetrical two-port networks (sections). The goal is to achieve a desired frequency response by selecting sections with appropriate image impedances and propagation constants.

*   **Termination:** The filter is terminated at both ends with the *image impedance* of the end sections. This ensures that the impedance seen looking into the input is the image impedance of the first section, and the impedance seen looking into the output is the image impedance of the last section.
*   **Cascading:** Multiple sections are cascaded to create the desired filter characteristic.

**Advantages:**

*   Conceptually straightforward.
*   Relatively easy to design and implement with discrete components.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Assumes the filter is terminated with its image impedance, which is often not the case in practical systems.
*   The image impedance varies significantly with frequency, especially near the cutoff frequencies. This leads to impedance mismatch and poor performance.
*   Does not provide optimal filter responses (e.g., maximally flat, equiripple).

### 2. Constant K Filters

Constant K filters are the simplest type of image parameter filters. They are characterized by having constant image impedance ($Z_0$) over the entire frequency range, or at least over the passband. This is achieved by using reactive components (inductors and capacitors) whose impedance is proportional to frequency or inversely proportional to frequency, in a symmetrical arrangement.

**2.1 Basic Structures:**

Constant K filters are based on symmetrical T- and $\pi$-sections.

**2.1.1 Constant K Low-Pass Filter (LPF)**

*   **T-section:**
    *   Series arm: Inductor ($L_1$)
    *   Shunt arms: Capacitors ($C_1$)
    *   [Diagram of T-section LPF with L1 in series and C1 in shunt]
    *   $Z_{01} = \sqrt{\frac{L_1}{C_1}}$ (constant, assuming L1 and C1 are ideal)
    *   Cutoff frequency: $\omega_c = \frac{2}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1}}$

*   **$\pi$-section:**
    *   Series arm: Capacitor ($C_1$)
    *   Shunt arms: Inductors ($L_1$)
    *   [Diagram of $\pi$-section LPF with C1 in series and L1 in shunt]
    *   $Z_{01} = \sqrt{\frac{L_1}{C_1}}$ (constant)
    *   Cutoff frequency: $\omega_c = \frac{2}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1}}$

**Key Property:** The impedance of the series element is proportional to frequency ($j\omega L_1$), and the impedance of the shunt element is inversely proportional to frequency ($\frac{1}{j\omega C_1}$). This leads to a constant image impedance.

**2.1.2 Constant K High-Pass Filter (HPF)**

*   **T-section:**
    *   Series arm: Capacitor ($L_1$)
    *   Shunt arms: Inductors ($C_1$)
    *   [Diagram of T-section HPF with L1 in series and C1 in shunt]
    *   $Z_{01} = \sqrt{\frac{L_1}{C_1}}$ (constant)
    *   Cutoff frequency: $\omega_c = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{L_1 C_1}}$

*   **$\pi$-section:**
    *   Series arm: Inductor ($C_1$)
    *   Shunt arms: Capacitors ($L_1$)
    *   [Diagram of $\pi$-section HPF with C1 in series and L1 in shunt]
    *   $Z_{01} = \sqrt{\frac{L_1}{C_1}}$ (constant)
    *   Cutoff frequency: $\omega_c = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{L_1 C_1}}$

**2.1.3 Constant K Band-Pass Filter (BPF)**

A band-pass filter can be constructed by combining L-C circuits in series and shunt arms to exhibit resonance at the desired band.

*   **T-section:**
    *   Series arm: Inductor ($L_1$) in series with a capacitor ($C_2$)
    *   Shunt arms: Capacitor ($C_1$) in parallel with an inductor ($L_2$)
    *   [Diagram of T-section BPF]
    *   The image impedance and cutoff frequencies depend on the values of $L_1, C_1, L_2, C_2$.

**2.1.4 Constant K Band-Elimination Filter (BEF)**

*   **T-section:**
    *   Series arm: Capacitor ($C_1$) in parallel with an inductor ($L_2$)
    *   Shunt arms: Inductor ($L_1$) in series with a capacitor ($C_2$)
    *   [Diagram of T-section BEF]

**2.2 Design Calculations for Constant K LPF:**

Let's consider designing a Constant K LPF with a desired characteristic impedance $Z_0$ and cutoff frequency $\omega_c$.

*   **T-section:**
    *   We need $Z_{01} = Z_0$ and $\omega_c = \frac{2}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1}}$.
    *   The impedance of the series arm is $Z_s = j\omega L_1$.
    *   The impedance of the shunt arms is $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_1}$.
    *   The image impedance for a T-section is given by $Z_{01} = \sqrt{Z_s (Z_s + 4Z_{sh})}$.
    *   For constant impedance, we need $Z_s \gg 4Z_{sh}$ or $Z_{sh} \gg 4Z_s$ depending on the frequency.
    *   A common assumption for constant impedance is that the product of impedance of series and shunt arms is constant, $Z_s Z_{sh} = k^2$.
    *   For a T-section LPF, if $Z_s = j\omega L_1$ and $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_1}$, then $Z_s Z_{sh} = \frac{L_1}{C_1}$.
    *   For the image impedance to be constant, $Z_{01} = \sqrt{Z_s Z_{sh}} = \sqrt{\frac{L_1}{C_1}}$.
    *   The cutoff frequency is determined by when the circuit becomes aperiodic (infinite impedance in T-section or zero impedance in $\pi$-section). This occurs when the series element impedance becomes 0 and the shunt element impedance becomes infinite at cutoff.
    *   For the T-section LPF, the transfer function becomes infinite when $j\omega L_1 + \frac{2}{j\omega C_1} = 0$, which means $\omega^2 L_1 C_1 = 2$, so $\omega_c = \frac{2}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1}}$.
    *   From these equations, we can derive the component values:
        *   $L_1 = \frac{Z_0}{\omega_c/2}$
        *   $C_1 = \frac{2}{Z_0 \omega_c}$

*   **$\pi$-section:**
    *   For a $\pi$-section LPF, the image impedance is $Z_{01} = \sqrt{\frac{Z_{sh} (Z_{sh} + 4Z_s)}{1}}$, where $Z_s = \frac{1}{j\omega C_1}$ and $Z_{sh} = j\omega L_1$.
    *   For constant impedance, $Z_{01} = \sqrt{\frac{L_1}{C_1}}$.
    *   The cutoff frequency occurs when $j\omega L_1 + \frac{2}{j\omega C_1} = 0$, which again gives $\omega_c = \frac{2}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1}}$.
    *   From these equations, we can derive the component values:
        *   $L_1 = \frac{Z_0}{\omega_c/2}$
        *   $C_1 = \frac{2}{Z_0 \omega_c}$

**Important Note:** For practical microwave filters, these discrete component values might be realized using distributed elements (transmission lines, stubs) rather than lumped elements, especially at higher frequencies.

**Example:** Design a Constant K LPF with $Z_0 = 50 \Omega$ and $\omega_c = 10^8$ rad/s.

Using the T-section formulas:
$L_1 = \frac{50}{10^8/2} = \frac{50}{5 \times 10^7} = 1 \times 10^{-6} H = 1 \mu H$
$C_1 = \frac{2}{50 \times 10^8} = \frac{2}{5 \times 10^9} = 0.4 \times 10^{-9} F = 0.4 nF$

**2.3 Limitations of Constant K Filters:**

*   **Poor Attenuation:** The attenuation in the stopband is gradual and not very sharp.
*   **Variable Image Impedance:** The image impedance is only truly constant under idealized conditions. In reality, it varies with frequency, leading to impedance mismatches and poor performance, especially outside the passband.

### 3. M-Derived Filters

M-derived filters were developed by Otto Zobel to overcome the limitations of Constant K filters, particularly the gradual attenuation and frequency-dependent image impedance. They achieve a sharper cutoff and better attenuation in the stopband by introducing an additional element to create a transmission zero at a specific frequency within the stopband.

**3.1 Concept:**

An M-derived filter is derived from a Constant K prototype filter by modifying one or more of its elements. This modification introduces a pair of coincident poles and zeros in the transfer function, resulting in a frequency where the impedance becomes infinite (for a T-section) or zero (for a $\pi$-section), creating a "peak" of attenuation.

*   **Parameter 'm':** A parameter 'm' (0 < m < 1) is used to specify the location of the transmission zero. The cutoff frequency of the derived section is shifted by a factor of 'm' compared to the original Constant K section.

**3.2 M-Derived LPF (T-section):**

Consider a Constant K LPF T-section with series impedance $Z_{sK}$ and shunt impedance $Z_{shK}$.
For an M-derived LPF T-section:
*   Series arm: $Z_s = m Z_{sK}$
*   Shunt arms: $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{m} Z_{shK} + (1-\frac{1}{m^2}) Z_{shK}$ (This formulation is more complex and often simplified by considering impedance transformations).

A more intuitive approach for the T-section derived LPF is:
*   Series arm: $Z_s = j\omega L_1$.
*   Shunt arms: $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_1} + \frac{1}{j\omega C_2}$ in parallel.
    *   The impedance of the shunt arms becomes: $Z_{sh} = \frac{\frac{1}{j\omega C_1} \times \frac{1}{j\omega C_2}}{\frac{1}{j\omega C_1} + \frac{1}{j\omega C_2}} = \frac{1}{j\omega (C_1 + C_2) - \omega^2 L_1 C_1 C_2}$.
    *   This structure creates a transmission zero when the denominator becomes zero, which happens at $\omega = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1 C_2 / (C_1+C_2)}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_1 (\frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2})}}$.

**Simplified Design Approach for M-Derived LPF T-section:**

Let the Constant K section have $Z_s = j\omega L_K$ and $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_K}$.
The image impedance is $Z_{0K} = \sqrt{L_K/C_K}$.
The cutoff frequency is $\omega_{cK} = 2/\sqrt{L_K C_K}$.

For the M-derived LPF T-section:
*   Series arm: $Z_s = j\omega L_1$.
*   Shunt arms: Consist of a capacitor $C'_1$ in series with a parallel combination of an inductor $L'_1$ and a capacitor $C'_2$.

A simpler structure often presented is:
*   Series arm: $Z_s = j\omega L_1$.
*   Shunt arms: A circuit that looks like a capacitor $C_1$ in series with a trap (parallel LC circuit) or just a modified capacitor.

**Let's use the standard definition derived from a Constant K prototype:**
A T-section derived from a Constant K LPF has:
*   Series impedance: $Z_s = j\omega L_1$
*   Shunt impedance: $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_1} || \frac{1}{j\omega C_2}$ (parallel combination)
    *   $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{j\omega (C_1+C_2) - \omega^2 L_1 C_1 C_2}$
    *   The transmission zero occurs when the denominator is zero: $j\omega (C_1+C_2) - \omega^2 L_1 C_1 C_2 = 0$.
    *   This gives a zero at $\omega_{\infty} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_1 (\frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2})}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_1 \frac{C_1+C_2}{C_1 C_2}}}$.

For a derived filter, we match the image impedance and cutoff frequency to a Constant K section.
*   The derived section's cutoff frequency $\omega_c = m \omega_{cK}$.
*   The derived section's image impedance $Z_{01}$ is matched to $Z_{0K}$.

**Component Design:**

For an M-derived LPF T-section with cutoff frequency $\omega_c$ and image impedance $Z_0$:
*   The series inductance $L_1$ and the shunt capacitance $C_1$ and $C_2$ are designed.
*   The parameter 'm' determines the location of the transmission zero: $\omega_\infty = \omega_c / m$.
*   The design equations for an M-derived T-section LPF are typically related to the Constant K components ($L_K, C_K$) as follows:
    *   $L_1 = m L_K$
    *   $C_1 = C_K \frac{2-m^2}{2}$
    *   $C_2 = C_K \frac{m^2-1}{2}$ (This implies $C_2$ is negative, which is not physically realizable. This highlights the conceptual nature or requires reinterpretation).

**A more practical and common representation for an M-derived LPF T-section with a transmission zero at $\omega_\infty$ and cutoff frequency $\omega_c$:**

*   Series element: Inductor $L_1$.
*   Shunt element: A parallel combination of a capacitor $C'_1$ and a series LC circuit ($L'_1$ and $C'_2$).

Consider the Constant K LPF prototype with $L_K$ and $C_K$.
The M-derived T-section LPF is designed such that its image impedance and cutoff frequency match the Constant K prototype.
The series arm is modified to be $Z_s = j\omega (m L_K)$.
The shunt arms are modified to have an impedance that, when combined with the series arm, yields the desired response.

**Design formulas for M-derived T-section LPF:**
(Matching $Z_0$ and $\omega_c$ to a Constant K LPF with $L_K$ and $C_K$)

*   Series inductance: $L_1 = m L_K$
*   Shunt capacitance: $C_1 = C_K \frac{2-m^2}{2}$
*   Additional series capacitance: $C_2 = \frac{C_K (1-m^2)}{2m}$

This is incorrect. Let's use a standard reference.

According to Collin (2/e), for an M-derived T-section LPF derived from a Constant K LPF with $L_K$ and $C_K$:

*   Series impedance: $Z_s = j\omega L_1$
*   Shunt impedance: $Z_{sh} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_1}$ in parallel with a series LC circuit $Z_{trap} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_2} + j\omega L_2$.
    *   The trap resonates at $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{L_2 C_2}$.
    *   The transmission zero is at $\omega_\infty = \omega_0$.

The design aims to match the Constant K parameters.
For a Constant K LPF with $\omega_{cK} = 2/\sqrt{L_K C_K}$ and $Z_{0K} = \sqrt{L_K/C_K}$:

*   **M-derived T-section LPF:**
    *   Series inductance: $L_1 = m L_K$
    *   Shunt capacitance: $C_1 = C_K \frac{2-m^2}{2}$
    *   Series inductance in the shunt arm: $L_2 = L_K \frac{1-m^2}{2m}$
    *   Shunt capacitance in the shunt arm: $C_2 = C_K \frac{1}{2m}$
    *   The transmission zero occurs at $\omega_\infty = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_2 C_2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(L_K \frac{1-m^2}{2m}) (C_K \frac{1}{2m})}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_K C_K \frac{1-m^2}{4m^2}}} = \frac{2}{ \sqrt{L_K C_K}} \frac{m}{\sqrt{1-m^2}} = \omega_{cK} \frac{m}{\sqrt{1-m^2}}$.
    *   The image impedance $Z_{01}$ is designed to match $Z_{0K}$ at the cutoff frequency.

**3.3 M-Derived $\pi$-section LPF:**

The design of the M-derived $\pi$-section LPF is dual to the T-section.
*   Series capacitance: $C_1 = m C_K$
*   Shunt inductance: $L_1 = L_K \frac{2-m^2}{2}$
*   Series inductance in the series arm: $L_2 = L_K \frac{1-m^2}{2m}$
*   Shunt capacitance in the series arm: $C_2 = C_K \frac{1}{2m}$
*   The transmission zero occurs at $\omega_\infty = \omega_{cK} \frac{m}{\sqrt{1-m^2}}$.

**3.4 Choosing 'm':**

*   A smaller 'm' (closer to 0) shifts the transmission zero closer to the cutoff frequency, resulting in a sharper cutoff.
*   However, as 'm' approaches 0, the values of $L_2$ and $C_2$ (or $L_2, C_2$ in the dual design) become very large or very small, making the filter impractical to realize.
*   Typical values for 'm' range from 0.2 to 0.8.

**3.5 Advantages of M-Derived Filters:**

*   **Sharper Cutoff:** The transmission zero provides a significant attenuation peak in the stopband close to the cutoff frequency, leading to a sharper transition.
*   **Improved Attenuation:** Better attenuation in the stopband compared to Constant K filters.

**3.6 Disadvantages of M-Derived Filters:**

*   **More Complex:** Require more components.
*   **Image Impedance Variation:** While improved, the image impedance still varies with frequency, especially outside the region of interest.

### 4. Composite Filters

Composite filters are formed by cascading multiple sections of Constant K and M-derived filters to achieve a desired attenuation characteristic over a wide frequency range. This method combines the simplicity of Constant K filters with the sharp cutoff of M-derived filters.

**4.1 Principle:**

*   **Initial Sections:** Constant K filters are often used as the first and last sections to provide a relatively constant image impedance at the filter's terminals, minimizing reflection losses when connected to a system with a fixed characteristic impedance.
*   **Intermediate Sections:** M-derived filters are used in between to provide the desired sharp attenuation in the stopband. Typically, M-derived sections with different 'm' values are used. The 'm' value of the first M-derived section is usually higher (e.g., 0.8) to get a gradual transition from the Constant K section. Subsequent M-derived sections use progressively lower 'm' values (e.g., 0.6, 0.4, 0.2) to create multiple transmission zeros and achieve a very sharp cutoff.

**4.2 Design Procedure:**

1.  **Determine Filter Specifications:** Specify the desired passband, stopband edge frequencies, and the characteristic impedance of the system.
2.  **Design a Constant K Prototype:** Design a Constant K filter (LPF, HPF, BPF, BEF) that meets the passband requirements. This sets the base component values.
3.  **Introduce M-Derived Sections:**
    *   For an LPF, design an M-derived T-section LPF with $m_1 = 0.8$ to be cascaded with the Constant K section. This section will have a cutoff frequency slightly higher than the Constant K cutoff.
    *   For steeper attenuation, cascade additional M-derived sections with decreasing 'm' values (e.g., $m_2 = 0.6$, $m_3 = 0.4$, etc.). Each section will have its transmission zero located at a progressively higher frequency within the stopband.
    *   A terminating M-derived section (often with $m=0.8$) is used at the end to provide a better impedance match to the system.
4.  **Calculate Component Values:** Calculate the specific values of inductors and capacitors for each section based on their respective design formulas and the 'm' parameter.

**Example:** Design a composite LPF with $Z_0 = 50 \Omega$, cutoff frequency $\omega_c = 10^8$ rad/s, and transmission zeros at $1.2 \times 10^8$, $1.4 \times 10^8$, and $1.6 \times 10^8$ rad/s.

*   **Constant K LPF:** Design a Constant K LPF with $\omega_{cK} = 10^8$ rad/s and $Z_{0K} = 50 \Omega$.
    *   $L_K = 1 \mu H$
    *   $C_K = 0.4 nF$

*   **M-Derived Sections:**
    *   **Section 1 (Terminating):** Design an M-derived T-section LPF with $m_1 = 0.8$ derived from the Constant K prototype.
        *   $L_1 = m_1 L_K = 0.8 \times 1 \mu H = 0.8 \mu H$.
        *   $C_1 = C_K \frac{2-m_1^2}{2} = 0.4 nF \frac{2 - (0.8)^2}{2} = 0.4 nF \frac{2 - 0.64}{2} = 0.4 nF \frac{1.36}{2} = 0.272 nF$.
        *   $L_2 = L_K \frac{1-m_1^2}{2m_1} = 1 \mu H \frac{1 - (0.8)^2}{2 \times 0.8} = 1 \mu H \frac{1 - 0.64}{1.6} = 1 \mu H \frac{0.36}{1.6} = 0.225 \mu H$.
        *   $C_2 = C_K \frac{1}{2m_1} = 0.4 nF \frac{1}{2 \times 0.8} = 0.4 nF \frac{1}{1.6} = 0.25 nF$.
        *   The transmission zero for this section is at $\omega_{\infty 1} = \omega_{cK} \frac{m_1}{\sqrt{1-m_1^2}} = 10^8 \frac{0.8}{\sqrt{1 - 0.64}} = 10^8 \frac{0.8}{\sqrt{0.36}} = 10^8 \frac{0.8}{0.6} = 1.33 \times 10^8$ rad/s. This is close to the desired $1.4 \times 10^8$.

    *   **Section 2:** Design another M-derived T-section LPF with a different 'm' value to achieve a transmission zero at $1.2 \times 10^8$ rad/s.
        *   $\omega_{\infty 2} = \omega_{cK} \frac{m_2}{\sqrt{1-m_2^2}} = 1.2 \times 10^8$.
        *   Solving for $m_2$: $\frac{m_2}{\sqrt{1-m_2^2}} = 1.2$. Square both sides: $\frac{m_2^2}{1-m_2^2} = 1.44$.
        *   $m_2^2 = 1.44 (1-m_2^2) = 1.44 - 1.44 m_2^2$.
        *   $2.44 m_2^2 = 1.44 \implies m_2^2 = \frac{1.44}{2.44} \approx 0.59$.
        *   $m_2 \approx \sqrt{0.59} \approx 0.77$.
        *   Calculate $L_1, C_1, L_2, C_2$ using $m_2 = 0.77$ and the Constant K values.

    *   **Section 3:** Design an M-derived T-section LPF with $m_3$ such that $\omega_{\infty 3} = 1.6 \times 10^8$ rad/s.
        *   $\frac{m_3}{\sqrt{1-m_3^2}} = 1.6$.
        *   $m_3^2 = 1.6^2 (1-m_3^2) = 2.56 (1-m_3^2) = 2.56 - 2.56 m_3^2$.
        *   $3.56 m_3^2 = 2.56 \implies m_3^2 = \frac{2.56}{3.56} \approx 0.719$.
        *   $m_3 \approx \sqrt{0.719} \approx 0.848$.

    *   **Final Configuration:** A composite filter could be: Constant K LPF - M-derived LPF ($m=0.77$) - M-derived LPF ($m=0.848$) - M-derived LPF ($m=0.8$, terminating). The actual design will involve careful cascading and impedance matching considerations.

**4.3 Realization in MICs:**

For microwave frequencies, lumped elements (L and C) are replaced by distributed elements:
*   **Inductors:** Realized using short-circuited stubs or meandering transmission lines.
*   **Capacitors:** Realized using open-circuited stubs or interdigital structures.

The design process remains the same conceptually, but the component values (length of stubs, dimensions of transmission lines) are calculated based on the desired impedance and frequency, and the propagation characteristics of the transmission line medium.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Q1. What is the main advantage of using M-derived filters over Constant K filters?**
**A1.** M-derived filters offer a sharper cutoff in the stopband due to the introduction of transmission zeros, leading to better attenuation compared to Constant K filters.

**Q2. A Constant K LPF T-section has $L_1 = 2 \mu H$ and $C_1 = 0.2 nF$. Calculate its cutoff frequency and image impedance.**
**A2.**
*   Cutoff frequency $\omega_c = \frac{2}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{(2 \times 10^{-6} H)(0.2 \times 10^{-9} F)}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{0.4 \times 10^{-15}}} = \frac{2}{0.632 \times 10^{-7.5}} \approx \frac{2}{0.2 \times 10^{-7}} \approx 10^8$ rad/s.
*   Image impedance $Z_0 = \sqrt{\frac{L_1}{C_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 10^{-6} H}{0.2 \times 10^{-9} F}} = \sqrt{10 \times 10^3} = \sqrt{10000} = 100 \Omega$.

**Q3. For an M-derived LPF T-section, if the transmission zero is located at $\omega_\infty$ and the cutoff frequency is $\omega_c$, how are they related through the parameter 'm'?**
**A3.** The relation is $\omega_\infty = \omega_c \frac{m}{\sqrt{1-m^2}}$.

**Q4. Explain the role of Constant K and M-derived sections in a composite filter.**
**A4.** Constant K sections are often used as terminating sections to provide a consistent image impedance. M-derived sections are used in between to introduce transmission zeros, thereby achieving sharper cutoff and increased stopband attenuation.

**Q5. A composite filter uses cascaded Constant K and M-derived sections. What is the primary design consideration for the 'm' values of successive M-derived sections?**
**A5.** The 'm' values are typically decreased for successive M-derived sections to place transmission zeros progressively further into the stopband, thus sharpening the filter's transition band.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Image Parameter Method:** Relies on terminating a network with its image impedance to ensure that the impedance seen at the other port is also its image impedance.
*   **Constant K Filters:** Simple, but have gradual cutoff and variable image impedance. $Z_0$ is constant.
*   **M-Derived Filters:** Offer sharper cutoff and better stopband attenuation by introducing transmission zeros. The parameter 'm' controls the location of this zero.
*   **Composite Filters:** Cascade Constant K and M-derived sections for optimized performance, combining the advantages of both.
*   **Practical Realization:** At microwave frequencies, lumped elements are replaced by distributed elements (stubs, transmission lines). The design principles remain, but component calculations involve electrical lengths and characteristic impedances.
*   **Limitations:** The image parameter method is a precursor to the more modern insertion loss method, which provides theoretically optimal filter responses.
