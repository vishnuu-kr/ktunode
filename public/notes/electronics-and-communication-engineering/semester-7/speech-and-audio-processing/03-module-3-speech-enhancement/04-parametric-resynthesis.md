# SPEECH AND AUDIO PROCESSING: Module 3: Speech Enhancement

## Topic: Parametric Resynthesis

---

### Introduction

Parametric resynthesis is a powerful technique in speech enhancement and speech synthesis that aims to reconstruct or modify a speech signal by modeling its underlying acoustic properties using a set of parameters. Instead of directly manipulating the waveform, parametric methods focus on estimating and then re-synthesizing the speech based on these estimated parameters. This approach offers several advantages, including noise reduction, bandwidth extension, and the ability to modify speech characteristics.

This topic builds upon the understanding of speech production and analysis gained in previous modules. It leverages the concept of modeling speech as a source-filter system, where the source is the excitation signal (e.g., glottal airflow for voiced sounds, turbulent noise for unvoiced sounds) and the filter is the vocal tract.

### Key Concepts and Definitions

#### 1. Speech Production Model (Source-Filter Model)

*   **Core Idea:** Speech is generated by a source (larynx, airflow) modulated by a time-varying filter (vocal tract).
*   **Voiced Sounds:** Produced by periodic vibration of the vocal cords (glottal source). The vocal cords produce a quasi-periodic train of pulses, and the resulting sound is shaped by the vocal tract.
*   **Unvoiced Sounds:** Produced by turbulent airflow through a constriction in the vocal tract (e.g., fricatives like /s/, /f/). The source is broadband noise.
*   **Parametric Representation:** The characteristics of both the source and the filter can be represented by a set of parameters.

*(**Reference:** O'Shaughnessy, Chapter 2: Speech Production Mechanisms)*
*(**Alignment:** CO1: To describe the fundamental concepts, principles, and theories of speech production.)*

#### 2. Speech Parameter Estimation

The goal is to estimate the parameters that best describe the speech signal, often in the presence of noise.

*   **Excitation Signal Parameters:**
    *   **Fundamental Frequency (F0) / Pitch:** The rate of vocal cord vibration. Crucial for distinguishing voiced and unvoiced segments and for prosody.
    *   **Voicing Decision:** Whether a segment of speech is voiced or unvoiced.
    *   **Glottal Pulse Shape (for voiced speech):** Describes the waveform of the airflow from the glottis. Can be modeled using parameters like the open quotient or skewness.
    *   **Noise Excitation Parameters (for unvoiced speech):** Describes the spectral tilt or characteristics of the broadband noise source.

*   **Vocal Tract Filter Parameters:**
    *   **Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) Coefficients:** These are widely used to model the vocal tract as an all-pole digital filter. The coefficients represent the resonant frequencies and bandwidths of the vocal tract.
    *   **Formant Frequencies and Bandwidths:** The resonant frequencies (formants) and their associated bandwidths of the vocal tract. These are directly related to the poles of the LPC model.
    *   **Spectral Envelope:** The overall shape of the speech spectrum, which is largely determined by the vocal tract.

*(**Reference:** Quatieri, Chapter 3: Signal Representation, Section 3.2.1: Time-Domain Models)*
*(**Alignment:** CO2: To analyse the speech signal in the time and frequency domain. K2)*

#### 3. Parametric Resynthesis

Once parameters are estimated, they are used to generate a new speech signal.

*   **Process:**
    1.  **Analysis:** Estimate speech parameters (F0, LPC coefficients, voicing, etc.) from the input speech signal. This step is often performed on short frames of speech (e.g., 20-30 ms).
    2.  **Modification (Optional):** Adjust the estimated parameters to achieve a desired enhancement (e.g., reduce noise, change pitch).
    3.  **Synthesis:** Generate a new speech signal using the modified or original estimated parameters. This involves using a vocoder (voice coder) that takes the parameters and synthesizes the waveform.

*   **Types of Parametric Coders/Synthesizers:**
    *   **Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) Synthesizer:** Uses the LPC coefficients to model the vocal tract. The excitation is synthesized based on F0 and voicing.
    *   **Homomorphic Vocoders:** Separate source and filter characteristics using cepstral analysis.
    *   **Other Parametric Models:** Including source-filter models with more explicit modeling of glottal source and vocal tract characteristics.

*(**Reference:** Quatieri, Chapter 6: Speech Synthesis, Section 6.2: Analysis-by-Synthesis)*
*(**Reference:** Rabinar, Chapter 5: Linear Predictive Coding, Section 5.5: LPC Synthesis)*
*(**Alignment:** CO3: To apply speech processing concepts in real-world applications. K3. This topic is directly about application.)*

### Parametric Speech Enhancement Techniques

Parametric resynthesis is a cornerstone of many speech enhancement algorithms. The core idea is to first analyze the noisy speech to estimate clean speech parameters, and then resynthesize clean speech using these estimated parameters.

#### 1. Noise Reduction using Parametric Models

*   **Concept:** Noise often affects both the excitation and the vocal tract characteristics. Parametric models help to isolate and reconstruct the clean speech components.
*   **Process:**
    1.  **Frame Analysis:** Divide the noisy speech into short, overlapping frames.
    2.  **Parameter Estimation:** For each frame, estimate:
        *   **Vocal Tract Parameters:** Typically LPC coefficients or formant-related parameters. Noise might blur the spectral peaks, so techniques aim to identify the underlying vocal tract resonances.
        *   **Excitation Parameters:** Estimate F0, voicing decision, and the characteristics of the excitation signal (glottal pulses for voiced, noise for unvoiced). Noise can distort the periodicity of voiced speech and introduce unwanted broadband components.
    3.  **Noise Estimation/Suppression:**
        *   **Spectral Subtraction (in a parametric context):** Estimate the noise spectrum from segments identified as silence or background noise. This spectral estimate can be used to "clean" the magnitude spectrum of the speech signal *before* LPC analysis, or the LPC coefficients themselves can be adapted to be more robust to noise.
        *   **F0 and Voicing Estimation in Noise:** Robust pitch tracking algorithms are crucial. Techniques might involve filtering in the cepstral domain or using autocorrelation methods with modified thresholds.
        *   **LPC Parameter Smoothing:** Noise can cause rapid fluctuations in LPC coefficients. Smoothing them across frames or using a more robust estimation method can improve resynthesis quality.
    4.  **Resynthesis:** Use a vocoder (e.g., LPC vocoder) with the *estimated clean* excitation and vocal tract parameters to generate the enhanced speech signal.

*   **Example:**
    *   Imagine a noisy recording of someone saying "hello." The noise might be a low-level hum.
    *   **Analysis:** LPC coefficients are estimated for each frame. The F0 contour is estimated.
    *   **Noise Reduction:** If the noise is broadband and relatively constant, techniques might try to suppress frequencies where the noise is dominant. For example, if the noise is a low-frequency hum, the LPC analysis might be adjusted to compensate for the boost in those frequencies. If the noise is impacting pitch tracking, a more robust pitch estimator might be used.
    *   **Resynthesis:** The estimated F0 and LPC parameters (presumably cleaner than if directly analyzed from the noisy signal) are fed into an LPC synthesizer to produce an enhanced "hello."

*(**Reference:** O'Shaughnessy, Chapter 11: Speech Enhancement, Section 11.4: Parametric Methods)*
*(**Alignment:** CO3: To apply speech processing concepts in real-world applications. K3. CO2: K2)*

#### 2. Bandwidth Extension

*   **Concept:** Speech signals often have their high-frequency components (above ~4 kHz) removed in telephone systems or due to compression. Parametric models can be used to "guess" or resynthesize these missing high frequencies.
*   **Process:**
    1.  **Analysis:** Analyze the available low-bandwidth speech (e.g., up to 4 kHz) to estimate LPC coefficients and F0. The LPC coefficients capture the spectral envelope, including the locations of formants.
    2.  **Parametric Estimation of Missing Frequencies:**
        *   **Spectral Envelope Extension:** LPC models inherently represent the spectral envelope with poles. If the LPC analysis can be performed accurately on the low-bandwidth signal, these poles can be extrapolated to higher frequencies.
        *   **Formant Tracking:** Formants in the missing high-frequency range (e.g., for fricatives like /s/ and /sh/) can be predicted based on the tracked formants in the lower bands and knowledge of speech production.
    3.  **Resynthesis:**
        *   **LPC Synthesis with Extended Spectrum:** The LPC model can be used to generate spectral energy in the higher bands.
        *   **Sinusoidal Coding Techniques:** These can be combined with parametric methods by using the estimated F0 and formant information to guide the synthesis of high-frequency sinusoidal components.

*   **Example:**
    *   A telephone conversation is limited to 4 kHz. The sound of "s" might be less clear because its primary energy is above 4 kHz.
    *   **Analysis:** LPC analysis of the 4 kHz speech is performed. The formant transitions in the lower bands are tracked.
    *   **Bandwidth Extension:** Based on the LPC coefficients and the observed formant movements, the synthesizer "predicts" the spectral peaks (formants) that would exist in the higher frequency bands.
    *   **Resynthesis:** The LPC synthesizer or a more advanced coder generates speech that sounds richer and more natural because it has synthesized high-frequency content.

*(**Reference:** Quatieri, Chapter 6: Speech Synthesis, Section 6.4: Bandwidth Extension)*
*(**Alignment:** CO3: To apply speech processing concepts in real-world applications. K3. CO5: K3)*

### Advantages of Parametric Resynthesis for Speech Enhancement

*   **Noise Reduction:** By separating the speech model from noise, parametric methods can effectively reduce background noise.
*   **Reduced Data Rate:** In speech coding, representing speech parametrically (e.g., LPC coefficients, F0) requires fewer bits than storing the entire waveform. While not directly an enhancement *feature*, it relates to the efficiency of representing speech characteristics.
*   **Speech Modification:** Allows for direct manipulation of speech characteristics like pitch, speaking rate, and voice quality by modifying the estimated parameters.
*   **Improved Intelligibility:** By preserving and enhancing crucial speech features (formants, F0), intelligibility can be improved, especially in noisy conditions.
*   **Bandwidth Extension:** Enables the recovery of missing high-frequency spectral information.

---

### Disadvantages and Challenges

*   **Articulatory Distortion:** If the parametric model is too simple or the estimation is poor, the resynthesized speech can sound unnatural or robotic.
*   **Sensitivity to Noise:** While used for enhancement, the *estimation* of parameters from heavily noisy speech can be challenging and prone to errors, which are then propagated during resynthesis.
*   **Computational Complexity:** Some parametric estimation algorithms can be computationally intensive.
*   **Voicing Errors:** Incorrect voicing decisions or F0 estimation can lead to significant artifacts.
*   **LPC Model Limitations:** The all-pole LPC model doesn't perfectly capture all speech production aspects, such as zeros (which are important for nasal sounds and fricatives).

---

### Practical Considerations and Implementation Details

*   **Frame Size and Overlap:** Typical frame sizes are 20-30 ms. Overlap is usually 50% or more to ensure smooth transitions between frames.
*   **Windowing:** Hamming, Hanning, or Blackman windows are applied to frames before analysis to reduce spectral leakage.
*   **LPC Analysis Methods:**
    *   **Autocorrelation Method:** Common, robust, and efficient.
    *   **Covariance Method:** More accurate but computationally heavier.
*   **Excitation Modeling:**
    *   **Impulse Train:** For voiced speech, synthesized by a series of impulses spaced by the pitch period.
    *   **White Noise:** For unvoiced speech.
    *   **More Complex Glottal Models:** E.g., Liljencrants' glottal pulse, Klatt's synthesizer models for more natural sounding speech.
*   **Post-processing:** Often, smoothing of estimated parameters or envelope shaping is applied to the resynthesized signal to improve naturalness.

### Practice Questions

**Question 1 (CO1, K1):**
Explain the fundamental principle of the source-filter model of speech production and how it relates to parametric resynthesis.

**Answer:**
The source-filter model posits that speech is generated by a sound source (like vocal cord vibration for voiced sounds or turbulent airflow for unvoiced sounds) which is then shaped by the resonant characteristics of the vocal tract. Parametric resynthesis leverages this by estimating parameters that describe both the source (e.g., pitch, voicing) and the filter (e.g., vocal tract resonances via LPC coefficients). These estimated parameters are then used in a vocoder to reconstruct or enhance the speech signal, effectively recreating the source-filter interaction.

---

**Question 2 (CO2, K2):**
What are the primary types of parameters that are typically estimated during the analysis stage of parametric resynthesis for speech enhancement? Briefly explain the role of each.

**Answer:**
The primary parameters estimated are:
1.  **Excitation Parameters:**
    *   **Fundamental Frequency (F0) / Pitch:** Determines the repetition rate of vocal cord vibration for voiced sounds, crucial for naturalness and prosody.
    *   **Voicing Decision:** Indicates whether a speech segment is voiced or unvoiced, dictating the type of excitation signal.
    *   **Excitation Signal Characteristics:** For voiced speech, the shape of the glottal pulse; for unvoiced speech, parameters describing the broadband noise source (e.g., spectral tilt).
2.  **Vocal Tract Filter Parameters:**
    *   **Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) Coefficients:** Represent the spectral envelope of the vocal tract, capturing its resonant frequencies (formants) and bandwidths.
    *   **Formant Frequencies and Bandwidths:** Directly relate to the poles of the LPC model and are key to vowel and consonant identity.

---

**Question 3 (CO3, K3):**
Describe a scenario where parametric resynthesis would be used for bandwidth extension. What specific parameters would be most important for this task?

**Answer:**
A scenario for bandwidth extension is improving the quality of a telephone conversation where the audio bandwidth is limited to 4 kHz, cutting off higher frequencies crucial for consonant clarity (e.g., /s/, /f/).

The most important parameters for bandwidth extension would be:
*   **LPC Coefficients (or Formant Frequencies/Bandwidths):** These capture the spectral envelope and the locations of vocal tract resonances. By analyzing the low-bandwidth signal, these parameters can be used to extrapolate the spectral envelope into the higher missing frequency bands.
*   **Fundamental Frequency (F0) and Voicing:** These are essential for the overall naturalness of the synthesized speech and help to correctly place the spectral content.

By accurately estimating these parameters from the limited bandwidth speech, a synthesizer can generate higher frequency components that mimic those that were originally present, thus extending the bandwidth.

---

**Question 4 (CO1, CO3, K1, K3):**
Discuss a limitation of using a simple LPC vocoder for parametric resynthesis in speech enhancement and how it might lead to unnatural-sounding speech.

**Answer:**
A key limitation of a simple LPC vocoder is its all-pole model of the vocal tract. The vocal tract also has **zeros**, particularly important for representing sounds like nasal consonants (which have both poles and zeros due to nasal cavities) and fricatives (where zeros and poles shape the noise spectrum). A purely all-pole model cannot accurately capture the spectral valleys (zeros) or the spectral fine structure of these sounds.

If parametric resynthesis relies solely on an LPC vocoder to enhance speech containing these sounds, the resynthesized output might sound distorted or lack the characteristic clarity of the original consonants. For example, the /s/ sound, which has significant energy in high frequencies shaped by spectral zeros and peaks, might be poorly reproduced, leading to a muffled or less intelligible /s/. This can result in unnatural-sounding speech and reduced intelligibility.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Parametric resynthesis is a two-step process:** Analysis (parameter estimation) and Synthesis (waveform generation).
*   **The Source-Filter model is the theoretical basis.**
*   **LPC coefficients are crucial for modeling the vocal tract.**
*   **F0 and voicing are critical for the excitation signal.**
*   **Accuracy of parameter estimation in noise is key to effective enhancement.**
*   **Parametric methods are used for both noise reduction and bandwidth extension.**
*   **Trade-offs exist between model simplicity, computational cost, and the naturalness of the resynthesized speech.**
