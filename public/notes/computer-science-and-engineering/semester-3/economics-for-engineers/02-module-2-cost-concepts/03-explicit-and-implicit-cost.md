## ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 2: Cost Concepts - Explicit and Implicit Cost

**Module:** 2. Cost Concepts
**Topic:** Explicit and Implicit Cost
**Description:** Understanding explicit and implicit costs is crucial for making informed engineering and business decisions. This topic explores the differences between these cost types and their impact on profitability analysis.

**Learning Outcomes:**

*   Define explicit and implicit costs and differentiate between them.
*   Identify examples of explicit and implicit costs in an engineering project or business scenario.
*   Explain the concept of opportunity cost and its relationship to implicit costs.
*   Calculate accounting profit and economic profit, considering both explicit and implicit costs.
*   Understand how explicit and implicit costs affect decision-making in engineering and business contexts.

---

**1. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Cost:** Resources sacrificed to achieve a specific objective. Costs can be tangible (money) or intangible (time, opportunities).

*   **Explicit Costs (Accounting Costs):**
    *   These are the out-of-pocket expenses or actual cash payments made by a firm for the use of resources.
    *   They are easily identifiable and can be tracked through accounting records.
    *   Examples include wages, rent, materials, utilities, depreciation (accounting depreciation), interest paid on loans, and marketing expenses.

*   **Implicit Costs (Economic Costs):**
    *   These represent the opportunity cost of using resources already owned by the firm. They are the value of the next best alternative foregone when using those resources.
    *   They do not involve direct cash payments, but they represent a real cost to the firm.
    *   Examples include the salary a business owner could have earned working for someone else (opportunity cost of their labor), the rent that could have been earned if the firm's building was leased out (opportunity cost of capital), and the return on investment that could have been earned if the firm's capital was invested elsewhere.

*   **Opportunity Cost:** The value of the best alternative foregone.  It's the potential benefit you miss out on when choosing one alternative over another.  Implicit costs are, by definition, opportunity costs.

*   **Accounting Profit:** Total Revenue - Explicit Costs.  This is what is typically reported in financial statements.

*   **Economic Profit:** Total Revenue - (Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs).  This takes into account the true cost of resources used and is a better indicator of economic success. A positive economic profit means the business is earning more than it could have earned by using its resources in their best alternative use.

---

**2. Differentiating Explicit and Implicit Costs**

| Feature        | Explicit Costs                                     | Implicit Costs                                                    |
|----------------|----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Nature         | Out-of-pocket expenses, cash payments              | Opportunity costs of using owned resources                       |
| Measurability  | Easily measurable, recorded in accounting books      | Difficult to measure, often estimated                             |
| Payment        | Involve direct payment                               | No direct payment involved                                          |
| Accounting    | Included in accounting profit calculations         | Not included in accounting profit calculations                    |
| Relevance      | Relevant for both accounting and economic decision-making | Crucially important for economic decision-making                |

**3. Examples of Explicit and Implicit Costs in Engineering Projects/Business Scenarios**

*   **Scenario: An Engineer Starting a Consulting Firm**

    *   **Explicit Costs:**
        *   Rent for office space
        *   Salary paid to employees
        *   Cost of software licenses
        *   Marketing expenses
        *   Cost of materials and supplies

    *   **Implicit Costs:**
        *   Salary the engineer could have earned working for a larger engineering firm (opportunity cost of their labor).
        *   Interest they could have earned if they had invested their startup capital in the stock market (opportunity cost of capital).
        *   Rent they could have received if they rented out a spare room in their house used as an office (opportunity cost of using their house as an office).

*   **Scenario:  A Manufacturing Company Considering Automation**

    *   **Explicit Costs:**
        *   Purchase price of the automated equipment
        *   Installation costs
        *   Training costs for employees
        *   Maintenance costs for the equipment
        *   Electricity to run the equipment

    *   **Implicit Costs:**
        *   Potential loss of employee morale and productivity if employees are concerned about job security (difficult to quantify).
        *   The lost interest income if the capital used to buy the equipment was instead invested elsewhere (opportunity cost of capital).

*   **Scenario:  A Software Developer Working on a Personal Project**

    *   **Explicit Costs:**
        *   Software licenses
        *   Cloud hosting fees
        *   Advertising costs (if any)

    *   **Implicit Costs:**
        *   The salary the developer could have earned working as a freelance developer during the time they worked on the project (opportunity cost of their labor).

**4. Calculating Accounting Profit and Economic Profit**

**Formulae:**

*   **Accounting Profit = Total Revenue - Explicit Costs**
*   **Economic Profit = Total Revenue - (Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs)**
*   **Economic Profit = Accounting Profit - Implicit Costs**

**Example:**

A small engineering firm has total revenue of $500,000 per year. Their explicit costs (salaries, rent, materials) are $300,000. The owner could earn $150,000 per year working for another firm.

*   **Accounting Profit = $500,000 - $300,000 = $200,000**
*   **Implicit Cost = $150,000 (Opportunity cost of the owner's labor)**
*   **Economic Profit = $500,000 - ($300,000 + $150,000) = $50,000**
*   **Economic Profit = $200,000 - $150,000 = $50,000**

In this case, while the accounting profit looks good at $200,000, the economic profit is only $50,000. This means the owner is only earning $50,000 more than they could have earned working for someone else.  If the economic profit was negative, the owner would be better off working elsewhere.

---

**5.  How Explicit and Implicit Costs Affect Decision-Making**

*   **Investment Decisions:** When considering an investment, it's crucial to consider not just the explicit costs (purchase price, operating expenses) but also the implicit costs (opportunity cost of capital, opportunity cost of management time). A project may look attractive based on accounting profit, but it might not be worthwhile if the economic profit is negative.

*   **Production Decisions:**  When deciding how much to produce, firms need to consider both explicit costs (raw materials, labor) and implicit costs (opportunity cost of using their factory space for one product instead of another).

*   **Pricing Decisions:** Firms must set prices that cover all costs, including explicit and implicit costs, to ensure long-term profitability and sustainability.

*   **Career Decisions:**  Engineers considering starting their own business need to weigh the explicit costs of running the business (rent, equipment) against the implicit cost of forgoing a stable salary and benefits at a larger company.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Economic profit provides a more accurate picture of profitability than accounting profit.**
*   **Opportunity cost is a crucial concept for understanding implicit costs.**
*   **Always consider both explicit and implicit costs when making business and engineering decisions.**
*   **Don't ignore implicit costs just because they aren't easily measurable.  Estimate them to the best of your ability.**

**6. Practice Questions/Exercises**

**Question 1:**

An engineer starts a consulting firm. Their revenue is $250,000 per year. Explicit costs (rent, salaries, utilities) are $150,000. The engineer could earn $80,000 per year working for another company.

a) Calculate the accounting profit.
b) Calculate the economic profit.
c) Should the engineer continue running the firm, based on economic profit?

**Answer:**

a) Accounting Profit = $250,000 - $150,000 = $100,000

b) Implicit Cost = $80,000 (Opportunity cost of the engineer's labor)
    Economic Profit = $250,000 - ($150,000 + $80,000) = $20,000
    Economic Profit = $100,000 - $80,000 = $20,000

c) Yes, the engineer should continue running the firm. Although the salary elsewhere is a good one, the business is profitable with an economic profit of $20,000, meaning they are earning $20,000 *more* than they could earn in their next best alternative.

**Question 2:**

A manufacturing company is considering buying a new machine that costs $50,000. They estimate it will increase revenue by $20,000 per year and have explicit operating costs of $8,000 per year. The company could invest the $50,000 in a bond yielding 5% per year.

a) Calculate the accounting profit from the machine.
b) Calculate the economic profit from the machine.
c) Should the company buy the machine, based on economic profit?

**Answer:**

a) Accounting Profit = $20,000 - $8,000 = $12,000

b) Implicit Cost = $50,000 * 0.05 = $2,500 (Opportunity cost of capital)
    Economic Profit = $20,000 - ($8,000 + $2,500) = $9,500
    Economic Profit = $12,000 - $2,500 = $9,500

c) Yes, the company should buy the machine. The economic profit is $9,500, which is higher than if they invested the money in the bond. This indicates the project is a worthwhile investment, providing a higher return than the alternative.

**Question 3:**

Explain the difference between accounting profit and economic profit. Which is a better measure of a firm's true profitability?

**Answer:**

Accounting profit is the difference between total revenue and explicit costs, representing the easily trackable cash flows of a business. Economic profit, on the other hand, considers both explicit and implicit costs (opportunity costs). Economic profit is a better measure of a firm's true profitability because it accounts for the full cost of resources used, including the value of the next best alternative foregone. It provides a more realistic assessment of whether a business is truly creating value.

**Question 4:**

Give three examples of implicit costs that a small business owner might face.

**Answer:**

1.  **Salary the owner could earn working elsewhere:** This represents the opportunity cost of the owner's time and skills.
2.  **Rent that could be earned from an owned building:** If the business uses a building owned by the owner, the potential rental income foregone is an implicit cost.
3.  **Interest that could be earned on invested capital:** If the owner invested personal funds in the business, the potential return on those funds in an alternative investment is an implicit cost.
