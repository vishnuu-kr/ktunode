# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - MODULE 3: MONETARY SYSTEM - Monetary System

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Describe the evolution of money from barter to modern fiat currency.
*   Explain the structure and functions of a central bank (using the Federal Reserve as an example).

## 1. Functions of Money and its Importance

*   **Definition:** Money is any generally accepted medium of exchange, a store of value, and a unit of account. It facilitates trade and economic activity.

*   **Functions of Money:**

    *   **Medium of Exchange:**  Money allows us to avoid the "double coincidence of wants" required in a barter system. Instead of directly exchanging goods for goods, we exchange goods for money, and then money for other goods.
        *   *Example:*  An engineer provides consulting services and receives money.  They then use that money to buy groceries, pay rent, etc.

    *   **Store of Value:** Money allows us to transfer purchasing power from the present to the future.  It holds its value over time (though inflation can erode that value).
        *   *Example:* An engineer saves money in a savings account for retirement.

    *   **Unit of Account:** Money provides a common measure of the value of goods and services. It allows us to compare prices and make rational economic decisions.
        *   *Example:*  Comparing the price of two different consulting services:  One charges $100/hour, the other charges $120/hour.

    *   **Standard of Deferred Payment:** Money enables borrowing and lending, allowing for transactions to occur over time.
        *   *Example:* Taking out a loan to start an engineering firm and repaying it over several years.

*   **Importance of Money:**

    *   **Facilitates Trade:**  Significantly reduces transaction costs compared to barter.
    *   **Encourages Specialization:**  Allows individuals and firms to focus on what they do best, leading to increased productivity.
    *   **Promotes Economic Growth:**  Facilitates investment, lending, and efficient resource allocation.
    *   **Enables Price Discovery:**  Money prices reflect relative scarcity and value, guiding resource allocation.

## 2. Evolution of Money

*   **Barter System:**  Direct exchange of goods and services. Inefficient due to the double coincidence of wants.

*   **Commodity Money:**  A good used as money that also has intrinsic value.
    *   *Examples:*  Gold, silver, salt, livestock, seashells.
    *   *Advantages:*  Intrinsically valuable, widely accepted.
    *   *Disadvantages:*  Can be bulky, difficult to transport, not always durable or divisible.

*   **Representative Money:**  Paper money backed by a commodity (e.g., gold standard).
    *   *Advantages:*  More convenient than commodity money.
    *   *Disadvantages:*  Requires trust in the issuing authority, vulnerable to runs on the bank (if people lose faith in the backing).

*   **Fiat Money:**  Money declared by a government to be legal tender. Its value is not based on any intrinsic value or backing.
    *   *Examples:*  U.S. Dollar, Euro, Yen.
    *   *Advantages:*  More flexible, easier to manage the money supply.
    *   *Disadvantages:*  Its value depends on faith in the government and monetary policy, susceptible to inflation if not managed properly.

## 3. Structure and Functions of a Central Bank (Federal Reserve Example)

*   **Definition:** A central bank is an independent government institution responsible for overseeing the monetary system, regulating banks, and controlling the money supply.

*   **Federal Reserve (The Fed):**  The central bank of the United States.

*   **Structure of the Fed:**

    *   **Board of Governors:**  Seven members appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.  Set monetary policy.
    *   **Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC):**  Composed of the Board of Governors and five Reserve Bank presidents.  Decides on open market operations (buying and selling government securities).
    *   **12 Federal Reserve Banks:**  Each serves a specific region of the U.S. They provide services to banks and the government.

*   **Functions of the Fed:**

    *   **Conducting Monetary Policy:**  Controlling the money supply and credit conditions to promote price stability (controlling inflation) and full employment.
    *   **Supervising and Regulating Banks:**  Ensuring the safety and soundness of the banking system.
    *   **Acting as a Lender of Last Resort:**  Providing emergency loans to banks during financial crises.
    *   **Providing Financial Services:**  Processing payments, transferring funds, and serving as the government's bank.

## 4. Money Supply and its Components (M1, M2, etc.)

*   **Definition:**  The money supply is the total amount of money circulating in an economy.

*   **Money Supply Aggregates:**  Different measures of the money supply, varying in liquidity.

    *   **M1:**  The most liquid form of money. Includes:
        *   Currency in circulation (coins and paper money)
        *   Demand deposits (checking accounts)
        *   Traveler's checks

    *   **M2:**  Includes M1 plus:
        *   Savings accounts
        *   Money market deposit accounts
        *   Small-denomination time deposits (CDs)
        *   Retail money market mutual funds

    *   **M3 (Less commonly used now):**  Includes M2 plus:
        *   Large-denomination time deposits
        *   Institutional money market funds
        *   Repurchase agreements
        *   Eurodollars

*   **Key Point:** The broader the definition of money, the less liquid it is.  M1 is the most liquid, while M3 (when used) is the least liquid.

## 5. Relationship between Money Supply, Inflation, and Interest Rates

*   **Money Supply and Inflation:**  In general, an increase in the money supply can lead to inflation if it grows faster than the economy's output.  This is explained by the *Quantity Theory of Money*:
    *   *Equation:* MV = PQ
        *   M = Money supply
        *   V = Velocity of money (how often money changes hands)
        *   P = Price level (inflation)
        *   Q = Quantity of goods and services produced (real GDP)

    *   Assuming V and Q are relatively stable in the short run, an increase in M will lead to an increase in P (inflation).

*   **Money Supply and Interest Rates:**  Changes in the money supply can influence interest rates.
    *   *Increased Money Supply:*  Typically leads to lower interest rates in the short run. More money available for lending reduces the cost of borrowing.
    *   *Decreased Money Supply:*  Typically leads to higher interest rates in the short run. Less money available for lending increases the cost of borrowing.
    *   *However,*  Inflation expectations can complicate this relationship. If people expect inflation to rise due to an increase in the money supply, lenders will demand higher interest rates to compensate for the loss of purchasing power.

*   **The Fisher Equation:**  Relates nominal interest rates, real interest rates, and inflation:

    *   *Equation:*  Nominal Interest Rate ≈ Real Interest Rate + Expected Inflation

## 6. Monetary Policy Tools and their Impact on the Economy

*   **Definition:** Monetary policy refers to actions taken by the central bank to manage the money supply and credit conditions to influence economic activity.

*   **Monetary Policy Tools of the Federal Reserve:**

    *   **Open Market Operations (OMO):**  Buying and selling U.S. government securities.
        *   *Buying Securities:*  Increases the money supply and lowers interest rates (expansionary policy).
        *   *Selling Securities:*  Decreases the money supply and raises interest rates (contractionary policy).
    *   **Discount Rate:**  The interest rate at which commercial banks can borrow money directly from the Fed.
        *   *Lowering the Discount Rate:*  Encourages banks to borrow more, increasing the money supply and lowering interest rates.
        *   *Raising the Discount Rate:*  Discourages banks from borrowing, decreasing the money supply and raising interest rates.
    *   **Reserve Requirements:**  The percentage of a bank's deposits that they are required to keep in reserve (either in their vault or on deposit at the Fed).
        *   *Lowering Reserve Requirements:*  Allows banks to lend out more money, increasing the money supply and lowering interest rates.
        *   *Raising Reserve Requirements:*  Forces banks to hold more money in reserve, decreasing the money supply and raising interest rates.
    *   **Interest on Reserves (IOR):** The interest rate the Fed pays to banks on the reserves they hold at the Fed.
         *   *Increasing IOR:*  Encourages banks to hold more reserves, decreasing the money supply and raising interest rates.
         *   *Decreasing IOR:*  Discourages banks from holding reserves, increasing the money supply and lowering interest rates.

*   **Impact of Monetary Policy on the Economy:**

    *   **Expansionary Monetary Policy (Lowering Interest Rates):**
        *   Encourages borrowing and investment.
        *   Increases aggregate demand.
        *   Leads to higher economic growth (potentially at the cost of higher inflation).
        *   Can decrease unemployment.
    *   **Contractionary Monetary Policy (Raising Interest Rates):**
        *   Discourages borrowing and investment.
        *   Decreases aggregate demand.
        *   Slows down economic growth.
        *   Helps to control inflation.
        *   Can increase unemployment.

## Practice Questions/Exercises:

1.  **Explain the difference between commodity money and fiat money. Give an example of each.**

    *   **Answer:** Commodity money has intrinsic value, like gold or silver. Fiat money is declared legal tender by the government and has no intrinsic value (e.g., the US dollar).

2.  **What are the three main functions of money? Explain each.**

    *   **Answer:**
        *   *Medium of Exchange:* Facilitates trade without requiring a double coincidence of wants.
        *   *Store of Value:* Allows purchasing power to be transferred to the future.
        *   *Unit of Account:* Provides a common measure of the value of goods and services.

3.  **Describe the structure of the Federal Reserve System.**

    *   **Answer:** The Fed has three main parts: the Board of Governors (7 members), the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), and 12 Federal Reserve Banks, each serving a specific region.

4.  **What is the FOMC, and what is its primary responsibility?**

    *   **Answer:** The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) is composed of the Board of Governors and five Reserve Bank presidents. Its primary responsibility is deciding on open market operations, which are the main tool for controlling the money supply.

5.  **What are the components of M1 and M2?**

    *   **Answer:**
        *   M1: Currency in circulation, demand deposits (checking accounts), and traveler's checks.
        *   M2: M1 plus savings accounts, money market deposit accounts, small-denomination time deposits (CDs), and retail money market mutual funds.

6.  **How does an increase in the money supply generally affect inflation?**

    *   **Answer:** An increase in the money supply, if it grows faster than the economy's output, generally leads to inflation, as there is more money chasing the same amount of goods and services.

7.  **How does the Fed use open market operations to influence the money supply and interest rates?**

    *   **Answer:**
        *   *Buying Securities:* Increases the money supply and lowers interest rates (expansionary policy).
        *   *Selling Securities:* Decreases the money supply and raises interest rates (contractionary policy).

8.  **What are the potential effects of an expansionary monetary policy on the economy?**

    *   **Answer:** Encourages borrowing and investment, increases aggregate demand, leads to higher economic growth, and can decrease unemployment. However, it also carries the risk of higher inflation.

9.  **A company is considering taking out a loan to expand its operations. The current interest rate is 6%. If the Fed announces it will be increasing the money supply through open market operations, what is the likely effect on the interest rate and how might that influence the company’s decision?**

    *   **Answer:**  Increasing the money supply through open market operations will likely lower the interest rate. This makes borrowing cheaper. This may influence the company’s decision positively, making them more likely to take out the loan because the cost of borrowing has decreased.

10. **Explain how the Fisher equation connects nominal interest rates, real interest rates, and inflation.**

    *   **Answer:**  The Fisher equation (Nominal Interest Rate ≈ Real Interest Rate + Expected Inflation) illustrates that the nominal interest rate compensates lenders for both the real return on investment and the expected loss of purchasing power due to inflation.

## Important Points to Remember:

*   Money is crucial for a modern economy because it facilitates trade and promotes economic growth.
*   Central banks play a vital role in managing the money supply and maintaining economic stability.
*   Monetary policy can have a significant impact on inflation, interest rates, and economic activity.
*   Understanding the relationship between money supply, inflation, and interest rates is essential for engineers who need to make informed financial decisions.
*   Fiat money's value is based on trust in the government and the central bank's ability to manage the money supply responsibly.
