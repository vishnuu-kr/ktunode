# NEXT GENERATION INTERACTION DESIGN - Module 2: User

## Topic: Contextual Inquiry for AR/VR

### Introduction

Contextual inquiry is a powerful user research method that involves observing and interviewing users in their natural environment while they perform tasks. For Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR) experiences, this method is particularly crucial because the "context" of interaction is inherently tied to the user's physical space, their actions within it, and the digital overlay or immersion provided by AR/VR. This topic explores how to effectively conduct contextual inquiries for AR/VR to gain deep insights into user needs, behaviors, and challenges.

---

### Learning Outcomes

This module aims to equip you with the knowledge and skills to:

*   **Understand the importance of contextual inquiry for AR/VR.**
*   **Identify key differences and considerations when applying contextual inquiry to AR/VR compared to traditional contexts.**
*   **Learn how to plan and prepare for a contextual inquiry session for AR/VR.**
*   **Master the techniques for conducting an AR/VR contextual inquiry, including observation and interviewing.**
*   **Analyze and interpret data gathered from AR/VR contextual inquiries.**
*   **Apply findings to inform the design of effective AR/VR experiences.**

### 1. Understanding the Importance of Contextual Inquiry for AR/VR

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Context-Specific Design:** AR/VR experiences are deeply embedded within the user's physical environment and their real-world tasks. Understanding this context is paramount for creating usable and effective experiences.
*   **Embodied Interaction:** AR/VR often involves physical movement, gestures, and manipulation of virtual objects within a real-world space. Contextual inquiry allows observation of these embodied interactions.
*   **Unforeseen Use Cases and Challenges:** Users may interact with AR/VR in ways designers didn't anticipate, or encounter physical or cognitive challenges related to the technology in their specific environment.
*   **Rich Qualitative Data:** Direct observation and interaction provide rich, nuanced data that goes beyond what can be gathered through surveys or lab-based usability testing alone.

**Why it's crucial for AR/VR:**

*   **Understanding Physical Environment:** AR/VR relies on the user's surroundings. Observing how users navigate, interact with physical objects, and how the AR/VR content integrates with their space is vital.
*   **Identifying Embodied Interaction Patterns:** Observing how users physically move, gesture, and interact with the AR/VR system in their natural environment reveals natural and efficient interaction patterns.
*   **Uncovering Environmental Constraints:** Light conditions, space limitations, distractions, and the presence of other people can significantly impact AR/VR use. Contextual inquiry helps identify these.
*   **Revealing Cognitive Load and Flow:** Observing users as they perform tasks in their real environment can reveal instances of cognitive overload, confusion, or seamless integration of the AR/VR experience.
*   **Validating Assumptions in Real-World Scenarios:** Design decisions made in a lab might not hold up in the dynamic reality of everyday use.

**Example:**
Imagine designing an AR application for warehouse workers to guide them through picking orders. A contextual inquiry in a real warehouse would reveal:
*   How workers navigate narrow aisles.
*   How they react to sudden forklift movements.
*   The difficulty of reading AR overlays in bright sunlight or dimly lit storage areas.
*   Whether the AR headset is comfortable to wear for extended periods while performing physical labor.

### 2. Key Differences and Considerations for AR/VR Contextual Inquiry

**Key Concepts:**

*   **"Context" Extends to Digital and Physical:** The context for AR/VR is a hybrid of the user's physical environment and the digital information/experience being presented.
*   **Observer's Role in the Hybrid Space:** The researcher is also a participant in the observed environment, needing to be aware of their own presence and potential impact.
*   **Ethical Considerations:** Privacy of the user's physical environment, data recording (video, audio), and ensuring comfort with the technology are heightened.
*   **Technology Integration Challenges:** The AR/VR device itself can be a point of friction. Observing how users wear, manage, and interact with the hardware is essential.
*   **Safety:** Ensuring the user's safety while they are potentially less aware of their physical surroundings due to AR/VR immersion is paramount.

**Specific Considerations for AR/VR:**

*   **Equipment Setup and Familiarity:** Users might need time to get accustomed to wearing AR/VR headsets. The inquiry might involve observing this setup and initial comfort.
*   **Field of View and Tracking Limitations:** Observe how users compensate for limited fields of view or tracking issues in their environment.
*   **Interaction with Physical Objects:** How do users naturally transition between interacting with virtual elements and real-world objects?
*   **Distractions and Interruptions:** How do real-world distractions (e.g., phone calls, people entering the space) affect AR/VR use?
*   **Annotation and Data Capture:** How to capture AR/VR specific data (e.g., what the user sees, their gaze, hand movements) in addition to environmental context.
*   **Researcher's Mobility and Observation Tools:** Researchers might need to move around the user and the environment. What tools (e.g., portable recorders, secondary cameras) are needed?

**Example:**
When observing a VR training simulation for surgeons, the researcher needs to consider:
*   The user's physical space in the operating room (or simulation lab) and any potential safety hazards.
*   The VR headset might impede the user's ability to communicate with a real supervisor.
*   The researcher might need to capture the user's hand movements (with motion controllers) and eye gaze data in addition to their verbal feedback.

### 3. Planning and Preparing for AR/VR Contextual Inquiry

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Define Research Goals:** What specific questions do you want to answer about the user and their interaction with AR/VR?
*   **Participant Selection:** Identify target users whose context is relevant to the AR/VR application.
*   **Scenario Design:** Create realistic tasks or scenarios that participants will perform using the AR/VR system.
*   **Logistics and Permissions:** Secure necessary access to locations, obtain permissions, and inform participants about the process.
*   **Data Collection Plan:** Decide what data to collect (observation notes, audio, video, AR/VR output) and how.
*   **Ethical Protocol:** Develop a clear consent form and ensure participant privacy.

**Steps for Planning:**

1.  **Define Clear Research Objectives:**
    *   What are you trying to learn? (e.g., "Understand how users navigate a complex building using AR guidance," "Identify usability issues with AR annotation tools in a manufacturing setting.")
2.  **Identify Target User Group and Context:**
    *   Who are the intended users?
    *   Where do they typically perform the tasks the AR/VR is intended for?
    *   What are the key characteristics of that environment?
3.  **Develop Realistic Scenarios/Tasks:**
    *   Design tasks that mimic real-world usage.
    *   Ensure tasks are specific enough to elicit observable behaviors and allow for detailed questions.
    *   **Example Scenario:** For an AR instruction manual for assembling furniture, a task could be: "Assemble the main frame of the coffee table using the AR guide."
4.  **Select Appropriate AR/VR Hardware and Software:**
    *   Choose the specific AR/VR devices and applications that will be used.
    *   Ensure the technology is functional and suitable for the planned tasks.
5.  **Create a Detailed Interview Guide (Flexible):**
    *   Prepare open-ended questions to probe user actions and thoughts.
    *   Include questions about their experience with the technology, their environment, and their task.
    *   **Example Questions:** "What are you seeing on the AR display right now?", "How does this overlay help you with this step?", "What challenges are you facing in this environment?"
6.  **Plan Data Collection Methods:**
    *   **Observation Notes:** Detailed notes on user actions, environmental factors, and interactions.
    *   **Audio Recording:** Capture user comments and researcher-user dialogue.
    *   **Video Recording:** Record the user's actions, their view (if possible via screen recording or forward-facing camera), and the AR/VR overlay.
    *   **AR/VR System Output:** Consider capturing the AR/VR application's data stream (e.g., gaze data, interaction logs, what the user is seeing).
7.  **Develop Consent Forms and Ethical Guidelines:**
    *   Clearly explain the purpose of the study, how data will be used, and ensure confidentiality.
    *   Get informed consent from participants.
    *   Consider the privacy of any other individuals present or captured in video recordings.
8.  **Logistics and Scheduling:**
    *   Arrange access to the location.
    *   Schedule sessions with participants, allowing ample time.
    *   Consider travel time and potential setup needs.
9.  **Pilot Test the Process:**
    *   Conduct a practice session with a colleague or a non-participant to refine scenarios, questions, and data collection methods.

### 4. Conducting an AR/VR Contextual Inquiry

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Apprenticeship Model:** The researcher acts as an apprentice, learning from the user's expertise in their domain.
*   **Partnership Model:** A collaborative approach where the researcher and user work together, with the user explaining their actions and the researcher asking probing questions.
*   **Active Listening and Probing:** Pay close attention to what the user says and does, and ask follow-up questions to clarify their thoughts and motivations.
*   **Non-Interruption (when appropriate):** Observe without interrupting unless clarification is essential, but be ready to engage when the user appears to be struggling or seeking guidance.
*   **Capturing the "Flow":** Understand the overall workflow and how the AR/VR fits into it.

**Techniques During the Session:**

1.  **Introduction and Setup:**
    *   Greet the participant and thank them for their time.
    *   Briefly reiterate the purpose of the study.
    *   Assist with or observe the setup of AR/VR hardware, ensuring comfort and functionality.
    *   **Researcher's Role:** Be supportive and minimize technical jargon.
2.  **Observation Phase:**
    *   Begin by observing the participant as they perform their tasks within their natural environment.
    *   Take detailed notes, focusing on:
        *   **User Actions:** What are they doing? What movements are they making? What gestures are they using?
        *   **AR/VR Interaction:** How are they interacting with the AR/VR interface? What buttons are they pressing? What voice commands are they using?
        *   **Environmental Factors:** Lighting, space, presence of others, distractions, physical objects.
        *   **User's State:** Expressions, posture, signs of confusion or satisfaction.
3.  **Interviewing Phase (Integrated or Separate):**
    *   **"Think Aloud" Protocol:** Encourage the user to verbalize their thoughts and feelings as they work.
    *   **Probing Questions:** Ask open-ended questions to understand *why* they are doing something, what they expect to happen, and what they are experiencing.
        *   "What were you thinking when you looked at that AR marker?"
        *   "How did that feedback make you feel?"
        *   "Is this how you usually approach this task, or is the AR changing your process?"
    *   **Clarification:** If the user does something unexpected, gently ask for clarification.
    *   **Focus on the "How" and "Why":** Don't just record what they do, but try to understand their reasoning and decision-making.
4.  **Handling AR/VR Specifics:**
    *   **Gaze and Attention:** If tracking is available, note where their gaze is directed. Ask them to confirm what they are focusing on.
    *   **Field of View (FOV) Issues:** If they seem to miss something, ask if they expected to see it or if they are used to looking in a particular area.
    *   **Interaction with Physical Objects:** Observe how they naturally switch between interacting with virtual elements and their physical surroundings.
    *   **Safety Checks:** Periodically ensure the user is aware of their physical surroundings and is safe.
5.  **Concluding the Session:**
    *   Thank the participant again.
    *   Ask any final wrap-up questions.
    *   Briefly debrief any key observations (without leading the participant).
    *   Confirm how the collected data will be used.

**Example of an Interview Snippet during an AR navigation task:**

*   **Researcher:** (Observing the user stop and look around) "What are you looking for right now?"
*   **User:** "I'm trying to find the next junction. The AR arrow disappeared when I turned this corner."
*   **Researcher:** "Okay. And what were you expecting to see when you turned the corner?"
*   **User:** "I expected the arrow to update and point down this new aisle."
*   **Researcher:** "Got it. And how does that missing arrow affect how you're navigating?"
*   **User:** "It makes me pause and have to re-orient myself, which slows me down a bit."

### 5. Analyzing and Interpreting Data from AR/VR Contextual Inquiries

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Affinity Diagramming:** A method for organizing large amounts of qualitative data into themes and patterns.
*   **Pattern Identification:** Look for recurring behaviors, challenges, insights, and preferences.
*   **User Journey Mapping:** Visualize the user's experience throughout the task, highlighting pain points and moments of delight.
*   **Storytelling:** Craft narratives that illustrate key findings and convey the user's experience effectively.
*   **Triangulation:** Compare findings from different data sources (observation notes, interviews, video) to validate insights.

**Steps for Analysis:**

1.  **Transcribe Recordings:** Convert all audio and video recordings into text.
2.  **Organize Raw Data:**
    *   Group notes, interview transcripts, and video clips by participant and task.
    *   If you captured AR/VR system data (e.g., gaze logs), organize that alongside.
3.  **Create an Affinity Diagram:**
    *   Write individual observations, quotes, or insights on sticky notes.
    *   Cluster similar notes together to form categories and themes.
    *   **AR/VR Specific Themes might include:**
        *   FOV limitations impacting task flow.
        *   Challenges with physical object occlusion of AR elements.
        *   Comfort issues with headset wear.
        *   Effectiveness of AR guidance in specific lighting.
        *   User adoption of virtual gestures.
        *   Integration of virtual information with physical cues.
4.  **Identify Key Themes and Patterns:**
    *   What are the most frequent observations?
    *   What are the most critical pain points or delightful moments?
    *   Are there distinct patterns of behavior across different users or contexts?
5.  **Develop User Personas or Archetypes:**
    *   Create representative profiles of your target users based on the gathered data, highlighting their needs, goals, behaviors, and context.
6.  **Create User Journey Maps:**
    *   Map out the user's steps during a specific task, noting their actions, thoughts, feelings, and any AR/VR interactions or challenges at each stage.
7.  **Synthesize Findings and Extract Insights:**
    *   Move beyond simply describing what happened to explaining what it means for the design.
    *   What are the underlying user needs or problems?
    *   What opportunities exist for innovation?
8.  **Prioritize Findings:**
    *   Rank the identified issues and opportunities based on their impact on the user experience and business goals.

**Example of an Insight:**
*   **Raw Observation:** "User repeatedly had to lift AR headset to see their physical surroundings clearly when talking to colleagues."
*   **Affinity Cluster:** "Interference with Communication," "Physical Comfort," "Workflow Disruption."
*   **Synthesized Insight:** "Current AR headset design hinders natural social interaction and requires frequent physical adjustments, disrupting workflow and leading to user fatigue. This suggests a need for improved form factors or more integrated communication features."

### 6. Applying Findings to Inform AR/VR Design

**Key Concepts:**

*   **User-Centered Design:** Ensure all design decisions are driven by the insights gained from user research.
*   **Iterative Design:** Use findings to refine existing designs and inform future iterations.
*   **Prototyping and Testing:** Create prototypes based on insights and test them with users.
*   **Design Recommendations:** Translate research findings into actionable design guidelines.

**How to Apply Findings:**

1.  **Prioritize Design Interventions:**
    *   Focus on addressing the most critical user pain points and capitalizing on identified opportunities.
2.  **Inform Design Requirements:**
    *   Translate insights into specific design requirements for the AR/VR experience.
    *   **Example:** If users struggled with AR overlay occlusion by their hands, a requirement might be: "AR elements must be designed to avoid overlap with the user's primary interaction hand."
3.  **Refine Interaction Design:**
    *   Adjust UI elements, gestures, voice commands, and feedback mechanisms based on observed user behavior and preferences.
    *   **Example:** If users consistently performed a specific gesture to access a menu, incorporate that gesture into the design.
4.  **Improve Information Architecture:**
    *   Determine how and when AR/VR information is presented, ensuring it aligns with user tasks and cognitive load.
    *   **Example:** If users found AR prompts too frequent, consider batching them or making them contextually triggered.
5.  **Address Environmental Factors:**
    *   Design for varying lighting conditions, spatial constraints, and potential distractions.
    *   **Example:** If users struggled with AR visibility in bright sunlight, design interfaces with high contrast or provide alternative display modes.
6.  **Iterate on Hardware/Form Factor:**
    *   Provide feedback on the usability of the AR/VR hardware itself, influencing future hardware selection or design.
7.  **Create Design Guidelines:**
    *   Develop best practices and guidelines for designing AR/VR experiences based on the study's outcomes.

**Example of Applying an Insight:**
*   **Insight:** "Users found it difficult to maintain focus on AR instructions while also performing precise physical tasks requiring dexterity."
*   **Design Recommendation:**
    *   **Reduce visual clutter:** Simplify the AR interface, providing only essential information at each step.
    *   **Augment with audio cues:** Supplement visual instructions with clear, concise audio cues for critical actions.
    *   **Use spatial anchoring:** Anchor virtual elements more robustly to physical objects to reduce perceptual drift.
    *   **Prototyping:** Develop a prototype with simplified AR and robust audio cues and test it against the original design to measure improvement in task completion time and accuracy.

### Practice Questions/Exercises

**Question 1:**
You are designing an AR application to assist maintenance technicians in repairing complex machinery. You plan to conduct contextual inquiries. What are two key differences you need to consider compared to conducting a contextual inquiry for a mobile app, and why are they important for AR?

**Answer 1:**
1.  **Physical Environment Integration:** For AR, the physical environment is not just a backdrop but an active part of the interaction. You need to consider how the AR overlay integrates with the machinery, the workspace layout, lighting conditions, and potential hazards. This is crucial because AR content must be contextually relevant and not impede physical tasks or safety.
2.  **Embodied Interaction and Hardware Wearability:** AR involves the user wearing hardware (e.g., headset or glasses) and interacting through physical movements and gestures. You need to observe how comfortable the hardware is, how users move with it, and how they naturally use their hands for both physical tasks and virtual interactions. This is important to ensure the technology enhances, rather than hinders, the user's ability to perform their job.

**Question 2:**
During a contextual inquiry for a VR training simulation for firefighters, you observe a participant hesitating before a simulated fire rescue. They seem confused about which tool to pick up. What is an effective probing question you could ask to understand their confusion, and what type of data would you aim to capture?

**Answer 2:**
*   **Effective Probing Question:** "What are you thinking about right now as you consider which tool to pick up?" or "What information are you looking for that might help you decide?" (These encourage the user to verbalize their thought process and the missing information.)
*   **Data to Capture:**
    *   **Verbalization:** The participant's spoken thoughts and answer to your question.
    *   **Visual Gaze:** If VR tracking is available, record where their gaze is directed to see if they are looking at available tools or at the simulated victim.
    *   **Hand/Controller Movement:** Observe which virtual tools they reach for or hesitate over.
    *   **Environmental Context:** Note the details of the simulated scene (e.g., the intensity of the simulated fire, the position of the victim).

**Question 3:**
After conducting several contextual inquiries for an AR application guiding tourists through a historical city, you've gathered a lot of notes. You want to synthesize these findings. Describe the process of using affinity diagramming to identify key themes.

**Answer 3:**
1.  **Extract Individual Ideas:** Write each distinct observation, insight, or user quote on a separate sticky note.
2.  **Initial Clustering:** Start grouping similar sticky notes together. Don't worry too much about broad categories initially; focus on what naturally fits together. For example, notes about difficulty reading text in sunlight would go together.
3.  **Develop Higher-Level Themes:** Once you have clusters, start creating overarching themes for those clusters. For instance, "Visibility Issues," "Navigation Challenges," "Interaction Friction," "Information Overload."
4.  **Refine and Iterate:** Review the clusters and themes. Are they distinct? Do some notes belong in multiple places? Rename themes for clarity. Continue rearranging until you have a coherent and meaningful organization of your findings. This process reveals recurring patterns and pain points across your participants.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Context is King:** AR/VR design is inseparable from the user's environment and their real-world activities.
*   **Embrace the Hybrid:** Understand that the context is a blend of physical and digital elements.
*   **Be an Apprentice:** Approach the user as the expert in their domain.
*   **Observe, Then Probe:** Let users perform tasks naturally before asking detailed questions.
*   **Focus on the "Why":** Go beyond describing behavior to understanding motivations and thought processes.
*   **Safety First:** Always prioritize the participant's physical safety during the inquiry.
*   **Data is Rich, but Needs Synthesis:** Collect diverse data (notes, audio, video) and use structured methods like affinity diagramming to extract actionable insights.
*   **Translate Insights into Design:** The ultimate goal is to use these findings to create better AR/VR experiences.
