## ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS: MODULE 2 - COST CONCEPTS - OLIGOPOLY

**Topic: Oligopoly (Features and Equilibrium of a Firm)**

**Learning Outcomes:**

*   Understand the key features of an oligopoly market structure.
*   Explain the interdependence of firms in an oligopoly.
*   Analyze the concept of a kinked demand curve and its implications for price stability.
*   Describe different models of oligopoly (Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg).
*   Determine the equilibrium output and price for a firm in an oligopoly.
*   Understand the role of collusion in oligopolistic markets.
*   Evaluate the efficiency and welfare implications of oligopoly.

**1. Introduction to Oligopoly**

*   **Definition:** An oligopoly is a market structure characterized by a small number of firms that have significant market power. These firms are interdependent, meaning that the actions of one firm significantly affect the actions and profits of other firms in the market.

*   **Key Characteristics:**

    *   **Few Sellers:** A small number of firms dominate the market.  Examples: Mobile phone carriers (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile), airplane manufacturers (Boeing, Airbus), automotive industry (historically, the Big Three).
    *   **Interdependence:**  Firms are highly aware of and responsive to each other's actions (pricing, output, advertising). This is the *defining* feature.
    *   **Barriers to Entry:** Significant barriers prevent new firms from entering the market, maintaining the dominance of existing firms.  Barriers can include:
        *   **High Capital Costs:**  Significant investment required to start operations.
        *   **Economies of Scale:** Existing firms have lower average costs due to their large size, making it difficult for new firms to compete.
        *   **Patents:**  Exclusive rights to produce a specific product or use a particular process.
        *   **Brand Loyalty:**  Existing firms have established strong brand names and customer loyalty.
        *   **Government Regulations:** Licenses or permits that are difficult to obtain.
    *   **Homogeneous or Differentiated Products:**  Firms may produce standardized products (e.g., steel) or differentiated products (e.g., automobiles).  Product differentiation helps firms gain market share and charge premium prices.
    *   **Non-Price Competition:** Due to interdependence, firms often avoid direct price competition, opting instead for non-price strategies such as advertising, product differentiation, and customer service.

**2. Interdependence and Strategic Interaction**

*   **Game Theory:** Oligopoly is often analyzed using game theory, which studies strategic decision-making in situations where the outcome of one player's actions depends on the actions of other players.

*   **Strategies:** Firms must consider the likely responses of their rivals when making decisions about:
    *   **Pricing:** Will rivals match a price cut? Will they retaliate with their own price cuts?
    *   **Output:**  How much should the firm produce, considering the output of its rivals?
    *   **Advertising:** How much should the firm spend on advertising to gain market share?
    *   **Product Development:**  What new products should the firm develop to stay ahead of the competition?

*   **Examples:** Consider two airlines, Airline A and Airline B, competing on a specific route.
    *   If Airline A lowers its prices, Airline B must decide whether to match the price cut (to maintain market share) or not (to avoid a price war that could reduce profits for both).
    *   If Airline A introduces a new, more comfortable seating option, Airline B must decide whether to invest in similar improvements to remain competitive.

**3. The Kinked Demand Curve Model**

*   **Purpose:**  Explains price rigidity (stability) often observed in oligopolistic markets.

*   **Assumptions:**
    *   If a firm *raises* its price, other firms will *not* follow suit (they will gain market share). This leads to a *highly elastic* demand curve above the current price.
    *   If a firm *lowers* its price, other firms *will* match the price cut (to avoid losing market share).  This leads to a *relatively inelastic* demand curve below the current price.

*   **The Kink:** The demand curve has a kink at the current market price.

*   **Marginal Revenue:** The marginal revenue (MR) curve has a vertical discontinuity at the quantity corresponding to the kink in the demand curve. This discontinuity implies that the firm can experience significant changes in costs without changing its price or output.

*   **Explanation of Price Rigidity:**  If marginal cost (MC) fluctuates within the range of the MR discontinuity, the firm will not change its price or output because the optimal output level (where MC = MR) remains the same.  Only a large shift in MC will induce a change in price.

*   **Limitations:** The kinked demand curve model *explains* price rigidity, but it does *not explain* how the initial price is determined. It's a model of price maintenance, not price determination.

**4. Oligopoly Models: Cournot, Bertrand, and Stackelberg**

These models provide frameworks for understanding how firms in an oligopoly make output and pricing decisions.

*   **Cournot Model (Quantity Competition):**

    *   **Assumptions:**
        *   Firms choose their output levels simultaneously.
        *   Each firm assumes that its rivals' output levels are fixed.
        *   Firms produce a homogeneous product.
        *   Each firm attempts to maximize its profit by choosing its output level, given its beliefs about the other firm's output.
    *   **Reaction Functions:** Each firm has a reaction function that shows its optimal output level as a function of its rivals' output level.
    *   **Equilibrium:** The Cournot equilibrium is the point where the reaction functions of all firms intersect. At this point, each firm is producing its best output level, given the output levels of its rivals.
    *   **Result:**  The total output is greater than in a monopoly but less than in perfect competition.  The price is lower than in a monopoly but higher than in perfect competition.

    **Example:** Two firms, Firm A and Firm B, are the only producers of a homogeneous product.  Market demand is P = 100 - Q, where Q = QA + QB. Both firms have a marginal cost of $10.

    1.  **Find the reaction functions:**
        *   Firm A's profit: πA = (100 - QA - QB)QA - 10QA
        *   Maximize πA with respect to QA: dπA/dQA = 100 - 2QA - QB - 10 = 0
        *   Firm A's reaction function: QA = (90 - QB)/2
        *   Similarly, Firm B's reaction function: QB = (90 - QA)/2
    2.  **Find the equilibrium:**
        *   Solve the reaction functions simultaneously:  QA = (90 - (90 - QA)/2)/2  => QA = 30
        *   QB = (90 - 30)/2 = 30
    3.  **Equilibrium Price and Output:**
        *   Q = QA + QB = 30 + 30 = 60
        *   P = 100 - 60 = $40

*   **Bertrand Model (Price Competition):**

    *   **Assumptions:**
        *   Firms choose their prices simultaneously.
        *   Firms produce a homogeneous product.
        *   Consumers will buy from the firm with the lowest price.
        *   If firms charge the same price, they split the market equally.
    *   **Equilibrium:** The Bertrand equilibrium is where both firms charge a price equal to their marginal cost (P = MC). This is the same outcome as in perfect competition.
    *   **Bertrand Paradox:**  The Bertrand model predicts that even with only two firms, the outcome will be perfectly competitive, which is often not observed in reality.
    *   **Why?** The assumption of perfectly homogeneous products and perfect information is unrealistic.

    **Example:**  Using the same example as above, but firms compete on price. If Firm A sets a price of $39.99, Firm B will undercut it with $39.98 to capture the entire market. This undercutting continues until both firms reach marginal cost ($10).  Therefore, P = $10, and the firm with the capacity will supply the entire market.

*   **Stackelberg Model (Sequential Quantity Competition):**

    *   **Assumptions:**
        *   One firm (the leader) chooses its output level first.
        *   The other firm (the follower) observes the leader's output level and then chooses its own output level.
    *   **First-Mover Advantage:** The leader has a first-mover advantage because it can influence the follower's output decision.
    *   **Equilibrium:** The leader anticipates the follower's reaction function and chooses its output level to maximize its profit, taking into account the follower's response.
    *   **Result:** The leader produces more output and earns higher profit than the follower. The total output is greater, and the price is lower than in the Cournot equilibrium.

    **Example:**  Continuing the example, assume Firm A is the leader.

    1.  **Follower's reaction function (Firm B):** QB = (90 - QA)/2  (same as Cournot)
    2.  **Leader's profit maximization (Firm A):**
        *   πA = (100 - QA - QB)QA - 10QA
        *   Substitute Firm B's reaction function:  πA = (100 - QA - (90 - QA)/2)QA - 10QA
        *   πA = (100 - QA - 45 + QA/2)QA - 10QA = (55 - QA/2)QA - 10QA
        *   Maximize πA: dπA/dQA = 55 - QA - 10 = 0 => QA = 45
    3.  **Follower's output (Firm B):** QB = (90 - 45)/2 = 22.5
    4.  **Equilibrium Price and Output:**
        *   Q = QA + QB = 45 + 22.5 = 67.5
        *   P = 100 - 67.5 = $32.5

**5. Collusion**

*   **Definition:**  An agreement among firms in an oligopoly to restrict output, raise prices, or divide the market.

*   **Types of Collusion:**
    *   **Explicit Collusion (Cartels):**  Formal agreements among firms to coordinate their actions.  Often illegal (e.g., OPEC).
    *   **Tacit Collusion:** Informal understanding among firms to cooperate without explicit communication.  More difficult to detect and prosecute.  *Price leadership* is a form of tacit collusion, where one firm sets the price and the others follow.
    *   **Coordination:**  Firms may coordinate their actions through industry associations or trade groups.

*   **Motivation for Collusion:** To increase profits by acting like a monopoly.

*   **Difficulties of Collusion:**
    *   **Incentive to Cheat:** Each firm has an incentive to cheat on the agreement by producing more output or lowering its price.
    *   **Enforcement Problems:** Difficult to monitor and enforce collusive agreements, especially tacit agreements.
    *   **Antitrust Laws:**  Collusion is illegal in many countries, making it risky for firms to engage in.
    *   **Entry of New Firms:** Successful collusion may attract new firms to enter the market, undermining the collusive agreement.

**6. Efficiency and Welfare Implications of Oligopoly**

*   **Compared to Perfect Competition:**
    *   **Higher Prices:** Oligopolies typically charge higher prices than perfectly competitive markets.
    *   **Lower Output:** Oligopolies produce less output than perfectly competitive markets.
    *   **Deadweight Loss:** Oligopolies create a deadweight loss, representing a loss of economic efficiency due to the underproduction of goods and services.
*   **Compared to Monopoly:**
    *   **Lower Prices:** Oligopolies may charge lower prices than monopolies, especially if firms are competing fiercely.
    *   **Higher Output:** Oligopolies may produce more output than monopolies.
*   **Potential Benefits of Oligopoly:**
    *   **Innovation:**  Firms in oligopolies may have more resources to invest in research and development, leading to innovation. The threat of competition may incentivize innovation.
    *   **Product Variety:**  Firms may offer a wider variety of products to cater to different consumer tastes.
    *   **Economies of Scale:**  Oligopolistic firms may be able to achieve economies of scale, lowering production costs.
*   **Overall Assessment:** The welfare implications of oligopoly are ambiguous. While oligopolies may generate some benefits, they also tend to be less efficient than perfectly competitive markets.

**7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Interdependence is Key:** The interdependence of firms is the defining characteristic of an oligopoly.
*   **Strategic Thinking:** Firms must think strategically and anticipate the actions of their rivals.
*   **No Single Model:**  There is no single, universally accepted model of oligopoly. Different models (Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg) provide different insights.
*   **Collusion is Unstable:** Collusive agreements are difficult to maintain due to the incentive to cheat and antitrust laws.
*   **Welfare Implications are Mixed:** Oligopoly can be less efficient than perfect competition but can also generate benefits through innovation and product variety.

5.  **Two firms compete in a Cournot duopoly. The market demand curve is P = 100 - Q, where Q = q1 + q2. Firm 1's marginal cost is MC1 = 10, and Firm 2's marginal cost is MC2 = 20. Find the Cournot equilibrium quantities, price, and profits for each firm.**

    *   **Answer:**

        1.  *Find Reaction Functions:*
            *   Firm 1 Profit: π1 = (100 - q1 - q2)q1 - 10q1
            *   FOC: dπ1/dq1 = 100 - 2q1 - q2 - 10 = 0 => q1 = (90 - q2)/2
            *   Firm 2 Profit: π2 = (100 - q1 - q2)q2 - 20q2
            *   FOC: dπ2/dq2 = 100 - q1 - 2q2 - 20 = 0 => q2 = (80 - q1)/2

        2.  *Solve for Equilibrium Quantities:*
            *   Substitute Firm 2's reaction function into Firm 1's: q1 = (90 - (80 - q1)/2)/2
            *   Solving for q1: q1 = 40/3 = 13.33
            *   q2 = (80 - 40/3)/2 = 100/3 = 33.33

        3.  *Find Equilibrium Price:*
            *   Q = q1 + q2 = 40/3 + 100/3 = 140/3 = 46.67
            *   P = 100 - Q = 100 - 140/3 = 160/3 = 53.33

        4.  *Find Profits:*
            *   π1 = (P - MC1)q1 = (53.33 - 10) * 13.33 = 43.33 * 13.33 = 577.64
            *   π2 = (P - MC2)q2 = (53.33 - 20) * 33.33 = 33.33 * 33.33 = 1110.89
