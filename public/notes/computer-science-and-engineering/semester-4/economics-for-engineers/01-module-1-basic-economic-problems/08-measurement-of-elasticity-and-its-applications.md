## ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 1: Basic Economic Problems
### Topic: Measurement of Elasticity and Its Applications

**Learning Outcomes:**

**1. Introduction to Elasticity**

*   **Definition:** Elasticity measures the responsiveness of one variable to a change in another. In economics, it primarily refers to the sensitivity of quantity demanded or supplied to changes in factors like price, income, or the price of related goods.
*   **Importance:** Understanding elasticity is crucial for making informed decisions about pricing, production, and resource allocation. Engineers can use it in project feasibility studies, demand forecasting and resource management.

**2. Types of Elasticity**

*   **2.1 Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)**
    *   **Definition:** Measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded of a good or service to a change in its price.
    *   **Formula:** PED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Price)
    *   **Types of PED:**
        *   **Elastic Demand (PED > 1):** Quantity demanded is highly responsive to price changes. A small price change leads to a larger percentage change in quantity demanded. E.g., Luxury items.
        *   **Inelastic Demand (PED < 1):** Quantity demanded is not very responsive to price changes. A price change leads to a smaller percentage change in quantity demanded. E.g., Essential goods like medicine.
        *   **Unit Elastic Demand (PED = 1):** Percentage change in quantity demanded is equal to the percentage change in price.
        *   **Perfectly Elastic Demand (PED = ∞):** Any price increase will lead to zero demand.  (Horizontal Demand Curve). E.g., theoretically, if a competitor sells identical products.
        *   **Perfectly Inelastic Demand (PED = 0):** Quantity demanded does not change regardless of price changes. (Vertical Demand Curve).  E.g., Life-saving drugs in the short run.

*   **2.2 Income Elasticity of Demand (YED)**
    *   **Definition:** Measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded of a good or service to a change in consumer income.
    *   **Formula:** YED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Income)
    *   **Types of YED:**
        *   **Normal Goods (YED > 0):** As income increases, demand increases.
            *   **Luxury Goods (YED > 1):** Demand increases more than proportionally with income increase.
            *   **Necessity Goods (0 < YED < 1):** Demand increases less than proportionally with income increase.
        *   **Inferior Goods (YED < 0):** As income increases, demand decreases. E.g., generic brands, used cars.

*   **2.3 Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand (CPED)**
    *   **Definition:** Measures the responsiveness of the quantity demanded of one good to a change in the price of another good.
    *   **Formula:** CPED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded of Good A) / (% Change in Price of Good B)
    *   **Types of CPED:**
        *   **Substitutes (CPED > 0):** As the price of Good B increases, demand for Good A increases. E.g., Tea and Coffee.
        *   **Complements (CPED < 0):** As the price of Good B increases, demand for Good A decreases. E.g., Cars and Gasoline.
        *   **Independent Goods (CPED = 0):** The price of Good B has no effect on the demand for Good A. E.g., Bread and Shoes.

*   **2.4 Price Elasticity of Supply (PES)**
    *   **Definition:** Measures the responsiveness of quantity supplied of a good or service to a change in its price.
    *   **Formula:** PES = (% Change in Quantity Supplied) / (% Change in Price)
    *   **Types of PES:**
        *   **Elastic Supply (PES > 1):** Quantity supplied is highly responsive to price changes.
        *   **Inelastic Supply (PES < 1):** Quantity supplied is not very responsive to price changes.
        *   **Unit Elastic Supply (PES = 1):** Percentage change in quantity supplied is equal to the percentage change in price.
        *   **Perfectly Elastic Supply (PES = ∞):** Any price decrease will lead to zero supply.
        *   **Perfectly Inelastic Supply (PES = 0):** Quantity supplied does not change regardless of price changes.  (Vertical Supply Curve). E.g., supply of historical artifacts.

**3. Methods for Measuring Elasticity**

*   **3.1 Point Elasticity:**
    *   **Definition:** Measures elasticity at a specific point on the demand or supply curve.
    *   **Formula for PED:** PED = (dQ/dP) * (P/Q)
        *   Where:
            *   dQ/dP = the derivative of quantity demanded with respect to price (slope of the demand curve at that point)
            *   P = price at the point
            *   Q = quantity demanded at the point
    *   **Use:**  Useful when analyzing small changes in price and quantity.

*   **3.2 Arc Elasticity:**
    *   **Definition:** Measures elasticity over a range or arc on the demand or supply curve.  It averages the prices and quantities.
    *   **Formula for PED:** PED = [(Q2 - Q1) / ((Q1 + Q2)/2)] / [(P2 - P1) / ((P1 + P2)/2)]
        *   Where:
            *   Q1 = initial quantity demanded
            *   Q2 = final quantity demanded
            *   P1 = initial price
            *   P2 = final price
    *   **Use:** More accurate than point elasticity when analyzing large changes in price and quantity. Avoids the problem of different elasticity values depending on the direction of the change.

**4. Elasticity and Revenue**

*   **Total Revenue (TR):** TR = Price (P) x Quantity (Q)
*   **Impact of PED on Total Revenue:**
    *   **Elastic Demand (PED > 1):**
        *   If Price Increases: TR Decreases
        *   If Price Decreases: TR Increases
    *   **Inelastic Demand (PED < 1):**
        *   If Price Increases: TR Increases
        *   If Price Decreases: TR Decreases
    *   **Unit Elastic Demand (PED = 1):**
        *   TR is maximized. Price changes do not change total revenue.
*   **Implications for Business Decisions:**
    *   If demand is elastic, businesses should consider lowering prices to increase total revenue.
    *   If demand is inelastic, businesses can increase prices to increase total revenue without significantly affecting quantity demanded.

**5. Factors Affecting Elasticity**

*   **5.1 Factors Affecting Price Elasticity of Demand:**
    *   **Availability of Substitutes:** More substitutes mean higher elasticity.
    *   **Necessity vs. Luxury:** Necessities tend to be inelastic; luxuries tend to be elastic.
    *   **Proportion of Income Spent:** The larger the proportion of income spent on a good, the more elastic the demand.
    *   **Time Horizon:** Demand tends to be more elastic in the long run than in the short run.  Consumers have more time to adjust their consumption patterns.
    *   **Brand Loyalty:** Strong brand loyalty makes demand less elastic.

*   **5.2 Factors Affecting Price Elasticity of Supply:**
    *   **Availability of Resources:**  If resources are readily available, supply will be more elastic.
    *   **Production Time:** The longer it takes to produce a good, the less elastic the supply.
    *   **Storage Capacity:** If a good can be stored easily, supply will be more elastic.
    *   **Excess Capacity:** Firms with excess capacity can respond more easily to price increases, leading to a more elastic supply.
    *   **Time Horizon:** Supply tends to be more elastic in the long run than in the short run. Producers have more time to adjust their production levels.

**6. Applications of Elasticity in Engineering**

*   **6.1 Infrastructure Projects:**
    *   Demand forecasting for transportation projects (roads, bridges, public transit).
    *   Analyzing the impact of toll prices on usage of roads and bridges.  (PED)
    *   Estimating the demand for utilities (water, electricity) in new developments. (YED)

*   **6.2 Resource Management:**
    *   Predicting the impact of price changes on the demand for resources (oil, water, minerals).  (PED)
    *   Evaluating the effectiveness of conservation policies and pricing strategies.
    *   Analyzing the impact of subsidies on the supply of renewable energy sources. (PES)

*   **6.3 Project Feasibility Studies:**
    *   Estimating the potential revenue and profitability of new projects.
    *   Assessing the sensitivity of project outcomes to changes in key variables (e.g., price of raw materials, demand for the product).
    *   Analyzing the market for engineered products or services.

*   **6.4 Supply Chain Management:**
    *   Understanding how changes in input costs impact supply and pricing decisions.
    *   Optimizing inventory levels based on demand elasticity.

**7. Practice Questions and Exercises**

1.  **Question:** Suppose the price of a software application increases from $50 to $60, and the quantity demanded decreases from 1000 units to 800 units.

    *   a) Calculate the price elasticity of demand using the arc elasticity method.
    *   b) Is the demand elastic or inelastic?
    *   c) What happens to total revenue if the price increases?

    **Answer:**

    *   a) PED = [(800-1000)/((1000+800)/2)] / [(60-50)/((50+60)/2)] =  [-200/900] / [10/55] = -0.222 / 0.182 = -1.22

    *   b) The demand is elastic (PED > 1 in absolute value).

    *   c) Total revenue decreases because the demand is elastic; a price increase leads to a proportionally larger decrease in quantity demanded.

2.  **Question:** The income elasticity of demand for organic food is 1.5. If consumer income increases by 5%, what is the expected percentage change in the quantity demanded of organic food?

    **Answer:**  YED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Income)
    1.  5 = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / 5%
    % Change in Quantity Demanded = 1.5 * 5% = 7.5%
    The quantity demanded of organic food is expected to increase by 7.5%.

3.  **Question:** The cross-price elasticity of demand between gasoline and electric cars is 0.2.  If the price of gasoline increases by 10%, what is the expected percentage change in the quantity demanded of electric cars?

    **Answer:**  CPED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded of Electric Cars) / (% Change in Price of Gasoline)
    0.  2 = (% Change in Quantity Demanded of Electric Cars) / 10%
    % Change in Quantity Demanded of Electric Cars = 0.2 * 10% = 2%
    The quantity demanded of electric cars is expected to increase by 2%.

4.  **Question:** The price elasticity of supply for a particular metal is 0.8.  If demand for the metal increases causing a 5% increase in price, what would be the expected percentage change in the quantity supplied?

    **Answer:** PES = (% Change in Quantity Supplied) / (% Change in Price)
    0.  8 = (% Change in Quantity Supplied) / 5%
    % Change in Quantity Supplied = 0.8 * 5% = 4%
    The quantity supplied is expected to increase by 4%.

**8. Important Points to Remember**

*   Elasticity values are often negative for price elasticity of demand due to the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.  However, we often consider the absolute value of PED when analyzing elasticity.
*   Elasticity is not constant along a linear demand curve. It changes as you move along the curve.
*   The concept of elasticity is a powerful tool for understanding and predicting market behavior.  Engineers should understand its applications.
*   Short-run elasticity can differ significantly from long-run elasticity.
*   Elasticity calculations are estimations, and the accuracy depends on the quality and reliability of the data used.
