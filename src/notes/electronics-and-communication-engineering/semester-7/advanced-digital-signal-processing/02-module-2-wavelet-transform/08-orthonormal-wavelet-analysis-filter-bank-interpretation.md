### 1. Introduction to Wavelet Transform and Filter Banks

The Wavelet Transform (WT) offers a time-frequency localization of signals, a capability that traditional Fourier Transform lacks. While the Fourier Transform decomposes a signal into sinusoids of different frequencies, the Wavelet Transform decomposes it into scaled and translated versions of a mother wavelet. This inherent multi-resolution analysis is elegantly represented and implemented using **filter banks**.

**Key Concept:** The Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) can be viewed as the output of a **perfectly reconstructing two-channel quadrature mirror filter (QMF) bank**.

---

### 2. The Two-Channel Filter Bank Structure

A two-channel filter bank is a system that splits a signal into two sub-bands (typically low-pass and high-pass) and then, under certain conditions, allows for the perfect reconstruction of the original signal.

#### 2.1 Analysis Filter Bank

The analysis stage decomposes the input signal $x[n]$ into two sub-band signals: the approximation (low-pass) and detail (high-pass).

*   **Low-Pass Filter (LPF):**  $h_0[n]$ - Filters out high frequencies, retaining the lower frequency components (approximation).
*   **High-Pass Filter (HPF):**  $h_1[n]$ - Filters out low frequencies, retaining the higher frequency components (detail).

**Structure:**

```
      x[n] ----> LOW-PASS FILTER (h0[n]) ----> y0[n] ----> Downsampler (2) ----> x0[n] (Approximation Coefficients)
             |
             |----> HIGH-PASS FILTER (h1[n]) ----> y1[n] ----> Downsampler (2) ----> x1[n] (Detail Coefficients)
```

*   $y_0[n] = x[n] * h_0[n]$ (Convolution with the low-pass filter)
*   $y_1[n] = x[n] * h_1[n]$ (Convolution with the high-pass filter)
*   The downsampling by 2 (denoted by $\downarrow 2$) reduces the sampling rate by half, as most of the signal's energy is concentrated in the lower frequency band after filtering. This is crucial for efficient representation and is a hallmark of multi-resolution analysis.

**Connection to Wavelet Transform:**

*   The output of the low-pass filter, $x_0[n]$, represents the **approximation coefficients** at a particular resolution level. These coefficients capture the smoother, lower-frequency aspects of the signal.
*   The output of the high-pass filter, $x_1[n]$, represents the **detail coefficients**. These coefficients capture the finer, higher-frequency details of the signal.

#### 2.2 Synthesis Filter Bank

The synthesis stage reconstructs the original signal from the sub-band signals.

*   **Low-Pass Synthesis Filter:** $g_0[n]$
*   **High-Pass Synthesis Filter:** $g_1[n]$

**Structure:**

```
                                     ^--------------> x̂[n] (Reconstructed Signal)
                                    /
      x0[n] ----> Upsampler (2) ----> LOW-PASS SYNTHESIS FILTER (g0[n])
             |
             |----> Upsampler (2) ----> HIGH-PASS SYNTHESIS FILTER (g1[n])
      x1[n] ----
```

*   The upsampling by 2 (denoted by $\uparrow 2$) inserts zeros between samples to increase the sampling rate.
*   The upsampled signals are then passed through the synthesis filters.
*   The outputs of the synthesis filters are added to reconstruct the signal.

**Relationship to Scaling and Wavelet Functions:**

In the context of orthonormal wavelet analysis, the synthesis filters are closely related to the scaling and wavelet functions:

*   The low-pass synthesis filter $g_0[n]$ is related to the **scaling function** $\phi(t)$.
*   The high-pass synthesis filter $g_1[n]$ is related to the **wavelet function** $\psi(t)$.

Specifically, for an orthonormal basis generated by a single mother wavelet and scaling function:

*   $g_0[n]$ is proportional to $\phi[n]$ (scaled and shifted versions of the scaling function).
*   $g_1[n]$ is proportional to $\psi[n]$ (scaled and shifted versions of the wavelet function).

### 3. Perfect Reconstruction (PR) Conditions

For the filter bank to perform a perfect reconstruction of the original signal, certain conditions must be met by the analysis and synthesis filters. This means that the reconstructed signal $\hat{x}[n]$ should ideally be a time-shifted version of the original signal $x[n]$, i.e., $\hat{x}[n] = C \cdot x[n-d]$ for some constant $C$ and delay $d$.

The primary conditions for perfect reconstruction in a two-channel filter bank are:

1.  **Aliasing Cancellation Condition:** This is the most critical condition. Aliasing is introduced by the downsampling process. The aliasing components from the two sub-bands must cancel each other out in the synthesis stage.
    *   $h_0(z) g_0(z) + h_1(z) g_1(z) = 2 z^{-d_1}$ (for no phase distortion)
    *   In terms of polynomials, this is often written as: $h_0(z)g_0(z) + h_1(z)g_1(z) = 2C z^{-d}$

2.  **No Distortion Condition (Amplitude and Phase):** For perfect reconstruction, the overall system should ideally introduce no amplitude or phase distortion.
    *   $h_0(z) g_0(z) + h_1(z) g_1(z) = 2 z^{-d}$ (where $d$ is the total delay)

3.  **Filter Relationship (for PR):** A common way to achieve PR is by setting the synthesis filters as follows:
    *   $g_0[n] = h_1[-n]$ (high-pass analysis filter conjugated and time-reversed, for PR)
    *   $g_1[n] = -h_0[-n]$ (low-pass analysis filter conjugated and time-reversed, for PR)
    *   This implies $G_0(z) = H_1(-z)$ and $G_1(z) = -H_0(-z)$.
    *   Substituting these into the aliasing cancellation condition:
        $h_0(z) H_1(-z) + h_1(z) (-H_0(-z)) = 2 z^{-d}$

    *   **Important Note:** For *orthonormal* wavelets, the conditions are stricter.

### 4. Orthonormal Wavelet Analysis and Filter Banks

Orthonormality in wavelet analysis implies that the scaled and translated versions of the mother wavelet and scaling function form an orthonormal basis. This means they are mutually orthogonal and have unit energy. In terms of filter banks, this translates to specific relationships between the filters.

#### 4.1 Orthonormality Conditions for Filters

For a filter bank to generate an orthonormal wavelet basis, the analysis and synthesis filters must satisfy:

1.  **Perfect Reconstruction:** As discussed above.
2.  **Quadrature Mirror Filter (QMF) Property (for orthonormal basis):**
    *   $h_1[n] = (-1)^n h_0[1-n]$  (This implies $H_1(z) = H_0(-z)$)
    *   This means the high-pass filter is related to the low-pass filter by a spectral inversion and a reversal in time.

3.  **Low-Pass Filter Properties:** The low-pass filter $h_0[n]$ must satisfy:
    *   **Symmetry/Antisymmetry:** Often, filters are chosen to be symmetric or antisymmetric for linear phase response.
    *   **Sum of Coefficients:** $\sum_n h_0[n] = \sqrt{2}$ (This relates to preserving the DC component and normalization).
    *   **Energy Preservation (for $h_0$ and $h_1$):**
        *   $\sum_n |h_0[n]|^2 = 1$
        *   $\sum_n |h_1[n]|^2 = 1$
        *   This is a crucial condition for orthonormality.

4.  **Relationship between Analysis and Synthesis Filters for Orthonormality:**
    *   $g_0[n] = h_0[n]$ (The synthesis low-pass filter is the same as the analysis low-pass filter)
    *   $g_1[n] = h_1[n]$ (The synthesis high-pass filter is the same as the analysis high-pass filter)

    Substituting these into the PR condition:
    $h_0(z) h_0(z) + h_1(z) h_1(z) = 2 z^{-d}$

    Using the QMF property $H_1(z) = H_0(-z)$:
    $H_0(z) H_0(z) + H_0(-z) H_0(-z) = 2 z^{-d}$

    If we choose $H_0(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+z^{-1})$, then $H_1(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1-z^{-1})$.
    $H_0(z)H_0(z) + H_1(z)H_1(z) = \frac{1}{2}(1+z^{-1})^2 + \frac{1}{2}(1-z^{-1})^2$
    $= \frac{1}{2}(1 + 2z^{-1} + z^{-2}) + \frac{1}{2}(1 - 2z^{-1} + z^{-2})$
    $= \frac{1}{2}(1 + 2z^{-1} + z^{-2} + 1 - 2z^{-1} + z^{-2})$
    $= \frac{1}{2}(2 + 2z^{-2}) = 1 + z^{-2}$

    This doesn't exactly match $2z^{-d}$ due to the simplicity of the filter. For perfect reconstruction and the specific case of Haar wavelet, the filters are:
    *   $h_0[n] = \begin{cases} 1/\sqrt{2} & n=0, 1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
    *   $h_1[n] = \begin{cases} 1/\sqrt{2} & n=0 \\ -1/\sqrt{2} & n=1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$

    These filters satisfy the QMF property and energy preservation, and lead to perfect reconstruction for the Haar wavelet.

**Summary of Orthonormal Filter Bank Properties:**

*   **Alias-free:** $H_0(z)G_0(z) + H_1(z)G_1(z) = 2z^{-d}$
*   **No Distortion:** $G_0(z) = H_0(z)$ and $G_1(z) = -H_1(z)$ (or other related forms). For orthonormality, $G_0(z) = H_0(z)$ and $G_1(z) = H_1(z)$ is a specific case.
*   **QMF Property:** $H_1(z) = H_0(-z)$
*   **Energy Preservation:** $|H_0(e^{j\omega})|^2 + |H_1(e^{j\omega})|^2 = 2$ for all $\omega$. Since $|H_1(e^{j\omega})|^2 = |H_0(e^{-j\omega})|^2 = |H_0(e^{j\omega})|^2$, this means $2|H_0(e^{j\omega})|^2 = 2$, so $|H_0(e^{j\omega})|^2 = 1$. This is a strong condition often relaxed for PR.

**For orthonormal bases derived from a single scaling function:**
The conditions are more stringent, directly relating to the properties of the scaling and wavelet functions:

*   $\sum_n \phi[n-k]\phi[n] = \delta[k]$ (Orthonormality of scaling function)
*   $\sum_n \psi[n-k]\psi[n] = \delta[k]$ (Orthonormality of wavelet function)
*   $\int \psi(t) \phi(t-k) dt = 0$ for all $k$ (Orthogonality between wavelet and scaling function)

These properties in the continuous domain translate to specific filter design requirements in the discrete domain. The filters $h_0[n]$ and $h_1[n]$ are essentially the impulse responses of the low-pass and high-pass filters that generate the scaling and wavelet functions through iterated convolutions.

### 5. Decomposition and Reconstruction Process

The process of wavelet analysis using filter banks is iterative.

#### 5.1 Multi-level Decomposition

At each level $J$, the signal is decomposed into approximation and detail coefficients. The approximation coefficients are then further decomposed in the next level, while the detail coefficients are typically stored.

**Level 1:**
$x[n] \xrightarrow{h_0, \downarrow 2} x_0[n]$ (Approximation Coeffs, $c_{A,1}[n]$)
$x[n] \xrightarrow{h_1, \downarrow 2} x_1[n]$ (Detail Coeffs, $c_{D,1}[n]$)

**Level 2:**
$x_0[n] \xrightarrow{h_0, \downarrow 2} x_{00}[n]$ (Approximation Coeffs, $c_{A,2}[n]$)
$x_0[n] \xrightarrow{h_1, \downarrow 2} x_{01}[n]$ (Detail Coeffs, $c_{D,2}[n]$)

And so on. This leads to a **tree-structured decomposition**.

#### 5.2 Reconstruction Process

The reconstruction process is the reverse of the decomposition. It involves upsampling and filtering, and then combining the results.

**Level 1 Reconstruction:**
$c_{A,1}[n] \xrightarrow{\uparrow 2, g_0} \hat{x}_0[n]$
$c_{D,1}[n] \xrightarrow{\uparrow 2, g_1} \hat{x}_1[n]$
$\hat{x}_0[n] + \hat{x}_1[n] \rightarrow \hat{x}[n]$ (Reconstructed signal)

**Multi-level Reconstruction:**
To reconstruct a signal decomposed to $J$ levels, you would start by combining the coefficients from level $J$, then reconstruct level $J-1$, and so on, until level 1.

$c_{A,J}[n], c_{D,J}[n] \xrightarrow{\uparrow 2, g_0, g_1} \text{approximation components at level J-1} \rightarrow \text{combined signal at level J-1}$
$\dots$
$\text{signal at level 2} \xrightarrow{\uparrow 2, g_0, g_1} \text{approximation components at level 1} \rightarrow \text{combined signal at level 1}$
$\text{signal at level 1} \xrightarrow{\uparrow 2, g_0, g_1} \hat{x}[n]$

### 6. Examples of Orthonormal Wavelets and their Filter Banks

*   **Haar Wavelet:** This is the simplest orthonormal wavelet.
    *   **Scaling Function:** $\phi(t) = \begin{cases} 1 & 0 \le t < 1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
    *   **Wavelet Function:** $\psi(t) = \begin{cases} 1 & 0 \le t < 1/2 \\ -1 & 1/2 \le t < 1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
    *   **Analysis Filters (Normalized):**
        *   $h_0[n] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\delta[n] + \delta[n-1]]$  (Low-pass)
        *   $h_1[n] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\delta[n] - \delta[n-1]]$  (High-pass)
    *   **Synthesis Filters:** $g_0[n] = h_0[n]$ and $g_1[n] = h_1[n]$.
    *   The Haar filters have finite support and are easy to implement. However, they are discontinuous, leading to less smooth reconstructions.

*   **Other Orthonormal Wavelets (e.g., Daubechies wavelets):** Daubechies wavelets (DbN) are a family of orthonormal wavelets that offer varying degrees of smoothness. The filters for Daubechies wavelets are not simple finite impulse responses but have longer, non-symmetric, and non-causal impulse responses. Their design is based on the properties of the scaling function and filter bank conditions.

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   The Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) can be implemented as a two-channel perfect reconstruction filter bank.
*   The analysis stage uses a low-pass ($h_0[n]$) and a high-pass ($h_1[n]$) filter, followed by downsampling by 2.
*   The synthesis stage uses upsampling by 2, followed by low-pass ($g_0[n]$) and high-pass ($g_1[n]$) filters, and then summation.
*   **Perfect Reconstruction (PR)** requires aliasing cancellation and no distortion conditions.
*   **Orthonormality** imposes stricter conditions, linking the synthesis filters to the analysis filters (often $g_0=h_0, g_1=h_1$) and requiring energy preservation.
*   The **QMF property** ($H_1(z) = H_0(-z)$) is crucial for PR in many filter bank designs.
*   The approximation coefficients ($c_A$) and detail coefficients ($c_D$) are the outputs of the low-pass and high-pass filters, respectively, after downsampling.
*   Multi-level decomposition involves iteratively applying the analysis filter bank to the approximation coefficients.
*   The Haar wavelet is the simplest example, with easily defined filters.

### 10. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary role of the downsampling operation in a wavelet filter bank analysis stage?
**Answer:** The downsampling operation by 2 reduces the sampling rate of the filtered signals. This is justified because the filtering process concentrates the signal's energy into narrower frequency bands, allowing for a more compact representation and reducing computational complexity without significant loss of information. It also leads to the multi-resolution aspect of the DWT.

**Question 2:** State the two main conditions required for a filter bank to achieve perfect reconstruction.
**Answer:**
1.  **Aliasing Cancellation:** The aliasing components introduced by the downsampling in the different sub-bands must cancel each other out in the synthesis stage.
2.  **No Distortion:** The overall system should not introduce amplitude or phase distortion, meaning the reconstructed signal should ideally be a time-shifted version of the original signal.

**Question 3:** How are the synthesis filters ($g_0[n], g_1[n]$) related to the analysis filters ($h_0[n], h_1[n]$) for an *orthonormal* wavelet basis derived from a single scaling function?
**Answer:** For an orthonormal basis generated from a single scaling function, the synthesis filters are typically the same as the analysis filters: $g_0[n] = h_0[n]$ and $g_1[n] = h_1[n]$. Additionally, the analysis filters must satisfy the QMF property ($H_1(z) = H_0(-z)$) and energy preservation ($|H_0(e^{j\omega})|^2 + |H_1(e^{j\omega})|^2 = 2$).

**Question 4:** Consider a simple two-channel filter bank with analysis filters $h_0[n] = [1, 1]$ and $h_1[n] = [1, -1]$. What property does this pair of filters lack for orthonormal wavelet analysis? (Assume appropriate normalization is missing for simplicity).
**Answer:** These filters, as given, would not satisfy the energy preservation property required for orthonormal wavelet analysis. Specifically, the sum of squares of coefficients for $h_0[n]$ is $1^2 + 1^2 = 2$, and for $h_1[n]$ is $1^2 + (-1)^2 = 2$. For orthonormality, these sums should ideally be 1 (after appropriate normalization and considering the QMF property). Also, the QMF property would need to be checked. If $h_0[n] = [1,1]$, its Z-transform is $H_0(z) = 1+z^{-1}$. For $h_1[n]$ to satisfy $H_1(z) = H_0(-z)$, it should be $H_1(z) = 1+(-z)^{-1} = 1-z^{-1}$, which corresponds to $h_1[n] = [1,-1]$ after accounting for potential time reversal and scaling. However, the energy preservation $|H_0(e^{j\omega})|^2 + |H_1(e^{j\omega})|^2 = 2$ must hold for the overall system to reconstruct without attenuation.

**Question 5:** If the input signal $x[n]$ is passed through a low-pass filter $h_0[n]$ and then downsampled by 2 to obtain $c_A[n]$, what does $c_A[n]$ represent in the context of wavelet analysis?
**Answer:** $c_A[n]$ represents the **approximation coefficients** of the signal $x[n]$ at the current decomposition level. These coefficients capture the lower-frequency components and the general trend or "approximation" of the signal.
