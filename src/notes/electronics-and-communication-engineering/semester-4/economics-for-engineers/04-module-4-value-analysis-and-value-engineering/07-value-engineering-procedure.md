## ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 4: Value Analysis and Value Engineering

### Topic: Value Engineering Procedure

---

### **Introduction to Value Engineering (VE)**

Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic and organized approach to providing the necessary functions in a project or product at the lowest possible cost without sacrificing quality, performance, or reliability. It focuses on **maximizing value**, which is defined as the ratio of function to cost:

**Value = Function / Cost**

*   **Key Concept:** The primary goal of VE is to **improve value** by either:
    *   Increasing the function while keeping the cost constant.
    *   Decreasing the cost while keeping the function constant.
    *   Increasing the function while decreasing the cost.
    *   Increasing the function by a larger proportion than the cost increase.

*   **Distinction from Value Analysis (VA):** While often used interchangeably, VE typically applies to **new designs or projects early in their development**, whereas VA is applied to **existing products or projects**. The procedures are largely similar. (Refer to Thuesen & Fabrycky, Engineering Economy).

---

### **The Value Engineering Procedure (The VE Job Plan)**

The VE procedure is typically broken down into a series of sequential phases, often referred to as the "VE Job Plan." This systematic approach ensures all aspects of a product or project are examined thoroughly.

**Phases of the VE Procedure:**

1.  **Information Phase (Orientation/Overview)**
2.  **Functional Phase (Analysis/Attacks)**
3.  **Creative Phase (Development/Speculation)**
4.  **Evaluation Phase (Judgment/Selection)**
5.  **Development Phase (Refinement/Pre-Development)**
6.  **Presentation Phase (Recommendation/Reporting)**
7.  **Implementation Phase (Action/Execution)**

---

### **1. Information Phase (Orientation/Overview)**

**Objective:** To gather all relevant information about the product or project, understand its purpose, scope, and current costs.

**Key Activities:**

*   **Define the Scope:** Clearly identify the product, system, or project to be analyzed.
*   **Gather Data:** Collect comprehensive information including:
    *   **Design Specifications:** Blueprints, drawings, material lists, manufacturing processes.
    *   **Performance Requirements:** How it functions, its operational parameters.
    *   **Cost Data:** Material costs, labor costs, overheads, tooling costs, testing costs, marketing costs, etc.
    *   **Market Information:** Customer needs, competitor products, pricing strategies.
    *   **History and Background:** Previous design iterations, reasons for current design.
*   **Understand the Function:** Identify the primary and secondary functions of the product/project. A function is what something *does*. (Refer to Park, Contemporary Engineering Economics).
    *   **Primary Function:** The main purpose for which the product/project exists.
    *   **Secondary Function:** Functions that support the primary function or are incidental to it.
*   **Basic vs. Secondary Functions:** Distinguishing between these is crucial for VE. Basic functions are essential for the product's existence; secondary functions are not.
*   **Cost Allocation:** Associate costs with specific functions or components.

**Example:** For a new car design, this phase would involve gathering all engineering drawings, cost breakdowns of components, performance targets (speed, fuel efficiency), safety regulations, and market research on consumer preferences.

**Important Point to Remember:** **Thorough data collection is fundamental to the success of VE.** Without accurate and complete information, subsequent phases will be flawed.

---

### **2. Functional Phase (Analysis/Attacks)**

**Objective:** To analyze and understand the functions performed by each component or element and their associated costs.

**Key Activities:**

*   **Functional Definition:** Clearly define each function in verb-noun pairs (e.g., "transmit torque," "contain fluid," "support weight"). This forces a precise understanding of "what does it do?"
*   **Functional Costing:** Allocate costs to each identified function. This helps in identifying high-cost functions.
*   **Functional Analysis:**
    *   **Prioritize Functions:** Identify which functions are critical (basic) and which are desirable (secondary).
    *   **Performance Measurement:** Quantify the performance of each function.
    *   **Cost-Function Relationship:** Analyze how much is spent on each function.
*   **Identify Areas for Improvement:** Focus on functions with high costs and low performance, or secondary functions that could be eliminated or combined.

**Example:** In analyzing a simple mechanical fastener (like a bolt and nut), functions might include "fasten components," "resist vibration," "provide alignment." The cost associated with "fastening components" might be high, and the VE team would explore alternative ways to achieve this function at a lower cost.

**Tools/Techniques:**
*   **Function Analysis System Technique (FAST):** A diagrammatic technique used to visually represent the relationships between functions, helping to identify redundant or high-cost functions. (Refer to Blank & Tarquin, Engineering Economy).
*   **Pareto Analysis:** Focus on the 20% of functions/components that contribute to 80% of the cost or problems.

**Important Point to Remember:** **Focus on "what the product does," not "what the product is."** This functional perspective liberates thinking from existing design constraints.

---

### **3. Creative Phase (Development/Speculation)**

**Objective:** To generate a wide range of alternative solutions or approaches to achieve the identified functions at a lower cost or with improved performance.

**Key Activities:**

*   **Brainstorming:** Encourage free-flowing ideas without initial criticism. The goal is quantity and diversity of ideas.
*   **Speculative Thinking:** Challenge existing assumptions and conventional approaches. Ask "how else can this function be achieved?"
*   **Consider Alternatives:** Explore different materials, manufacturing processes, design concepts, standardization, simplification, and elimination of unnecessary features.
*   **Seek Unrelated Solutions:** Look for inspiration from other industries or fields.

**Example:** Continuing the fastener example, brainstorming alternatives for "fasten components" could include: welding, adhesives, rivets, snap-fits, interlocking designs, or even redesigning the components to eliminate the need for fastening altogether.

**Key Principles:**
*   **Quantity breeds quality:** The more ideas generated, the higher the chance of finding a good one.
*   **Defer judgment:** Avoid evaluating ideas during this phase.
*   **Encourage wild ideas:** Sometimes the most outlandish ideas lead to breakthroughs.

**Important Point to Remember:** **Think broadly and without limitation.** This is where innovation happens.

---

### **4. Evaluation Phase (Judgment/Selection)**

**Objective:** To critically assess the generated ideas and select the most promising ones for further development based on feasibility, cost-effectiveness, and impact.

**Key Activities:**

*   **Screening Ideas:** Filter out impractical or unfeasible ideas.
*   **Feasibility Analysis:** Evaluate technical feasibility, manufacturing capabilities, and market acceptance.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Estimate the potential cost savings and benefits of each selected alternative. (Refer to Paneerselvam, Engineering Economics). This involves:
    *   Estimating the cost of implementing the new design.
    *   Estimating the resulting cost savings (material, labor, manufacturing, etc.).
    *   Considering the impact on quality, performance, and reliability.
*   **Risk Assessment:** Identify potential risks associated with each alternative.
*   **Selection Criteria:** Develop criteria for choosing the best alternatives, such as:
    *   Cost reduction potential
    *   Performance improvement
    *   Quality enhancement
    *   Ease of implementation
    *   Return on investment (ROI)

**Example:** Evaluating the fastener alternatives: Welding might be technically feasible but could increase production time. Adhesives might be cost-effective but require specific curing conditions. Snap-fits might offer rapid assembly but could compromise strength. A cost-benefit analysis would quantify these trade-offs.

**Important Point to Remember:** **Be objective and critical.** Focus on the ideas that offer the greatest value improvement.

---

### **5. Development Phase (Refinement/Pre-Development)**

**Objective:** To flesh out the selected ideas, develop them into concrete proposals, and refine the technical and economic details.

**Key Activities:**

*   **Detailed Design:** Develop detailed drawings, specifications, and prototypes for the chosen alternatives.
*   **Cost Estimation Refinement:** Obtain accurate cost data for materials, labor, and manufacturing processes for the proposed changes.
*   **Performance Testing:** Conduct tests to validate that the proposed changes meet or exceed the required performance standards.
*   **Life Cycle Cost Analysis:** Consider the long-term costs, including maintenance, operation, and disposal, associated with the proposed changes. (Refer to Thuesen & Fabrycky, Engineering Economy).
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Develop plans to address identified risks.

**Example:** For the chosen fastener alternative (e.g., snap-fit), this phase would involve creating detailed CAD models, selecting appropriate plastics, designing the snap-fit mechanism, conducting stress tests, and estimating the precise manufacturing cost per unit.

**Important Point to Remember:** **Turn ideas into viable plans.** This phase requires detailed engineering and economic analysis.

---

### **6. Presentation Phase (Recommendation/Reporting)**

**Objective:** To present the VE findings and recommendations to decision-makers in a clear and persuasive manner, justifying the proposed changes.

**Key Activities:**

*   **Prepare a Formal Report:** Document the entire VE process, including the information gathered, functional analysis, generated alternatives, evaluation, and detailed proposals.
*   **Develop a Presentation:** Summarize the key findings, highlight the benefits (cost savings, performance improvements), and outline the implementation plan.
*   **Quantify Benefits:** Clearly state the expected cost savings, productivity gains, or quality improvements in monetary terms.
*   **Address Concerns:** Be prepared to answer questions and address potential objections from management.
*   **Gain Approval:** Secure the necessary approvals to proceed with implementation.

**Example:** Presenting the snap-fit solution might involve showing the cost savings compared to the bolt and nut, demonstrating the improved assembly time through a video, and providing a clear financial justification (ROI, payback period).

**Important Point to Remember:** **Persuasion is key.** Clearly articulate the value proposition of the proposed VE changes.

---

### **7. Implementation Phase (Action/Execution)**

**Objective:** To put the approved VE recommendations into action.

**Key Activities:**

*   **Procurement:** Order new materials or components.
*   **Manufacturing Process Changes:** Update tooling, machinery, or assembly procedures.
*   **Training:** Train personnel on new procedures or designs.
*   **Production/Construction:** Implement the changes in the actual product or project.
*   **Monitoring and Verification:** Track the actual cost savings and performance improvements to confirm the success of the VE effort.
*   **Documentation Update:** Ensure all relevant documentation (drawings, manuals) reflects the implemented changes.

**Example:** Implementing the snap-fit would involve updating manufacturing lines to produce the components with the new feature, retraining assembly workers, and monitoring production to ensure the cost savings are realized.

**Important Point to Remember:** **Follow-through is essential.** The VE process is not complete until the changes are implemented and their benefits validated.

### **Key Concepts and Definitions Recap:**

*   **Value:** Function / Cost
*   **Function:** What something *does*.
*   **Basic Function:** The essential purpose of a product or component.
*   **Secondary Function:** A function that is not essential to the primary purpose.
*   **VE Job Plan:** The systematic, multi-phase procedure for conducting VE.
*   **FAST Diagram:** A graphical tool for functional analysis.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Comparing the costs of a proposed change against its benefits.
*   **Life Cycle Cost:** Total cost of a product/project from conception to disposal.

### **Practice Questions:**

1.  **Define Value Engineering and explain its primary objective.**
    *   **Answer:** Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic and organized approach to providing the necessary functions in a project or product at the lowest possible cost without sacrificing quality, performance, or reliability. Its primary objective is to maximize value, defined as the ratio of Function to Cost.

2.  **List the seven phases of the Value Engineering Job Plan in order.**
    *   **Answer:** The seven phases are: 1. Information, 2. Functional, 3. Creative, 4. Evaluation, 5. Development, 6. Presentation, 7. Implementation.

3.  **During which phase of the VE procedure is brainstorming primarily used? Explain why.**
    *   **Answer:** Brainstorming is primarily used in the **Creative Phase**. This is because the objective of this phase is to generate a wide range of alternative solutions without initial criticism, and brainstorming is an effective technique for encouraging free-flowing ideas and creativity to achieve this.

4.  **What is the key difference between a basic function and a secondary function in the context of VE? Provide an example.**
    *   **Answer:** A **basic function** is the essential purpose for which a product or component exists. A **secondary function** is a supporting function that is not critical to the primary purpose or is incidental.
        *   **Example:** For a flashlight, the **basic function** is to "produce light." A **secondary function** might be "provide a grip" or "act as a temporary stand." VE would focus on ensuring the "produce light" function is achieved efficiently, while exploring ways to improve or eliminate the "provide a grip" function if it adds unnecessary cost.

5.  **Explain the importance of the Information Phase in the VE procedure.**
    *   **Answer:** The Information Phase is critical because it involves gathering all relevant data about the product or project, including design specifications, performance requirements, and cost breakdowns. Thorough and accurate data collection in this phase is fundamental to the success of all subsequent VE phases, enabling effective functional analysis and the generation of meaningful alternatives.

6.  **How is Cost-Benefit Analysis used in the VE process? Which phase does it primarily belong to?**
    *   **Answer:** Cost-Benefit Analysis is used in the **Evaluation Phase** of VE. It involves estimating the potential cost savings and benefits (e.g., improved performance, reduced material usage) of each selected alternative and comparing them against the cost of implementing the proposed change. This analysis helps in objectively selecting the most economically viable and beneficial alternatives for further development.

### **Important Points to Remember (Summary):**

*   **Value = Function / Cost:** This is the core principle.
*   **Focus on Functions:** Analyze what a product *does*, not just what it *is*.
*   **Systematic Approach:** The VE Job Plan provides a structured framework.
*   **Creativity is Crucial:** Encourage diverse ideas in the Creative Phase.
*   **Data is King:** Accurate information underpins all VE activities.
*   **Quantify Benefits:** Present clear, measurable advantages of VE proposals.
*   **Implementation is Key:** The VE process is not complete until changes are made and validated.
