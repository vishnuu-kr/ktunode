## Electrical System Design and Estimation: Module 2 - General Requirements for Electrical Installations

This module focuses on the fundamental principles and practical aspects of designing and estimating material quantities for electrical installations in various types of buildings. We will explore how to translate design requirements into a bill of materials, adhering to Indian Standards and best practices.

### Topic: Design and Estimation of Material Quantity for Electrical Installations in Small Residential Buildings/Flats/Factories (Micro-Project)

This topic is crucial for developing practical skills in electrical installation, bridging the gap between theoretical design and real-world implementation. It directly contributes to Course Outcomes CO1, CO2, and CO3.

### 1. Introduction to Electrical Installation Design and Estimation

**Key Concept:** Electrical installation design involves planning the layout, selection of components, and wiring system to meet the power and lighting needs of a building safely and efficiently. Estimation involves quantifying the materials and labor required for the installation.

**Learning Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **LO1:** Understand the general requirements for electrical installations.
*   **LO2:** Design electrical wiring for residential and commercial consumers.
*   **LO3:** Design electrical installation for industrial consumers.

**CO Alignment:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding Indian standards is fundamental to all aspects of estimation.
*   **CO2 (K3):** Designing residential wiring is a core component of this topic.
*   **CO3 (K3):** While the focus is on "small" factories, the principles of industrial installation are introduced.

**Key Definitions:**

*   **Electrical Installation:** The complete set of electrical equipment installed in a building or part of a building to provide power, lighting, and other electrical services.
*   **Estimation:** The process of calculating the total cost of materials, labor, and other expenses for an electrical installation project.
*   **Bill of Materials (BOM):** A comprehensive list of all materials required for the installation, including quantities and specifications.
*   **Load Schedule:** A document detailing the electrical loads (connected and demand) for different circuits and appliances within the building.
*   **Wiring Diagram/Layout:** A visual representation of the electrical circuits, including the location of outlets, switches, lighting points, and conduits.

**Importance of Estimation:**

*   **Budgeting and Cost Control:** Ensures the project stays within allocated funds.
*   **Procurement:** Facilitates the timely ordering of materials.
*   **Tendering:** Essential for preparing competitive bids for projects.
*   **Project Planning:** Helps in scheduling and resource allocation.
*   **Avoiding Wastage:** Accurate estimation minimizes over-ordering and material wastage.

**Referenced Material:**

*   **National Electrical Code (IS 732):** Provides guidelines for safe and efficient electrical installations.
*   **National Building Code of India (NBC 2016):** Contains general requirements for building services, including electrical installations.
*   **Electrical Design Estimating Costing by K. B. Raina, S. K. Bhattacharya:** A primary resource for estimation methodologies.
*   **Electrical estimating and costing by S. Singh, and R. D. Singh:** Offers practical approaches to estimation.

### 2. General Requirements for Electrical Installations (as per IS Codes)

**Key Concepts:** Safety, reliability, efficiency, and compliance with standards are paramount.

**Learning Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **LO1:** Understand the general requirements for electrical installations.
*   **LO2:** Design electrical wiring for residential and commercial consumers.
*   **LO3:** Design electrical installation for industrial consumers.

**CO Alignment:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Directly addresses the understanding of Indian standards.
*   **CO2 (K3):** These requirements are the foundation for residential design.
*   **CO3 (K3):** Basic industrial requirements are covered.

**Key Requirements:**

*   **Safety of Persons and Property:**
    *   Protection against electric shock (earthing, insulation).
    *   Protection against fire (proper conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit protection).
    *   Protection against mechanical damage to wiring.
    *   **Referenced Material:** National Electrical Code (IS 732), National Building Code of India (NBC 2016) - Fire Safety Chapter.
*   **Adequacy of Supply:**
    *   Proper sizing of service lines, distribution boards, and final circuits to meet the connected load.
    *   Provision for future expansion.
*   **Reliability and Continuity of Supply:**
    *   Use of quality materials.
    *   Proper termination and jointing of conductors.
    *   Appropriate selection of protective devices.
*   **Efficiency:**
    *   Minimizing voltage drop in conductors.
    *   Efficient lighting design.
*   **Compliance with Standards:** Adherence to IS codes and local regulations.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The primary goal of any electrical installation is safety.
*   All installations must be designed and executed by qualified personnel.
*   Regular inspection and maintenance are crucial for long-term safety and reliability.

### 3. Designing Electrical Installations for Small Residential Buildings/Flats

**Key Concepts:** Load calculation, circuit design, material selection, and layout planning.

**Learning Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **LO2:** Design electrical wiring for residential and commercial consumers.
*   **LO1:** Understand the general requirements for electrical installations.

**CO Alignment:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding IS codes for residential wiring.
*   **CO2 (K3):** This is the primary focus.

**Steps for Design and Estimation:**

1.  **Understanding the Building Plan:**
    *   Obtain the architectural drawings.
    *   Identify the rooms, their usage, and the location of fixtures (lights, fans, sockets, appliances).
    *   Note the main entry point for the electrical service.

2.  **Load Calculation:**
    *   **Connected Load:** Sum of the power ratings of all electrical appliances and fixtures installed.
    *   **Demand Load:** The maximum power that is likely to be consumed at any given time, considering diversity factors. Diversity factors are applied because not all appliances are used simultaneously.
        *   **Example:** In a residential flat, a calculation might involve:
            *   Lights: 5 points x 100W = 500W
            *   Fans: 3 points x 75W = 225W
            *   5A Sockets: 8 points x 200W (assumed load per socket) = 1600W
            *   15A Sockets: 2 points x 1500W (for AC, geyser) = 3000W
            *   Total Connected Load = 500 + 225 + 1600 + 3000 = 5325W
            *   **Diversity Factor (Example for Residential, as per IS 732):**
                *   First 3000W: 100%
                *   Next 3000W: 40%
                *   Balance: 20%
            *   Demand Load = (3000 x 1) + (2325 x 0.40) = 3000 + 930 = 3930W (approx. 4kW)
    *   **Referenced Material:** Electrical Design Estimating Costing by K. B. Raina, S. K. Bhattacharya (Chapter on Load Calculation).

3.  **Circuit Design:**
    *   **Lighting Circuits:** Typically for lights and ceiling fans.
    *   **Power Circuits:** For sockets, appliances like AC, geysers, washing machines, etc.
    *   **Sub-circuits:** Dividing the installation into manageable sections for safety and ease of maintenance.
    *   **Protection:** Each circuit must be protected by a Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB) or fuse of appropriate rating.
        *   **Example:** A lighting circuit might use a 6A MCB, while a power circuit for an AC might use a 16A MCB.
    *   **Referenced Material:** National Electrical Code (IS 732), National Lighting Code 2010.

4.  **Conductor Sizing:**
    *   Select conductor sizes (wires) based on the connected load, circuit breaker rating, voltage drop, and ambient temperature.
    *   **Referenced Material:** National Electrical Code (IS 732) for conductor sizing tables.

5.  **Wiring System:**
    *   **Concealed Wiring:** Wires are run inside conduits embedded in walls and ceilings. This is the most common and aesthetically pleasing method for residential buildings.
    *   **Surface Wiring:** Wires are run on the surface of walls and ceilings, often in plastic conduits or casing-capping. Less common in modern residential settings.
    *   **Choice of Conduit:** PVC conduits are widely used. Steel conduits may be used in specific areas for mechanical protection.

7.  **Layout and Wiring Diagram:**
    *   Mark the location of switches, sockets, lights, and conduits on the building plan.
    *   Draw a single-line diagram or detailed wiring diagram showing the circuit connections.

**Practice Question 1:**

Calculate the demand load for a residential flat with the following connected loads:
*   Lighting: 600W
*   Fans: 300W
*   5A Sockets: 10 points @ 150W each
*   15A Sockets: 2 points @ 1000W each (for a water heater and a microwave)

Use the following diversity factors:
*   First 3000W: 100%
*   Next 3000W: 40%
*   Balance: 20%

**Answer:**
*   Connected Load = 600W + 300W + (10 x 150W) + (2 x 1000W) = 600W + 300W + 1500W + 2000W = 4400W (4.4kW)
*   Demand Load = (3000W x 1) + (1400W x 0.40) = 3000W + 560W = 3560W (3.56kW)

### 4. Designing Electrical Installations for Small Factories (Micro-Project)

**Key Concepts:** Higher power demands, industrial-grade materials, specific safety regulations, and robust wiring systems.

**Learning Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **LO3:** Design electrical installation for industrial consumers.
*   **LO1:** Understand the general requirements for electrical installations.
*   **LO2:** Design electrical wiring for commercial consumers (principles are similar).

**CO Alignment:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Adhering to IS codes for industrial safety.
*   **CO3 (K3):** Focus on industrial installations.

**Key Considerations for Small Factories:**

*   **Higher Loads:** Machinery, production equipment, and higher power lighting systems.
*   **Heavy Duty Components:** Industrial-grade switches, sockets, conduits, and cables.
*   **Industrial Wiring Systems:**
    *   **Concealed Conduit Wiring:** Can be used, but often requires larger conduits and robust installation methods.
    *   **Surface Conduit Wiring:** More common, using rigid steel conduits or PVC conduits for mechanical protection.
    *   **Cable Trays and Trunking:** For carrying multiple cables safely and efficiently, especially in areas with high cable density.
    *   **Busbar Trunking Systems:** For high power distribution.
*   **Motor Circuits:** Specific design considerations for starting, protection (overload relays), and speed control of electric motors.
*   **Safety Standards:** Stricter safety requirements for industrial environments, including emergency stop systems and enhanced earthing.
*   **Lighting:** High-bay lighting, task lighting, and emergency lighting systems.
*   **Power Factor Correction:** Capacitors may be required to improve the power factor.
*   **Earthing:** More extensive earthing system, including earthing for all machinery and equipment.

**Estimation for a Small Factory (Example - Components to Consider):**

*   **Main Incoming Supply Panel:** Including main incomer, busbars, outgoing feeders, and protection devices (MCCBs, fuses).
*   **Distribution Boards:** For various sections of the factory.
*   **Heavy Gauge Cables:** For machinery and power circuits.
*   **Industrial Grade Conduits and Trunking:** Steel conduits, heavy-duty PVC conduits, cable trays.
*   **Industrial Sockets and Switches:** Rated for higher currents and more robust.
*   **Motor Control Centers (MCCs):** For controlling multiple motors.
*   **Lighting Fixtures:** High bay lights, fluorescent fittings, LED panels.
*   **Earthing Materials:** Earth pits, electrodes, GI pipes, GI strips, earth clamps.
*   **Wires and Cables:** Various sizes for power, control, and lighting.
*   **Cable Glands and Accessories:** For secure cable entry into enclosures.

**Referenced Material:**

*   **National Electrical Code (IS 732):** Particularly chapters related to industrial installations and motor circuits.
*   **Electrical Systems Design by M. K. Giridharan:** Provides insights into industrial power distribution.
*   **Residential Commercial and Industrial Systems by H. Joshi:** Covers industrial wiring and protection.

**Practice Question 2:**

When estimating materials for a small factory, what are three key differences in material selection compared to a residential flat?

**Answer:**
1.  **Higher Current Ratings:** Industrial sockets, switches, and MCBs/MCCBs will have significantly higher current ratings to handle machinery loads.
2.  **Mechanical Protection:** Robust conduits (like steel conduits or heavy-duty PVC) and cable trunking/trays are more likely to be used for better mechanical protection of cables.
3.  **Industrial Grade Components:** Materials like industrial plugs and sockets, motor starters, and heavy-duty distribution boards are specific to industrial applications.

### 5. Estimating Costs and Labour

**Key Concepts:** Material cost, labor cost, overheads, profit, and tender preparation.

**Learning Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **LO1:** Understand the general requirements for electrical installations.
*   **LO2:** Design electrical wiring for residential and commercial consumers.
*   **LO3:** Design electrical installation for industrial consumers.

**CO Alignment:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding costing standards.
*   **CO2 (K3):** Applying cost estimation to residential projects.
*   **CO3 (K3):** Applying cost estimation to industrial projects.

**Elements of Cost:**

*   **Material Cost:** Directly from the Bill of Materials (BOM), plus a percentage for wastage and contingency.
*   **Labour Cost:**
    *   Based on the estimated time required for various tasks (conduit bending, wire pulling, termination, switchboard wiring, testing).
    *   Labour rates vary based on skill (wireman, electrician, supervisor) and location.
    *   **Referenced Material:** Electrical Design Estimating Costing by K. B. Raina, S. K. Bhattacharya; A Course in Electrical Installation Estimating and Costing by J. B. Gupta (Chapters on Labour Estimation).
*   **Overheads:** Indirect costs associated with running the business (rent, utilities, insurance, administrative staff). Typically a percentage of the direct costs.
*   **Profit:** The desired return on investment for the contractor.

**Tender Preparation:**

*   A tender is a formal offer to carry out the work at a specific price.
*   It includes the detailed estimate of materials, labor, overheads, and profit.
*   **Referenced Material:** Electrical Design Estimating Costing by K. B. Raina, S. K. Bhattacharya; Electrical estimating and costing by S. Singh, and R. D. Singh.

**Practice Question 3:**

If the total material cost for a small residential project is ₹27,550 (as per the example BOM), and you estimate labor cost to be 40% of material cost, and overheads and profit combined at 25% of the total material and labor cost, what would be the estimated total cost?

**Answer:**
*   Material Cost = ₹27,550
*   Labour Cost = 40% of ₹27,550 = 0.40 * 27,550 = ₹11,020
*   Total Material & Labour Cost = ₹27,550 + ₹11,020 = ₹38,570
*   Overheads & Profit = 25% of ₹38,570 = 0.25 * 38,570 = ₹9,642.50
*   Estimated Total Cost = ₹38,570 + ₹9,642.50 = ₹48,212.50

### 6. Earthing and Protection (General Requirements)

**Key Concepts:** Safety through earthing, types of protective devices.

**Learning Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **LO1:** Understand the general requirements for electrical installations.
*   **LO5:** Design various earthing methods and protection.

**CO Alignment:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding IS codes for earthing.
*   **CO5 (K3):** Introduction to earthing and protection methods.

**Earthing:**

*   **Purpose:** To provide a low-resistance path for fault currents to flow to the earth, preventing dangerous voltage buildup on equipment enclosures and ensuring the proper operation of protective devices.
*   **Methods (as per IS 3043):**
    *   **Pipe Earthing:** Using a perforated GI pipe driven into the ground.
    *   **Plate Earthing:** Using a GI or copper plate buried in the ground.
    *   **Rod Earthing:** Using copper or GI rods driven into the ground.
*   **Earthing Conductor Sizing:** Determined by the size of the phase conductor, as per IS 732.
*   **Earthing Electrode Resistance:** The resistance of the earthing system should be low, typically below 5 ohms for power installations and below 1 ohm for sensitive equipment.

**Protective Devices:**

*   **Fuses:** Overcurrent protection devices that melt and break the circuit when the current exceeds a safe limit.
*   **Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs):** Electromechanical devices that provide overcurrent and short-circuit protection. They can be reset after tripping.
*   **Residual Current Devices (RCDs) / Earth Leakage Circuit Breakers (ELCBs):** Protect against electric shock by detecting small leakage currents to earth.
*   **Moulded Case Circuit Breakers (MCCBs):** For higher current ratings and more robust protection in industrial and commercial applications.

**Referenced Material:**

*   **National Electrical Code (IS 732):** Chapters on Earthing and Protection.
*   **IS 3043:** Code of Practice for Earthing.
*   **National Building Code of India (NBC 2016):** Sections on Electrical Installations.

**Important Point to Remember:**

*   Proper earthing is non-negotiable for electrical safety. All metal parts of electrical equipment that are not intentionally live must be effectively earthed.

### 7. Integration of PV Systems (Brief Mention)

**Key Concepts:** Solar power generation, grid-tied and off-grid systems.

**Learning Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **LO2:** Design electrical wiring for residential and commercial consumers and integration of PV systems.

**CO Alignment:**

*   **CO2 (K3):** Understanding integration of PV systems.

**Brief Overview:**

*   When designing electrical systems for residential buildings, the integration of rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) systems is becoming increasingly common.
*   This involves installing solar panels, an inverter, charge controllers (for off-grid systems), batteries (for off-grid or hybrid systems), and connecting them to the building's electrical distribution system.
*   Estimation for PV systems would include solar panels, mounting structures, inverters, wiring, DC and AC disconnects, and potentially batteries.
*   This aspect requires specialized knowledge beyond basic wiring estimation but is an important consideration for modern building design.

**Referenced Material:**

*   **Residential Commercial and Industrial Systems by H. Joshi:** May contain sections on renewable energy integration.

### Summary of Key Concepts and Takeaways

*   **Safety First:** Always prioritize safety in design and material selection.
*   **Code Compliance:** Adhere strictly to Indian Standards (IS codes) and the National Building Code.
*   **Accurate Load Calculation:** The foundation for proper circuit design and material sizing.
*   **Diversity Factors:** Essential for realistic demand load calculations in residential and commercial settings.
*   **Material BOM:** A well-structured BOM is crucial for accurate estimation.
*   **Labour Estimation:** Consider the time and skill required for installation tasks.
*   **Earthing is Critical:** Ensure a robust and effective earthing system.
*   **Context Matters:** Material selection and design approaches differ significantly between residential and industrial installations.
