# Power System Lab: Module 13 - Relay Testing: Overcurrent / Earth Fault Relays

## Introduction

Protective relays are crucial components in power systems, designed to detect abnormal conditions and initiate corrective actions to prevent damage and ensure system stability. This module focuses on the testing of overcurrent and earth fault relays, which are fundamental protection elements. Understanding their operational principles and how to test them is essential for ensuring the reliability and safety of the power system.

## 1. Overcurrent and Earth Fault Relays: Fundamentals

### 1.1 Purpose of Overcurrent and Earth Fault Protection

*   **Overcurrent Protection:** Detects and isolates fault currents that exceed a predetermined safe limit. These faults can be phase-to-phase or phase-to-ground.
*   **Earth Fault Protection:** Specifically detects and isolates fault currents flowing to ground (earth). This is crucial for safety and preventing damage to equipment.

### 1.2 Types of Overcurrent and Earth Fault Relays

This module will cover the testing of three primary categories of relays:

#### 1.2.1 Electromechanical Relays

*   **Principle of Operation:** Operate based on the magnetic effect of current. They typically employ an armature that moves under the influence of a magnetic field generated by the fault current, thereby closing contacts to initiate a trip signal.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Sensing Element:** Usually a shaded-pole induction disk or armature.
    *   **Operating Mechanism:** Spring-loaded contacts, trip coil.
    *   **Time/Current Setting:** Adjustable components that influence the relay's operating time and current threshold.
*   **Advantages:** Robust, simple, reliable, relatively inexpensive.
*   **Disadvantages:** Slower response times, less accurate than static or numerical relays, limited functionality.

#### 1.2.2 Static Relays

*   **Principle of Operation:** Employ solid-state electronic components (transistors, diodes, operational amplifiers) to detect fault conditions and generate trip signals. They process the current and voltage signals electronically.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Input Circuit:** Transducers (CTs, PTs) to convert power system quantities into suitable levels.
    *   **Signal Conditioning:** Amplification, filtering, rectification.
    *   **Logic Circuit:** Electronic gates and comparators to implement the protection logic.
    *   **Output Circuit:** Solid-state switching devices (thyristors, transistors) to initiate the trip.
*   **Advantages:** Faster response times, higher accuracy, greater sensitivity, can incorporate multiple functions.
*   **Disadvantages:** More susceptible to electromagnetic interference (EMI) and voltage transients, require a stable DC power supply.

#### 1.2.3 Numerical Relays

*   **Principle of Operation:** Utilize microprocessors and digital signal processing (DSP) techniques to analyze power system quantities. They sample analog signals, convert them to digital, and process them using algorithms.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Analog Input Module:** CT/VT inputs with analog-to-digital converters (ADCs).
    *   **Digital Signal Processor (DSP):** Executes protection algorithms.
    *   **Microprocessor:** Manages system functions, communication, and user interface.
    *   **Memory:** Stores algorithms, settings, and event logs.
    *   **Output Module:** Digital output contacts for tripping.
    *   **Communication Interface:** For setting, monitoring, and data exchange.
*   **Advantages:** Highly versatile, advanced features (self-diagnostics, event logging, communication capabilities), superior accuracy, ability to implement complex protection schemes, less sensitive to EMI.
*   **Disadvantages:** Higher initial cost, require trained personnel for operation and maintenance, dependence on software.

### 1.3 Characteristics of Overcurrent and Earth Fault Relays

The performance of these relays is defined by their characteristic curves, which plot the operating time against the magnitude of the fault current.

#### 1.3.1 Inverse Time Characteristics

*   **Definition:** The operating time of the relay is inversely proportional to the magnitude of the fault current. Higher fault currents result in shorter operating times.
*   **Purpose:** To provide discrimination between healthy and fault conditions and to ensure that faults are cleared as quickly as possible while avoiding nuisance tripping during minor overloads.
*   **Types of Inverse Time Characteristics:**
    *   **Standard Inverse (SI):** A common characteristic used for general-purpose overcurrent protection.
    *   **Very Inverse (VI):** Provides a steeper inverse slope, offering faster clearing of high fault currents.
    *   **Extremely Inverse (EI):** Even steeper slope, providing very rapid clearing of very high fault currents.
    *   **Definite Time (DT):** The relay operates in a fixed time regardless of the fault current magnitude, once the pickup current is exceeded. This is less common for primary protection but can be used for specific applications or as part of a combined scheme.

#### 1.3.2 Time Multiplier Setting (TMS) / Dial Setting (TDS)

*   **Definition:** A factor that adjusts the operating time of the relay for a given fault current magnitude. A higher TMS/TDS will increase the operating time.

#### 1.3.3 Plug Setting (PS) / Current Setting (CS) / Pickup Current

*   **Definition:** The minimum current at which the relay starts to operate. For overcurrent relays, this is typically expressed as a percentage of the relay's rated current. For earth fault relays, it's the residual current or the current through the earth fault element.

### 1.4 Earth Fault Detection Methods

*   **Residual Current Method:** Measures the vector sum of the phase currents. Under normal conditions, the sum is zero. During a ground fault, the residual current is non-zero and is used to detect the fault. This is typically achieved by summing the currents from the three phases via the CTs.
*   **Neutral Current Method:** For solidly grounded systems, the current flowing in the neutral conductor can be directly measured.

## 2. Relay Testing Procedures

The primary objective of relay testing is to verify that the relay operates correctly and within specified tolerances under various fault conditions. This ensures that it provides adequate protection to the power system.

### 2.1 Types of Relay Tests

#### 2.1.1 Pre-commissioning Tests (Type Tests & Routine Tests)

*   **Type Tests:** Performed by the manufacturer to verify that a relay design meets specified standards and performance criteria.
*   **Routine Tests:** Performed by the manufacturer on every relay produced to ensure consistency and detect manufacturing defects.

#### 2.1.2 Commissioning Tests (Field Tests)

*   **Purpose:** To verify that the relay and its associated wiring and CTs/PTs are correctly installed and functioning as intended in the actual power system environment.
*   **Key Tests:**
    *   **Continuity Test:** Verifies that all electrical connections are intact and free from shorts.
    *   **Insulation Resistance Test:** Checks the integrity of insulation to prevent leakage currents.
    *   **CT/VT Ratio and Polarity Test:** Ensures that the current and voltage transformers are correctly connected and providing accurate representations of system quantities.
    *   **Relay Operating Tests:** Functional tests to verify the relay's pickup, timing, and tripping characteristics.

#### 2.1.3 Periodic Maintenance Tests

*   **Purpose:** To periodically check the relay's performance and ensure it continues to operate correctly over its service life. The frequency of these tests depends on the relay type, its criticality, and the utility's maintenance policy.

### 2.2 Relay Testing Equipment

*   **Primary Injection Test Set:**
    *   **Function:** Injects a high current directly into the primary side of the CT to simulate fault currents.
    *   **Principle:** If a primary current of 'I_primary' is injected, the secondary current is 'I_primary / CT_ratio'.
    *   **Advantages:** More accurate for CT testing, bypasses the CT's burden.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires high-capacity current sources.
*   **Secondary Injection Test Set (Relay Test Set):**
    *   **Function:** Injects currents and voltages into the secondary circuits of the CTs and PTs (or directly into the relay's input terminals) to simulate fault conditions.
    *   **Principle:** Simulates the secondary current/voltage output from the CTs/PTs.
    *   **Advantages:** Versatile, can simulate various fault types and magnitudes, easier to control and measure relay performance.
    *   **Commonly Used Types:**
        *   **Analog Test Sets:** Use analog circuitry to generate test signals.
        *   **Digital/Microprocessor-Based Test Sets:** Offer automated testing, pre-programmed test routines, and advanced analysis capabilities.
*   **CT Analyzer:** Used specifically for testing the characteristics of Current Transformers.

### 2.3 Testing of Electromechanical Overcurrent/Earth Fault Relays

#### 2.3.1 Essential Parameters to Test:

*   **Pickup Current:** The minimum current at which the relay's operating element begins to move.
*   **Operating Time:** The time taken for the relay to close its tripping contacts after the fault current exceeds the pickup value.
*   **Reset Current:** The current at which the relay contacts open after the fault is cleared.
*   **Characteristic Curve Verification:** Ensuring the relay's operating time matches the specified inverse time characteristic for various fault current multiples.
*   **Earth Fault Element Operation:** Testing the sensitivity and timing of the residual or neutral earth fault element.

#### 2.3.2 Test Procedure (Secondary Injection):

1.  **Visual Inspection:** Check for any physical damage, loose connections, or signs of overheating.
2.  **Continuity and Insulation Tests:** Perform these as described in Section 2.1.2.
3.  **CT Ratio and Polarity:** Verify these if not previously done.
4.  **Setting the Relay:**
    *   Set the **Plug Setting (PS)** or **Current Setting (CS)** to the desired value. This determines the pickup current.
    *   Set the **Time Multiplier Setting (TMS)** or **Dial Setting (TDS)** to the desired value. This adjusts the operating time for a given current.
5.  **Pickup Test:**
    *   Apply a current slightly below the set pickup value and observe that the relay does not operate.
    *   Gradually increase the current until the relay just operates (closes its contacts). Record this current. This should be within the specified tolerance of the PS/CS.
6.  **Timing Test:**
    *   Apply a multiple of the pickup current (e.g., 2x, 5x, 10x PS).
    *   Measure the time taken for the relay to trip using the secondary injection test set's timer.
    *   Compare the measured time with the expected time from the relay's characteristic curve for the given current and TMS/TDS.
    *   Repeat this for several current multiples to verify the inverse time characteristic.
7.  **Reset Test:**
    *   After tripping, reduce the injected current to a value below the reset current. The relay contacts should open.
8.  **Earth Fault Element Test:**
    *   If the relay has a separate earth fault element, repeat the pickup and timing tests for the residual or neutral current input.

#### 2.3.3 Important Considerations for Electromechanical Relays:

*   **Burden:** The impedance of the relay coil and associated wiring can affect the CT performance. This should be accounted for.
*   **DC Offset:** In real faults, there's often a DC offset component in the fault current, which can significantly affect the transient behavior of electromechanical relays. Testing with simulated DC offset might be necessary.
*   **Wear and Tear:** Mechanical parts can wear out, affecting performance.

### 2.4 Testing of Static Overcurrent/Earth Fault Relays

#### 2.4.1 Essential Parameters to Test:

*   **Pickup Current:** The threshold current for operation.
*   **Operating Time:** The time taken to trip.
*   **Time-Current Characteristic:** Verification of the programmed or selected inverse time characteristics.
*   **Reset Time:** The time taken to reset after the fault is cleared.
*   **Load-Encroachment Test:** Ensuring the relay does not operate for currents below the specified overload limits.
*   **Phase and Earth Fault Sensitivity:** For relays with both phase and earth fault elements, their individual performance must be verified.

#### 2.4.2 Test Procedure (Secondary Injection using Digital Test Sets):

1.  **Visual Inspection and Pre-checks:** As before.
2.  **Connect Test Set:** Connect the digital test set to the relay's secondary terminals, simulating CT and PT inputs.
3.  **Set Relay Parameters:**
    *   Input the relay's settings (pickup current, TMS/TDS, characteristic type) into the test set.
    *   Configure the test set to simulate the desired fault conditions (e.g., phase-to-ground, phase-to-phase faults).
4.  **Pickup Test:**
    *   Inject current at the pickup setting and verify operation.
    *   Inject current below the pickup setting and verify non-operation.
5.  **Timing Test:**
    *   The test set will automatically inject a series of fault currents at different multiples of the pickup value and record the operating times.
    *   The test set compares these measured times with the expected times based on the selected characteristic and settings, generating a test report.
6.  **Characteristic Verification:**
    *   The test set can plot the actual relay characteristic and compare it against the theoretical characteristic, highlighting any deviations.
7.  **Load-Encroachment Test:**
    *   Inject currents at specified overload levels (e.g., 1.2x, 1.5x PS) and verify that the relay does not trip within a defined time (e.g., a few seconds).
8.  **Injection of Other Voltages/Frequencies:** Depending on the relay's design, tests for sensitivity to voltage variations or frequency deviations might be performed.

#### 2.4.3 Important Considerations for Static Relays:

*   **Accuracy of Test Set:** The accuracy of the digital test set is paramount for verifying the relay's performance.
*   **Waveform Simulation:** Advanced test sets can simulate complex fault waveforms, including DC offset and harmonics.
*   **Self-Diagnosis:** Static relays often have built-in self-diagnostic capabilities that can be accessed during testing.

### 2.5 Testing of Numerical Overcurrent/Earth Fault Relays

Numerical relays offer the most comprehensive testing capabilities due to their digital nature.

#### 2.5.1 Essential Parameters to Test:

*   All parameters tested for static relays, plus:
*   **Event Logging:** Verification of stored fault data and operating logs.
*   **Communication Functionality:** Testing of protocols (e.g., Modbus, IEC 61850) for setting and data retrieval.
*   **Protection Features:** Verification of all programmed protection functions and logic.
*   **Analog Input/Output Accuracy:** Ensuring accurate representation of system quantities.
*   **Differential Protection (if integrated):** If the numerical relay also incorporates differential protection, this needs separate testing.

#### 2.5.2 Test Procedure (Using dedicated Numerical Relay Test Software):

1.  **Connect Test Set:** Connect a sophisticated digital test set capable of simulating complex waveforms and communication protocols.
2.  **Load Relay Software/Settings:** The test set can directly communicate with the relay to download its settings and protection schemes.
3.  **Execute Pre-programmed Test Routines:** Digital test sets come with extensive libraries of test routines for various protection functions and relay manufacturers. The user can select appropriate routines.
4.  **Simulate Faults:** Inject currents and voltages to simulate a wide range of fault types (phase-to-phase, phase-to-ground, ground faults at different locations), magnitudes, and durations. The test set can also simulate system pre-fault conditions.
5.  **Analyze Results:**
    *   The test software automatically analyzes the relay's response (pickup, timing, trip signals) against expected values.
    *   It generates detailed reports, often graphically displaying characteristic curves and highlighting any deviations or failures.
6.  **Event Log Retrieval and Analysis:** The test set can retrieve event logs from the relay, allowing verification of fault records and the relay's internal diagnostics.
7.  **Communication Test:** Test the relay's communication interfaces to ensure reliable data exchange with SCADA or other control systems.
8.  **Functional Tests:** Test specific logic or interlocking schemes programmed into the relay.

#### 2.5.3 Important Considerations for Numerical Relays:

*   **Software Updates:** Ensure the relay's firmware and the test set's software are up-to-date.
*   **Configuration:** The complexity of numerical relays means that accurate configuration and understanding of the protection schemes are crucial for effective testing.
*   **Cybersecurity:** For relays with communication capabilities, cybersecurity aspects might need consideration.

## 4. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Explain the fundamental difference between the operating principle of an electromechanical overcurrent relay and a static overcurrent relay.

**Answer 1:**
The fundamental difference lies in their sensing and signal processing mechanisms.
*   **Electromechanical relays** rely on the physical movement of an armature or disc, driven by the magnetic field produced by the fault current, to close contacts.
*   **Static relays** use solid-state electronic components (transistors, ICs) to sense the fault current, process the signal, and initiate a trip using electronic switching devices.

**Question 2:**
What is the purpose of the Time Multiplier Setting (TMS) on an inverse time overcurrent relay? If a relay has a TMS of 0.2 and a fault occurs causing 5 times the pickup current, and the relay's characteristic states that at 5x pickup with TMS=1.0, the time is 1 second, what would be the approximate operating time for this fault?

**Answer 2:**
The Time Multiplier Setting (TMS) is used to adjust the operating time of an inverse time overcurrent relay for a given fault current magnitude. A higher TMS increases the operating time.

Given:
*   Base operating time at 5x pickup with TMS=1.0 is 1 second.
*   Actual TMS is 0.2.

Approximate operating time = Base operating time × Actual TMS
Approximate operating time = 1 second × 0.2 = **0.2 seconds**.

**Question 3:**
Describe the difference between primary injection and secondary injection testing of relays. Which method is generally preferred for verifying the relay's operational characteristics, and why?

**Answer 3:**
*   **Primary Injection:** Involves injecting a high current directly into the primary winding of the Current Transformer (CT). The CT then transforms this current to its secondary side, which is connected to the relay.
*   **Secondary Injection:** Involves injecting a current and voltage (simulating CT and PT outputs) directly into the secondary terminals of the CTs/PTs or the relay's input circuits.

**Preference:** **Secondary injection** is generally preferred for verifying the relay's operational characteristics (pickup, timing, characteristic curves).
**Reason:**
*   **Versatility:** Secondary injection allows for easier simulation of various fault magnitudes and types, and also provides voltage inputs for voltage-dependent relays.
*   **Control:** It offers precise control over the injected current and voltage and direct measurement of the relay's operating time.
*   **Safety:** It's generally safer as it involves lower primary voltages and currents (though high secondary currents are still injected).
*   **CT Independent:** It isolates the relay performance from the CT performance, allowing for separate testing of each component. Primary injection is primarily used to test the CTs themselves.

**Question 4:**
List three key advantages of numerical relays over static relays.

**Answer 4:**
Three key advantages of numerical relays over static relays are:
1.  **Advanced Functionality and Versatility:** Numerical relays can incorporate multiple protection functions, logic schemes, and advanced algorithms (e.g., adaptive protection) in a single unit. They are highly configurable through software.
2.  **Improved Accuracy and Sensitivity:** Digital signal processing allows for more precise measurements and faster detection of faults, often with higher sensitivity and better discrimination.
3.  **Enhanced Communication and Data Logging:** Numerical relays typically feature communication ports for remote setting, monitoring, and fault data retrieval (event logging, oscillography), facilitating analysis and troubleshooting.

**Question 5 (Lab Scenario):**
You are performing a secondary injection test on an electromechanical overcurrent relay with the following settings:
*   Plug Setting (PS) = 1 Amp
*   Time Multiplier Setting (TMS) = 0.4
*   Characteristic: Standard Inverse (refer to a standard SI curve if available, otherwise assume a standard relationship for calculation)

You inject a current of 5 Amps into the relay's secondary. Based on a typical SI curve, a current of 5 Amps (5 times pickup) with TMS = 1.0 should result in an operating time of approximately 1 second.

a) What is the expected operating time for this relay under the given conditions?
b) If the measured operating time is 0.5 seconds, what could be the potential issue with the relay?

**Answer 5:**
a) **Expected operating time:**
The operating time of an inverse time relay is generally proportional to the TMS.
Expected time = (Time at TMS=1.0) × (Actual TMS)
Expected time = 1 second × 0.4 = **0.4 seconds**.

b) **Potential issue if measured time is 0.5 seconds:**
If the measured operating time is 0.5 seconds instead of the expected 0.4 seconds, it indicates that the relay is operating slower than specified. Potential issues could include:
*   **Mechanical Wear:** Friction in the moving parts (disc, pivot) of the electromechanical relay could be increasing.
*   **Weak Spring Tension:** The spring that opposes the movement of the disc might have weakened.
*   **Incorrect Setting of TMS:** Although unlikely if the setting was confirmed, it's a possibility.
*   **Burden Issues:** The connected burden (e.g., wiring resistance) might be higher than anticipated, affecting CT performance and relay operation.
*   **CT Saturation (less likely with secondary injection):** If the CT itself is saturating at high currents (not directly tested by secondary injection, but a factor in overall system behavior), it could affect the current seen by the relay. However, a slower response at 5x pickup is more indicative of internal relay issues.

## 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Safety First:** Always follow safety procedures when working with power system equipment and testing apparatus. Ensure proper grounding and isolation.
*   **Understand Relay Settings:** Before testing, thoroughly understand the relay's settings and the protection scheme it's part of.
*   **Use Appropriate Test Equipment:** Ensure your test set is calibrated and suitable for the type of relay being tested.
*   **Refer to Standards and Manufacturer Data:** Always consult relevant IEC/IEEE standards and the manufacturer's operating and testing manuals.
*   **Document Everything:** Keep detailed records of all tests performed, including settings, injected values, measured times, and any anomalies observed.
*   **CT Performance is Crucial:** The accuracy of the CTs significantly impacts the relay's performance. CT saturation can lead to under-reaching protection.
*   **Discrimination:** Relay testing is not just about verifying individual relay operation but also ensuring that the entire protection system provides correct discrimination between faults and healthy conditions.
