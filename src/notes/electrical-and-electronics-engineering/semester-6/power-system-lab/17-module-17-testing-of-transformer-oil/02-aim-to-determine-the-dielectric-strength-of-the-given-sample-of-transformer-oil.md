# Power System Lab: Module 17 - Testing of Transformer Oil

## Topic: Determination of Dielectric Strength of Transformer Oil

**Aim:** To determine the dielectric strength of the given sample of transformer oil.

---

### 1. Introduction to Transformer Oil and its Importance

Transformer oil, also known as insulating oil, is a crucial component in electrical transformers. Its primary functions are:

*   **Electrical Insulation:** It prevents electrical arcing between energized parts and the transformer core or casing.
*   **Cooling:** It dissipates heat generated by the transformer windings and core, preventing overheating.
*   **Protection:** It prevents moisture ingress and oxidation of the internal components.

The **dielectric strength** of transformer oil is a measure of its ability to withstand electrical stress without breaking down. A lower dielectric strength indicates a weakened insulating capability, which can lead to transformer failure. Therefore, regular testing of transformer oil is essential for ensuring the safe and reliable operation of power transformers.

---

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Dielectric Strength:** The maximum electric field strength that a material can withstand without undergoing dielectric breakdown. It is typically expressed in kilovolts per millimeter (kV/mm) or megavolts per meter (MV/m).
*   **Dielectric Breakdown:** The phenomenon where an insulating material, when subjected to a sufficiently high electric field, loses its insulating properties and becomes electrically conductive. This often manifests as a spark or arc.
*   **Dielectric Breakdown Voltage (DBV):** The voltage at which dielectric breakdown occurs in a given insulating medium under specified conditions.
*   **Gap Distance:** The distance between the electrodes in the test cell containing the transformer oil. This distance is critical in determining the breakdown voltage.
*   **AC Voltage:** Alternating current voltage, which is commonly used for testing transformer oil. The frequency is typically 50 Hz or 60 Hz.
*   **Shape of Electrodes:** The shape of the electrodes used in the test cell significantly influences the electric field distribution. Spherical or rounded electrodes are commonly used to promote uniform field distribution and prevent localized stress concentrations.

---

### 3. Principle of Testing Transformer Oil for Dielectric Strength

The principle behind determining the dielectric strength of transformer oil is to subject a sample of the oil to a gradually increasing AC voltage until dielectric breakdown occurs. The voltage at which breakdown occurs, measured across a known gap distance between two electrodes immersed in the oil, is the dielectric breakdown voltage (DBV).

The dielectric strength is then calculated using the formula:

$$ \text{Dielectric Strength (kV/mm)} = \frac{\text{Dielectric Breakdown Voltage (kV)}}{\text{Gap Distance (mm)}} $$

---

### 4. Apparatus Required

The following apparatus are typically used for testing the dielectric strength of transformer oil:

*   **Transformer Oil Testing Kit (BDV Tester):** This is a specialized instrument that generates a high AC voltage and measures the breakdown voltage. It usually consists of:
    *   **Variable High Voltage Transformer (Variac):** To control and gradually increase the voltage.
    *   **High Voltage Transformer:** To step up the voltage to the required levels.
    *   **Voltage Meter:** To measure the applied voltage (often a calibrated voltmeter).
    *   **Test Cell:** A vessel designed to hold the oil sample and the electrodes. It is usually made of glass or a transparent insulating material.
    *   **Electrodes:** Typically spherical or cylindrical electrodes with rounded edges, mounted at a fixed distance within the test cell.
    *   **Safety Interlocks and Earth Connections:** Crucial for safe operation.
*   **Clean Glassware:** Beakers, sample containers for collecting and storing oil.
*   **Stirring Rod/Mixer:** To ensure homogeneity of the oil sample.
*   **Filter Paper/Cotton Wool:** For filtering out any suspended particles.
*   **Thermometer:** To measure the oil temperature during the test.

---

### 5. Procedure for Testing

The following steps are generally followed to determine the dielectric strength of transformer oil:

1.  **Sample Preparation:**
    *   Collect a fresh sample of transformer oil in a clean, dry container. Avoid any contamination during collection.
    *   Allow the oil to settle for a sufficient period (e.g., 24 hours) to permit any suspended impurities or air bubbles to rise to the surface or settle down.
    *   Gently warm the oil if it is very viscous or cold to ensure better handling and oil flow during filling the test cell. *Ensure the temperature does not exceed the recommended limits for storage and testing.*

2.  **Test Cell Setup:**
    *   Carefully clean the test cell and electrodes with a lint-free cloth and, if necessary, with a small amount of the oil being tested to ensure no residual contaminants are present.
    *   Fill the test cell with the prepared transformer oil sample, ensuring that the electrodes are completely immersed.
    *   Set the gap distance between the electrodes to the standard specified value (e.g., 2.5 mm as per IS 6792 or IEC 60156). *The gap distance must be accurately known.*

3.  **Electrical Connections:**
    *   Connect the test cell to the BDV tester as per the manufacturer's instructions. Ensure proper grounding of the apparatus.
    *   Ensure all safety precautions are in place before commencing the test.

4.  **Voltage Application and Measurement:**
    *   Set the variac to its minimum position.
    *   Turn on the BDV tester and the high voltage transformer.
    *   Gradually increase the applied AC voltage at a uniform rate (e.g., 2 kV/sec or 3 kV/sec as per standards).
    *   Observe the oil sample in the test cell.
    *   When the oil breaks down (indicated by a spark or arc across the electrodes), immediately note the voltage reading on the voltmeter. This is the dielectric breakdown voltage (DBV).
    *   Immediately reduce the voltage to zero.

5.  **Repeat the Test:**
    *   Drain the tested oil from the test cell and refill with a fresh portion of the same sample. This is important to avoid repeated breakdown in the same oil, which can alter its properties.
    *   Repeat steps 2 to 4 at least five times with fresh oil samples in the test cell for each test.

6.  **Data Analysis and Calculation:**
    *   Record the DBV readings for each of the tests.
    *   Calculate the average of these DBV readings.
    *   Calculate the dielectric strength using the average DBV and the known gap distance:
        $$ \text{Dielectric Strength (kV/mm)} = \frac{\text{Average DBV (kV)}}{\text{Gap Distance (mm)}} $$

### 6. Relevant Standards

*   **IS 6792 (Indian Standard):** Methods for testing of electrical insulating oils.
*   **IEC 60156 (International Electrotechnical Commission):** Insulating liquids - Determination of the dielectric strength in consequence of an electrical breakdown (<bos>60,000 V/2.5 mm).
*   **ASTM D1816:** Standard Test Method for Dielectric Breakdown Voltage of Insulating Oils of Petroleum Origin at Commercial Power Frequencies.
*   **ASTM D877:** Standard Test Method for Dielectric Breakdown Voltage of Insulating Oils of Petroleum Origin at Commercial Power Frequencies.

*It is crucial to adhere to the specific standard specified by the laboratory or the relevant governing body.*

### 7. Factors Affecting Dielectric Strength

Several factors can influence the dielectric strength of transformer oil:

*   **Presence of Impurities:**
    *   **Moisture:** Water is a major contaminant and significantly reduces the dielectric strength. Even small amounts of moisture can form conductive paths.
    *   **Particulate Matter:** Solid particles like dust, sludge, or metallic debris can act as initiation points for breakdown.
    *   **Dissolved Gases:** Gases dissolved in the oil can form bubbles under high electric fields, leading to partial discharge and eventual breakdown.
*   **Temperature:** While generally the dielectric strength of oil slightly increases with temperature up to a certain point, very high temperatures can lead to degradation and the formation of by-products that reduce insulation.
*   **Electrode Geometry and Surface Finish:** Sharp edges or rough surfaces on electrodes can cause localized electric field enhancement, leading to premature breakdown.
*   **Rate of Voltage Increase:** A slower rate of voltage application generally allows for the formation of partial discharge and bubble formation, potentially leading to a lower observed DBV.
*   **Frequency of Applied Voltage:** Higher frequencies can sometimes lead to different breakdown mechanisms.
*   **Age and Oxidation:** Over time, transformer oil can oxidize, forming sludge and acidic by-products, which degrade its insulating properties.

---

### 8. Interpretation of Results

The dielectric strength of new transformer oil is typically expected to be in the range of **25 kV/mm to 35 kV/mm**, depending on the grade and manufacturer.

*   **Low Dielectric Strength:** Indicates the presence of contaminants like moisture, particulate matter, or degraded oil. It signals the need for oil treatment (e.g., filtration, de-gassing, drying) or replacement.
*   **High Dielectric Strength:** Confirms the good insulating quality of the oil.

### 11. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Define dielectric strength of transformer oil and state its importance in a power transformer.

**Answer:** Dielectric strength is the maximum electric field a material can withstand without breakdown. For transformer oil, it's crucial as it provides electrical insulation between the live parts and the transformer core/casing, and its breakdown can lead to short circuits and catastrophic failure.

**Question 2:** What are the common contaminants that reduce the dielectric strength of transformer oil?

**Answer:** The common contaminants are moisture (water), particulate matter (dust, sludge, metal particles), and dissolved gases.

**Question 3:** If the breakdown voltage of transformer oil is measured to be 40 kV across a gap of 2 mm, what is its dielectric strength in kV/mm?

**Answer:**
Dielectric Strength = Breakdown Voltage / Gap Distance
Dielectric Strength = 40 kV / 2 mm = **20 kV/mm**

**Question 4:** Why is it necessary to repeat the test multiple times with fresh oil samples?

**Answer:** Repeating the test multiple times with fresh samples from the same batch helps to obtain a more reliable average value and minimize the impact of random errors or variations in individual tests. It also avoids the effect of repeated breakdown, which can alter the oil's properties.

**Question 5:** What is the typical range for the dielectric strength of new, good quality transformer oil?

**Answer:** The typical range is between 25 kV/mm and 35 kV/mm.
