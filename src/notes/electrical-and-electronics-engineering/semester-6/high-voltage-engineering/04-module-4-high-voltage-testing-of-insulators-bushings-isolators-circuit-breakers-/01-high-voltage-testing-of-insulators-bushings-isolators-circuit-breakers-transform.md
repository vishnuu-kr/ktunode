# HIGH VOLTAGE ENGINEERING: Module 4 - High Voltage Testing of Electrical Apparatus

## Introduction

High voltage testing is crucial in ensuring the reliability and safety of electrical equipment operating under high voltage conditions. These tests verify the insulation strength and performance characteristics of various apparatus against potential electrical stresses. This module focuses on the testing of key high voltage components including insulators, bushings, isolators, circuit breakers, transformers, surge diverters, and cables.

**Key Concept:** **Dielectric Strength:** The maximum electric field strength a material can withstand before breakdown occurs. This is a fundamental property tested in high voltage equipment.

**Reference:** This module draws heavily from Chapters related to testing of specific equipment in **Wadhwa (2011)**, **Kuchler (2018)**, **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, and **Kuffel et al. (2005)**.

---

## 1. High Voltage Testing of Insulators

Insulators are designed to prevent the flow of current between conductors at different potentials and between conductors and ground. Their performance is critical for the overall insulation integrity of the power system.

### 1.1 Types of Insulators and Their Testing Needs

*   **Porcelain Insulators:**
    *   **Pin Type:** Used for distribution lines.
    *   **Suspension Type:** Used for transmission lines, stacked to achieve desired voltage rating.
    *   **Shackle Type:** Used for low voltage lines or at dead-end positions.
*   **Glass Insulators:** Similar applications to porcelain insulators.
*   **Polymer Insulators (Composite Insulators):** Made of fiberglass core with weather-shedding polymer housing. Offer advantages like lighter weight and better anti-pollution performance.

### 1.2 Key Tests for Insulators

*   **Dry Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the ability of the insulator to withstand power frequency (50/60 Hz) AC voltage under dry conditions.
    *   **Procedure:** The insulator is subjected to a gradually increasing power frequency voltage until breakdown or flashover occurs. The test is conducted at standard atmospheric conditions.
    *   **Standard Voltage:** Typically 1.5 to 2 times the rated working voltage.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.
*   **Wet Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To simulate the effect of rain and pollution on insulator performance.
    *   **Procedure:** A fine spray of water with a specific resistivity is applied to the insulator while it is subjected to a power frequency voltage.
    *   **Reference:** **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, Chapter 7.
*   **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test (Lightning and Switching Surge):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the insulator's ability to withstand transient overvoltages caused by lightning strikes or switching operations.
    *   **Procedure:** The insulator is subjected to standard impulse voltage waveforms (1.2/50 µs for lightning, 250/2500 µs for switching). The test is conducted to determine the impulse flashover/puncture voltage.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 9.
*   **Partial Discharge (PD) Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To detect and measure small discharges that occur within the insulation when subjected to a high AC voltage, but below the full flashover voltage. PD indicates localized breakdown, which can lead to eventual failure.
    *   **Procedure:** Using sensitive detection equipment (capacitive coupling, resonant circuits) to measure the small pulses of charge.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuchler (2018)**, Chapter 15.
*   **Mechanical Strength Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the mechanical integrity of the insulator under tensile or compression loads.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying specific mechanical loads to the insulator.

### 1.3 Important Points to Remember for Insulators

*   The performance of insulators is significantly affected by environmental conditions like pollution, humidity, and temperature.
*   Composite insulators are becoming increasingly popular due to their improved performance in polluted environments.
*   PD testing is crucial for detecting internal flaws that may not be apparent in withstand voltage tests.

## 2. High Voltage Testing of Bushings

Bushings are essential components that provide a conductive path through a grounded enclosure (like a transformer tank) for a conductor, while maintaining electrical insulation.

### 2.1 Types of Bushings

*   **Condenser Bushings:** Most common for high voltage applications, especially for transformers. They use layers of metallized paper (stress grading) to distribute the electric field uniformly.
*   **Plain Bushings:** Used for lower voltages where capacitive grading is not required.

### 2.2 Key Tests for Bushings

*   **Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the insulation strength of the bushing under normal operating voltages.
    *   **Procedure:** Similar to insulator testing, the bushing is subjected to a gradually increasing power frequency voltage.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.
*   **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure the bushing can withstand transient overvoltages.
    *   **Procedure:** Subjecting the bushing to standard lightning and switching impulse waveforms.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 9.
*   **Partial Discharge (PD) Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To detect internal voids or defects in the insulation of condenser bushings.
    *   **Procedure:** Measuring PD activity using specialized equipment.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuchler (2018)**, Chapter 15.
*   **Capacitance and Tan Delta (Dissipation Factor) Measurement:**
    *   **Purpose:** For condenser bushings, this test checks the integrity of the insulating paper and the grading layers. An increase in capacitance or tan delta can indicate moisture ingress or degradation of insulation.
    *   **Procedure:** Using a Schering bridge or similar instruments at power frequency and potentially at a higher frequency (e.g., 1 kHz) to assess the insulation condition.
    *   **Reference:** **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, Chapter 7.
*   **Radio Interference (RI) and Corona Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the voltage at which visible corona discharge or radio noise begins. This is important for minimizing interference with communication systems.
    *   **Procedure:** Detecting corona pulses or radio noise with sensitive receivers.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.

### 2.3 Important Points to Remember for Bushings

*   Condenser bushings are designed to control the electric field gradient along their length, preventing excessive stress at the edges.
*   Capacitance and tan delta measurements are critical "health check" tests for condenser bushings.
*   Proper installation and sealing are crucial to prevent moisture ingress, which degrades insulation.

## 3. High Voltage Testing of Isolators (Disconnectors)

Isolators are used to electrically disconnect sections of a circuit or to isolate equipment from the high voltage supply. They are generally operated in a no-load condition.

### 3.1 Types of Isolators

*   **Horizontal Break Isolators**
*   **Vertical Break Isolators**
*   **Double Break Isolators**
*   **Pantograph Isolators**

### 3.2 Key Tests for Isolators

*   **Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test (Dry and Wet):**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the ability to withstand power frequency voltages under both dry and wet conditions. This includes testing the air insulation distance.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying voltage between phases, between phase and earth, and across open contacts.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.
*   **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure they can withstand transient overvoltages, particularly across the open contacts.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying standard lightning and switching impulse waveforms.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 9.
*   **Contact Resistance Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure low resistance at the contacts, minimizing power loss and heating.
    *   **Procedure:** Using a low voltage, high current method (e.g., Kelvin bridge) to measure contact resistance.
    *   **Reference:** **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, Chapter 7.
*   **Mechanical Strength Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the structural integrity and operating mechanism.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying specified mechanical loads.
*   **Making Capacity Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the ability to close the isolator under fault currents.
    *   **Procedure:** Subjecting the isolator to specified making currents.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuchler (2018)**, Chapter 11.

### 3.3 Important Points to Remember for Isolators

*   Isolators are designed to be operated at no load. Attempting to operate them under load can cause severe damage due to arcing.
*   The air insulation distance between contacts and between phases is a critical factor in their design and testing.
*   Contact integrity is vital for minimizing losses and ensuring reliable operation.

## 4. High Voltage Testing of Circuit Breakers

Circuit breakers are crucial protective devices that interrupt fault currents in the power system, thereby preventing damage to equipment and maintaining system stability. Their testing is complex and aims to verify their interrupting and switching capabilities.

### 4.1 Types of Circuit Breakers

*   **Oil Circuit Breakers (OCBs):** Bulk oil and minimum oil.
*   **Air Blast Circuit Breakers (ABCBs):** Axial and radial flow.
*   **SF6 Circuit Breakers:** Most common in modern high voltage systems.
*   **Vacuum Circuit Breakers (VCBs):** Common in medium voltage, but also used in some high voltage applications.

### 4.2 Key Tests for Circuit Breakers

*   **Dielectric Tests:**
    *   **Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test (Dry and Wet):**
        *   **Purpose:** To verify the insulation strength between phases and to earth.
        *   **Procedure:** Applying power frequency voltage across open and closed contacts.
        *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.
    *   **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test:**
        *   **Purpose:** To verify insulation strength against transient overvoltages.
        *   **Procedure:** Applying lightning and switching impulse voltages.
        *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 9.
*   **Short-Circuit Tests (Current Interruption Tests):**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the breaker's ability to interrupt fault currents at rated capacity. This is the most critical test.
    *   **Types of Short-Circuit Tests:**
        *   **Making Test:** To check the breaker's ability to close against a fault current.
        *   **Breaking Test:** To check the breaker's ability to open and interrupt a fault current. This includes:
            *   **First Pole to Clear:** Testing the first phase to interrupt the fault.
            *   **Symmetrical Current Breaking:** Testing the breaker's capability to interrupt symmetrical fault currents.
            *   **Asymmetrical Current Breaking:** Testing interruption of fault currents with a DC offset.
            *   **Current Zero Characteristic Tests:** Evaluating the breaker's performance at the current zero, especially relevant for SF6 breakers.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuchler (2018)**, Chapter 11; **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 8.
*   **Load Current and Out-of-Phase Current Switching Tests:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the breaker's ability to switch normal load currents and to interrupt currents during out-of-phase conditions (e.g., due to system synchronization issues).
    *   **Reference:** **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, Chapter 8.
*   **Re-striking Voltage (RV) and Recovery Voltage (RV):**
    *   **Key Concepts:**
        *   **Restriking Voltage (RV):** The transient voltage that appears across the breaker contacts immediately after current zero, which tries to re-establish the arc.
        *   **Recovery Voltage (RV):** The steady-state voltage that appears across the contacts after the transient has died down.
    *   **Testing Significance:** The ability of the breaker to withstand and extinguish the arc depends on the characteristics of the restriking voltage (magnitude, frequency, rate of rise). Testing involves simulating these conditions.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 11; **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 8.
*   **Contact Resistance Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure low resistance at closed contacts.
    *   **Procedure:** Low voltage, high current measurement.
*   **Mechanical Operation Tests:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the reliability and speed of the operating mechanism.
    *   **Procedure:** Testing open/close operations, timing of contact movement.
*   **SF6 Gas Pressure and Purity Tests:**
    *   **Purpose:** For SF6 breakers, monitoring gas pressure and purity is crucial for effective arc quenching.
    *   **Procedure:** Gas density sensors, gas sampling for analysis.

### 4.3 Important Points to Remember for Circuit Breakers

*   Short-circuit testing is essential for circuit breakers to prove their interrupting capacity. This is typically done in specialized high power testing laboratories.
*   The concept of restriking voltage and its characteristics are fundamental to understanding circuit breaker arc extinction.
*   SF6 breakers are highly efficient due to the excellent dielectric and arc-quenching properties of SF6 gas.

## 5. High Voltage Testing of Transformers

Transformers are vital for voltage transformation in power systems. Their insulation system is critical for their reliable operation.

### 5.1 Types of Transformers

*   **Power Transformers**
*   **Distribution Transformers**
*   **Instrument Transformers (CTs and PTs)**

### 5.2 Key Tests for Transformers

*   **Dielectric Tests:**
    *   **Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test:**
        *   **Purpose:** To verify the insulation strength of the windings and bushings.
        *   **Procedure:** Applied between windings and to earth.
        *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.
    *   **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test (Lightning and Switching):**
        *   **Purpose:** To ensure the transformer can withstand transient overvoltages.
        *   **Procedure:** Applying standard impulse waveforms to the line terminals. Testing of windings and bushings.
        *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 9.
*   **Partial Discharge (PD) Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To detect internal insulation defects (voids, moisture) in the winding insulation and oil.
    *   **Procedure:** Measuring PD activity under AC voltage.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuchler (2018)**, Chapter 15.
*   **Turns Ratio Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the correct winding ratios.
    *   **Procedure:** Using a turns ratio meter.
*   **Polarity Test (for single-phase transformers):**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure correct connections for parallel operation.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying a low voltage and checking the output polarity.
*   **DC Resistance Measurement of Windings:**
    *   **Purpose:** To check for open circuits, poor connections, and winding integrity.
    *   **Procedure:** Using a micro-ohmmeter or Kelvin bridge.
*   **Load Test (for smaller transformers):**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify performance under load conditions (efficiency, temperature rise).
    *   **Procedure:** Applying a load and measuring parameters. For large transformers, this is often replaced by equivalent tests like the back-to-back test.
*   **Short-Circuit Impedance Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the impedance of the transformer, which affects load sharing and fault current levels.
    *   **Procedure:** Short-circuiting the low voltage winding and applying voltage to the high voltage winding.
*   **Dissipation Factor (Tan Delta) and Capacitance Measurement:**
    *   **Purpose:** To assess the condition of the insulation system. Similar to bushings.
    *   **Procedure:** Using a Schering bridge.
    *   **Reference:** **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, Chapter 7.

### 5.3 Important Points to Remember for Transformers

*   The insulation system of a transformer (oil and paper) is crucial and is tested rigorously.
*   Impulse testing is vital for large transformers, as they are exposed to lightning surges.
*   PD and Tan Delta tests provide valuable insights into the "health" of the transformer's insulation.

## 6. High Voltage Testing of Surge Diverters (Lightning Arresters)

Surge diverters are protective devices that limit overvoltages by providing a low-impedance path to earth for surge currents.

### 6.1 Types of Surge Diverters

*   **Expulsion Type:** Uses a gas-filled tube that extinguishes the power follow current.
*   **Valve Type:** Consists of series-connected series gaps and valve blocks (ZnO or Silicon Carbide). Modern surge arresters are primarily of the metal oxide varistor (MOV) type, which uses ZnO blocks.

### 6.2 Key Tests for Surge Diverters

*   **Power Frequency Sparkover Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the voltage at which the diverter starts to conduct at power frequency.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying a gradually increasing power frequency voltage.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.
*   **Impulse Sparkover Voltage Test (Lightning and Switching):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the voltage at which the diverter operates under transient overvoltages. This is a critical parameter for protection.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying standard impulse waveforms and measuring the peak voltage when flashover occurs.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 9.
*   **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the insulation of the diverter body.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying impulse voltage between the line terminal and earth.
*   **Residual Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To measure the voltage across the diverter when it is conducting a specified surge current. This is the "protective level" of the surge arrester.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying impulse currents (e.g., 8/20 µs) and measuring the peak voltage across the diverter.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuchler (2018)**, Chapter 13; **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, Chapter 9.
*   **1-minute Dry Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the insulation between the line terminal and earth.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying power frequency voltage for one minute.
*   **Di/dt Test (Rate of Rise of Current):**
    *   **Purpose:** For ZnO arresters, to assess their ability to withstand the rate of rise of current during a surge.
*   **Long Duration Current Pulse Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To assess the energy handling capability of the ZnO blocks.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying long duration impulse currents (e.g., 2000 µs).
*   **AC Leakage Current Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To monitor the condition of the ZnO blocks. Elevated leakage current can indicate degradation or impending failure.
    *   **Procedure:** Measuring leakage current at rated power frequency voltage.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 12.

### 6.3 Important Points to Remember for Surge Diverters

*   The primary function of a surge diverter is to limit overvoltages to a safe level for the protected equipment.
*   Residual voltage is a key performance parameter, indicating how effectively the diverter "clamps" the voltage.
*   Regular monitoring of leakage current is crucial for identifying deteriorating ZnO arresters.

## 7. High Voltage Testing of Cables

Cables are used for transmitting electrical power, often underground. Their insulation is critical for preventing failures.

### 7.1 Types of Cables

*   **Paper Insulated Lead Sheathed (PILCS) Cables:** Older technology.
*   **Mass Impregnated Non-Draining (MIND) Cables:** An improvement on PILCS.
*   **Extruded Solid Dielectric Cables (XLPE, EPR):** Most common for modern medium and high voltage applications.
*   **Oil-Filled Cables:** Used for very high voltages and long distances.

### 7.2 Key Tests for Cables

*   **High Voltage DC (HVDC) Proof Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the insulation strength of the cable. DC testing is often used for routine factory and site testing as it is less susceptible to surface effects and can detect insulation defects more readily.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying a DC voltage (typically 2 to 3 times the rated AC voltage) for a specified duration. However, care must be taken as DC can stress insulation differently than AC, and excessive DC stress can be detrimental.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5; **Naidu & Kamaraju (2004)**, Chapter 7.
*   **AC Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify insulation strength under AC operating conditions.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying AC voltage for a short duration.
    *   **Reference:** **Wadhwa (2011)**, Chapter 5.
*   **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure the cable can withstand lightning and switching surges.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying standard impulse waveforms to the cable.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuffel et al. (2005)**, Chapter 9.
*   **Partial Discharge (PD) Measurement:**
    *   **Purpose:** To detect internal voids, defects, or water trees in extruded cables.
    *   **Procedure:** Measuring PD activity under AC voltage, often using the "iguana" or "tan delta + PD" test method.
    *   **Reference:** **Kuchler (2018)**, Chapter 15.
*   **Tan Delta (Dissipation Factor) Measurement:**
    *   **Purpose:** To assess the condition of the insulation and detect moisture or degradation.
    *   **Procedure:** Using a Schering bridge.
*   **Capacitance Measurement:**
    *   **Purpose:** To check for cable damage or changes in insulation.
    *   **Procedure:** Measuring capacitance at power frequency.
*   **Heat Run Test (Load Cycle Test):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the temperature rise of the cable and its accessories under continuous load.
    *   **Procedure:** Applying rated current for an extended period.

### 7.3 Important Points to Remember for Cables

*   Cable testing is performed at both the factory (type tests) and the site (routine tests) to ensure quality and reliability.
*   HVDC testing is common for routine cable testing, but its interpretation requires understanding the differences in insulation stress compared to AC.
*   PD measurements are critical for extruded dielectric cables, which are susceptible to internal defects that can lead to water treeing and failure.

## Answers to Practice Questions

**Multiple Choice Answers:**

1.  **c) Short-circuit test**
2.  **b) Impulse sparkover voltage test**
3.  **b) Capacitance and Tan Delta measurement**
4.  **c) Extruded dielectric cables and transformer insulation**
5.  **c) Circuit breakers**

**Short Answer Answers:**

1.  **Restriking Voltage (RV):** The transient voltage that appears across the breaker contacts immediately after current zero, which tries to re-establish the arc. It has high frequency and high rate of rise.
    **Recovery Voltage (RV):** The steady-state voltage that appears across the contacts after the transient restriking voltage has died down. It is typically the system voltage. The breaker must withstand both to prevent re-ignition.

2.  The **Tan Delta (Dissipation Factor) test** measures the dielectric losses in the insulation. For transformers and bushings, an increase in Tan Delta indicates degradation of the insulation, such as moisture ingress or thermal aging. It helps in assessing the insulation condition and predicting its remaining life.

3.  **Power Frequency Withstand Voltage Test:** Verifies the ability to withstand continuous operating voltages and temporary overvoltages under normal atmospheric conditions.
    **Impulse Withstand Voltage Test:** Verifies the ability to withstand transient overvoltages caused by lightning or switching operations, which have very short durations and high rates of rise.

4.  **Purpose of HVDC Testing for Cables:** DC testing is often preferred for routine factory and site acceptance tests because it is simpler to generate and measure, less affected by surface conditions (like corona), and can detect gross insulation defects.
    **Limitations:** DC voltage stresses insulation differently from AC voltage. A cable that passes a DC withstand test might fail an AC withstand test. Excessive DC stress can also lead to irreversible degradation of some insulation materials (e.g., space charge build-up). The relationship between AC and DC withstand voltages is not linear and depends on the cable design and insulation material.

5.  The key parameters measured during surge arrester testing are:
    *   **Impulse Sparkover Voltage:** The peak voltage at which the arrester conducts for a lightning impulse (e.g., 1.2/50 µs) and switching impulse (e.g., 250/2500 µs).
    *   **Residual Voltage:** The peak voltage across the arrester when it conducts a specified surge current (e.g., 8/20 µs). This is the protective level.
    *   **AC Leakage Current:** Measures the current flowing through the arrester at rated power frequency voltage. Elevated or unstable leakage current can indicate degradation of the arrester's components (especially ZnO blocks).
    *   **Power Frequency Sparkover Voltage:** The voltage at which conduction begins at 50/60 Hz.
    *   **Energy Handling Capability:** Assessed through long duration current pulse tests.

## Important Points to Remember for the Entire Module

*   **Standardization:** Most high voltage tests are governed by international standards (IEC, IEEE) to ensure comparability and reliability.
*   **Test Setup:** Proper grounding, shielding, and clearances are critical during HV testing to ensure safety and accurate results.
*   **Failure Modes:** Understanding how equipment can fail under HV stress is key to interpreting test results.
*   **Routine vs. Type Tests:** Differentiate between tests performed on every piece of equipment (routine) and tests performed on a design basis (type).
*   **Environmental Conditions:** Temperature, humidity, and atmospheric pressure can affect test results and must be accounted for.
*   **Partial Discharge:** A sensitive indicator of insulation deterioration that can precede complete breakdown.
*   **Impulse Testing:** Crucial for verifying performance against transient overvoltages prevalent in power systems.
