# DESIGN OF SOLAR PV SYSTEMS

## Module 2: Solar Thermal System

### Topic: Solar Thermal System - Principle of Conversion of Solar Radiation into Heat

### 1. Introduction to Solar Thermal Systems

Solar thermal systems are designed to capture solar radiation and convert it directly into thermal energy (heat). This heat can then be used for a variety of applications, including water heating, space heating, industrial process heat, and even electricity generation (through solar thermal power plants). This module focuses on the fundamental principles of how solar radiation is converted into heat.

**Key Concept:** The core principle is the absorption of solar radiation by a collector material, which then heats up.

**Relevance to Course Outcomes:** This topic directly contributes to **CO1: Explain the basics of solar energy conversion systems**, as understanding solar thermal principles is foundational to comprehending various solar energy technologies.

---

### 2. Solar Radiation: The Energy Source

Before understanding conversion, it's crucial to understand the energy source itself.

**2.1. What is Solar Radiation?**

Solar radiation is electromagnetic radiation emitted by the Sun. It travels through space and reaches the Earth's atmosphere and surface.

**2.2. Components of Solar Radiation Reaching the Earth's Surface:**

As solar radiation passes through the Earth's atmosphere, it undergoes scattering and absorption. The radiation that reaches the surface can be broadly categorized into:

*   **Direct Radiation (Beam Radiation):** This is the radiation that travels directly from the Sun's disc to the Earth's surface without being significantly scattered or absorbed by the atmosphere. It is highly directional.
    *   **Important Point:** Direct radiation is essential for concentrating solar power applications and for achieving higher temperatures.
*   **Diffuse Radiation:** This is the solar radiation that has been scattered by atmospheric particles (like dust, water vapor, and air molecules) and clouds. It comes from all directions in the sky.
    *   **Important Point:** Diffuse radiation is significant, especially on cloudy days, and contributes to the overall solar energy received.
*   **Reflected Radiation:** This is the solar radiation that bounces off the Earth's surface and surrounding objects. It is generally less significant than direct and diffuse radiation but can contribute to the energy received by a collector.

**Reference:** *Solar Energy-Fundamentals, Design, Modelling and Applications* by G.N. Tiwari (Chapter 2: Solar Radiation) extensively covers these aspects.

### 3. The Principle of Conversion: Absorption and Heat Transfer

The fundamental principle of converting solar radiation into heat involves two primary steps:

**3.1. Absorption of Solar Radiation:**

*   **The Collector:** The heart of a solar thermal system is the **solar collector**, which is designed to absorb as much solar radiation as possible.
*   **Absorber Material:** The collector typically features an **absorber surface** made of materials with high absorptivity in the solar spectrum (wavelengths from 0.3 to 3 micrometers). Common materials include:
    *   Black painted surfaces (flat black paint)
    *   Selective coatings (materials that absorb solar radiation efficiently but emit thermal radiation poorly)
*   **Mechanism:** When solar radiation falls on the absorber surface, the photons are absorbed, and their energy is converted into thermal energy, increasing the kinetic energy of the atoms and molecules in the absorber material, leading to an increase in temperature.

**Example:** Imagine a black t-shirt on a sunny day versus a white t-shirt. The black t-shirt absorbs more sunlight and feels hotter because its surface has a higher absorptivity.

**Reference:** *Solar Photovoltaics: Fundamentals, Technologies And Applications* by Chetan Singh Solanki (Chapter on Solar Thermal Collectors) discusses absorber materials and their properties.

**3.2. Heat Transfer:**

Once the absorber material heats up, the thermal energy needs to be transferred to a working fluid (like water, air, or an oil) for practical use. There are three main modes of heat transfer involved:

*   **Conduction:** Heat is transferred through direct contact within the absorber material and from the absorber to the working fluid.
    *   **Example:** Heat from the absorber surface is conducted through its thickness to reach the fluid flowing through tubes attached to it.
*   **Convection:** Heat is transferred by the movement of the working fluid. As the fluid comes into contact with the heated absorber, it heats up and moves away, carrying the thermal energy.
    *   **Example:** Hot fluid rising in a pipe and cooler fluid descending to be heated.
*   **Radiation:** The heated absorber also emits thermal radiation. This can be heat lost to the surroundings or can be intentionally absorbed by another surface if designed to do so (e.g., in concentrating solar collectors).

**Important Point:** Efficient heat transfer is crucial to maximize the useful heat gained from the absorbed solar radiation. Minimizing heat losses to the environment is equally important.

### 4. Types of Solar Collectors and their Principles

While the fundamental principle of absorption remains the same, different collector designs enhance efficiency and achieve different temperature ranges.

**4.1. Flat Plate Collectors:**

*   **Principle:** A flat absorber plate (often painted black or with a selective coating) is enclosed in an insulated box with a transparent cover (glass or plastic). Heat is transferred to a fluid circulating through tubes attached to or integrated within the absorber plate.
*   **Heat Transfer:** Conduction from absorber to tubes, convection from tubes to fluid, and some heat loss through convection and radiation from the collector surface.
*   **Applications:** Low to medium temperature applications like domestic water heating and space heating.
*   **Reference:** *Solar Energy-Fundamentals, Design, Modelling and Applications* by G.N. Tiwari (Chapter 4: Solar Thermal Collectors) provides detailed information on flat plate collectors.

**4.2. Evacuated Tube Collectors:**

*   **Principle:** Consists of a series of glass tubes, each containing an absorber fin inside a vacuum. The vacuum significantly reduces heat loss by convection and conduction.
*   **Heat Transfer:** Solar radiation passes through the outer glass tube and is absorbed by the absorber fin. Heat is then transferred to a fluid flowing through an inner tube. The vacuum between the tubes minimizes heat loss.
*   **Applications:** Similar to flat plate collectors but generally more efficient, especially in colder climates or when higher temperatures are required.
*   **Example:** Often seen in residential solar water heating systems.

**4.3. Concentrating Solar Collectors:**

*   **Principle:** Uses mirrors or lenses to focus (concentrate) direct solar radiation onto a smaller absorber area. This significantly increases the intensity of radiation on the absorber, allowing for much higher temperatures.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Parabolic Troughs:** Use a parabolic reflector to focus sunlight onto a receiver tube placed at the focal line.
    *   **Parabolic Dishes:** Use a parabolic dish reflector to focus sunlight onto a receiver at the focal point.
    *   **Heliostats:** Large, flat mirrors that track the sun and reflect its radiation onto a central receiver.
*   **Heat Transfer:** High absorption efficiency due to concentrated radiation. Heat transfer to the working fluid is critical. Heat losses can be significant at high temperatures due to radiation.
*   **Applications:** High-temperature applications, including solar thermal power generation (for steam production to drive turbines).
*   **Reference:** *Solar Photovoltaics: Fundamentals, Technologies And Applications* by Chetan Singh Solanki (Chapter on Concentrating Solar Collectors) and *Grid Integration of Solar Photovoltaic Systems* by D.P. Kothari, M Jamil. (Chapter on Solar Thermal Power Systems) touch upon these aspects for power generation.

### 5. Factors Affecting Heat Conversion Efficiency

Several factors influence how effectively solar radiation is converted into usable heat:

*   **Solar Irradiance:** The intensity of solar radiation (W/m²) directly impacts the amount of energy available.
*   **Collector Design and Material Properties:**
    *   **Absorptivity and Emissivity:** High absorptivity for solar radiation and low emissivity for thermal radiation (selective coatings) are desirable.
    *   **Heat Transfer Area and Fluid Flow Rate:** Efficient heat transfer requires adequate surface area and optimized fluid flow.
*   **Heat Losses:**
    *   **Conduction Losses:** Through insulation and structural components.
    *   **Convection Losses:** From the collector surface to the surrounding air.
    *   **Radiation Losses:** Emission of thermal radiation from the absorber surface.
*   **Angle of Incidence:** The angle at which solar radiation strikes the collector surface. Maximum absorption occurs when the radiation is perpendicular to the surface. This is why solar tracking systems are used in some applications.
*   **Ambient Temperature and Wind Speed:** Higher ambient temperatures and wind speeds increase heat losses from the collector.

### 6. Energy Balance of a Solar Collector

A solar collector gains energy from absorbed solar radiation and loses energy through various mechanisms. The useful heat gain is the difference between absorbed energy and losses.

**Key Equation (Conceptual):**

*   **Useful Heat Gain (Q_u)** = **Absorbed Solar Radiation (Q_a)** - **Heat Losses (Q_L)**

Where:
*   **Q_a** is proportional to the incident solar radiation, collector area, and absorptivity.
*   **Q_L** is influenced by collector design, temperature difference between the collector and ambient, and environmental conditions.

**Reference:** *Solar Energy-Fundamentals, Design, Modelling and Applications* by G.N. Tiwari (Chapter 4) provides detailed energy balance equations for collectors.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   Solar thermal conversion relies on the **absorption** of solar radiation by an absorber surface.
*   The **absorber material's properties** (absorptivity, emissivity) are critical for efficient conversion.
*   **Heat transfer** (conduction, convection, radiation) is essential to move the generated heat to a working fluid.
*   **Minimizing heat losses** to the environment is as important as maximizing absorption.
*   Different **collector types** (flat plate, evacuated tube, concentrating) are designed for different temperature ranges and applications by optimizing these principles.

### 9. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** What are the three main components of solar radiation that reach the Earth's surface?
    **Answer:** Direct radiation, diffuse radiation, and reflected radiation.

**Question 2:** Briefly explain the two primary steps involved in converting solar radiation into heat.
    **Answer:** The first step is the absorption of solar radiation by an absorber material. The second step is the transfer of this thermal energy to a working fluid.

**Question 3:** Why are selective coatings used in solar thermal collectors?
    **Answer:** Selective coatings have high absorptivity for solar radiation and low emissivity for thermal radiation. This maximizes the absorption of incoming sunlight while minimizing the re-emission of heat, thereby improving efficiency.

**Question 4:** Which type of solar collector is best suited for generating high temperatures required for power generation, and why?
    **Answer:** Concentrating solar collectors are best suited for high temperatures. They use mirrors or lenses to focus direct solar radiation onto a smaller absorber area, significantly increasing the energy flux and achieving higher temperatures.

**Question 5 (Application):** You are designing a simple solar water heater for a household. What type of collector would you likely choose and why? What material would you ideally use for the absorber plate?

    **Answer:**
    *   **Collector Type:** A **flat plate collector** would be a suitable choice for domestic water heating. It is cost-effective, relatively simple to manufacture, and can achieve the required temperatures for hot water.
    *   **Absorber Material:** An **all-black painted copper absorber plate** or a plate with a **selective coating** would be ideal. Copper offers excellent thermal conductivity. Black paint provides good absorptivity, while a selective coating would further enhance efficiency by reducing thermal re-radiation.
