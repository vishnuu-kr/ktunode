# CIRCUITS & NETWORKS: Module 1 - Mesh and Nodal Analysis (Review) - DC Circuit Applications

This module serves as a crucial review of fundamental circuit analysis techniques – Mesh Analysis and Nodal Analysis – with a specific focus on their application to Direct Current (DC) circuits. Mastering these methods is essential for effectively solving more complex AC circuits and understanding various network behaviors as outlined in the Course Outcomes.

## 1. Introduction to Circuit Analysis Techniques

Circuit analysis is the process of determining the voltages and currents in all parts of an electrical circuit. For DC circuits, we often deal with sources that provide a constant voltage or current. Mesh and Nodal analysis are powerful, systematic methods that can be applied to any planar circuit, regardless of the complexity of the network.

### 1.1. Key Concepts & Definitions

*   **Node:** A point in a circuit where two or more circuit elements are connected.
*   **Branch:** A path between two nodes.
*   **Mesh:** A loop within a planar circuit that does not contain any other smaller loops.
*   **Planar Circuit:** A circuit that can be drawn on a plane such that no two branches cross each other.
*   **Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL):** The algebraic sum of currents entering a node is equal to the algebraic sum of currents leaving the node. (Sum of currents at a node = 0).
*   **Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL):** The algebraic sum of voltage rises around any closed loop in a circuit is equal to the algebraic sum of voltage drops. (Sum of voltages around a loop = 0).
*   **Ohm's Law:** The voltage across a resistor is directly proportional to the current flowing through it, and the constant of proportionality is the resistance ($V = IR$).
*   **Dependent Sources:** Sources whose voltage or current output depends on the voltage or current elsewhere in the circuit.
*   **Independent Sources:** Sources whose voltage or current output is not dependent on any other part of the circuit.
