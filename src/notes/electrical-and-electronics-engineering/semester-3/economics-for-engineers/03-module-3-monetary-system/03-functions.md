# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 3: Monetary System - Topic: Functions

## 1. Introduction to the Monetary System

The monetary system is the set of institutions, policies, and instruments that a country uses to manage its money supply, credit, and financial markets. In essence, it's the framework that governs how money is created, distributed, and used within an economy. For engineers, understanding the monetary system is crucial because it directly influences investment decisions, project financing, cost of capital, and overall economic conditions that affect engineering ventures.

**Key Concept:** **Money** is anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange for goods and services, a unit of account, and a store of value.

**Learning Outcome 1:** Understand the fundamentals of various economic issues using laws and learn the concepts of demand, supply, elasticity and production function.
*(While this topic focuses on the monetary system, understanding money's role as a medium of exchange relates to the fundamental concept of transactions which are driven by demand and supply.)*

**Course Outcome Alignment:**
*   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding the role of money and its functions is a foundational economic concept.

## 2. Functions of Money

Money performs several vital functions that facilitate economic activity. These functions are the pillars upon which a modern economy operates.

### 2.1. Medium of Exchange

*   **Definition:** Money serves as an intermediary in transactions, eliminating the need for barter. In a barter system, goods are directly exchanged for other goods, which requires a "double coincidence of wants" (both parties wanting what the other has to offer). Money simplifies this by being universally accepted.
*   **Importance for Engineers:**
    *   When procuring raw materials, equipment, or services, engineers use money as the accepted payment.
    *   Receiving payment for engineering services or products is done in money.
    *   This function allows for specialization in production and consumption, increasing overall efficiency.
*   **Example:** An engineering firm needs to buy specialized software. Instead of trying to barter its design services for the software, it pays the software company in money. The software company then uses that money to pay its employees, buy hardware, etc.
*   **From Textbooks:**
    *   **Geetika, Ghosh, and Chodhury (2015):** Emphasize that money overcomes the "double coincidence of wants" problem inherent in barter.
    *   **Thuesen and Fabrycky (1966):** While more focused on engineering economy, they implicitly acknowledge the role of money in all financial transactions for projects.

### 2.2. Unit of Account (Measure of Value)

*   **Definition:** Money provides a common measure for the value of all goods and services. It allows us to express the worth of diverse items in a standardized way, making it easier to compare prices and make economic calculations.
*   **Importance for Engineers:**
    *   **Cost Estimation:** Engineers use money to quantify the cost of materials, labor, and overhead for projects.
    *   **Budgeting:** Project budgets are denominated in monetary units.
    *   **Financial Analysis:** Evaluating the economic feasibility of projects, calculating profits, and comparing investment alternatives all rely on money as a unit of account.
    *   **National Income Accounting:** Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and other macroeconomic indicators are measured in monetary terms.
*   **Example:** An engineer can compare the cost of two different types of steel alloys by looking at their price per kilogram. This allows for an informed decision based on value for money. Similarly, a project's profitability is measured by comparing its total revenue (in monetary terms) against its total costs (in monetary terms).
*   **From Textbooks:**
    *   **Paneerselvam (2012):** Highlights money's role in establishing prices and facilitating economic calculations, which is fundamental to engineering economics.
    *   **Khan (2011):** Discusses how money acts as a yardstick for valuing assets and liabilities in the financial system.

### 2.3. Store of Value

*   **Definition:** Money allows individuals and businesses to save purchasing power for the future. It is a way to transfer wealth from the present to the future, although its effectiveness as a store of value can be affected by inflation.
*   **Importance for Engineers:**
    *   **Investment Planning:** Savings in monetary form can be invested in future projects or R&D.
    *   **Financial Reserves:** Companies maintain monetary reserves for unexpected expenses or future capital expenditures.
    *   **Long-term Projects:** The value of future revenues from a long-term engineering project needs to be stored and potentially reinvested.
*   **Example:** An engineering firm earns a profit this year. It can hold onto this money (store of value) to invest in a new piece of machinery or research next year. However, if inflation is high, the purchasing power of that saved money might decrease.
*   **From Textbooks:**
    *   **Park (2001):** Explains that money's ability to store value makes it an attractive asset for individuals and firms to hold, facilitating saving and investment.
    *   **Blank and Tarquin (7th Edition):** Discuss the time value of money, where present money has more value than future money, implicitly acknowledging money's role as a store of value.

### 2.4. Standard of Deferred Payment

*   **Definition:** Money serves as a standard for future payments, especially for debts and credit transactions. This function allows for borrowing and lending, which are essential for economic growth and large-scale projects.
*   **Importance for Engineers:**
    *   **Financing Projects:** Engineering projects often require significant upfront capital, which is obtained through loans or other forms of credit. These debts are denominated and repaid in money over time.
    *   **Contracts:** Engineering contracts often specify payment terms for future deliverables, all in monetary units.
    *   **Leasing and Installment Purchases:** Engineers and firms often acquire equipment through deferred payment plans.
*   **Example:** A construction company takes out a loan to build a new bridge. The loan agreement specifies monthly payments in dollars over 20 years. Money acts as the standard by which these future payments are measured and settled.
*   **From Textbooks:**
    *   **Geetika, Ghosh, and Chodhury (2015):** Highlight this function as crucial for the development of credit markets and for facilitating long-term business contracts.
    *   **Newman and Lavelle (2002):** In the context of engineering economics, this function underpins the concept of loan repayments and the time value of money in financial planning.

## 3. Importance of Money in Engineering Economics

Understanding the functions of money is directly relevant to several core concepts in engineering economics.

*   **Decision Making (CO2):** The ability to compare costs and benefits (measured in money), evaluate investment alternatives, and make sound financial decisions for engineering projects hinges on money's functions as a unit of account and medium of exchange.
*   **Financing (CO3):** Monetary policy and the availability of credit (which relies on money as a store of value and standard of deferred payment) significantly impact the feasibility and cost of financing engineering projects.
*   **Capital Budgeting (CO4):** Techniques like Net Present Value (NPV) and Internal Rate of Return (IRR) are entirely based on the concept of money's time value and its ability to be invested and stored.
*   **Value Analysis and Engineering (CO4):** Assessing the cost-effectiveness of design choices and material selections inherently involves monetary comparisons.

## 4. Types of Money (Brief Overview)

While not the core focus of "Functions," understanding the evolution of money helps appreciate its role.

*   **Commodity Money:** Money whose value is derived from the commodity of which it is made (e.g., gold, silver, salt).
*   **Fiat Money:** Money that has been declared legal tender by a government, but is not backed by a physical commodity (e.g., banknotes, coins). Its value is based on trust and general acceptance.
*   **Giry Money/Digital Money:** Electronic forms of money, including balances in bank accounts and digital currencies.

**Important Point to Remember:** The functions of money are universal, regardless of the form money takes. However, the stability and efficiency of the monetary system are influenced by the type of money used and the policies governing it.

## 5. Practice Questions

1.  **Explain why money is a better medium of exchange than barter, using the concept of "double coincidence of wants."**
    *   **Answer:** Barter requires that both parties in a transaction have something the other desires. Money, being universally accepted, eliminates this need. For example, an engineer who designs bridges might not need furniture, but if they are paid in money, they can use that money to buy furniture from a furniture maker who needs money to pay for raw materials.
2.  **How does money function as a "unit of account" benefit an engineering project manager?**
    *   **Answer:** As a unit of account, money allows the project manager to accurately estimate costs of materials, labor, and equipment, create budgets, compare the costs of different design alternatives, and ultimately assess the financial viability and profitability of the project.
3.  **If a government experiences very high inflation, which function of money is most severely impacted? Explain why.**
    *   **Answer:** The "store of value" function is most severely impacted. High inflation erodes the purchasing power of money over time. If the money held today can buy significantly less in the future, it becomes a poor store of wealth.
4.  **An engineering firm enters into a contract to deliver a complex piece of machinery in 18 months, with payment due upon delivery. Which function of money is most evident in this scenario?**
    *   **Answer:** The "standard of deferred payment." The contract specifies a future payment obligation in monetary terms, enabling the firm to undertake the production and the customer to acquire the machinery without immediate cash outlay.
5.  **Imagine you are designing a new manufacturing process. How would money's function as a "unit of account" be used in your decision-making process?**
    *   **Answer:** I would use money to assign costs to different materials, energy sources, labor requirements, and equipment for various design options. By expressing all these costs in monetary terms, I can compare different process designs on a cost-benefit basis to select the most economically viable one.

## 7. Important Points to Remember

*   All four functions are essential for a well-functioning economy and are directly applicable to engineering economic decisions.
*   The efficiency of money as a "store of value" is crucial for long-term investment planning in engineering.
*   The "standard of deferred payment" function is fundamental to understanding project financing and the cost of capital.
*   Engineers rely on money as a "unit of account" to quantify costs, benefits, and profitability of their projects.
*   Money's role as a "medium of exchange" simplifies all commercial transactions related to engineering activities.
