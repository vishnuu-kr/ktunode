# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 4: Value Analysis and Value Engineering

## Topic: Value Engineering Procedure

---

### **Introduction to Value Engineering (VE)**

Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic and organized approach to identifying opportunities for improving the value of a product, service, or process. It focuses on maximizing the function and minimizing the cost without sacrificing quality, reliability, performance, or safety.

*   **Value (V)** is defined as the ratio of Function (F) to Cost (C): **V = F / C**.
*   **Objective of VE:** To improve value by increasing function, reducing cost, or both.

---

### **Relationship to Value Analysis (VA)**

While often used interchangeably, there's a subtle difference:

*   **Value Analysis (VA):** Primarily applied to *existing* products or services, usually in the manufacturing or purchasing phase, with a focus on cost reduction.
*   **Value Engineering (VE):** Applied during the *design and development* phase of a product or service, aiming to achieve the required functions at the lowest possible life cycle cost.

Both methodologies share the same core principles and are driven by a multidisciplinary team.

*(Referenced from: Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury, Chapter on Cost-Output Relations)*

### **The Value Engineering Procedure (The VE Job Plan)**

The VE procedure is a structured, phased approach that guides the VE team through the process. It typically consists of the following phases:

#### **Phase 1: Information Phase**

*   **Objective:** To gather all necessary information about the product, service, or process to be analyzed. This phase establishes a thorough understanding of the "as-is" state.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Define the Scope:** Clearly identify the product, component, or process under study.
    *   **Gather Data:** Collect comprehensive data on:
        *   **Function:** What does it do? (Primary and Secondary functions)
        *   **Cost:** All costs associated with the item (design, material, labor, manufacturing, overhead, marketing, etc.).
        *   **Performance:** How well does it perform its functions?
        *   **Quality:** Specifications, tolerances, reliability data.
        *   **Materials and Manufacturing Processes:** Details of what it's made of and how it's made.
        *   **Customer Requirements:** What does the user need and expect?
        *   **Marketplace:** Competitor products, pricing, industry standards.
        *   **Regulatory Requirements:** Safety, environmental, legal constraints.
    *   **Organize Data:** Present the gathered information in a clear, understandable format (e.g., charts, graphs, reports).
*   **Tools/Techniques:** Brainstorming, interviews, site visits, documentation review, cost breakdown structures, functional diagrams.
*   **Important Point:** The quality of information gathered in this phase directly impacts the effectiveness of subsequent phases.

#### **Phase 2: Functional Analysis Phase**

*   **Objective:** To identify and understand the functions performed by the item and their relative importance. This is the core of VE.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Define Functions:** Identify all functions the item performs.
        *   **Primary Function:** The main purpose for which the item exists. (e.g., for a screw, it's to fasten).
        *   **Secondary Function:** Additional functions that support the primary function. (e.g., for a screw, it might be to provide a smooth surface or electrical insulation).
        *   **Tertiary Functions:** Often non-essential or detrimental functions.
    *   **Use the "What" Method:** Ask "What does it do?" for each component or action. The answer to "What does it do?" becomes the action verb for the next "What" question.
    *   **Prioritize Functions:** Determine the criticality or importance of each function.
    *   **Cost/Function Analysis:** Allocate costs to each function to understand where the most value can be achieved. Identify functions that are over-served or under-served by cost.
*   **Tools/Techniques:**
    *   **Function Analysis System Technique (FAST Diagramming):** A graphical method to organize and visualize functions, showing their relationships, dependencies, and time sequences. It helps identify the "best" way to achieve a function.
        *   *How it works:* Functions are expressed as verb-noun pairs (e.g., "Transmit Torque," "Support Load"). They are arranged in a logical sequence, moving from "Why" (the ultimate purpose) to "How" (the lowest level of detail).
    *   **Cost-Function Matrix:** A table that lists functions and their associated costs, helping to pinpoint high-cost functions with low importance.
*   **Important Point:** Focus on *what the item does*, not *what it is*. This shifts the perspective from components to their purposes.

*(Referenced from: Engineering Economy by R. Paneerselvam, Chapter 7 on Engineering Design and Cost Considerations)*
*(Referenced from: Contemporary Engineering Economics by Chan S. Park, Chapter 11 on Value Engineering and Analysis)*

#### **Phase 3: Creative Phase (Brainstorming/Ideation)**

*   **Objective:** To generate a wide range of alternative solutions or ideas to achieve the identified functions at a lower cost or with improved performance.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Brainstorming:** Generate as many ideas as possible without judgment. Quantity over quality at this stage.
    *   **Techniques:**
        *   **Brainstorming:** Free association of ideas.
        *   **Checklists:** Using lists of proven VE approaches or questions.
        *   **Attribute Listing:** Breaking down an item into its attributes and thinking of alternatives for each.
        *   **Synectics:** Using analogies and metaphors to stimulate thinking.
        *   **Value Improvement Suggestions (VIS):** Specific ideas to modify or replace components/processes.
*   **Principles of Brainstorming:**
    *   **Defer Judgment:** No criticism of ideas.
    *   **Encourage Wild Ideas:** The more unusual, the better.
    *   **Build on the Ideas of Others:** Combine and improve existing ideas.
    *   **Go for Quantity:** Generate as many ideas as possible.
*   **Important Point:** Encourage a non-judgmental environment to foster creativity.

*(Referenced from: Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury, Chapter on Innovation and Entrepreneurship - principles of creative problem solving are applicable)*

#### **Phase 4: Evaluation Phase (Analysis/Development)**

*   **Objective:** To analyze and evaluate the generated ideas based on feasibility, cost-effectiveness, potential benefits, and risks.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Filter Ideas:** Eliminate impractical or unachievable ideas.
    *   **Develop Promising Ideas:** Flesh out the viable ideas into concrete proposals.
    *   **Analyze Alternatives:**
        *   **Cost Estimation:** Estimate the cost of implementing each alternative.
        *   **Benefit Analysis:** Quantify the expected benefits (cost savings, performance improvements, etc.).
        *   **Feasibility Study:** Assess technical, operational, and economic feasibility.
        *   **Risk Assessment:** Identify potential risks and mitigation strategies.
        *   **Life Cycle Cost Analysis:** Consider all costs over the entire life of the product/service.
    *   **Select the Best Alternative(s):** Choose the proposal(s) that offer the greatest improvement in value.
*   **Tools/Techniques:**
    *   **Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA):** Comparing the total expected costs against the total expected benefits.
    *   **Break-Even Analysis:** Determining the point where costs and revenues are equal.
    *   **Payback Period:** The time it takes for an investment to be recovered.
    *   **Net Present Value (NPV) / Internal Rate of Return (IRR):** Capital budgeting techniques to evaluate investment projects.
    *   **Sensitivity Analysis:** Examining how changes in key variables affect the outcome.
*   **Important Point:** Use objective criteria and analytical tools to make informed decisions.

*(Referenced from: Engineering Economy by H. G. Thuesen, W. J. Fabrycky, Chapters on Economic Decision Making and Project Evaluation)*
*(Referenced from: Engineering Economics and analysis by Donald G. Newman, Jerome P. Lavelle, Chapters on Cost Estimation and Evaluation of Alternatives)*
*(Course Outcome CO4: Make use of the possibilities of value analysis and engineering, and solve simple business problems using break even analysis, cost benefit analysis and capital budgeting techniques.)*

#### **Phase 5: Development Phase (Proposal/Presentation)**

*   **Objective:** To develop detailed proposals for the selected alternatives and present them to management for approval and implementation.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Prepare Detailed Proposal:** Document the chosen solution, including:
        *   Problem statement and current approach.
        *   Proposed VE solution and its technical details.
        *   Estimated costs of implementation.
        *   Quantified benefits (cost savings, performance improvements, etc.).
        *   Risk assessment and mitigation.
        *   Implementation plan.
        *   Return on Investment (ROI) analysis.
    *   **Present to Management:** Clearly and concisely communicate the value proposition of the proposed solution.
    *   **Gain Approval:** Secure the necessary resources and authorization for implementation.
*   **Important Point:** A well-prepared and persuasive presentation is crucial for gaining acceptance of the VE proposal.

#### **Phase 6: Implementation Phase**

*   **Objective:** To put the approved VE proposal into action and ensure its successful execution.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Action Plan:** Develop a detailed plan for implementing the changes.
    *   **Resource Allocation:** Assign resources (personnel, budget, equipment).
    *   **Execution:** Carry out the implementation according to the plan.
    *   **Project Management:** Oversee the implementation process.
*   **Important Point:** Effective project management is critical for successful implementation.

#### **Phase 7: Verification Phase (Follow-up)**

*   **Objective:** To verify that the implemented changes have achieved the expected benefits and to document the results.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Monitor Performance:** Track the product, service, or process after implementation.
    *   **Measure Results:** Quantify the actual cost savings, performance improvements, and other benefits achieved.
    *   **Compare Actual vs. Expected:** Analyze any deviations and understand the reasons.
    *   **Document Savings:** Record the realized savings and benefits for future reference and learning.
    *   **Continuous Improvement:** Identify opportunities for further optimization.
*   **Important Point:** This phase closes the loop and provides valuable feedback for future VE efforts.

### **Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Value:** The relationship between function and cost (V = F/C).
*   **Function:** The primary or secondary purpose of a product or service.
*   **Cost:** All expenses incurred in designing, manufacturing, marketing, and supporting a product or service.
*   **FAST Diagram:** A graphical tool used in the functional analysis phase to organize functions and identify the "how" and "why" of their existence.
*   **Brainstorming:** A creative problem-solving technique used to generate a large number of ideas.
*   **Life Cycle Cost (LCC):** The total cost of a product or service from its inception to its disposal.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA):** A systematic process for calculating and comparing benefits and costs of a project, decision, or government policy.
*   **Break-Even Point:** The level of sales at which total revenue equals total costs.

### **Examples**

**Example 1: Simplifying a Mechanical Assembly**

*   **Product:** A complex bracket assembly with multiple parts.
*   **Information Phase:** Gathered data on all components, their costs, assembly time, and function (supporting a specific load).
*   **Functional Analysis:** Identified the primary function: "support load." Secondary functions included "ensure alignment" and "provide attachment points." Found that one small metal shim was performing a minor alignment function but was costly to manufacture and install.
*   **Creative Phase:** Brainstormed alternatives:
    *   Redesign the main bracket to incorporate the shim's function.
    *   Use a different fastening method that eliminated the need for the shim.
    *   Source a cheaper shim material.
*   **Evaluation Phase:**
    *   Redesigning the bracket offered significant cost savings by eliminating the shim, reducing parts, and simplifying assembly. Estimated a 20% reduction in overall bracket cost.
    *   The alternative fastening method was technically feasible but had a higher initial investment cost.
*   **Development/Implementation:** Proposed redesigning the bracket.
*   **Verification:** After implementation, confirmed the 20% cost reduction and improved assembly efficiency.

**Example 2: Improving a Software Service**

*   **Service:** Online customer support portal.
*   **Information Phase:** Analyzed website traffic, user feedback, support ticket volume, and the cost of maintaining the portal.
*   **Functional Analysis:** Primary function: "Provide customer support." Secondary functions: "Answer FAQs," "Allow ticket submission," "Track ticket status." Noticed a high volume of simple, repetitive queries that were being manually answered, increasing support costs.
*   **Creative Phase:** Brainstormed:
    *   Develop an AI-powered chatbot to answer common FAQs.
    *   Enhance the FAQ section with better search functionality.
    *   Implement self-service troubleshooting guides.
*   **Evaluation Phase:** The chatbot solution showed the highest potential for cost savings by deflecting a significant portion of incoming queries. Estimated a 30% reduction in support staff time spent on routine questions.
*   **Development/Implementation:** Piloted and then rolled out the AI chatbot.
*   **Verification:** Monitored ticket volume and user satisfaction. Confirmed a significant drop in manually handled queries and positive user feedback on the chatbot's responsiveness.

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

1.  **Value Engineering (VE)** is a systematic and organized approach to identify opportunities for improving the value of a product, service, or process. Its core objective is to **maximize function and minimize cost** without sacrificing quality, reliability, performance, or safety, thereby **increasing value (V = F/C)**.
2.  The key phases of the Value Engineering procedure are:
    *   **Information Phase:** Gathering all relevant data.
    *   **Functional Analysis Phase:** Identifying and analyzing functions and their costs.
    *   **Creative Phase:** Generating alternative solutions.
    *   **Evaluation Phase:** Analyzing and selecting the best alternatives.
    *   **Development Phase:** Preparing and presenting proposals.
    *   **Implementation Phase:** Putting approved solutions into action.
    *   **Verification Phase:** Monitoring and documenting results.
3.  **Value Analysis (VA)** is typically applied to *existing* products or services, focusing on cost reduction during manufacturing or procurement. **Value Engineering (VE)** is applied during the *design and development* phase, aiming to achieve functions at the lowest life cycle cost from the outset.
4.  The **FAST (Functional Analysis System Technique) diagram** is used in the functional analysis phase to graphically organize functions, show their interrelationships and dependencies, and identify the "how" and "why" of their existence. It helps the team understand how to achieve the required functions in the most efficient way.
5.  **Payback Period Calculation:**
    Payback Period = Initial Investment / Annual Savings
    Payback Period = $50,000 / $15,000 = **3.33 years**
6.  A scenario where a multidisciplinary team is essential: **Designing a new electric vehicle.**
    *   **Mechanical Engineers:** For powertrain, chassis, and body design.
    *   **Electrical Engineers:** For battery systems, motor control, and electronics.
    *   **Materials Scientists:** For selecting lightweight and durable materials.
    *   **Software Engineers:** For vehicle control systems and infotainment.
    *   **Manufacturing Engineers:** For designing efficient production processes.
    *   **Marketing/Sales:** For understanding customer needs and market viability.
    *   **Finance/Cost Analysts:** For managing budgets and ensuring cost-effectiveness.
    This diverse expertise ensures all aspects of the product are considered from its inception, maximizing its value and minimizing potential design flaws or cost overruns.

---

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Value is Function divided by Cost.** The goal is to improve this ratio.
*   **Focus on Functions, not Features.** Understand what the product *does*, not just what it *is*.
*   **Multidisciplinary Team is Crucial.** Diverse perspectives lead to better solutions.
*   **Creativity without Judgment.** The brainstorming phase requires an open mind.
*   **Systematic Approach.** The VE Job Plan provides a structured methodology.
*   **Life Cycle Costing.** Consider all costs, not just initial purchase price.
*   **Verification is Key.** Ensure implemented changes deliver the promised benefits.
