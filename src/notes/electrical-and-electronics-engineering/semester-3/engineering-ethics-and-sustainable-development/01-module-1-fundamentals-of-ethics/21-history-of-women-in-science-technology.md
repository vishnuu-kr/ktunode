### 1. Introduction: Why Study the History of Women in Science & Technology?

*   **Understanding Historical Injustice and Bias:** Recognizing past discrimination against women in STEM fields is crucial for ethical engineering practice (CO1). It highlights the importance of fairness, equity, and challenging implicit biases.
*   **Challenging Stereotypes:** The history of women in STEM often involves overcoming societal expectations and stereotypes that limited their opportunities. This helps us recognize and dismantle similar barriers today.
*   **Appreciating Diverse Contributions:** Many significant scientific and technological advancements were made by women who were often overlooked or uncredited. Acknowledging these contributions enriches our understanding of innovation and promotes a more inclusive view of progress. This directly relates to CO2.
*   **Informing Future Practices:** Learning from the past allows us to build more equitable and supportive environments for women and underrepresented groups in STEM, crucial for sustainable development and diverse problem-solving (CO3, CO4, CO5).

### 2. Early Contributions (Pre-20th Century)

While formal access to education and professional recognition was limited, women made vital contributions in various scientific and technological spheres.

*   **Ancient World:**
    *   **Hypatia of Alexandria (c. 350–415 AD):** A renowned philosopher, astronomer, and mathematician. She taught in Alexandria and is credited with commentaries on astronomical works. Her life and tragic death highlight the challenges faced by intellectual women in antiquity.
    *   **Emilie du Châtelet (1706-1749):** A French natural philosopher and mathematician. She translated and commented on Newton's *Principia Mathematica*, adding her own insights into physics. She also conducted experiments on the nature of heat. Her work bridged the gap between Newtonian physics and later developments.

*   **Industrial Revolution Era:**
    *   **Ada Lovelace (1815-1852):** Often considered the first computer programmer. She worked with Charles Babbage on his Analytical Engine and wrote an algorithm intended to be processed by the machine, demonstrating its potential beyond mere calculation. Her foresight into the capabilities of computing is foundational to modern technology.
        *   **Key Concept:** **Algorithm** - A step-by-step procedure or set of rules to be followed in calculations or other problem-solving operations, especially by a computer.
    *   **Marie Curie (1867-1934):** A Polish and naturalized-French physicist and chemist who conducted pioneering research on radioactivity. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, the first person and only woman to win the Nobel Prize twice, and the only person to win the Nobel Prize in two different scientific fields (Physics in 1903, Chemistry in 1911).
        *   **Key Concept:** **Radioactivity** - The emission of ionizing radiation or particles caused by the spontaneous disintegration of atomic nuclei.
        *   **Impact:** Her work laid the foundation for nuclear physics and medicine. She also developed mobile radiography units during WWI, showcasing applied scientific innovation.

### 3. The 20th Century: Increasing Participation and Overcoming Barriers

The 20th century saw greater, though still limited, opportunities for women in STEM.

*   **The World Wars:**
    *   During both World Wars, women entered traditionally male-dominated fields to fill labor shortages. This included roles in engineering, manufacturing, and scientific research.
    *   **Example:** "Rosie the Riveter" symbolized the millions of women who worked in factories and shipyards, contributing significantly to the war effort. Many of these women performed highly technical tasks.
    *   **Ethical Implication:** This period highlighted how societal norms, rather than inherent ability, often dictated who had access to technical professions.

*   **Key Figures and Contributions:**
    *   **Grace Hopper (1906-1992):** A pioneering computer scientist and United States Navy rear admiral. She was instrumental in the development of early computers and invented the first compiler for a computer programming language. Her work on COBOL significantly advanced business computing.
        *   **Key Concept:** **Compiler** - A computer program that translates source code written in a high-level programming language into machine code that a computer can execute.
        *   **Quote:** "It's easier to ask forgiveness than permission." (Reflecting her innovative and sometimes boundary-pushing approach).
    *   **Rachel Carson (1907-1964):** A marine biologist and conservationist. Her book *Silent Spring* (1962) documented the detrimental effects of pesticides on the environment, sparking the modern environmental movement.
        *   **Key Concept:** **Environmentalism** - Advocacy for or working to protect the natural environment from destruction or pollution.
        *   **Impact:** Her work directly contributed to the ban of DDT and the creation of the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) in the US. This aligns with CO3 and CO5.
    *   **Katherine Johnson (1918-2020), Dorothy Vaughan (1910-2008), and Mary Jackson (1921-2005):** African American mathematicians ("human computers") at NASA whose calculations were critical to the success of early U.S. spaceflights. Their story, popularized by the book and film *Hidden Figures*, sheds light on racial and gender discrimination within the space program.
        *   **Key Concept:** **Human Computer** - In the early days of space exploration, women mathematicians performed complex calculations by hand that were essential for mission success.
        *   **Ethical Implication:** Their story exemplifies the systemic barriers faced by women of color in STEM, underscoring the need for inclusive practices and recognition (CO1, CO2).

*   **Challenges Faced:**
    *   **Limited Access to Education:** Many universities did not admit women into science and engineering programs until well into the 20th century.
    *   **Lack of Recognition:** Women's contributions were often attributed to male colleagues or simply ignored.
    *   **Societal Expectations:** Women were often discouraged from pursuing careers in STEM, with pressure to prioritize domestic roles.
    *   **Hostile Work Environments:** Even when admitted, women often faced discrimination, harassment, and lack of support in workplaces.

### 4. The Late 20th Century and Beyond: Advocacy and Increasing Inclusion

Efforts to address the underrepresentation of women in STEM gained momentum.

*   **Feminist Movements and Advocacy:**
    *   The broader feminist movements of the mid-to-late 20th century raised awareness about gender inequality across all professions, including STEM.
    *   Organizations were formed to support women in science and engineering, providing networks, mentorship, and advocating for policy changes.

*   **Policy Changes:**
    *   Legislation promoting equal opportunity and anti-discrimination in education and employment began to open doors for women.

*   **Current Landscape:**
    *   While significant progress has been made, women remain underrepresented in many STEM fields, particularly in leadership positions and certain engineering disciplines (e.g., mechanical, electrical).
    *   The gender pay gap persists in STEM.
    *   Implicit bias and workplace culture continue to be challenges.

*   **Relevance to Engineering Ethics and Sustainable Development:**
    *   **Diverse Perspectives for Innovation:** As stated by **Justin Oakley** in *Virtue Ethics and Professional Roles*, ethical engineers should cultivate virtues like fairness and justice. Recognizing and rectifying historical injustices against women in STEM is a matter of justice. Furthermore, diverse teams, including those with gender diversity, are often more innovative and better equipped to solve complex problems, including those related to sustainability (CO4, CO5).
    *   **Inclusive Design:** Engineering solutions must consider the needs of all members of society. A lack of diversity in the engineering workforce can lead to biased designs that fail to serve or even harm certain groups. Understanding the historical exclusion of women highlights the importance of inclusive design principles.
    *   **Ethical Responsibility to Mentor and Support:** Engineers have an ethical responsibility (CO1) to create environments where all individuals can thrive. This includes actively mentoring and supporting women and other underrepresented groups entering the field.
    *   **Sustainability and Social Equity:** Sustainable development is not just about environmental protection; it is also about social equity. Addressing gender inequality in STEM is a crucial component of achieving a just and sustainable society (CO3). **Bert J. M. de Vries** in *Sustainability Science* emphasizes the interconnectedness of environmental, social, and economic dimensions of sustainability.

### 5. Highlighting Important Points to Remember

*   **The invisibility of women's contributions has been a persistent issue throughout history.**
*   **Societal norms and institutional barriers, not lack of capability, have been the primary reasons for women's underrepresentation.**
*   **Key figures like Ada Lovelace, Marie Curie, Grace Hopper, and Rachel Carson made foundational contributions to technology and science.**
*   **The "human computers" at NASA demonstrate both extraordinary talent and the systemic discrimination faced by women of color.**
*   **Understanding this history is crucial for ethical engineering practice, promoting gender sensitivity, and building inclusive, innovative teams for sustainable development.**
*   **Engineering ethics demands fairness, justice, and the creation of equitable opportunities for all.** (CO1, CO2)
*   **A diverse engineering workforce is essential for tackling complex global challenges like climate change and achieving sustainable development.** (CO4, CO5)

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1 (K3):**
Explain how understanding the history of women in science and technology can help an engineer apply the principles of engineering ethics in their professional life. Provide at least one specific example.

**Answer:**
Understanding this history helps an engineer recognize past injustices and systemic biases. For instance, knowing that women like Katherine Johnson were often overlooked despite their critical contributions reinforces the ethical imperative for engineers to ensure fair recognition and credit for all team members' work. This promotes honesty and integrity, core ethical principles. It also encourages proactive efforts to create inclusive environments where everyone's contributions are valued, preventing the repetition of past discriminatory practices.

**Question 2 (K4):**
Discuss how historical exclusion of women from STEM fields might impact contemporary engineering design and problem-solving related to sustainability.

**Answer:**
Historical exclusion has led to a less diverse pool of engineers, potentially resulting in design assumptions and solutions that do not adequately consider the needs or perspectives of half the population. For example, if historical transportation infrastructure was designed predominantly by men, it might not have prioritized the safety or accessibility needs of women, who often have different commuting patterns (e.g., combining work with childcare drop-offs). In sustainability, a lack of diverse perspectives might lead to overlooking how environmental solutions impact different genders or social groups disproportionately, hindering the equitable development needed for true sustainability.

**Question 3 (K3/K4):**
Identify a key female scientist or engineer discussed in these notes and describe one significant contribution they made, linking it to the broader context of scientific or technological advancement.

**Answer:**
Ada Lovelace is a key figure. Her contribution was recognizing the potential of Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine beyond mere numerical calculation. She envisioned that such machines could process symbols and potentially create music or art, effectively predicting the concept of general-purpose computing. This foresight laid the conceptual groundwork for modern computer science and artificial intelligence, a technological advancement that has profoundly reshaped society and continues to influence how we approach complex problems, including those in sustainable development.

**Question 4 (Exercise - Research Based):**
Choose one woman scientist or engineer not explicitly detailed in these notes (e.g., Hedy Lamarr, Rosalind Franklin, Chien-Shiung Wu) and research her contributions. Write a short paragraph explaining her impact and the challenges she may have faced due to her gender.

**Answer (Example for Hedy Lamarr):**
Hedy Lamarr, a famous Hollywood actress, was also a brilliant inventor. During World War II, she co-patented a "frequency-hopping" spread spectrum technology designed to guide torpedoes without being jammed. While initially overlooked by the military, her concept became a foundational technology for modern wireless communications, including Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and GPS. Lamarr faced challenges as her scientific contributions were often overshadowed by her fame as an actress, and she struggled to gain serious recognition for her technical acumen in a male-dominated field.
