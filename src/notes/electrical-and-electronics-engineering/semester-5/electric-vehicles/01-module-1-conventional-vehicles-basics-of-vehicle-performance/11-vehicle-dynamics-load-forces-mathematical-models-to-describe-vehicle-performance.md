# Electric Vehicles: Module 1 - Conventional Vehicles: Basics of Vehicle Performance

## Topic: Vehicle Dynamics & Load Forces: Mathematical Models to Describe Vehicle Performance

**Course Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **CO1: Familiarise the performance of conventional vehicles and electric vehicles (Knowledge Level: K2)** - This topic lays the foundational understanding of how forces act on any vehicle, whether conventional or electric, to enable performance analysis.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

This topic will enable understanding of the fundamental forces acting on a vehicle and how to mathematically model them to predict vehicle performance. This includes:

*   Understanding the basic forces that oppose vehicle motion.
*   Developing mathematical models to represent these forces.
*   Relating these forces to vehicle acceleration and speed.
*   Understanding the concept of tractive effort.

---

### 1. Introduction to Vehicle Dynamics

Vehicle dynamics is the study of how vehicles behave when in motion. It involves understanding the forces acting on a vehicle and their effect on its movement, such as acceleration, braking, and cornering. For electric vehicles (EVs), a thorough understanding of these principles, derived from conventional vehicle dynamics, is crucial for designing efficient and performant powertrains.

**Key Concept:** The fundamental principle is that for a vehicle to accelerate, the driving force (tractive effort) generated by the powertrain must overcome the sum of all opposing forces.

---

### 2. Forces Opposing Vehicle Motion

These are the forces that a vehicle's powertrain must overcome to move and accelerate. They are generally categorized as:

#### 2.1. Rolling Resistance ($F_r$)

This is the force that opposes the motion of a wheel rolling on a surface. It arises from the deformation of the tire and the road surface.

*   **Key Factors:**
    *   Tire pressure and construction
    *   Road surface condition (asphalt, concrete, gravel)
    *   Vehicle load (weight)
    *   Tire inflation pressure (lower pressure increases rolling resistance)
*   **Mathematical Model:**
    *   $F_r \approx C_r \times W$
    Where:
        *   $F_r$ is the rolling resistance force (Newtons, N).
        *   $C_r$ is the coefficient of rolling resistance (dimensionless). This coefficient is empirically derived and varies significantly.
        *   $W$ is the total weight of the vehicle (Newtons, N), $W = m \times g$, where $m$ is mass and $g$ is acceleration due to gravity (approx. 9.81 m/s²).
    *   Alternatively, $F_r \approx C_{rr} \times N$, where $N$ is the normal force on the wheels. For a level road, $N$ is equal to the weight on the wheels.
*   **Typical Values:**
    *   $C_r$ for pneumatic tires on asphalt: 0.01 to 0.015
    *   $C_r$ for pneumatic tires on rough surfaces: 0.03 to 0.05
    *   $C_r$ for solid tires: 0.015 to 0.03 (though less common in modern vehicles)

**Insight from Textbooks:**
*   Chau (2015) emphasizes that rolling resistance is a significant component of total resistance, especially at lower speeds, and directly impacts EV range. He notes that advances in tire design can significantly reduce $C_r$.
*   Miller (2010) and Mi et al. (2011) also highlight rolling resistance as a key parameter in hybrid and electric vehicle performance calculations, often incorporating it into their powertrain efficiency models.

#### 2.2. Aerodynamic Drag ($F_a$)

This is the force exerted by the air on a moving vehicle. It increases significantly with speed.

*   **Key Factors:**
    *   Vehicle speed (most dominant factor)
    *   Vehicle shape and surface smoothness (aerodynamic design)
    *   Frontal area of the vehicle
    *   Air density
*   **Mathematical Model:**
    *   $F_a = 0.5 \times \rho \times v^2 \times C_d \times A$
    Where:
        *   $F_a$ is the aerodynamic drag force (Newtons, N).
        *   $\rho$ (rho) is the air density (kg/m³). At sea level and 15°C, it's approximately 1.225 kg/m³.
        *   $v$ is the vehicle speed relative to the air (m/s).
        *   $C_d$ is the drag coefficient (dimensionless). This is a measure of how aerodynamically streamlined the vehicle is.
        *   $A$ is the frontal area of the vehicle (m²).
*   **Important Note:** Aerodynamic drag is proportional to the *square* of the velocity. This means it becomes a dominant opposing force at higher speeds.

**Insight from Textbooks:**
*   Ehsani et al. (Reference Book) likely discusses the importance of $C_d$ and $A$ in the context of EV design, as aerodynamic efficiency is critical for maximizing range. They might provide typical $C_d$ values for different vehicle types.
*   Mi et al. (2011) will likely include aerodynamic drag in their performance equations for HEVs and EVs, highlighting its impact on fuel/energy consumption.

#### 2.3. Grade Resistance ($F_g$)

This is the force that opposes a vehicle's motion when it is traveling uphill. It's essentially the component of the vehicle's weight acting parallel to the road surface.

*   **Key Factors:**
    *   Vehicle weight
    *   Gradient (slope) of the road
*   **Mathematical Model:**
    *   $F_g = W \times \sin(\theta)$
    Where:
        *   $F_g$ is the grade resistance force (Newtons, N).
        *   $W$ is the total weight of the vehicle (Newtons, N).
        *   $\theta$ (theta) is the angle of the road with respect to the horizontal.
    *   **Approximation for small angles:** For small gradients (typical road slopes), $\sin(\theta) \approx \tan(\theta) = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$. Let the gradient be represented by $G$ (e.g., 5% grade means $G=0.05$). Then, $\sin(\theta) \approx G$.
        *   $F_g \approx W \times G$
*   **Note:** When descending a hill, the grade resistance acts in the direction of motion, potentially assisting acceleration or requiring braking to control speed.

#### 2.4. Inertial Force (Acceleration Resistance) ($F_{accel}$)

This is the force required to accelerate the vehicle. According to Newton's second law, $F = ma$.

*   **Key Factors:**
    *   Vehicle mass
    *   Rate of acceleration
*   **Mathematical Model:**
    *   $F_{accel} = m \times a$
    Where:
        *   $F_{accel}$ is the inertial force (Newtons, N).
        *   $m$ is the mass of the vehicle (kg).
        *   $a$ is the acceleration of the vehicle (m/s²).

**Important Point:** In vehicle dynamics, it's often convenient to consider the *total resistance force* that needs to be overcome.

---

### 3. Total Resistance Force ($F_{total}$)

The total force that the vehicle's propulsion system must overcome to maintain a constant speed or to accelerate is the sum of the individual opposing forces.

*   **Mathematical Model (for level road, constant speed, no wind):**
    *   $F_{total} = F_r + F_a$
*   **Mathematical Model (for uphill, accelerating):**
    *   $F_{total} = F_r + F_a + F_g + F_{accel}$
*   **Combined Resistance:** Often, rolling resistance and grade resistance are combined for simpler models, especially for uphill travel:
    *   $F_{total} = F_r + F_a + F_g$ (for accelerating on a grade)

---

### 4. Tractive Effort ($F_{tractive}$)

Tractive effort is the driving force generated by the vehicle's powertrain at the tire-road interface. It is the force that propels the vehicle forward and overcomes the total resistance forces.

*   **Key Concept:** For the vehicle to accelerate, $F_{tractive}$ must be greater than $F_{total}$.
*   **Mathematical Model:**
    *   $F_{tractive} = T_{engine} \times G_{total} \times \eta_{drive train}$ (for conventional vehicles, simplified)
    *   $F_{tractive} = T_{motor} \times G_{total} \times \eta_{drive train}$ (for EVs)
    Where:
        *   $T_{engine}$ / $T_{motor}$ is the torque delivered by the engine/motor.
        *   $G_{total}$ is the overall gear ratio in the transmission and final drive.
        *   $\eta_{drive train}$ is the efficiency of the drivetrain (e.g., gearbox, differential, driveshafts).
    *   At the wheel, $F_{tractive} = \frac{T_{wheel}}{r_{wheel}}$, where $T_{wheel}$ is the torque at the wheel and $r_{wheel}$ is the effective wheel radius.
    *   So, $F_{tractive} = \frac{T_{motor} \times G_{total} \times \eta_{drive train}}{r_{wheel}}$

**Insight from Textbooks:**
*   Krishnan (Reference Book) on Permanent Magnet Synchronous and Brushless DC Motors Drives would detail the torque generation capabilities of electric motors, which directly translates to available tractive effort after considering drivetrain efficiency and gearing.
*   Hussein (2003) likely provides a comprehensive overview of how tractive effort is calculated for both conventional and electric powertrains, including the role of gearing and efficiency.

---

### 5. Vehicle Performance Equation

By equating the tractive effort to the total resistance force, we can establish the fundamental equation for vehicle motion.

*   **For constant velocity on a level road:**
    *   $F_{tractive} = F_r + F_a$
*   **For acceleration on a level road:**
    *   $F_{tractive} = F_r + F_a + F_{accel}$
    *   $F_{tractive} = F_r + F_a + m \times a$
*   **For acceleration on an incline:**
    *   $F_{tractive} = F_r + F_a + F_g + m \times a$

This equation is crucial for understanding and predicting a vehicle's acceleration capability, top speed, and power requirements.

---

### 6. Power Required and Available

*   **Power Required ($P_{required}$):** The power needed to overcome the total resistance forces.
    *   $P_{required} = F_{total} \times v$
    *   Where $v$ is the vehicle velocity.
*   **Power Available ($P_{available}$):** The power delivered by the motor to the wheels.
    *   $P_{available} = P_{motor} \times \eta_{drive train}$
    *   Where $P_{motor}$ is the power output of the electric motor.

For the vehicle to accelerate, $P_{available} > P_{required}$.

**Key Concept:** The maximum speed (top speed) of a vehicle is reached when the power available equals the power required to overcome the total resistance at that speed. At this point, acceleration is zero.

---

### 7. Incorporating EV Specifics (Preview for later modules)

While the fundamental forces are the same for conventional and electric vehicles, the **source** of the tractive effort and the **efficiency** of delivering that effort differ significantly.

*   **EV Powertrain:** Electric motors provide high torque from standstill, simplifying the drivetrain (often direct drive or single-speed gearbox).
*   **Efficiency:** Electric motors and their power electronics are generally more efficient than internal combustion engines and their associated transmissions, especially over a wide operating range.

### Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
A car has a mass of 1500 kg. It is traveling on a level road at 20 m/s (approximately 72 km/h). The coefficient of rolling resistance ($C_r$) is 0.012, and the frontal area ($A$) is 2.2 m². The drag coefficient ($C_d$) is 0.3. The density of air ($\rho$) is 1.2 kg/m³. Calculate the total resistance force.

**Answer 1:**
1.  **Weight (W):**
    $W = m \times g = 1500 \, \text{kg} \times 9.81 \, \text{m/s}^2 = 14715 \, \text{N}$
2.  **Rolling Resistance ($F_r$):**
    $F_r = C_r \times W = 0.012 \times 14715 \, \text{N} = 176.58 \, \text{N}$
3.  **Aerodynamic Drag ($F_a$):**
    $F_a = 0.5 \times \rho \times v^2 \times C_d \times A$
    $F_a = 0.5 \times 1.2 \, \text{kg/m}^3 \times (20 \, \text{m/s})^2 \times 0.3 \times 2.2 \, \text{m}^2$
    $F_a = 0.5 \times 1.2 \times 400 \times 0.3 \times 2.2 = 158.4 \, \text{N}$
4.  **Total Resistance ($F_{total}$):**
    $F_{total} = F_r + F_a = 176.58 \, \text{N} + 158.4 \, \text{N} = 334.98 \, \text{N}$

**Question 2:**
What is the tractive effort required to accelerate this car from rest to 20 m/s in 10 seconds on a level road? Assume the drivetrain efficiency is 90%. What is the average power required from the motor?

**Answer 2:**
1.  **Acceleration (a):**
    $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{20 \, \text{m/s} - 0 \, \text{m/s}}{10 \, \text{s}} = 2 \, \text{m/s}^2$
2.  **Inertial Force ($F_{accel}$):**
    $F_{accel} = m \times a = 1500 \, \text{kg} \times 2 \, \text{m/s}^2 = 3000 \, \text{N}$
3.  **Total Resistance Force at 20 m/s (from Question 1):**
    $F_{total} = 334.98 \, \text{N}$
4.  **Total Force to Accelerate ($F_{total\_accel}$):**
    $F_{total\_accel} = F_r + F_a + F_{accel}$
    *Note: $F_r$ and $F_a$ at the end of acceleration (20 m/s) are used for simplicity in this calculation. A more precise calculation would integrate these forces over time.*
    $F_{total\_accel} = 176.58 \, \text{N} + 158.4 \, \text{N} + 3000 \, \text{N} = 3335.98 \, \text{N}$
5.  **Tractive Effort ($F_{tractive}$):**
    $F_{tractive} = F_{total\_accel} = 3335.98 \, \text{N}$
6.  **Average Velocity:**
    $v_{avg} = \frac{0 \, \text{m/s} + 20 \, \text{m/s}}{2} = 10 \, \text{m/s}$
7.  **Average Power Required to Overcome Resistance:**
    $P_{required\_avg} = F_{total} \times v_{avg} = 334.98 \, \text{N} \times 10 \, \text{m/s} = 3349.8 \, \text{W}$
8.  **Average Power Required for Acceleration:**
    $P_{accel\_avg} = F_{accel} \times v_{avg} = 3000 \, \text{N} \times 10 \, \text{m/s} = 30000 \, \text{W}$
9.  **Total Average Power Required at the Wheels:**
    $P_{wheels\_required\_avg} = P_{required\_avg} + P_{accel\_avg} = 3349.8 \, \text{W} + 30000 \, \text{W} = 33349.8 \, \text{W}$
10. **Average Power Required from the Motor:**
    $P_{motor\_required\_avg} = \frac{P_{wheels\_required\_avg}}{\eta_{drive train}}$
    $P_{motor\_required\_avg} = \frac{33349.8 \, \text{W}}{0.90} \approx 37055 \, \text{W}$ or $37.06 \, \text{kW}$

**Question 3:**
A vehicle weighs 18,000 N and is traveling up a 5% grade. What is the grade resistance force?

**Answer 3:**
1.  **Grade Resistance ($F_g$):**
    Using the approximation for small angles:
    $F_g \approx W \times G$
    $F_g \approx 18000 \, \text{N} \times 0.05 = 900 \, \text{N}$

---

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Forces Opposing Motion:** Rolling resistance ($F_r$), Aerodynamic drag ($F_a$), Grade resistance ($F_g$), and Inertial force ($F_{accel}$) are the primary forces that a vehicle's powertrain must overcome.
*   **Speed Dependence:** Aerodynamic drag is proportional to the square of velocity, making it crucial at higher speeds. Rolling resistance is relatively constant with speed, but can be affected by tire design and inflation.
*   **Tractive Effort:** This is the driving force generated by the vehicle's powertrain at the wheel-road interface.
*   **Performance Equation:** The fundamental balance is $F_{tractive} = F_{total} + m \times a$.
*   **Power:** Power is the rate at which work is done, calculated as force times velocity ($P = F \times v$).
*   **EV Advantage:** Electric motors offer high torque from zero RPM, which directly translates to strong initial tractive effort, and higher drivetrain efficiencies.
