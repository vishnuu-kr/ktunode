# Module 3: Introduction to EHVAC and HVDC: Principle, Advantages/Disadvantages

## Topic: Introduction to Energy Markets (Regulated and Deregulated Systems)

---

### **1. Introduction to Energy Markets**

An **energy market** is a platform where electricity is bought and sold. It determines the price of electricity based on supply and demand. The structure and operation of energy markets have evolved significantly over time, driven by the need for efficiency, competition, and the integration of diverse generation sources.

---

### **2. Regulated vs. Deregulated Energy Systems**

The fundamental difference between regulated and deregulated energy systems lies in who controls the generation, transmission, and distribution of electricity and how prices are determined.

#### **2.1. Regulated Energy Systems (Vertically Integrated Utilities)**

In a regulated system, a single entity, often a **vertically integrated utility**, is responsible for all aspects of electricity provision:

*   **Generation:** The utility owns and operates power plants.
*   **Transmission:** The utility owns and maintains the high-voltage transmission network.
*   **Distribution:** The utility owns and operates the low-voltage distribution network delivering power to consumers.
*   **Sales:** The utility sells electricity directly to end-users.

**Key Characteristics of Regulated Systems:**

*   **Single Entity Control:** One company manages the entire electricity supply chain.
*   **Government Oversight:** Prices and operations are heavily regulated by government bodies (e.g., Public Utility Commissions).
*   **Cost-Plus Pricing:** Prices are often determined by the utility's costs plus a regulated rate of return (profit).
*   **Limited Competition:** There is little to no competition in generation or sales.
*   **System Planning:** The utility plans and invests in generation, transmission, and distribution to meet projected demand.
*   **Focus on Reliability and Affordability:** The primary goals are to ensure a reliable supply of electricity at reasonable prices.

**Advantages of Regulated Systems:**

*   **Coordination and Efficiency:** Centralized planning and operation can lead to efficient system integration and dispatch, especially in smaller or less complex grids.
*   **Simplified Planning:** The utility has a clear mandate to meet demand, simplifying long-term infrastructure planning.
*   **Consumer Protection:** Government regulation aims to protect consumers from monopolistic practices and ensure fair pricing.
*   **Easier Integration of Large, Centralized Generation:** Historically, this model was well-suited for large, conventional power plants.

**Disadvantages of Regulated Systems:**

*   **Lack of Incentive for Innovation:** Without competition, there's less pressure to adopt new technologies or improve efficiency beyond regulatory requirements.
*   **Potential for Inefficiency:** Utilities may not be as incentivized to reduce costs if they can simply pass them on to consumers with a guaranteed profit margin.
*   **Slow Response to Market Changes:** Adapting to new generation sources (like renewables) or changing consumer demands can be slower.
*   **Limited Consumer Choice:** Consumers have no choice in their electricity supplier.

**References:**

*   Wadhwa C. L., *Electrical Power Systems*, 8th edition, Chapter 1 discusses the evolution of power systems which implicitly covers the transition from regulated structures.
*   Mehta V. K. and Rohit Mehta, *Principles of Power System*, 4th edition reprint, sections on power system economics and planning would touch upon regulated aspects.

#### **2.2. Deregulated Energy Systems (Competitive Markets)**

Deregulated energy systems, also known as **restructured energy markets**, unbundle the electricity supply chain into separate business functions:

*   **Generation:** Independent Power Producers (IPPs) own and operate power plants and compete to sell electricity.
*   **Transmission:** Remains a regulated or contracted monopoly, often managed by an **Independent System Operator (ISO)** or **Regional Transmission Organization (RTO)**. Transmission access is open to all generators.
*   **Distribution:** Distribution companies (often utilities) own and operate the local delivery networks, but their role shifts to delivering electricity purchased from the market.
*   **Retail Supply/Sales:** New companies (suppliers or marketers) compete to sell electricity to end-users, who can choose their supplier.

**Key Characteristics of Deregulated Systems:**

*   **Unbundling:** Separation of generation, transmission, and distribution functions.
*   **Competition:** Encourages competition in generation and often retail sales.
*   **Market-Based Pricing:** Electricity prices are determined by supply and demand in organized wholesale markets.
*   **Independent System Operator (ISO) / Regional Transmission Organization (RTO):** An independent entity responsible for the reliable operation of the transmission grid, managing the wholesale market, and ensuring grid security.
*   **Consumer Choice:** End-users can choose their electricity supplier and potentially different pricing plans.
*   **Focus on Efficiency and Innovation:** Competition is intended to drive down costs and encourage the adoption of new technologies.

**How Deregulated Markets Work (Simplified):**

1.  **Generators:** Offer to sell electricity into the market at a certain price.
2.  **ISO/RTO:** Dispatches the lowest-cost generators that can meet the demand while maintaining grid stability. This is often done through various market mechanisms (e.g., day-ahead market, real-time market).
3.  **Transmission:** Is used to move electricity from generators to loads. Transmission charges are typically regulated and paid by market participants.
4.  **Distribution Companies:** Deliver electricity to end-users, who pay a distribution charge (regulated) plus the market price for energy from their chosen supplier.

**Types of Wholesale Markets:**

*   **Energy Market:** Buys and sells energy (kWh).
*   **Ancillary Services Market:** Buys services needed to maintain grid reliability, such as frequency regulation, voltage support, and operating reserves.
*   **Capacity Market:** Ensures sufficient generation capacity is available to meet future demand.

**Advantages of Deregulated Systems:**

*   **Lower Prices:** Competition among generators can drive down wholesale electricity prices.
*   **Increased Efficiency:** Companies are incentivized to operate efficiently and reduce costs.
*   **Innovation and Technology Adoption:** Competition encourages investment in new generation technologies, including renewables and energy efficiency.
*   **Consumer Choice and Customization:** Consumers can choose suppliers and plans that best suit their needs (e.g., green energy, fixed prices).
*   **Improved Responsiveness:** Markets can adapt more quickly to changes in demand and supply conditions.

**Disadvantages of Deregulated Systems:**

*   **Market Volatility:** Prices can fluctuate significantly due to supply/demand imbalances, fuel costs, and unexpected outages.
*   **Risk of Market Manipulation:** Potential for gaming or manipulation by market participants.
*   **Transmission Congestion Management:** Managing the flow of electricity across the grid can be complex and lead to congestion costs.
*   **Challenges for Renewables Integration:** While promoting renewables, integrating intermittent sources into a market-driven system requires sophisticated market rules and grid management.
*   **Complexity:** The market structures and rules can be very complex.
*   **Potential for Underinvestment in Transmission:** If transmission investment isn't adequately incentivized, it can become a bottleneck.

**Examples:**

*   **USA:** Many regions have restructured, with ISOs/RTOs like PJM, ERCOT, NYISO.
*   **UK:** Pioneered electricity market liberalization.
*   **Australia:** National Electricity Market (NEM).

**References:**

*   Wadhwa C. L., *Electrical Power Systems*, 8th edition, Chapter 1 likely discusses the restructuring and its implications.
*   Mehta V. K. and Rohit Mehta, *Principles of Power System*, 4th edition reprint, may have sections on power system restructuring and market economics.
*   Badri Ram and D.N. Viswakarma, *Power System Protection and Switchgear*, 2nd edition, might indirectly touch upon how market dynamics influence the deployment and operation of protective devices, though its primary focus is protection.
*   Khan B. H., *Non-conventional energy sources*, 3rd edition, would be highly relevant in a deregulated context, as it discusses how renewable energy sources are integrated and traded in competitive markets.

### **3. Learning Outcomes Addressed**

This topic directly relates to the overall understanding of how electricity is generated, transmitted, and consumed, and the economic frameworks governing these activities.

*   **CO1 (Learn different types of power generating systems and schedule generation appropriate for a given area):** Understanding energy markets is crucial for comprehending how different generation types (e.g., fossil fuels, renewables, nuclear) compete for dispatch and how their output is scheduled to meet demand in either a regulated or deregulated environment. Price signals in deregulated markets directly influence scheduling decisions.
*   **CO2 (Evaluate the electrical performance of any transmission line):** While not directly about electrical performance, market structures can influence investment in transmission infrastructure, impacting its capacity and utilization, which in turn affects electrical performance. Congestion management in deregulated markets highlights the importance of transmission capability.

*   **CO4 (Demonstrate the working of relays and switch gear for protection schemes):** Market operations and the presence of multiple independent generators and loads in a deregulated system necessitate robust and sophisticated protection schemes to ensure grid stability and reliability, which is the domain of protection and switchgear. Ensuring secure market transactions and preventing cascading failures due to faults is paramount.
*   **CO5 (Design a simple ac electrical distribution system as per the standards):** In a deregulated distribution sector, the utility (or a separate distribution network operator) still manages the physical network. Understanding the energy market allows for designing distribution systems that can accommodate distributed generation, electric vehicles, and demand-side management programs, which are often enabled by market mechanisms.

### **4. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Vertically Integrated Utility:** A single company controlling generation, transmission, and distribution.
*   **Restructuring/Deregulated Market:** Unbundling of the electricity sector to introduce competition.
*   **Independent System Operator (ISO) / Regional Transmission Organization (RTO):** An independent entity managing the grid and wholesale electricity market.
*   **Wholesale Market:** Where electricity generators sell power to load-serving entities or marketers.
*   **Retail Market:** Where electricity is sold to end-consumers.
*   **Unbundling:** Separating generation, transmission, and distribution functions.
*   **Price Signals:** Market-driven prices that influence the behavior of generators and consumers.
*   **Ancillary Services:** Services needed to maintain grid reliability.
*   **Capacity Market:** Ensures sufficient generation capacity is available.
*   **Consumer Choice:** The ability of end-users to select their electricity supplier.
*   **Market-Based Pricing:** Prices determined by supply and demand.
*   **Cost-Plus Pricing:** Prices determined by costs plus a regulated profit margin.

---

### **5. Examples**

*   **Regulated:** A small municipal utility that owns its power plants and delivers electricity to its residents. Residents have no choice of supplier. Prices are set by the city council or a public utility commission.
*   **Deregulated:** A region where multiple power generators (e.g., a coal plant, a solar farm, a wind farm, a gas turbine plant) bid to sell electricity into a wholesale market managed by an ISO. Consumers can choose to buy electricity from a retail marketer that sources power from this market, potentially opting for a plan that guarantees a certain percentage of renewable energy.

---

### **6. Important Points to Remember**

*   Regulated systems prioritize **stability and controlled pricing** through single-entity ownership and government oversight.
*   Deregulated systems aim for **efficiency, innovation, and consumer choice** through competition and unbundling.
*   The **ISO/RTO** is a critical component of a deregulated system, ensuring grid reliability and managing the market.
*   Price volatility is a significant challenge in deregulated markets.
*   The transition to deregulation involves complex policy and market design considerations.

### **7. Practice Questions**

**Question 1:**
Describe the primary characteristic that differentiates a regulated energy system from a deregulated energy system.

**Answer:**
The primary differentiating characteristic is the presence or absence of competition and the structure of ownership. In a regulated system, a single, vertically integrated utility controls generation, transmission, and distribution, with prices set by regulators. In a deregulated system, these functions are unbundled, and competition is introduced, particularly in generation, with prices determined by market forces.

**Question 2:**
What is the role of an Independent System Operator (ISO) in a deregulated energy market?

**Answer:**
An ISO is responsible for the reliable operation of the transmission grid, managing the wholesale electricity market, dispatching generation based on market clearing prices, and ensuring grid security. It acts as a neutral administrator, ensuring fair access to the grid for all market participants.

**Question 3:**
List two advantages and two disadvantages of deregulated energy systems compared to regulated systems.

**Answer:**
**Advantages:**
1.  **Lower Prices:** Competition can drive down wholesale electricity costs.
2.  **Increased Efficiency & Innovation:** Incentives for cost reduction and adoption of new technologies.

**Disadvantages:**
1.  **Market Volatility:** Prices can fluctuate significantly.
2.  **Complexity:** Market rules and operations can be intricate.

**Question 4:**
How does understanding energy markets relate to Course Outcome 1 (CO1: Learn different types of power generating systems and schedule generation appropriate for a given area)?

**Answer:**
In a regulated system, generation scheduling is dictated by the utility's plan and cost-minimization directives from regulators. In a deregulated system, the market price of electricity becomes the primary driver for generation scheduling. Understanding energy markets allows one to see how different generation types (e.g., expensive gas turbines vs. low-marginal-cost renewables) are dispatched based on their bids into the market, influencing how often and when they operate. This is crucial for scheduling generation to meet demand efficiently and economically.
