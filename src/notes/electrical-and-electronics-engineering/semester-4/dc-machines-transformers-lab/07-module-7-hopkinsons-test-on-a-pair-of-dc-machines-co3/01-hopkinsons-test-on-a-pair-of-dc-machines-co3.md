# DC MACHINES & TRANSFORMERS LAB - Module 7: Hopkinson’s Test on a Pair of DC Machines

**Course Outcome Addressed:** CO3: Investigate the losses and efficiency in DC machines by conducting no-load tests (Knowledge Level: K3)

## 1. Introduction to Hopkinson's Test

Hopkinson's test, also known as the **back-to-back test** or **regenerative test**, is a crucial experimental method for determining the losses and efficiency of DC machines, particularly DC shunt motors. It's a highly efficient method because it allows testing a machine under load without dissipating the full load power as heat in resistors, thus saving a significant amount of energy.

**Key Concept:** The test utilizes two identical DC machines, one acting as a motor and the other as a generator. The mechanical output of the motor is coupled to the mechanical input of the generator. The generator's electrical output is then fed back to the DC supply that powers the motor, creating a closed-loop system.

**Why is it important?**
*   **Energy Saving:** The primary advantage is the reduction in power consumption from the mains supply, as only the losses in the system are drawn from the supply.
*   **No Load Testing under Load Conditions:** It allows for testing a DC machine under full load conditions without requiring a separate large load bank.
*   **Loss Determination:** It effectively separates and quantifies various losses (iron losses, friction and windage losses, copper losses) in the machines.
*   **Efficiency Calculation:** Enables accurate calculation of efficiency at different load points.

**Reference:**
*   **Bimbhra, P.S. (2021). Electrical Machinery.** Chapter on testing of DC machines will detail the principles and procedures for Hopkinson's test.
*   **Kothari, D.P. & Nagrath, I.J. (2017). Electric Machines.** This textbook will also provide a thorough explanation of the back-to-back testing methodology.

## 2. Principle of Operation

The Hopkinson's test is based on the principle of **energy regeneration**. One machine (Machine 1) is run as a motor from the DC supply. Its mechanical output is used to drive the second machine (Machine 2), which is connected as a generator. The electrical output of Machine 2 is then fed back to the DC supply, but with a slight adjustment in voltage (usually by adjusting the field of the generator) to compensate for the losses occurring in both machines.

**Scenario:**
*   Machine 1 (Motor): Takes electrical power from the supply and delivers mechanical power to the shaft.
*   Machine 2 (Generator): Takes mechanical power from the shaft and delivers electrical power back to the supply.

**Energy Balance:**
The power drawn from the supply is equal to the sum of all the losses in both machines.
$P_{supply} = \text{Losses in Machine 1} + \text{Losses in Machine 2}$

Since the two machines are identical, we can assume:
*   Iron losses and friction & windage losses are approximately the same for both machines when operating at the same speed and field excitation.
*   Copper losses ($I^2R$ losses) will differ based on the armature currents of each machine.

## 3. Experimental Setup

A typical experimental setup for Hopkinson's test involves:

*   **Two identical DC shunt machines:** These can be DC shunt motors or compound motors, but shunt connection is most common. They should be identical in ratings (HP, voltage, speed).
*   **DC Power Supply:** To power the motor and excite the fields of both machines.
*   **Rheostats:**
    *   **Field Rheostat for Machine 1 (Motor):** To control the speed of the motor.
    *   **Field Rheostat for Machine 2 (Generator):** To control the generator voltage and hence the load on the system.
*   **Load Rheostat (Variable):** Connected in series with the generator armature circuit to adjust the armature current of the generator (and thus the load).
*   **Voltmeter:**
    *   To measure the supply voltage ($V_S$).
    *   To measure the terminal voltage of Machine 1 ($V_1$).
    *   To measure the terminal voltage of Machine 2 ($V_2$).
*   **Ammeter:**
    *   To measure the supply current ($I_S$).
    *   To measure the armature current of Machine 1 ($I_{a1}$).
    *   To measure the armature current of Machine 2 ($I_{a2}$).
    *   To measure the field current of Machine 1 ($I_{f1}$).
    *   To measure the field current of Machine 2 ($I_{f2}$).
*   **Wattmeter:**
    *   To measure the input power to Machine 1 ($P_{in1}$).
    *   Alternatively, using ammeters and voltmeters to calculate input power ($P_{in1} = V_1 \times I_{a1}$).
*   **Coupling:** A mechanical coupling (e.g., a flexible coupling) to connect the shafts of the two machines.
