# Marketing Management: Module 2 - Consumer Behaviour: Product Decision

## 1. Concept of a Product

**Definition:** A **product** is anything that can be offered to a market for attention, acquisition, use, or consumption that might satisfy a want or need. It is more than just a physical object; it encompasses **services, ideas, places, organizations, people, and even experiences** (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., p. 3).

**Key Components of a Product:**

*   **Core Benefit/Service:** The fundamental need that the product satisfies for the consumer.
    *   *Example:* A hotel room's core benefit is lodging.
*   **Actual Product:** The physical attributes of the product, including its features, design, brand name, packaging, and quality levels.
    *   *Example:* The hotel room's actual product includes the bed, bathroom, TV, decor, and brand of the hotel.
*   **Augmented Product:** Additional services and benefits that surround the actual product. These differentiate the product and build customer loyalty.
    *   *Example:* The hotel room's augmented product might include room service, free Wi-Fi, complimentary breakfast, concierge services, or loyalty program benefits.

**Importance of Understanding the Product Concept:**

*   Helps marketers identify and fulfill customer needs and wants effectively.
*   Provides a framework for product differentiation and competitive advantage.
*   Guides product development and innovation.
*   Crucial for building strong brand equity.

## 2. Types of Products

Products can be classified in various ways, which helps marketers tailor their strategies.

### 2.1. Business Products vs. Consumer Products

This is a primary classification based on the buyer and the purpose of purchase.

#### 2.1.1. Business Products (Industrial Goods)

**Definition:** Products purchased for use in the production of other goods and services, for use in a business operation, or for resale to others. The demand for business products is often **derived demand** (demand that ultimately comes from (derives) from demand for consumer goods).

**Characteristics:**

*   Fewer buyers but larger purchase volumes.
*   Closer buyer-seller relationships.
*   More professional purchasing.
*   Demand is more inelastic (less sensitive to price changes).

**Types of Business Products (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., pp. 272-274):**

*   **Materials and Parts:**
    *   **Raw Materials:** Farm products and natural products (e.g., wheat, cotton, oil).
    *   **Manufactured Materials and Parts:** Component materials (e.g., steel, chemicals) and component parts (e.g., tires, electric motors).
*   **Capital Items:** Long-lasting goods that facilitate the buyer's production or operations.
    *   **Installations:** Buildings and heavy machinery (e.g., factories, generators).
    *   **Accessory Equipment:** Tools and office equipment (e.g., hand tools, forklifts, computers).
*   **Supplies and Services:** Short-term goods and services that support buyer operations.
    *   **Operating Supplies:** Lubricants, coal, typing paper.
    *   **Maintenance and Repair Items:** Paint, nails, cleaning supplies.
    *   **Business Services:** Janitorial, repair, legal, consulting, advertising services.

#### 2.1.2. Consumer Products

**Definition:** Products purchased by final consumers for personal consumption. Marketers further classify consumer products based on how consumers buy them.

**Types of Consumer Products (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., pp. 258-260):**

*   **Convenience Products:**
    *   **Definition:** Products that consumers usually buy frequently, immediately, and with minimal comparison and buying effort.
    *   **Characteristics:** Low price, widely distributed, mass promotion.
    *   *Examples:* Soap, candy, newspapers, fast food.
*   **Shopping Products:**
    *   **Definition:** Products that consumers, in the process of selection and purchase, characteristically compare on bases such as suitability, quality, price, and style.
    *   **Characteristics:** Less frequent purchase, more personal selling and advertising, higher price.
    *   *Examples:* Furniture, clothing, major appliances, used cars.
*   **Specialty Products:**
    *   **Definition:** Products with unique characteristics or brand identification for which a significant group of buyers is willing to make a special purchase effort.
    *   **Characteristics:** Strong brand preference and loyalty, low comparison of brands, low price sensitivity, carefully targeted promotion.
    *   *Examples:* Specific brands of cars, high-end photography equipment, designer clothing, specialized medical services.
*   **Unsought Products:**
    *   **Definition:** Products that the consumer either does not know about or knows about but does not normally consider buying.
    *   **Characteristics:** Requires aggressive advertising and personal selling.
    *   *Examples:* Life insurance, blood donation, funeral services, encyclopedias (often).

### 2.2. Service Product

**Definition:** A **service** is any act or performance that one party can offer to another that is essentially intangible and does not result in the ownership of anything. Its production may or may not be tied to a physical product.

**Characteristics of Services (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., p. 302):**

*   **Intangibility:** Services cannot be seen, tasted, felt, heard, or smelled before they are bought. (e.g., medical examination, air travel).
*   **Inseparability:** Services are typically produced and consumed at the same time, and in many cases, in the presence of the consumer (e.g., haircuts, restaurant meals).
*   **Variability (Heterogeneity):** The quality of services depends on who provides them, when, where, and how they are provided. (e.g., restaurant service can vary significantly).
*   **Perishability:** Services cannot be stored for later sale or use (e.g., empty seats on an airplane, unsold hotel rooms).

**Marketing Challenges for Services:**

*   Managing quality and consistency.
*   Handling the inseparability of production and consumption.
*   Dealing with intangibility (making benefits tangible).
*   Addressing perishability (managing capacity and demand).

**Examples:** Banking, hospitality, transportation, healthcare, education, consulting.

### 2.3. Commodity Product

**Definition:** A **commodity** is a basic good used in commerce that is interchangeable with other goods of the same type. Commodities are most often used as inputs in the production of other goods or services.

**Characteristics:**

*   **Standardized:** Little to no differentiation between producers.
*   **Price-Driven:** Competition is primarily based on price.
*   **High Volume, Low Margin:** Profitability relies on large sales volumes.
*   **Supply and Demand Dominant:** Market prices are heavily influenced by global supply and demand forces.

**Examples:** Agricultural products (wheat, corn, rice), natural resources (oil, gas, metals), basic chemicals.

**Marketing Challenge:** Differentiation is extremely difficult. Marketers often focus on reliability of supply, logistical efficiency, and building trust in their ability to deliver consistently.

### 2.4. Technology Product

**Definition:** Products that are heavily reliant on scientific advancement and innovation, offering new functionalities or improved performance due to technological breakthroughs.

**Characteristics:**

*   **Rapid Innovation:** Subject to quick obsolescence and frequent upgrades.
*   **High R&D Investment:** Significant costs associated with development.
*   **Complex Features:** May require education for consumers to understand and use.
*   **Network Effects:** Value can increase as more users adopt the technology.
*   **Intellectual Property:** Often protected by patents and copyrights.

**Examples:** Smartphones, software, artificial intelligence systems, electric vehicles, advanced medical equipment.

**Marketing Strategies:** Focus on features, benefits, performance, ease of use, and often leverage early adopters and influencers. Building ecosystems and compatibility are also key.

### 2.5. Customized Product

**Definition:** Products that are tailored to meet the specific needs and preferences of an individual customer or a small group of customers. Also known as made-to-order or bespoke products.

**Characteristics:**

*   **Customer-Centric:** Designed around individual requirements.
*   **High Value:** Often command premium pricing.
*   **Close Customer Interaction:** Requires significant communication and collaboration between the seller and buyer.
*   **Longer Lead Times:** Production can take longer due to customization.
*   **Lower Production Volume:** Typically produced in smaller quantities.

**Examples:** Custom-tailored suits, bespoke furniture, specialized software solutions for a business, custom-built computers, architectural designs.

**Marketing Strategies:** Focus on relationship building, understanding customer needs deeply, and emphasizing unique benefits and craftsmanship.

## 3. New Product Development (NPD)

**Definition:** The process of bringing a new product to the market. This is a critical function for companies seeking growth and competitive advantage.

**The New Product Development Process (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., pp. 288-296; Arun Kumar & N Meenakshi, 2nd Ed., pp. 343-348):**

1.  **Idea Generation:** The systematic search for new product ideas.
    *   **Sources:**
        *   **Internal:** R&D departments, employees, sales force.
        *   **External:** Customers, suppliers, competitors, distributors, trade shows, universities, government agencies, crowdsourcing.
    *   **Methods:** Brainstorming, focus groups, customer surveys, trend analysis.
2.  **Idea Screening:** Filtering new product ideas to eliminate those that don't have potential.
    *   **Criteria:** Market attractiveness, competitive landscape, company resources, technical feasibility, profitability potential.
    *   **Purpose:** Reduce the number of ideas to a manageable few.
3.  **Concept Development and Testing:** Developing promising ideas into product concepts and testing them with target consumers.
    *   **Product Concept:** A detailed version of the new product idea stated in meaningful consumer terms.
    *   **Concept Testing:** Presenting the product concept to target consumers to gauge their reaction and determine market potential. (e.g., surveys, focus groups).
4.  **Marketing Strategy Development:** Designing an initial marketing strategy for the new product.
    *   Includes defining the target market, positioning, marketing mix (product, price, place, promotion), and sales/profit goals.
5.  **Business Analysis:** Reviewing the sales, costs, and profit projections for the new product to determine if it meets the company's objectives.
    *   Includes forecasting sales, estimating costs, and analyzing profitability.
6.  **Product Development:** Turning the product concept into a physical product or a fully developed service.
    *   Involves R&D, engineering, design, and testing.
7.  **Test Marketing:** Introducing the new product and its proposed marketing program into realistic market settings.
    *   **Purpose:** To gather information on how the product and marketing program will perform in the real market before full-scale launch. Reduces risk.
    *   **Methods:** Controlled test markets, simulated test markets, standard test markets.
8.  **Commercialization:** Introducing the new product into the market.
    *   Involves planning the launch timing, geographical rollout, target audience, and marketing communications.

**Importance of NPD:**

*   Drives business growth and revenue.
*   Helps companies stay competitive.
*   Meets evolving customer needs.
*   Can lead to market leadership.

## 4. Product Idea

**Definition:** A **product idea** is a thought or concept for a new product that could satisfy a customer need or solve a customer problem. It is the initial spark that begins the NPD process.

**Characteristics of a Good Product Idea:**

*   Addresses a genuine customer need or want.
*   Offers a clear benefit or value proposition.
*   Is feasible from a technical and production standpoint.
*   Has market potential (sufficient demand and willingness to pay).
*   Aligns with the company's strategic goals and capabilities.

**Sources of Product Ideas (Reiteration for emphasis):**

*   **Customer Insights:** Direct feedback, surveys, complaints, usage patterns.
*   **Employee Suggestions:** Salespeople, customer service representatives, R&D teams.
*   **Competitor Analysis:** Observing competitor successes and failures.
*   **Market Trends:** Identifying shifts in consumer behavior, technology, and societal values.
*   **Supplier and Distributor Feedback:** Insights from those involved in the supply chain.
*   **Scientific and Technological Advancements:** Discoveries that can be translated into new products.

## 5. Product Life Cycle (PLC)

**Definition:** The course of a product's sales and profits over its lifetime. It involves several stages, each requiring different marketing strategies.

**The Product Life Cycle Stages (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., pp. 278-282; Arun Kumar & N Meenakshi, 2nd Ed., pp. 319-323):**

1.  **Introduction Stage:**
    *   **Characteristics:** New product, low sales, high costs (R&D, marketing), often negative profits, few competitors.
    *   **Marketing Objective:** Create product awareness and trial.
    *   **Strategies:**
        *   **Product:** Offer a basic product.
        *   **Price:** Skimming (high price) or penetration (low price).
        *   **Place:** Build selective distribution.
        *   **Promotion:** Heavy spending on advertising and publicity to build awareness among early adopters and dealers.
2.  **Growth Stage:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Rapidly rising sales, increasing profits, emerging competitors, product improvements and new models.
    *   **Marketing Objective:** Maximize market share.
    *   **Strategies:**
        *   **Product:** Offer product extensions, service, and warranty.
        *   **Price:** Maintain or slightly reduce price to penetrate the market.
        *   **Place:** Build intensive distribution.
        *   **Promotion:** Reduce advertising and sales promotion to a level that continues to inform consumers and persuade them to buy.
3.  **Maturity Stage:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Sales growth slows down, peaks, and then starts to decline, profits level off or decline, intense competition, market saturation. Most products are in this stage.
    *   **Marketing Objective:** Maximize profit while defending market share.
    *   **Strategies:**
        *   **Product:** Differentiate the brand/product, modify the product (quality, features, style).
        *   **Price:** Match or beat competitors' prices.
        *   **Place:** Maintain intensive distribution.
        *   **Promotion:** Increase advertising and sales promotion to encourage brand switching and remind consumers. Focus on differentiation and benefits.
4.  **Decline Stage:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Sales and profits decline, decreasing competition, obsolescence or changing tastes.
    *   **Marketing Objective:** Reduce expenditure and milk the brand, or divest.
    *   **Strategies:**
        *   **Product:** Phase out weak items, cut back on product variations.
        *   **Price:** Cut price.
        *   **Place:** Become selective in distribution, phase out unprofitable outlets.
        *   **Promotion:** Reduce advertising to the minimum level needed to retain hardcore loyalists.

**Important Considerations for PLC:**

*   **Not all products follow this exact pattern.** Some may skip stages, have shorter lifecycles, or even be revived.
*   **The length and shape of the PLC vary** depending on the product, market, and competitive dynamics.
*   **Marketers can influence the PLC** through strategic decisions.

## 6. Brand

**Definition:** A **brand** is a name, term, sign, symbol, design, or a combination of these, intended to identify the goods or services of one seller or group of sellers and to differentiate them from those of competitors. It's the promise a company makes to its customers.

**What is a Brand? (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., pp. 238-239):**

*   More than just a name; it's the consumer's perception of the product or company.
*   Represents value, trust, quality, and emotional connection.
*   A strategic asset that creates competitive advantage.

**Brand Equity:** The differential effect that brand knowledge has on consumer response to the marketing of that brand. High brand equity leads to:

*   Greater brand loyalty.
*   Lower marketing costs.
*   Increased leverage with trade.
*   Greater resilience to competitive actions.
*   Opportunities for brand extensions.

### 6.1. Brand Attributes

**Definition:** **Brand attributes** are the tangible and intangible characteristics that identify a brand and differentiate it from competitors. They are the building blocks of a brand's image and promise.

**Categories of Brand Attributes (Aaker's Brand Identity Model, as discussed in Aaker & McLoughlin, 3rd Ed.):**

*   **Product Attributes:**
    *   Features, functionality, design, quality, performance, packaging.
    *   *Example:* Apple's sleek design, user-friendly interface, and high-quality materials for its iPhones.
*   **Customer Benefits:**
    *   **Functional Benefits:** What the product does for the customer (e.g., cleans clothes, provides transportation).
    *   **Emotional Benefits:** How the product makes the customer feel (e.g., safe, sophisticated, happy).
    *   **Self-Expressive Benefits:** How the customer feels they express themselves through the product (e.g., stylish, successful).
    *   *Example:* Volvo's functional benefit of safety, which translates to emotional benefits of peace of mind.
*   **Brand Personality:**
    *   The set of human characteristics associated with a brand.
    *   *Examples:* Sophisticated (Chanel), rugged (Jeep), trustworthy (BMW), youthful (Red Bull).
*   **Brand Values and Beliefs:**
    *   The underlying principles, ethics, and mission of the brand.
    *   *Example:* Patagonia's commitment to environmental sustainability.
*   **Brand Associations:**
    *   Anything linked in memory to a brand. These can be anything from a logo to a celebrity endorser, a country of origin, or a particular experience.
    *   *Example:* Nike's association with athletic achievement and inspirational athletes like Michael Jordan.

**Importance of Attributes:**

*   Define what the brand stands for.
*   Communicate the brand's value proposition.
*   Create a memorable and distinct brand identity.
*   Influence consumer perception and preference.

### 6.2. Building a Brand Name

**Definition:** The process of creating a distinctive and memorable name that identifies a product or company and conveys its essence.

**Criteria for a Good Brand Name (Kotler & Keller, 15th Ed., p. 247):**

*   **Suggests something about the product's benefits or qualities:** (e.g., "QuickClean" for a cleaning product).
*   **Is easy to pronounce, recognize, and remember:** (e.g., "Google," "Apple").
*   **Is a distinctive name:** Stands out from competitors.
*   **Is extensible:** Allows for new products in related categories (brand extensions).
*   **Translates well:** Works across different languages and cultures if international expansion is planned.
*   **Can be legally registered and protected:** Avoids trademark infringement.

**Brand Naming Strategies:**

*   **Manufacturer's Brand:** The product is sold under the manufacturer's own brand name (e.g., Samsung televisions).
*   **Private/Store Brand:** The product is sold under a retailer's or wholesaler's brand name (e.g., Walmart's "Great Value" products).
*   **Individual Branding:** Giving each product a unique name (e.g., Procter & Gamble has separate brands like Tide, Pampers, Gillette). This isolates the brands if one fails.
*   **Family Branding (Umbrella Branding):** Using the same brand name for a group of related products (e.g., Honda cars, Honda motorcycles, Honda generators). This leverages brand equity.
*   **Corporate Branding:** Using the company name to brand products (e.g., General Electric appliances, GE aircraft engines).

**Brand Name Selection Process:**

1.  **Identify the core message and target audience.**
2.  **Brainstorm potential names.**
3.  **Screen names based on the criteria above.**
4.  **Conduct linguistic and cultural checks.**
5.  **Check for trademark availability.**
6.  **Conduct market research and testing.**

### 6.3. Strategies of Corporate Branding

**Definition:** **Corporate branding** refers to the practice of promoting the company as a whole, rather than individual products or services. The goal is to build a strong, positive identity and reputation for the entire organization.

**Why Corporate Branding?**

*   Builds trust and credibility for all products under the corporate umbrella.
*   Attracts and retains talent.
*   Enhances investor relations.
*   Provides a unified message and identity.
*   Supports brand extensions.

**Key Strategies for Corporate Branding (Aaker & McLoughlin, 3rd Ed. often discusses this in the context of brand architecture and identity):**

1.  **Develop a Clear Corporate Vision and Mission:** The brand should reflect the company's core purpose and long-term goals.
2.  **Consistent Visual Identity:** A unified logo, color palette, typography, and design language across all touchpoints.
    *   *Example:* IBM's consistent use of its blue logo and clean design.
3.  **Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC):** Ensuring all marketing messages and activities are coordinated to present a consistent brand image.
    *   This includes advertising, PR, digital marketing, social media, and employee communications.
4.  **Strong Corporate Culture:** The internal culture and values of the company should align with the external brand promise. Employees are brand ambassadors.
    *   *Example:* Zappos' focus on customer service as a core cultural value that translates into their brand.
5.  **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and Sustainability:** Demonstrating commitment to ethical practices, social impact, and environmental sustainability can significantly enhance corporate brand reputation.
    *   *Example:* Unilever's Sustainable Living Plan, which is integral to its corporate brand.
6.  **Thought Leadership and Expertise:** Positioning the company and its leaders as experts in their industry through content creation, speaking engagements, and research.
7.  **Customer Relationship Management (CRM) and Customer Experience:** Ensuring a consistently positive experience across all customer interactions reinforces the corporate brand promise.
    *   *Example:* Disney's reputation for creating magical customer experiences that extend beyond its theme parks.
8.  **Strategic Partnerships and Alliances:** Collaborating with other reputable organizations can leverage and enhance the corporate brand.

## Answers to Practice Questions

**A1. (CO1, CO3) Classification:**
    a) **Shopping Product:** Consumers compare features, quality, and price.
    b) **Convenience Product:** Purchased frequently with minimal effort.
    c) **Specialty Product:** Unique characteristics and brand identification lead to a special purchase effort.
    d) **Unsought Product:** Consumers may not actively seek or even know about it, requiring aggressive marketing.

**A2. (CO1, CO2) Business vs. Consumer Product:**
    *   **Business Products:** Purchased for use in business operations, production of other goods, or resale. Demand is often derived.
        *   *Example:* A factory owner buying a large industrial lathe.
    *   **Consumer Products:** Purchased by final consumers for personal consumption.
        *   *Example:* An individual buying a book for leisure reading.

**A3. (CO1) Characteristics of Services:**
    The four main characteristics are:
    *   **Intangibility**
    *   **Inseparability**
    *   **Variability (Heterogeneity)**
    *   **Perishability**

**A4. (CO1, CO2) Commodity Product Marketing Challenge:**
    The primary challenge is **differentiation**. Since commodities are standardized and interchangeable, competition often revolves around **price**, and building brand preference is difficult. Marketers focus on reliability, logistics, and customer service.

**A5. (CO1, CO2) Introduction Stage & Strategies:**
    *   **Introduction Stage:** Characterized by low sales, high costs, and often negative profits. The main goal is to create awareness and encourage trial. Competitors are few.
    *   **Marketing Strategies:**
        *   **Product:** Offer a basic version of the product.
        *   **Price:** Use **price skimming** (high price to recover development costs from early adopters) or **penetration pricing** (low price to gain market share quickly).
        *   **Place:** Build selective distribution channels.
        *   **Promotion:** Invest heavily in advertising and publicity to build awareness among early adopters and channels.

**A6. (CO1, CO3) Building Brand Equity for Organic Coffee:**
    Key brand attributes could include:
    *   **Product Attributes:** Origin of beans, roasting process, unique flavor profiles, sustainable packaging.
    *   **Customer Benefits:** Health benefits (organic), ethical sourcing (fair trade), superior taste, energizing experience, supporting environmental causes.
    *   **Brand Personality:** Earthy, authentic, sophisticated, vibrant, conscious.
    *   **Brand Values:** Sustainability, fair labor practices, quality, community support.
    *   **Brand Associations:** A specific coffee-growing region, a healthy lifestyle, a sense of well-being.

**A7. (CO1, CO2) Criteria for Brand Name Selection:**
    Two important criteria:
    1.  **Suggests something about the product's benefits or qualities:** This helps consumers understand what the product is about.
    2.  **Is easy to pronounce, recognize, and remember:** A memorable and easily communicated name is crucial for recall and word-of-mouth.
    *   *(Other valid criteria include being distinctive, extensible, legally protectable, and translating well internationally).*

**A8. (CO1, CO2, CO6) Corporate Branding:**
    *   **Concept:** Corporate branding is the practice of promoting the company as a whole, rather than focusing solely on individual products. It aims to build a strong, unified identity and reputation for the entire organization, encompassing its values, mission, and overall customer experience.
    *   **Example:** **Apple Inc.** Apple effectively uses corporate branding by consistently presenting itself as an innovator in technology and design across all its product lines (iPhones, Macs, iPads, Apple Watch) and services (App Store, Apple Music). Their focus on user experience, sleek aesthetics, and an ecosystem of interconnected products reinforces the Apple brand itself, building immense loyalty and trust in the corporation. Their advertising often highlights the "Apple way" of doing things, emphasizing creativity and simplicity, which are core corporate brand attributes.

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Products are more than just physical goods; they are bundles of benefits.** Understanding the core benefit, actual product, and augmented product is crucial.
*   **The classification of products (consumer vs. business, convenience, shopping, specialty, unsought) helps tailor marketing strategies.**
*   **Services have unique characteristics (intangibility, inseparability, variability, perishability) that require specific marketing approaches.**
