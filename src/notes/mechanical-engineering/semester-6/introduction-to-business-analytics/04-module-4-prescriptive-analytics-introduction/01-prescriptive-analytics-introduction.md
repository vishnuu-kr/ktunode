# Module 4: Prescriptive Analytics - Introduction

## 1. Introduction to Prescriptive Analytics

**What is Prescriptive Analytics?**

Prescriptive analytics is the most advanced form of analytics, aiming to **recommend specific actions** to achieve desired outcomes. It goes beyond predicting what will happen (predictive analytics) and forecasting what might happen (forecasting) by telling us **what we should do**.

*   **Goal:** To guide decision-making and optimize business processes by suggesting the best course of action.
*   **Key Question Answered:** "What should we do?"
*   **Relationship to Other Analytics:** Builds upon descriptive and predictive analytics. It uses the insights from these earlier stages to formulate recommendations.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:** CO6: Understand the basic concepts in prescriptive analytics. (K2)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Decision Optimization:** Finding the best possible solution from a set of alternatives, considering various constraints and objectives.
*   **Simulation:** Modeling real-world processes to understand their behavior and test different scenarios.
*   **Rules-Based Systems:** Using predefined rules to guide decisions.
*   **Recommendation Engines:** Systems that suggest products, services, or content to users based on their preferences and behavior.

**Textbook References:**

*   **U Dinesh Kumar:** Emphasizes prescriptive analytics as the "next frontier" in business analytics, focusing on its ability to drive proactive and optimal decision-making.
*   **R. Sharda, D. Delen & E. Turban:** Position prescriptive analytics as the bridge between insights and action, highlighting its role in operational efficiency and strategic advantage.

## 2. The Role of Prescriptive Analytics in Business Decision Making

Prescriptive analytics plays a crucial role in transforming raw data into actionable insights that directly influence business decisions. It moves beyond understanding past trends or predicting future events to actively shaping business outcomes.

**How it Enhances Decision Making:**

*   **Optimizing Resource Allocation:** Determining the most efficient way to distribute limited resources (e.g., budget, personnel, inventory).
*   **Improving Operational Efficiency:** Identifying bottlenecks and suggesting process improvements to reduce costs and increase throughput.
*   **Enhancing Customer Experience:** Providing personalized recommendations and tailored offers to improve customer satisfaction and loyalty.
*   **Mitigating Risks:** Identifying potential risks and suggesting strategies to avoid or minimize their impact.
*   **Strategic Planning:** Informing long-term strategies by simulating the impact of different business decisions.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:** CO1: Understand the fundamentals of business analytics and how it is becoming competitive strategy for many organisations. (K2), CO2: Understand the Importance of analytics in decision making and problem solving. (K2)

**Examples:**

*   **Supply Chain Management:** Prescribing optimal inventory levels to minimize holding costs and stockouts, or determining the best routes for delivery vehicles.
*   **Marketing:** Recommending the most effective marketing campaigns and channels to reach target customer segments.
*   **Finance:** Suggesting optimal investment portfolios or credit scoring models that recommend whether to approve or deny a loan.
*   **Healthcare:** Recommending treatment plans for patients based on their medical history and condition.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Gert H. N. Laursen & Jesper Thorlund:** Highlight how prescriptive analytics elevates business intelligence beyond simple reporting by providing direct guidance for action.
*   **J. R. Evans:** Discusses the practical applications of prescriptive analytics in various business functions, emphasizing its impact on operational and strategic decisions.

## 3. Key Techniques and Methodologies in Prescriptive Analytics

Prescriptive analytics employs a range of sophisticated techniques to generate recommendations. These techniques often involve mathematical modeling and optimization.

**Key Methodologies:**

*   **Optimization Models:**
    *   **Linear Programming (LP):** Used to optimize a linear objective function subject to linear constraints.
        *   **Example:** Determining the optimal product mix to maximize profit given production capacity and raw material availability.
    *   **Integer Programming (IP):** Similar to LP, but variables must be integers.
        *   **Example:** Deciding which projects to invest in to maximize return within a fixed budget, where projects are either fully funded or not.
    *   **Non-linear Programming:** Used when the objective function or constraints are non-linear.
        *   **Example:** Pricing strategies where demand is a non-linear function of price.
*   **Simulation:**
    *   **Monte Carlo Simulation:** Uses random sampling to model the probability of different outcomes in a process that cannot be easily predicted due to the intervention of random variables.
        *   **Example:** Simulating stock market fluctuations to assess investment risk.
    *   **Discrete-Event Simulation:** Models a system as a sequence of events, where each event marks a change of state in the system.
        *   **Example:** Simulating a manufacturing process to identify bottlenecks and optimize workflow.
*   **Decision Trees:** While often used for classification and prediction, advanced decision trees can also guide prescriptive actions by outlining optimal paths based on outcomes.
*   **Rule-Based Systems (Expert Systems):** Employs a set of "if-then" rules, often derived from expert knowledge, to make decisions.
    *   **Example:** A fraud detection system that flags transactions based on predefined suspicious patterns.
*   **Machine Learning Algorithms for Optimization:**
    *   **Reinforcement Learning:** Algorithms learn to make a sequence of decisions by trying to maximize a reward signal.
        *   **Example:** Training a robot to navigate a maze or an autonomous vehicle to drive.
    *   **Recommendation Systems (Collaborative Filtering, Content-Based Filtering):** Suggest items based on user behavior or item attributes.

## 4. Prescriptive Analytics vs. Other Forms of Analytics

Understanding the distinct role of prescriptive analytics requires comparing it with descriptive, diagnostic, and predictive analytics.

| Analytics Type       | Question Answered                 | Focus                                     | Output                                       | Example                                                                            |
| :------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Descriptive**      | What happened?                    | Summarizing past data.                    | Reports, dashboards, visualizations.         | Sales performance report last quarter.                                             |
| **Diagnostic**       | Why did it happen?                | Identifying causes and relationships.     | Root cause analysis, drill-downs.            | Identifying why sales declined in a specific region.                               |
| **Predictive**       | What will happen?                 | Forecasting future events and trends.     | Forecasts, probabilities, predictions.       | Predicting next quarter's sales volume.                                            |
| **Prescriptive**     | What should we do?                | Recommending actions to achieve goals.    | Recommendations, optimized solutions, plans. | Suggesting marketing spend allocation to maximize predicted sales.                 |

**Learning Outcome Alignment:** CO1: Understand the fundamentals of business analytics and how it is becoming competitive strategy for many organisations. (K2), CO6: Understand the basic concepts in prescriptive analytics. (K2)

**Important Point to Remember:** Prescriptive analytics is the ultimate goal for many organizations as it directly drives action and leads to improved outcomes. It leverages the insights from the other forms of analytics.

## 5. Business Performance Management and Prescriptive Analytics

Business Performance Management (BPM) is a continuous process of planning, monitoring, and managing an organization's performance to achieve its strategic goals. Prescriptive analytics is a powerful enabler of effective BPM.

**How Prescriptive Analytics Supports BPM:**

*   **Goal Setting & Strategy Alignment:** By simulating different scenarios, prescriptive analytics can help set realistic and achievable performance targets aligned with business strategy.
*   **Performance Monitoring:** While descriptive analytics shows current performance, prescriptive analytics can suggest adjustments needed to stay on track or to exceed targets.
*   **Actionable Insights for Improvement:** Prescriptive recommendations directly inform actions aimed at improving key performance indicators (KPIs).
*   **Resource Optimization for Performance:** Prescriptive models can guide the allocation of resources to initiatives that are predicted to yield the greatest performance improvements.
*   **Risk Management in BPM:** Prescriptive analytics can identify potential risks to achieving performance goals and suggest mitigation strategies.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:** C07: Understand the essence of business performance management and analytics in business support functions. (Knowledge Level: K2)

**Examples:**

*   **Sales Performance:** Prescriptive analytics might recommend specific sales training programs or incentives for underperforming regions to improve sales figures.
*   **Customer Satisfaction:** Analyzing customer feedback and suggesting personalized service improvements or product offerings to boost satisfaction scores.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** Identifying and recommending process changes in manufacturing to reduce production cycle times and improve throughput.

**Textbook References:**

*   **R. Sharda, D. Delen & E. Turban:** Discuss the integration of analytics, including prescriptive, into performance management frameworks to drive strategic execution.
*   **Gert H. N. Laursen & Jesper Thorlund:** Emphasize how prescriptive analytics moves beyond reporting on performance to actively guiding managerial decisions for performance enhancement.

## 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs):**

1.  Which type of analytics answers the question "What should we do?"
    a) Descriptive Analytics
    b) Diagnostic Analytics
    c) Predictive Analytics
    d) Prescriptive Analytics

2.  Linear Programming is a technique primarily used in which type of analytics?
    a) Descriptive Analytics
    b) Diagnostic Analytics
    c) Predictive Analytics
    d) Prescriptive Analytics

3.  Which of the following is a key objective of prescriptive analytics?
    a) To describe past events
    b) To identify the root cause of a problem
    c) To forecast future trends
    d) To recommend optimal actions

4.  Monte Carlo Simulation is a technique that helps in:
    a) Describing historical data patterns
    b) Explaining why certain events occurred
    c) Predicting the likelihood of different outcomes through random sampling
    d) Recommending the best course of action based on predefined rules

**Short Answer Questions:**

5.  Briefly explain the difference between predictive and prescriptive analytics.
6.  Provide one example of how prescriptive analytics can be used in supply chain management.
7.  What is the primary goal of Business Performance Management (BPM), and how does prescriptive analytics contribute to it?

**Practical Exercise (Conceptual):**

8.  Imagine you are managing an e-commerce website.
    *   **Descriptive:** You have data on customer browsing history and past purchases. What kind of descriptive analysis could you perform?
    *   **Predictive:** Based on this data, what could you predict about a customer's future behavior?
    *   **Prescriptive:** What specific recommendations could you *prescribe* to this customer to increase the likelihood of a purchase or to improve their experience?

## 7. Answers to Practice Questions

1.  **d) Prescriptive Analytics**
2.  **d) Prescriptive Analytics**
3.  **d) To recommend optimal actions**
4.  **c) Predicting the likelihood of different outcomes through random sampling** (While it aids prescriptive, its core function is modeling uncertainty to inform decisions.)
5.  **Difference between Predictive and Prescriptive Analytics:**
    *   **Predictive Analytics** focuses on forecasting what *will* happen in the future based on historical data and trends. It answers the question "What is likely to happen?"
    *   **Prescriptive Analytics** goes a step further by recommending specific actions that *should* be taken to achieve desired outcomes or to influence future events. It answers the question "What should we do?"
6.  **Example in Supply Chain Management:** Prescriptive analytics can be used to determine the optimal inventory levels for different products across various warehouse locations to minimize holding costs while ensuring sufficient stock to meet demand, thus preventing stockouts.
7.  **Business Performance Management (BPM) Goal & Prescriptive Analytics Contribution:** The goal of BPM is to continuously plan, monitor, and manage an organization's performance to achieve its strategic objectives. Prescriptive analytics contributes by providing actionable recommendations that guide managers on what steps to take to improve performance, optimize resource allocation, and mitigate risks, directly supporting the achievement of strategic goals.
8.  **E-commerce Website Exercise:**
    *   **Descriptive:** Analyze which product categories are most frequently viewed, which products are most often added to the cart but not purchased, customer demographics, and average order value.
    *   **Predictive:** Predict which customers are most likely to churn, which products a customer is likely to buy next based on their browsing history, or the probability of a customer responding to a specific promotion.
    *   **Prescriptive:**
        *   **For a customer who frequently browses a specific product category but doesn't buy:** Prescribe a targeted discount offer on an item from that category or recommend similar, potentially more appealing, products.
        *   **For a customer with items in their cart but not checked out:** Prescribe a reminder email with a limited-time offer or highlight related complementary products to encourage completion of the purchase.
        *   **For a customer identified as likely to churn:** Prescribe a personalized loyalty reward or a special discount on their next purchase.

## 8. Important Points to Remember

*   Prescriptive analytics is the **highest level of analytical maturity**, moving from understanding to action.
*   It leverages insights from **descriptive, diagnostic, and predictive analytics** to formulate recommendations.
*   Key techniques include **optimization models (LP, IP), simulation, and rule-based systems.**
*   The primary goal is to **guide decision-making and optimize business outcomes.**
*   Prescriptive analytics is a critical component of effective **Business Performance Management (BPM)**.
*   It helps organizations gain a significant **competitive advantage** by enabling data-driven, optimized decisions.
