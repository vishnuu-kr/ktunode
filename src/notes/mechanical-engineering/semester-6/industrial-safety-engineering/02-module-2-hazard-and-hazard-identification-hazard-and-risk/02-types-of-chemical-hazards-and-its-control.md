# Industrial Safety Engineering: Module 2 - Hazard and Hazard Identification: Hazard and Risk

## Topic: Types of Chemical Hazards and Its Control

---

### **Introduction**

This module focuses on understanding chemical hazards, a critical aspect of industrial safety. Chemical hazards pose significant risks to workers, the environment, and the public. This topic will delve into the various types of chemical hazards, their effects, and the essential control measures to mitigate these risks. This aligns with **CO2: Describe the different types of hazards and apply hazard identification tools.**

---

### **1. Understanding Chemical Hazards**

#### **1.1. Definition of Chemical Hazard**
A chemical hazard is any hazard that arises from the properties of a chemical substance. Exposure to these substances, whether through inhalation, skin contact, ingestion, or injection, can lead to adverse health effects or physical damage.

#### **1.2. Classification of Chemical Hazards**
Chemical hazards can be broadly classified based on their inherent properties and the potential harm they can cause.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Toxicity:** The degree to which a substance can damage an organism.
*   **Corrosivity:** The ability of a substance to destroy or damage materials, including living tissue, through chemical action.
*   **Flammability:** The ease with which a substance ignites and burns.
*   **Reactivity:** The tendency of a substance to undergo chemical reactions, potentially releasing energy or hazardous byproducts.
*   **Explosivity:** The ability of a substance to rapidly decompose or react, producing a large volume of gas and heat, resulting in a detonation or explosion.
*   **Asphyxiation:** The state or process of being deprived of oxygen, which can lead to unconsciousness or death; this can be caused by inert gases displacing oxygen.
*   **Sensitization:** The development of an allergic reaction after repeated exposure to a substance.

### **2. Types of Chemical Hazards and Their Effects**

#### **2.1. Health Hazards**

These hazards affect the body's systems through various exposure routes.

*   **Toxics/Poisons:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that can cause harm or death when ingested, inhaled, or absorbed through the skin.
    *   **Effects:** Can range from mild irritation to severe organ damage, cancer, reproductive issues, or death, depending on the substance, dose, and duration of exposure.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Acute Toxicity:** Lead (neurological damage), Cyanides (cellular respiration inhibition), Mercury (neurological and kidney damage).
        *   **Chronic Toxicity:** Asbestos (lung cancer, mesothelioma), Benzene (leukemia), Vinyl Chloride (liver cancer).
    *   **Reference:** *Industrial Safety, Health and Environment management systems* by R.K Jain discusses toxicology and exposure limits in detail.

*   **Corrosives:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that cause damage to living tissue and materials upon contact.
    *   **Effects:** Chemical burns on skin and eyes, damage to respiratory tract if inhaled, destruction of materials.
    *   **Examples:** Strong acids (Sulfuric acid, Hydrochloric acid), Strong alkalis (Sodium hydroxide, Potassium hydroxide).
    *   **Reference:** Grimaldi and Simonds, *Safety Management*, likely covers corrosive materials and first-aid measures.

*   **Irritants:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that cause inflammation or discomfort on contact with skin, eyes, or respiratory tract, but do not cause permanent damage.
    *   **Effects:** Redness, itching, burning sensation, coughing, sneezing.
    *   **Examples:** Ammonia, Chlorine gas, some solvents.

*   **Sensitizers:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that can cause an allergic reaction after initial exposure, with subsequent exposures leading to a more severe reaction.
    *   **Effects:** Allergic dermatitis, asthma, respiratory sensitization.
    *   **Examples:** Isocyanates (used in polyurethane production), Epoxy resins, certain metal salts.

*   **Carcinogens, Mutagens, Teratogens (CMTs):**
    *   **Definition:**
        *   **Carcinogens:** Substances that can cause cancer.
        *   **Mutagens:** Substances that can cause genetic mutations.
        *   **Teratogens:** Substances that can cause birth defects.
    *   **Effects:** Cancer, heritable genetic damage, developmental abnormalities in offspring.
    *   **Examples:** Asbestos (carcinogen), Ethylene oxide (carcinogen, mutagen), Thalidomide (teratogen).
    *   **Reference:** *Occupational Safety and Health Management* by Thomas J. Anton would likely detail the health effects of these substances.

*   **Asphyxiants:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that reduce the amount of oxygen available to the body, leading to suffocation.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Simple Asphyxiants:** Inert gases that displace oxygen (e.g., Nitrogen, Argon, Carbon Dioxide).
        *   **Chemical Asphyxiants:** Substances that interfere with the body's ability to use oxygen (e.g., Carbon Monoxide).
    *   **Effects:** Dizziness, headache, unconsciousness, death due to lack of oxygen.
    *   **Examples:** Carbon Monoxide (binds to hemoglobin), Nitrogen in confined spaces.

#### **2.2. Physical Hazards**

These hazards are related to the physical properties of the chemical substance, often involving energy release.

*   **Flammables/Combustibles:**
    *   **Definition:**
        *   **Flammable:** Substances that ignite easily at relatively low temperatures.
        *   **Combustible:** Substances that require a higher temperature to ignite.
    *   **Key Concepts:**
        *   **Flash Point:** The lowest temperature at which a liquid gives off enough vapor to form an ignitable mixture with air.
        *   **Flammability Limits (LEL/UEL):** The range of concentrations of a flammable vapor in air that will burn or explode.
        *   **Autoignition Temperature:** The lowest temperature at which a substance will spontaneously ignite in a normal atmosphere without an external source of ignition.
    *   **Effects:** Fires, explosions, thermal burns.
    *   **Examples:** Solvents (Acetone, Ethanol), Flammable gases (Propane, Acetylene), Combustible dusts (flour, coal dust).
    *   **Reference:** Blake, *Industrial safety*, would likely cover fire prevention and control for flammable materials.

*   **Explosives:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that can undergo rapid decomposition, producing a large volume of gas and heat, resulting in an explosion.
    *   **Effects:** Blast waves, fragmentation, thermal radiation, significant structural damage.
    *   **Examples:** Dynamite, Nitroglycerin, certain peroxides, some metal powders (under specific conditions).

*   **Oxidizers:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that readily yield oxygen or other oxidizing agents, and therefore, by themselves may not burn, but will increase the combustion rate of other materials.
    *   **Effects:** Intensify fires, can cause spontaneous ignition of combustible materials.
    *   **Examples:** Hydrogen Peroxide, Potassium Permanganate, Chlorates, Nitrates.

*   **Reactives:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that can undergo a violent chemical reaction when exposed to heat, shock, friction, or contact with other substances.
    *   **Effects:** Explosion, fire, release of toxic gases, pressure build-up.
    *   **Examples:** Water-reactive substances (e.g., Sodium, Potassium), Air-reactive substances, substances that polymerize explosively, organic peroxides.
    *   **Reference:** AIChE/CCPS, *Guidelines for Hazard Evaluation Procedures*, is a primary resource for understanding chemical reactivity hazards.

*   **Unstable Substances:**
    *   **Definition:** Substances that can decompose or polymerize exothermically, potentially leading to explosion or uncontrolled release of energy.
    *   **Examples:** Acetylene (can decompose explosively under pressure), Peroxides, some polymers.

### **3. Hazard Identification and Assessment for Chemical Hazards**

Identifying chemical hazards is the first step in controlling them. This involves understanding the properties of the chemicals used and the processes involved.

#### **3.1. Tools and Techniques for Hazard Identification**

*   **Safety Data Sheets (SDS) / Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS):**
    *   **Definition:** Documents providing comprehensive information about a chemical substance or mixture, including its properties, hazards, and safety precautions.
    *   **Importance:** Essential for understanding the risks associated with a chemical.
    *   **Content:** Identification, Hazard(s) identification, Composition/information on ingredients, First-aid measures, Fire-fighting measures, Accidental release measures, Handling and storage, Exposure controls/personal protection, Physical and chemical properties, Stability and reactivity, Toxicological information, Ecological information, Disposal considerations, Transport information, Regulatory information.
    *   **Reference:** *Safety Management System and Documentation training Programme handbook* by Paul S V would likely detail the importance and content of SDS/MSDS.

*   **Chemical Inventory:**
    *   **Definition:** A complete list of all chemicals present in a workplace.
    *   **Importance:** Helps track quantities, locations, and potential interactions.

*   **Process Hazard Analysis (PHA):**
    *   **Definition:** Systematic methods for identifying, evaluating, and controlling hazards associated with industrial processes.
    *   **Techniques:**
        *   **HAZOP (Hazard and Operability Study):** A structured and systematic examination of a planned or existing process, designed to identify potential hazards and operability problems.
        *   **What-If Analysis:** A brainstorming technique where "what-if" questions are posed to identify potential deviations and their consequences.
        *   **Checklist Analysis:** Using pre-defined checklists to ensure all potential hazards are considered.
        *   **Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA):** A step-by-step approach for identifying all possible failures in a design, manufacturing or service process.
    *   **Reference:** AIChE/CCPS, *Guidelines for Hazard Evaluation Procedures*, is a definitive source for PHA methodologies.

*   **Walkthrough Inspections/Audits:**
    *   **Definition:** On-site inspections to observe work practices, identify unsafe conditions, and review safety procedures.

*   **Incident Investigation:**
    *   **Definition:** Analyzing accidents and near misses to identify root causes, including chemical hazards.

#### **3.2. Risk Assessment**

*   **Definition:** The process of evaluating the risk associated with identified hazards. It involves estimating the likelihood of an adverse event occurring and the severity of its consequences.
*   **Risk = Likelihood x Consequence**
*   **Importance:** To prioritize control measures based on the level of risk.

### **4. Control Measures for Chemical Hazards**

The hierarchy of controls is a fundamental principle in managing chemical hazards, prioritizing the most effective measures.

#### **4.1. Hierarchy of Controls**

1.  **Elimination/Substitution:**
    *   **Definition:** Removing the hazardous chemical or replacing it with a less hazardous one.
    *   **Example:** Replacing a highly toxic solvent with water-based cleaning agents.

2.  **Engineering Controls:**
    *   **Definition:** Modifying the work environment or process to reduce exposure.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Ventilation Systems:** Local Exhaust Ventilation (LEV) to capture contaminants at the source (e.g., fume hoods, extraction arms), general dilution ventilation.
        *   **Enclosure/Containment:** Isolating hazardous processes or materials (e.g., glove boxes, closed systems).
        *   **Process Modification:** Altering process parameters to reduce the generation of hazardous substances.
        *   **Automation:** Reducing manual handling of hazardous chemicals.
    *   **Reference:** Blake, *Industrial safety*, would likely discuss ventilation and containment as primary safety measures.

3.  **Administrative Controls:**
    *   **Definition:** Implementing work practices and procedures to minimize exposure.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Safe Work Procedures (SWPs):** Developing and enforcing detailed procedures for handling, storage, and disposal of chemicals.
        *   **Training and Education:** Educating workers on the hazards of chemicals and safe handling practices.
        *   **Work Scheduling:** Rotating tasks to limit exposure duration.
        *   **Signage and Labeling:** Clear labeling of chemicals and hazardous areas.
        *   **Emergency Preparedness:** Developing spill response plans and emergency procedures.
    *   **Reference:** Jain, *Industrial Safety, Health and Environment management systems*, emphasizes the role of training and procedures.

4.  **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):**
    *   **Definition:** Equipment worn by workers to protect themselves from chemical hazards.
    *   **Important Note:** PPE is the *last line of defense* and should not be relied upon as the primary control measure.
    *   **Selection:** Based on the specific hazard and exposure route (as discussed in **CO4**).
        *   **Respiratory Protection:** Respirators (e.g., N95 masks, half-face/full-face respirators with appropriate cartridges, supplied-air respirators).
        *   **Skin Protection:** Gloves (e.g., nitrile, neoprene, butyl rubber depending on the chemical), Aprons, Chemical-resistant suits.
        *   **Eye Protection:** Safety glasses, Goggles, Face shields.
        *   **Foot Protection:** Chemical-resistant safety boots.
    *   **Reference:** CO4 specifically addresses PPE selection and use.

#### **4.2. Specific Control Strategies**

*   **Storage and Handling:**
    *   Store chemicals in designated, well-ventilated areas.
    *   Segregate incompatible chemicals (e.g., acids from alkalis, oxidizers from flammables).
    *   Use appropriate containers and labeling.
    *   Ensure proper handling procedures to prevent spills and releases.
    *   Refer to SDS for specific storage and handling recommendations.

*   **Emergency Response:**
    *   **Spill Control:** Having readily available spill kits and trained personnel.
    *   **First Aid:** Ensuring availability of eyewash stations, safety showers, and trained first aid providers.
    *   **Fire Fighting:** Having appropriate fire extinguishers and trained personnel for dealing with chemical fires.
    *   **Evacuation Procedures:** Clear evacuation plans in case of major incidents.

### **5. Regulatory Framework and Standards**

Various regulations and standards govern the safe handling and management of chemicals in the workplace. These include:

*   **Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS):** Standardizes hazard communication globally.
*   **Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) Standards (in the US):** Regulations related to hazard communication, permissible exposure limits (PELs), and process safety management.
*   **National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) Standards:** Guidelines related to fire safety, including the NFPA 704 hazard identification system.

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Know Your Chemicals:** Always refer to the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for comprehensive information on any chemical.
*   **Hierarchy of Controls:** Prioritize elimination, substitution, and engineering controls over administrative controls and PPE.
*   **Segregation is Key:** Store incompatible chemicals separately to prevent dangerous reactions.
*   **Ventilation Matters:** Adequate ventilation, especially local exhaust ventilation, is crucial for controlling airborne chemical exposures.
*   **Training is Essential:** Ensure all personnel handling chemicals are properly trained on their hazards and safe handling procedures.
*   **Emergency Preparedness:** Have robust plans and equipment for spills, fires, and medical emergencies.
*   **Risk Assessment is Dynamic:** Regularly review and update risk assessments as processes or chemicals change.

### **8. Practice Questions & Exercises**

**Question 1:**
List and briefly explain any five major types of health hazards associated with chemicals in an industrial setting.
**Answer:**
1.  **Toxics/Poisons:** Substances that cause harm or death when absorbed. Effects can range from mild irritation to severe organ damage.
2.  **Corrosives:** Substances that damage tissue and materials on contact, causing chemical burns.
3.  **Irritants:** Substances causing inflammation or discomfort on contact but not permanent damage.
4.  **Sensitizers:** Substances causing allergic reactions upon repeated exposure.
5.  **Carcinogens:** Substances that can cause cancer.

**Question 2:**
What are the "Hierarchy of Controls" in managing chemical hazards? Provide an example for each level, starting from the most effective.
**Answer:**
1.  **Elimination/Substitution:** Replacing a toxic solvent with a less toxic one.
2.  **Engineering Controls:** Installing a fume hood to capture chemical vapors at the source.
3.  **Administrative Controls:** Implementing a strict procedure for handling a corrosive acid.
4.  **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Wearing chemical-resistant gloves when handling a solvent.

**Question 3:**
Explain the significance of a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) in managing chemical hazards. What are some key sections you would look for?
**Answer:**
An SDS provides vital information about a chemical's properties, hazards, and safe handling procedures. Key sections include: Hazard(s) identification, First-aid measures, Fire-fighting measures, Handling and storage, Exposure controls/personal protection, and Stability and reactivity.

**Question 4:**
Differentiate between a flammable substance and a combustible substance based on their flash points.
**Answer:**
Flammable substances have a low flash point and ignite easily at ambient temperatures. Combustible substances have a higher flash point and require heating before they can ignite.

**Question 5 (Scenario-based):**
You are working in a chemical plant and notice that a worker is handling a highly volatile and flammable solvent without any eye protection. What is the immediate action you should take? What are the potential chemical hazards involved, and what control measures are missing?
**Answer:**
*   **Immediate Action:** Stop the worker from continuing the task and inform the supervisor immediately. Ensure the worker is moved to a safe area if there's an immediate risk.
*   **Potential Chemical Hazards:**
    *   **Flammability:** High risk of fire or explosion due to the solvent's volatility.
    *   **Health Hazards:** Inhalation of vapors could cause respiratory irritation or systemic toxicity; skin contact could cause irritation or absorption. Eye contact could cause severe irritation or damage.
*   **Missing Control Measures:**
    *   **PPE:** Lack of eye protection (safety glasses/goggles), possibly lack of appropriate gloves and respiratory protection.
    *   **Engineering Controls:** Possibly inadequate local exhaust ventilation.
    *   **Administrative Controls:** Lack of strict adherence to safe work procedures or inadequate supervision.
