# Industrial Safety Engineering: Module 3 - Occupational Health and Safety: Safety and Health Training

## Topic: Respiratory and Non-Respiratory Equipment

### Introduction

This topic focuses on essential personal protective equipment (PPE) that safeguards workers from various occupational hazards. We will delve into the crucial role of respiratory equipment in protecting against airborne contaminants and explore various types of non-respiratory equipment designed for different bodily protection. Understanding the selection, use, and limitations of this equipment is vital for preventing accidents and ensuring a healthy work environment.

This topic directly supports **Course Outcome 4 (CO4): Explain about personal protective equipment, its selection, safety performance & indicators.** and contributes to understanding broader occupational health hazards (CO3).

---

### 1. Respiratory Protective Equipment (RPE)

**Definition:** Respiratory Protective Equipment (RPE) is designed to protect the wearer from inhaling hazardous airborne substances, such as dusts, fumes, mists, gases, vapors, and biological agents.

**Importance:**
*   Prevents immediate and long-term health effects from inhalation of harmful substances.
*   Crucial in environments where engineering controls (e.g., ventilation) are insufficient or not feasible.
*   Essential for emergency response and maintenance activities.

**Types of Respiratory Hazards:**
*   **Particulates:** Dust, fumes, mists, smoke (e.g., welding fumes, silica dust, asbestos fibers).
*   **Gases and Vapors:** Chemical gases, organic vapors, refrigerant leaks (e.g., chlorine gas, benzene vapor, ammonia).
*   **Oxygen-Deficient Atmospheres:** Confined spaces where oxygen levels are below 19.5% (e.g., tanks, silos, pits).

**Selection Criteria for RPE:**
The selection of appropriate RPE depends on several factors:
*   **Nature of the hazard:** Is it particulate, gas/vapor, or oxygen deficiency?
*   **Concentration of the hazard:** How high is the level of the airborne contaminant?
*   **Assigned Protection Factor (APF):** The level of protection a respirator provides.
*   **Work activity:** The type of work being performed (e.g., strenuous activity, sedentary work).
*   **Fit of the respirator:** A proper fit is paramount for effective protection.
*   **User's physical condition:** Any pre-existing respiratory or cardiovascular conditions.
*   **Comfort and usability:** The respirator should be comfortable enough for the duration of use.

**Classification of RPE:**

RPE can be broadly categorized into two main types:

#### 1.1 Air-Purifying Respirators (APRs)

**Description:** APRs remove specific contaminants from the air through filters or cartridges before the air is inhaled by the wearer. They are only effective in atmospheres where there is sufficient oxygen (generally above 19.5%).

**Types of APRs:**

*   **Half-Facepiece Respirators:**
    *   **Description:** Cover the nose and mouth.
    *   **Protection:** Protect against particulates and/or gases/vapors.
    *   **Filters/Cartridges:**
        *   **Particulate Filters (P-Series):**
            *   **P100 (HEPA):** Highly efficient filter, captures at least 99.97% of airborne particles. Used for very fine dusts, asbestos, lead.
            *   **P95:** Captures at least 95% of airborne particles.
            *   **P99:** Captures at least 99% of airborne particles.
        *   **Gas and Vapor Cartridges:** Color-coded for specific contaminants (e.g., black for organic vapors, green for acid gases, yellow for sulfur dioxide, white for chlorine).
    *   **Examples:** Used in painting, woodworking, light chemical handling.
    *   **Key Point (Jain, 2000):** The effectiveness of an APR depends entirely on the correct selection of the filter or cartridge for the specific hazard present.

*   **Full-Facepiece Respirators:**
    *   **Description:** Cover the entire face, including the eyes.
    *   **Protection:** Provide higher protection than half-facepiece respirators due to a better seal and protection for the eyes against irritants.
    *   **Filters/Cartridges:** Same types as half-facepiece respirators.
    *   **Examples:** Used in situations with higher concentrations of hazards, or where eye irritation is also a concern (e.g., heavy chemical spraying, certain cleaning operations).
    *   **Important Point (Grimaldi & Simonds, 2001):** Full-facepiece respirators provide a higher Assigned Protection Factor (APF) than half-facepiece respirators.

*   **Powered Air-Purifying Respirators (PAPRs):**
    *   **Description:** Use a blower motor to draw ambient air through a filter and deliver it to the wearer's facepiece (hood, helmet, or tight-fitting facepiece).
    *   **Protection:** Provide a higher level of protection and are generally more comfortable to wear for extended periods as they do not rely on the user's breathing to draw air through the filters.
    *   **Examples:** Used in manufacturing, pharmaceutical industries, construction.

#### 1.2 Atmosphere-Supplying Respirators

**Description:** These respirators supply clean air from an independent source to the wearer, making them suitable for oxygen-deficient atmospheres or where contaminant concentrations are very high.

**Types of Atmosphere-Supplying Respirators:**

*   **Supplied-Air Respirators (SARs):**
    *   **Description:** Provide breathing air from a source external to the respirator, such as a compressed air cylinder or a compressor. The air is delivered through a hose.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Airline Respirators:** Air is supplied from a compressor through a hose. Requires an oil-free air supply.
        *   **Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA):** The air supply is carried by the wearer in a cylinder attached to the respirator.
    *   **Examples:** SCBA is vital for firefighters, emergency response teams, and for entry into highly hazardous environments. Airline respirators are used in painting, sandblasting, and confined space entry where air quality from the source is guaranteed.
    *   **Key Point (Anton, 1989):** SCBA provides the highest level of protection among all respirators and is essential for situations where the air is immediately dangerous to life or health (IDLH).

*   **Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA):**
    *   **Description:** Consists of a facepiece, a breathing apparatus, and a cylinder of compressed air. The wearer carries their entire air supply.
    *   **Protection:** Provides the highest level of protection and allows for complete mobility in any atmosphere.
    *   **Usage:** Firefighting, hazardous material response, confined space entry into atmospheres with unknown or toxic contaminants or oxygen deficiency.
    *   **Important Consideration (Blake, 1973):** SCBA requires proper training and maintenance due to its complexity and critical role in life-saving situations.

**Fit Testing and Maintenance of RPE:**

*   **Fit Testing:** Crucial to ensure a tight seal between the respirator and the wearer's face. Types include qualitative (QRFT) and quantitative (QNFT) fit testing.
    *   **Qualitative Fit Testing (QLFT):** Uses a test agent (e.g., irritant smoke, sweet or bitter solution) to assess the seal.
    *   **Quantitative Fit Testing (QNFT):** Uses a machine to measure the amount of air leaking into the respirator.
*   **Maintenance:** Regular inspection, cleaning, disinfection, and storage are essential for maintaining the effectiveness and lifespan of RPE.
    *   **Inspection:** Check for damage to the facepiece, straps, exhalation valves, and filters/cartridges.
    *   **Cleaning:** Clean according to manufacturer's instructions.
    *   **Storage:** Store in a clean, dry place away from contaminants and physical damage.

**Limitations of RPE:**
*   Can be uncomfortable to wear, especially for long periods.
*   Can impede communication and vision.
*   Requires proper training for use, maintenance, and fit testing.
*   Limited lifespan for filters and cartridges.
*   Not effective in oxygen-deficient atmospheres (for APRs).
*   Can cause heat stress.

### 2. Non-Respiratory Protective Equipment

**Definition:** Non-respiratory protective equipment encompasses a wide range of gear designed to protect various parts of the body from specific hazards in the workplace.

**Importance:**
*   Prevents injuries such as cuts, abrasions, burns, impacts, chemical splashes, and falls.
*   Enhances worker safety and confidence.
*   Reduces the severity and cost of workplace injuries.

**Types of Non-Respiratory Protective Equipment:**

#### 2.1 Head Protection

*   **Description:** Protects the head from falling objects, impacts, electrical hazards, and sometimes chemical splashes.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Hard Hats (Safety Helmets):**
        *   **Class A:** For general protection against impacts.
        *   **Class B:** For protection against electrical hazards.
        *   **Class C:** For protection against impact only (no electrical protection).
    *   **Caps and Bump Caps:** Offer minimal protection against minor bumps and scrapes.
*   **Examples:** Construction sites, manufacturing plants, mining, electrical work.
*   **Key Point (Jain, 2000):** The type of hard hat selected must be appropriate for the specific hazard (e.g., Class B for electrical work).

#### 2.2 Eye and Face Protection

*   **Description:** Protects the eyes and face from flying particles, dust, chemical splashes, harmful rays, and impacts.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Safety Glasses:** Protect against flying particles and minor impacts.
    *   **Goggles:** Provide a tighter seal around the eyes, protecting against dust, chemical splashes, and impacts from all directions.
    *   **Face Shields:** Protect the entire face from splashes, flying particles, and moderate impact hazards. Often worn over safety glasses or goggles.
    *   **Welding Helmets and Goggles:** Specifically designed with tinted lenses to protect against intense light and UV/IR radiation during welding.
*   **Examples:** Grinding, machining, chemical handling, welding, construction.
*   **Important Point (Grimaldi & Simonds, 2001):** Prescription eyeglasses are not a substitute for safety glasses. Employees who wear prescription glasses should use safety glasses with prescription lenses or wear protective prescription goggles over their regular glasses.

#### 2.3 Hearing Protection

*   **Description:** Protects against noise-induced hearing loss by reducing the level of sound entering the ear.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Earplugs:** Inserted into the ear canal. Available in disposable or reusable foam or silicone.
    *   **Earmuffs:** Cover the entire outer ear. Provide a higher level of protection than earplugs in many cases.
*   **Selection:** Based on the Noise Reduction Rating (NRR) and the specific noise environment.
*   **Examples:** Manufacturing plants, construction sites, airports, anywhere with high noise levels.
*   **Key Point (Anton, 1989):** Hearing protection is crucial in any environment where noise levels exceed 85 decibels (dB) for an 8-hour time-weighted average.

#### 2.4 Hand Protection

*   **Description:** Protects hands from cuts, abrasions, punctures, burns, chemicals, and electrical shock.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Leather Gloves:** Good for general protection against abrasion and mild impacts.
    *   **Fabric Gloves:** Suitable for general handling and protection against dirt and minor abrasions.
    *   **Rubber/Neoprene/Nitrile Gloves:** Offer excellent protection against chemicals, solvents, and some acids.
    *   **PVC Gloves:** Provide resistance to a wide range of chemicals.
    *   **Insulated Gloves:** Protect against heat and cold.
    *   **Cut-Resistant Gloves:** Made from materials like Kevlar to protect against cuts.
    *   **Electrical Gloves:** Made of insulating rubber and rated for specific voltage levels.
*   **Examples:** Construction, chemical handling, manufacturing, electrical work, food processing.
*   **Important Point (Jain, 2000):** The correct glove material must be chosen based on the specific chemical or hazard being handled. Refer to chemical resistance charts.

#### 2.5 Foot Protection

*   **Description:** Protects feet from falling objects, punctures, electrical hazards, and chemical splashes.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Safety Shoes/Boots:** Typically have steel or composite toe caps to protect against crushing injuries.
    *   **Puncture-Resistant Soles:** Protect against nails and sharp objects.
    *   **Chemical-Resistant Boots:** Made from materials like rubber or PVC for protection against chemical spills.
    *   **Electrical Hazard (EH) Rated Boots:** Designed to provide a secondary source of protection against accidental contact with live electrical circuits.
*   **Examples:** Construction sites, manufacturing, warehouses, workshops, anywhere with foot hazards.
*   **Key Point (Blake, 1973):** Employers have a responsibility to provide appropriate safety footwear where hazards exist.

#### 2.6 Body Protection

*   **Description:** Protects the body from chemicals, heat, cold, electrical hazards, molten metal, and flying particles.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Aprons:** Protect the front of the body from splashes and stains.
    *   **Coveralls/Boiler Suits:** Full-body protection against dirt, grease, and minor hazards. Can be made from various materials for different hazards (e.g., flame-resistant, chemical-resistant).
    *   **Chemical-Resistant Suits:** Fully encapsulating or partial suits made from materials impermeable to specific chemicals.
    *   **Flame-Resistant (FR) Clothing:** Made from materials that resist ignition and self-extinguish when the ignition source is removed.
    *   **High-Visibility Clothing:** Made with fluorescent materials and retroreflective strips to make the wearer more visible in low-light or busy environments.
    *   **Leather Aprons and Sleeves:** Protect against sparks and moderate heat.
*   **Examples:** Welding, chemical processing, laboratories, construction, road work, emergency response.
*   **Important Point (AIChE/CCPS, 1992):** In high-risk chemical environments, full chemical protective suits may be required to prevent any contact with hazardous substances.

#### 2.7 Fall Protection

*   **Description:** Prevents falls from height and mitigates injuries if a fall occurs.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Safety Harness:** Distributes the force of a fall across the body.
    *   **Lanyards:** Connect the harness to an anchor point. Can be shock-absorbing.
    *   **Retractable Lifelines/Self-Retracting Lifelines (SRLs):** Similar to seatbelts; they allow movement but automatically arrest a fall.
    *   **Guardrails, Safety Nets, and Hole Covers:** These are engineering controls that prevent a fall from occurring in the first place, but are mentioned here as part of the overall fall protection system.
*   **Examples:** Construction, maintenance at height, window cleaning, working on roofs.
*   **Key Point (Waring, 1996):** A comprehensive fall protection program includes training, equipment inspection, and rescue plans.

### 3. Safety Performance and Indicators Related to PPE

*   **Safety Performance:** Refers to how well an organization is managing its safety processes and achieving its safety goals. The use and effectiveness of PPE are critical components of this performance.
*   **Safety Indicators:** Measurable metrics used to track and assess safety performance.

**Key Indicators related to PPE:**

*   **PPE Compliance Rate:** Percentage of employees observed wearing the correct PPE for their task.
*   **Number of PPE Violations:** Incidents where employees are found not using required PPE.
*   **Number of PPE-Related Injuries:** Injuries that occurred because PPE was not used, was used incorrectly, or was inadequate.
*   **PPE Inspection and Maintenance Records:** Frequency and completeness of inspections and maintenance for PPE.
*   **Training Completion Rates for PPE:** Percentage of employees who have completed required PPE training.
*   **Near Misses involving PPE:** Incidents where an injury was narrowly avoided due to or in spite of PPE usage.
*   **Employee Feedback on PPE:** Surveys or feedback mechanisms to gauge the comfort, fit, and perceived effectiveness of PPE.

**Improving Safety Performance through PPE:**

*   **Effective Training:** Ensure workers understand why PPE is necessary, how to use it correctly, and its limitations.
*   **Proper Selection:** Choose PPE that is appropriate for the specific hazard and task.
*   **Regular Inspections and Maintenance:** Ensure PPE is in good condition and functioning correctly.
*   **Enforcement of PPE Policies:** Consistent and fair enforcement of PPE requirements.
*   **Employee Involvement:** Encourage employees to provide feedback on PPE and participate in selection processes.
*   **Root Cause Analysis of PPE-Related Incidents:** Investigate why PPE failures or non-compliance occurred to prevent recurrence.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Hierarchy of Controls:** PPE is the last line of defense. Engineering controls and administrative controls should be prioritized.
*   **Fit is Paramount:** Even the best PPE is useless if it doesn't fit properly.
*   **Training is Non-Negotiable:** Workers must be trained on the correct use, limitations, and maintenance of their PPE.
*   **Selection is Hazard-Specific:** Always choose PPE that is appropriate for the specific hazard and task.
*   **Maintenance Matters:** Regularly inspect and maintain PPE to ensure its effectiveness.
*   **Limitations Exist:** Understand what PPE can and cannot protect against.

### Practice Questions

**Multiple Choice Questions:**

1.  Which of the following is NOT a type of air-purifying respirator?
    a) Half-facepiece respirator
    b) Full-facepiece respirator
    c) Supplied-air respirator
    d) Powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR)
    **Answer: c)** (Supplied-air respirators are atmosphere-supplying, not air-purifying.)

2.  For protection against extremely fine dusts like asbestos, which particulate filter is most effective?
    a) P95
    b) P99
    c) P100 (HEPA)
    d) N95
    **Answer: c)** (P100 filters have the highest efficiency for fine particulates.)

3.  Which class of hard hat provides protection against electrical hazards?
    a) Class A
    b) Class B
    c) Class C
    d) Class D
    **Answer: b)** (Class B is for electrical protection.)

4.  Which type of hand protection is best suited for handling corrosive chemicals like strong acids?
    a) Leather gloves
    b) Cotton gloves
    c) Nitrile or Neoprene gloves
    d) Kevlar gloves
    **Answer: c)** (Nitrile and Neoprene offer good resistance to many chemicals.)

**Short Answer Questions:**

5.  What are the two main categories of Respiratory Protective Equipment (RPE)? Briefly describe each.
    **Answer:**
    *   **Air-Purifying Respirators (APRs):** These respirators remove specific contaminants from the air through filters or cartridges. They require sufficient oxygen in the atmosphere.
    *   **Atmosphere-Supplying Respirators:** These respirators supply breathing air from an independent source, making them suitable for oxygen-deficient or highly contaminated atmospheres.

6.  Why is fit testing essential for respirators?
    **Answer:** Fit testing is essential to ensure a tight seal between the respirator and the wearer's face. A proper seal is critical for the respirator to effectively filter out airborne contaminants; without it, contaminated air can leak into the breathing zone.

7.  List three examples of non-respiratory PPE and the hazards they protect against.
    **Answer:**
    *   **Safety Glasses:** Protect eyes from flying particles and minor impacts.
    *   **Safety Boots with Steel Toes:** Protect feet from crushing injuries by falling objects.
    *   **Hearing Protection (Earmuffs/Earplugs):** Protect ears from noise-induced hearing loss.

8.  What is a key safety indicator related to PPE usage in a workplace?
    **Answer:** A key indicator is the **PPE Compliance Rate**, which measures the percentage of employees observed correctly using their required PPE. Other valid answers include the number of PPE-related injuries or the frequency of PPE inspections.
