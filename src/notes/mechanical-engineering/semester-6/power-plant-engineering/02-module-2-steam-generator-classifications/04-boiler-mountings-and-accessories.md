# Module 2: Steam Generator Classifications - Boiler Mountings and Accessories

## 1. Introduction

Boilers are the heart of thermal power plants, responsible for generating steam by heating water. To ensure safe, efficient, and reliable operation, boilers are equipped with various essential components known as **boiler mountings** and **accessories**. This module delves into the classification and functions of these vital parts.

**Key Concept:** Boiler mountings are safety and essential operational devices fitted directly on the boiler drum or shell, while accessories are auxiliary devices that improve efficiency and performance but are not directly attached to the boiler shell.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** This topic directly contributes to **CO1 (Explain the layout, components and working of steam, gas, hydro, and nuclear power plants)** by detailing the critical components of steam generation.

## 2. Boiler Mountings

Boiler mountings are safety devices that are mounted directly on the boiler shell or drum. Their primary function is to ensure the safe operation of the boiler by monitoring and controlling critical parameters.

### 2.1. Water Level Indicators

These devices display the water level inside the boiler drum. Maintaining the correct water level is crucial to prevent overheating of boiler tubes (if water level is too low) or flooding of the steam pipes (if water level is too high).

*   **Gauges Glass (Water Gauge):**
    *   **Description:** A transparent glass tube fitted vertically to the boiler drum. The water level in the glass directly reflects the water level inside the drum.
    *   **Working Principle:** Based on the principle of communicating vessels.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Plain Sight Glass:** A single glass tube.
        *   **Reflex Gauge:** Features a prism-like construction that refracts light, making the water level appear darker and more distinct. This is preferred for high-pressure boilers.
        *   **Transparent Gauge:** Consists of two plates of thick glass with a narrow gap between them, allowing the water level to be seen directly.
    *   **Safety Features:**
        *   **Try-cocks:** Three brass cocks located below the gauge glass. They are used to verify the accuracy of the gauge glass reading. The bottom cock, when opened, will discharge water if the level is correct, steam if the level is too low, and nothing if the level is too high.
        *   **Automatic Shut-off Valve (Ball Valve):** Incorporated within the gauge glass fitting. If the glass breaks, a ball within the fitting will be forced into the opening by the steam pressure, thereby shutting off the steam supply and preventing excessive water loss.

*   **High and Low Water Alarms:**
    *   **Description:** Audible or visual alarms that activate when the water level deviates significantly from the safe operating range.
    *   **Working Principle:** Often employ floats or other sensing mechanisms that trigger an electrical or mechanical signal.

**Important Point to Remember:** The gauge glass should always be checked before starting a boiler to ensure an adequate water level.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Directly relates to **CO1** by explaining a fundamental component for the safe operation of a steam plant.

### 2.2. Pressure Measuring Devices

These devices measure and indicate the steam pressure inside the boiler.

*   **Pressure Gauge (Bourdon Gauge):**
    *   **Description:** The most common type of pressure gauge used in boilers. It consists of a curved, flattened tube (Bourdon tube) made of brass or phosphor bronze.
    *   **Working Principle:** When steam enters the Bourdon tube, it tends to straighten due to the internal pressure. This straightening motion is transmitted through a linkage mechanism to a pointer, which moves across a calibrated scale to indicate the pressure.
    *   **Location:** Typically mounted on the steam drum or boiler shell.
    *   **Siphon Tube:** A U-shaped tube filled with water is usually placed between the steam supply and the pressure gauge. This prevents live steam from coming into direct contact with the Bourdon tube, protecting it from damage due to high temperature and pulsations.

**Important Point to Remember:** The pressure gauge must be calibrated regularly to ensure accurate readings.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Essential for **CO1**, as pressure is a critical parameter in steam generation.

---

### 2.3. Steam Outlet Control

These devices regulate the flow of steam from the boiler.

*   **Main Stop Valve (Blow-off Valve):**
    *   **Description:** A robust valve installed on the steam outlet pipe. It controls the supply of steam to the main steam pipe.
    *   **Function:** It is the primary valve for isolating the boiler from the steam header. It should be opened slowly to avoid water hammer.
    *   **Types:** Often a globe valve or a gate valve, designed for high-pressure and high-temperature steam service.

*   **Safety Valves:**
    *   **Description:** The most critical safety device on a boiler. They are designed to automatically release excess steam pressure when it exceeds a predetermined safe limit, preventing catastrophic boiler failure.
    *   **Working Principle:** Spring-loaded or dead-weight loaded valves. When the steam pressure acting on the valve disc exceeds the force exerted by the spring or weight, the valve opens, releasing steam. Once the pressure drops, the valve closes.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Spring-Loaded Safety Valve:** Most common in modern boilers.
        *   **Lever-Type Safety Valve:** Older design, less common now.
    *   **Capacity:** The total discharge capacity of all safety valves fitted to a boiler must be sufficient to prevent the pressure from rising more than 5% above the maximum allowable working pressure.
    *   **Blow-down:** When the valve opens, it releases steam. This is called "blowing down."
    *   **Setting:** Safety valves are usually set to open at a pressure slightly above the normal operating pressure.

**Important Point to Remember:** Safety valves must be tested regularly (often weekly) by lifting the lever to ensure they are functioning correctly and not stuck.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Crucial for **CO1** due to their role in preventing boiler explosions.

### 2.4. Water Discharge Control

These devices are used to remove impurities from the boiler water.

*   **Blow-off Valve (Bottom Blow-off Valve):**
    *   **Description:** A valve located at the lowest point of the boiler shell or drum.
    *   **Function:** Used for blowing down the boiler. This process removes sediment, sludge, and dissolved solids that accumulate at the bottom of the boiler, preventing them from interfering with heat transfer and potentially causing overheating or corrosion.
    *   **Operation:** Operated intermittently and for short durations.
    *   **Types:** Often a quick-opening valve (like a lever-operated gate valve) to allow for rapid discharge.

*   **Surface Blow-off Valve (Skimmer Valve):**
    *   **Description:** Located at the normal water level in the steam drum.
    *   **Function:** Used to remove suspended solids and oily matter that float on the surface of the boiler water. This is typically operated more frequently than the bottom blow-off.

**Important Point to Remember:** Proper blow-down procedures are essential for maintaining water quality and boiler efficiency.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Contributes to **CO1** by explaining the maintenance of the steam generation system.

## 3. Boiler Accessories

Boiler accessories are auxiliary devices that are not fitted directly onto the boiler shell but are connected to it to improve the efficiency, performance, and safety of the steam generation process.

### 3.1. Feed Water Control Devices

These accessories regulate the supply of feedwater to the boiler.

*   **Feed Check Valve:**
    *   **Description:** A non-return valve fitted in the feedwater pipe, just before the water enters the boiler drum.
    *   **Function:** Prevents boiler water from flowing back into the feedwater pump and supply line when the pump stops or when the boiler pressure is higher than the pump pressure.

*   **Feed Pump:**
    *   **Description:** A pump that supplies water to the boiler at a pressure slightly higher than the boiler steam pressure.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Reciprocating Pump:** Positive displacement pump.
        *   **Centrifugal Pump:** Most common in large power plants.
        *   **Injectors:** Devices that use steam to force feedwater into the boiler. They are simpler and have no moving parts but are less efficient and sensitive to pressure variations.

*   **Feed Water Regulator:**
    *   **Description:** An automatic device that maintains the water level in the boiler within a narrow range by controlling the flow of feedwater.
    *   **Working Principle:** Can be based on the differential expansion of metals, floats, or steam pressure. For example, a three-element feedwater regulator utilizes drum water level, steam flow, and feedwater flow to maintain precise control.

**Important Point to Remember:** Consistent and reliable feedwater supply is critical for preventing boiler damage.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Essential for **CO1** by explaining the system that replenishes water for steam generation.

---

### 3.2. Draught Production Devices

Draught is the difference in pressure between the atmosphere and the furnace, which causes the combustion air to flow through the furnace and the flue gases to exit the chimney.

*   **Natural Draught:**
    *   **Description:** Achieved by the difference in density between the hot flue gases inside the chimney and the cooler ambient air. The taller the chimney, the greater the draught.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, no energy consumption.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Limited draught, dependent on atmospheric conditions, requires large chimneys.

*   **Artificial Draught:**
    *   **Description:** Produced by mechanical means (fans).
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Forced Draught:** A fan (forcing fan) is placed at the base of the furnace to blow air into the combustion chamber. The furnace and ash pit are under positive pressure.
        *   **Induced Draught:** A fan (induced draught fan) is placed at the chimney base to suck flue gases from the furnace. The furnace and ash pit are under negative pressure (slight vacuum).
        *   **Balanced Draught:** A combination of forced and induced draught. A forcing fan supplies air to the furnace, and an induced draught fan removes flue gases from the furnace. This system maintains the furnace at approximately atmospheric pressure, providing better control over combustion.

**Important Point to Remember:** Draught control is crucial for efficient combustion and heat transfer in the boiler.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Directly relates to **CO1** by explaining the system that facilitates combustion, a key aspect of steam power plants.

### 3.3. Fuel Burning Devices

These are the systems that introduce and burn fuel within the boiler furnace.

*   **Stokers:**
    *   **Description:** Mechanical devices used for feeding solid fuel (coal) into the furnace.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Underfeed Stokers:** Fuel is pushed up from beneath the burning coal bed.
        *   **Overfeed Stokers:** Fuel is fed onto the top of the fuel bed.
        *   **Spreader Stokers:** Fuel is thrown onto the grate, and combustion occurs in suspension and on the fuel bed.

*   **Burners (for Oil and Gas Firing):**
    *   **Description:** Devices that atomize and mix fuel oil or gas with air for efficient combustion in the furnace.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Rotary Cup Burners:** Atomize oil by centrifugal force.
        *   **Pressure Atomizing Burners:** Atomize oil by forcing it through small orifices under high pressure.
        *   **Gas Burners:** Designed to mix gas with air in precise ratios.

**Important Point to Remember:** The design of the fuel burning device significantly impacts combustion efficiency and pollutant emissions.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Crucial for **CO1** and **CO5** as it directly relates to the fuel used and potential pollution.

---

### 3.4. Air Preheaters

*   **Description:** Heat exchangers that preheat the combustion air using the heat from the flue gases before they enter the furnace.
*   **Function:**
    *   Increases the temperature of combustion air, leading to more efficient combustion and higher furnace temperatures.
    *   Improves boiler efficiency by recovering waste heat from flue gases.
    *   Reduces the temperature of flue gases leaving the stack, which can help with pollution control (e.g., preventing acid dew point formation).
*   **Types:**
    *   **Recuperative Air Preheater:** Combustion air and flue gases flow through separate passages within the heat exchanger.
        *   **Tubular Air Preheater:** Flue gases pass through tubes, and air flows around them.
        *   **Plate Air Preheater:** Alternating passages for air and flue gases separated by plates.
    *   **Regenerative Air Preheater (Ljungstrom Air Preheater):** A rotating matrix (often made of metal plates or wire mesh) is alternately heated by flue gases and then cooled by combustion air.

**Important Point to Remember:** Air preheaters significantly improve boiler efficiency by recovering waste heat.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Directly contributes to **CO2** by influencing thermal efficiency and **CO1** by explaining a component that enhances performance.

---

### 3.5. Economizers

*   **Description:** Heat exchangers placed in the path of flue gases before they enter the chimney. They preheat the feedwater using the heat from the flue gases.
*   **Function:**
    *   Increases boiler efficiency by recovering heat from flue gases that would otherwise be lost.
    *   Reduces the amount of fuel required for a given steam output.
    *   Preheats feedwater, reducing thermal stress on the boiler drum.
*   **Working Principle:** Flue gases pass over tubes containing feedwater, transferring heat to the water.
*   **Placement:** Typically installed in the flue gas path after the superheater (if present) and before the air preheater (if present).

**Important Point to Remember:** Economizers are a key measure for improving the overall thermal efficiency of a boiler.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Directly relates to **CO2** by improving the thermal efficiency of the Rankine cycle.

---

### 3.6. Superheaters

*   **Description:** Heat exchangers placed in the path of steam after it leaves the boiler drum. They further heat the steam using the heat from the flue gases.
*   **Function:**
    *   Increases the temperature of the steam above its saturation temperature, making it **superheated steam**.
    *   Superheated steam has a higher enthalpy, leading to more work output in the turbine.
    *   Prevents condensation of steam in the turbine blades, reducing erosion and improving efficiency.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Convection Superheater:** Steam flows through tubes that are directly exposed to hot flue gases.
    *   **Radiant Superheater:** Tubes are placed in the hottest part of the furnace, receiving heat primarily through radiation.
    *   **Reheater:** Used in large power plants to reheat steam that has already passed through the high-pressure turbine stages, improving the overall efficiency of the steam cycle.

**Important Point to Remember:** Superheating is crucial for achieving high thermal efficiency and protecting the steam turbine.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Directly relates to **CO1** and **CO3** by explaining the process of generating superheated steam, which is vital for turbine performance.

---

### 3.7. Desuperheaters (Attemperators)

*   **Description:** Devices used to reduce the temperature of superheated steam to a desired level.
*   **Function:**
    *   Used when the process requires steam at a lower temperature than that produced by the superheater.
    *   Prevents excessive temperatures in certain parts of the plant or for specific applications.
*   **Working Principle:** Typically involves injecting a controlled amount of cool feedwater directly into the superheated steam. The water evaporates, absorbing heat from the steam and reducing its temperature.

**Important Point to Remember:** Desuperheaters are used for precise temperature control of steam.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Contributes to **CO1** by explaining a method for controlling steam temperature for various plant requirements.

---

### 3.8. Steam Traps

*   **Description:** Automatic devices that remove condensate (water formed from steam condensation) and non-condensable gases from steam lines and equipment without allowing live steam to escape.
*   **Function:**
    *   Ensures that only dry steam reaches the equipment (e.g., turbine, heat exchanger).
    *   Prevents water hammer in steam lines, which can cause significant damage.
    *   Improves heat transfer efficiency by removing insulating condensate.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Mechanical Steam Traps:** Operate based on the difference in density or thermal expansion between steam and condensate.
        *   **Float Type:** Uses a float that rises with condensate and falls with steam.
        *   **Thermostatic Type:** Uses a bimetallic strip or a capsule that expands with heat and contracts with cold.
    *   **Thermodynamic Steam Traps:** Operate based on the difference in pressure between steam and condensate.
        *   **Disc Type:** A simple and robust design.

**Important Point to Remember:** Proper selection and maintenance of steam traps are vital for steam system efficiency and safety.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:** Contributes to **CO1** by ensuring efficient steam delivery and preventing operational issues like water hammer.

## 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
What is the primary function of a safety valve in a boiler, and why is it considered the most critical safety device?

**Answer:**
The primary function of a safety valve is to automatically release excess steam pressure from the boiler when it exceeds a predetermined safe limit. It is considered the most critical safety device because its failure to operate can lead to a catastrophic boiler explosion due to over-pressurization. It acts as the final barrier against over-pressure conditions.

**Question 2:**
Differentiate between boiler mountings and boiler accessories, providing at least two examples of each.

**Answer:**
*   **Boiler Mountings:** These are essential safety and operational devices fitted directly onto the boiler shell or drum. They are crucial for monitoring and controlling basic boiler functions and safety.
    *   Examples: Gauge glass, pressure gauge, safety valve, blow-off valve.
*   **Boiler Accessories:** These are auxiliary devices that are connected to the boiler but not mounted on its shell. They are primarily used to improve the efficiency, performance, and operational ease of the boiler.
    *   Examples: Feed pump, economizer, air preheater, superheater, steam trap.

**Question 3:**
Explain the purpose of an economizer and an air preheater in a thermal power plant boiler. How do they contribute to overall efficiency?

**Answer:**
*   **Economizer:** An economizer preheats the feedwater entering the boiler by absorbing heat from the flue gases before they are discharged. This recovers waste heat that would otherwise be lost up the chimney, thus increasing the thermal efficiency of the boiler and the Rankine cycle.
*   **Air Preheater:** An air preheater preheats the combustion air using the heat from the flue gases. This leads to more efficient combustion, higher furnace temperatures, and a further recovery of waste heat from the flue gases, thereby improving the overall efficiency of the boiler.

Both economizers and air preheaters enhance boiler efficiency by recovering waste heat from the flue gases, reducing the fuel consumption required to produce a given amount of steam.

**Question 4:**
What is the role of a steam trap, and what can happen if steam traps fail to function correctly?

**Answer:**
The role of a steam trap is to automatically remove condensate and non-condensable gases from steam systems (like steam lines and heat exchangers) without allowing live steam to escape.

If steam traps fail to function correctly:
*   **Failure to open (Blocked):** Condensate will accumulate, leading to reduced heat transfer efficiency and potentially water hammer in steam lines when steam pockets form and collapse.
*   **Failure to close (Leaking):** Live steam will continuously blow through the trap, wasting energy and fuel, and reducing the overall system efficiency.

**Question 5:**
A boiler is operating at 10 bar gauge pressure. If the safety valve is set to open at 11 bar gauge, what is this setting considered in terms of safety?

**Answer:**
This setting is considered safe. The safety valve is set to open at a pressure slightly above the normal operating pressure (10 bar gauge). This ensures that it only activates when there is a genuine over-pressurization issue, preventing unnecessary steam release during normal operation. The general rule is that safety valves should prevent the pressure from rising more than 5% above the maximum allowable working pressure.

## 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Boiler Mountings are primarily safety devices**, directly attached to the boiler shell.
*   **Boiler Accessories are performance enhancement devices**, connected to the boiler.
*   The **gauge glass and try-cocks** are essential for monitoring water level.
*   **Safety valves** are the ultimate protection against over-pressurization.
*   **Blow-off valves** maintain water quality and prevent scale.
*   **Economizers and air preheaters** significantly improve boiler efficiency by recovering waste heat.
*   **Superheating steam** increases its enthalpy and prevents condensation in turbines.
*   **Steam traps** are critical for efficient steam utilization and preventing water hammer.
*   Understanding the function and maintenance of these components is vital for the safe and efficient operation of any steam power plant.
