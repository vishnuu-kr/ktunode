# Module 4: Energy Storage - Pollution and Noise Control in Power Plants

This module focuses on understanding the environmental impact of power plants, specifically pollution and noise generated by various power generation technologies, and how these are mitigated. While the primary focus of Module 4 is Energy Storage, pollution and noise control are crucial aspects of power plant engineering that influence the design, operation, and societal acceptance of any power generation method, including those coupled with energy storage.

## 1. Introduction to Environmental Concerns in Power Plants

Power plants, irrespective of their energy source, can have significant environmental impacts. These impacts are primarily categorized into air pollution, water pollution, land pollution, and noise pollution. Understanding and controlling these impacts is vital for sustainable power generation and meeting societal expectations.

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Pollution:** The introduction of harmful substances or energy into the environment that cause adverse changes.
*   **Noise Pollution:** Unwanted or disturbing sound that can affect the health and well-being of humans and other organisms.
*   **Sustainable Power Generation:** Generating electricity in a way that meets current needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

**References:**
*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017). *Power Plant Technology*. McGraw Hill Education.** - Provides a comprehensive overview of various power plant types and their operational aspects, including emission control.
*   **Nag, P. K. (2017). *Power Plant Engineering*. McGraw Hill Education.** - Covers the fundamental principles of power plant engineering, including environmental regulations and pollution control measures.

## 2. Air Pollution from Power Plants

Air pollution from thermal power plants (coal, oil, gas) is a major concern. The combustion of fossil fuels releases various pollutants into the atmosphere.

### 2.1 Major Air Pollutants and Their Sources

*   **Sulfur Dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$):**
    *   **Source:** Combustion of sulfur-containing fuels (especially coal and heavy fuel oil).
    *   **Effects:** Acid rain, respiratory problems, damage to vegetation and buildings.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017), Nag (2017).

*   **Nitrogen Oxides ($\text{NO}_x$):**
    *   **Source:** High-temperature combustion in furnaces and engines, primarily from the reaction of nitrogen and oxygen in the air.
    *   **Effects:** Smog formation, acid rain, respiratory irritant, contribution to ozone depletion.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017), Nag (2017).

*   **Particulate Matter (PM):**
    *   **Source:** Incomplete combustion of fuels, ash from coal combustion, soot.
    *   **Effects:** Respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, reduced visibility, soiling of surfaces.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017), Nag (2017).

*   **Carbon Monoxide ($\text{CO}$):**
    *   **Source:** Incomplete combustion of carbonaceous fuels.
    *   **Effects:** Toxic gas, reduces oxygen-carrying capacity of blood.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017).

*   **Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$):**
    *   **Source:** Complete combustion of carbonaceous fuels.
    *   **Effects:** Primary greenhouse gas, contributes to climate change and global warming.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017), Nag (2017).

*   **Other Pollutants:** Fly ash, mercury, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), etc.

### 2.2 Air Pollution Control Technologies

**a) Control of Sulfur Dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$)**

*   **Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD):**
    *   **Wet Scrubbing:** Most common method. Flue gas is contacted with a slurry of limestone or lime, which reacts with $\text{SO}_2$ to form calcium sulfite ($\text{CaSO}_3$) or calcium sulfate ($\text{CaSO}_4$, gypsum).
        *   **Process:** $\text{SO}_2 + \text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_3 + \text{CO}_2$
        *   **Process:** $\text{SO}_2 + \text{CaCO}_3 + 1/2 \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_4 + \text{CO}_2$
    *   **Dry Scrubbing:** Flue gas is contacted with a dry sorbent (e.g., lime) or a slurry that is sprayed into the gas stream and evaporates.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017) discusses various FGD systems. Nag (2017) also details these processes.

*   **Fuel Pre-treatment:**
    *   **Coal Washing:** Removes some of the pyritic sulfur before combustion.
    *   **Coal Gasification:** Converts coal into a synthesis gas, allowing sulfur removal before combustion.

**b) Control of Nitrogen Oxides ($\text{NO}_x$)**

*   **Combustion Modifications:**
    *   **Low-$\text{NO}_x$ Burners (LNB):** Design burners to create staged combustion, limiting oxygen availability and reducing peak flame temperatures.
    *   **Overfire Air (OFA):** Injecting air above the primary combustion zone to complete combustion with lower peak temperatures.
    *   **Flue Gas Recirculation (FGR):** Recirculating a portion of the flue gas back into the combustion air to lower flame temperature.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017) provides details on burner designs.

*   **Post-Combustion Treatments:**
    *   **Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR):** Injects ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) into the flue gas, which reacts with $\text{NO}_x$ over a catalyst to produce nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) and water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
        *   **Process:** $\text{NO}_x + \text{NH}_3 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{catalyst}} \text{N}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
    *   **Selective Non-Catalytic Reduction (SNCR):** Similar to SCR but operates at higher temperatures and without a catalyst. Ammonia or urea is injected directly into the furnace.
    *   **References:** Nag (2017) thoroughly explains SCR and SNCR.

**c) Control of Particulate Matter (PM)**

*   **Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs):**
    *   **Principle:** Particles in the flue gas are electrically charged and then collected on oppositely charged plates.
    *   **Components:** Discharge electrodes, collecting plates, rapping mechanisms.
    *   **Efficiency:** Can achieve very high removal efficiencies (over 99%).
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017) provides detailed principles and design of ESPs.

*   **Fabric Filters (Baghouses):**
    *   **Principle:** Flue gas passes through a fabric medium (bags), which physically traps the particulate matter.
    *   **Operation:** Bags are periodically cleaned by shaking, pulsing air, or reversing the gas flow.
    *   **Efficiency:** Highly efficient, especially for finer particles.
    *   **References:** Nag (2017) explains the operation and types of fabric filters.

*   **Mechanical Collectors (Cyclones):**
    *   **Principle:** Uses centrifugal force to separate larger particles from the gas stream.
    *   **Efficiency:** Effective for larger particles but less efficient for smaller ones. Often used as a pre-collector.
    *   **References:** El Wakil (2017) covers mechanical collectors.

**d) Control of $\text{CO}_2$ (Carbon Capture and Storage - CCS)**

*   While $\text{CO}_2$ is a byproduct of all fossil fuel combustion, advanced technologies aim to capture it.
*   **Post-Combustion Capture:** Separating $\text{CO}_2$ from flue gas after combustion (e.g., using amine solvents).
*   **Pre-Combustion Capture:** Converting fuel into syngas and then separating $\text{CO}_2$ before combustion.
*   **Oxy-fuel Combustion:** Burning fuel in pure oxygen, producing a flue gas primarily of $\text{CO}_2$ and water, making $\text{CO}_2$ capture easier.
