# THERMAL ENGINEERING LAB-2: Module 11 - Analysis of Automobile Exhaust Gas and Flue Gas

## Topic: Analysis of Automobile Exhaust Gas and Flue Gas Using Exhaust Gas Analyzer

This module focuses on understanding the composition of exhaust gases from internal combustion (IC) engines and flue gases from combustion processes, and how to analyze them using an exhaust gas analyzer. This analysis is crucial for assessing engine performance, fuel efficiency, and environmental impact.

### 1. Introduction to Exhaust Gas and Flue Gas Analysis

**1.1 What are Exhaust Gases and Flue Gases?**

*   **Exhaust Gases:** The gaseous products of combustion expelled from an internal combustion engine (e.g., petrol, diesel engines). These gases contain unburnt fuel, partially burnt fuel products, and products of complete combustion, along with air.
*   **Flue Gases:** The gaseous products of combustion expelled from a combustion process, typically from boilers, furnaces, or industrial stacks. These gases are often from burning fossil fuels like coal, oil, or natural gas.

**1.2 Why Analyze Exhaust/Flue Gases?**

*   **Performance Evaluation:** The composition of exhaust gases directly indicates the efficiency of the combustion process in an IC engine or a furnace.
*   **Emission Control:** Identifying and quantifying pollutants like Carbon Monoxide (CO), Hydrocarbons (HC), Nitrogen Oxides (NOx), and Particulate Matter (PM) is essential for meeting environmental regulations.
*   **Fuel Efficiency:** Incorrect air-fuel ratios or incomplete combustion lead to wasted fuel, which can be identified through exhaust gas analysis.
*   **Troubleshooting:** Abnormal exhaust gas compositions can point to specific engine or combustion system problems (e.g., misfiring, improper fuel delivery, worn engine parts).
*   **Safety:** High concentrations of CO are highly toxic and can be dangerous.

### 2. Key Components of Exhaust/Flue Gases

The composition of exhaust and flue gases varies depending on the fuel type, combustion conditions (air-fuel ratio, temperature), and engine design. Common components include:

| Nitrogen        | $N_2$            | The primary component of air, largely inert during combustion but can react at high temperatures to form NOx.                                 | Inert, dilutes the combustion products.                                                                                                       |
| Oxygen          | $O_2$            | Unconsumed oxygen from the intake air. Its presence indicates excess air or incomplete combustion.                                         | Indicates excess air (lean operation) or incomplete combustion.                                                                               |
| Carbon Dioxide  | $CO_2$           | Product of complete combustion of carbon in the fuel.                                                                                        | Greenhouse gas, indicator of complete combustion.                                                                                             |
| Water Vapor     | $H_2O$           | Product of complete combustion of hydrogen in the fuel and air.                                                                              | Indicator of complete combustion. Its presence can affect the dew point and cause corrosion.                                                  |

Occurs when there is insufficient oxygen or low combustion temperatures.                          | Highly toxic, reduces fuel efficiency, indicates poor combustion. **(CO2 is the desired product of carbon combustion)**                          |
| Unburnt Hydrocarbons | $HC$             | Unburnt or partially burnt fuel molecules. Occurs due to incomplete combustion, fuel wall wetting, or misfires.                           | Toxic, contributes to smog formation, indicates poor combustion and fuel wastage.                                                               |

**Important Point to Remember:** The relative amounts of $CO_2$, $CO$, and $HC$ are key indicators of combustion efficiency. High $CO$ and $HC$ with low $CO_2$ signifies incomplete combustion and wasted fuel.

### 3. The Exhaust Gas Analyzer

**3.1 What is an Exhaust Gas Analyzer?**

An exhaust gas analyzer is an electronic instrument used to measure the concentration of specific gases in exhaust or flue gas samples. It typically draws a sample of the gas through a probe and analyzes it using various sensing technologies.

**3.2 Types of Gas Sensors:**

*   **Non-Dispersive Infrared (NDIR) Sensors:** The most common type for $CO$, $CO_2$, and $HC$. They work by passing infrared light through the gas sample. Different gases absorb specific wavelengths of IR light, and the analyzer measures the amount of light absorbed to determine the gas concentration.
*   **Electrochemical Sensors:** Used for gases like $O_2$ and $NO_x$. These sensors use a chemical reaction between the gas and an electrolyte to generate an electrical current proportional to the gas concentration.
*   **Flame Ionization Detectors (FID):** Commonly used for measuring $HC$. The gas sample is burned in a hydrogen-rich flame, and the resulting ions are collected to produce a current.
