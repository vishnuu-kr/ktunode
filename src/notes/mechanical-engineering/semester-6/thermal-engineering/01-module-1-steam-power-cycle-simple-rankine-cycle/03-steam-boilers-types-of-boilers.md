# Thermal Engineering: Module 1 - Steam Power Cycle: Simple Rankine Cycle

## Topic: Steam Boilers: Types of Boilers

---

### **Introduction to Steam Boilers**

Steam boilers are essential components in thermal power plants, where they are responsible for generating high-pressure steam from water. This steam then drives a turbine, which in turn powers a generator to produce electricity. The primary function of a boiler is to transfer heat from a fuel source (like coal, oil, gas, or nuclear fuel) to water, converting it into steam.

**Key Concept:** **Heat Transfer** is the fundamental principle behind boiler operation. Heat is transferred from the combustion gases (or other heat sources) to the water within the boiler tubes.

**Definition:** A **steam boiler** is a closed vessel in which water is heated and converted into steam under pressure.

**Relevance to Rankine Cycle (CO1 - K4):** The boiler is the first and most critical component in the Simple Rankine Cycle. It is responsible for adding heat to the working fluid (water) at constant pressure, transforming it from a liquid to a superheated vapor. The efficiency of the Rankine cycle is directly influenced by the boiler's ability to generate steam at the desired pressure and temperature.

---

### **Classification of Boilers**

Boilers can be classified based on various criteria. Understanding these classifications is crucial for selecting the appropriate boiler for a specific application.

**1. Based on Water/Steam Circulation:**

*   **Fire-Tube Boilers:** In these boilers, hot combustion gases pass through tubes surrounded by water.
    *   **Principle:** Heat is transferred from the hot gases inside the tubes to the water outside the tubes.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Generally used for low to medium pressure applications (up to 20 bar).
        *   Lower steam generation rates compared to water-tube boilers.
        *   Simpler construction and less expensive.
        *   More prone to explosion if water level drops because the tubes are directly exposed to heat and pressure.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Cochran Boiler:** A vertical, multitubular, externally fired boiler. (Rudra Moorthy, pg. 145)
        *   **Lancashire Boiler:** A horizontal, two-flue, externally fired boiler. (Rudra Moorthy, pg. 142)
        *   **Locomotive Boiler:** A horizontal, multitubular boiler designed for mobile applications, featuring a fire-tube design. (R.K. Rajput, pg. 185)
        *   **Cornish Boiler:** A horizontal, internally fired boiler with a single flue. (Rudra Moorthy, pg. 140)
    *   **Important Point:** The tubes carry the hot gases, and the shell contains the water.

*   **Water-Tube Boilers:** In these boilers, water circulates through tubes, and the hot combustion gases flow over the outside of these tubes.
    *   **Principle:** Heat is transferred from the hot gases outside the tubes to the water flowing inside.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Suitable for high pressure and high steam generation rates.
        *   Safer for higher pressures because if a tube ruptures, only a small amount of water is released, rather than a massive explosion.
        *   More complex construction and expensive.
        *   Higher efficiency due to better heat transfer.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Babcock and Wilcox Boiler:** A widely used natural circulation water-tube boiler. (Rudra Moorthy, pg. 148) It consists of a steam drum, water tubes, and a firebox. (R.K. Rajput, pg. 188)
        *   **La Mont Boiler:** A forced circulation water-tube boiler. (Rudra Moorthy, pg. 150)
        *   **Benson Boiler:** A supercritical pressure boiler with no steam drum, employing once-through circulation. (R.K. Rajput, pg. 190)
        *   **Yarrow Boiler:** A Yarrow type boiler, often a three-drum design, known for its high steam output. (Mahesh Rathore, pg. 55)
    *   **Important Point:** The tubes carry the water, and the shell/furnace contains the hot gases.

**2. Based on Firing:**

*   **Externally Fired Boilers:** The furnace is located outside the main boiler shell. Combustion gases then pass through the boiler tubes.
    *   **Examples:** Lancashire Boiler, Cornish Boiler, Cochran Boiler.

*   **Internally Fired Boilers:** The furnace is located inside the boiler shell or shell-like structure.
    *   **Examples:** Locomotive Boiler, Vertical Boilers, many Water-Tube Boilers.

**3. Based on Position:**

*   **Horizontal Boilers:** The axis of the boiler is horizontal.
    *   **Examples:** Lancashire Boiler, Cornish Boiler.

*   **Vertical Boilers:** The axis of the boiler is vertical.
    *   **Examples:** Cochran Boiler.

*   **Inclined Boilers:** The axis of the boiler is inclined.

**4. Based on Pressure:**

*   **Low Pressure Boilers:** Typically operate below 15 bar.
*   **Medium Pressure Boilers:** Operate between 15 and 60 bar.
*   **High Pressure Boilers:** Operate above 60 bar.
*   **Supercritical Boilers:** Operate above the critical pressure of water (221.2 bar), where there is no distinction between liquid and vapor phases. (Benson Boiler is an example).

**5. Based on Type of Draft:**

*   **Natural Draft:** The flow of flue gases is due to the difference in density between the hot flue gases and the surrounding atmospheric air. Achieved using a tall chimney.
*   **Artificial Draft:** Achieved using fans.
    *   **Forced Draft:** A fan (forced draft fan) blows air into the furnace.
    *   **Induced Draft:** A fan (induced draft fan) located after the boiler and before the chimney creates a suction that pulls flue gases through the system.
    *   **Balanced Draft:** Both forced and induced draft fans are used.

### **Detailed Examination of Key Boiler Types**

**a) Lancashire Boiler**

*   **Type:** Fire-tube, externally fired, horizontal.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Large cylindrical shell.
    *   Two large internal flues (furnace tubes) running horizontally through the shell.
    *   Fire grates located at the front, where fuel is burnt.
    *   Combustion gases pass through the flues, then divide and flow back through side flues under the shell, and finally return to the front via a back flue under the shell.
    *   Equipped with a steam dome for dry steam collection and a blow-off cock for removing sediment.
*   **Advantages:** Simple construction, relatively low cost, large heating surface.
*   **Disadvantages:** Low steam generation rate, low efficiency, unsuitable for high pressures, requires large floor space.
*   **Applications:** Used for low-pressure steam requirements in small industries, heating systems.
*   **Reference:** Rudra Moorthy, pg. 142; R.K. Rajput, pg. 183.

**b) Cochran Boiler**

*   **Type:** Fire-tube, vertical, multitubular, internally fired.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Vertical cylindrical shell with a hemispherical top.
    *   Internal furnace tube at the bottom.
    *   Firebox at the bottom.
    *   Multitubular combustion chamber at the back.
    *   Flue gases pass from the furnace tube to the combustion chamber, then through numerous fire tubes and finally exit through a chimney.
*   **Advantages:** Compact, requires less floor space, good steam generation for its size, relatively high efficiency for a fire-tube boiler.
*   **Disadvantages:** Not suitable for very high pressures, difficult to clean and maintain due to vertical orientation.
*   **Applications:** Used in smaller industrial applications, marine propulsion (historically), and process heating.
*   **Reference:** Rudra Moorthy, pg. 145; R.K. Rajput, pg. 186.

**c) Babcock and Wilcox Boiler**

*   **Type:** Water-tube, natural circulation, inclined tubes.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Large horizontal steam drum.
    *   Inclined water tubes connecting the steam drum to a lower water drum (mud drum).
    *   Furnace located below the inclined tubes.
    *   Boiler baffles used to direct the flow of hot gases across the tubes for efficient heat transfer.
    *   Often includes a superheater section to increase steam temperature.
*   **Working:** Water from the steam drum flows down through the downcomers to the mud drum. Water is heated in the inclined tubes, becomes less dense, and rises back to the steam drum, creating natural circulation. Hot gases from the furnace pass over these tubes.
*   **Advantages:** High steam generation rate, high pressure operation, efficient heat transfer, safety due to water-tube design.
*   **Disadvantages:** More complex construction and higher cost.
*   **Applications:** Widely used in thermal power stations, large industrial plants.
*   **Reference:** Rudra Moorthy, pg. 148; R.K. Rajput, pg. 188; Mahesh Rathore, pg. 53.

**d) Benson Boiler**

*   **Type:** Water-tube, forced circulation (once-through), supercritical pressure.
*   **Construction:**
    *   No steam drum or water drum.
    *   Water is pumped at high pressure through a series of tubes arranged around the furnace.
    *   The tubes form a continuous circuit from the feedwater inlet to the steam outlet.
    *   Combustion gases flow over these tubes.
*   **Working:** Water is pumped in, heated, and directly converted into superheated steam in a continuous flow. Pressure is maintained above the critical pressure.
*   **Advantages:** High thermal efficiency due to supercritical operation, smaller size for a given capacity, no risk of drum explosion.
*   **Disadvantages:** Very high pressure operation requires robust construction, complex control systems, higher capital cost.
*   **Applications:** Modern high-efficiency thermal power plants.
*   **Reference:** R.K. Rajput, pg. 190.

### **Key Components of a Boiler**

While the types of boilers vary, most modern boilers share several key components:

*   **Furnace/Firebox:** Where fuel combustion takes place.
*   **Heating Surface:** The area through which heat is transferred from the combustion gases to the water/steam. This includes tubes, fireboxes, and shells.
*   **Steam Drum:** A reservoir for steam and water, separating steam from water and allowing for natural circulation.
*   **Water Drum/Mud Drum:** A lower drum where water collects before being heated.
*   **Superheater:** A section of tubes where steam is further heated after leaving the steam drum, increasing its temperature and energy content. This is crucial for improving turbine efficiency (CO2 - K3).
*   **Economizer:** A heat exchanger installed in the flue gas path after the boiler, preheating the feedwater using waste heat from the flue gases. This improves overall boiler efficiency. (Rudra Moorthy, pg. 152).
*   **Air Preheater:** A heat exchanger that preheats the combustion air using waste heat from the flue gases, leading to more complete combustion and higher efficiency. (Rudra Moorthy, pg. 153).
*   **Safety Valves:** Essential safety devices to prevent over-pressurization.
*   **Blow-off Cock:** Used to remove sludge and dissolved solids from the boiler water.
*   **Water Level Indicator:** Shows the water level inside the boiler.

---

### **Boiler Performance Parameters**

*   **Equivalent Evaporation:** The amount of water evaporated per hour from and at 100°C.
*   **Boiler Efficiency:** The ratio of the heat absorbed by the water and steam to the heat supplied by the fuel.
    *   $\eta_{boiler} = \frac{\text{Heat absorbed by water}}{\text{Heat supplied by fuel}}$
    *   Heat absorbed by water = (Mass of steam generated/hour) × (Enthalpy of steam - Enthalpy of feedwater)
    *   Heat supplied by fuel = (Mass of fuel burnt/hour) × (Calorific value of fuel)
*   **Boiler Power:** The rate at which steam is generated, usually expressed in kg/hr or tonnes/hr.
*   **Rating:** Often expressed in boiler horse power (BHP), where 1 BHP = 33,475 Btu/hr (or approximately 9.81 kW) of heat absorption.

### **Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** Differentiate between fire-tube and water-tube boilers. List two advantages of water-tube boilers over fire-tube boilers.

**Answer:**

| Feature        | Fire-Tube Boiler                                   | Water-Tube Boiler                                      |
| :------------- | :------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Circulation**| Hot gases pass through tubes, water surrounds them | Water passes through tubes, hot gases surround them    |
| **Pressure**   | Low to medium pressure (up to 20 bar)              | High pressure (above 60 bar)                           |
| **Steam Rate** | Lower                                              | Higher                                                 |
| **Safety**     | Less safe at high pressures                        | Safer at high pressures                                |
| **Cost**       | Lower                                              | Higher                                                 |
| **Size**       | Larger for a given capacity                        | More compact for a given capacity                      |
| **Examples**   | Lancashire, Cochran, Locomotive                    | Babcock & Wilcox, Benson, Yarrow                       |

**Advantages of Water-Tube Boilers over Fire-Tube Boilers:**

1.  **Higher Pressure Operation:** Water-tube boilers can safely operate at much higher pressures due to their design, where the pressure is contained within smaller diameter tubes rather than a large shell.
2.  **Higher Steam Generation Rate:** They are designed for larger heating surfaces and better circulation, allowing for a significantly higher rate of steam production, making them suitable for large power plants.
3.  **Increased Safety:** Rupture of a single tube in a water-tube boiler releases a small amount of water, whereas a rupture in a fire-tube boiler can be catastrophic due to the large volume of water under pressure.

**Question 2:** A boiler generates 5000 kg of steam per hour at a pressure of 10 bar (saturated). The feedwater is supplied at 80°C. Calculate the heat absorbed by the water and the boiler efficiency if the boiler consumes 1000 kg of fuel per hour with a calorific value of 30,000 kJ/kg. Assume the enthalpy of saturated steam at 10 bar is 2776 kJ/kg and the specific heat of water is approximately 4.18 kJ/kg°C.

**Answer:**

**Given:**
*   Mass of steam generated, $m_s = 5000$ kg/hr
*   Pressure of steam = 10 bar
*   Enthalpy of saturated steam at 10 bar, $h_g = 2776$ kJ/kg
*   Feedwater temperature, $t_{fw} = 80$ °C
*   Specific enthalpy of feedwater, $h_f = 4.18 \times 80$ kJ/kg = 334.4 kJ/kg (Assuming specific heat of water is constant at 4.18 kJ/kg°C and enthalpy at 0°C is 0).
*   Mass of fuel consumed, $m_f = 1000$ kg/hr
*   Calorific value of fuel, $CV = 30,000$ kJ/kg

**Calculations:**

1.  **Heat absorbed by water:**
    *   Heat absorbed = $m_s \times (h_g - h_f)$
    *   Heat absorbed = $5000$ kg/hr $\times (2776 - 334.4)$ kJ/kg
    *   Heat absorbed = $5000 \times 2441.6$ kJ/hr
    *   **Heat absorbed = 12,208,000 kJ/hr**

2.  **Heat supplied by fuel:**
    *   Heat supplied = $m_f \times CV$
    *   Heat supplied = $1000$ kg/hr $\times 30,000$ kJ/kg
    *   **Heat supplied = 30,000,000 kJ/hr**

3.  **Boiler Efficiency:**
    *   $\eta_{boiler} = \frac{\text{Heat absorbed by water}}{\text{Heat supplied by fuel}}$
    *   $\eta_{boiler} = \frac{12,208,000 \text{ kJ/hr}}{30,000,000 \text{ kJ/hr}}$
    *   $\eta_{boiler} = 0.40693$
    *   **$\eta_{boiler} = 40.69\%$**

**Question 3:** Which type of boiler is typically used in thermal power plants for high-pressure steam generation? Explain briefly why.

**Answer:** **Water-tube boilers** are typically used in thermal power plants for high-pressure steam generation. This is because their design, where water flows through tubes exposed to furnace heat, is inherently safer and more efficient at high pressures compared to fire-tube boilers. The smaller diameter of the tubes can withstand higher pressures with less stress on the material, and the effective circulation allows for rapid steam production and better heat transfer. Examples include Babcock & Wilcox and Benson boilers. (CO1 - K4, CO2 - K3)

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   The boiler is the heart of the steam power cycle, responsible for generating steam.
*   Fire-tube boilers are simpler and used for lower pressures, while water-tube boilers are used for high pressures and higher steam capacities.
*   Safety is paramount; understanding the risks associated with different boiler types is crucial.
*   Components like superheaters and economizers enhance the efficiency of the boiler and the overall power cycle.
*   Boiler efficiency calculation involves comparing the heat absorbed by the working fluid to the heat supplied by the fuel.
*   The choice of boiler depends on factors such as required pressure, steam capacity, fuel used, and cost.
