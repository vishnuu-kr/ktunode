### 1. Introduction to Boilers in the Rankine Cycle

The boiler is the heart of the steam power plant, responsible for converting water into high-pressure, high-temperature steam. This steam then drives the turbine, making the boiler a critical component in the simple Rankine cycle. Its primary function is to supply the necessary heat energy to the working fluid (water).

**Key Concept:** The boiler is a heat exchanger where thermal energy from a fuel source (like coal, oil, natural gas, or nuclear fission) is transferred to water, causing it to vaporize and superheat.

**Reference (Rudra Moorthy):** Chapter 1, "Introduction to Steam Boilers," discusses the fundamental role of boilers in steam generation and their importance in power plants.

### 2. Boiler Mountings

Boiler mountings are essential fittings mounted directly on the boiler shell or drum to ensure safe and efficient operation. They are primarily safety devices and operational indicators.

#### 2.1. Water Level Indicators

**Purpose:** To continuously indicate the water level inside the boiler drum. Maintaining the correct water level is crucial for preventing overheating of the boiler tubes and ensuring efficient steam generation.

**Types:**

*   **Gauge Glass (Water Gauge):**
    *   **Description:** A transparent glass tube connected to the boiler drum at both the top and bottom. The water level in the glass tube directly reflects the water level in the drum due to the principle of communicating vessels.
    *   **Working:** The bottom connection allows water into the glass, and the top connection allows steam. The difference in density between water and steam in the glass tube creates a visible level.
    *   **Safety:** Usually equipped with safety glass (like Pyrex) and shut-off valves to isolate it from the boiler in case of breakage.
    *   **Rudra Moorthy:** Section 2.3, "Water Level Indicators," details the construction and operation of gauge glasses.
    *   **R.K Rajput:** Chapter 5, "Boiler Mountings and Accessories," provides diagrams and explanations of gauge glasses.
    *   **Example:** If the water level in the gauge glass is too low, it indicates a risk of overheating the boiler tubes. If it's too high, it can lead to water carryover into the steam, reducing turbine efficiency.

*   **Three-Way Blow-off Cock:**
    *   **Purpose:** Used in conjunction with the gauge glass to verify the accuracy of the water level indication.
    *   **Working:** It connects the gauge glass to both the drum and the blow-off pipe. By opening the blow-off cock, steam or water can be discharged. If steam comes out first, followed by water, it confirms the water level is correct. If only steam comes out, the water level is too low. If only water comes out, the level is too high.

#### 2.2. Pressure Gauges

**Purpose:** To indicate the steam pressure inside the boiler. This is vital for monitoring the operational pressure and ensuring it does not exceed the design limits.

**Types:**

*   **Bourdon Tube Pressure Gauge:**
    *   **Description:** The most common type. It consists of a curved, flattened tube (Bourdon tube) sealed at one end. As pressure increases inside the tube, it tends to straighten. This movement is amplified by a mechanism and transmitted to a pointer that moves across a calibrated scale.
    *   **Rudra Moorthy:** Section 2.1, "Pressure Gauges," explains the principle of operation of Bourdon gauges.
    *   **R.K Rajput:** Chapter 5, "Boiler Mountings and Accessories," provides a detailed explanation and diagram.
    *   **Example:** A sudden drop in pressure might indicate a leak or a valve failure.

#### 2.3. Safety Valves

**Purpose:** The most crucial safety device. They are designed to automatically release excess steam pressure from the boiler when it reaches a predetermined safe limit, preventing catastrophic failure (explosion).

**Types:**

*   **Deadweight Safety Valve:**
    *   **Description:** A weight is placed on a valve seat. When the boiler pressure exceeds the pressure exerted by the weight, the valve lifts, releasing steam.
    *   **Limitations:** Weights can shift, and it's not suitable for high pressures or varying boiler positions.

*   **Spring-Loaded Safety Valve:**
    *   **Description:** A spring is compressed by a lever mechanism to hold the valve closed. When the boiler pressure acts on the valve disc, it overcomes the spring force and lifts the valve, releasing steam.
    *   **Advantages:** More reliable, can be adjusted for different pressures, and suitable for higher pressures.
    *   **Rudra Moorthy:** Section 2.2, "Safety Valves," covers different types and their working principles.
    *   **R.K Rajput:** Chapter 5, "Boiler Mountings and Accessories," extensively discusses spring-loaded safety valves.
    *   **Important Point:** Most modern boilers use spring-loaded safety valves. Usually, two safety valves are fitted to a boiler.

#### 2.4. Steam Stop Valve (Main Stop Valve)

**Purpose:** To control the flow of steam from the boiler to the main steam pipe leading to the turbine. It's the primary valve for isolating the boiler from the steam system.

**Design:** Typically a globe valve or a gate valve, designed to withstand high pressures and temperatures. It is usually located at the end of the main steam pipe connected to the boiler drum.

#### 2.5. Blow-off Cock/Valve

**Purpose:** To periodically discharge a small quantity of water and sediment from the bottom of the boiler. This process is called "blowing down" and is essential for removing accumulated sludge, scale, and dissolved solids, which can reduce heat transfer efficiency and cause corrosion.

**Location:** Fitted at the lowest point of the boiler.

**R.K Rajput:** Chapter 5, "Boiler Mountings and Accessories," details the function and placement of blow-off cocks.

#### 2.6. Feed Check Valve (Feed Valve)

**Purpose:** To control the admission of feedwater into the boiler and to prevent the boiler water from flowing back into the feed pump when the pump is not running or its pressure drops.

**Location:** Fitted on the feed pipe between the feedwater pump and the boiler drum.

**Working:** It's a non-return valve (check valve) that opens when the feedwater pressure is greater than the boiler pressure and closes when the boiler pressure is higher.

### 3. Boiler Accessories

Boiler accessories are external fittings that assist in the efficient and effective operation of the boiler, but they are not directly mounted on the boiler shell.

#### 3.1. Feedwater Pump

**Purpose:** To supply feedwater to the boiler at a pressure slightly higher than the boiler steam pressure.

**Types:**
*   **Reciprocating Pumps:** Positive displacement pumps, often used for smaller capacities.
*   **Centrifugal Pumps:** More common for larger capacities due to their continuous flow and higher delivery rates.
*   **Injectors:** Used in older or smaller boiler systems. They use the kinetic energy of a high-velocity steam jet to entrain and pump the feedwater into the boiler without the need for a mechanical pump.

**Rudra Moorthy:** Chapter 3, "Feedwater Appliances," discusses feedwater pumps and injectors.

#### 3.2. Economizer

**Purpose:** A heat exchanger placed in the path of flue gases (exhaust gases from the furnace) to preheat the feedwater before it enters the boiler drum. This recovers waste heat from the flue gases, thereby increasing the overall thermal efficiency of the boiler.

**Working:** Flue gases pass over tubes containing feedwater. Heat is transferred from the flue gases to the feedwater.

**Rudra Moorthy:** Section 2.4, "Economizers," provides a detailed explanation of their function and benefits.

**Example:** By preheating feedwater from 100°C to 150°C using flue gases, the amount of fuel required to convert water into steam is reduced.

**Important Point:** Preheating feedwater with economizers improves boiler efficiency by utilizing waste heat.

#### 3.3. Air Preheater

**Purpose:** A heat exchanger used to preheat the combustion air before it enters the furnace. This is done by recovering heat from the flue gases leaving the boiler.

**Working:** Flue gases pass over tubes containing combustion air. Heat is transferred from the flue gases to the air.

**Benefits:**
*   **Improved Combustion:** Hotter air leads to more efficient and complete combustion of the fuel.
*   **Increased Boiler Efficiency:** Recovering heat from flue gases reduces heat loss, thereby increasing overall efficiency.
*   **Higher Furnace Temperatures:** This can lead to better heat transfer rates within the furnace.

**Rudra Moorthy:** Section 2.5, "Air Preheaters," covers their operation and advantages.

#### 3.4. Superheater

**Purpose:** To heat the steam leaving the boiler drum (saturated steam) to a higher temperature at the same pressure. This results in superheated steam.

**Benefits of Superheated Steam:**
*   **Increased Enthalpy:** Superheated steam has a higher internal energy and enthalpy, leading to more work output from the turbine.
*   **Reduced Turbine Blade Erosion:** Prevents condensation of steam within the turbine, which can cause significant erosion of turbine blades.
*   **Improved Overall Cycle Efficiency:** Higher steam temperature increases the thermal efficiency of the Rankine cycle.

**Types:**
*   **Convection Superheaters:** Placed in the path of hot flue gases, absorbing heat primarily through convection.
*   **Radiation Superheaters:** Placed directly in the furnace, absorbing heat through radiation.
*   **Conduction Superheaters:** Less common, relying on direct contact with hot metal surfaces.

**Rudra Moorthy:** Section 2.6, "Superheaters," explains their design, function, and types.

**R.K Rajput:** Chapter 5, "Boiler Mountings and Accessories," provides insights into superheaters.

**Important Point:** Superheating is a key step in improving the efficiency of the Rankine cycle.

#### 3.5. Reheater (Not typically part of a simple Rankine cycle, but mentioned for completeness in advanced boilers)

**Purpose:** In more complex power plants (like reheat Rankine cycles), steam is partially expanded through the high-pressure turbine, then sent back to the boiler to be reheated before entering the low-pressure turbine.

**Benefits:** Further increases the overall efficiency of the power plant and reduces moisture content in the exhaust steam.

### 4. Function of Boiler Mountings and Accessories in the Rankine Cycle

*   **Boiler Mountings:** Ensure the safe and controlled operation of the boiler, which is the primary heat input device for the Rankine cycle. Incorrect water level or excessive pressure can lead to operational failure or safety hazards.
*   **Boiler Accessories:** Enhance the efficiency and performance of the steam generation process.
    *   **Economizer & Air Preheater:** Recover waste heat from flue gases, reducing fuel consumption and increasing overall plant efficiency (CO1).
    *   **Superheater:** Increases the enthalpy of steam, leading to more work output from the turbine and preventing condensation issues (CO1, CO2).
    *   **Feedwater Pump:** Supplies the working fluid (water) to the boiler at the required pressure to maintain the cycle.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
What is the primary purpose of a safety valve in a boiler?
(a) To control steam flow
(b) To indicate water level
(c) To release excess steam pressure
(d) To preheat feedwater

**Answer 1:**
(c) To release excess steam pressure. (This is a critical safety device as discussed in Section 2.3).

**Question 2:**
Which boiler accessory is used to recover heat from flue gases and preheat the feedwater before it enters the boiler drum?
(a) Air Preheater
(b) Superheater
(c) Economizer
(d) Injector

**Answer 2:**
(c) Economizer. (As explained in Section 3.2, economizers are designed for this purpose).

**Question 3:**
Why is superheating of steam beneficial in a Rankine cycle?
(a) It reduces the enthalpy of steam.
(b) It increases the chances of condensation in the turbine.
(c) It increases the overall thermal efficiency and reduces turbine blade erosion.
(d) It lowers the pressure inside the boiler.

**Answer 3:**
(c) It increases the overall thermal efficiency and reduces turbine blade erosion. (Superheating adds energy to the steam and keeps it in a gaseous state for longer during expansion in the turbine, as detailed in Section 3.4).

**Question 4:**
The gauge glass is a critical mounting for indicating:
(a) Steam pressure
(b) Water level
(c) Flue gas temperature
(d) Feedwater flow rate

**Answer 4:**
(b) Water level. (Section 2.1 covers the function of the gauge glass).

**Question 5:**
An air preheater improves boiler efficiency by:
(a) Increasing the pressure of combustion air.
(b) Recovering heat from flue gases to preheat combustion air.
(c) Directly heating the water in the boiler drum.
(d) Reducing the volume of flue gases.

**Answer 5:**
(b) Recovering heat from flue gases to preheat combustion air. (This is the core function of an air preheater as explained in Section 3.3).

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Safety First:** Boiler mountings are primarily safety devices. Overlooking their function can lead to severe accidents.
*   **Efficiency Gains:** Boiler accessories like economizers, air preheaters, and superheaters are crucial for maximizing the thermal efficiency of the Rankine cycle by recovering waste heat and increasing steam energy.
*   **Water Level Control:** Accurate monitoring and control of the water level in the boiler drum are paramount for safe and efficient operation.
*   **Pressure Management:** Safety valves and pressure gauges ensure the boiler operates within its safe pressure limits.
*   **Superheated Steam:** Is preferred over saturated steam for higher efficiency and better turbine performance.
