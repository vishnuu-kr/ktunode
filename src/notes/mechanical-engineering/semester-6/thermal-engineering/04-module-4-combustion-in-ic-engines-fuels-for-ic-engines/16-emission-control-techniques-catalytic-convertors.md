# Thermal Engineering: Module 4 - Combustion in IC Engines: Fuels for IC Engines

## Topic: Emission Control Techniques – Catalytic Converters

---

### **Introduction to Emission Control in IC Engines**

*   **Problem:** Internal Combustion (IC) engines, while efficient for power generation, are significant contributors to atmospheric pollution. Incomplete combustion and high-temperature reactions lead to the formation of harmful pollutants.
*   **Key Pollutants from IC Engines:**
    *   **Hydrocarbons (HC):** Unburnt fuel, contribute to smog and are carcinogenic.
    *   **Carbon Monoxide (CO):** A poisonous gas formed due to incomplete combustion.
    *   **Oxides of Nitrogen (NOx):** Primarily NO and NO2, formed at high combustion temperatures. Contribute to smog, acid rain, and respiratory problems.
    *   **Particulate Matter (PM):** Soot, especially from diesel engines, linked to respiratory and cardiovascular diseases.
    *   **Sulfur Dioxide (SO2):** From sulfur content in fuels, contributes to acid rain.
*   **Regulatory Standards:** Governments worldwide have imposed strict emission standards (e.g., Euro norms, EPA standards) to limit these pollutants, driving the need for effective emission control technologies.
*   **Course Outcome Alignment:** This topic directly supports **CO4: Explain the combustion phenomenon and pollution in IC engines** (Knowledge Level: K2) by detailing how pollution is mitigated.

### **Catalytic Converters: The Primary Emission Control Device**

Catalytic converters are essential exhaust emission control devices that use chemical catalysts to convert harmful pollutants in the exhaust gas into less harmful substances.

#### **1. Working Principle of Catalytic Converters**

*   **Catalysis:** Catalytic converters rely on the principle of catalysis, where a catalyst speeds up a chemical reaction without being consumed in the process.
*   **Three-Way Catalytic Converter (TWC):** The most common type used in modern gasoline engines. It simultaneously converts three major pollutants:
    1.  **Oxidation of Carbon Monoxide (CO) to Carbon Dioxide (CO2):**
        $2CO + O_2 \rightarrow 2CO_2$
    2.  **Oxidation of Unburnt Hydrocarbons (HC) to Carbon Dioxide (CO2) and Water (H2O):**
        $Hydrocarbons + O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2O$ (General representation)
        *Example: $C_8H_{18} + 12.5O_2 \rightarrow 8CO_2 + 9H_2O$*
    3.  **Reduction of Oxides of Nitrogen (NOx) to Nitrogen (N2):**
        $2NOx \rightarrow xO_2 + N_2$ (General representation)
        *Example: $2NO \rightarrow N_2 + O_2$*
        *Example: $2NO_2 \rightarrow N_2 + 2O_2$*

#### **2. Components of a Catalytic Converter**

*   **Housing:** Typically a stainless steel casing that encloses the internal components.
*   **Substrate (Monolith):**
    *   A ceramic honeycomb structure (e.g., cordierite) or a metallic foil structure.
    *   Provides a large surface area for the catalyst to be coated.
    *   Allows exhaust gases to flow through with minimal pressure drop.
    *   **Rudra Moorthy (2003):** Mentions the honeycomb structure's high cell density (e.g., 400-900 cells per square inch) to maximize surface area.
    *   **R.K Rajput (2010):** Highlights the importance of low thermal expansion coefficient for the ceramic substrate to withstand rapid temperature changes.
*   **Washcoat:**
    *   A porous layer of aluminum oxide (alumina, Al2O3) or a mixture of oxides (e.g., ceria-zirconia).
    *   Applied to the substrate, it further increases the effective surface area for catalyst dispersion.
    *   Ceria (CeO2) is crucial for oxygen storage, releasing oxygen during lean conditions (excess air) and absorbing it during rich conditions (excess fuel), thus helping the converter operate efficiently across varying air-fuel ratios.
*   **Catalyst:**
    *   Precious metals, typically Platinum (Pt), Palladium (Pd), and Rhodium (Rh).
    *   **Platinum (Pt):** Primarily an oxidation catalyst (CO, HC).
    *   **Palladium (Pd):** Also an oxidation catalyst (CO, HC), often more effective at lower temperatures than Pt.
    *   **Rhodium (Rh):** Primarily a reduction catalyst (NOx).
    *   **J.B. Heywood (2011):** Emphasizes the synergistic effect of these metals. Rhodium's unique ability to reduce NOx is vital for the three-way conversion.
    *   The catalysts are dispersed in very fine particles (nanoparticles) on the washcoat to maximize their surface area and catalytic activity.

#### **3. Operating Conditions and Requirements for Catalytic Converters**

*   **Operating Temperature (Light-off Temperature):** Catalytic converters require a specific minimum temperature to become active. This is known as the "light-off" temperature.
    *   Typically ranges from 200°C to 300°C for CO and HC oxidation, and slightly higher for NOx reduction.
    *   The engine must reach operating temperature for the converter to be effective. This is why cold starts are a significant source of emissions.
*   **Air-Fuel Ratio (AFR):**
    *   **Stoichiometric AFR (λ=1):** The ideal AFR for complete combustion.
    *   **Staggered Operation:** Three-way catalytic converters are most efficient when the engine operates very close to the stoichiometric AFR.
    *   **Lean Mixture (λ > 1):** Excess oxygen, good for HC and CO oxidation but limits NOx reduction.
    *   **Rich Mixture (λ < 1):** Excess fuel, good for NOx reduction but limits HC and CO oxidation.
    *   **Oxygen Sensor (Lambda Sensor):** Crucial for maintaining the AFR near stoichiometric. It monitors the oxygen content in the exhaust and provides feedback to the Engine Control Unit (ECU) to adjust fuel injection.
    *   **R.K Rajput (2010):** States that the efficiency of a TWC drops significantly when the AFR deviates from stoichiometric.
*   **Catalyst Poisoning:** Certain substances can deactivate the catalyst, reducing its effectiveness.
    *   **Lead (Pb):** Historically used in gasoline as an anti-knock agent. Lead deposits coat the catalyst surface, rendering it inactive. This led to the phasing out of leaded gasoline.
    *   **Sulfur (S):** Can form sulfates that block active sites. Using low-sulfur fuels is essential.
    *   **Phosphorus (P):** From engine oil additives, can poison the catalyst. Using API-certified low-ash oils is important.
    *   **Silicon (Si):** From coolant leaks into the combustion chamber.

#### **4. Types of Catalytic Converters**

*   **Two-Way Catalytic Converter (Oxidation Catalyst):**
    *   Older technology, primarily oxidizes CO and HC to CO2 and H2O.
    *   Does not reduce NOx.
    *   Used in diesel engines or older gasoline engines where NOx reduction was not a primary concern.
*   **Three-Way Catalytic Converter (TWC):**
    *   Combines oxidation and reduction functions.
    *   Most common in modern gasoline engines.
    *   Requires precise AFR control (stoichiometric).
*   **Diesel Oxidation Catalyst (DOC):**
    *   Used in diesel engines.
    *   Oxidizes CO, HC, and particulate matter (especially soluble organic fraction - SOF).
    *   Does not reduce NOx.
*   **Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR):**
    *   Used primarily in modern diesel engines to reduce NOx emissions.
    *   Requires a reductant (e.g., Urea, often supplied as Diesel Exhaust Fluid - DEF or AdBlue).
    *   Urea decomposes into ammonia (NH3), which then reacts with NOx in the presence of a catalyst to form N2 and H2O.
    *   $4NO + 4NH_3 + O_2 \rightarrow 4N_2 + 6H_2O$
    *   $6NO_2 + 8NH_3 \rightarrow 7N_2 + 12H_2O$
    *   **V Sajith and Shijo Thomas (2017):** Discuss SCR as a crucial technology for meeting stringent diesel emission standards.
*   **Lean NOx Trap (LNT) or Lean NOx Catalyst:**
    *   Another technology for reducing NOx in lean-burn engines (especially diesel).
    *   Stores NOx during lean operation.
    *   Periodically switches to a rich or stoichiometric condition to release the stored NOx and reduce it to N2 using the catalyst.

#### **5. Advantages and Disadvantages of Catalytic Converters**

*   **Advantages:**
    *   Significant reduction in harmful HC, CO, and NOx emissions.
    *   Passive operation (once heated) with no moving parts.
    *   Relatively compact and durable.
    *   Essential for meeting emission regulations.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **High Cost:** Due to the use of precious metals.
    *   **Temperature Dependent:** Ineffective during cold starts.
    *   **Catalyst Poisoning:** Susceptible to damage from lead, sulfur, and oil additives.
    *   **Back Pressure:** The honeycomb structure can create some back pressure in the exhaust system, slightly affecting engine performance.
    *   **Requires Precise AFR Control:** TWC needs sophisticated engine management systems.
    *   **Production of CO2:** While CO2 is a greenhouse gas, it is less immediately harmful than CO or HC.

#### **6. Maintenance and Lifespan**

*   **Lifespan:** Typically 80,000 to 160,000 km, but can vary significantly based on driving conditions, fuel quality, and engine maintenance.
*   **Maintenance:**
    *   Use unleaded gasoline.
    *   Use API-certified engine oils with low sulfur and phosphorus content.
    *   Ensure proper engine tuning and maintenance to avoid rich or lean mixtures.
    *   Address engine misfires promptly, as unburnt fuel entering the converter can overheat and damage it.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   Catalytic converters are vital for reducing air pollution from IC engines.
*   The Three-Way Catalytic Converter (TWC) is standard for gasoline engines, addressing CO, HC, and NOx.
*   Precious metals (Pt, Pd, Rh) are the active catalysts.
*   Rh is crucial for NOx reduction.
*   Catalytic converters require high temperatures (light-off) to function effectively.
*   Operation near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio (λ=1) is critical for TWC efficiency.
*   Oxygen sensors are essential for maintaining the correct AFR.
*   Lead, sulfur, and phosphorus are catalyst poisons.
*   SCR is increasingly important for diesel NOx control.

### **Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** What are the three main pollutants converted by a three-way catalytic converter (TWC)?
**Answer:** CO (Carbon Monoxide), HC (Hydrocarbons), and NOx (Oxides of Nitrogen).

**Question 2:** Which precious metal is primarily responsible for the reduction of NOx in a TWC?
**Answer:** Rhodium (Rh).

**Question 3:** What is meant by the "light-off" temperature of a catalytic converter?
**Answer:** The minimum temperature at which the catalyst becomes active and effectively converts pollutants.

**Question 4:** Why is operating the engine near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio (AFR) crucial for a TWC?
**Answer:** A TWC performs oxidation and reduction simultaneously. Operating at stoichiometric AFR provides the right balance of oxygen and fuel to maximize the conversion of all three pollutants (CO, HC, and NOx). Deviations (lean or rich) favor one reaction over the other, reducing overall efficiency.

**Question 5:** List at least three substances that can poison a catalytic converter.
**Answer:** Lead (Pb), Sulfur (S), and Phosphorus (P).

**Question 6:** Explain the role of a washcoat in a catalytic converter, mentioning a common material used.
**Answer:** The washcoat (e.g., alumina or ceria-zirconia) is applied to the substrate to further increase the surface area available for the dispersion of the precious metal catalysts. Ceria also acts as an oxygen storage material.

**Question 7:** Briefly describe the function of Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) in emission control.
**Answer:** SCR is used mainly in diesel engines to reduce NOx emissions by injecting a reductant (like urea/DEF) which forms ammonia. The ammonia then reacts with NOx over a catalyst to produce harmless nitrogen and water.
