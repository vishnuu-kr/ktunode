# Industrial Tribology: Module 3 - Principles of Lubrication: Hydrodynamic Lubrication

## Topic: Additives and their Functions & Criteria for Selecting Lubricants

---

### **1. Introduction to Lubrication and Additives**

Lubrication is the process of reducing friction and wear between moving surfaces by introducing a lubricant. While base oils form the bulk of a lubricant, additives are crucial chemical compounds incorporated to enhance or impart specific properties that the base oil alone cannot provide. This section will delve into the role of additives and the systematic approach to selecting appropriate lubricants for industrial applications, particularly within the context of hydrodynamic lubrication.

**Key Concept:** Lubricants are complex formulations, not just simple base oils. Additives are the key to tailoring their performance to specific operating conditions.

---

### **2. Additives and Their Functions**

Additives are chemical substances added to base oils in small concentrations (typically 0.01% to 20% by weight) to improve the performance of the lubricant and protect the machinery. They are essential for achieving optimal lubrication under demanding conditions often encountered in industrial settings, including those relying on hydrodynamic lubrication.

**Learning Outcome:** Understand the purpose and variety of lubricant additives and their specific functions.
**Course Outcome Alignment:** CO1 (Explain fundamental principles of Tribology - understanding lubricant behavior).

#### **2.1. Common Types of Lubricant Additives and Their Functions:**

*   **Antioxidants (Oxidation Inhibitors):**
    *   **Function:** Prevent or slow down the degradation of the base oil due to oxidation. Oxidation leads to increased viscosity, sludge formation, and the generation of corrosive acidic by-products.
    *   **Mechanism:** They interfere with the free radical chain reactions that cause oxidation.
        *   **Primary Antioxidants (Radical Scavengers):** Amines and phenols react with peroxy radicals to form stable products. (Stachowiak & Batchelor, 2000)
        *   **Secondary Antioxidants (Peroxide Decomposers):** Sulfur and phosphorus compounds decompose hydroperoxides into non-radical products.
    *   **Example:** Engine oils, hydraulic fluids, turbine oils operating at elevated temperatures.

*   **Anti-wear (AW) Agents:**
    *   **Function:** Reduce wear under boundary or mixed lubrication regimes where the lubricant film is insufficient to fully separate surfaces. They form protective films on metal surfaces.
    *   **Mechanism:** React with metal surfaces under localized high temperatures and pressures to form a sacrificial film (e.g., metal sulfides, phosphates).
    *   **Example:** Zinc dialkyldithiophosphates (ZDDP) are common AW agents in engine oils. Extreme Pressure (EP) additives are a more potent form of AW agents for very high loads. (Bhushan, 2013)

*   **Extreme Pressure (EP) Additives:**
    *   **Function:** Prevent catastrophic seizure and welding of surfaces under very high loads and temperatures, where even AW agents may not be sufficient.
    *   **Mechanism:** Similar to AW agents but react more readily at higher temperatures and pressures to form robust, high-melting-point films (e.g., iron chlorides, sulfides).
    *   **Example:** Sulfurized fats, chlorinated paraffins, phosphorus compounds used in gear oils for heavy-duty applications. (Hutchings, 2017)

*   **Friction Modifiers (FMs):**
    *   **Function:** Reduce friction coefficients, particularly in boundary and mixed lubrication regimes. They can also be used to modify the stick-slip behavior.
    *   **Mechanism:** Adsorb onto the metal surfaces and reduce the shear strength of the interface. They can be polar (fatty acids, esters) or non-polar.
    *   **Example:** Used in engine oils for fuel economy improvement and in automatic transmission fluids to ensure smooth clutch engagement.

*   **Viscosity Index Improvers (VIIs):**
    *   **Function:** Reduce the rate of change of viscosity with temperature. They help maintain a suitable viscosity over a wider temperature range, crucial for starting in cold conditions and operating at high temperatures.
    *   **Mechanism:** Long-chain polymers that uncoil and provide viscosity at higher temperatures but are coiled at lower temperatures, thus minimizing viscosity increase.
    *   **Example:** Multi-grade engine oils (e.g., 10W-30), hydraulic fluids for outdoor equipment. (Williams, 2005)

*   **Pour Point Depressants (PPDs):**
    *   **Function:** Lower the pour point of the lubricant, which is the lowest temperature at which the oil will flow. This is critical for lubricants used in cold environments.
    *   **Mechanism:** Interfere with the formation of wax crystal networks in mineral oils at low temperatures.
    *   **Example:** Engine oils for cold climates, hydraulic fluids for hydraulic excavators operating in winter.

*   **Detergents:**
    *   **Function:** Keep surfaces clean by preventing the formation of deposits and neutralizing acidic combustion by-products.
    *   **Mechanism:** Polar compounds that attach to particles and acidic species, keeping them suspended in the oil or preventing them from adhering to metal surfaces.
    *   **Example:** Engine oils, particularly diesel engine oils.

*   **Dispersants:**
    *   **Function:** Keep insoluble contaminants (sludge, soot) finely dispersed in the oil, preventing agglomeration and settling.
    *   **Mechanism:** Similar to detergents, they attach to particles and prevent them from clumping together.
    *   **Example:** Engine oils, especially those handling soot from combustion.

*   **Anti-foam Agents:**
    *   **Function:** Prevent the formation of stable foam, which can reduce lubrication effectiveness and lead to cavitation.
    *   **Mechanism:** Reduce the surface tension of the liquid, causing small bubbles to coalesce into larger ones that break more easily.
    *   **Example:** Silicone-based compounds used in hydraulic systems and turbine oils.

*   **Corrosion Inhibitors (Rust Inhibitors):**
    *   **Function:** Protect metal surfaces from corrosion caused by water or acidic contaminants.
    *   **Mechanism:** Adsorb onto the metal surface to form a protective barrier.
    *   **Example:** Engine oils, hydraulic oils, and metalworking fluids.

*   **Emulsifiers/Demulsifiers:**
    *   **Emulsifiers:** Promote the formation of stable oil-in-water or water-in-oil emulsions.
    *   **Demulsifiers:** Promote the separation of oil and water.
    *   **Example:** Emulsifiers in metalworking fluids; demulsifiers in turbine oils to separate water ingress.

**Important Point to Remember:** The choice and concentration of additives are critical. Overdosing can sometimes be detrimental, leading to synergistic negative effects.

### **3. Criteria for Selecting Lubricants**

Selecting the correct lubricant is a complex process that involves matching the lubricant's properties to the specific demands of the machinery and its operating environment. This ensures optimal performance, longevity, and efficiency. The principles of hydrodynamic lubrication play a significant role in this selection.

**Learning Outcome:** Understand the key factors and considerations involved in selecting appropriate lubricants for industrial applications.
**Course Outcome Alignment:** CO4 (Select and Evaluate Lubricants and Surface Treatments).

#### **3.1. Key Factors for Lubricant Selection:**

*   **Operating Temperature:**
    *   **Consideration:** The lubricant must maintain an appropriate viscosity across the entire operating temperature range (from start-up to maximum operating temperature).
    *   **Hydrodynamic Relevance:** Viscosity is the most critical property for generating the hydrodynamic film. Insufficient viscosity leads to film breakdown, while excessive viscosity increases drag and power loss.
    *   **Additives:** VIIs and PPDs are crucial for lubricants operating over wide temperature variations.

*   **Load:**
    *   **Consideration:** The magnitude and nature of the load (steady, shock, impact) dictate the required film strength and load-carrying capacity.
    *   **Hydrodynamic Relevance:** Higher loads require higher lubricant viscosity or the use of EP/AW additives to prevent film rupture and surface contact.
    *   **Additives:** EP and AW additives are essential for high-load applications.

*   **Speed:**
    *   **Consideration:** The relative speed between the moving surfaces influences the rate of shearing and the lubricant's ability to generate a full hydrodynamic film.
    *   **Hydrodynamic Relevance:** Higher speeds generally promote thicker hydrodynamic films for a given viscosity. However, high speeds can also increase operating temperatures, necessitating careful viscosity selection.
    *   **Additives:** Friction modifiers can be beneficial at higher speeds to reduce drag.

*   **Operating Environment:**
    *   **Consideration:** Contaminants (dust, water, chemicals), presence of reactive gases, and potential for leakage into the environment influence the lubricant choice.
    *   **Hydrodynamic Relevance:** Contamination can disrupt the hydrodynamic film and lead to wear.
    *   **Additives:** Rust inhibitors and demulsifiers are important if water is present. Dispersants and detergents help manage contaminants.

*   **Materials of Construction:**
    *   **Consideration:** The compatibility of the lubricant with the materials of the machine components (metals, elastomers, polymers) is crucial.
    *   **Hydrodynamic Relevance:** Lubricant additives can sometimes react with seal materials or coatings, affecting their performance.
    *   **Additives:** Certain additives might be incompatible with specific seal materials.

*   **Type of Machinery/Application:**
    *   **Consideration:** Different machines have unique lubrication requirements.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Gears:** Require high EP/AW additives due to high contact pressures.
        *   **Engines:** Require a complex blend of detergents, dispersants, AW/EP additives, and antioxidants to handle combustion by-products and temperature variations.
        *   **Hydraulic Systems:** Need good shear stability, thermal stability, and demulsibility.
        *   **Turbines:** Require excellent oxidation stability, demulsibility, and rust prevention for long service life.

*   **Lubricant Specifications and Standards:**
    *   **Consideration:** Adherence to industry standards (e.g., ISO VG, AGMA, SAE) and OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) recommendations is vital.
    *   **Hydrodynamic Relevance:** These standards often specify viscosity grades and performance characteristics necessary for proper hydrodynamic film formation.

*   **Desired Lubricant Life and Maintenance Intervals:**
    *   **Consideration:** The choice of lubricant and its additive package can influence how long it remains effective and the frequency of oil changes.
    *   **Additives:** High-quality antioxidants and base oils can extend lubricant life.

*   **Cost and Availability:**
    *   **Consideration:** While performance is paramount, economic factors play a role.
    *   **Balance:** The most expensive lubricant is not always the best; a cost-effective solution that meets all performance requirements is ideal.

**Example:** For a heavily loaded gear reducer operating at elevated temperatures, a mineral oil with a high viscosity grade (e.g., ISO VG 220 or 320) fortified with EP additives would be selected. In contrast, a hydraulic system in a cold environment might require a low-viscosity oil (e.g., ISO VG 32) with VI improvers and pour point depressants.

**Reference:** Lansdown (2003) provides detailed guidance on lubricant selection for various applications, emphasizing the interplay of operating conditions and additive chemistry.

### **4. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
A machine component operating under moderate load and temperature requires a lubricant that can maintain its viscosity over a wide temperature range. Which type of additive would be most crucial for this application?
a) Antioxidant
b) Pour Point Depressant
c) Viscosity Index Improver
d) Anti-foam Agent

**Answer 1:**
c) Viscosity Index Improver. This additive is specifically designed to reduce the rate of viscosity change with temperature, ensuring a more stable viscosity across a wide operating range.

**Question 2:**
Explain the primary role of Extreme Pressure (EP) additives in lubrication. Under what conditions are they typically required?

**Answer 2:**
EP additives are designed to prevent catastrophic wear and seizure of metal surfaces under very high loads and temperatures. They react with metal surfaces to form sacrificial, high-melting-point films (e.g., sulfides, chlorides) that prevent direct metal-to-metal contact and welding. They are typically required in applications like heavily loaded gearboxes, metalworking operations, and high-pressure bearings.

**Question 3:**
List any three key criteria that must be considered when selecting a lubricant for an industrial application. For each criterion, briefly explain its importance.

**Answer 3:**
1.  **Operating Temperature:** Crucial for viscosity. The lubricant must remain fluid at low temperatures (for start-up) and not become too thin at high temperatures (to maintain film strength), impacting hydrodynamic film formation.
2.  **Load:** Determines the required film strength. High loads necessitate lubricants with higher viscosity or EP/AW additives to prevent film breakdown and surface damage.
3.  **Materials of Construction:** Ensures compatibility. Certain additives or base oils might degrade seals or corrode specific metals, impacting machinery longevity.

**Question 4:**
What is the difference between an Anti-wear (AW) agent and an Extreme Pressure (EP) additive?

**Answer 4:**
Both AW and EP additives form protective films on metal surfaces. However, EP additives are more reactive and are designed to function under much higher temperatures and pressures than AW agents. EP additives typically form more robust, higher-melting-point films, making them suitable for preventing seizure under severe conditions where AW agents might fail.

**Question 5 (Scenario-based):**
You are tasked with recommending a lubricant for a hydraulic pump in a cement plant. The pump operates continuously at temperatures ranging from 30°C to 70°C and experiences moderate pressures. The environment is dusty and occasionally humid.
a) What are the primary properties you would look for in the base oil?
b) What types of additives would be essential for this application? Justify your choices.

**Answer 5:**
a) **Primary Properties of Base Oil:**
    *   **Appropriate Viscosity:** Likely a mid-range viscosity grade (e.g., ISO VG 32 or 46) for hydraulic systems, to ensure adequate film thickness for hydrodynamic lubrication under moderate pressures and temperatures.
    *   **Good Thermal and Oxidative Stability:** To withstand the operating temperatures and prevent degradation over time, especially in a potentially hot environment like a cement plant.
    *   **Good Demulsibility:** To allow any water ingress to separate from the oil.

b) **Essential Additives:**
    *   **Antioxidants:** To prevent oil degradation due to the operating temperatures and potential for oxidation, extending the lubricant's life.
    *   **Anti-wear (AW) Additives:** To protect pump components (gears, vanes, pistons) from wear under moderate pressures, supplementing the hydrodynamic film.
    *   **Rust Inhibitors:** Essential due to the dusty and humid environment, protecting metal surfaces from corrosion caused by moisture.
    *   **Dispersants/Detergents:** To help manage any dust contamination that might enter the system, keeping it suspended and preventing sludge formation.
    *   **Anti-foam Agents:** To prevent foam formation, which can compromise pump efficiency and lubrication.

### **5. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Additives are critical:** They transform base oils into high-performance lubricants tailored for specific conditions.
*   **Synergistic effects:** Additives often work together. The formulation is as important as the individual additives.
*   **Lubricant selection is a multi-factorial process:** Consider temperature, load, speed, environment, and materials.
*   **Hydrodynamic lubrication relies heavily on viscosity:** The choice of viscosity grade and VI improvers is paramount.
*   **OEM recommendations are usually the starting point:** Always consult the equipment manufacturer's specifications.
*   **Lubricant analysis:** Regularly analyzing used oil can help determine lubricant health and predict potential equipment issues.
