# Quality Engineering and Management: Module 3 - Tools and Techniques in TQM: Affinity Diagram

## Topic: Causes of Variation in Quality

---

### **Introduction**

In the realm of Total Quality Management (TQM), understanding and controlling variation is paramount. Variation, in any process, leads to inconsistencies and deviations from the desired quality standards. Identifying the root causes of this variation is the first crucial step towards process improvement. This module focuses on the Affinity Diagram, a tool that helps in organizing and categorizing a large number of ideas or data points, often used to explore the underlying causes of variation.

---

### **Learning Outcomes Covered in this Topic:**

*   **Identify the types of variation and their sources.**
*   **Explain the relationship between variation and quality.**
*   **Discuss the importance of identifying and controlling causes of variation.**

---

### **Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Quality:** The totality of features and characteristics of a product or service that bear on its ability to satisfy stated or implied needs. (Besterfield et al., Fifth Edition, 2018)
*   **Variation:** The degree to which data points or measurements differ from each other or from a central value. In quality management, variation is the enemy of consistency and predictability.
*   **Cause of Variation:** Any factor or event that contributes to the deviation of a process output from its target or expected value.
*   **Common Cause Variation (Chance Variation/Random Variation):** Variation inherent in a process, arising from the natural, predictable, and unavoidable randomness of the system. These are generally difficult to pinpoint to a single cause and are often reduced by improving the system itself. (Montgomery, Third Edition)
*   **Special Cause Variation (Assignable Cause Variation):** Variation arising from specific, identifiable, and often unusual events or factors that are not part of the normal process. These causes can usually be identified and eliminated. (Montgomery, Third Edition)
*   **Process Stability:** A state where a process exhibits only common cause variation, meaning its output is predictable within a certain range.
*   **Process Capability:** The ability of a process to consistently produce output that meets specified requirements.

---

### **The Relationship Between Variation and Quality**

*   **Quality is the absence of variation.** (Crosby, 1979) The less variation in a process, the higher the quality of the output.
*   **Variation leads to unpredictability.** If a process has high variation, its output will be inconsistent, making it difficult to meet customer expectations reliably.
*   **Variation increases costs.** Rework, scrap, warranty claims, and customer dissatisfaction are all consequences of excessive variation.
*   **Controlling variation is key to improving quality.** By identifying and reducing variation, organizations can achieve more consistent results, leading to higher customer satisfaction and reduced costs.

---

### **Types of Variation and Their Sources**

#### 1. Common Cause Variation (Chance Variation)

*   **Definition:** Inherent variability present in any process due to the collective effect of numerous small, random factors. These are often difficult or impossible to eliminate without fundamental changes to the process or system.
*   **Sources:**
    *   **Natural wear and tear of machinery:** Tools gradually dull, lubricants degrade.
    *   **Slight variations in raw materials:** Minor differences in the composition or properties of incoming materials.
    *   **Environmental fluctuations:** Temperature, humidity, vibration that are normal for the operating environment.
    *   **Subtle differences in worker performance:** Even skilled workers have natural variations in their execution of tasks.
    *   **Random interactions between process elements.**
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Present in all processes.
    *   Statistically predictable within a range.
    *   When only common causes are present, the process is considered "in statistical control."
*   **Example:** The slight variation in the length of screws produced by a well-maintained automatic screw machine. Even though the machine is calibrated, there will be small differences in each screw's length due to the natural tolerances of its components and the materials.

#### 2. Special Cause Variation (Assignable Cause Variation)

*   **Definition:** Variation that arises from specific, identifiable, and external factors that are not inherent to the process. These causes are usually sporadic and can be detected and removed to bring the process back into statistical control.
*   **Sources:**
    *   **Equipment malfunction:** A machine breaking down or not being properly maintained.
    *   **Operator error:** A mistake made by a worker due to lack of training, fatigue, or misunderstanding.
    *   **Defective raw materials:** A batch of components that is significantly different from specification.
    *   **Environmental anomalies:** A sudden surge in temperature or humidity that affects the process.
    *   **New or untrained personnel.**
    *   **Changes in process parameters:** Incorrect settings on machinery.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Sporadic and unpredictable in the long run if not addressed.
    *   When present, the process is considered "out of statistical control."
    *   Can usually be traced to a specific source and eliminated.
*   **Example:** A batch of screws produced by the same machine suddenly becomes significantly longer than usual because a worker incorrectly adjusted a setting without realizing it. This is an assignable cause that needs to be identified and corrected.

---

### **Importance of Identifying and Controlling Causes of Variation**

*   **Process Improvement:** Identifying special causes allows for their elimination, bringing the process into statistical control. Understanding common causes helps in making fundamental changes to the process itself to reduce overall variability. (Mitra, Second Edition, 1998)
*   **Predictability:** A process in statistical control is predictable, allowing for better forecasting of output and capacity planning.
*   **Cost Reduction:** By reducing variation, organizations can minimize defects, rework, scrap, and warranty costs.
*   **Customer Satisfaction:** Consistent product or service quality leads to greater customer satisfaction and loyalty.
*   **Decision Making:** Understanding the nature of variation provides a sound basis for making informed decisions about process changes and resource allocation.
*   **Foundation for Statistical Quality Control:** Tools like control charts (discussed in subsequent modules) are designed to differentiate between common and special cause variation. (Montgomery, Third Edition)

### **The Affinity Diagram and its Role in Identifying Causes of Variation**

While this module's primary focus is on the causes of variation, the Affinity Diagram is introduced as a tool to organize and analyze these causes.

*   **Purpose:** To group a large number of ideas, opinions, or data points into naturally related categories. This is particularly useful when exploring complex problems like the causes of quality variation where numerous potential factors might exist.
*   **How it helps:**
    1.  **Brainstorming:** A team can brainstorm potential causes of variation for a specific issue. This might generate dozens or hundreds of ideas.
    2.  **Organization:** The Affinity Diagram allows the team to sort these ideas without pre-defined categories. Ideas are written on sticky notes, and then moved around until natural groupings emerge.
    3.  **Insight:** The emerging clusters of ideas help in understanding the major categories of variation drivers, making it easier to focus improvement efforts.
*   **Relevance to this topic:** The Affinity Diagram can be used in the initial stages of problem-solving to gather and organize all potential causes of variation identified by a team. For example, when trying to understand why a manufacturing process has inconsistent product dimensions, a team might use an Affinity Diagram to group brainstormed causes into categories like "Machine Issues," "Material Defects," "Operator Practices," and "Environmental Factors."

### **Highlighting Important Points to Remember**

*   **Variation is inherent in all processes.** The goal is not to eliminate all variation, but to control it.
*   **Distinguish clearly between common and special cause variation.** This distinction is critical for choosing the right improvement strategy.
*   **Special causes are disruptive and must be eliminated.** They make processes unstable and unpredictable.
*   **Common causes are systemic and require process improvement.** They define the natural spread of a stable process.
*   **Understanding the sources of variation is the first step to controlling it.**
*   **The Affinity Diagram is a tool for *organizing* potential causes of variation, not for *analyzing* them statistically.** It helps in initial exploration and categorization.

### **Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Differentiate between common cause variation and special cause variation, providing an example for each in the context of a restaurant serving food.

**Answer:**
*   **Common Cause Variation:** Inherent, random variation that is part of the system.
    *   *Example (Restaurant):* Slight variations in cooking time for a steak due to minor fluctuations in stove temperature or the thickness of the steak itself, even when using the same recipe and equipment. These are expected and contribute to the natural range of cooking times.
*   **Special Cause Variation:** Variation due to specific, identifiable, and often external factors.
    *   *Example (Restaurant):* A particular chef, who is new and untrained, consistently overcooks the steaks because they are not familiar with the equipment or cooking times. This is an assignable cause that can be addressed through training or reassignment. Another example would be a power surge affecting the oven's temperature.

**Question 2:**
Why is it important for a manufacturing company to identify and control the causes of variation in its products?

**Answer:**
It is important for a manufacturing company to identify and control the causes of variation to:
1.  **Ensure Product Consistency:** Deliver products that meet customer specifications reliably.
2.  **Reduce Costs:** Minimize scrap, rework, warranty claims, and customer complaints.
3.  **Improve Predictability:** Make production output more predictable for better planning and scheduling.
4.  **Enhance Customer Satisfaction:** Build trust and loyalty by consistently meeting or exceeding expectations.
5.  **Achieve Process Stability:** Bring processes into statistical control, making them more manageable and improvable.

**Question 3:**
Consider a customer service call center. Brainstorm five potential causes of variation in the average call handling time and then suggest how an Affinity Diagram could be used to organize these.

**Answer:**
**Brainstormed Potential Causes of Variation in Call Handling Time:**

1.  Customer’s technical issue complexity
2.  Agent's product knowledge
3.  Availability of knowledge base articles
4.  System response time (CRM, ticketing system)
5.  Customer's communication clarity
6.  Agent's training level
7.  Call volume affecting agent focus
8.  Quality of headset/audio connection
9.  Interruption by other agents/supervisors
10. Complexity of troubleshooting steps required

**How an Affinity Diagram could be used:**

A team at the call center would write each of these (and any other brainstormed causes) onto separate sticky notes. Then, they would start grouping similar ideas together without predefined categories. Potential groupings might emerge such as:

*   **Agent Factors:** (Agent's product knowledge, Agent's training level, Interruption by other agents/supervisors)
*   **System/Tool Factors:** (Availability of knowledge base articles, System response time, Quality of headset/audio connection)
*   **Customer Factors:** (Customer’s technical issue complexity, Customer's communication clarity)
*   **Process Factors:** (Complexity of troubleshooting steps required, Call volume affecting agent focus)

This organization helps the team see the main areas contributing to variation, allowing them to prioritize where to focus their improvement efforts.
