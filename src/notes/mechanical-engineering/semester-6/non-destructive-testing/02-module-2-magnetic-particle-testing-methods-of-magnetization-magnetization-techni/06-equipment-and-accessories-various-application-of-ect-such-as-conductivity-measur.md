# Non-Destructive Testing: Module 2 - Magnetic Particle Testing & Eddy Current Testing Applications

## Introduction to Magnetic Particle Testing (MPT)

Magnetic Particle Testing (MPT) is a non-destructive testing (NDT) method used to detect surface and slightly subsurface discontinuities in ferromagnetic materials. It relies on the principle that a magnetic field will be disturbed by a discontinuity in the material, causing magnetic flux leakage. This leakage attracts magnetic particles, making the discontinuity visible.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Ferromagnetic Materials:** Materials that are strongly attracted to magnets and can be magnetized, such as iron, nickel, cobalt, and their alloys. MPT is only applicable to these materials.
*   **Magnetic Flux:** The flow of magnetic field lines through a material.
*   **Discontinuity:** An indication of a flaw or imperfection in the material, such as cracks, laps, seams, inclusions, or porosity.
*   **Magnetic Flux Leakage:** The phenomenon where magnetic field lines escape from the surface of a magnetized material due to the presence of a discontinuity.

## Methods of Magnetization and Magnetization Techniques in MPT

To perform MPT, the ferromagnetic material must be magnetized. The direction and strength of the magnetic field are crucial for detecting different types of discontinuities.

### 1. Methods of Magnetization:

The goal is to establish a magnetic field within the part being inspected.

*   **Producing a Magnetic Field:**
    *   **Using Permanent Magnets:** Simple for small, portable applications, but limited in strength and flexibility.
    *   **Using Electromagnets:** Provide stronger, controllable magnetic fields. This is the most common method in industrial applications.

### 2. Magnetization Techniques:

These are specific ways to apply the magnetic field to the test piece. The choice of technique depends on the shape of the part and the orientation of the expected discontinuities.

#### a) Head Shot Technique (Circular Magnetization):

*   **Principle:** Current is passed through the component itself, typically from one end to another. This induces a magnetic field that flows circularly around the current path.
*   **How it works:** The test piece is placed between two electrical contacts (prods or a head stock). A high current is passed through the component for a short duration.
*   **Discontinuities Detected:** Primarily detects **transverse discontinuities** (those running perpendicular to the direction of current flow).
*   **Example:** Inspecting a cylindrical shaft for cracks on its outer surface. The current flows along the length of the shaft, creating a circular magnetic field.
*   **Important Note:** Care must be taken to avoid arc strikes, which can damage the surface.

#### b) Cold Shot Technique (Central Conductor or Coil Technique):

*   **Principle:** Current is passed through a central conductor that passes through the hollow section of the test piece, or through a coil wrapped around the component. This induces a magnetic field that flows longitudinally within the component.
*   **How it works:**
    *   **Central Conductor:** A conductor is inserted through the bore of hollow parts (like pipes or rings). Current flows through the conductor, creating a circular magnetic field within the part.
    *   **Coil/Solonoid:** The component is placed inside a coil (solenoid). When current flows through the coil, it generates a longitudinal magnetic field.
*   **Discontinuities Detected:** Primarily detects **longitudinal discontinuities** (those running parallel to the direction of current flow/coil axis).
*   **Example:** Inspecting a pipe for longitudinal cracks. A central conductor is passed through the pipe, and current flows, creating a circular field that encircles the pipe.
*   **Important Note:** The central conductor method is effective for hollow parts, while the coil method is versatile for various shapes.

#### c) Central Conductor Testing:

*   This is a specific application of the "cold shot" technique, as described above, focusing on hollow cylindrical parts.
*   **Advantages:** Efficient for internal inspection of hollow parts where external magnetization might be less effective.

#### d) Magnetization Using Yokes (Articulated Yokes):

*   **Principle:** Yokes are U-shaped or handheld magnetic poles that are placed on the surface of the test piece. They create a localized magnetic field that passes through the area between the poles.
*   **How it works:** A powerful electromagnet (yoke) is brought into contact with the surface of the part. The poles of the yoke induce a magnetic field that penetrates the material between them.
*   **Discontinuities Detected:** Best suited for detecting discontinuities that are **perpendicular to the direction of the magnetic field lines**, which are generally found on the surface and subsurface.
*   **Types of Yokes:**
    *   **AC Yokes:** Produce a fluctuating magnetic field, effective for surface indications.
    *   **DC Yokes:** Produce a constant magnetic field, better for detecting slightly subsurface indications.
*   **Example:** Inspecting welds on a flat plate or the surface of a shaft where a specific area needs to be examined. The yoke is placed across the area of interest.
*   **Important Note:** The spacing of the yoke poles is critical. For optimal detection, the poles should be placed close together (typically 4-6 inches or 100-150 mm apart).

## Equipment and Accessories for Magnetic Particle Testing

Effective MPT requires specialized equipment and consumables.

### Equipment:

*   **Magnetic Particle Inspection Unit:** This is the main piece of equipment, typically incorporating a power source, controls for current and magnetic field strength, and electrical contacts.
    *   **Power Supply:** Provides the necessary current to create magnetic fields.
    *   **Current Controls:** Allow adjustment of the current flow for optimal magnetization.
    *   **Timing Controls:** For pulsed magnetization (head shot technique).
*   **Yokes (AC/DC):** Articulated or handheld electromagnets for localized magnetization.
*   **Coils:** For wrapping around components to create longitudinal fields.
*   **Central Conductors:** Rods or cables inserted into hollow parts.
*   **Test Prods:** For direct electrical contact with the workpiece.
*   **Magnetic Particle Application Devices:**
    *   **Spray Cans:** For dry powder or wet suspension application.
    *   **Powder Blower:** For dry powder application.
    *   **Tanks/Buckets:** For wet suspension.
*   **Demagnetization Equipment:** To remove residual magnetism after inspection.
*   **Light Source:** White light for dry particles, UV light (black light) for fluorescent particles.
*   **Measuring Instruments:**
    *   **Gaussmeter/Fluxmeter:** To measure the magnetic field strength and ensure it is within acceptable limits.

### Accessories:

*   **Magnetic Particles:**
    *   **Dry Powder:** Fine iron particles, usually black or red, used on rough surfaces or at higher temperatures.
    *   **Wet Particles:**
        *   **Visible (Black or Red):** Fine iron particles suspended in a carrier liquid (water or oil) for use with white light.
        *   **Fluorescent (Green or Yellow-Green):** Particles coated with fluorescent material, used with UV light for enhanced sensitivity, especially for fine cracks.
*   **Carrier Liquids:**
    *   **Water-based:** Often used with water-conditioning agents.
    *   **Oil-based:** Provide a smoother surface for particle adherence.
    *   **Light Oils:** Minimizing viscosity to allow free movement of particles.
*   **Cleaners/Degreasers:** To remove surface contaminants before inspection.
*   **Calibration Standards:** To verify the performance of the MPT equipment.

## Eddy Current Testing (ECT) - Applications

Eddy Current Testing (ECT) is an electromagnetic NDT method used for detecting surface and near-surface flaws in conductive materials. It is based on the principle of electromagnetic induction. When an alternating current flows through a coil placed near a conductive material, it induces eddy currents within the material. These eddy currents, in turn, generate their own magnetic field, which opposes the field from the coil. Any discontinuity in the material alters the path and magnitude of these eddy currents, causing a change in the impedance of the coil, which can be measured and interpreted.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Eddy Currents:** Circular electrical currents induced in a conductive material by a changing magnetic field.
*   **Conductivity:** The ability of a material to conduct electric current. Higher conductivity leads to stronger eddy currents.
*   **Permeability:** The ability of a material to support the formation of a magnetic field within itself.
*   **Frequency:** The rate at which the alternating current in the coil changes. Higher frequencies are more sensitive to surface defects, while lower frequencies penetrate deeper.
*   **Coil Impedance:** The opposition to current flow in the coil, which changes with the presence of eddy currents.

### Various Applications of Eddy Current Testing:

ECT is a versatile technique with a wide range of applications beyond simple defect detection.

#### 1. Conductivity Measurement:

*   **Principle:** The conductivity of a conductive material directly affects the magnitude of eddy currents induced in it. Higher conductivity leads to stronger eddy currents, which in turn affect the coil's impedance. By measuring the impedance changes, the material's conductivity can be accurately determined.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Material Sorting:** Distinguishing between different metal alloys based on their conductivity.
    *   **Heat Treatment Verification:** Assessing the effectiveness of heat treatment processes by measuring changes in conductivity.
    *   **Alloy Identification:** Identifying specific alloys in manufacturing and quality control.
*   **Example:** In the automotive industry, ECT can be used to verify that the correct alloy of aluminum is used for a specific component, ensuring proper performance and safety.

#### 2. Hardness Measurement:

*   **Principle:** Hardness in metals is often related to their microstructure, which in turn influences their electrical conductivity and magnetic permeability. Changes in these properties, caused by variations in hardness, can be detected by ECT.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Heat Treatment Monitoring:** Checking if a material has reached the desired hardness level after hardening and tempering.
    *   **Quality Control:** Ensuring consistent hardness across manufactured batches.
*   **Example:** After carburizing a steel component, ECT can be used to measure the surface hardness without physically damaging the part.

#### 3. Defect Detection:

This is the most common application of ECT.

*   **Principle:** Discontinuities like cracks, pits, voids, and surface irregularities disrupt the flow of eddy currents, causing measurable changes in the coil's signal.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Surface Crack Detection:** Highly effective for finding surface cracks in metals, even very fine ones.
    *   **Subsurface Defect Detection:** Can detect defects beneath the surface, though sensitivity decreases with depth.
    *   **Corrosion Detection:** Identifying localized corrosion or pitting.
    *   **Weld Inspection:** Detecting flaws in welds.
    *   **Heat Exchanger Tube Inspection:** A primary application for detecting cracks, pitting, and wall thinning in the tubes of heat exchangers.
*   **Example:** Inspecting aircraft components for fatigue cracks is a critical application where ECT plays a vital role in ensuring structural integrity and flight safety.

#### 4. Coating Thickness Measurement:

*   **Principle:** ECT can be used to measure the thickness of non-conductive coatings on a conductive substrate or vice versa. The electromagnetic field generated by the probe is influenced by the thickness of the coating.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Paint Thickness Measurement:** Measuring the thickness of paint layers on metal surfaces.
    *   **Plating Thickness:** Verifying the thickness of electroplated coatings (e.g., chromium, nickel).
    *   **Insulation Thickness:** Measuring the thickness of insulating layers on wires or cables.
*   **Example:** In the automotive finishing industry, ECT is routinely used to measure the thickness of paint applied to car bodies, ensuring uniform coverage and aesthetic quality.

## Practice Questions and Answers

**Instructions:** Choose the best answer for each question.

**Question 1:**
Which of the following materials is NOT suitable for Magnetic Particle Testing?
a) Iron
b) Aluminum
c) Cobalt
d) Steel

**Question 2:**
The Head Shot technique of magnetization is most effective for detecting:
a) Longitudinal discontinuities
b) Transverse discontinuities
c) Subsurface discontinuities only
d) All types of discontinuities equally

**Question 3:**
In Eddy Current Testing, what is the primary factor that determines the depth of penetration of eddy currents?
a) Material conductivity
b) Coil impedance
c) Test frequency
d) Probe diameter

**Question 4:**
Which ECT application relies on the principle that changes in microstructure affect electrical conductivity?
a) Coating thickness measurement
b) Defect detection
c) Conductivity measurement
d) Hardness measurement

**Question 5:**
Fluorescent magnetic particles are used in conjunction with:
a) White light
b) UV light (black light)
c) Infrared light
d) Laser light

---

**Answers:**

**Answer 1:**
b) Aluminum
**Explanation:** MPT is only applicable to ferromagnetic materials. Aluminum is a non-ferromagnetic (paramagnetic) material.

**Answer 2:**
b) Transverse discontinuities
**Explanation:** The circular magnetic field induced by the head shot technique is most sensitive to flaws running perpendicular to the current flow, which are transverse discontinuities.

**Answer 3:**
c) Test frequency
**Explanation:** The depth of penetration is inversely proportional to the square root of the frequency (skin effect). Higher frequencies penetrate less, while lower frequencies penetrate deeper.

**Answer 4:**
d) Hardness measurement
**Explanation:** Hardness in metals is often linked to microstructure, which influences properties like conductivity and permeability, making them detectable by ECT.

**Answer 5:**
b) UV light (black light)
**Explanation:** Fluorescent particles are designed to fluoresce (emit light) when exposed to UV light, making even very faint indications highly visible.

## Important Points to Remember

*   **MPT Applicability:** Magnetic Particle Testing is strictly limited to ferromagnetic materials.
*   **Magnetization is Key:** The effectiveness of MPT hinges on establishing the correct magnetic field orientation and strength to detect the discontinuities of interest.
*   **Two Main Field Orientations:** Longitudinal and circular magnetic fields are generated using different techniques to detect flaws oriented in specific directions.
*   **Visible vs. Fluorescent Particles:** Visible particles are used with white light, while fluorescent particles provide higher sensitivity under UV light.
*   **ECT Versatility:** Eddy Current Testing is a powerful electromagnetic NDT method with applications ranging from basic defect detection to sophisticated material characterization like conductivity and hardness measurement, and coating thickness.
*   **Frequency in ECT:** The choice of test frequency in ECT is critical for optimizing defect detection at specific depths and for various material properties.
