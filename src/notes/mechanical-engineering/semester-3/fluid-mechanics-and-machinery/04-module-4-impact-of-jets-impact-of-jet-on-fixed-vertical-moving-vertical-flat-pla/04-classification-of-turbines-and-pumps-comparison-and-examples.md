# Fluid Mechanics and Machinery: Module 4 - Impact of Jets & Turbo Machines

## Topic: Classification of Turbines and Pumps: Comparison and Examples

### 1. Introduction: Turbo Machines and Their Role

Turbo machines are rotary machines that transfer energy between a rotor and a fluid. They are essential for many industrial and energy applications.

*   **Turbines:** Extract energy from a moving fluid and convert it into mechanical energy (usually rotational). Examples include hydropower plants, wind turbines, and steam turbines.
*   **Pumps:** Impart energy to a fluid, increasing its pressure and/or velocity. Examples include water pumps, oil pumps, and fans.

**Key Concept:** The fundamental principle behind turbo machines is the **Euler's Turbine Equation**, which relates the work done by or on the fluid to the change in angular momentum and tangential velocities.

### 2. Classification of Turbines

Turbines are broadly classified based on several criteria:

#### 2.1. Based on the Nature of Fluid Used

*   **Water Turbines (Hydraulic Turbines):** Utilize the energy of moving water.
    *   **Reaction Turbines:** The fluid pressure changes as it passes through the runner. The runner is fully enclosed and submerged in water.
        *   **Francis Turbine:** Most common type, suitable for medium head and medium flow rates.
        *   **Propeller Turbine:** High flow rate, low head. Runner blades are fixed or adjustable.
        *   **Kaplan Turbine:** Similar to propeller but with adjustable runner blades, allowing for efficient operation over a wide range of heads and flow rates.
    *   **Impulse Turbines:** The fluid is directed onto the runner at atmospheric pressure, and the pressure remains constant throughout the runner.
        *   **Pelton Turbine:** High head, low flow rate. Jet of water strikes buckets on the periphery of the runner.

*   **Steam Turbines:** Utilize the energy of high-pressure steam.
    *   **Impulse Turbines:** Steam expands in nozzles, creating a high-velocity jet that strikes the blades.
    *   **Reaction Turbines:** Steam expands gradually through both stationary and moving blades.

*   **Gas Turbines:** Utilize the energy of hot combustion gases.
    *   Similar to steam turbines in principle, but operate with a gas as the working fluid.

*   **Wind Turbines:** Utilize the kinetic energy of wind.
    *   **Horizontal Axis Wind Turbines (HAWTs):** Rotor axis is horizontal.
    *   **Vertical Axis Wind Turbines (VAWTs):** Rotor axis is vertical.

#### 2.2. Based on the Head of Water (for Hydraulic Turbines)

*   **High Head Turbines:** Pelton Turbine (Head > 300m)
*   **Medium Head Turbines:** Francis Turbine (Head between 60m and 300m)
*   **Low Head Turbines:** Propeller & Kaplan Turbines (Head < 60m)

#### 2.3. Based on the Direction of Flow Through the Runner

*   **Tangential Flow Turbines:** Fluid strikes the runner tangentially (e.g., Pelton Turbine).
*   **Radial Flow Turbines:** Fluid flows radially inwards or outwards through the runner (e.g., Francis Turbine).
*   **Axial Flow Turbines:** Fluid flows parallel to the axis of rotation of the runner (e.g., Propeller & Kaplan Turbines).
*   **Mixed Flow Turbines:** Fluid flows radially inwards and then axially outwards (e.g., Francis Turbine).

#### 2.4. Based on the Action of Water on the Runner

*   **Impulse Turbines:** The entire pressure energy of water is converted into kinetic energy by a nozzle before it strikes the runner. The jet strikes the buckets at atmospheric pressure.
*   **Reaction Turbines:** The water is under pressure as it passes through the runner. The runner is completely filled with water and enclosed in a casing. Both kinetic and pressure energy of water contribute to the work done.

#### **Important Point to Remember:**
The classification helps in selecting the appropriate turbine for a given set of operating conditions (head, flow rate, power output).

### 3. Classification of Pumps

Pumps are classified based on their working principle and the type of energy they impart to the fluid.

#### 3.1. Based on the Working Principle

*   **Dynamic Pumps (Rotodynamic Pumps):** Impart energy to the fluid by increasing its velocity and then converting this velocity head into pressure head.
    *   **Centrifugal Pumps:** Most common type. Fluid enters axially at the center of a rotating impeller and is discharged radially outwards due to centrifugal force.
        *   **Radial Flow Pumps:** Fluid primarily moves radially.
        *   **Axial Flow Pumps:** Fluid moves primarily axially.
        *   **Mixed Flow Pumps:** Fluid moves both radially and axially.
    *   **Axial Flow Pumps:** Impeller blades are designed to impart a direct axial thrust to the fluid. Suitable for low head and high flow rates.
    *   **Regenerative Pumps:** Fluid passes through a series of chambers and vanes, gaining energy through a regenerative action.

*   **Positive Displacement Pumps:** Deliver a fixed quantity of fluid per revolution or cycle, regardless of the discharge pressure. They trap a fixed volume of fluid and force it into the discharge pipe.
    *   **Rotary Pumps:** Use rotating elements to displace fluid.
        *   **Gear Pumps:** Two meshing gears trap and move fluid.
        *   **Screw Pumps:** Rotating screws move fluid axially.
        *   **Lobe Pumps:** Similar to gear pumps but with lobes instead of gears.
        *   **Vane Pumps:** Rotating vanes slide in and out of slots in a rotor.
    *   **Reciprocating Pumps:** Use a piston or diaphragm that moves back and forth to displace fluid.
        *   **Piston Pumps:** A piston moves within a cylinder.
        *   **Plunger Pumps:** Similar to piston pumps but with a solid plunger.
        *   **Diaphragm Pumps:** A flexible diaphragm moves to displace fluid.

#### 3.2. Based on the Fluid Handled

*   **Water Pumps:** For domestic, industrial, and agricultural water supply.
*   **Oil Pumps:** For pumping crude oil, refined products.
*   **Chemical Pumps:** For corrosive or hazardous chemicals.
*   **Slurry Pumps:** For transporting abrasive mixtures of solids and liquids.
*   **Air Pumps (Fans/Blowers):** For moving large volumes of air at low pressures.

#### **Important Point to Remember:**
The choice of pump depends on the required flow rate, pressure head, fluid properties (viscosity, corrosiveness), and efficiency requirements.

### 4. Comparison of Turbines and Pumps

| Feature            | Turbines                                                                 | Pumps                                                                     |
| :----------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Energy Transfer**| Fluid energy $\to$ Mechanical energy                                     | Mechanical energy $\to$ Fluid energy                                      |
| **Purpose**        | Generate power                                                           | Move fluids, increase pressure                                            |
| **Fluid Action**   | Fluid expands and loses energy                                           | Fluid is compressed and gains energy                                      |
| **Pressure Change**| Pressure generally decreases as fluid passes through the runner            | Pressure generally increases as fluid passes through the impeller/rotor   |
| **Key Parameters** | Head, Flow Rate, Specific Speed, Power Output                            | Flow Rate, Head, Power Input, Specific Speed                              |
| **Working Principle** | Utilizes kinetic and/or pressure energy of fluid                        | Imparts kinetic and/or pressure energy to fluid                           |
| **Runner/Impeller**| Designed to extract energy from the fluid                                | Designed to impart energy to the fluid                                    |
| **Flow Direction** | Can be tangential, radial, axial, or mixed flow                          | Can be radial, axial, or mixed flow                                       |
| **Efficiency**     | Typically very high (up to 95%) for well-designed machines               | Varies, but can be very high (up to 90%) for some types                     |
| **Applications**   | Hydropower generation, wind energy, thermal power plants, industrial drives | Water supply, irrigation, chemical processing, oil transportation, HVAC |

### 5. Examples and Applications

#### 5.1. Turbine Examples

*   **Pelton Turbine:**
    *   **Application:** High head hydropower plants (e.g., located in mountainous regions where water can be stored at a high elevation).
    *   **Example:** Grand Coulee Dam (USA) has Pelton turbines for its power generation.
*   **Francis Turbine:**
    *   **Application:** Medium head hydropower plants, often used in large-scale hydroelectric projects.
    *   **Example:** Hoover Dam (USA) uses Francis turbines.
*   **Kaplan Turbine:**
    *   **Application:** Low head hydropower plants, often run-of-river projects where the head is relatively small but the flow rate is high (e.g., along large rivers).
    *   **Example:** Many European river-based hydropower stations utilize Kaplan turbines.
*   **Wind Turbines:**
    *   **Application:** Renewable energy generation worldwide.
    *   **Example:** Offshore wind farms (e.g., Hornsea Wind Farm in the UK) and onshore wind farms.

#### 5.2. Pump Examples

*   **Centrifugal Pump:**
    *   **Application:** Widely used in domestic water supply, irrigation, industrial processes, sewage systems, and circulating cooling water.
    *   **Example:** The pump you use to fill your water tank at home is likely a centrifugal pump. Large centrifugal pumps are used in municipal water treatment plants.
*   **Axial Flow Pump:**
    *   **Application:** Drainage of large areas, flood control, low head irrigation, and circulation in cooling towers where large volumes of water need to be moved against minimal head.
    *   **Example:** Pumping water from a river to a low-lying agricultural field.
*   **Reciprocating Pump (Piston/Plunger Pump):**
    *   **Application:** High pressure applications, such as hydraulic presses, oil well drilling, and metering pumps for precise chemical injection.
    *   **Example:** The pump in a car's braking system is a type of reciprocating pump.
*   **Gear Pump:**
    *   **Application:** Lubrication systems, fuel injection systems, hydraulic power units, and pumping viscous fluids.
    *   **Example:** Used in automotive engines to circulate lubricating oil.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Classify the following turbines based on the nature of fluid used and the direction of flow through the runner. Briefly explain why each classification is appropriate.
    a) Pelton Turbine
    b) Francis Turbine
    c) Kaplan Turbine

**Answer 1:**
    a) **Pelton Turbine:**
        *   **Nature of Fluid:** Water Turbine (Hydraulic Turbine) - uses the energy of moving water.
        *   **Direction of Flow:** Tangential Flow - the jet of water strikes the runner buckets tangentially.
        *   **Explanation:** Pelton turbines are designed for high head and low flow, where a high-velocity jet is most effective. The jet impinges on the curved buckets on the periphery, causing tangential motion.

    b) **Francis Turbine:**
        *   **Nature of Fluid:** Water Turbine (Hydraulic Turbine) - uses the energy of moving water.
        *   **Direction of Flow:** Mixed Flow - water enters radially and exits axially.
        *   **Explanation:** Francis turbines are suitable for medium heads and flow rates. The water enters the runner through guide vanes, flowing radially inwards, and then exits axially.

    c) **Kaplan Turbine:**
        *   **Nature of Fluid:** Water Turbine (Hydraulic Turbine) - uses the energy of moving water.
        *   **Direction of Flow:** Axial Flow - water flows parallel to the axis of the runner.
        *   **Explanation:** Kaplan turbines are used for low heads and high flow rates. The runner has propeller-like blades, and the water flows axially through them, imparting rotation.

**Question 2:** A pump is needed to lift 100 m³ of water per minute to a height of 20 meters. The fluid is clean water. Which type of pump would be most suitable for this application and why?

**Answer 2:**
    For lifting 100 m³/min (which is a relatively large flow rate) to a height of 20 meters (which is a moderate head), a **Centrifugal Pump** would be the most suitable choice.

    **Reasoning:**
    *   **Flow Rate:** Centrifugal pumps are very efficient at handling large flow rates.
    *   **Head:** They are also capable of generating moderate to high heads.
    *   **Fluid:** For clean water, centrifugal pumps are the standard and most economical choice.
    *   **Alternatives:**
        *   Axial flow pumps are better for very low heads and extremely high flow rates, which is not the case here.
        *   Positive displacement pumps (like reciprocating or gear pumps) are generally used for higher heads and lower flow rates, or for viscous fluids, and would be less efficient and more costly for this specific application.

**Question 3:** Differentiate between impulse and reaction turbines, highlighting the key differences in their operating principles and applications.

**Answer 3:**

| Feature            | Impulse Turbine (e.g., Pelton Turbine)                       | Reaction Turbine (e.g., Francis, Kaplan)                   |
| :----------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pressure of Water** | Water enters the runner at atmospheric pressure. No pressure drop across the runner. | Water is under pressure as it passes through the runner. Runner is fully enclosed. |
| **Energy Conversion**| Entire pressure energy of water is converted into kinetic energy in the nozzle before striking the runner. | Both pressure and kinetic energy of water are utilized to produce work. |
| **Runner Design**  | Buckets designed to efficiently deflect the high-velocity jet. | Runner designed with airfoil-like blades to interact with the flowing, pressurized fluid. |
| **Specific Speed** | Generally lower specific speed.                              | Generally higher specific speed.                             |
| **Head Range**     | High head applications (e.g., > 300m).                       | Medium to low head applications (e.g., < 300m).            |
| **Flow Rate**      | Low flow rates.                                              | Medium to high flow rates.                                 |
| **Applications**   | High head hydropower stations, mountainous regions.          | Medium head hydropower stations, run-of-river projects.    |

**Question 4:** A pump is designed to convey a viscous oil. Which class of pumps would be more appropriate and why?

**Answer 4:**
    For conveying viscous oil, **Positive Displacement Pumps** would be more appropriate than dynamic pumps (like centrifugal pumps).

    **Reasoning:**
    *   **Viscosity Effect:** The viscosity of the fluid creates significant frictional losses in dynamic pumps, drastically reducing their efficiency and head-generating capability.
    *   **Positive Displacement Action:** Positive displacement pumps trap a fixed volume of fluid and force it forward. This mechanism is less affected by viscosity and can effectively move viscous fluids.
    *   **Specific Types:** Within positive displacement pumps, **Gear Pumps** or **Screw Pumps** are particularly well-suited for viscous fluids as their internal clearances can be managed to maintain efficiency, and they provide a smooth, continuous flow.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Energy Transformation:** The core difference between turbines and pumps lies in the direction of energy transformation: fluid energy to mechanical energy (turbines) vs. mechanical energy to fluid energy (pumps).
*   **Head and Flow Rate:** These are critical parameters dictating the type of turbine or pump to be selected for a specific application.
*   **Euler's Turbine Equation:** This fundamental equation underpins the theoretical understanding of energy transfer in all turbo machines.
*   **Classification is a Tool:** Understanding the different classifications helps in making informed decisions for engineering applications.
*   **Efficiency:** While all turbo machines aim for high efficiency, the operating conditions greatly influence this. Matching the machine to the application is key.
