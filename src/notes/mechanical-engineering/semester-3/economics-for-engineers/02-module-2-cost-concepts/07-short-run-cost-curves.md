# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 2: Cost Concepts - Short-Run Cost Curves

## 1. Introduction to Short-Run Costs

**Definition:** In economics, the short-run is a period during which at least one factor of production is fixed. In a manufacturing context, this often means that the firm cannot change its plant size, machinery, or the amount of capital invested. However, it can change its output by altering variable inputs such as labor, raw materials, and energy.

**Key Concept:** Short-run costs are the costs incurred by a firm when at least one input is fixed. These costs are primarily driven by the relationship between output and variable inputs.

**(Referenced from: Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury, Chapter 5: Cost Concepts)**

## 2. Total Cost (TC) in the Short Run

**Definition:** Total Cost (TC) is the sum of all costs incurred in producing a certain level of output. In the short run, TC is divided into Total Fixed Costs (TFC) and Total Variable Costs (TVC).

**Formula:**
$TC = TFC + TVC$

### 2.1 Total Fixed Costs (TFC)

**Definition:** Total Fixed Costs (TFC) are costs that do not vary with the level of output in the short run. These costs are incurred even if the firm produces zero output.

**Examples:**
*   Rent of factory building
*   Salaries of permanent administrative staff
*   Depreciation of machinery
*   Interest on capital borrowed
*   Insurance premiums

**Characteristics:**
*   TFC remains constant regardless of output.
*   TFC is zero only when the firm shuts down completely.
*   Graphically, TFC is represented by a horizontal straight line.

**(Referenced from: Engineering Economics by R. Paneerselvam, Chapter 4: Cost Concepts)**

### 2.2 Total Variable Costs (TVC)

**Definition:** Total Variable Costs (TVC) are costs that change directly with the level of output in the short run. These costs are incurred only when production takes place.

**Examples:**
*   Wages of production workers (paid hourly or based on output)
*   Raw material costs
*   Fuel and power consumed in production
*   Packaging costs

**Characteristics:**
*   TVC is zero when output is zero.
*   TVC increases as output increases.
*   The shape of the TVC curve is typically an "S" shape, initially rising at a decreasing rate due to increasing returns to variable factors, and then rising at an increasing rate due to diminishing returns to variable factors.

**(Referenced from: Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury, Chapter 5: Cost Concepts)**

## 3. Short-Run Average Cost Curves

These curves show the cost per unit of output at different levels of production.

### 3.1 Average Fixed Cost (AFC)

**Definition:** Average Fixed Cost (AFC) is the total fixed cost divided by the quantity of output.

**Formula:**
$AFC = TFC / Q$ (where Q is the quantity of output)

**Characteristics:**
*   AFC always declines as output increases. This is because the fixed cost is spread over a larger number of units.
*   AFC approaches zero as output becomes very large, but never actually reaches zero.
*   Graphically, AFC is a rectangular hyperbola.

**(Referenced from: Engineering Economy by H. G. Thuesen, W. J. Fabrycky, Chapter 6: Cost Concepts)**

### 3.2 Average Variable Cost (AVC)

**Definition:** Average Variable Cost (AVC) is the total variable cost divided by the quantity of output.

**Formula:**
$AVC = TVC / Q$

**Characteristics:**
*   AVC initially declines as output increases due to increasing returns to the variable factor.
*   AVC reaches a minimum point and then begins to rise as diminishing returns set in.
*   The AVC curve is typically U-shaped. The minimum point of the AVC curve occurs at the point where marginal productivity of the variable factor is at its maximum.

**(Referenced from: Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury, Chapter 5: Cost Concepts)**

### 3.3 Average Total Cost (ATC)

**Definition:** Average Total Cost (ATC) is the total cost divided by the quantity of output. It represents the total cost per unit of output.

**Formula:**
$ATC = TC / Q$
$ATC = AFC + AVC$

**Characteristics:**
*   ATC is also U-shaped.
*   The ATC curve lies above the AVC curve. The vertical distance between ATC and AVC is the AFC. As AFC decreases, the gap between ATC and AVC narrows.
*   The ATC curve is at its minimum at a higher output level than the AVC curve.

**(Referenced from: Engineering Economics by R. Paneerselvam, Chapter 4: Cost Concepts)**

## 4. Short-Run Marginal Cost Curve (MC)

**Definition:** Marginal Cost (MC) is the change in total cost resulting from a one-unit increase in output. It represents the cost of producing one additional unit of output.

**Formula:**
$MC = \Delta TC / \Delta Q$
or, if $\Delta Q = 1$, $MC = \Delta TC$

**Characteristics:**
*   MC is derived from the TVC curve. It is the rate of change of TVC.
*   MC is also U-shaped. It initially falls due to increasing returns and then rises due to diminishing returns.
*   The MC curve intersects the AVC and ATC curves at their minimum points.
*   When MC is below AVC or ATC, AVC and ATC are falling.
*   When MC is above AVC or ATC, AVC and ATC are rising.
*   When MC equals AVC or ATC, AVC and ATC are at their minimum points.

**(Referenced from: Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury, Chapter 5: Cost Concepts and Contemporary Engineering Economics by Chan S. Park, Chapter 7: Cost and Economic Analysis)**

## 5. Relationship Between Cost Curves

The relationships between the various short-run cost curves are crucial for understanding firm behavior:

*   **MC and TVC:** MC is the slope of the TVC curve.
*   **MC and AVC:** MC intersects AVC at its minimum point. Before the minimum, MC < AVC. After the minimum, MC > AVC.
*   **MC and ATC:** MC intersects ATC at its minimum point. Before the minimum, MC < ATC. After the minimum, MC > ATC.
*   **AFC, AVC, and ATC:** ATC is the sum of AFC and AVC ($ATC = AFC + AVC$). The vertical distance between ATC and AVC is AFC, which decreases as output increases.

## 6. Graphical Representation of Short-Run Cost Curves

It is essential to visualize these relationships. Imagine a graph with Quantity (Q) on the x-axis and Cost ($) on the y-axis.

*   **TFC:** Horizontal line at a positive value.
*   **TVC:** Starts at zero, initially flattens, then rises steeply (S-shaped).
*   **TC:** Starts at the TFC level (at Q=0) and follows the shape of TVC but is always higher by the TFC amount.
*   **AFC:** Declining curve, approaching the x-axis (rectangular hyperbola).
*   **AVC:** U-shaped curve.
*   **MC:** U-shaped curve, steeper than AVC, intersecting AVC and ATC at their minimums.
*   **ATC:** U-shaped curve, lies above AVC, and its minimum is at a higher output than AVC's minimum.

**(Referenced from: Engineering Economy by H. G. Thuesen, W. J. Fabrycky, Chapter 6: Cost Concepts and Contemporary Engineering Economics by Chan S. Park, Chapter 7: Cost and Economic Analysis)**

## 7. Short-Run Production Function and Cost Curves

The shape of the short-run cost curves is a direct consequence of the short-run production function, which describes the relationship between inputs (especially the variable input) and output.

*   **Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns:** As more units of a variable input are added to a fixed input, the marginal product of the variable input will eventually decrease.
    *   **Impact on TVC:** Initially, as marginal product increases, TVC rises at a decreasing rate. As marginal product decreases, TVC rises at an increasing rate.
    *   **Impact on MC:** Consequently, MC initially falls (when MP is rising) and then rises (when MP is falling).
    *   **Impact on AVC:** Similarly, AVC falls when the marginal product of the variable input is rising and rises when the marginal product is falling.

**(Referenced from: Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury, Chapter 5: Cost Concepts)**

## 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Short-run:** At least one factor is fixed.
*   **Fixed Costs:** Do not change with output (e.g., rent).
*   **Variable Costs:** Change directly with output (e.g., raw materials).
*   **AFC:** Always falls.
*   **AVC & ATC:** U-shaped.
*   **MC:** U-shaped and intersects AVC and ATC at their minimums.
*   **The Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns is the primary driver of the shapes of the short-run cost curves.**
*   Understanding these curves is crucial for making optimal production decisions in the short run.

## 9. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Given the following data for a firm in the short run:

| Output (Q) | TFC ($) | TVC ($) |
| :--------- | :------ | :------ |
| 0          | 100     | 0       |
| 1          | 100     | 50      |
| 2          | 100     | 90      |
| 3          | 100     | 120     |
| 4          | 100     | 160     |
| 5          | 100     | 210     |

Calculate and fill in the following: TC, AFC, AVC, ATC, and MC.

**Solution 1:**

| Output (Q) | TFC ($) | TVC ($) | TC ($) = TFC + TVC | AFC ($) = TFC/Q | AVC ($) = TVC/Q | ATC ($) = TC/Q | MC ($) = ΔTC/ΔQ |
| :--------- | :------ | :------ | :----------------- | :-------------- | :-------------- | :------------- | :-------------- |
| 0          | 100     | 0       | 100                | -               | -               | -              | -               |
| 1          | 100     | 50      | 150                | 100.00          | 50.00           | 150.00         | 50              |
| 2          | 100     | 90      | 190                | 50.00           | 45.00           | 95.00          | 40              |
| 3          | 100     | 120     | 220                | 33.33           | 40.00           | 73.33          | 30              |
| 4          | 100     | 160     | 260                | 25.00           | 40.00           | 65.00          | 40              |
| 5          | 100     | 210     | 310                | 20.00           | 42.00           | 62.00          | 50              |

**Question 2:**
Which short-run cost curve is always falling as output increases?
a) AVC
b) ATC
c) AFC
d) MC

**Answer 2:** c) AFC (Average Fixed Cost)

**Question 3:**
What is the relationship between the Marginal Cost (MC) curve and the Average Total Cost (ATC) curve?
a) MC intersects ATC at its highest point.
b) MC intersects ATC at its lowest point.
c) MC is always below ATC.
d) MC is always above ATC.

**Answer 3:** b) MC intersects ATC at its lowest point.

**Question 4:**
If a firm's Total Fixed Cost is $500, what is its AFC when it produces 10 units?

**Answer 4:**
$AFC = TFC / Q = \$500 / 10 = \$50$

**Question 5:**
When does the Average Variable Cost (AVC) typically start to rise?
a) When marginal product starts to fall.
b) When total cost starts to fall.
c) When average fixed cost starts to fall.
d) When total variable cost is zero.

**Answer 5:** a) When marginal product starts to fall. This is due to the law of diminishing marginal returns.
