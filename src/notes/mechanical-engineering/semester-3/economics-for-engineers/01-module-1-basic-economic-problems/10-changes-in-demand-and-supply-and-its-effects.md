# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS: Module 1: Basic Economic Problems

## Topic: Changes in Demand and Supply and its Effects

This module delves into the fundamental concepts of demand and supply, crucial for understanding how markets function and how economic decisions are made in engineering contexts. We will explore the factors that influence these concepts and how shifts in demand and supply impact equilibrium prices and quantities. This knowledge is essential for making informed economic decisions in engineering projects and for understanding the broader economic environment in which engineering firms operate.

**Course Outcomes Addressed:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Understand the fundamentals of various economic issues using laws and learn the concepts of demand, supply, elasticity and production function.
*   **CO2 (K3):** Develop decision-making capability by applying concepts relating to costs and revenue, and acquire knowledge regarding the functioning of firms in different market situations.

---

### 1. Understanding Demand

Demand refers to the quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various prices during a specific period.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Law of Demand:** Ceteris paribus (all other factors remaining constant), as the price of a good or service increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa. This inverse relationship is graphically represented by a downward-sloping demand curve.
    *   *Textbook Reference:* Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury (2015) emphasizes the "ceteris paribus" assumption as critical to understanding the law of demand.
*   **Demand Schedule:** A tabular representation of the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity demanded.
*   **Demand Curve:** A graphical representation of the demand schedule, showing the relationship between price and quantity demanded.

**Factors Affecting Demand (Determinants of Demand):**

These factors cause a *shift* in the entire demand curve.

*   **Price of the Good:** This leads to a *movement along* the demand curve, not a shift.
*   **Income of Consumers:**
    *   **Normal Goods:** Demand increases as income increases. (e.g., new car, luxury vacations)
    *   **Inferior Goods:** Demand decreases as income increases. (e.g., public transport, second-hand clothing)
        *   *Example:* If an engineer's salary increases, they are more likely to buy a new car (normal good) and less likely to rely on public transport (inferior good).
*   **Prices of Related Goods:**
    *   **Substitute Goods:** Goods that can be used in place of each other. If the price of a substitute increases, the demand for the original good increases. (e.g., coffee and tea, butter and margarine)
        *   *Example:* If the price of coffee rises significantly, consumers may switch to tea, increasing the demand for tea.
    *   **Complementary Goods:** Goods that are used together. If the price of a complement increases, the demand for the original good decreases. (e.g., printers and ink cartridges, cars and gasoline)
        *   *Example:* If the price of gasoline increases substantially, the demand for cars might decrease.
*   **Tastes and Preferences:** Changes in consumer tastes, fashion, and preferences can affect demand. (e.g., growing popularity of electric vehicles)
*   **Expectations of Future Prices:** If consumers expect prices to rise in the future, they may increase their current demand.
*   **Number of Buyers:** An increase in the number of consumers in the market will increase demand.
*   **Government Policies:** Taxes on goods can decrease demand, while subsidies can increase demand.

**Graphical Representation of Demand Shifts:**

*   **Increase in Demand:** The demand curve shifts to the *right*. This means consumers are willing to buy more at every price.
*   **Decrease in Demand:** The demand curve shifts to the *left*. This means consumers are willing to buy less at every price.

### 2. Understanding Supply

Supply refers to the quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at various prices during a specific period.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Law of Supply:** Ceteris paribus, as the price of a good or service increases, the quantity supplied increases, and vice versa. This direct relationship is graphically represented by an upward-sloping supply curve.
    *   *Textbook Reference:* Thuesen and Fabrycky (1966) in *Engineering Economy* often discuss supply in the context of production costs and output levels, highlighting how higher prices incentivize greater production.
*   **Supply Schedule:** A tabular representation of the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity supplied.
*   **Supply Curve:** A graphical representation of the supply schedule, showing the relationship between price and quantity supplied.

**Factors Affecting Supply (Determinants of Supply):**

These factors cause a *shift* in the entire supply curve.

*   **Price of the Good:** This leads to a *movement along* the supply curve, not a shift.
*   **Input Prices (Cost of Production):** If the cost of resources used to produce a good (e.g., labor, raw materials, energy) increases, the supply decreases.
    *   *Example:* An increase in the cost of steel will likely decrease the supply of automobiles, as manufacturing becomes more expensive.
*   **Technology:** Improvements in technology generally lead to lower production costs and increased supply.
    *   *Example:* Advancements in automation in manufacturing can lead to higher output and thus increased supply of manufactured goods.
        *   *Textbook Reference:* Park (2001) in *Contemporary Engineering Economics* discusses the impact of technological advancements on production and supply.
*   **Number of Sellers:** An increase in the number of producers in the market will increase supply.
*   **Producer Expectations:** If producers expect prices to rise in the future, they may decrease their current supply to sell later at a higher price.
*   **Government Policies:** Taxes on production can decrease supply, while subsidies can increase supply. Regulations (e.g., environmental standards) can also increase production costs and decrease supply.
*   **Prices of Related Goods in Production:** If a producer can produce two goods, and the price of one increases, they may shift resources to produce more of that good, decreasing the supply of the other.

**Graphical Representation of Supply Shifts:**

*   **Increase in Supply:** The supply curve shifts to the *right*. This means producers are willing to sell more at every price.
*   **Decrease in Supply:** The supply curve shifts to the *left*. This means producers are willing to sell less at every price.

### 3. Market Equilibrium: The Interaction of Demand and Supply

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Equilibrium Price:** The price at which the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. This is where the demand and supply curves intersect.
*   **Equilibrium Quantity:** The quantity of a good or service that is both demanded and supplied at the equilibrium price.
*   **Surplus (Excess Supply):** Occurs when the market price is above the equilibrium price, leading to quantity supplied exceeding quantity demanded. Producers will lower prices to clear inventory.
*   **Shortage (Excess Demand):** Occurs when the market price is below the equilibrium price, leading to quantity demanded exceeding quantity supplied. Consumers will bid prices up to acquire the limited goods.

### 4. Changes in Demand and Supply and Their Effects on Equilibrium

**Understanding the Impact of Shifts:**

When determinants of demand or supply change, the respective curves shift, leading to a new equilibrium price and quantity.

**Scenarios and Their Effects:**

| Change in Determinant                     | Effect on Demand/Supply | New Equilibrium Price | New Equilibrium Quantity |
| :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------ | :-------------------- | :----------------------- |
| **Increase in Demand**                    | Demand curve shifts right | Increases             | Increases                |
| *Example:* Rise in consumer income for a normal good. |                           |                       |                          |
| **Decrease in Demand**                    | Demand curve shifts left  | Decreases             | Decreases                |
| *Example:* Fall in consumer income for a normal good. |                           |                       |                          |
| **Increase in Supply**                    | Supply curve shifts right | Decreases             | Increases                |
| *Example:* Technological advancement reduces production costs. |                           |                       |                          |
| **Decrease in Supply**                    | Supply curve shifts left  | Increases             | Decreases                |
| *Example:* Increase in the price of raw materials. |                           |                       |                          |

**Combined Shifts (More Complex Scenarios):**

When both demand and supply shift simultaneously, the effect on either price or quantity might be indeterminate without knowing the magnitude of the shifts.

*   **Demand Increases, Supply Decreases:**
    *   Equilibrium Price: **Increases** (both shifts push price up)
    *   Equilibrium Quantity: **Indeterminate** (demand shift pushes quantity up, supply shift pushes quantity down)
        *   *Example:* Increased demand for smartphones due to new features, coupled with a global chip shortage reducing smartphone production.
*   **Demand Decreases, Supply Increases:**
    *   Equilibrium Price: **Decreases** (both shifts push price down)
    *   Equilibrium Quantity: **Indeterminate** (demand shift pushes quantity down, supply shift pushes quantity up)
        *   *Example:* Decreased demand for coal due to environmental concerns, coupled with increased efficiency in coal mining operations.
*   **Both Demand and Supply Increase:**
    *   Equilibrium Price: **Indeterminate** (demand shift pushes price up, supply shift pushes price down)
    *   Equilibrium Quantity: **Increases** (both shifts push quantity up)
        *   *Example:* Increased demand for electric vehicles due to environmental awareness, coupled with technological advancements making EVs cheaper to produce.
*   **Both Demand and Supply Decrease:**
    *   Equilibrium Price: **Indeterminate** (demand shift pushes price down, supply shift pushes price up)
    *   Equilibrium Quantity: **Decreases** (both shifts push quantity down)
        *   *Example:* Decreased demand for traditional landline phones due to mobile technology, coupled with a decline in manufacturers due to reduced profitability.

**Graphical Analysis of Shifts:**

It is crucial for engineers to be able to visualize these shifts on a demand-supply graph to understand the resulting changes in market outcomes.

*   **Start with an initial equilibrium (D1, S1).**
*   **Draw the new demand or supply curve based on the shift.**
*   **Identify the new intersection point, which represents the new equilibrium price and quantity.**

### 5. Relevance to Engineering

Understanding changes in demand and supply is vital for engineers in various aspects:

*   **Product Design and Development:** Anticipating changes in consumer demand helps engineers design products that meet market needs.
    *   *Example:* An automotive engineer designing a new vehicle needs to consider current and future demand trends for fuel efficiency, electric powertrains, and advanced safety features.
*   **Production Planning and Forecasting:** Predicting changes in supply (due to input costs, technology, etc.) and demand helps in planning production volumes, inventory management, and resource allocation.
    *   *Example:* A manufacturing engineer must forecast demand for a component and be aware of potential supply chain disruptions that could affect the availability and cost of raw materials.
*   **Pricing Strategies:** Understanding market dynamics helps in setting competitive prices for products and services.
    *   *Example:* An engineer involved in project bidding needs to understand the demand for their engineering services and the supply of competing firms to price their bids effectively.
*   **Investment Decisions:** Evaluating the potential demand and supply conditions for new technologies or projects is crucial for making sound investment decisions.
    *   *Textbook Reference:* Engineering Economy texts like Thuesen and Fabrycky (1966) and Blank and Tarquin (7th Ed.) provide frameworks for evaluating investments, where market demand and supply analysis is a prerequisite for estimating revenues and costs.
*   **Resource Management:** Understanding the supply of essential resources (materials, energy) and the demand for them informs efficient resource utilization.
    *   *Example:* An environmental engineer working on water treatment plants needs to understand the demand for clean water and the supply of water resources, as well as the impact of changing environmental regulations on supply.

### 6. Important Points to Remember:

*   **Demand vs. Quantity Demanded:** A change in the price of the good causes a movement *along* the demand curve (change in quantity demanded). A change in any other determinant causes a *shift* in the entire demand curve (change in demand).
*   **Supply vs. Quantity Supplied:** A change in the price of the good causes a movement *along* the supply curve (change in quantity supplied). A change in any other determinant causes a *shift* in the entire supply curve (change in supply).
*   **Equilibrium is dynamic:** It is constantly adjusting to changes in the underlying determinants of demand and supply.
*   **Ceteris Paribus:** This assumption is crucial for analyzing the effect of one factor at a time. In reality, multiple factors often change simultaneously.
*   **Graphical analysis is key:** Visualizing shifts on a demand-supply diagram provides a clear understanding of market adjustments.
*   **Engineering decisions are influenced by market forces:** Engineers must be aware of economic principles like demand and supply to make effective decisions.

### 7. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1:**
If the price of gasoline (a complementary good for cars) increases significantly, what is the likely effect on the demand for cars? Illustrate this with a demand curve shift.

**Answer:**
An increase in the price of gasoline will lead to a **decrease in the demand for cars**. This is because cars and gasoline are complementary goods. Consumers will be less willing to buy cars if the cost of operating them (i.e., the price of gasoline) rises.
*   **Illustration:** The demand curve for cars will shift to the **left**.

**Question 2:**
A new, more efficient manufacturing technology is developed for producing smartphones. What is the likely effect on the supply of smartphones, assuming the price remains unchanged initially? Illustrate this with a supply curve shift.

**Answer:**
The development of a more efficient technology will **increase the supply of smartphones**. This is because the cost of production will decrease, allowing manufacturers to produce more smartphones at any given price.
*   **Illustration:** The supply curve for smartphones will shift to the **right**.

**Question 3:**
Suppose there is an increase in the demand for electric vehicles (EVs) due to growing environmental awareness, and simultaneously, a government subsidy is introduced for EV manufacturers, reducing their production costs. Describe the impact on the equilibrium price and quantity of EVs.

**Answer:**
*   **Effect of Increased Demand:** Pushes equilibrium price and quantity **up**.
*   **Effect of Increased Supply (due to subsidy):** Pushes equilibrium price **down** and equilibrium quantity **up**.
*   **Combined Effect:**
    *   Equilibrium Quantity: Will **increase** (as both shifts push quantity up).
    *   Equilibrium Price: The net effect is **indeterminate**. If the increase in demand is larger than the increase in supply, the price will rise. If the increase in supply is larger, the price will fall. If they are equal in their impact on price, the price may remain unchanged.

**Question 4:**
A firm manufactures specialized industrial pumps. If the price of the key raw material (e.g., stainless steel) increases, and at the same time, there is a surge in demand for these pumps due to new infrastructure projects, analyze the effect on the equilibrium price and quantity.

**Answer:**
*   **Effect of Increased Raw Material Price (Decrease in Supply):** Pushes equilibrium price **up** and equilibrium quantity **down**.
*   **Effect of Surge in Demand (Increase in Demand):** Pushes equilibrium price and quantity **up**.
*   **Combined Effect:**
    *   Equilibrium Price: Will **increase** (as both shifts push price up).
    *   Equilibrium Quantity: The net effect is **indeterminate**. If the increase in demand is larger than the decrease in supply, the quantity will increase. If the decrease in supply is larger, the quantity will decrease.

**Exercise 1 (Conceptual):**
Explain why a rise in the price of computers would likely lead to a decrease in the demand for software. Identify the relationship between computers and software.

**Answer:**
Computers and software are **complementary goods**. This means they are used together. If the price of computers increases, consumers will likely buy fewer computers. Since fewer computers are being used, there will be less demand for the software that runs on them. This leads to a decrease in the demand curve for software.

**Exercise 2 (Analytical):**
Imagine the market for solar panels. If government policies encourage solar energy adoption (increasing demand) and simultaneously, breakthroughs in manufacturing reduce the cost of producing solar panels (increasing supply), what would be the most certain outcome for the market equilibrium?

**Answer:**
The most certain outcome is an **increase in the equilibrium quantity of solar panels traded**. While the effect on the equilibrium price is uncertain (depending on the relative magnitudes of the demand and supply shifts), both an increase in demand and an increase in supply independently lead to a higher equilibrium quantity.
