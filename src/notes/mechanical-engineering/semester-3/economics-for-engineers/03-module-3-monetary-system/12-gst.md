# Economics for Engineers - Module 3: Monetary System

## Topic: Goods and Services Tax (GST)

---

### **Introduction to GST**

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based tax levied on every value addition. It has replaced most indirect taxes in India, such as excise duty, service tax, VAT, etc. The primary aim of GST is to create a unified national market by subsuming multiple indirect taxes and eliminating cascading effects of taxes.

**Key Concept:** **Cascading Effect of Taxes:** This refers to the phenomenon where taxes are levied on taxes at different stages of production and distribution, leading to an increase in the final price of the goods or services. GST aims to eliminate this by providing a mechanism for input tax credit.

---

### **1. Objectives of GST**

*   **To consolidate indirect taxes:** Bring various indirect taxes under a single umbrella.
*   **To eliminate cascading effect:** Ensure that tax is levied only on the value added at each stage.
*   **To create a common national market:** Facilitate seamless movement of goods and services across states.
*   **To increase tax revenue:** Broaden the tax base and improve compliance.
*   **To simplify the tax structure:** Make tax administration easier for both businesses and the government.
*   **To reduce tax evasion:** Enhance transparency and accountability.

---

### **2. Types of GST**

Since India is a federal country, GST is levied concurrently by the Central and State governments. There are three components of GST:

*   **Central GST (CGST):** Levied by the Central Government on intra-state supplies of goods and services.
*   **State GST (SGST):** Levied by the State Government on intra-state supplies of goods and services.
    *   **Intra-state supply:** When the supplier and the place of supply are in the same state.
*   **Integrated GST (IGST):** Levied by the Central Government on inter-state supplies of goods and services.
    *   **Inter-state supply:** When the supplier and the place of supply are in different states.

**Example:**
If a business sells goods within Maharashtra, both CGST and SGST will be levied. If the same business sells goods to a business in Karnataka, IGST will be levied.

---

### **3. How GST Works: Input Tax Credit (ITC)**

The most crucial mechanism in GST is the Input Tax Credit (ITC). This allows businesses to claim credit for taxes paid on inputs (raw materials, capital goods, services) used in their business. This credit can be used to offset the tax payable on their outputs.

**Process:**

1.  **Supplier pays tax:** When a business (say, Manufacturer A) buys inputs and pays GST on them, they get an ITC.
2.  **Manufacturer A sells to Distributor B:** Manufacturer A adds value and charges GST on the selling price to Distributor B. From this GST, Manufacturer A deducts the ITC they paid on inputs.
3.  **Distributor B sells to Retailer C:** Distributor B adds value and charges GST on the selling price to Retailer C. From this GST, Distributor B deducts the ITC they paid to Manufacturer A.
4.  **Retailer C sells to End Consumer:** Retailer C adds value and charges GST on the selling price to the End Consumer. From this GST, Retailer C deducts the ITC they paid to Distributor B.

**Key Concept:** **Value Addition:** GST is levied on the value added at each stage of the supply chain. ITC ensures that tax is not levied on the previously taxed value.

**Example (Illustrative - assuming a single tax rate for simplicity):**

*   **Stage 1: Manufacturer:**
    *   Buys raw materials for ₹1,000 + 10% GST (₹100).
    *   Pays ₹1,100. Gets ITC of ₹100.
    *   Manufactures goods and sells them for ₹2,000 + 10% GST (₹200).
    *   **Tax payable:** ₹200 (output tax) - ₹100 (ITC) = ₹100.
    *   **Revenue for Manufacturer:** ₹2,000 (value added) + ₹100 (tax collected) = ₹2,100.

*   **Stage 2: Distributor:**
    *   Buys goods from Manufacturer for ₹2,000 + 10% GST (₹200).
    *   Pays ₹2,200. Gets ITC of ₹200.
    *   Sells goods to Retailer for ₹2,500 + 10% GST (₹250).
    *   **Tax payable:** ₹250 (output tax) - ₹200 (ITC) = ₹50.
    *   **Revenue for Distributor:** ₹2,500 (value added) + ₹50 (tax collected) = ₹2,550.

*   **Stage 3: Retailer:**
    *   Buys goods from Distributor for ₹2,500 + 10% GST (₹250).
    *   Pays ₹2,750. Gets ITC of ₹250.
    *   Sells goods to End Consumer for ₹3,000 + 10% GST (₹300).
    *   **Tax payable:** ₹300 (output tax) - ₹250 (ITC) = ₹50.
    *   **Revenue for Retailer:** ₹3,000 (value added) + ₹50 (tax collected) = ₹3,050.

**Total Tax Paid by the chain:** ₹100 (Manufacturer) + ₹50 (Distributor) + ₹50 (Retailer) = ₹200.
This ₹200 is 10% of the final sale price of ₹2,000 (initial value of goods sold by manufacturer to distributor). The tax is levied only on the value addition at each stage.

---

### **4. GST Rates in India**

GST in India has a multi-rate structure. The most common rates are:

*   **0%:** Essential goods (e.g., cereals, milk, salt, fresh vegetables).
*   **5%:** Common goods (e.g., processed foods, some medicines, footwear up to ₹500).
*   **12%:** Certain goods and services (e.g., butter, ghee, fruit juices, mobile phones, common hotel stays).
*   **18%:** Most goods and services (e.g., hair oil, soaps, toothpastes, automobiles, restaurants with turnover above ₹1 crore).
*   **28%:** Luxury and sin goods (e.g., cars, tobacco products, aerated drinks, hotel stays above ₹7,500).

There is also a **Special Rate** of 0.25% on rough diamonds and 3% on gold.

---

### **5. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Supply:** Includes all forms of sale, transfer, barter, exchange, license, rental, lease, or disposal of goods or services or both.
*   **Goods:** Every kind of movable property other than money and securities.
*   **Services:** Anything other than goods.
*   **Taxable Supply:** A supply of goods or services or both which is leviable to tax under the GST Act.
*   **Place of Supply:** Determines whether a supply is intra-state or inter-state. This is crucial for deciding whether CGST/SGST or IGST applies.
*   **Input Tax:** The tax paid by a registered person on the inward supply of goods or services or both.
*   **Output Tax:** The tax chargeable by a registered person in respect of the outward supply of goods or services or both.
*   **Registered Person:** A person who is registered under the GST provisions.
*   **Composition Scheme:** An optional scheme for small taxpayers to pay GST at a lower rate on their turnover, without the complexities of ITC.

### **6. GST and Engineering Economics**

GST has significant implications for engineering economics, particularly in cost analysis, project evaluation, and pricing decisions.

*   **Cost Calculation:** Engineers need to accurately incorporate GST in their cost estimates for projects, materials, and services. This includes considering the GST rates on inputs and the ability to claim ITC.
*   **Pricing Strategies:** GST influences the final price of goods and services produced by engineering firms. Understanding how GST impacts costs and pricing is crucial for competitive pricing.
*   **Break-Even Analysis:** The inclusion of GST affects the calculation of fixed and variable costs, thereby impacting the break-even point.
    *   **Impact on Costs:**
        *   **Direct Costs:** GST on raw materials, components, and consumables directly increases the variable cost per unit.
        *   **Indirect Costs:** GST on services like consulting, repairs, and maintenance adds to the overheads.
        *   **Capital Costs:** GST on machinery, equipment, and construction materials impacts the initial capital outlay.
    *   **Impact on Revenue:** The selling price of a product or service will now include GST, which is collected on behalf of the government. However, the revenue recognized by the firm is the price before GST.
*   **Value Analysis:** In value analysis, understanding the tax component of costs is important to identify areas for cost reduction without compromising on value. For instance, sourcing materials from vendors who offer better GST compliance might be beneficial.
*   **Cash Flow Management:** GST requires businesses to manage cash flows effectively, as they need to pay GST on outward supplies before receiving payment from customers in some cases, and claim ITC by matching invoices.

**Reference (Implicitly from Textbooks):** While the provided textbooks may not have specific chapters solely dedicated to GST (as it's a relatively recent tax reform in India compared to the publication dates), the principles of cost accounting, pricing, and project evaluation discussed in **Managerial Economics by Geetika, Piyali Ghosh and Chodhury** and **Engineering Economy by R. Paneerselvam** are directly impacted by the GST regime. For instance, understanding the concept of **cost-plus pricing** or **value-based pricing** requires incorporating tax implications.

### **7. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
A manufacturing company purchases raw materials worth ₹50,000 for which it pays 18% GST. It then manufactures finished goods and sells them for ₹90,000, charging 18% GST. What is the net GST liability of the company?

**Answer 1:**
*   GST paid on raw materials (Input Tax): 18% of ₹50,000 = ₹9,000
*   GST collected on finished goods (Output Tax): 18% of ₹90,000 = ₹16,200
*   Net GST Liability = Output Tax - Input Tax
*   Net GST Liability = ₹16,200 - ₹9,000 = ₹7,200

**Question 2:**
An engineering project involves purchasing a specialized machine for ₹10,00,000. The GST rate applicable on this machine is 12%. The company plans to use this machine for production. If the company sells its finished products at ₹15,00,000 and charges 18% GST, and the machine's cost is absorbed over 5 years (assuming depreciation is not considered for simplicity and ignoring other costs), how does GST affect the initial cash outflow and the cash inflow related to GST on sales?

**Answer 2:**
*   **Initial Cash Outflow (for the machine):**
    *   Cost of Machine: ₹10,00,000
    *   GST @ 12%: 0.12 * ₹10,00,000 = ₹1,20,000
    *   Total Cash Outflow = ₹10,00,000 + ₹1,20,000 = ₹11,20,000
    *   **Note:** The GST paid on capital goods is eligible for Input Tax Credit. So, while ₹1,20,000 is an initial outflow, it can be claimed back as ITC against future GST liabilities on sales.

*   **Cash Inflow related to GST on Sales (per cycle, assuming the machine is used to produce goods sold for ₹15,00,000):**
    *   Sales Revenue: ₹15,00,000
    *   GST @ 18%: 0.18 * ₹15,00,000 = ₹2,70,000
    *   Total Cash Received from Customer = ₹15,00,000 + ₹2,70,000 = ₹17,70,000
    *   **Net GST Payable (assuming no other input taxes for simplicity):** ₹2,70,000 (Output Tax). The ₹1,20,000 ITC on the machine can be used to offset this.
    *   Net GST payment = ₹2,70,000 - ₹1,20,000 = ₹1,50,000.

**Question 3 (Conceptual):**
Explain how the introduction of GST would impact the Break-Even Point (BEP) of an engineering firm that manufactures customized machinery. Consider the effect on variable costs and the potential for claiming input tax credits.

**Answer 3:**
The introduction of GST would likely impact the BEP in the following ways:

*   **Increase in Variable Costs:** GST on raw materials, components, and consumables used in manufacturing will directly increase the variable cost per unit of machinery. This alone would tend to increase the BEP (higher sales volume needed to cover costs).
*   **Potential for Tax Neutrality through ITC:** However, the firm can claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on all these inputs. This means the GST paid on inputs is not a final cost to the firm. The net effect on variable costs depends on the efficiency of the ITC mechanism and any non-creditable taxes or restrictions.
*   **Impact on Fixed Costs:** GST on services (e.g., repair, maintenance, professional fees) and other overheads will also increase fixed costs, potentially increasing the BEP.
*   **Revenue Recognition:** The selling price of the machinery will include GST, but the firm's actual revenue is the price before GST. This distinction is crucial for BEP calculations.
*   **Overall Effect:** The net impact on BEP depends on the interplay between increased input costs (offset by ITC) and increased fixed costs. If the firm can effectively utilize ITC and the GST rate on its outputs is higher than the weighted average GST rate on its inputs, the net increase in variable costs might be manageable. However, if there are significant GST complexities, compliance issues, or non-creditable input taxes, the BEP is likely to increase.

### **8. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Destination-Based:** GST is levied at the point of consumption, not origin.
*   **Value Addition:** Tax is applied only on the value added at each stage.
*   **Input Tax Credit (ITC):** The backbone of GST, allowing businesses to get credit for taxes paid on inputs.
*   **CGST, SGST, IGST:** Understanding the difference is crucial for determining tax liability in intra-state and inter-state transactions.
*   **Compliance:** Proper invoicing, documentation, and timely filing of returns are essential for GST compliance.
*   **Impact on Engineering Projects:** GST influences cost estimation, project feasibility, and financial planning for engineering firms. Engineers must factor in GST in all economic analyses.
*   **Tax Invoices:** A valid tax invoice is a prerequisite for claiming ITC. It must contain specific details like GSTIN, HSN/SAC codes, taxable value, and the tax amount.
