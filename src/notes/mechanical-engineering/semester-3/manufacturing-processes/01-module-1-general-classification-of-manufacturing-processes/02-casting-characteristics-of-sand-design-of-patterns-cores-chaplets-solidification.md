### 1. Introduction to Casting

**Definition:** Casting is a manufacturing process in which molten metal is poured into a mold cavity and allowed to solidify. The solidified metal takes the shape of the mold cavity.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Mold:** A hollow cavity into which molten metal is poured.
*   **Molten Metal:** Metal in a liquid state, heated above its melting point.
*   **Solidification:** The process of metal transforming from liquid to solid state within the mold.
*   **Casting:** The final solidified metal object.

**Advantages of Casting:**

*   Complex shapes can be produced easily.
*   Suitable for large and heavy parts.
*   Wide range of materials can be cast (ferrous and non-ferrous).
*   Can produce parts with intricate internal features.
*   Relatively low tooling costs for some casting methods.

**Disadvantages of Casting:**

*   Surface finish may not be as good as machining.
*   Dimensional accuracy can be lower than other methods.
*   Can have internal defects like porosity and inclusions.
*   Machining may be required for critical dimensions and surfaces.

**Classification of Casting Processes:**

*   **Based on Mold Material:**
    *   **Expendable Mold Casting:** The mold is destroyed after each casting (e.g., Sand Casting, Investment Casting, Shell Molding, Plaster Mold Casting).
    *   **Permanent Mold Casting:** The mold is reusable (e.g., Die Casting, Centrifugal Casting, Permanent Mold Gravity Die Casting).
*   **Based on Filling Mechanism:**
    *   Gravity Casting
    *   Low-Pressure Casting
    *   High-Pressure Casting (Die Casting)
    *   Centrifugal Casting

### 2. Characteristics of Sand in Sand Casting

Sand casting is the most common and oldest casting process. The molding sand is crucial for forming the mold cavity.

**Ideal Sand Properties:**

*   **Refractoriness:** Ability to withstand high temperatures of molten metal without melting or reacting. Silica (SiO₂) and Zirconia (ZrSiO₄) are common refractory materials.
*   **Permeability:** Ability of gases and vapors to escape from the mold cavity through the sand. This prevents blowholes in the casting. Green sand (moist sand) typically has good permeability.
*   **Green Strength:** Strength of the sand mixture when it is damp (green condition). It allows the mold to be handled and assembled without collapsing.
*   **Dry Strength:** Strength of the sand after the moisture has been removed. Important for molds that are baked.
*   **Collapsibility:** Ability of the sand to break down easily after solidification, allowing for easy removal of the casting.
*   **Flowability:** Ability of the sand to fill all parts of the pattern, especially in corners and fine details.
*   **Chemical Stability:** Resistance to chemical reactions with the molten metal.
*   **Friability:** Ease with which the sand can be crumbled after solidification.

**Types of Molding Sand:**

*   **Green Sand:** A mixture of silica sand, clay (like bentonite), and water. Used for general-purpose casting.
*   **Dry Sand:** Sand that has been dried or baked after ramming. It has higher strength but lower permeability compared to green sand.
*   **Loam Sand:** Contains a higher percentage of clay, providing greater strength but lower permeability.
*   **Core Sand:** Used for making cores (discussed later). It has higher strength and refractoriness than molding sand.
*   **Facing Sand:** Applied directly to the pattern surface to provide a good surface finish to the casting. It contains additives like coal dust, charcoal, or cereal binders.

### 3. Design of Patterns

A pattern is a replica of the final casting, but with some modifications to account for shrinkage, machining allowances, and draft.

**Purpose of Patterns:**

*   To create the mold cavity.
*   To facilitate easy removal from the mold.
*   To account for metal shrinkage during solidification and cooling.
*   To provide allowances for machining surfaces.
*   To simplify the molding process.

**Types of Patterns:**

*   **Solid or Single-Piece Pattern:** Simple shapes, not suitable for complex cavities. Difficult to remove from the mold.
*   **Gated Pattern:** Includes a runner and gate system attached to the pattern, simplifying the molding process.
*   **Match-Plate Pattern:** A pattern divided into two halves, each mounted on opposite sides of a match plate. Used in molding machines for high-volume production.
*   **Cope and Drag Pattern:** The pattern is divided into two halves, corresponding to the cope and drag parts of the mold.
*   **Loose Piece Pattern:** For complex shapes or those with undercuts, extra pieces are attached to the main pattern to facilitate removal.

**Pattern Allowances:**

*   **Shrinkage Allowance:** Compensates for the contraction of metal during solidification and cooling. This is the most significant allowance. It's added to the dimensions of the pattern.
    *   **Linear Shrinkage:** `Pattern Dimension = Casting Dimension * (1 + Shrinkage Percentage)`
    *   Example: For cast iron, shrinkage is about 1% (0.01). A 100 mm casting dimension would require a 100 mm * (1 + 0.01) = 101 mm pattern dimension.
*   **Draft Allowance:** Taper added to vertical surfaces of the pattern to allow for easy removal from the mold. It's usually a slight angle outwards.
*   **Machining Allowance:** Extra material added to surfaces that will be machined to achieve final dimensions and surface finish. It depends on the casting method, material, and required finish.
*   **Distortion Allowance:** For castings with complex or thin sections that might warp during cooling, a slight distortion is incorporated into the pattern.
*   **Rapping Allowance:** A slight reduction in pattern size to account for tapping or rapping the pattern to loosen it from the mold before removal.

**References:**

*   **Kalpakjian & Schmid, Chapter on Casting:** Covers pattern design and allowances.
*   **Rao, Chapter 3 (Patterns):** Provides detailed information on pattern types, allowances, and materials.

### 4. Cores and Chaplets

**Cores:**

*   **Definition:** A core is a separate insert made of sand (or other material) placed in the mold cavity to form internal surfaces or passages (holes) within the casting.
*   **Purpose:** To create hollow sections or internal cavities in the casting that cannot be formed by the mold cavity alone.
*   **Materials:** Typically made from core sand, which is bonded with organic or inorganic binders and then baked to achieve high strength.
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   Must be strong enough to resist the ferrostatic pressure of molten metal.
    *   Must have sufficient collapsibility to break away easily after solidification.
    *   Must be permeable to allow gases to escape.
    *   Must be designed to withstand the pouring temperature.
*   **Types of Cores:**
    *   **Green Sand Cores:** Used for simpler internal passages, often supported by chaplets.
    *   **Baked Sand Cores:** Used for more complex or critical internal features, offering higher strength and stability.
    *   **Shell Cores:** Made from a mixture of fine sand and phenolic resin, cured by heat. They offer good dimensional accuracy and smooth surfaces.
    *   **No-Bake Cores:** Bonded by chemical reaction at room temperature, eliminating the need for baking.

**Chaplets:**

*   **Definition:** Chaplets are metal supports embedded in the mold walls and extending into the core or mold cavity.
*   **Purpose:** To support cores or other projections within the mold cavity, preventing them from sagging or moving due to the pressure of molten metal.
*   **Materials:** Usually made from the same metal as the casting or a metal with a similar melting point to prevent galvanic corrosion and minimize discontinuities.
*   **Design:** They are typically designed to melt or fuse with the casting metal during pouring, thus becoming an integral part of the casting.
*   **Common Types:**
    *   **Straight or Bent Wafers:** Used for supporting flat surfaces.
    *   **Studs:** Used for supporting projections or cores.
    *   **Anchor Chaplets:** Designed to anchor into the casting metal.

**References:**

*   **Rao, Chapter 4 (Cores and Chaplets):** Covers the purpose, types, and design of cores and chaplets.
*   **Ghosh & Mallik, Chapter 1:** Mentions the use of cores and chaplets in sand casting.

### 5. Solidification of Metals and Chvorinov's Rule

**Solidification Process:**

1.  **Liquid Metal:** Molten metal is poured into the mold.
2.  **Chilling:** Heat transfer occurs from the molten metal to the mold walls. The metal in contact with the mold starts to solidify, forming a shell.
3.  **Crystal Growth:** Solidification proceeds inwards from the mold walls. Dendritic growth is common for many alloys.
4.  **Contraction:** As the metal solidifies and cools, it contracts in volume. This contraction can lead to shrinkage porosity if not properly compensated for by feeding.
5.  **Feeding:** Molten metal from a riser (discussed later) flows into the solidifying casting to compensate for shrinkage, ensuring a sound casting.

**Factors Affecting Solidification Time:**

*   **Casting Geometry:** Thicker sections take longer to solidify.
*   **Mold Material:** Molds with high thermal conductivity (e.g., metal molds) extract heat faster, leading to shorter solidification times.
*   **Metal Properties:** Specific heat, latent heat of fusion, and thermal conductivity of the metal influence solidification.
*   **Pouring Temperature:** Higher pouring temperatures generally increase solidification time.

**Chvorinov's Rule:**

*   **Concept:** Chvorinov's rule provides a relationship between the solidification time and the volume-to-surface area ratio of a casting section. It states that the solidification time ($T_s$) is proportional to the square of the ratio of volume ($V$) to surface area ($A$).
*   **Formula:**
    $T_s = K (\frac{V}{A})^2$
    Where:
    *   $T_s$ = Solidification time
    *   $K$ = Chvorinov's constant (depends on the casting material, mold material, and pouring temperature)
    *   $V$ = Volume of the casting section
    *   $A$ = Surface area of the casting section that is in contact with the mold

*   **Significance:**
    *   It helps in determining the relative solidification times of different parts of a casting.
    *   Thicker sections (higher V/A ratio) solidify slower than thinner sections.
    *   It is fundamental to the design of risers, as risers must solidify last to feed the casting.

**Chvorinov's Constant (K):**

*   The value of $K$ is determined empirically and depends on the specific casting and mold combination.
*   For a given casting process and material, $K$ can be considered constant.
*   Typical values of $K$ (in minutes/in² or minutes/cm²) can be found in foundry handbooks. For sand casting, $K$ is usually higher than for metal molds.

### 6. Elements of a Gating System

**Purpose of Gating System:**

The gating system is a channel through which molten metal flows from the pouring basin into the mold cavity. Its primary functions are:

*   To guide the molten metal into the mold cavity smoothly and efficiently.
*   To minimize turbulence and oxidation of the molten metal stream.
*   To prevent the ingress of sand or other foreign particles into the mold cavity.
*   To ensure the mold cavity fills completely.

**Elements of a Gating System:**

1.  **Pouring Basin (Sprue Basin):**
    *   **Function:** A funnel-shaped reservoir at the top of the sprue that receives the molten metal from the ladle. It reduces turbulence and allows for initial skimming of slag.
    *   **Design:** Usually designed with a larger cross-sectional area than the sprue to dampen the initial impact of the molten metal.

2.  **Sprue:**
    *   **Function:** A vertical channel connecting the pouring basin to the runner.
    *   **Design:**
        *   **Straight Sprue:** Simple but can cause aspiration (drawing air into the molten metal stream) if not properly designed.
        *   **Tapered Sprue:** The cross-sectional area decreases downwards. This helps to maintain the velocity of the molten metal and prevent aspiration, ensuring a solid stream. The taper ratio is typically designed based on the principle of continuity equation for incompressible flow.
        *   **Baffle Sprue:** A sprue with internal baffles to reduce the velocity and turbulence of the molten metal.

3.  **Runner:**
    *   **Function:** A horizontal channel that distributes the molten metal from the sprue to the gates.
    *   **Design:** Should be designed to carry the molten metal with minimal velocity and turbulence to the gates.

4.  **Gate:**
    *   **Function:** The opening through which the molten metal enters the mold cavity from the runner.
    *   **Design:**
        *   **Number of Gates:** Depends on the size and shape of the casting.
        *   **Placement:** Strategically placed to ensure proper filling of the mold cavity and avoid erosion of the mold walls or cores.
        *   **Size:** The total cross-sectional area of the gates influences the filling rate.
        *   **Types:**
            *   **Top Gate:** Metal enters from the top of the mold. Can cause turbulence.
            *   **Bottom Gate:** Metal enters from the bottom of the mold. Minimizes turbulence and oxidation.
            *   **Side Gate:** Metal enters from the side.
            *   **Horn Gate:** A tapered gate used to reduce turbulence.
            *   **Tab Gate:** A small gate connecting the runner to the mold cavity, often used in die casting.

**Gating Ratios:**

*   The ratio of the cross-sectional areas of the sprue, runner, and gates is crucial for proper metal flow.
*   A common gating ratio is **1:2:2** (Sprue Area : Runner Area : Total Gate Area). This is a general guideline and can vary based on the casting material and design.
*   A **1:3:6** ratio is also sometimes used.
*   The goal is to ensure that the sprue is the choke point to control the metal flow rate and prevent aspiration.

**References:**

*   **Rao, Chapter 6 (Gating and Risering):** Provides a comprehensive explanation of gating system elements, design principles, and gating ratios.
*   **Ghosh & Mallik, Chapter 1:** Covers basic gating system components.

### 7. Risers

**Purpose of Risers:**

*   **Function:** A riser is a reservoir of molten metal connected to the casting, designed to feed molten metal into the casting during solidification to compensate for volumetric contraction (shrinkage).
*   **Requirement:** The riser must solidify last, ensuring it remains molten while the casting is still solidifying.

**Types of Risers:**

*   **Based on Direction of Feed:**
    *   **Top Riser:** Placed on the top surface of the casting. Feeds the casting downwards.
    *   **Side Riser:** Placed on the side of the casting. Feeds the casting horizontally.
    *   **Stir Riser:** Placed below the casting, feeding upwards. Used for heavy sections at the bottom.

*   **Based on Heat Transfer:**
    *   **Open Riser:** Exposed to the atmosphere. Loses heat rapidly.
    *   **Blind Riser:** Enclosed within the sand mold. Insulated to prolong solidification time.
    *   **Insulated Riser:** Wrapped with insulating material to slow down solidification.
    *   **Hot Riser:** Intentionally heated before pouring to keep it molten longer.

*   **Based on Shape/Form:**
    *   **Cylindrical Riser:** Common shape.
    *   **Rectangular Riser:**
    *   **Spherical Riser:** Has the lowest surface area to volume ratio, making it efficient for feeding.
    *   **Cast-to-Size Riser:** Designed to be almost the exact size of the casting section it feeds.

**Riser Design Principles:**

*   **Solidification Time:** The riser must solidify last. Chvorinov's rule is used to determine the size of the riser.
    *   $T_{riser} = K (\frac{V_{riser}}{A_{riser}})^2$
    *   $T_{casting} = K (\frac{V_{casting}}{A_{casting}})^2$
    *   We need $T_{riser} > T_{casting}$.
*   **Volume:** The riser must contain enough molten metal to compensate for the shrinkage of the casting.
*   **Shape:** A shape with a low surface-area-to-volume ratio (like a sphere) is more efficient as it solidifies slower.
*   **Placement:** Must be placed in a location that can effectively feed the entire casting, usually adjacent to the heaviest section.
*   **Connection:** The connection (neck) between the riser and the casting should be sized such that it solidifies last.

**Riser Effectiveness:**

*   **Riser-to-Casting Volume Ratio:** A common starting point for riser design. For steel casting, it's often around 1:1. For aluminum, it might be lower.
*   **Shape Factor:** The V/A ratio is critical. A higher V/A ratio leads to longer solidification time.
*   **Insulation:** Effective insulation (e.g., using exothermic sleeves or insulating materials) can significantly improve riser efficiency.

**References:**

*   **Rao, Chapter 6 (Gating and Risering):** Comprehensive coverage of riser types, design, and effectiveness.
*   **Ghosh & Mallik, Chapter 1:** Discusses the role of risers in preventing shrinkage defects.

### 8. Chills

**Purpose of Chills:**

*   **Function:** Chills are chilling devices made of highly conductive materials (usually metal, like cast iron or steel) that are placed in the mold at specific locations. They are used to:
    1.  **Accelerate Solidification:** To achieve directional solidification, ensuring that the riser feeds the casting properly. They are placed in sections that would otherwise solidify last.
    2.  **Improve Uniformity of Solidification:** To promote more uniform cooling throughout the casting.
    3.  **Reduce Shrinkage Defects:** By promoting directional solidification towards the riser.
    4.  **Reduce Riser Size:** By making certain sections solidify faster, the overall size of the riser can be reduced.

**Types of Chills:**

*   **Internal Chills:** Placed inside the mold cavity, in contact with the molten metal (e.g., metal inserts).
*   **External Chills:** Placed on the outer surfaces of the mold.

**Design Considerations:**

*   **Material:** High thermal conductivity material (cast iron, steel).
*   **Shape:** Designed to fit the geometry of the casting.
*   **Placement:** Strategically located in thicker sections or areas prone to shrinkage.
*   **Contact:** Good thermal contact between the chill and the molten metal is essential for effective heat extraction.

**References:**

*   **Rao, Chapter 7 (Chills):** Explains the purpose, types, and application of chills.
*   **Ghosh & Mallik, Chapter 1:** Briefly mentions the use of chills.

### 9. Numerical Problems

**Example Problem 1: Shrinkage Allowance**

A cylindrical casting of 100 mm diameter and 200 mm length is to be made from a metal that shrinks by 1.5% linearly upon solidification. Calculate the dimensions of the pattern.

**Solution:**

*   Shrinkage percentage = 1.5% = 0.015
*   Pattern Diameter = Casting Diameter $\times$ (1 + Shrinkage Percentage)
    Pattern Diameter = 100 mm $\times$ (1 + 0.015) = 100 mm $\times$ 1.015 = 101.5 mm
*   Pattern Length = Casting Length $\times$ (1 + Shrinkage Percentage)
    Pattern Length = 200 mm $\times$ (1 + 0.015) = 200 mm $\times$ 1.015 = 203 mm

**Example Problem 2: Chvorinov's Rule**

Two cylindrical castings, A and B, made of the same material and cast in the same sand mold, have the following dimensions:

*   Casting A: Diameter = 50 mm, Length = 100 mm
*   Casting B: Diameter = 100 mm, Length = 50 mm

Calculate the ratio of their solidification times ($T_A / T_B$).

**Solution:**

*   **Casting A:**
    *   Volume ($V_A$) = $\pi (\frac{D_A}{2})^2 L_A = \pi (\frac{50}{2})^2 \times 100 = \pi (25)^2 \times 100 = 62500\pi \text{ mm}^3$
    *   Surface Area ($A_A$) = $2 \times \pi (\frac{D_A}{2})^2 + \pi D_A L_A = 2 \times \pi (25)^2 + \pi \times 50 \times 100 = 1250\pi + 5000\pi = 6250\pi \text{ mm}^2$
    *   $\frac{V_A}{A_A} = \frac{62500\pi}{6250\pi} = 10 \text{ mm}$

*   **Casting B:**
    *   Volume ($V_B$) = $\pi (\frac{D_B}{2})^2 L_B = \pi (\frac{100}{2})^2 \times 50 = \pi (50)^2 \times 50 = 125000\pi \text{ mm}^3$
    *   Surface Area ($A_B$) = $2 \times \pi (\frac{D_B}{2})^2 + \pi D_B L_B = 2 \times \pi (50)^2 + \pi \times 100 \times 50 = 5000\pi + 5000\pi = 10000\pi \text{ mm}^2$
    *   $\frac{V_B}{A_B} = \frac{125000\pi}{10000\pi} = 12.5 \text{ mm}$

*   Using Chvorinov's Rule: $T_s = K (\frac{V}{A})^2$
    *   $T_A = K (10)^2 = 100K$
    *   $T_B = K (12.5)^2 = 156.25K$

*   Ratio of solidification times:
    $\frac{T_A}{T_B} = \frac{100K}{156.25K} = \frac{100}{156.25} = 0.64$

**Practice Questions:**

1.  A casting is to be made with a dimension of 250 mm. If the metal used has a solidification shrinkage of 2%, what should be the corresponding pattern dimension? (Answer: 255 mm)
2.  Calculate the V/A ratio for a cube of side 100 mm. If it has a solidification time of 10 minutes, what would be the solidification time of a sphere with the same volume, cast in the same material and mold? (Hint: V/A for a sphere with radius r is r/3).
    *   Cube V/A = 100 / 6 = 16.67 mm.
    *   Let $T_s = K (V/A)^2$. So, $10 = K (16.67)^2$, which gives $K = 10 / (16.67)^2 \approx 0.036$.
    *   For a sphere with the same volume, find the radius. $V_{cube} = 100^3 = 10^6 \text{ mm}^3$. $V_{sphere} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.
    *   $10^6 = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \implies r^3 = \frac{3 \times 10^6}{4\pi} \approx 238732 \implies r \approx 62.03 \text{ mm}$.
    *   V/A for sphere = $r/3 = 62.03 / 3 \approx 20.68 \text{ mm}$.
    *   $T_{sphere} = K (V/A)^2 \approx 0.036 \times (20.68)^2 \approx 15.35 \text{ minutes}$.
    *   **(Answer: Approximately 15.35 minutes)**

### 10. Defects in Castings

Defects are undesirable features that reduce the quality, strength, or appearance of the casting.

**Common Defects and Their Causes:**

1.  **Gas Holes/Porosity:**
    *   **Appearance:** Spherical or elongated cavities filled with gas.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Dissolved gases in the molten metal being released during solidification.
        *   Insufficient mold permeability, trapping mold gases.
        *   Moisture in the mold or core.
        *   Low pouring temperature.
        *   Turbulent pouring.

2.  **Shrinkage Cavities:**
    *   **Appearance:** Irregular cavities formed due to volume contraction during solidification.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Inadequate feeding by the riser.
        *   Improper riser design or placement.
        *   Use of chills in incorrect locations.
        *   Sections of the casting solidifying at different rates without proper feeding.

3.  **Cold Shuts:**
    *   **Appearance:** A line on the surface where two streams of molten metal met but did not fuse properly.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Molten metal streams losing too much heat before they meet.
        *   Low pouring temperature.
        *   Low fluidity of the metal.
        *   Gating system not designed to ensure proper fusion.

4.  **Misrun:**
    *   **Appearance:** The casting fails to fill the mold completely.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Insufficient pouring of molten metal.
        *   Low pouring temperature.
        *   Low fluidity of the metal.
        *   Insufficient gating.
        *   Mold cavity is too complex or has thin sections.

5.  **Inclusions (Sand, Slag, Oxides):**
    *   **Appearance:** Foreign particles trapped within the casting.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Erosion of the mold or core walls by the molten metal stream.
        *   Turbulent pouring, carrying mold particles into the cavity.
        *   Failure to skim slag or oxides from the molten metal.
        *   Use of dirty pouring ladles or crucibles.

6.  **Cracks (Hot Tears and Cold Tears):**
    *   **Hot Tears:** Occur during solidification when the casting is still partially liquid and weak, and is subjected to tensile stress.
        *   **Causes:** Restricted contraction during cooling, especially at corners or junctions of varying section thickness. Improper pattern allowances.
    *   **Cold Tears:** Occur during cooling below the solidification temperature due to external stresses or internal stresses from uneven cooling.
        *   **Causes:** Machining stresses, handling stresses.

7.  **Distortion/Warping:**
    *   **Appearance:** The casting is not dimensionally straight or as per the intended shape.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Uneven cooling and contraction.
        *   Internal stresses built up during cooling.
        *   Improper removal of cores or casting from the mold.
        *   Pattern design without distortion allowances.

8.  **Scabs:**
    *   **Appearance:** A roughly cylindrical piece of sand that has been incorporated into the surface of the casting.
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Poor mold ramming, creating weak spots.
        *   Low mold strength, allowing molten metal to penetrate into the mold.
        *   Low mold permeability, trapping gases that push sand into the metal.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Pattern allowances** are crucial for producing accurate castings. Shrinkage allowance is the most significant.
*   **Chvorinov's rule** is fundamental for understanding solidification and designing risers. The V/A ratio is key.
*   A properly designed **gating system** controls metal flow, minimizes turbulence, and prevents defects. The sprue is the choke.
*   **Risers** must solidify last to feed shrinkage. Their size and placement are critical.
*   **Chills** accelerate solidification in specific areas to promote directional solidification towards the riser.
*   **Mold properties** (permeability, strength, refractoriness) directly impact casting quality.
*   Understanding the **causes of defects** is essential for implementing preventive measures.
