# THERMAL ENGINEERING LAB-1: Module 18 - Experimental Study of Dropwise and Filmwise Condensation

## Topic: Experimental Study of Dropwise and Filmwise Condensation

This module delves into the experimental investigation of two fundamental modes of condensation: dropwise and filmwise. Understanding the differences in heat transfer performance between these modes is crucial for optimizing the design and efficiency of various thermal systems.

---

### 1. Introduction to Condensation

**Definition:** Condensation is the phase transition of a substance from a gaseous state into a liquid state. In thermal engineering, it typically refers to the condensation of a vapor on a cooled surface.

**Importance:** Condensation occurs in numerous applications, including:
*   **Power Generation:** Condensers in steam power plants and nuclear reactors.
*   **Refrigeration and Air Conditioning:** Evaporators and condensers in HVAC systems.
*   **Chemical Processing:** Distillation columns and heat exchangers.
*   **Electronics Cooling:** Dissipating heat from electronic components.

**Driving Force:** The primary driving force for condensation is the temperature difference between the vapor and the cooled surface. This difference leads to heat transfer from the vapor to the surface, causing it to condense.

---

### 2. Modes of Condensation

When a vapor comes into contact with a cooled surface, it can condense in one of two primary modes:

#### 2.1. Filmwise Condensation

*   **Description:** In filmwise condensation, the condensed liquid forms a continuous, smooth film that spreads over the entire cooled surface. The vapor condenses at the liquid-vapor interface, and heat is transferred through the liquid film to the cooled surface.
*   **Mechanism:** The liquid film acts as a thermal resistance. As the film grows thicker, the resistance to heat transfer increases, leading to a lower heat transfer coefficient.
*   **Visual Appearance:** A continuous, often opaque or translucent liquid layer covering the surface.
*   **Heat Transfer Coefficient:** Generally lower compared to dropwise condensation due to the insulating effect of the liquid film.

**Reference (Cengel, 4th Ed., Chapter 10):** Cengel discusses film condensation on various surfaces, including vertical plates, cylinders, and spheres. He presents analytical solutions for the heat transfer coefficients, emphasizing the role of fluid properties and geometric parameters.

**Example:**
*   Condensation of steam on a cold, clean, untreated vertical copper tube.

#### 2.2. Dropwise Condensation

*   **Description:** In dropwise condensation, the condensed liquid forms discrete droplets on the cooled surface. These droplets grow and eventually merge and slide off the surface due to gravity or surface tension effects.
*   **Mechanism:** Heat transfer occurs primarily through the bare surface areas between the droplets and at the base of the droplets where direct contact with the cooled surface is maintained. This mode exhibits significantly higher heat transfer coefficients than filmwise condensation.
*   **Visual Appearance:** Numerous small, spherical or hemispherical droplets scattered across the surface.
*   **Heat Transfer Coefficient:** Much higher than filmwise condensation because the liquid is removed efficiently, exposing fresh surface areas for condensation.

**Reference (Cengel, 4th Ed., Chapter 10):** Cengel highlights the significantly higher heat transfer coefficients achieved with dropwise condensation and discusses the challenges in maintaining this mode over extended periods due to surface degradation.
