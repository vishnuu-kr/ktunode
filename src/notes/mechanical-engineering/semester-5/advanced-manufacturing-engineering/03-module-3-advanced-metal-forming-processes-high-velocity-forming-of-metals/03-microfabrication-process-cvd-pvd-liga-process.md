# ADVANCED MANUFACTURING ENGINEERING

## Module 3: Advanced Metal Forming Processes: High Velocity Forming of Metals

### Topic: Microfabrication Processes: CVD, PVD, LIGA Process

---

**Introduction:**

This module delves into advanced metal forming processes, with a specific focus on microfabrication techniques. While the broader context is high-velocity forming, this topic specifically introduces three key microfabrication processes: Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD), Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD), and the LIGA process. These processes are crucial for creating intricate micro-scale structures and coatings, enabling advancements in fields like microelectronics, MEMS (Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems), and optics.

---

### 1. Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)

**Definition:**
CVD is a versatile microfabrication technique used to deposit thin films of various materials onto a substrate through a chemical reaction of gaseous precursors. The reaction typically occurs at elevated temperatures, leading to the formation of a solid film on the substrate surface.

**Key Concepts & Principles:**

*   **Precursors:** Gaseous chemical compounds containing the elements to be deposited.
*   **Reaction Chamber:** A controlled environment where the deposition occurs.
*   **Substrate:** The material onto which the thin film is deposited.
*   **Chemical Reactions:** The core of CVD, involving the decomposition and recombination of precursor gases to form the desired solid material.
*   **Mass Transport:** Movement of precursor gases from the bulk gas phase to the substrate surface.
*   **Surface Reactions:** Adsorption of precursors onto the surface, diffusion, chemical reaction, and desorption of by-products.
*   **Heat and Mass Transfer:** Crucial for controlling deposition rate, film quality, and uniformity.

**Types of CVD:**

*   **Atmospheric Pressure CVD (APCVD):** Operates at atmospheric pressure. Simple but can lead to gas-phase reactions and poor uniformity.
*   **Low-Pressure CVD (LPCVD):** Operates at reduced pressure. Reduces gas-phase reactions, leading to better uniformity and conformality. Commonly used in semiconductor manufacturing.
*   **Plasma-Enhanced CVD (PECVD):** Uses plasma to break down precursor gases at lower temperatures. Allows deposition on temperature-sensitive substrates.
*   **Metal-Organic CVD (MOCVD):** Uses metal-organic precursors. Widely used for depositing compound semiconductors and advanced materials.
*   **Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD):** A self-limiting ALD process that deposits films one atomic layer at a time, offering excellent conformality and thickness control.

**Process Steps (General):**

1.  **Substrate Preparation:** Cleaning and preparing the substrate surface.
2.  **Loading:** Placing the substrate into the reaction chamber.
3.  **Precursor Introduction:** Introducing the gaseous precursors into the chamber.
4.  **Deposition:** Chemical reaction and film formation on the substrate.
5.  **By-product Removal:** Venting or purging the chamber to remove gaseous by-products.
6.  **Unloading:** Removing the deposited substrate.

**Applications:**

*   Deposition of silicon nitride (SiN), silicon dioxide (SiO2), polysilicon in semiconductor manufacturing.
*   Hard coatings (e.g., TiN, Al2O3) for cutting tools and wear-resistant components. (Reference: *Advanced Machining Processes* by Jain V.K.)
*   Deposition of diamond and diamond-like carbon (DLC) for optical and electronic applications.
*   Fabrication of MEMS devices.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   CVD is a chemical process, so precursor purity and reaction chemistry are critical.
*   Temperature, pressure, and gas flow rates are key process parameters.
*   Conformality (ability to deposit uniformly on complex geometries) is a significant advantage of CVD, especially LPCVD and ALD.

### 2. Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD)

**Definition:**
PVD is a group of vacuum deposition methods used to deposit thin solid films onto a substrate. The material to be deposited is physically vaporized in a vacuum and then condenses onto the substrate surface.

**Key Concepts & Principles:**

*   **Vacuum Environment:** Essential to prevent contamination and ensure a clear path for vaporized material.
*   **Source Material:** The solid material to be deposited.
*   **Vaporization Mechanism:** Physical means of converting the source material into a vapor phase.
*   **Transport:** Movement of vaporized atoms/molecules from the source to the substrate.
*   **Condensation:** Deposition of vaporized species onto the substrate surface to form a solid film.
*   **Nucleation and Growth:** The initial stages of film formation, where small clusters (nuclei) form and grow into a continuous film.

**Types of PVD:**

*   **Evaporation:**
    *   **Thermal Evaporation:** The source material is heated to its evaporation temperature in a vacuum.
        *   *Resistive Heating:* Using a resistive filament.
        *   *Electron Beam Evaporation (E-beam):* Using a focused electron beam to vaporize the source. Offers higher purity and better control.
    *   **Flash Evaporation:** Rapid evaporation of a material by heating it quickly.
*   **Sputtering:**
    *   **Diode Sputtering:** Two electrodes with the substrate placed on the anode.
    *   **Triode Sputtering:** Adds a third electrode to control the plasma.
    *   **Magnetron Sputtering:** Uses magnetic fields to confine the plasma and increase deposition rates and ion bombardment.
        *   *DC Sputtering:* For conductive targets.
        *   *RF Sputtering:* For non-conductive targets.
    *   **Reactive Sputtering:** Introducing reactive gases (e.g., oxygen, nitrogen) into the sputtering chamber to deposit compound films (e.g., nitrides, oxides).

**Process Steps (General):**

1.  **Substrate Preparation:** Cleaning and preparing the substrate surface.
2.  **Loading:** Placing the substrate into the vacuum chamber.
3.  **Vacuum Pump-down:** Evacuating the chamber to the desired vacuum level.
4.  **Vaporization:** Energizing the source material to vaporize it.
5.  **Deposition:** Vaporized material condenses on the substrate.
6.  **Unloading:** Removing the deposited substrate after venting the chamber.

**Applications:**

*   **Decorative Coatings:** Gold, chrome, titanium nitride (TiN) for watches, jewelry, automotive parts.
*   **Optical Coatings:** Anti-reflective coatings, filters, mirrors.
*   **Wear-Resistant Coatings:** TiN, TiAlN for cutting tools and molds. (Reference: *Advanced Machining Processes* by Jain V.K.)
*   **Electrical Contacts:** Thin films of gold, platinum for electrical connectors.
*   **Semiconductor Manufacturing:** Deposition of metals like aluminum, copper, tungsten.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   PVD processes generally occur at lower temperatures than CVD.
*   PVD films can be less conformal than CVD films, especially in deep trenches or complex geometries.
*   The purity of the source material and the vacuum level are crucial for film quality.
*   Sputtering offers better control over film composition and stoichiometry, especially with reactive sputtering.

### 3. LIGA Process

**Definition:**
LIGA is a microfabrication process that combines X-ray lithography, electrodeposition, and molding to create high-aspect-ratio microstructures with excellent precision and surface finish. The name LIGA is derived from the German words Lithographie (Lithography), Galvanik (Electrodeposition), and Abformung (Molding).

**Key Concepts & Principles:**

*   **X-ray Lithography:** Utilizes high-energy X-rays for pattern transfer. Offers high resolution and deep penetration, enabling the creation of high-aspect-ratio structures.
    *   **Mask:** A patterned absorber on a transparent membrane, used to define the microstructure.
    *   **Photoresist:** A material that changes its solubility when exposed to X-rays. Typically, PMMA (Polymethyl methacrylate) is used as the resist.
*   **Electrodeposition (Galvanik):** Used to fill the patterned resist mold with a conductive material, typically metals like nickel, copper, or alloys.
*   **Molding (Abformung):** The metal part is demolded from the electroplated structure. The resist can be removed by dissolution. The electroplated mold can then be used for subsequent replication via injection molding or hot embossing.

**Process Steps:**

1.  **X-ray Lithography:**
    *   A substrate (often conductive, e.g., silicon) is coated with a thick layer of X-ray-sensitive resist (e.g., PMMA).
    *   The resist is exposed to high-energy X-rays through a patterned mask.
    *   The exposed or unexposed regions (depending on the resist type) are selectively removed by a developer, creating a mold in the resist.
2.  **Electrodeposition (Galvanik):**
    *   The developed resist mold is immersed in an electroplating bath.
    *   A conductive material (e.g., nickel) is electroplated into the cavities of the resist mold.
    *   The electroplating process continues until the desired metal thickness is achieved, forming a free-standing metal structure or a metal shell over the resist.
3.  **Molding (Abformung) & Replication:**
    *   The resist mold is removed by dissolution, leaving the electroformed metal structure.
    *   This electroformed metal structure can serve as a mold itself.
    *   The metal mold can then be used for mass replication of microstructures using techniques like:
        *   **Injection Molding:** Molten plastic is injected into the metal mold.
        *   **Hot Embossing:** A heated plastic material is pressed into the metal mold.
        *   **Hot Stamping:** A heated plastic foil is pressed into the metal mold.

**Materials Used:**

*   **Resist:** PMMA (for X-ray lithography), sometimes SU-8 for UV lithography alternatives.
*   **Electroplated Materials:** Nickel (Ni), Copper (Cu), Nickel-Iron alloys (NiFe), Gold (Au).
*   **Substrates:** Silicon (Si), Quartz, Glass.
*   **Molded Materials:** Polymers (e.g., PEEK, PC, PMMA, POM), sometimes ceramics or composites.

**Advantages:**

*   **High Aspect Ratios:** Capable of producing structures with very high aspect ratios (height to width ratio).
*   **High Precision and Resolution:** Excellent dimensional accuracy and fine features.
*   **Excellent Surface Finish:** The electroplated surfaces are typically very smooth.
*   **Material Diversity:** Allows fabrication of microstructures in various metals and replication in polymers.
*   **Mass Production:** Enables cost-effective mass production through replication molding.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Expensive Equipment:** Requires specialized and costly X-ray lithography equipment and synchrotrons.
*   **Complex Process:** Multi-step process requiring careful control.
*   **Resist Limitations:** PMMA can be difficult to handle and has limited chemical resistance.
*   **Mask Fabrication:** Complex and costly mask fabrication.

**Applications:**

*   **MEMS Devices:** Micro-actuators, sensors, micro-fluidic channels.
*   **Medical Devices:** Micro-needles, surgical tools, micro-filters.
*   **Optical Components:** Micro-lenses, gratings, diffractive optical elements.
*   **Micro-gears and Micro-motors:** Precision micro-mechanical components.
*   **Inkjet Printheads:** Precision nozzles.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   LIGA is a hybrid process combining lithography, electrodeposition, and molding.
*   X-ray lithography is the critical patterning step for achieving high aspect ratios.
*   Electrodeposition forms the primary metal structure.
*   Replication molding is key for mass production.

### Practice Questions and Exercises:

**Question 1:**
Compare and contrast CVD and PVD in terms of their underlying mechanisms, typical operating conditions, and applications. Which process would be more suitable for depositing a highly conformal coating into a deep trench? Justify your answer.

**Answer:**
*   **Mechanisms:** CVD relies on chemical reactions of gaseous precursors. PVD involves the physical vaporization and condensation of solid materials.
*   **Operating Conditions:** CVD typically involves elevated temperatures and gaseous reactants. PVD is performed in a vacuum with energy input to vaporize the source material.
*   **Applications:** CVD is used for films like SiO2, SiN, polysilicon. PVD is used for metal films, hard coatings.
*   **Conformal Coating:** CVD, particularly LPCVD or ALD, is more suitable for highly conformal coatings into deep trenches due to the gas-phase diffusion and surface reaction mechanisms that allow uniform coverage, whereas PVD's line-of-sight deposition can lead to shadowing effects.

**Question 2:**
Describe the key stages of the LIGA process. What are the main advantages and disadvantages of using X-ray lithography in this process?

**Answer:**
*   **Key Stages:** X-ray Lithography (patterning resist), Electrodeposition (filling the mold), Molding/Replication (mass production).
*   **Advantages of X-ray Lithography:** High resolution, deep penetration enabling high aspect ratios, minimal diffraction effects.
*   **Disadvantages of X-ray Lithography:** Requires expensive synchrotron radiation sources, complex mask fabrication, potential for radiation damage to the resist.

**Question 3:**
A micro-gear with a thickness of 100 micrometers and a gear tooth width of 20 micrometers is to be fabricated. Which of the discussed microfabrication processes (CVD, PVD, LIGA) would be most appropriate, and why? Consider the aspect ratio.

**Answer:**
The micro-gear has an aspect ratio of 100 µm / 20 µm = 5. While other processes could potentially achieve this, the **LIGA process** is particularly well-suited for fabricating micro-mechanical components like gears with high precision and high aspect ratios.
*   **LIGA:** Its X-ray lithography step allows for deep penetration, creating the 100 µm thickness with vertical walls, and electrodeposition can precisely fill these trenches to form the gear teeth. The replication molding step also allows for mass production of these gears.
*   **CVD/PVD:** While CVD can deposit films, creating a 100 µm thick solid gear directly through deposition would be extremely slow and potentially difficult to control aspect ratios precisely compared to LIGA. PVD, due to its line-of-sight nature, would struggle to fill the gaps between teeth uniformly at such an aspect ratio without significant shadowing.

**Question 4:**
Explain the role of plasma in PECVD and sputtering processes.

**Answer:**
*   **PECVD:** Plasma is used to ionize and dissociate the precursor gases. This allows the chemical reactions to occur at lower substrate temperatures than thermal CVD, making it suitable for temperature-sensitive substrates. The energized species in the plasma contribute to film deposition.
*   **Sputtering:** Plasma (typically a noble gas like Argon) is used to generate energetic ions. These ions bombard the target material, physically knocking atoms off its surface. These sputtered atoms then travel to the substrate and condense to form the film.

### Important Points to Remember Summary:

*   **CVD:** Chemical reaction of gases, good conformality, versatile for various materials.
*   **PVD:** Physical vaporization and condensation in vacuum, line-of-sight deposition, good for metals and hard coatings.
*   **LIGA:** Hybrid process (X-ray lithography, electrodeposition, molding), ideal for high-aspect-ratio, high-precision microstructures, enables mass production.
*   Understanding process parameters is crucial for achieving desired film properties and microstructure dimensions in all these techniques.
*   These microfabrication processes are foundational for MEMS, microelectronics, and other advanced technologies.
