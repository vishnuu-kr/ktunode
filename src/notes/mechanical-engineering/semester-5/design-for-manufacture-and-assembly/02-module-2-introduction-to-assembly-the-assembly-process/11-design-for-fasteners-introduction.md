# Module 2: Introduction to Assembly: The Assembly Process - Design for Fasteners: Introduction

## 1. Introduction to Fasteners in Product Assembly

Fasteners are critical components used to join or secure two or more parts together in a product assembly. Their selection and design significantly impact the ease, cost, and reliability of the assembly process. This section introduces the fundamental role of fasteners in Design for Manufacture and Assembly (DFMA).

### Key Concepts:

*   **Fastener:** A mechanical device that joins or affixes two or more objects together.
*   **Assembly:** The process of putting together the individual components of a product to create a functional whole.
*   **DFMA (Design for Manufacture and Assembly):** A design philosophy that emphasizes designing products to be easy and cost-effective to manufacture and assemble.

### Importance of Fasteners in DFMA:

*   **Assembly Time & Cost:** The number, type, and complexity of fasteners directly influence the time and cost required for assembly.
*   **Ease of Handling:** The design of fasteners should facilitate easy manipulation and insertion by assemblers (manual or automated).
*   **Tooling Requirements:** Different fasteners require specific tools, which can add to complexity and cost if not managed effectively.
*   **Disassembly:** In some cases, fasteners are designed to allow for easy disassembly for maintenance or repair.
*   **Reliability:** Properly selected and applied fasteners ensure the structural integrity and longevity of the product.

## 2. Types of Fasteners and Their Assembly Considerations

A wide variety of fasteners are available, each with its own advantages and disadvantages in terms of assembly. Understanding these differences is crucial for effective DFMA.

### Common Types of Fasteners:

1.  **Screws:**
    *   **Threaded Fasteners:** Engage with pre-tapped holes or nuts.
    *   **Self-Tapping Screws:** Create their own threads as they are driven into a material.
        *   *Thread-Cutting Screws:* Have a cutting flute.
        *   *Thread-Forming Screws:* Deform the material to create threads.
    *   **Common Screw Types:** Machine screws, wood screws, sheet metal screws, cap screws.
    *   **Head Types:** Phillips, Slotted, Torx, Hex, Socket Head.

2.  **Bolts:** Similar to screws but typically used with a nut. They are usually longer and have a heavier duty design.

3.  **Nuts:** Used in conjunction with bolts or screws to create a threaded connection.
    *   **Common Nut Types:** Hex nuts, lock nuts, wing nuts.

4.  **Rivets:** Permanent fasteners that deform to create a mechanical lock.
    *   **Solid Rivets:** Require access to both sides for hammering.
    *   **Blind Rivets (Pop Rivets):** Can be installed from one side.

5.  **Pins:** Used for alignment or to prevent relative movement.
    *   **Dowel Pins:** Precision cylindrical pins for alignment.
    *   **Spring Pins:** Split or coiled pins that provide spring tension.
    *   **Cotter Pins:** Used to secure nuts or other fasteners.

6.  **Snap-Fit Fasteners:** Integral plastic features designed to flex and snap into place, eliminating the need for separate fasteners.

### Assembly Considerations for Different Fastener Types:

*   **Screws/Bolts:**
    *   **Handling:** Easy to grip and orient.
    *   **Insertion:** Requires alignment of threads.
    *   **Driving:** Requires appropriate tools (screwdrivers, wrenches).
    *   **Torquing:** Can be torqued to specific specifications for secure connections.
    *   **Disassembly:** Generally easy to disassemble by unscrewing.

*   **Rivets:**
    *   **Handling:** Can be fiddly, especially small rivets.
    *   **Insertion:** Requires alignment of holes.
    *   **Setting:** Requires specialized tooling (rivet guns).
    *   **Disassembly:** Difficult, often requiring drilling out.

*   **Pins:**
    *   **Handling:** Generally easy to handle.
    *   **Insertion:** Requires precise hole alignment.
    *   **Securing:** Some pins (e.g., cotter pins) require additional fastening steps.

*   **Snap-Fit Fasteners:**
    *   **Handling:** Integrated into the part.
    *   **Assembly:** Often a simple push or snap action.
    *   **Disassembly:** Can be difficult or impossible without damage.

## 3. Design Guidelines for Fastener Selection and Application (Relating to CO1 & CO2)

Effective fastener design in DFMA focuses on minimizing the number of fasteners, simplifying their handling and insertion, and ensuring the most appropriate type is chosen for the application.

### Key Design Principles:

1.  **Minimize Fastener Usage:**
    *   **Combine Parts:** Design components to reduce the number of separate parts requiring fastening.
    *   **Integrate Features:** Utilize snap-fits, living hinges, or interlocking features where possible to eliminate fasteners.
    *   **Part Consolidation:** Design single parts that perform the function of multiple parts previously held together by fasteners.

2.  **Standardize Fasteners:**
    *   **Use Common Sizes and Types:** Employ standard metric or imperial fasteners to reduce inventory and tooling costs.
    *   **Limit Variety:** Restrict the number of different fastener types and sizes used within a single product.

3.  **Simplify Handling and Insertion:**
    *   **Lead-in Chamfers:** Provide generous chamfers on screw heads, bolt heads, and mating holes to guide them into place.
    *   **Self-Aligning Features:** Design features that help align the fastener with its mating hole automatically.
    *   **Avoid Fumbling:** Choose fasteners that are easy to grip and orient. For manual assembly, avoid tiny or fiddly fasteners. For automated assembly, ensure feeders can reliably present the fasteners.
    *   **Thread Engagement:** Ensure sufficient thread engagement for a secure connection without excessive tightening.

4.  **Ease of Driving/Securing:**
    *   **Appropriate Drive Types:** Select drive types (e.g., Torx, Phillips) that are less prone to cam-out (slipping) for improved driving efficiency and reduced damage.
    *   **Access for Tools:** Ensure adequate clearance for driving tools to reach the fastener head.
    *   **Avoid Special Tools:** Minimize the use of fasteners requiring specialized or single-purpose tools.

5.  **Consider the Assembly Environment:**
    *   **Manual vs. Automated Assembly:** Design fasteners differently based on whether the assembly will be performed manually or by robots.
    *   **Torquing Requirements:** If specific torque is required, select fasteners and design the joint to facilitate this.

6.  **Disassembly Requirements:**
    *   **Serviceability:** If the product needs to be disassembled for maintenance or repair, choose fasteners that allow for easy removal.
    *   **Permanent Assembly:** For products that are not intended for disassembly, permanent fasteners like rivets or welds might be more appropriate.

### Examples:

*   **Snap-fit:** A plastic cover that snaps onto a base using integrated tabs and slots, eliminating the need for screws. (References: Molloy, Tilley, Warman)
*   **Standardization:** Using only M4 socket head cap screws throughout a product to simplify tool selection and inventory. (References: Boothroyd, Dewhurst, Knight)
*   **Lead-in:** A tapered end on a screw to help it enter a pre-tapped hole more easily. (References: Boothroyd)
*   **Tool Access:** Designing the spacing between parts to allow a standard screwdriver or wrench to fit comfortably around a screw or bolt head. (References: Boothroyd, Dewhurst, Knight)

## 4. Fasteners in Specific Manufacturing Processes (Relating to CO3, CO4, CO5 - indirectly)

While this topic is primarily about the *assembly* aspect of fasteners, their interaction with manufacturing processes is crucial. The design of the parts to be fastened must consider the manufacturing method.

### Fasteners and Machining (CO3):

*   **Hole Preparation:** Holes for screws, bolts, or pins need to be machined (drilled, reamed).
    *   **Hole Location and Tolerance:** Precise location and tolerance are critical for fastener alignment.
    *   **Hole Finish:** The surface finish of the hole affects thread engagement and pin fit.
    *   **Counterboring/Countersinking:** Machining operations to recess screw heads or create a conical seat for countersunk screws.
*   **Tapping:** Creating internal threads for screws.
    *   **Tapping Operations:** Designing parts that are easily tapped, considering tap drill sizes and thread forms.
    *   **Thread Quality:** Ensuring good thread quality for reliable fastening.

### Fasteners and Casting/Injection Moulding (CO4):

*   **Bosses and Inserts:**
    *   **Bosses:** Protrusions designed to provide thickness for threading or mounting. Design considerations include draft angles and avoiding sink marks.
    *   **Threaded Inserts:** Pre-formed metallic threads that are moulded into plastic parts. This provides stronger and more durable threads than direct moulding.
        *   *Design:* Inserts should have features that prevent rotation and pull-out.
*   **Hole Moulding:** Holes can be moulded directly, but often require secondary operations for precise thread engagement.
*   **Snap-fit Features:** These are often integral to plastic parts manufactured via injection moulding, directly addressing assembly without fasteners.

### Fasteners and Welding (CO5 - indirectly):

*   **Fastener Selection with Welded Joints:** While welding itself is a joining method, fasteners might be used in conjunction with welded components.
    *   **Access:** Ensure fasteners used in welded assemblies have adequate access for driving and removal.
    *   **Heat Distortion:** Consider how welding heat might affect fasteners or the joint integrity.
*   **Dissimilar Materials:** If welding dissimilar materials, fasteners might be a more reliable joining method for certain combinations.

### Textual References:

*   **Engineering Design: A Materials and Processing Approach (Dieter):** Provides a comprehensive understanding of material properties and processing methods, which informs the design of features for fasteners in relation to machining and moulding.

## 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
According to DFMA principles, what is the primary goal when designing for fasteners?

**Answer:**
The primary goal is to **minimize the number of fasteners used** and to ensure that the fasteners selected are **easy to handle, orient, and insert** during the assembly process, thereby reducing assembly time and cost.

**Question 2:**
List three design features that can improve the ease of manual assembly of screws.

**Answer:**
1.  **Lead-in chamfers:** On the screw threads and the mating hole to guide insertion.
2.  **Appropriate drive type:** For example, Torx or Phillips heads are less prone to cam-out than slotted heads.
3.  **Sufficient clearance for tools:** To allow easy access for screwdrivers or wrenches.

**Question 3:**
Explain the concept of fastener standardization and its importance in DFMA.

**Answer:**
Fastener standardization means using a limited number of common fastener types, sizes, and drive systems within a product. This is important because it:
*   Reduces the variety of tools required for assembly.
*   Simplifies inventory management.
*   Potentially lowers purchasing costs due to higher volume of common items.
*   Reduces the learning curve for assemblers.

**Question 4:**
What is a "boss" in the context of plastic parts for fastening, and what is a key design consideration when moulding a boss for a screw?

**Answer:**
A "boss" is a cylindrical protrusion on a plastic part that provides extra thickness for a threaded feature (like a screw or insert) or for mounting. A key design consideration when moulding a boss for a screw is to **ensure sufficient wall thickness** around the threaded hole to prevent cracking and provide adequate thread engagement. Also, considerations for draft angles and avoiding sink marks are important.

**Question 5:**
Why might a designer choose a snap-fit feature over a screw for joining two plastic parts?

**Answer:**
A designer might choose a snap-fit feature over a screw to:
*   **Eliminate separate components:** The snap-fit features are integral to the plastic parts, reducing part count.
*   **Reduce assembly time:** Snap-fits typically involve a simple push or snap action, which is much faster than inserting and driving screws.
*   **Lower cost:** Eliminating fasteners and reducing assembly labor can significantly reduce overall product cost.
*   **Aesthetics:** Snap-fits can often provide a cleaner aesthetic compared to exposed screw heads.

## 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Fasteners are a primary contributor to assembly cost and time.** Minimizing their use is paramount in DFMA.
*   **Standardization of fasteners is key** to reducing tooling, inventory, and assembly complexity.
*   **Ease of handling and insertion** should be a primary consideration in fastener selection and hole design.
*   **Lead-in features (chamfers)** significantly improve the success rate and speed of fastener insertion.
*   **Tool access** is critical for both manual and automated assembly.
*   **Consider the entire product lifecycle:** If disassembly is required, choose fasteners that facilitate it.
*   **Integrate fastening features** into the primary components whenever possible (e.g., snap-fits).
*   **Design holes and mating features** with the specific manufacturing process in mind (machining, moulding).
