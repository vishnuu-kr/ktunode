### 1. Introduction to Sand Casting

Sand casting is one of the oldest and most widely used metal casting processes. It involves creating a mold from compacted sand, into which molten metal is poured. Once the metal solidifies, the sand mold is broken away to reveal the finished casting.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Casting:** A manufacturing process where a molten material is poured into a mold, which contains a hollow cavity of the desired shape, and then allowed to solidify.
*   **Mold:** A hollow form or cavity into which molten material is poured to create a desired shape.
*   **Pattern:** A replica of the final part, slightly larger to account for shrinkage during solidification and machining.
*   **Sand Mold:** A mold created from sand, often mixed with a binder (like clay or a chemical binder) to hold the sand grains together.

**Referenced Textbooks/Concepts:**

*   **Boothroyd, Dewhurst, Knight (2010):** Emphasizes the importance of understanding core manufacturing processes like casting for effective DFMA. They highlight how casting can produce complex shapes but requires careful design to minimize defects and facilitate assembly.

*   **Molloy, Tilley, Warman (1998):** Would frame sand casting within a broader context of manufacturability, focusing on how the process limitations and capabilities influence product design choices.

### 2. The Sand Casting Process

The sand casting process can be broken down into several key stages:

#### 2.1. Pattern Making

*   **Purpose:** To create an exact replica of the desired part, with allowances for shrinkage, machining, and draft.
*   **Materials:** Wood, metal (aluminum, steel), plastic, or even wax for investment casting (though this is a different mold type, the pattern concept is similar).
*   **Types of Patterns:**
    *   **One-piece (or loose) patterns:** Simple shapes, suitable for small production runs where ease of handling is paramount. Requires a split mold.
    *   **Two-piece patterns:** The most common type. The pattern is split along the parting line of the mold. Each half is placed in a separate half of the flask.
    *   **Match-plate patterns:** A pattern that is mounted on a plate with two sides, allowing for simultaneous molding of both halves of the mold. Efficient for higher production.
    *   **Gated patterns:** A pattern that includes the gating system (sprue, runner, gate) as part of the pattern assembly. Simplifies mold making.

**Design Considerations for Patterns:**

*   **Parting Line:** The line where the two halves of the mold meet. Should be placed in a location that minimizes the complexity of the mold and casting, and avoids undercuts.
*   **Draft:** A slight taper on vertical surfaces to allow for easy removal of the pattern from the mold and the casting from the mold. Typically 1-2 degrees.
*   **Allowance:**
    *   **Shrinkage Allowance:** Patterns are made larger than the final casting to compensate for contraction as the metal cools. This varies by metal.
    *   **Machining Allowance:** Extra material is added to surfaces that will be machined to achieve final dimensions and surface finish.
    *   **Distortion Allowance:** For parts prone to warping during cooling.

#### 2.2. Core Making (If required)

*   **Purpose:** To create internal cavities or passages within the casting.
*   **Materials:** Sand mixed with binders. Cores are typically baked to achieve strength.
*   **Placement:** Cores are placed within the mold cavity before the molten metal is poured.

**Design Considerations for Cores:**

*   **Support:** Cores need to be adequately supported to prevent sagging or breaking under the pressure of molten metal. Core prints (extensions of the core that sit in recesses in the mold) are used for this.
*   **Ventilation:** Cores must be vented to allow gases to escape during casting.
*   **Smoothness:** A smooth surface finish on cores reduces the risk of casting defects and facilitates core removal.

#### 2.3. Mold Making

*   **Flask:** A rigid frame that holds the molding sand. Typically consists of two parts: the **cope** (top half) and the **drag** (bottom half).
*   **Molding Sand:**
    *   **Composition:** Silica sand (SiO2), clay (e.g., bentonite) as a binder, moisture, and other additives.
    *   **Properties:** Good collapsibility (breaks down easily after casting), permeability (allows gases to escape), strength (holds its shape), thermal stability, and refractoriness (resists high temperatures).
*   **Compaction (Ramming):** The sand is compacted around the pattern to give the mold its strength. This can be done manually or using mechanical rammers.
*   **Pattern Removal:** Once rammed, the pattern is carefully removed, leaving the mold cavity.
*   **Core Placement:** If cores are used, they are positioned in the mold cavity.
*   **Mold Assembly:** The cope and drag halves of the mold are closed and secured.

**Design Considerations for Mold Making:**

*   **Parting Line:** Crucial for ease of mold separation and casting removal.
*   **Runner System:** The channel through which molten metal flows from the sprue to the mold cavity. It should be designed to minimize turbulence and erosion of the mold.
*   **Gate:** The opening where the runner connects to the mold cavity. It controls the flow rate of molten metal.
*   **Sprue:** The vertical channel through which molten metal is poured into the mold. It should be designed to prevent turbulence and aspiration of air.
*   **Risers:** Reservoirs of molten metal that feed the casting as it solidifies, compensating for shrinkage and preventing porosity.

#### 2.4. Melting and Pouring

*   **Melting:** The metal is melted in a furnace (e.g., cupola, induction furnace, electric arc furnace) to the appropriate pouring temperature.
*   **Pouring:** Molten metal is transferred to the mold cavity through the sprue. The pouring rate is critical to avoid turbulence and incomplete filling.

#### 2.5. Shakeout and Finishing

*   **Shakeout:** After solidification and cooling, the mold is broken apart (shaken out) to release the casting.
*   **Cleaning:** Excess metal (gates, risers) is removed, and the casting is cleaned (e.g., shot blasting, grinding) to remove sand and achieve the desired surface finish.
*   **Inspection:** The casting is inspected for defects.

### 3. Advantages and Disadvantages of Sand Casting

#### 3.1. Advantages:

*   **Versatility:** Can produce parts of almost any size and complexity.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Relatively low tooling costs, especially for low-volume production. Sand molds are inexpensive to create.
*   **Wide Range of Materials:** Suitable for casting a wide variety of ferrous and non-ferrous metals.
*   **Intricate Shapes:** Can produce complex internal and external geometries.
*   **Large Components:** Ideal for casting very large and heavy parts.

#### 3.2. Disadvantages:

*   **Surface Finish:** Typically results in a rougher surface finish compared to other casting methods (e.g., die casting).
*   **Dimensional Accuracy:** Lower dimensional accuracy and tighter tolerances are achievable compared to precision casting methods.
*   **Production Rate:** Generally a slower process, especially for high-volume production.
*   **Labor Intensive:** Can be labor-intensive, particularly for mold making.
*   **Porosity and Defects:** Susceptible to internal defects like porosity, shrinkage, and gas entrapment if not properly controlled.

### 4. Design Considerations for Sand Castings (Relating to DFMA)

Sand casting offers significant design freedom, but to maximize efficiency and minimize costs and defects, specific design guidelines should be followed. This directly aligns with **CO4: Design and improve parts for better casting and injection moulding.**

#### 4.1. Section Thickness

*   **Uniformity:** Maintain uniform section thickness as much as possible. Variations in thickness can lead to differential cooling rates, causing stress, warping, and shrinkage defects.
*   **Transition:** If thickness changes are unavoidable, transition gradually (e.g., using fillets) to avoid stress concentrations.
*   **Recommended Range:** Generally, section thicknesses between 3mm and 25mm are ideal. Thicker sections require careful design of feeders and chilling to ensure proper solidification.

**Example:** Instead of a sharp transition from a thin rib to a thick base, use a generous fillet radius.

#### 4.2. Draft Angles

*   **Purpose:** To facilitate the removal of the pattern from the sand mold and the casting from the mold.
*   **Application:** Required on all surfaces perpendicular to the parting line.
*   **Typical Values:** 1-2 degrees is common. Vertical surfaces may require more draft if they are deep or have rough surface finishes.

**Importance for DFMA:** Proper draft reduces the risk of mold erosion and casting damage during ejection, simplifying handling and downstream processes.

#### 4.3. Parting Line Location

*   **Impact:** Affects mold complexity, potential for flash (metal escaping between mold halves), and casting surface finish.
*   **Guidelines:**
    *   Place it on a flat surface if possible.
    *   Avoid locating it on intricate features or areas requiring high precision.
    *   Minimize the surface area of the parting line to reduce flash.
    *   Consider how the part will be assembled and handled after casting.

**Importance for DFMA:** An optimal parting line simplifies mold assembly, reduces the need for post-casting cleanup (reducing assembly effort), and can improve dimensional control.

#### 4.4. Avoid Undercuts

*   **Definition:** Undercuts are features that prevent the direct removal of the pattern from the mold.
*   **Solutions:**
    *   **Cores:** Use cores to create internal undercuts.
    *   **Loose Pieces:** Employ loose pieces in the pattern that are removed separately from the mold.
    *   **Redesign:** If possible, redesign the part to eliminate undercuts.

**Importance for DFMA:** Undercuts significantly increase mold complexity and cost. Eliminating or designing for them with simple core/loose piece mechanisms reduces manufacturing time and potential assembly issues.

#### 4.5. Ribs and Fillets

*   **Ribs:** Used to increase the strength of thin sections.
    *   **Design:** Ribs should be thinner than the main section they are supporting (typically 50-80% of the base thickness) to avoid hot spots and shrinkage.
    *   **Attachment:** Join ribs to the main body with fillets.
*   **Fillets:** Curved transitions between surfaces.
    *   **Purpose:** Reduce stress concentrations and improve metal flow.
    *   **Size:** Use generous fillet radii where possible.

**Importance for DFMA:** Proper rib and fillet design enhances structural integrity while avoiding casting defects, leading to more reliable parts that may require less rework before assembly.

#### 4.6. Gates and Risers

*   **Gates:** Designed to allow molten metal to flow into the mold cavity.
    *   **Types:** Top gates, bottom gates, horn gates, finger gates.
    *   **Considerations:** Minimize turbulence, erosion, and oxide formation.
*   **Risers:** Act as reservoirs of molten metal to compensate for shrinkage during solidification.
    *   **Placement:** Should be placed to feed the last areas to solidify.
    *   **Size:** Sized appropriately to remain molten longer than the casting.

**Importance for DFMA:** Proper gating and risering are critical for casting quality. Poor design can lead to significant defects requiring extensive post-casting repair, impacting assembly.

#### 4.7. Tolerance and Surface Finish

*   **Limitations:** Sand casting generally has looser tolerances and rougher surface finishes compared to other methods.
*   **Design Implications:**
    *   If tight tolerances or smooth surfaces are required, design for subsequent machining or grinding operations.
    *   Account for machining allowance.
    *   Consider how the surface finish will affect function (e.g., sealing surfaces).

**Importance for DFMA:** Understanding these limitations upfront allows designers to specify appropriate allowances and choose secondary operations that are cost-effective, minimizing assembly challenges due to mismatched parts.

### 5. Sand Casting in the Context of DFMA

Sand casting is a foundational process in DFMA because it allows for the creation of complex geometries that might be difficult or expensive to achieve with other methods. However, to maximize its DFMA benefits:

*   **Part Consolidation:** Sand casting can often consolidate multiple components into a single cast part, reducing the number of assembly operations and potential failure points. This aligns with **CO2: Apply the knowledge of General design principles for manufacturability.**
*   **Simplicity of Assembly:** By designing cast parts with features that facilitate easy assembly (e.g., integral locating features, appropriate tolerances), the overall assembly time and cost can be reduced. This relates to **CO1: Apply the knowledge of Design Guidelines for Manual Assembly.**
*   **Material Selection:** Choosing the right metal for sand casting, considering its castability and mechanical properties, is crucial for both manufacturability and the performance of the assembled product.
*   **Machining Integration:** Design for sand casting should always consider the subsequent machining operations needed to achieve final dimensions and surface finishes. This ties into **CO3: Design and improve parts for better machinability.**

### 6. Key Points to Remember

*   Sand casting is a versatile and cost-effective process for complex and large parts.
*   Key elements include pattern, mold, molten metal, and solidification.
*   Design considerations like uniform thickness, draft, parting line location, and avoidance of undercuts are crucial for efficient manufacturing.
*   Proper gating and risering are essential for casting quality.
*   Sand casting generally produces rougher surfaces and looser tolerances than other methods, often requiring secondary operations.
*   In DFMA, sand casting's strength lies in part consolidation and its ability to create complex shapes that facilitate simpler assembly.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Explain the purpose of draft in sand casting and provide typical values. Why is draft important for DFMA?

**Answer:** Draft is the taper given to vertical surfaces of a pattern to facilitate its removal from the sand mold without damaging the mold cavity. Typical values are 1-2 degrees. It's important for DFMA because it prevents mold erosion and casting damage during ejection, leading to simpler and faster handling, reduced rework, and fewer assembly issues related to damaged parts.

**Question 2:** What are the main advantages of sand casting compared to die casting?

**Answer:**
*   **Cost:** Lower tooling costs, making it suitable for low-volume production.
*   **Size and Complexity:** Can produce larger and more complex shapes than die casting.
*   **Material Variety:** Wider range of metals can be sand cast.
*   **Intricate Internal Features:** Easier to create complex internal cavities using cores.

**Question 3:** A designer is creating a cast aluminum bracket. The bracket has a main body with integral ribs for stiffness. What design consideration regarding the ribs should the designer address to prevent casting defects?

**Answer:** The designer should ensure that the ribs are thinner than the main body of the bracket (typically 50-80% of the base thickness). This prevents the ribs from acting as hot spots, which can lead to differential cooling and shrinkage defects in the thicker sections. Also, generous fillets should be used where the ribs meet the main body.

**Question 4:** Identify two potential design features that would increase the cost and complexity of a sand-cast part.

**Answer:**
1.  **Undercuts:** Features that prevent the direct removal of the pattern from the mold, requiring the use of cores or loose pieces, thereby increasing mold complexity and cost.
2.  **Sharp Corners/Abrupt Changes in Thickness:** These can lead to stress concentrations, poor metal flow, and differential cooling, potentially causing defects like hot tears and shrinkage porosity, requiring more intricate gating/risering or leading to higher scrap rates.

**Question 5:** How can sand casting contribute to part consolidation in product design, and what DFMA benefits does this offer?

**Answer:** Sand casting allows for the creation of intricate shapes and the integration of multiple features (like mounting points, bosses, and reinforcing ribs) into a single cast component. This can replace assemblies that would otherwise require multiple separate parts to be manufactured and then joined. The DFMA benefits of part consolidation include:
*   Reduced assembly time and labor costs.
*   Fewer fasteners or joining operations.
*   Improved product rigidity and reduced weight.
*   Fewer potential points of failure (e.g., fewer welds or joints).
*   Simplified inventory management.
