## DESIGN OF MACHINE ELEMENTS: Module 3: Bearings and Lubrication: Introduction to Lubrication

---

### **1. Introduction to Lubrication**

**1.1 Definition of Lubrication**
Lubrication is the process of applying a lubricant between two moving surfaces in contact to reduce friction, wear, and heat generation. It aims to create a film of lubricant that separates the moving parts, preventing direct metal-to-metal contact.

**1.2 Purpose and Functions of Lubrication**
*   **Reduce Friction:** Minimizes the resistance to motion between surfaces, leading to reduced power loss and improved efficiency.
*   **Reduce Wear:** Prevents direct contact between asperities of mating surfaces, thereby minimizing abrasive, adhesive, and fatigue wear.
*   **Remove Heat:** Lubricants absorb heat generated by friction and dissipate it away from the contact surfaces, preventing overheating and thermal expansion issues.
*   **Prevent Corrosion:** Lubricants form a protective barrier on metal surfaces, preventing contact with moisture and corrosive agents.
*   **Provide Sealing:** In some applications, lubricants can seal gaps between moving parts, preventing the ingress of contaminants like dust and dirt, and the egress of working fluids.
*   **Transmit Power:** In some hydrostatic lubrication systems, the lubricant itself can be used to transmit power.
*   **Dampen Shock and Noise:** The viscous nature of lubricants can absorb some of the shock and noise generated by the operation of machinery.

**1.3 Types of Lubrication Systems**
Based on the degree of separation between moving surfaces, lubrication systems are classified as:

*   **Boundary Lubrication:** Occurs when the lubricant film is very thin, and direct metal-to-metal contact is possible. Surface irregularities are in intermittent contact. This is common during start-up, shutdown, and at low speeds/high loads.
    *   **Key Concept:** Relies on the chemical properties of lubricant additives (e.g., anti-wear agents) that form protective films on the surfaces.
    *   **Example:** Manual greasing of a plain bearing during assembly.
*   **Mixed Lubrication (or Partial Lubrication):** A transition state between boundary and hydrodynamic lubrication where both thin lubricant films and some asperity contact exist.
    *   **Key Concept:** A combination of the characteristics of boundary and hydrodynamic lubrication.
*   **Hydrodynamic Lubrication:** Occurs when a continuous film of lubricant completely separates the moving surfaces. The lubricant is carried into the converging gap between the surfaces, creating a pressurized film that supports the load.
    *   **Key Concept:** Requires relative motion between surfaces and a specific geometry (converging wedge). The lubricant viscosity is crucial.
    *   **Example:** A journal bearing operating at sufficient speed.
*   **Elastohydrodynamic Lubrication (EHL):** Similar to hydrodynamic lubrication but occurs in highly loaded, relatively compliant contacts (e.g., rolling element bearings, gears). The high pressure in the lubricant film causes elastic deformation of the surfaces, contributing to the film formation.
    *   **Key Concept:** Involves elastic deformation of the mating surfaces due to high contact pressures.
    *   **Example:** Rolling contact between ball and raceway in a ball bearing.
*   **Hydrostatic Lubrication:** The lubricant is supplied to the bearing under external pressure by a pump, creating a film that completely separates the surfaces, even at zero relative motion.
    *   **Key Concept:** Load is supported by pressurized fluid supplied externally.
    *   **Example:** High-precision machine tool spindles.

### **2. Fundamentals of Friction and Wear**

**2.1 Friction**
Friction is the force that opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact. It is primarily caused by molecular adhesion and mechanical interlocking of surface asperities.

*   **Types of Friction:**
    *   **Static Friction:** The friction force that must be overcome to initiate motion between two surfaces at rest.
    *   **Kinetic Friction (Sliding Friction):** The friction force that opposes motion when surfaces are sliding relative to each other.
    *   **Rolling Friction:** The resistance to motion when one surface rolls over another. It is generally much lower than sliding friction.

*   **Laws of Friction (Amontons-Coulomb Laws):**
    1.  The friction force is directly proportional to the normal force between the surfaces.
    2.  The friction force is independent of the apparent area of contact. (This is an approximation; actual behavior can depend on the real area of contact).
    3.  The coefficient of kinetic friction is less than the coefficient of static friction.
    4.  The friction force is independent of the sliding velocity (for typical speeds).

*   **Coefficient of Friction ($\mu$)**: The ratio of the friction force ($F_f$) to the normal force ($F_N$).
    *   $F_f = \mu F_N$

**2.2 Wear**
Wear is the progressive loss of material from the surfaces of a machine element due to relative motion.

*   **Types of Wear:**
    *   **Abrasive Wear:** Occurs when hard particles or hard asperities on one surface plough grooves into a softer surface.
        *   **Example:** Sand or grit entering a plain bearing.
    *   **Adhesive Wear:** Occurs when surfaces are brought into very close contact, leading to the formation of adhesive junctions. These junctions can break, transferring material from one surface to another or causing material removal.
        *   **Example:** Metal-to-metal contact under boundary lubrication.
    *   **Fatigue Wear:** Occurs due to repeated stress cycles, leading to the formation and propagation of cracks, eventually causing material to be removed in the form of flakes or pits. Common in rolling contact.
        *   **Example:** Pitting in rolling element bearings.
    *   **Corrosive Wear:** Occurs when chemical or electrochemical reactions lead to material degradation, which is then removed by mechanical action.
        *   **Example:** Rust formation and subsequent removal from a shaft.

**2.3 Role of Lubricant in Friction and Wear Reduction**
Lubricants reduce friction by:
*   Providing a physical barrier between surfaces.
*   Reducing the shear strength of the interface.

Lubricants reduce wear by:
*   Preventing direct metal-to-metal contact (hydrodynamic and EHL).
*   Forming adsorbed films or chemical layers that are weaker than the base metal (boundary lubrication).
*   Trapping wear particles and preventing them from causing further abrasive wear.

### **3. Properties of Lubricants**

The selection of a lubricant depends on its properties and the operating conditions.

**3.1 Viscosity**
*   **Definition:** The internal resistance of a fluid to flow. It is a measure of the fluid's "thickness."
*   **Importance:** Crucial for forming a separating film in hydrodynamic and EHL lubrication. Higher viscosity generally leads to thicker films but also higher viscous friction.
*   **Measurement:** Typically measured using viscosity units like Saybolt Universal Seconds (SUS), Kinematic Viscosity (e.g., centistokes, cSt), or Dynamic Viscosity (e.g., centipoise, cP).
*   **Viscosity Index (VI):** A measure of how much a lubricant's viscosity changes with temperature.
    *   **High VI:** The viscosity changes less with temperature, making it suitable for a wider operating temperature range.
    *   **Low VI:** The viscosity changes significantly with temperature.
*   **Viscosity-Temperature Relationship:** Lubricant viscosity generally decreases with increasing temperature. (Refer to Bhandari, Chapter 11 for detailed discussion on viscosity-temperature relationships and viscosity index).

**3.2 Viscosity Index Improvers (VIIs)**
*   **Definition:** Additives that are added to lubricants to increase their Viscosity Index. They are polymers that uncoil and thicken the oil at higher temperatures.
*   **Function:** Allow a lubricant to maintain a usable viscosity over a wider temperature range.

**3.3 Pour Point**
*   **Definition:** The lowest temperature at which an oil will flow under specific conditions.
*   **Importance:** Dictates the minimum operating temperature for the lubricant.
*   **Measurement:** Determined by cooling a sample and observing its flow.

**3.4 Flash Point**
*   **Definition:** The lowest temperature at which a lubricant gives off sufficient vapor to ignite momentarily when an external ignition source is applied.
*   **Importance:** Indicates the volatility of the lubricant and the risk of fire. Higher flash points are generally desirable for safety.

**3.5 Fire Point**
*   **Definition:** The lowest temperature at which a lubricant gives off sufficient vapor to ignite and burn continuously when an external ignition source is applied.
*   **Importance:** Related to the flash point, indicating a higher risk of sustained combustion.

**3.6 Cloud Point**
*   **Definition:** The temperature at which wax crystals begin to form and cause the oil to appear cloudy when cooled.
*   **Importance:** Relevant for lubricants used in cold environments.

**3.7 Oxidation Stability**
*   **Definition:** The resistance of a lubricant to degradation due to chemical reactions with oxygen at elevated temperatures.
*   **Importance:** Oxidation leads to increased viscosity, sludge formation, and corrosive acidic byproducts. Lubricants with good oxidation stability last longer. Antioxidant additives are used to improve this property.

**3.8 Oiliness (Lubricity)**
*   **Definition:** The ability of a lubricant to reduce friction and prevent wear under boundary lubrication conditions. This is largely due to the presence of polar additives that adsorb onto the metal surfaces.
*   **Importance:** Critical in situations where a full lubricant film cannot be maintained.

**3.9 Film Strength**
*   **Definition:** The ability of a lubricant to withstand the high pressures at the contact interface without breaking down.
*   **Importance:** Directly related to preventing metal-to-metal contact.

**3.10 Thermal Stability**
*   **Definition:** The resistance of a lubricant to thermal decomposition at high temperatures.
*   **Importance:** Prevents the lubricant from breaking down into undesirable products at elevated operating temperatures.

**3.11 Detergency and Dispersancy**
*   **Detergency:** The ability of a lubricant to keep surfaces clean by preventing deposit formation (sludge, varnish).
*   **Dispersancy:** The ability of a lubricant to keep insoluble contaminants (like soot or wear debris) suspended in the bulk fluid, preventing them from agglomerating and depositing on surfaces.

### **4. Types of Lubricants**

Lubricants are broadly categorized based on their base fluid and consistency.

**4.1 Liquid Lubricants (Oils)**
*   **Definition:** Hydrocarbon oils (mineral oils) or synthetic oils with additives. They provide good lubrication, cooling, and can be easily pumped and filtered.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Mineral Oils:** Derived from crude oil refining. They are cost-effective and widely used.
    *   **Synthetic Oils:** Man-made oils with specific properties, offering superior performance at extreme temperatures, better oxidation resistance, and lower volatility. Examples include polyalphaolefins (PAOs), esters, silicones.
    *   **Vegetable Oils:** Biodegradable and environmentally friendly, but often have limitations in terms of thermal and oxidative stability.
*   **Applications:** Most common type of lubricant, used in engines, gears, bearings, hydraulic systems.

**4.2 Semi-Solid Lubricants (Greases)**
*   **Definition:** A semi-solid lubricant consisting of a base oil, a thickener, and additives. The thickener gives grease its structure and consistency.
*   **Thickeners:**
    *   **Soap Thickeners:** Lithium, calcium, sodium, aluminum soaps.
    *   **Non-Soap Thickeners:** Polyurea, clay, silica gel, etc.
*   **Consistency:** Graded by the National Lubricating Grease Institute (NLGI) number, from 000 (very fluid) to 6 (very stiff).
*   **Advantages:** Remain in place in casings, ideal for lubricating bearings and joints where oil leakage would be a problem.
*   **Disadvantages:** Offer less efficient cooling and are more difficult to filter than oils.
*   **Applications:** Rolling element bearings, chassis lubrication, open gears.

**4.3 Solid Lubricants**
*   **Definition:** Solid materials that reduce friction between surfaces, often used in extreme conditions where liquid or semi-solid lubricants fail.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Graphite:** Excellent at high temperatures and pressures.
    *   **Molybdenum Disulfide (MoS2):** Good under high loads and in vacuum.
    *   **Tungsten Disulfide (WS2):** Similar to MoS2, but better in vacuum.
    *   **PTFE (Teflon):** Very low coefficient of friction, excellent chemical resistance.
    *   **Boron Nitride:** Used in high-temperature applications.
*   **Applications:** High-temperature bearings, vacuum environments, dry lubrication coatings, extreme pressure applications.

### **5. Lubricant Additives**

Additives are chemicals blended into base oils to enhance their properties or impart new ones.

*   **Anti-wear (AW) Agents:** Form protective films on metal surfaces to prevent wear under boundary conditions (e.g., ZDDP, sulfurized olefins).
*   **Extreme Pressure (EP) Additives:** Form protective films under very high loads and temperatures where AW agents fail (e.g., sulfurized fats, chlorinated hydrocarbons).
*   **Antioxidants:** Inhibit the oxidation of the base oil, extending its life (e.g., phenolic antioxidants, aromatic amines).
*   **Rust and Corrosion Inhibitors:** Form protective films on metal surfaces to prevent attack by moisture and acids.
*   **Detergents:** Keep engine parts clean by preventing sludge and varnish formation.
*   **Dispersants:** Keep insoluble contaminants suspended in the oil.
*   **Viscosity Index Improvers (VIIs):** As discussed earlier.
*   **Pour Point Depressants:** Improve the low-temperature flow properties of the lubricant.
*   **Antifoam Agents:** Reduce the tendency of oil to form foam.
*   **Friction Modifiers:** Adjust the friction characteristics of the lubricant.

### **6. Lubrication of Machine Elements (Introduction)**

This section provides a brief overview, with detailed design aspects covered in later modules.

*   **Sliding Contact Bearings (Plain Bearings):**
    *   **Lubrication:** Hydrodynamic, boundary, or mixed lubrication is employed. The lubricant film separates the shaft from the bearing surface.
    *   **Lubrication Methods:**
        *   **Splash Lubrication:** Moving parts dip into an oil bath and splash oil onto other parts.
        *   **Wick Lubrication:** A wick draws oil from a reservoir to the bearing surface.
        *   **Ring Lubrication:** A ring dips into an oil bath and carries oil to the shaft and bearing.
        *   **Pad Lubrication:** Lubricant is supplied to pads that support the journal.
        *   **Forced Lubrication:** Oil is pumped to the bearing under pressure.
    *   **Refer to:** Bhandari, Chapter 11; Norton, Chapter 13.
    *   **Course Outcome Alignment:** CO3 (Develop of Sliding contact bearing for industrial applications).

*   **Rolling Contact Bearings (Ball and Roller Bearings):**
    *   **Lubrication:** Primarily elastohydrodynamic (EHL) lubrication. A thin film of lubricant separates the rolling elements and raceways.
    *   **Lubrication Methods:**
        *   **Grease Lubrication:** Most common for sealed-for-life bearings or where oil leakage is a concern.
        *   **Oil Lubrication:**
            *   **Splash Lubrication:** Used in gearboxes and higher speed applications.
            *   **Circulating Oil Lubrication:** Oil is pumped to the bearing and returned.
            *   **Mist Lubrication:** A fine mist of oil particles is blown into the bearing housing.
    *   **Refer to:** Bhandari, Chapter 10; Norton, Chapter 12.
    *   **Course Outcome Alignment:** CO4 (Choose a suitable Rolling contact bearing from manufacturer’s Catalogue for a specific application).

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Purpose:** Lubrication is crucial for reducing friction, wear, and heat in machine elements.
*   **Types of Lubrication:** Understand the differences between boundary, hydrodynamic, EHL, and hydrostatic lubrication.
*   **Viscosity is Key:** The viscosity of a lubricant is its most critical property for effective lubrication.
*   **Operating Conditions Dictate Choice:** Temperature, load, speed, and environment are key factors in selecting the right lubricant and lubrication system.
*   **Additives Enhance Performance:** Lubricant additives play a vital role in improving performance and durability.
*   **Material Compatibility:** Ensure the lubricant is compatible with the bearing materials and sealing elements.
*   **Proper Maintenance:** Regular replenishment or changing of lubricants is essential for maintaining machine health.

### **8. Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:**
Define lubrication and state its primary functions in machine elements.

**Answer:**
Lubrication is the process of applying a lubricant between two moving surfaces in contact to reduce friction, wear, and heat generation. Its primary functions include:
*   Reducing friction.
*   Minimizing wear.
*   Dissipating heat.
*   Preventing corrosion.
*   Providing a seal.

**Question 2:**
Differentiate between hydrodynamic lubrication and hydrostatic lubrication.

**Answer:**
*   **Hydrodynamic Lubrication:** Relies on the relative motion between surfaces and a converging film geometry to generate a pressurized lubricant film that separates the surfaces. It requires a minimum speed to establish the film.
*   **Hydrostatic Lubrication:** Uses an external pump to supply lubricant under pressure to the bearing clearances, creating a separating film independent of relative motion. It can support loads even at zero speed.

**Question 3:**
Explain the significance of Viscosity Index (VI) for a lubricant.

**Answer:**
Viscosity Index (VI) indicates how much a lubricant's viscosity changes with temperature. A lubricant with a high VI maintains its viscosity better over a wider temperature range, making it suitable for applications with significant temperature variations. A low VI lubricant experiences a larger viscosity change with temperature.

**Question 4:**
List any four common types of lubricant additives and their functions.

**Answer:**
1.  **Anti-wear (AW) Agents:** Prevent wear under boundary lubrication by forming protective films.
2.  **Antioxidants:** Inhibit oil oxidation and degradation.
3.  **Rust Inhibitors:** Protect metal surfaces from rust and corrosion.
4.  **Viscosity Index Improvers (VIIs):** Improve the lubricant's viscosity-temperature characteristics.

**Question 5:**
Which type of lubrication is most prevalent in rolling element bearings, and what is the key lubricant property that enables this type of lubrication?

**Answer:**
The most prevalent type of lubrication in rolling element bearings is Elastohydrodynamic Lubrication (EHL). The key lubricant property enabling this is its **viscosity** and **film strength** (or its ability to form a pressurized film under high loads and deform the surfaces elastically).
