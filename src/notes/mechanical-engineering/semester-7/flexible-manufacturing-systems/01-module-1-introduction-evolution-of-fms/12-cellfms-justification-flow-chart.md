# Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS) - Module 1: Introduction: Evolution of FMS

## Topic: Cell/FMS Justification Flowchart

---

### **1. Introduction to FMS Justification**

Before delving into the flowchart, it's crucial to understand *why* FMS justification is important. Implementing an FMS or a Flexible Manufacturing Cell (FMC) is a significant capital investment. Therefore, a rigorous justification process is essential to ensure that the system aligns with the company's strategic goals, technological capabilities, and financial objectives. This process helps in evaluating the feasibility and potential benefits of adopting such advanced manufacturing technologies.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Justification:** The process of proving the economic and strategic viability of an investment.
*   **FMS/FMC:** Flexible Manufacturing Systems/Cells are highly automated production systems capable of producing a variety of parts with minimal human intervention.
*   **Capital Investment:** The initial funds required to acquire and implement the FMS/FMC.
*   **Return on Investment (ROI):** A performance measure used to evaluate the efficiency of an investment or compare the efficiency of a number of different investments.

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding the basic components and functions of FMS is a prerequisite for justifying its implementation.
*   **CO3 (K3):** While CAD/CAM are part of FMS implementation, the justification process often considers the benefits derived from integrating these technologies.
*   **General:** The entire justification process is about making informed decisions regarding the adoption of FMS, which is fundamental to understanding its role in modern manufacturing.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Luggen (1991):** Likely discusses the business case and justification aspects of FMS adoption.
*   **Maleki (1991):** May offer insights into the financial evaluation and strategic considerations for FMS.

### **2. The Need for a Justification Process**

Traditional justification methods, primarily focused on tangible benefits like direct labor savings, may not fully capture the advantages of FMS. FMS offers intangible benefits that are crucial for long-term competitiveness.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Tangible Benefits:** Measurable financial gains (e.g., reduced direct labor costs, increased throughput).
*   **Intangible Benefits:** Difficult-to-quantify advantages (e.g., improved quality, increased flexibility, faster new product introduction, enhanced customer satisfaction, better employee morale).
*   **Strategic Benefits:** Aligning manufacturing capabilities with long-term business goals (e.g., market share growth, competitive advantage).

**Alignment with Course Outcomes:**

*   **CO1 (K2):** Understanding the functions of FMS helps in identifying both tangible and intangible benefits.
*   **CO2 (K1):** Features like unattended machining contribute to tangible benefits (e.g., 24/7 operation), while overall flexibility supports strategic goals.

### **3. Cell/FMS Justification Flowchart: A Step-by-Step Approach**

A justification flowchart provides a structured way to assess the suitability of an FMS or FMC. While specific flowcharts can vary, the general steps remain consistent.

**General Steps in a Cell/FMS Justification Flowchart:**

1.  **Problem/Opportunity Identification:**
    *   Recognize existing manufacturing bottlenecks, inefficiencies, or market demands for greater flexibility.
    *   **Example:** High lead times for custom parts, inability to efficiently produce small batches of mixed products, or a need to respond quickly to changing customer orders.
    *   **Alignment:** CO1 (K2) - Understanding FMS functions helps identify areas where it can provide solutions.

2.  **Define Objectives and Requirements:**
    *   Clearly state what the proposed FMS/FMC should achieve.
    *   Identify specific part families, production volumes, quality standards, and flexibility requirements.
    *   **Example:** "Reduce average part production time by 30% for a family of 50 different parts, with batch sizes ranging from 10 to 100 units."
    *   **Alignment:** CO1 (K2) - Understanding FMS components and layouts helps in defining these requirements. CO2 (K1) - Requirements like unattended machining fall under this stage.

3.  **Explore Alternatives:**
    *   Consider other manufacturing solutions, including traditional automation, manual methods, or outsourcing.
    *   Evaluate the pros and cons of each alternative against the defined objectives.
    *   **Example:** Comparing FMS with a dedicated transfer line, cellular manufacturing, or even manual machining with advanced CNC machines.
    *   **Alignment:** CO1 (K2) - Understanding FMS capabilities allows for a fair comparison with other systems.

4.  **Develop FMS/FMC Concept:**
    *   Based on objectives, define the scope, components, layout, and functionalities of the proposed FMS/FMC.
    *   This might involve selecting machines, material handling systems, control systems, and software.
    *   **Example:** A cell with 3 CNC machining centers, an automated guided vehicle (AGV) for pallet transport, and a robotic loading/unloading station.
    *   **Alignment:** CO1 (K2) - Directly relates to understanding components and configurations. CO4 (K2) - Focuses on material movement and storage.

5.  **Gather Data and Estimate Costs:**
    *   Collect detailed information on capital costs (machines, software, installation, training).
    *   Estimate operational costs (labor, maintenance, energy, consumables).
    *   **Example:** Quotes for CNC machines, AGVs, software licenses, and projected annual maintenance budgets.
    *   **Alignment:** CO3 (K3) - CAD/CAM integration costs are part of this.

6.  **Quantify Benefits (Tangible & Intangible):**
    *   **Tangible:** Calculate savings from reduced labor, reduced work-in-progress (WIP), improved material utilization, higher throughput, reduced scrap, and lower inventory.
    *   **Intangible:** Assess benefits like improved quality, faster response to market changes, increased machine utilization, better employee skill development, and enhanced competitive positioning. Assign financial values where possible.
    *   **Example:** Reduced setup times, increased OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness), and cost savings from fewer defects.
    *   **Alignment:** CO1 (K2), CO2 (K1) - Understanding FMS features allows for better benefit quantification.

7.  **Perform Financial Analysis:**
    *   Utilize financial metrics to evaluate the investment's attractiveness.
    *   **Key Metrics:**
        *   **Payback Period:** Time required for the cumulative benefits to equal the initial investment.
        *   **Net Present Value (NPV):** The difference between the present value of cash inflows and the present value of cash outflows over a period of time.
        *   **Internal Rate of Return (IRR):** The discount rate at which the NPV of all the cash flows from a particular project equals zero.
        *   **Return on Investment (ROI):** (Net Profit / Cost of Investment) * 100.
    *   **Example:** Calculating that an FMS investment will have an NPV of $1 million over 5 years, with an IRR of 15%.
    *   **Alignment:** General - The financial viability is a core justification element.

8.  **Perform Strategic Analysis:**
    *   Evaluate how the FMS/FMC aligns with the company's overall business strategy.
    *   Consider its impact on competitive advantage, market responsiveness, and long-term growth.
    *   **Example:** Does the FMS enable the company to enter new markets or offer new, complex products?
    *   **Alignment:** CO1 (K2), CO2 (K1) - Understanding the strategic implications of FMS features.

9.  **Risk Assessment:**
    *   Identify potential risks associated with the FMS implementation (e.g., technological obsolescence, integration issues, operator training, market shifts).
    *   Develop mitigation strategies for identified risks.
    *   **Example:** Risk of software incompatibility, potential for machine downtime, or resistance from workforce.
    *   **Alignment:** General - Crucial for any large investment.

10. **Decision Making:**
    *   Based on the financial and strategic analysis, and considering the risks, decide whether to proceed with the FMS/FMC implementation.
    *   **Example:** If financial metrics are favorable and strategic alignment is strong, approve the investment.
    *   **Alignment:** General - The culmination of the justification process.

11. **Implementation & Monitoring (Post-Decision):**
    *   If approved, proceed with implementation.
    *   Continuously monitor performance against the original objectives and financial projections.
    *   **Alignment:** CO3 (K3) - Implementation involves CAD/CAM considerations.

### **4. Key Considerations for FMS Justification**

*   **Focus on Total Cost of Ownership:** Beyond initial purchase price, consider installation, training, maintenance, software upgrades, and eventual decommissioning.
*   **Scenario Planning:** Analyze the FMS performance under different production scenarios (e.g., high demand, low demand, product mix changes).
*   **Benchmarking:** Compare potential FMS performance against industry best practices or competitors.
*   **Long-Term Perspective:** FMS investments are strategic and often have a longer payback period than traditional equipment. Evaluate their contribution to long-term business survival and growth.
*   **Flexibility as an Asset:** Quantify the value of being able to produce new parts or adapt to market changes quickly.

**Highlight Important Points to Remember:**

*   FMS justification requires a blend of financial and strategic analysis.
*   Intangible benefits are often as important as tangible benefits for FMS.
*   A thorough understanding of the FMS's capabilities and limitations is crucial for accurate justification.
*   The flowchart provides a structured approach, but flexibility in its application is often necessary.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1 (CO1, CO3 - K2/K3):**
A company is considering implementing a small Flexible Manufacturing Cell (FMC) for producing a range of precision engine components. List five key benefits (tangible or intangible) that this FMC might offer, and explain how CAD/CAM integration could contribute to one of these benefits.

**Answer:**
*   **Increased Flexibility:** Ability to produce a variety of components with different designs and batch sizes, adapting to changing market demands.
*   **Improved Quality:** Consistent machining accuracy and reduced human error due to automation, leading to lower scrap rates.
*   **Higher Throughput:** Automated material handling and reduced setup times can lead to increased production output.
*   **Reduced Lead Times:** Faster processing and less WIP can shorten the time from order to delivery.
*   **Enhanced Machine Utilization:** Machines can operate for longer periods, potentially unattended, maximizing their productive time.

*   **CAD/CAM Contribution to Benefit (e.g., Increased Flexibility):** CAD designs can be directly linked to CAM software to generate machining programs automatically. This rapid programming capability for new or modified part designs significantly enhances the FMC's ability to switch between different component families, thereby increasing its flexibility.

**Question 2 (CO2 - K1):**
What is the primary difference in justification approach between a traditional, dedicated automation system and a Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS)?

**Answer:**
The primary difference lies in the emphasis on **flexibility** as a quantifiable benefit for FMS. Traditional automation justification focuses heavily on efficiency and cost reduction for producing a *specific* product or a very limited range of products. In contrast, FMS justification must also account for the value of being able to adapt to product variations, produce different part families, and respond to market changes. This often involves valuing intangible benefits like reduced new product introduction time and market responsiveness, which are less relevant for dedicated automation.

**Question 3 (CO1, CO2 - K2/K1):**
Imagine you are presenting an FMS justification proposal. Which financial metrics (from Section 3) would you consider most important to demonstrate the economic viability, and why?

**Answer:**
*   **Net Present Value (NPV):** This is crucial as it accounts for the time value of money, providing a more accurate picture of the project's long-term profitability. A positive NPV indicates that the project is expected to generate more value than its cost.
*   **Internal Rate of Return (IRR):** This metric shows the project's profitability as a rate of return. If the IRR exceeds the company's hurdle rate (minimum acceptable rate of return), the project is considered financially attractive.
*   **Payback Period:** While less sophisticated than NPV or IRR, it's often used for its simplicity and provides a quick understanding of how long it will take to recover the initial investment. It can be important for companies with tight cash flow concerns.
*   **Return on Investment (ROI):** A straightforward measure of profitability, easily understood by stakeholders.

**Question 4 (Exercise):**
A company is evaluating an FMS proposal with the following estimated data:
*   Initial Investment: $5,000,000
*   Annual Tangible Benefits: $1,500,000
*   Annual Intangible Benefits (valued): $500,000
*   Project Lifespan: 5 years
*   Discount Rate: 10%

Calculate the Payback Period and NPV for this investment. (Assume benefits are realized at the end of each year and the initial investment is at Year 0).

**Answer:**
*   **Payback Period:**
    *   Total Annual Benefits = $1,500,000 + $500,000 = $2,000,000
    *   Payback Period = Initial Investment / Total Annual Benefits
    *   Payback Period = $5,000,000 / $2,000,000 = **2.5 years**

*   **Net Present Value (NPV):**
    *   Year 0: -$5,000,000
    *   Year 1: $2,000,000 / (1.10)^1 = $1,818,181.82
    *   Year 2: $2,000,000 / (1.10)^2 = $1,652,892.56
    *   Year 3: $2,000,000 / (1.10)^3 = $1,502,629.60
    *   Year 4: $2,000,000 / (1.10)^4 = $1,366,026.91
    *   Year 5: $2,000,000 / (1.10)^5 = $1,241,842.65

    *   NPV = Sum of Present Values of Benefits - Initial Investment
    *   NPV = ($1,818,181.82 + $1,652,892.56 + $1,502,629.60 + $1,366,026.91 + $1,241,842.65) - $5,000,000
    *   NPV = $7,581,573.54 - $5,000,000 = **$2,581,573.54**
