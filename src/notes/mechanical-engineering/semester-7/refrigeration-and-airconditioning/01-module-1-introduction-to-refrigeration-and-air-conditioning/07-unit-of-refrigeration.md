# Module 1: Introduction to Refrigeration and Air Conditioning
## Topic: Unit of Refrigeration

This topic focuses on defining and understanding the fundamental unit used to quantify the capacity of refrigeration systems.

---

### 1. Introduction to Refrigeration and its Purpose

*   **Refrigeration** is the process of removing heat from a space or substance to maintain it at a temperature lower than its surroundings.
*   **Purpose of Refrigeration:**
    *   Food preservation and storage.
    *   Cooling of beverages and pharmaceuticals.
    *   Comfort cooling in buildings (air conditioning).
    *   Industrial processes (e.g., chemical manufacturing, ice making).
    *   Specialized applications (e.g., medical equipment, scientific research).

---

### 2. Need for a Standard Unit of Refrigeration

*   To compare the performance and capacity of different refrigeration systems objectively.
*   To specify the cooling load required for a particular application.
*   To facilitate trade and standardization in the refrigeration industry.

---

### 3. The Ton of Refrigeration (TR)

The most common unit of refrigeration in practice is the **Ton of Refrigeration (TR)**.

*   **Definition:** One Ton of Refrigeration is defined as the rate of heat removal required to freeze one US ton (2000 pounds) of water at $0^\circ C$ ($32^\circ F$) into ice at $0^\circ C$ ($32^\circ F$) in 24 hours.

*   **Derivation:**
    *   Latent heat of fusion of ice = $334.7 \text{ kJ/kg}$ or $144 \text{ Btu/lb}$.
    *   1 US ton = $2000 \text{ lb}$.
    *   Time = 24 hours.

    *   **In kJ/hr:**
        Heat to be removed = (Mass of water) $\times$ (Latent heat of fusion)
        Heat to be removed = $(2000 \text{ lb} \times 0.453592 \text{ kg/lb}) \times 334.7 \text{ kJ/kg}$
        Heat to be removed $\approx 907.185 \text{ kg} \times 334.7 \text{ kJ/kg}$
        Heat to be removed $\approx 303655 \text{ kJ}$

        Rate of heat removal = $\frac{303655 \text{ kJ}}{24 \text{ hours}} \approx 12652.3 \text{ kJ/hr}$

    *   **In kJ/s (kW):**
        1 TR = $\frac{12652.3 \text{ kJ/hr}}{3600 \text{ s/hr}} \approx 3.5146 \text{ kJ/s}$
        Therefore, **1 TR $\approx 3.517 \text{ kW}$**

    *   **In Btu/hr:**
        Heat to be removed = $(2000 \text{ lb}) \times (144 \text{ Btu/lb})$
        Heat to be removed = $288000 \text{ Btu}$

        Rate of heat removal = $\frac{288000 \text{ Btu}}{24 \text{ hours}} = 12000 \text{ Btu/hr}$

    *   **In Btu/min:**
        1 TR = $\frac{12000 \text{ Btu/hr}}{60 \text{ min/hr}} = 200 \text{ Btu/min}$

*   **Common Equivalents of 1 TR:**
    *   1 TR = $3.517 \text{ kW}$
    *   1 TR = $12,000 \text{ Btu/hr}$
    *   1 TR = $200 \text{ Btu/min}$
    *   1 TR = $503.85 \text{ kcal/hr}$ (since 1 kcal $\approx$ 4.187 kJ)

*   **Origin of the Term "Ton":** Historically, ice was transported in tons, and a refrigeration system's capacity was often compared to its ability to produce the equivalent amount of ice.

---

### 4. Other Units of Refrigeration

While TR is prevalent, other units are also used, especially in different regions or for specific contexts.

*   **Kilowatt (kW):** This is the SI unit for power and is increasingly used as a direct measure of refrigeration capacity.
    *   $1 \text{ kW} \approx 0.2843 \text{ TR}$
    *   $1 \text{ TR} \approx 3.517 \text{ kW}$

*   **British Thermal Unit per Hour (Btu/hr):** Commonly used in the US, especially for smaller systems like residential air conditioners.
    *   $1 \text{ Btu/hr} \approx 0.000083 \text{ TR}$
    *   $1 \text{ TR} = 12,000 \text{ Btu/hr}$

*   **Kilocalorie per hour (kcal/hr):** Used in some parts of the world.
    *   $1 \text{ kcal/hr} \approx 0.00198 \text{ TR}$
    *   $1 \text{ TR} \approx 503.85 \text{ kcal/hr}$

---

### 5. Refrigeration Capacity vs. Power Consumption

*   **Refrigeration Capacity:** The rate at which a system removes heat from the refrigerated space (measured in TR, kW, Btu/hr, etc.). This is the "cooling output."
*   **Power Consumption:** The rate at which the system consumes energy (typically electrical energy for the compressor and fans) to achieve the cooling (measured in Watts, Kilowatts, Horsepower, etc.). This is the "energy input."

*   **Important Note:** Refrigeration capacity is **not** the same as power consumption. A larger TR rating means a greater cooling capacity. The efficiency of the system is determined by the ratio of cooling capacity to power consumption (e.g., Coefficient of Performance - COP).

---

### 6. Application Examples

*   **Domestic Refrigerator:** Typically has a capacity of 0.1 to 0.2 TR.
*   **Residential Air Conditioner:** Commonly rated between 1 to 5 TR.
*   **Commercial HVAC Systems:** Can range from 5 TR to hundreds of TRs.
*   **Large Industrial Chillers:** Can have capacities in the hundreds or even thousands of TRs.

---

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   The Ton of Refrigeration (TR) is the standard unit, representing the rate of heat removal.
*   $1 \text{ TR} \approx 3.517 \text{ kW}$ or $12,000 \text{ Btu/hr}$.
*   Refrigeration capacity is the cooling output, while power consumption is the energy input.
*   Understanding the unit of refrigeration is crucial for system selection, sizing, and performance evaluation.

---

### 8. Practice Questions

1.  **What is the definition of a Ton of Refrigeration (TR)?**
    *   **Answer:** One Ton of Refrigeration is the rate of heat removal equivalent to freezing 2000 lb of water at $32^\circ F$ into ice at $32^\circ F$ in 24 hours.

2.  **Convert a refrigeration capacity of 5 TR into kW.**
    *   **Answer:** $5 \text{ TR} \times 3.517 \text{ kW/TR} = 17.585 \text{ kW}$

3.  **If a residential air conditioner has a capacity of 18,000 Btu/hr, what is its capacity in Tons of Refrigeration?**
    *   **Answer:** $\frac{18000 \text{ Btu/hr}}{12000 \text{ Btu/hr/TR}} = 1.5 \text{ TR}$

4.  **Explain the difference between refrigeration capacity and power consumption.**
    *   **Answer:** Refrigeration capacity is the rate of heat removal (cooling output), while power consumption is the rate of energy used by the system (energy input). They are not the same; efficiency relates them.

5.  **Which unit is most commonly used in India for refrigeration capacity?**
    *   **Answer:** The Ton of Refrigeration (TR).
