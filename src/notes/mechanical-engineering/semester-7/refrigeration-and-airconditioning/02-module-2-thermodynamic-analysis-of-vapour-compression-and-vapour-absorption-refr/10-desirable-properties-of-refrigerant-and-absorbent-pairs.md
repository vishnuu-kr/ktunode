# REFRIGERATION AND AIR CONDITIONING - Module 2: Thermodynamic Analysis of Vapour Compression and Vapour Absorption Refrigeration Systems

## Topic: Desirable Properties of Refrigerant and Absorbent Pairs

---

### 1. Introduction

This topic focuses on the essential properties that make a refrigerant and absorbent pair suitable for use in vapour compression and vapour absorption refrigeration systems. The efficiency, safety, and economic viability of these systems are directly influenced by the selection of appropriate working fluids. This section will delve into the desirable characteristics of both refrigerants and absorbents, as well as the crucial properties of the pairs themselves.

---

### 2. Desirable Properties of Refrigerants

Refrigerants are the working fluids that undergo phase changes (evaporation and condensation) to absorb and reject heat, thereby producing a cooling effect. Their properties significantly impact the performance and operation of vapour compression refrigeration systems.

**2.1 Thermodynamic Properties:**

*   **Suitable Evaporating and Condensing Temperatures:** At given operating pressures, the refrigerant should have evaporating temperatures low enough for the desired refrigeration effect and condensing temperatures high enough for efficient heat rejection to the environment.
    *   **Example:** For a typical household refrigerator, the evaporator temperature might be around -18°C (0°F), while the condenser temperature could be around 55°C (131°F). The refrigerant must possess appropriate saturation pressure-temperature characteristics to achieve these. (Arora C.P., 4th Ed., Ch. 4)
*   **High Latent Heat of Vaporization:** A high latent heat means that a larger amount of heat can be absorbed or rejected during phase change with a smaller mass flow rate of the refrigerant. This leads to higher Coefficient of Performance (COP).
*   **Moderate Specific Volume of Vapor:** A lower specific volume of vapor at the evaporator outlet leads to smaller compressor displacement, reducing initial cost and power consumption.
*   **Good Thermal Conductivity:** High thermal conductivity facilitates efficient heat transfer in the evaporator and condenser.
*   **Non-corrosive:** The refrigerant should not corrode the materials used in the refrigeration system components (pipes, compressor, etc.).
*   **Thermodynamically Stable:** The refrigerant should not decompose or change its chemical composition under operating conditions (temperature and pressure).

**2.2 Physical and Chemical Properties:**

*   **Non-toxic:** Refrigerants should be safe for human contact in case of leakage. While absolute non-toxicity is rare, the level of toxicity should be very low.
*   **Non-flammable and Non-explosive:** For safety reasons, refrigerants should not burn or explode in the presence of air or ignition sources.
*   **Odorless or Slight Odor:** A slight odor can be beneficial in detecting leaks, but strong odors are undesirable.
*   **Chemically Inert:** The refrigerant should not react with lubricating oil or other materials in the system.
*   **Leak Detection:** It should be possible to easily detect leaks. This can be through visual methods, electronic leak detectors, or by adding a tracer dye.

**2.3 Economic and Environmental Properties:**

*   **Low Cost:** The refrigerant should be affordable for widespread use.
*   **Readily Available:** Easy availability ensures that the system can be recharged or repaired without difficulty.
*   **Environmentally Friendly:**
    *   **Zero Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP):** Refrigerants should not contribute to the depletion of the stratospheric ozone layer. (Arora C.P., 4th Ed., Ch. 4)
    *   **Low Global Warming Potential (GWP):** Refrigerants should have a minimal impact on climate change by trapping less heat in the atmosphere. (Ahamadul Ameen, 2020, Ch. 3)
*   **Compatible with Lubricants:** The refrigerant must be miscible or compatible with the lubricating oil used in the compressor to ensure proper lubrication and prevent damage.

**Important Point to Remember:** The trend in refrigerant selection is moving towards those with zero ODP and low GWP due to environmental regulations like the Montreal Protocol and subsequent amendments.

### 3. Desirable Properties of Absorbents

Absorbents are substances that absorb the refrigerant in vapour absorption refrigeration systems (VARS). Their properties are critical for the efficient functioning of the system.

**3.1 Thermodynamic Properties:**

*   **High Affinity for Refrigerant Vapor:** The absorbent must readily absorb the refrigerant vapor at the evaporator pressure and temperature.
*   **Low Vapor Pressure:** The absorbent should have a very low vapor pressure at the generator temperature. This ensures that most of the refrigerant vapor is absorbed and that minimal absorbent vapor is carried over to the condenser.
*   **High Latent Heat of Solution:** A high latent heat of solution (heat released during absorption) is beneficial as it can be rejected to the cooling water in the absorber, reducing the cooling load on the absorber. However, excessive latent heat can make the process difficult to manage.
*   **Low Specific Heat:** A low specific heat of the absorbent solution (especially the strong solution) is desirable to minimize the heat required for pumping and heating in the generator.
*   **Good Thermal Stability:** The absorbent should not decompose or undergo chemical changes at the high temperatures encountered in the generator.

**3.2 Physical and Chemical Properties:**

*   **Non-corrosive:** The absorbent should not corrode the materials of the system components.
*   **Chemically Stable and Inert:** It should not react with the refrigerant or the system materials.
*   **Non-toxic and Non-flammable:** For safety reasons, the absorbent should pose minimal health risks and should not be combustible.
*   **Good Heat Transfer Characteristics:** The absorbent solution should have good thermal conductivity and viscosity to facilitate efficient heat transfer.

**3.3 Economic and Environmental Properties:**

*   **Low Cost:** The absorbent should be inexpensive to make the system economically viable.
*   **Readily Available:** Easy availability ensures the continuous operation of the system.
*   **Environmentally Friendly:** Should not pose significant environmental hazards.

### 4. Desirable Properties of Refrigerant-Absorbent Pairs

The effectiveness of a VARS relies not only on the individual properties of the refrigerant and absorbent but also on their interaction as a pair.

**4.1 Thermodynamic Properties of the Pair:**

*   **Wide Range of Operating Temperatures:** The pair should allow for efficient operation over the desired range of evaporator and generator temperatures.
*   **High Solubility of Refrigerant in Absorbent:** High solubility ensures that a large amount of refrigerant can be absorbed at the lower temperature (evaporator) and easily liberated at the higher temperature (generator).
*   **Low Solubility of Absorbent in Refrigerant:** This prevents the absorbent from being carried over with the refrigerant vapor to the condenser, which would reduce the efficiency of the system.
*   **Favorable Pressure-Temperature-Concentration (P-T-x) Relationship:** The P-T-x diagram for the pair should allow for the desired evaporation and generation pressures at practical temperatures.

**4.2 Physical and Chemical Properties of the Pair:**

*   **Non-reactive:** The refrigerant and absorbent should not react chemically with each other.
*   **Compatible with Lubricants:** If a lubricant is used, it should be compatible with both the refrigerant and the absorbent.
*   **Low Viscosity:** Low viscosity of the solution is desirable to reduce pumping power and improve heat transfer.
*   **Non-foaming:** Foaming can reduce the efficiency of the absorber and potentially lead to carryover of liquid to the compressor in some variations.

**4.3 Economic and Environmental Properties of the Pair:**

*   **Low Overall Cost:** The combined cost of the refrigerant and absorbent should be reasonable.
*   **Safety:** The pair should be safe to handle and operate.

### 5. Examples of Refrigerant-Absorbent Pairs

The most common and successful refrigerant-absorbent pair used in VARS is **Lithium Bromide (LiBr) - Water**.

*   **Refrigerant: Water**
    *   **Advantages:** Non-toxic, non-flammable, environmentally friendly (zero ODP, zero GWP), low cost, readily available. High latent heat.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Freezing point is 0°C. This limits its use for applications requiring sub-zero temperatures. High vapor pressure at common refrigeration temperatures.
*   **Absorbent: Lithium Bromide (LiBr)**
    *   **Advantages:** High affinity for water vapor, low vapor pressure, good thermal stability.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Corrosive, especially at higher temperatures and in the presence of air. Can crystallize if the concentration becomes too high, leading to system blockage. Requires additives (e.g., chromium chromate) to mitigate corrosion.

Another common pair, particularly for lower temperature applications (air conditioning and domestic refrigeration), is **Ammonia (NH₃) - Water**.

*   **Refrigerant: Ammonia (NH₃)**
    *   **Advantages:** Excellent thermodynamic properties, high latent heat, zero ODP and GWP. Efficient for cooling.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Toxic and has a pungent odor. Flammable and explosive within certain limits. Corrosive to copper and brass, requiring the use of steel components.
*   **Absorbent: Water**
    *   **Advantages:** Non-toxic, non-flammable, readily available, low cost.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Its vapor pressure is significant at the generator temperature, leading to some refrigerant loss. It has a lower affinity for ammonia compared to LiBr for water.

**Reference:** (Arora S.C. & Domkundwar, 2018, Ch. 9)

### 7. Answers to Practice Questions

**Multiple Choice Questions:**

1.  **b) High latent heat of vaporization** (This allows for more heat transfer per unit mass.)
2.  **b) Non-toxic and non-flammable** (Safety is paramount.)
3.  **b) Absorbent** (It absorbs the refrigerant, which is water in this case.)
4.  **c) Zero ODP and low GWP** (To minimize environmental impact.)

**Short Answer Questions:**

1.  Four desirable thermodynamic properties of a refrigerant are:
    *   Suitable evaporating and condensing temperatures at operating pressures.
    *   High latent heat of vaporization.
    *   Moderate specific volume of vapor.
    *   Good thermal conductivity.
    *   (Others: Non-corrosive, thermodynamically stable)

2.  A low vapor pressure of the absorbent is important to ensure that most of the refrigerant vapor is absorbed in the absorber and that minimal absorbent vapor is carried over to the condenser. This separation of fluids is crucial for efficient operation.

3.  **Advantages of water as a refrigerant:** Non-toxic, non-flammable, environmentally friendly (zero ODP, zero GWP), low cost, readily available, high latent heat.
    **Disadvantages of water as a refrigerant:** High freezing point (0°C), limiting its use for sub-zero cooling. High vapor pressure at typical refrigeration temperatures, requiring high-pressure components.

4.  **ODP (Ozone Depletion Potential):** Measures a substance's ability to deplete the stratospheric ozone layer. Refrigerants with zero ODP are preferred to protect the ozone layer.
    **GWP (Global Warming Potential):** Measures a substance's contribution to the greenhouse effect and climate change. Refrigerants with low GWP are preferred to mitigate global warming.

**Problem Solving (Conceptual):**

1.  For an evaporator temperature of -10°C, water has a very high vapor pressure (around 2.87 mbar). This would require a very large volume of water vapor for a given mass flow rate, leading to large and inefficient compressors in a vapour compression system. Furthermore, operating at -10°C with water would mean the system is operating below its freezing point, posing a significant risk of ice formation and system blockage. Ammonia, on the other hand, has a more suitable vapor pressure at -10°C (around 2900 mbar or 2.9 bar) which is manageable for a vapour compression system.
    In a LiBr-Water absorption system operating at -10°C, the low vapor pressure of water at this temperature is beneficial for the absorption process. However, the primary challenge with LiBr-Water is its high freezing point of 0°C for the water refrigerant. Therefore, this pair is unsuitable for applications requiring sub-zero temperatures. For sub-zero applications, Ammonia-Water is a more appropriate choice as Ammonia's freezing point is very low.

### 9. Key Takeaways

*   The selection of refrigerants and absorbents is critical for the efficiency, safety, and environmental impact of refrigeration and air conditioning systems.
*   Refrigerants must possess suitable thermodynamic, physical, chemical, and environmental properties.
*   Absorbents must have high affinity for the refrigerant, low vapor pressure, and good thermal stability.
*   Refrigerant-absorbent pairs are evaluated based on their combined properties and compatibility.
*   Environmental regulations are driving the adoption of refrigerants with zero ODP and low GWP.
*   LiBr-Water and Ammonia-Water are common refrigerant-absorbent pairs, each with its own advantages and limitations.
