## Topic: Combustion Chamber Geometry and Arrangements

### **1. Introduction to Combustion Chambers**

The combustion chamber (also known as the combustor or burner) is a critical component of a gas turbine engine where the fuel is mixed with compressed air and burned to produce a high-temperature, high-pressure gas stream. This high-energy gas then expands through the turbine to produce work.

*   **Primary Function:** To burn fuel efficiently and completely at high temperatures while maintaining stable combustion and producing a uniform temperature profile for the turbine inlet.
*   **Key Considerations:**
    *   Complete combustion of fuel.
    *   Minimization of pressure drop.
    *   Stable combustion over a wide range of operating conditions.
    *   Uniform temperature distribution at the turbine inlet.
    *   Low emissions of pollutants (e.g., NOx, CO, unburnt hydrocarbons).
    *   Durability and reliability of the chamber and its components.

**(Refer to Ganesan, Chapter 7: Combustion Chambers)**

### **2. Classification of Combustion Chambers**

Combustion chambers are broadly classified based on their physical arrangement and design principles. The main types are:

*   **Can Combustor (Tubular Combustor):**
    *   **Description:** Consists of a series of individual cylindrical combustion chambers, each with its own fuel injector and igniter. These are housed within a common casing.
    *   **Arrangement:** Typically 6-8 individual cans arranged circumferentially around the engine axis.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Simpler to design and manufacture.
        *   Easier to remove and replace individual cans for maintenance.
        *   Good flame stability.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Can be heavier and bulkier than other types.
        *   Potential for uneven temperature distribution at the turbine inlet due to the discrete nature of the cans.
        *   Higher pressure losses can occur due to the multiple entry points into the turbine casing.
    *   **Application:** Older and smaller gas turbines, turbo-prop engines.

*   **Annular Combustor:**
    *   **Description:** A single, continuous annular chamber formed between the compressor discharge casing and an inner liner. Fuel injectors and igniters are distributed along the annular space.
    *   **Arrangement:** Occupies the space between the compressor and turbine, surrounding the engine axis.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   More compact and lighter than can combustors.
        *   More uniform temperature distribution at the turbine inlet, leading to improved turbine life.
        *   Lower pressure losses due to a single flow path.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   More complex to design and manufacture.
        *   Maintenance can be more challenging as the entire chamber may need to be accessed.
        *   Potential for flame instability if not carefully designed.
    *   **Application:** Modern high-performance gas turbines, turbo-jet, and turbofan engines.

*   **Cannular (Segmented Annular) Combustor:**
    *   **Description:** A compromise between can and annular designs. It consists of a number of individual combustion tubes or cans that are arranged circumferentially within a single annular casing. Each can has its own liner and fuel nozzle.
    *   **Arrangement:** Individual cans within an annular casing.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Combines some of the advantages of both can and annular designs, offering better temperature uniformity than can combustors and easier maintenance than annular combustors.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Still more complex than can combustors.
    *   **Application:** Intermediate designs, often found in older or medium-sized engines.

### **3. Key Features and Design Considerations of Combustion Chambers**

Regardless of the type, all combustion chambers share common design features and must address specific challenges.

#### **3.1. Airflow Zones within the Combustor**

The compressed air entering the combustion chamber is divided into different zones to facilitate the combustion process.

*   **Primary Zone:**
    *   **Function:** Where the majority of fuel is injected and primary combustion occurs. Requires a high proportion of the total air.
    *   **Characteristics:** High temperature, high turbulence for good mixing.
    *   **Air Requirement:** Typically 15-25% of the total air.

*   **Secondary Zone:**
    *   **Function:** Where additional air is introduced to complete the combustion of any unburnt fuel and to dilute the hot combustion gases to a temperature suitable for the turbine inlet.
    *   **Characteristics:** Lower temperature, continued mixing.
    *   **Air Requirement:** Typically 25-35% of the total air.

*   **Dilution Zone:**
    *   **Function:** Further dilution of the combustion products with the remaining air to achieve the desired turbine inlet temperature (TIT).
    *   **Characteristics:** Lowest temperature, controlled mixing.
    *   **Air Requirement:** Typically 40-60% of the total air.

**(Refer to Ganesan, Chapter 7: Combustion Chambers)**

#### **3.2. Components of a Combustion Chamber**

*   **Outer Casing:**
    *   **Function:** Encloses the entire combustion process and contains the high-pressure gases.
    *   **Material:** Typically made of high-strength steel alloys capable of withstanding high temperatures and pressures.

*   **Liner (Combustion Liner/Can):**
    *   **Function:** The inner shell where combustion actually takes place. It protects the outer casing from direct exposure to the extreme heat.
    *   **Material:** Made of high-temperature resistant alloys (e.g., Inconel) with cooling features.
    *   **Cooling:** Essential to prevent the liner from melting. Common cooling methods include:
        *   **Liner Cooling:** Airflow through holes or slots in the liner walls (effusion cooling, transpiration cooling).
        *   **Thermal Barrier Coatings (TBCs):** Ceramic coatings applied to the liner surface to reduce heat transfer into the metal.

*   **Fuel Injectors (Nozzles):**
    *   **Function:** To atomize the fuel and inject it into the combustion zone in a fine spray for efficient mixing with air.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Atomizing Nozzles:** Use pressure or air to break liquid fuel into fine droplets.
        *   **Gas Injectors:** For gaseous fuels.
    *   **Arrangement:** Positioned at the front of the combustion chamber.

*   **Igniter:**
    *   **Function:** Initiates combustion at startup. Typically an electrical spark igniter.
    *   **Operation:** Only operates during engine start-up; once combustion is stable, it is turned off.

*   **Swirler:**
    *   **Function:** Induces swirl in the incoming air, promoting efficient mixing of fuel and air in the primary zone, which leads to stable combustion and complete burning.
    *   **Placement:** Located at the front of the liner.

*   **Stiffening Rings:**
    *   **Function:** Provide structural support to the liner against pressure forces and thermal stresses.

*   **Flame Tube/Liner Holes:**
    *   **Primary Air Holes:** For initial mixing and combustion.
    *   **Secondary Air Holes:** For dilution and further combustion.
    *   **Tertiary Air Holes:** For dilution and cooling of the liner.
    *   **Cooling Air Holes:** For transpiration cooling of the liner.

**(Refer to Ganesan, Chapter 7: Combustion Chambers, Yahya, Chapter 8: Combustion Chambers)**

### **4. Combustion Process and Flame Stabilization**

Achieving stable and efficient combustion requires careful design to ensure proper mixing of fuel and air and to maintain the flame within the chamber.

#### **4.1. Flame Stabilization Mechanisms**

*   **Recirculation Zones:**
    *   **Mechanism:** Swirling the air and/or using bluff bodies (e.g., a flameholder at the front of the liner) creates recirculation zones where the combustion gases are mixed with fresh reactants. This preheats the reactants and provides a pocket of hot gases to re-ignite any unburnt fuel, thus stabilizing the flame.
    *   **Example:** Swirlers at the front of the liner create a central recirculation zone.

*   **Fuel-Air Ratio Control:**
    *   **Mechanism:** Maintaining the fuel-air ratio within the flammability limits of the mixture is crucial. The division of air into primary, secondary, and dilution zones helps manage this. The primary zone operates at a rich or near-stoichiometric mixture for efficient burning, while the secondary and dilution zones lean out the mixture to control temperature and ensure complete combustion.

*   **Residence Time:**
    *   **Mechanism:** The time the fuel-air mixture spends within the combustion zone must be sufficient for complete combustion to occur. The volume of the chamber and the flow rate of air determine the residence time.

#### **4.2. Combustion Efficiency**

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the actual heat released by the fuel to the theoretical heat that could be released if the fuel burned completely.
*   **Factors Affecting Efficiency:**
    *   Fuel atomization and mixing quality.
    *   Air-fuel ratio.
    *   Residence time.
    *   Temperature and pressure.
    *   Turbulence.
*   **Target:** High combustion efficiency (typically > 98%) is desired for optimal engine performance and fuel economy.

**(Refer to Ganesan, Chapter 7: Combustion Chambers, Mathur & Sharma, Chapter 11: Combustion Chambers)**

### **5. Turbine Inlet Temperature (TIT) and its Impact**

*   **Definition:** The temperature of the gases entering the first stage of the turbine.
*   **Significance:** TIT is a critical parameter that directly impacts the engine's power output and thermal efficiency. Higher TIT generally leads to higher performance.
*   **Limitation:** The maximum TIT is limited by the metallurgical capabilities of the turbine blades and nozzle guide vanes, which are exposed to the highest temperatures.
*   **Role of Dilution Air:** The large amount of dilution air introduced in the secondary and dilution zones is essential for reducing the combustion products' temperature to a level that the turbine can withstand.
*   **Temperature Profile:** A uniform temperature profile across the turbine inlet is desirable to prevent localized overheating of turbine components, which can lead to premature failure. Annular combustors generally provide a more uniform profile than can combustors.

**(Refer to Ganesan, Chapter 7: Combustion Chambers, Cohen, Chapter 7: Combustion Chambers)**

### **6. Emissions Control**

Modern gas turbine combustion chambers are designed with emission control as a major consideration.

*   **Major Pollutants:**
    *   **Nitrogen Oxides (NOx):** Formed at high temperatures due to the reaction of nitrogen and oxygen in the air.
    *   **Carbon Monoxide (CO):** Produced by incomplete combustion, especially at rich fuel-air ratios or poor mixing.
    *   **Unburnt Hydrocarbons (UHC):** Also a result of incomplete combustion, often in leaner regions or near the liner walls.
    *   **Soot (Particulate Matter):** Formed from incomplete combustion of fuel at rich conditions.

*   **Strategies for Emission Reduction:**
    *   **Lean Premixed Combustion:** Pre-mixing fuel and air at a lean (excess air) condition before combustion significantly reduces NOx formation due to lower peak flame temperatures.
    *   **Rich Burn, Quick Quench, Lean Mix (RQL):** A three-stage combustion process designed to minimize NOx.
        *   **Rich Burn (Primary Zone):** Promotes complete combustion of fuel with minimal NOx formation.
        *   **Quick Quench:** Rapidly mixes the hot combustion gases with cold air to prevent NOx formation.
        *   **Lean Mix (Secondary/Dilution Zone):** Ensures complete combustion of any remaining intermediates and reduces CO and UHC.
    *   **Staged Combustion:** Introducing fuel in multiple stages to control the combustion process and temperature profile.

**(Refer to Ganesan, Chapter 7: Combustion Chambers, Yahya, Chapter 8: Combustion Chambers)**

### **7. Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** Briefly describe the three main types of gas turbine combustion chambers and their typical applications.

**Answer 1:**
*   **Can Combustor:** Consists of individual cylindrical chambers. Used in older/smaller engines, turbo-props. Easier maintenance, simpler design.
*   **Annular Combustor:** Single continuous annular chamber. Used in modern high-performance engines (jets, turbofans). More compact, lighter, better temperature uniformity.
*   **Cannular Combustor:** Individual cans within an annular casing. Compromise design, better temperature uniformity than can, easier maintenance than annular.

**Question 2:** Explain the purpose of the primary, secondary, and dilution zones within a combustion chamber.

**Answer 2:**
*   **Primary Zone:** Fuel injection and initial combustion. High turbulence for good mixing.
*   **Secondary Zone:** Completes combustion of any unburnt fuel and starts diluting the hot gases.
*   **Dilution Zone:** Further dilutes the combustion products with excess air to achieve the desired Turbine Inlet Temperature (TIT).

**Question 3:** What are the main functions of the liner in a gas turbine combustion chamber? How is it protected from the high temperatures?

**Answer 3:** The liner is the inner shell where combustion occurs. It protects the outer casing from direct exposure to extreme heat. It is protected by:
    *   **Liner Cooling:** Airflow through holes or slots in the liner walls (effusion/transpiration cooling).
    *   **Thermal Barrier Coatings (TBCs):** Ceramic coatings that reduce heat transfer.

**Question 4:** How does a swirler contribute to stable combustion in a gas turbine combustor?

**Answer 4:** A swirler induces swirl in the incoming air. This creates a recirculation zone at the front of the combustor where hot combustion gases mix with fresh fuel and air. This preheats the reactants and provides a pocket of hot gases that re-ignites any unburnt fuel, thus stabilizing the flame and preventing it from being blown out by the high-speed airflow.

**Question 5:** Discuss the primary factors that influence the formation of Nitrogen Oxides (NOx) in a gas turbine combustion chamber and briefly describe a common strategy to mitigate NOx emissions.

**Answer 5:** NOx formation is primarily influenced by:
    *   **High Combustion Temperatures:** NOx forms rapidly at temperatures above 1500°C.
    *   **Availability of Oxygen and Nitrogen:** Present in the air.
    *   **Residence Time at High Temperatures:** Longer exposure at high temperatures increases NOx.

A common strategy to mitigate NOx emissions is **Lean Premixed Combustion**. In this approach, fuel and air are thoroughly mixed before entering the combustion zone, creating a lean (excess air) mixture. This results in lower peak flame temperatures, significantly reducing NOx formation.

### **8. Important Points to Remember**

*   The combustion chamber is where chemical energy from fuel is converted into thermal energy of the gas.
*   Combustion chamber design is a trade-off between complete combustion, minimal pressure loss, stable operation, uniform temperature profile, and low emissions.
*   Annular combustors are preferred in modern engines due to their compactness, lighter weight, and better temperature uniformity.
*   Liner cooling is crucial for the durability of the combustion chamber.
*   Flame stabilization relies on creating recirculation zones and maintaining the fuel-air mixture within flammability limits.
*   Turbine Inlet Temperature (TIT) is a key performance parameter, but it's limited by material capabilities.
*   Emission control, particularly for NOx, CO, and UHC, is a significant design challenge, addressed through techniques like lean premixing.
