# Gas Turbine and Jet Propulsion: Module 3 - Combustion - Theory of Combustion

## Topic: Diffuser

This topic focuses on the function and design principles of diffusers within the context of gas turbine and jet propulsion systems, specifically how they relate to the combustion process and overall performance.

---

### 1. Introduction to Diffusers in Gas Turbines

Diffusers play a crucial role in managing the flow of working fluid within a gas turbine engine. While the primary combustion event occurs in the combustor, the diffuser's function is to prepare the flow *entering* the combustor and *leaving* downstream components for subsequent stages. Understanding the diffuser's behavior is essential for optimizing the combustion process and the overall engine efficiency.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Diffuser:** A diverging duct that converts kinetic energy of a fluid into potential energy (static pressure).
*   **Purpose in Gas Turbines:**
    *   **Inlet Diffuser (before compressor):** To decelerate the incoming air and increase its static pressure, reducing the velocity from ambient conditions to the compressor inlet requirements. (Though less relevant to the *combustion* module directly, it sets the stage for compressor operation).
    *   **Inter-stage Diffusers (between compressor stages):** To recover static pressure and decelerate the flow between compressor stages, improving efficiency and managing flow characteristics.
    *   **After-combustor Diffuser (after turbine):** To decelerate the high-velocity exhaust gases from the turbine, recovering static pressure and preparing the flow for expansion through a nozzle or for further processing. This is particularly important in jet propulsion.
    *   **Combustor Inlet Diffuser:** This is the most direct connection to the combustion process. It decelerates the high-velocity air from the compressor exit to a lower velocity suitable for stable combustion in the combustor.

**Relevance to Combustion (CO1, CO3):**

The velocity and pressure of the air entering the combustor significantly impact:
*   **Flame Stability:** Too high a velocity can blow the flame out, while too low a velocity can lead to unstable combustion and increased emissions.
*   **Combustion Efficiency:** The flow pattern and turbulence generated by the diffuser influence mixing of fuel and air, affecting how completely the fuel burns.
*   **Pressure Drop:** An inefficient diffuser will cause a significant pressure drop, reducing the overall engine efficiency.

### 2. Aerodynamic Principles of Diffusers

The operation of a diffuser is governed by fundamental principles of fluid dynamics, primarily the conservation of energy.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Bernoulli's Equation:** For incompressible, inviscid flow, the sum of static pressure, dynamic pressure (½ρv²), and potential energy remains constant along a streamline.
    *   $P_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 + \rho g h_1 = P_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2 + \rho g h_2$
    *   In most gas turbine applications, changes in height ($h$) are negligible, so the equation simplifies to: $P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = constant$.
*   **Energy Conversion:** In a diffuser, as the velocity ($v$) decreases, the static pressure ($P$) must increase to conserve energy.
*   **Area-Velocity Relationship:** In a diverging duct, the cross-sectional area ($A$) increases, and for a constant mass flow rate ($\dot{m} = \rho A v$), the velocity ($v$) must decrease.
*   **Mach Number:** For compressible flow, the Mach number (M = v/a, where 'a' is the speed of sound) is a critical parameter. Diffusers are designed to reduce the Mach number.
    *   **Subsonic Diffusers (M < 1):** Diverging passages are effective in reducing velocity and increasing pressure.
    *   **Supersonic Diffusers (M > 1):** A converging-diverging (de Laval) nozzle is required to decelerate supersonic flow. The converging section reduces velocity and increases pressure, but if the Mach number is still supersonic, a diverging section can further decelerate and increase pressure. A simple diverging duct will *accelerate* supersonic flow.

**Flow Regimes and Considerations:**

*   **Isentropic Diffusion:** Ideal diffusion where flow is reversible and adiabatic (no heat transfer). This represents the maximum possible pressure recovery.
*   **Real Diffusers:** In reality, diffusers experience:
    *   **Friction Losses:** Viscous effects at the walls cause a pressure drop.
    *   **Flow Separation:** If the divergence angle is too large or the diffuser is too long, the flow can detach from the walls, leading to inefficiency and reduced pressure recovery. This is a major design consideration.

**Design Parameters:**

*   **Divergence Angle:** The angle of the diverging walls.
    *   Small angles (e.g., 7-10 degrees for total angle) minimize flow separation and maximize pressure recovery but result in longer diffusers.
    *   Larger angles are more compact but increase the risk of separation.
*   **Area Ratio:** The ratio of the exit area to the inlet area ($A_2/A_1$). A higher area ratio generally leads to lower exit velocity and higher pressure recovery, but also requires careful design to avoid separation.
*   **Length-to-Diameter Ratio (L/D):** Influences friction losses and the feasibility of achieving a desired area ratio without separation.

**Relevance to Combustion (CO1, CO3):**

*   The velocity reduction in the combustor inlet diffuser is crucial for creating a low-velocity zone for stable flame anchoring.
*   The pressure recovery in the diffuser contributes to the overall pressure rise in the engine, impacting thermodynamic cycle efficiency.
*   Understanding flow separation is vital to ensure uniform flow distribution into the combustor, preventing localized hot spots and inefficient burning.

### 3. Types of Diffusers and Their Application in Gas Turbines

Different diffuser designs are employed depending on the specific location and flow conditions within the gas turbine engine.

**Key Types:**

*   **Conical Diffusers:** Simple diffusers with straight diverging walls forming a cone.
*   **Annular Diffusers:** Used where the flow is axisymmetric, such as around a central body or within an annular passage. Common in the transition from compressor to combustor.
*   **Vane Diffusers (or Stator Diffusers):** Incorporate stationary guide vanes to direct the flow and prevent separation, improving efficiency and allowing for larger divergence angles. Often found in the transition from compressor to combustor.
*   **Transition Ducts:** These are often more complex shapes, combining diffusing and turning actions, used to guide flow from one component to another with different geometric orientations (e.g., axial compressor outlet to annular combustor inlet).

**Specific Applications within a Gas Turbine Engine:**

*   **Compressor Outlet to Combustor Inlet:** This is a critical application. The diffuser here must decelerate the high-velocity, moderately pressurized air from the compressor to a velocity and pressure distribution that is optimal for the combustor. Annular and vane diffusers are commonly used here to manage the complex annular flow.
    *   **Vanes (stators) in vane diffusers** can help to straighten the flow and reduce swirl from the compressor, aiding stable combustion.
*   **Between Turbine Stages:** In multi-stage turbines, diffusers are sometimes used between stages to recover pressure and manage the flow, though less common than in compressors.
*   **Exhaust Diffuser (after turbine):** In jet engines, the exhaust gases leaving the turbine are still at high velocity and pressure. An exhaust diffuser (often a simple diverging nozzle) can convert some of this kinetic energy back into static pressure, increasing thrust by reducing the exhaust velocity. This is a crucial component in understanding jet propulsion thrust generation (CO4).

**Relevance to Combustion (CO1, CO3, CO4):**

*   The design of the diffuser from the compressor to the combustor directly impacts the air velocity, pressure, and flow uniformity entering the combustion chamber, influencing combustion stability and efficiency.
*   The exhaust diffuser in a jet engine affects the velocity of the exiting gas, which is a primary factor in producing thrust (CO4).

### 4. Performance Parameters and Analysis of Diffusers

Evaluating diffuser performance is crucial for optimizing engine efficiency.

**Key Performance Metrics:**

*   **Pressure Recovery Coefficient ($C_p$):** A measure of how effectively a diffuser converts kinetic energy into static pressure.
    *   $C_p = \frac{P_{2s} - P_1}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_1 v_1^2}$
    *   Where:
        *   $P_{2s}$ is the static pressure at the diffuser exit.
        *   $P_1$ is the static pressure at the diffuser inlet.
        *   $\frac{1}{2}\rho_1 v_1^2$ is the dynamic pressure at the diffuser inlet.
    *   **Ideal Diffuser:** Has the highest possible $C_p$ for a given area ratio.
    *   **Real Diffuser:** $C_p$ is lower due to losses.
*   **Velocity Ratio ($v_2/v_1$):** The ratio of exit velocity to inlet velocity. In a diffuser, this should be less than 1.
*   **Pressure Recovery Ratio ($P_2/P_1$):** The ratio of total pressure at the exit to the total pressure at the inlet. Ideally, for an adiabatic diffuser, total pressure should be constant, but real diffusers have losses, so $P_{2total} < P_{1total}$.
*   **Diffusion Factor (or Velocity Gradient Parameter):** A dimensionless parameter that quantifies the adverse pressure gradient experienced by the fluid, which is a key driver of flow separation.

**Analytical Tools:**

*   **Momentum Integral Method:** A mathematical technique used to analyze boundary layer development in diffusers and predict flow separation.
*   **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD):** Modern design relies heavily on CFD simulations to model the complex, often turbulent, and compressible flow within diffusers, predicting pressure recovery, velocity profiles, and identifying potential separation regions.

**Losses in Diffusers:**

*   **Friction Losses:** Due to viscous shear at the duct walls.
*   **Diffuser Wall Friction Losses:** The direct consequence of friction acting along the diverging walls.
*   **Flow Separation Losses:** Significant losses occur if flow separation takes place, as the effective flow area is reduced, and a recirculation zone is formed.
*   **Exit Losses:** If the diffuser exit conditions are not well-matched to the downstream component, additional losses can occur.

**Relevance to Combustion (CO1, CO3):**

*   A high pressure recovery coefficient ($C_p$) means less energy is wasted in the diffuser, improving the overall thermal efficiency of the engine (CO1).
*   Understanding the diffusion factor helps engineers design diffusers that prevent flow separation, ensuring uniform flow into the combustor, which is critical for efficient and stable combustion (CO3).
*   The pressure loss in the diffuser directly impacts the stagnation pressure available for combustion and subsequent expansion through the turbine.

**Textbook References:**

*   **Ganesan (2017):** Will likely present methods for calculating pressure recovery and discuss empirical correlations for diffuser efficiency.
*   **Yahya (2011):** Offers detailed derivations of performance parameters and analysis methods.
*   **Cohen (2019):** Provides advanced analytical methods and design criteria for maximizing diffuser performance.

### 5. Diffusers and Combustion Stability (Key Linkage)

The primary role of the diffuser in this combustion module is its influence on the conditions of the air entering the combustor.

**Key Considerations:**

*   **Velocity Profile:** The diffuser's job is to reduce the high-velocity, swirled flow from the compressor to a lower, more uniform velocity distribution at the combustor inlet.
    *   **Low Velocity Zone:** Essential for anchoring the flame. The fuel is typically injected into a central recirculation zone created by swirlers or bluff bodies, where the velocity is low enough for the flame to remain stable.
    *   **Uniformity:** A uniform velocity profile across the combustor annulus ensures even fuel-air mixing and combustion, preventing localized hot spots and incomplete combustion.
*   **Turbulence:** The diffuser, especially a vane diffuser, can induce controlled turbulence that aids in the mixing of fuel and air, enhancing the reaction rate and improving combustion efficiency. However, excessive turbulence or separation can be detrimental.
*   **Pressure Drop:** The diffuser introduces a pressure drop, which represents a loss of energy. This pressure drop must be minimized through efficient design, as it reduces the overall efficiency of the gas turbine cycle.
*   **Swirl Control:** In many modern engines, the compressor exit flow has a significant swirl. The combustor inlet diffuser, often in conjunction with swirlers, must manage this swirl to create the desired recirculation zones for flame stabilization.

**Examples:**

*   **Turbojet/Turbofan Engine:** Air from the compressor enters an annular diffuser which then feeds into the annular combustor. The diffuser decelerates the air and ensures a suitable velocity distribution for the fuel injectors and primary zone.
*   **Large Angle Diffusers:** If the divergence angle is too large, the flow might separate, leading to a turbulent wake behind the separation points. This wake could potentially destabilize the flame or lead to incomplete combustion in that region.

**Relevance to Combustion (CO1, CO3):**

*   **CO1:** The efficiency of the diffuser affects the pressure ratio across the engine, impacting the overall thermodynamic cycle efficiency.
*   **CO3:** The diffuser's influence on velocity, turbulence, and flow uniformity directly dictates the combustion process's stability, efficiency, and emissions. A poorly designed diffuser can lead to engine operational issues.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Trade-off between $C_p$ and Separation:** There's a fundamental trade-off between achieving a high pressure recovery coefficient and avoiding flow separation.
*   **Design is Crucial:** The shape, divergence angle, and length of a diffuser are critical design parameters.
*   **Impact on Combustor:** The diffuser is the bridge between the compressor and the combustor; its performance directly impacts the combustor's ability to operate efficiently and stably.
*   **Jet Engine Thrust:** In jet engines, the exhaust diffuser is a key component for converting kinetic energy into static pressure, which directly influences net thrust.

### Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Define the primary function of a diffuser in a gas turbine engine, particularly in the context of its role before the combustor.

**Answer:** The primary function of a diffuser before the combustor is to decelerate the high-velocity air exiting the compressor and convert its kinetic energy into static pressure. This results in a lower and more uniform velocity profile at the combustor inlet, which is essential for achieving stable and efficient combustion.

**Question 2:** Explain the relationship between static pressure, velocity, and area in a subsonic diffuser based on Bernoulli's principle.

**Answer:** According to Bernoulli's principle for subsonic flow, as the area of a diverging duct (diffuser) increases, the velocity of the fluid decreases. To conserve energy, this decrease in kinetic energy (due to lower velocity) is converted into an increase in static pressure.

**Question 3:** What is the pressure recovery coefficient ($C_p$), and why is it an important performance parameter for diffusers?

**Answer:** The pressure recovery coefficient ($C_p$) quantifies how effectively a diffuser converts kinetic energy into static pressure. It is defined as the ratio of the static pressure rise in the diffuser to the inlet dynamic pressure. A higher $C_p$ indicates a more efficient diffuser, meaning less energy is lost as pressure drop, which contributes to better overall engine efficiency.

**Question 4:** What is flow separation in a diffuser, and what are its consequences?

**Answer:** Flow separation occurs when the fluid boundary layer detaches from the diffuser walls, typically due to an excessive divergence angle or a strong adverse pressure gradient. This results in a turbulent wake behind the separation point, reduced effective flow area, significant pressure losses, and potentially uneven flow distribution into the downstream component (e.g., the combustor), leading to inefficiencies and unstable operation.

**Question 5:** How does a diffuser's performance influence combustion stability?

**Answer:** The diffuser's performance directly impacts combustion stability by controlling the velocity, turbulence, and uniformity of the air entering the combustor. A well-designed diffuser creates a low-velocity zone necessary for flame anchoring and ensures uniform mixing of fuel and air. Conversely, a poorly designed diffuser leading to flow separation can cause flame instability, incomplete combustion, and operational problems.
