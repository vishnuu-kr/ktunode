## 1. General Layout of a Thermal Power Plant

A thermal power plant converts the chemical energy of fuel into electrical energy through a series of interconnected systems. The general layout is designed to optimize efficiency, safety, and operational flow.

**Key Components and their Arrangement:**

*   **Boiler House:** This is the heart of the plant where fuel is burnt to generate high-pressure steam. It houses the boiler, economizer, superheater, reheater, air preheater, and associated auxiliaries.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Emphasizes the importance of boiler design for efficient heat transfer and steam generation.
    *   **Nag (2017):** Details the different types of boilers and their operational characteristics.
*   **Turbine Room:** Contains the steam turbine(s) connected to the generator. The turbine converts the thermal energy of steam into mechanical energy.
    *   **Nagpal & Sharma (2012):** Discusses the arrangement of turbines (e.g., high-pressure, intermediate-pressure, low-pressure) and their alignment with generators.
*   **Condenser:** Located below the turbine, it condenses the exhaust steam from the turbine back into water using cooling water.
*   **Cooling Tower/River/Lake:** Provides cooling water for the condenser.
*   **Generator:** Converts the mechanical energy from the turbine into electrical energy.
*   **Control Room:** Houses the instrumentation and control systems for monitoring and operating the plant.
*   **Fuel Handling and Storage Area:** Where fuel (coal, oil, gas) is received, stored, and prepared for burning.
*   **Ash Handling and Disposal Area:** For collecting, transporting, and disposing of ash generated from fuel combustion.
*   **Chimney:** Discharges flue gases from the boiler to the atmosphere.
*   **Water Treatment Plant:** Purifies raw water for boiler feed.
*   **Electrical Switchyard:** For stepping up the generated voltage and transmitting electricity.

**Typical Layout Considerations:**

*   **Proximity of Components:** Boilers should be close to turbines for efficient steam piping. Condensers should be near cooling water sources.
*   **Accessibility:** All components should be easily accessible for maintenance and operation.
*   **Safety:** Fire hazards and emissions need to be managed.
*   **Transportation:** Efficient movement of fuel, ash, and personnel.

**Example:** A typical coal-fired thermal power plant layout will have the coal handling plant on one side, leading to the boiler house. The boiler house will be adjacent to the turbine hall, which houses the turbine and generator. The condenser will be below the turbine, connected to cooling towers or a water source. The chimney will be located downwind of the main plant structures.

## 2. Site Selection for Thermal Power Plants

The selection of a suitable site is crucial for the economic and environmental viability of a thermal power plant.

**Key Factors for Site Selection:**

1.  **Proximity to Fuel Source:**
    *   **Coal-fired plants:** Near coal mines to reduce transportation costs.
    *   **Oil/Gas-fired plants:** Near oil/gas pipelines or ports.
    *   **Importance:** Fuel transportation is a significant cost component.
    *   **Reference:** Gujjar, M. K. (2012). *Power Plant Engineering*. PHI Learning Pvt. Ltd. discusses the impact of transportation costs on plant economics.

2.  **Proximity to Load Center:**
    *   Locating the plant near major consumption areas reduces transmission losses and costs.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Highlights the trade-off between fuel proximity and load center proximity, as well as transmission line costs.

3.  **Availability of Cooling Water:**
    *   Thermal power plants require large quantities of water for condensing steam and cooling equipment.
    *   Sources: Rivers, lakes, seas, or cooling towers.
    *   **Nag (2017):** Details the water requirements and the impact of water availability on plant location.

4.  **Topography and Soil Conditions:**
    *   The site should be relatively flat and have stable soil to support heavy machinery like boilers and turbines.
    *   Avoidance of flood-prone areas.

5.  **Environmental Considerations:**
    *   **Air Pollution:** Prevailing wind direction should be considered to minimize pollution impact on populated areas.
    *   **Ash Disposal:** Availability of land for ash disposal (ponds or landfills).
    *   **Noise Pollution:** Adequate distance from residential areas.
    *   **Water Pollution:** Proper discharge of cooling water to avoid thermal pollution.
    *   **Nagpal & Sharma (2012):** Emphasize the growing importance of environmental impact assessments in site selection.

6.  **Land Availability:**
    *   Sufficient land is required for the plant itself, fuel storage, ash disposal, cooling towers, administrative buildings, and future expansion.

7.  **Accessibility for Transportation:**
    *   Good road and rail connectivity for transporting equipment, fuel, and personnel.

8.  **Safety:**
    *   Distance from seismic zones, densely populated areas, and potential hazards.

9.  **Government Regulations and Policies:**
    *   Adherence to zoning laws, environmental regulations, and land-use policies.

## 3. Fuel Handling, Storage, and Burning Systems

These systems are critical for ensuring a continuous and efficient supply of fuel to the boiler.

### 3.1. Fuel Handling Systems

**For Coal:**

*   **Receiving:** Coal is received via railways wagons or trucks. Unloading is done using wagon tipplers or manual unloading.
*   **Crushing:** Large lumps of coal are crushed into smaller sizes in crushers (e.g., hammer mills, jaw crushers) to improve combustion.
*   **Sieving/Screening:** Coal is sieved to separate different size fractions. Oversized coal goes back for crushing, and undersized coal might be used in certain combustion systems.
*   **Conveying:** Coal is transported from the receiving point to storage and then to the boiler using conveyor belts, bucket elevators, or pneumatic conveyors.
*   **Storage:** Coal is stored in yards. Covered storage is preferred to prevent degradation and reduce fire risk.

**For Oil:**

*   **Receiving:** Oil is received via tankers or pipelines.
*   **Pumping:** Oil is pumped from storage tanks to the boiler through pipelines.
*   **Heating:** Heavy fuel oil may need to be heated to reduce viscosity for efficient atomization.
*   **Filtration:** Oil is filtered to remove impurities.

**For Gas:**

*   **Pipeline:** Natural gas is usually supplied through pipelines and directly fed to the boiler.
*   **Pressure Regulation:** Gas pressure is regulated before entering the burner.

### 3.2. Fuel Storage Systems

*   **Coal:**
    *   **Open Yards:** Common, but susceptible to weather and fire.
    *   **Covered Storage:** Protects coal from rain and fire hazards.
    *   **Stacking:** Coal is stacked in piles. The height and shape of stacks are important for stability and fire prevention.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Discusses the potential for spontaneous combustion in coal piles and preventative measures.
*   **Oil:**
    *   **Storage Tanks:** Large steel tanks are used.
    *   **Safety:** Tanks are often located in bunded areas (containment walls) to prevent oil spills. Fire prevention systems are essential.
*   **Gas:**
    *   **Pipeline Storage:** Gas is stored under pressure in pipelines.
    *   **Gas Holders:** Used for smaller plants or to buffer supply fluctuations.

### 3.3. Fuel Burning Systems

The goal is to efficiently burn the fuel to generate heat for steam production.

**For Coal:**

1.  **Underfeed Stokers:** Fuel is fed from below the burning bed. Suitable for smaller boilers.
2.  **Overfeed Stokers:** Fuel is fed from above.
3.  **Pulverized Coal Firing:**
    *   Coal is finely ground (powdered) and blown into the furnace with air.
    *   **Advantages:** High combustion efficiency, rapid heat release, easier control, smaller furnace volume.
    *   **Process:** Coal is fed to pulverizers (ball mills, roller mills) where it is ground to a fine powder. Hot air from the air preheater is used to dry and transport the pulverized coal to the burners.
    *   **Nag (2017):** Provides detailed descriptions of various pulverizer types and pulverized coal combustion.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Explains the fluid dynamics and combustion kinetics in pulverized coal furnaces.
4.  **Fluidized Bed Combustion (FBC):**
    *   Fuel particles are suspended in a stream of air, creating a "fluidized bed."
    *   **Advantages:** High combustion efficiency, lower NOx emissions (due to lower combustion temperatures), ability to burn low-grade fuels and incorporate SO2 sorbents (like limestone) directly into the bed.
    *   **Types:** Bubbling FBC (BFBC) and Circulating FBC (CFBC).
    *   **Nagpal & Sharma (2012):** Discusses the advantages of FBC technology, particularly for emissions control.

**For Oil:**

*   **Oil Burners:** Oil is atomized (broken into fine droplets) and mixed with air for combustion. Atomization can be mechanical (high pressure) or pressure atomization.

**For Gas:**

*   **Gas Burners:** Gas is mixed with air in specific ratios and burnt in the furnace.

## 4. Dust and Ash Handling System

Combustion of fuel, especially coal, produces fly ash and bottom ash, which need to be removed efficiently and safely.

### 4.1. Types of Ash

*   **Bottom Ash:** Heavier ash particles that fall to the bottom of the furnace or grate.
*   **Fly Ash:** Lighter ash particles carried along with flue gases, collected by dust collectors.

### 4.2. Dust Collection Systems (for Fly Ash)

These systems are crucial for environmental compliance and preventing equipment damage.

1.  **Mechanical Dust Collectors:**
    *   **Cyclone Separators:** Use centrifugal force to separate larger ash particles from the flue gas. Flue gas enters tangentially, creating a vortex. Heavier particles move to the outer wall and fall down.
    *   **Multi-cyclones:** Multiple smaller cyclones arranged in parallel to improve efficiency.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple design, low cost, low pressure drop.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Low efficiency for fine particles.

2.  **Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs):**
    *   **Principle:** Utilizes electrostatic forces to remove fine particles from flue gas.
    *   **Process:**
        *   **Ionization:** Flue gas passes through a region with high-voltage electrodes (discharge electrodes) that ionize the dust particles, giving them a negative charge.
        *   **Collection:** The charged particles are then attracted to positively charged collecting electrodes (plates).
        *   **Rapping:** The collected dust is periodically removed from the plates by mechanical rapping, allowing it to fall into hoppers.
    *   **Advantages:** Very high collection efficiency for fine particles (over 99%), suitable for large power plants.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High capital cost, sensitive to gas temperature and resistivity, requires significant space.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Provides detailed principles and design aspects of ESPs.

3.  **Fabric Filters (Baghouses):**
    *   **Principle:** Flue gas is passed through fabric bags, where dust particles are trapped on the bag surface.
    *   **Process:** Gas enters the bags and clean gas exits. Dust accumulates on the outside (or inside) of the bags, forming a dust cake that enhances filtration.
    *   **Cleaning:** Bags are cleaned by pulse-jetting compressed air or by shaking mechanisms.
    *   **Advantages:** High collection efficiency for very fine particles, can operate at lower temperatures.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Fabric can be damaged by high temperatures or corrosive gases, requires regular maintenance.

### 4.3. Ash Handling Systems

Once collected, ash needs to be transported from hoppers to disposal areas.

1.  **Hydraulic Ash Handling System:**
    *   **Process:** Ash is transported using water as the conveying medium. Ash hoppers are flushed with water, creating an ash-water slurry.
    *   **Components:** Ash slurry pumps, pipelines.
    *   **Disposal:** The slurry is pumped to ash ponds or lagoons.
    *   **Advantages:** Robust, can handle large volumes and abrasive materials.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High water consumption, potential for water pollution if not managed properly, large land area for ash ponds.

2.  **Mechanical Ash Handling System:**
    *   **Process:** Ash is transported using mechanical conveyors (screw conveyors, belt conveyors, drag chain conveyors) or pneumatic systems.
    *   **Pneumatic Systems:** Use air pressure or vacuum to move ash.
        *   **Dense Phase:** Ash is moved in slugs with low air velocity.
        *   **Dilute Phase:** Ash is suspended in a high-velocity air stream.
    *   **Advantages:** Lower water consumption, no water pollution issues.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher wear and tear on equipment, can be dusty if not sealed properly.

**Ash Disposal:**

*   **Ash Ponds:** Large engineered basins designed to hold ash slurry. Requires significant land area and careful environmental management.
*   **Dry Disposal:** Ash is dewatered and transported to landfills or used as construction material.
*   **Utilization:** Fly ash is increasingly used in concrete, cement production, and road construction, reducing disposal needs.
    *   **Nagpal & Sharma (2012):** Discusses the environmental benefits and applications of fly ash utilization.

## 5. Chimney and Draught

The chimney's primary function is to discharge flue gases safely to the atmosphere at a sufficient height to prevent them from accumulating at ground level and causing pollution. Draught is the difference in pressure that causes the flow of flue gases through the boiler and chimney.

### 5.1. Chimney

*   **Purpose:**
    *   To carry flue gases away from the plant and surrounding areas.
    *   To ensure adequate dispersion of pollutants into the atmosphere.
    *   To provide sufficient draught for combustion.
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   **Height:** Determined by pollution dispersion models, ensuring ground-level concentrations remain within permissible limits. Factors include emission rate, wind speed, and atmospheric stability.
    *   **Diameter:** Related to the volume of flue gases.
    *   **Material:** Typically concrete or brick, lined with acid-resistant material.
*   **Nag (2017):** Provides details on chimney design, including height calculation based on dispersion models.

### 5.2. Draught

Draught is the pressure difference that moves air into the furnace and flue gases through the boiler and up the chimney.

**Types of Draught:**

1.  **Natural Draught:**
    *   **Cause:** Created by the difference in density between the hot flue gases inside the chimney and the cooler atmospheric air outside.
    *   **Mechanism:** Hot gases are lighter and rise, creating a lower pressure at the base of the chimney, which draws in combustion air.
    *   **Draught Force:** $P_d = (h_c \rho_a - h_c \rho_g) g = h_c (\rho_a - \rho_g) g$
        Where:
        *   $P_d$ = Draught pressure
        *   $h_c$ = Height of chimney
        *   $\rho_a$ = Density of atmospheric air
        *   $\rho_g$ = Density of flue gas
        *   $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity
    *   **Advantages:** No power consumption, simple.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Dependent on atmospheric temperature and chimney height. Becomes insufficient for large boilers or when burning fuels requiring more air. Low efficiency.
    *   **El Wakil (2017):** Discusses the physics behind natural draught and its limitations.

2.  **Mechanical Draught:**
    *   Uses fans to create the necessary pressure difference.
    *   **a) Forced Draught:**
        *   **Mechanism:** A fan (forced draught fan) blows air into the furnace or ash pit.
        *   **Result:** The pressure inside the furnace is slightly above atmospheric pressure.
        *   **Advantages:** Ensures adequate air supply for combustion, allows burning of low-grade fuels, enables smaller boilers.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Flue gases can leak out from furnace openings due to positive pressure.
    *   **b) Induced Draught:**
        *   **Mechanism:** A fan (induced draught fan or exhaust fan) located at the chimney base or outlet of the boiler, sucks the flue gases through the boiler and discharges them up the chimney.
        *   **Result:** The pressure inside the furnace and boiler passages is below atmospheric pressure.
        *   **Advantages:** Prevents leakage of flue gases, helps draw in combustion air, allows for controlled gas flow.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Fan blades are exposed to hot and abrasive flue gases, requiring robust construction.
    *   **c) Balanced Draught:**
        *   **Mechanism:** Uses both a forced draught fan and an induced draught fan.
        *   **Result:** The pressure inside the furnace is maintained close to atmospheric pressure.
        *   **Advantages:** Offers precise control over combustion air and flue gas flow, minimizes air infiltration and gas leakage, leading to higher efficiency and better emission control.
        *   **Nagpal & Sharma (2012):** Discusses the advantages of balanced draught for optimal boiler operation.

**Important Note:** In modern large thermal power plants, a combination of **forced draught** (to supply combustion air) and **induced draught** (to pull flue gases through the boiler and up the chimney) is commonly used to achieve **balanced draught**, allowing for efficient combustion and emission control.

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   Site selection is a multi-faceted decision balancing economic, environmental, and operational factors.
*   Efficient fuel handling and preparation are crucial for optimal combustion.
*   Pulverized coal firing and Fluidized Bed Combustion (FBC) are dominant coal combustion technologies.
*   Dust collection (ESP, Fabric Filters) is essential for meeting environmental regulations.
*   Hydraulic and mechanical systems are used for ash transport and disposal.
*   Draught is necessary for gas flow; mechanical draught (forced, induced, or balanced) is preferred in modern plants for better control and efficiency.
*   Chimney height is determined by pollution dispersion requirements.

### **Practice Questions and Answers:**

**Question 1:** List and briefly explain any five important factors to consider during the site selection of a thermal power plant.

**Answer:**
1.  **Proximity to Fuel Source:** Minimizes transportation costs for fuel (e.g., coal, oil, gas).
2.  **Proximity to Load Center:** Reduces transmission losses and costs.
3.  **Availability of Cooling Water:** Essential for the condenser to convert steam back to water.
4.  **Environmental Considerations:** Impact on air and water quality, land availability for ash disposal.
5.  **Topography and Soil Conditions:** Site stability for heavy machinery and construction.
6.  **Accessibility:** Good road/rail access for equipment, fuel, and personnel.

**Question 2:** Differentiate between natural draught and mechanical draught in a thermal power plant.

**Answer:**
*   **Natural Draught:** Relies on the density difference between hot flue gases and ambient air. It's simple but less controllable and less effective for large plants.
*   **Mechanical Draught:** Uses fans (forced draught, induced draught, or both) to create the pressure difference for airflow. It offers better control, higher efficiency, and is essential for modern large thermal power plants.

**Question 3:** Explain the principle of operation of an Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP).

**Answer:** An ESP ionizes dust particles in the flue gas using high-voltage discharge electrodes, imparting a negative charge. These charged particles are then attracted to positively charged collecting plates. The collected dust is periodically removed by rapping.

**Question 4:** What are the two main types of ash produced in a coal-fired thermal power plant, and how are they typically handled?

**Answer:**
*   **Bottom Ash:** Heavier ash that settles at the bottom of the furnace. Typically handled by hydraulic systems (water flushing) or mechanical conveyors.
*   **Fly Ash:** Lighter ash carried by flue gases. Collected by dust collectors like ESPs or fabric filters, and then transported via hydraulic or mechanical systems.

**Question 5:** Why is balanced draught preferred in modern thermal power plants?

**Answer:** Balanced draught, achieved by using both forced and induced draught fans, allows precise control over the combustion air supply and flue gas removal. This helps maintain furnace pressure close to atmospheric, minimizing air infiltration (reducing efficiency losses) and flue gas leakage (improving safety and control). It leads to higher combustion efficiency and better emission control.
