# Power Plant Engineering: Module 2 - Steam Generator Classifications

## Introduction to Steam Generators (Boilers)

Steam generators, commonly known as boilers, are essential components in thermal power plants. They are closed vessels in which water is heated and converted into steam under pressure. The steam generated is then used to drive turbines, which in turn generate electricity.

**Key Concept:** The primary function of a boiler is to transfer heat from a fuel source (combustion of coal, oil, gas, etc.) to water to produce high-pressure, high-temperature steam.

**Reference:**
*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017). *Power Plant Technology*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Chapter on Steam Generators)
*   **Nag, P. K. (2017). *Power Plant Engineering*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Chapter on Boilers)

## Boiler Classifications

Boilers can be classified based on various criteria:

### 1. Based on Flow of Water and Hot Gases

This is a fundamental classification that differentiates boilers based on how water and combustion gases interact.

*   **Fire-Tube Boilers:**
    *   **Definition:** In these boilers, hot gases produced from combustion pass through tubes, and the water surrounds these tubes.
    *   **Mechanism:** Heat transfer occurs from the hot gases inside the tubes to the surrounding water.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Simpler construction and lower initial cost.
        *   Less prone to scaling and leakage.
        *   Can withstand higher pressures to some extent.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Limited steam generation capacity.
        *   Lower efficiency compared to water-tube boilers.
        *   Not suitable for very high pressures.
    *   **Examples:** Cochran, Lancashire, Cornish, and Locomotive boilers.

*   **Water-Tube Boilers:**
    *   **Definition:** In these boilers, water flows through tubes, and the hot gases produced from combustion pass around these tubes.
    *   **Mechanism:** Heat transfer occurs from the hot gases outside the tubes to the water inside.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Higher steam generation capacity.
        *   Higher thermal efficiency.
        *   Can operate at very high pressures and temperatures.
        *   Safer in case of tube failure (only a small amount of water is in the tubes).
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   More complex construction and higher initial cost.
        *   Requires more frequent maintenance and cleaning.
        *   More prone to scaling if water quality is poor.
    *   **Examples:** Babcock and Wilcox, Stirling, Foster-Wheeler boilers.

### 2. Based on Position of Furnace

*   **Internal Fired Boilers:** The furnace is located inside the boiler shell (e.g., Lancashire, Cornish boilers).
*   **External Fired Boilers:** The furnace is located outside the boiler shell (e.g., Babcock and Wilcox, Stirling boilers).

### 3. Based on Position of Tubes

*   **Horizontal Boilers:** Tubes are arranged horizontally (e.g., Lancashire, Cornish boilers).
*   **Vertical Boilers:** Tubes are arranged vertically (e.g., Vertical High-Speed Boiler).
*   **Inclined Boilers:** Tubes are inclined (e.g., Locomotive boiler).

### 4. Based on Pressure

*   **Low-Pressure Boilers:** Operate at pressures up to 0.5 bar gauge.
*   **Medium-Pressure Boilers:** Operate at pressures between 0.5 bar and 20 bar gauge.
*   **High-Pressure Boilers:** Operate at pressures above 20 bar gauge.

### 5. Based on Smoke/Gas Passage

*   **Simple Vertical Boilers:** Single smoke-box.
*   **Compound Vertical Boilers:** Multiple smoke-boxes.

## Specific Boiler Types (from Module 2 Topic)

### 1. Cochran Boiler (Fire-Tube)

*   **Description:** A compact, vertical, fire-tube boiler. It has a cylindrical shell with a furnace flue inside. Hot gases pass through the flue, then to a combustion chamber at the rear, and finally through a bank of fire tubes that pass back through the water space to the front smoke-box, from where they exit via the chimney.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Vertical cylindrical shell.
    *   Internal furnace flue (corrugated for strength and heat transfer).
    *   Combustion chamber at the rear.
    *   Fire tubes connecting the combustion chamber to the front.
    *   Water space surrounding the flue and tubes.
    *   Brick-lined grate for fuel combustion.
*   **Working:**
    1.  Fuel is burnt on the grate, producing hot gases.
    2.  Hot gases pass through the corrugated furnace flue.
    3.  Gases enter the rear combustion chamber.
    4.  Gases then pass through the fire tubes, transferring heat to the surrounding water.
    5.  Gases exit through the chimney.
*   **Advantages:** Compact, simple to operate, good for small industrial applications and steam heating.
*   **Disadvantages:** Limited steam output, lower efficiency, vertical orientation is not ideal for large capacities.
*   **Reference:**
    *   **Nag, P. K. (2017). *Power Plant Engineering*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Section on Fire-Tube Boilers)

### 2. Lancashire Boiler (Fire-Tube)

*   **Description:** A horizontal, fire-tube boiler characterized by two large internal furnace flues running the length of the shell. It's suitable for moderate pressures and capacities.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Long horizontal cylindrical shell.
    *   Two large internal furnace flues, each with a grate at the front.
    *   Combustion chamber at the rear, dividing the flue gases into two paths.
    *   Fire tubes (smaller diameter) located below the main furnace flues.
    *   Ashpit below the grate.
    *   Brick setting provides insulation and flue gas passages under the shell and around the sides.
*   **Working:**
    1.  Fuel is burnt on the grates within the furnace flues.
    2.  Hot gases from each flue pass to the rear combustion chamber.
    3.  From the rear combustion chamber, gases are split and pass through the fire tubes located below the main flues.
    4.  After passing through the fire tubes, the gases are collected in the side flues and then travel to the front to exit via the chimney.
*   **Advantages:** Simple construction, robust, capable of generating moderate amounts of steam, suitable for heating and low-pressure process steam.
*   **Disadvantages:** Large floor space required, lower efficiency, limited pressure capacity.
*   **Reference:**
    *   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017). *Power Plant Technology*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Section on Lancashire Boiler)
    *   **Nagpal, G. R., & Sharma, S. C. (2012). *Power Plant Engineering*. KHANNA Publishers.**

### 3. Cornish Boiler (Fire-Tube)

*   **Description:** A horizontal, fire-tube boiler with a single internal furnace flue. It's a simpler and smaller version of the Lancashire boiler.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Horizontal cylindrical shell.
    *   One large internal furnace flue, with a grate at the front.
    *   Combustion chamber at the rear.
    *   Fire tubes located below the furnace flue.
    *   Ashpit.
    *   Brick setting similar to the Lancashire boiler.
*   **Working:**
    1.  Fuel is burnt on the grate within the furnace flue.
    2.  Hot gases pass to the rear combustion chamber.
    3.  Gases then pass through the fire tubes located below the furnace flue.
    4.  Finally, gases are collected in the side flues and exit via the chimney.
*   **Advantages:** Simpler and cheaper than Lancashire boiler, occupies less space.
*   **Disadvantages:** Lower steam generation capacity than Lancashire boiler, lower efficiency.
*   **Reference:**
    *   **Gupta, M. K. (2012). *Power Plant Engineering*. PHI Learning Pvt. Ltd.** (Chapter on Boilers)

### 4. Locomotive Boiler (Fire-Tube)

*   **Description:** A horizontal, multi-tubular, fire-tube boiler designed for compactness and high evaporative capacity, commonly used in steam locomotives. It features a firebox at the rear and a large number of small fire tubes.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Horizontal cylindrical shell.
    *   Rectangular firebox at the rear (often exposed to the atmosphere or enclosed in a casing), with a grate.
    *   Combustion chamber (in some designs) behind the firebox.
    *   A large number of small fire tubes extending from the firebox/combustion chamber to the front tube plate.
    *   Smokebox at the front where gases collect before exiting via the chimney.
    *   The shell is surrounded by water.
    *   A "Belpaire" firebox or "round top" firebox may be used for increased heating surface and strength.
*   **Working:**
    1.  Fuel is burnt on the grate in the firebox.
    2.  Hot gases pass through the many fire tubes.
    3.  Heat is transferred from the gases in the tubes to the surrounding water.
    4.  Gases collect in the smokebox and are expelled through the chimney, often with the assistance of steam ejected from the blastpipe (which also draws in combustion air).
*   **Advantages:** High evaporative capacity, compact design, rapid steam generation, good for mobile applications.
*   **Disadvantages:** Lower efficiency, rapid wear and tear due to vibration and varying loads, difficult to clean.
*   **Reference:**
    *   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017). *Power Plant Technology*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Section on Locomotive Boiler)

### 5. Babcock and Wilcox Boiler (Water-Tube)

*   **Description:** A very common type of water-tube boiler, characterized by inclined water tubes connected to a steam drum above and a mud drum below. It's known for its flexibility in fuel usage and efficient steam generation.
*   **Construction:**
    *   Large steam drum (horizontal cylinder) at the top.
    *   Mud drum (lower horizontal drum) at the bottom.
    *   Inclined water tubes connecting the steam drum and mud drum, forming the heating surface.
    *   Header pipes connecting the water tubes to the drums.
    *   Downcomers: Large pipes connecting the steam drum to the mud drum, facilitating water circulation.
    *   Superheater: Placed in the path of hot flue gases to further heat the steam.
    *   Economizer: Placed in the path of flue gases before the chimney to preheat the feedwater.
    *   Air preheater: Used to preheat the combustion air.
    *   Furnace: Typically at the front or bottom, with grates or pulverised fuel burners.
    *   Brick setting: Forms the casing and directs flue gas flow.
*   **Working:**
    1.  Water from the steam drum flows down through the downcomers to the mud drum.
    2.  Water then rises through the inclined water tubes.
    3.  Hot flue gases from the furnace pass over the water tubes, transferring heat.
    4.  Water inside the tubes is heated, and steam bubbles form.
    5.  The steam-water mixture rises to the steam drum, where steam is separated from water.
    6.  Dry steam can be further heated in the superheater.
    7.  Flue gases are cooled in the economizer and air preheater before exiting.
*   **Advantages:** High efficiency, large steam generating capacity, can use various fuels, safe operation due to low water volume in tubes, quick steam generation.
*   **Disadvantages:** Higher initial cost, requires a constant supply of treated feedwater, complex construction.
*   **Reference:**
    *   **Nag, P. K. (2017). *Power Plant Engineering*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Chapter on Water-Tube Boilers)
    *   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017). *Power Plant Technology*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Section on Babcock and Wilcox Boiler)

### 6. Stirling Boiler (Water-Tube)

*   **Description:** A water-tube boiler characterized by three large drums arranged in a triangular configuration. It's designed for high efficiency and rapid steam generation.
*   **Construction:**
    *   One steam drum (at the top).
    *   Two smaller water drums (one at the front, one at the rear, both lower than the steam drum).
    *   Inclined water tubes connecting the steam drum to the water drums.
    *   A bent tube section in the middle for better gas circulation and heat transfer.
    *   Superheater, economizer, and air preheater (similar to Babcock and Wilcox).
    *   Furnace located at the front or bottom.
    *   Brick setting directs flue gas flow.
*   **Working:**
    1.  Water from the steam drum flows to the lower water drums.
    2.  Water then rises through the inclined tubes to the steam drum.
    3.  Hot flue gases from the furnace pass over the tubes in multiple passes, maximizing heat transfer.
    4.  Steam is separated in the steam drum and can be superheated.
*   **Advantages:** High efficiency, rapid steam generation, compact design, can handle varying loads, good for power generation.
*   **Disadvantages:** Higher initial cost, requires treated feedwater.
*   **Reference:**
    *   **Nagpal, G. R., & Sharma, S. C. (2012). *Power Plant Engineering*. KHANNA Publishers.**
    *   **Gupta, M. K. (2012). *Power Plant Engineering*. PHI Learning Pvt. Ltd.**

## High-Pressure Boilers

As power generation demands increased, the need for higher steam pressures and temperatures for improved thermal efficiency became paramount. This led to the development of high-pressure boilers.

**Key Concept:** High-pressure boilers operate at pressures significantly above atmospheric pressure, typically above 20 bar, and often exceeding 100 bar and even 200 bar in supercritical and ultra-supercritical power plants.

**Characteristics of High-Pressure Boilers:**

*   **Water-Tube Design:** Almost exclusively water-tube designs are used due to their inherent safety and ability to withstand high pressures.
*   **Increased Heating Surfaces:** Designed with extensive heating surfaces to maximize heat transfer.
*   **Forced Circulation:** In some high-pressure boilers, forced circulation of water through tubes is employed to ensure adequate cooling and prevent overheating, especially at very high pressures where natural circulation might be insufficient.
*   **Use of Special Alloys:** High temperatures and pressures necessitate the use of special steel alloys for tubes and drums to maintain structural integrity and prevent creep.
*   **Advanced Control Systems:** Require sophisticated control systems for managing water level, steam pressure, combustion, and safety.
*   **Superheaters and Reheaters:** Integral parts for achieving high steam temperatures, crucial for thermodynamic efficiency.

**Types of High-Pressure Boilers:**

*   **Supercritical Boilers:** Operate at pressures above the critical pressure of water (221.2 bar). At these pressures, there is no distinction between water and steam, and no phase change occurs. They offer higher thermal efficiency.
*   **Ultra-Supercritical Boilers:** Operate at even higher pressures and temperatures (e.g., >300 bar, >600°C) to achieve maximum thermal efficiency.

**Reference:**
*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017). *Power Plant Technology*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Chapter on Advanced Boiler Designs)
*   **Nag, P. K. (2017). *Power Plant Engineering*. McGraw Hill Education.** (Section on High Pressure Boilers)

## Boiler Mountings and Accessories

Boiler mountings are essential fittings attached directly to the boiler shell for safe operation. Accessories are auxiliary devices fitted to the boiler or the associated pipework to improve its efficiency, safety, or operation.

### Boiler Mountings (Essential for Safe Operation)

1.  **Water Level Indicator (Gauge Glass):**
    *   **Purpose:** To indicate the level of water inside the boiler.
    *   **Description:** A vertical glass tube fitted with valves at the top and bottom. Water from the boiler fills the glass to the same level as inside.
    *   **Importance:** Crucial to prevent damage from overheating (if water is too low) or water carry-over to the turbine (if water is too high).
    *   **Reference:** *Nag, P. K. (2017).*

2.  **Pressure Gauge:**
    *   **Purpose:** To indicate the pressure of steam inside the boiler.
    *   **Description:** A Bourdon gauge or diaphragm gauge connected to the steam space.
    *   **Importance:** Essential for monitoring the operating pressure and ensuring it stays within safe limits.
    *   **Reference:** *Nag, P. K. (2017).*

3.  **Safety Valves:**
    *   **Purpose:** To release excess steam pressure automatically when it exceeds a safe limit.
    *   **Description:** Spring-loaded or dead-weight loaded valves designed to open at a predetermined pressure and close when the pressure drops. Multiple safety valves are usually fitted.
    *   **Importance:** The most critical safety device to prevent catastrophic boiler explosion.
    *   **Reference:** *El Wakil, M. M. (2017).*

4.  **Steam Stop Valve (Main Stop Valve):**
    *   **Purpose:** To control the flow of steam from the boiler to the main steam pipe.
    *   **Description:** A gate valve or globe valve located on the steam outlet pipe.
    *   **Importance:** Allows isolation of the boiler from the steam line for maintenance or shutdown.
    *   **Reference:** *Nagpal, G. R., & Sharma, S. C. (2012).*

5.  **Blow-off Cock (or Valve):**
    *   **Purpose:** To periodically blow out the accumulated sediment, scale, and impurities from the bottom of the boiler.
    *   **Description:** A heavy-duty valve or cock fitted at the lowest point of the boiler.
    *   **Importance:** Helps maintain water quality and prevent scaling and sludge formation, improving efficiency and safety.
    *   **Reference:** *Gupta, M. K. (2012).*

6.  **Feed Check Valve (Non-Return Valve):**
    *   **Purpose:** To allow feedwater to enter the boiler but prevent boiler water from flowing back into the feed pipe when the feed pump stops.
    *   **Description:** A valve that opens in one direction only.
    *   **Importance:** Ensures continuous water supply to the boiler and protects the feed pump.
    *   **Reference:** *El Wakil, M. M. (2017).*

7.  **Manhole and Mudhole Doors:**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide access for inspection, cleaning, and repair inside the boiler.
    *   **Description:** Strong, securely bolted doors fitted with gaskets. Manholes are larger for human access, while mudholes are smaller for clearing sediment.
    *   **Importance:** Essential for routine maintenance and ensuring the internal condition of the boiler is maintained.
    *   **Reference:** *Nag, P. K. (2017).*

### Boiler Accessories (Improve Efficiency and Operation)

1.  **Economizer:**
    *   **Purpose:** To preheat the feedwater using the heat from the flue gases before it enters the boiler.
    *   **Description:** A heat exchanger consisting of a bank of tubes through which feedwater flows while hot flue gases pass over them.
    *   **Importance:** Improves boiler efficiency by recovering waste heat from flue gases, reducing fuel consumption.
    *   **Reference:** *El Wakil, M. M. (2017).*

2.  **Superheater:**
    *   **Purpose:** To further heat the saturated steam produced in the boiler to a higher temperature (superheated steam).
    *   **Description:** A bank of tubes, usually made of special steel alloys, placed in the path of hot flue gases, often after the boiler tubes but before the steam drum outlet.
    *   **Importance:** Superheated steam has higher energy content and reduces condensation losses in the turbine, leading to improved overall plant efficiency and reduced turbine erosion.
    *   **Reference:** *Nag, P. K. (2017).*

3.  **Air Preheater:**
    *   **Purpose:** To preheat the combustion air using the heat from the flue gases.
    *   **Description:** A heat exchanger (e.g., tubular or regenerative type) where flue gases and combustion air pass through.
    *   **Importance:** Improves combustion efficiency by providing hotter air, which leads to more complete burning of fuel and higher boiler efficiency. It also recovers residual heat from flue gases.
    *   **Reference:** *Nagpal, G. R., & Sharma, S. C. (2012).*

4.  **Steam Drum:**
    *   **Purpose:** Acts as a reservoir for steam and water, separates steam from water, and distributes water to the boiler tubes.
    *   **Description:** A large, horizontal cylindrical vessel at the top of a water-tube boiler.
    *   **Importance:** Essential for the operation of water-tube boilers, providing a central point for steam separation and water distribution.
    *   **Reference:** *El Wakil, M. M. (2017).*

5.  **Fusible Plug:**
    *   **Purpose:** A safety device designed to melt and release steam if the water level in the boiler drops dangerously low.
    *   **Description:** A metal plug made of an alloy with a low melting point, screwed into the boiler crown sheet. It is covered by water when the level is normal.
    *   **Importance:** Acts as a last resort safety measure if the water level indicator fails or is ignored, preventing overheating and potential explosion.
    *   **Reference:** *Gupta, M. K. (2012).*

6.  **Feed Pump:**
    *   **Purpose:** To supply feedwater to the boiler at a pressure higher than the boiler steam pressure.
    *   **Description:** Typically a centrifugal or reciprocating pump.
    *   **Importance:** Essential for maintaining the water level and continuous operation of the boiler.
    *   **Reference:** *Nag, P. K. (2017).*

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Fire-tube vs. Water-tube:** This is the most fundamental classification. Remember which contains hot gases inside tubes (fire-tube) and which contains water inside tubes (water-tube).
*   **Safety:** Boiler mountings are critical safety devices. Understand the purpose of each, especially safety valves and water level indicators.
*   **Efficiency:** Accessories like economizers and superheaters significantly improve boiler efficiency and steam quality.
*   **Application:** Different boiler types are suited for different applications based on capacity, pressure requirements, and fuel availability.
*   **High Pressure = High Efficiency:** Generally, higher steam pressures and temperatures (achieved with advanced boilers) lead to higher thermal efficiency in power plants.

## Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Differentiate between fire-tube and water-tube boilers, providing one advantage and one disadvantage for each.

**Answer:**
*   **Fire-Tube Boilers:**
    *   **Definition:** Hot gases pass through tubes surrounded by water.
    *   **Advantage:** Simpler construction, lower initial cost.
    *   **Disadvantage:** Limited steam capacity and lower efficiency.
*   **Water-Tube Boilers:**
    *   **Definition:** Water passes through tubes surrounded by hot gases.
    *   **Advantage:** Higher steam capacity and higher efficiency.
    *   **Disadvantage:** More complex construction and higher initial cost.

**Question 2:** What is the primary function of a safety valve on a boiler? Name two other essential boiler mountings.

**Answer:**
*   **Primary Function of Safety Valve:** To automatically release excess steam pressure from the boiler when it exceeds a safe predetermined limit, thus preventing a catastrophic explosion.
*   **Other Essential Mountings:**
    1.  Water Level Indicator (Gauge Glass)
    2.  Pressure Gauge
    3.  Steam Stop Valve
    4.  Blow-off Cock
    5.  Feed Check Valve

**Question 3:** How does a superheater improve the performance of a steam power plant?

**Answer:** A superheater heats the saturated steam produced in the boiler to a higher temperature (superheated steam). This increases the enthalpy of the steam, leading to:
    1.  **Higher Turbine Efficiency:** Superheated steam has more internal energy, allowing for more work extraction in the turbine.
    2.  **Reduced Condensation Losses:** The higher temperature reduces the tendency for steam to condense within the turbine stages.
    3.  **Reduced Turbine Erosion:** Less moisture in the steam passing through the turbine blades reduces erosion.

**Question 4:** Which type of boiler is characterized by two large internal furnace flues and is designed for horizontal operation?

**Answer:** The Lancashire boiler.

**Question 5:** Explain the role of an economizer in a boiler system.

**Answer:** An economizer is a heat exchanger that preheats the feedwater by utilizing the heat from the flue gases leaving the boiler. This recovered heat reduces the amount of fuel required to convert the feedwater into steam, thereby increasing the overall thermal efficiency of the boiler.
