## Module 2: Steam Generator Classifications

### Topic: Steam Generator Classifications

This module focuses on understanding the fundamental principles and various classifications of steam generators, which are crucial components in thermal power plants. This knowledge directly contributes to understanding the layout and working of steam power plants (CO1).

---

### 1. Introduction to Steam Generators

A **steam generator**, also known as a **boiler**, is a closed vessel in which water is heated, vaporized, and converted into steam under pressure. Steam generated in these units serves as the working fluid in steam turbines, driving generators to produce electricity. The efficiency and performance of the entire power plant are heavily dependent on the design and operation of the steam generator.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Working Fluid:** In thermal power plants, steam is the primary working fluid that carries thermal energy from the heat source to the turbine.
*   **Heat Transfer:** The fundamental process in a steam generator involves transferring heat from a fuel combustion or other high-temperature source to water.
*   **Pressure Vessel:** Steam generators are designed as pressure vessels to withstand the high pressures and temperatures of the steam.

**References:**

*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017).** *Power Plant Technology*. McGraw Hill Education. (Likely covers fundamental thermodynamic principles and heat transfer aspects relevant to boilers.)
*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. McGraw Hill Education. (This is a core textbook and will extensively cover boiler types, working, and performance.)

### 2. Classification of Steam Generators

Steam generators can be classified based on several criteria, reflecting their design, operation, and application. Understanding these classifications helps in selecting the appropriate boiler for a specific power plant.

#### 2.1. Classification Based on Water/Steam Circulation

This is a primary classification based on how water circulates through the boiler tubes and gets converted into steam.

##### 2.1.1. Fire-Tube Boilers

*   **Description:** In fire-tube boilers, hot combustion gases pass through tubes surrounded by water. The heat is transferred from the gases to the water through the tube walls.
*   **Working Principle:** Fuel is burnt in a furnace, producing hot flue gases. These gases flow through tubes, transferring heat to the surrounding water within the boiler shell.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Simple construction.
    *   Lower initial cost.
    *   Can operate at lower pressures.
    *   Less prone to tube failures due to internal pressure.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Limited to low-pressure and low-capacity applications.
    *   Lower thermal efficiency compared to water-tube boilers.
    *   Slower steaming rate.
    *   Shell is subjected to high temperatures, leading to potential shell damage.
*   **Examples:** Lancashire boiler, Cornish boiler, Cochran boiler, Scotch marine boiler.
*   **Applications:** Small industrial plants, heating systems, auxiliary boilers.
*   **Important Point:** The shell is exposed to high temperatures, making it a limiting factor for pressure and capacity.

##### 2.1.2. Water-Tube Boilers

*   **Description:** In water-tube boilers, water circulates through tubes, and the hot combustion gases flow over the outer surface of these tubes.
*   **Working Principle:** Fuel is burnt in a furnace, generating hot flue gases. These gases flow around the water-filled tubes, transferring heat to the water, which then vaporizes and forms steam.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Can operate at very high pressures and temperatures.
    *   Higher steam generation capacity.
    *   Higher thermal efficiency.
    *   Quicker steaming rate.
    *   Water is inside the tubes, so the pressure is contained within smaller diameter tubes, making it safer at high pressures.
    *   Can accommodate superheaters, economizers, and air preheaters more effectively.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   More complex construction.
    *   Higher initial cost.
    *   More susceptible to tube failures due to internal scale formation or impurities.
    *   Require treated and pure feedwater to prevent scaling and corrosion.
*   **Examples:** Babcock-Wilcox boiler, Foster-Wheeler boiler, Radiant boiler, Superheater boilers.
*   **Applications:** Thermal power plants, large industrial plants, marine propulsion.
*   **Important Point:** Water-tube boilers are the standard for large-scale power generation due to their ability to handle high pressures and capacities.

**References:**

*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Chapter 3, "Steam Boilers." This chapter will detail the principles, advantages, and disadvantages of both fire-tube and water-tube boilers with specific examples and diagrams.
*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017).** *Power Plant Technology*. Likely discusses the heat transfer mechanisms in both types, which is critical for understanding their efficiency differences.

**Practice Question 1:**
Differentiate between fire-tube and water-tube boilers, highlighting their respective advantages, disadvantages, and typical applications.

**Answer 1:**
| Feature          | Fire-Tube Boilers                                      | Water-Tube Boilers                                          |
| :--------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Circulation**  | Hot gases pass through tubes; water surrounds tubes.   | Water passes through tubes; hot gases surround tubes.       |
| **Pressure**     | Low to medium pressure (typically < 16 bar)            | High pressure (typically > 60 bar)                          |
| **Capacity**     | Low to medium steam generation capacity.               | High steam generation capacity.                             |
| **Efficiency**   | Lower thermal efficiency.                              | Higher thermal efficiency.                                  |
| **Construction** | Simpler, less expensive.                               | More complex, more expensive.                               |
| **Safety**       | Shell is the pressure boundary; prone to damage at high temp. | Tubes are pressure boundaries; safer at high pressure.      |
| **Feedwater**    | Less critical purity requirement.                      | High purity feedwater is essential to prevent scaling.      |
| **Applications** | Small industrial, heating, auxiliary.                  | Thermal power plants, large industrial.                     |

#### 2.2. Classification Based on Heating Surface

This classification relates to how the heating surface is arranged and how combustion gases interact with it.

##### 2.2.1. Vertical Boilers

*   **Description:** The shell is oriented vertically. Combustion gases and water circulation can be either fire-tube or water-tube configuration.
*   **Examples:** Cochran boiler (fire-tube), vertical fire-tube boiler.
*   **Applications:** Small industrial use, portable boilers.

##### 2.2.2. Horizontal Boilers

*   **Description:** The shell is oriented horizontally.
*   **Examples:** Lancashire boiler, Cornish boiler, Locomotive boiler, Scotch marine boiler (all fire-tube). Babcock-Wilcox boiler (water-tube).
*   **Applications:** Widely used in power plants and industries.

##### 2.2.3. Inclined Boilers

*   **Description:** The shell is inclined. This design aids in natural circulation due to gravity.
*   **Examples:** Cornish boiler can be slightly inclined.
*   **Applications:** Less common in modern power plants.

#### 2.3. Classification Based on Fuel Used

The type of fuel burned dictates the boiler's furnace design and auxiliaries.

##### 2.3.1. Coal-Fired Boilers

*   **Description:** Designed to burn coal efficiently. Requires systems for coal handling, feeding, ash removal, and flue gas cleaning.
*   **Types of Firing:**
    *   **Hand Firing:** Manual feeding of coal.
    *   **Mechanical Firing:**
        *   **Grate Firing:** Spreader stokers, chain grates, rocking grates, underfeed stokers. Coal is fed onto a grate where it burns.
        *   **Pulverized Coal Firing:** Coal is ground into a fine powder and blown into the furnace with air to burn in suspension. This is the most common method in large power plants for high efficiency.
*   **Important Point:** Pulverized coal firing offers better combustion control and higher efficiency.

##### 2.3.2. Oil-Fired Boilers

*   **Description:** Designed to burn liquid fuels (furnace oil, diesel oil). Requires oil burners to atomize the oil for efficient combustion.
*   **Advantages:** Easy to handle and control, higher efficiency compared to solid fuels, cleaner combustion.
*   **Disadvantages:** Fuel cost can be higher, requires storage tanks.

##### 2.3.3. Gas-Fired Boilers

*   **Description:** Designed to burn gaseous fuels (natural gas, blast furnace gas). Requires gas burners and gas supply systems.
*   **Advantages:** Cleanest combustion, highest efficiency, easiest to control, minimal ash handling.
*   **Disadvantages:** Fuel availability and cost can be a factor.

##### 2.3.4. Waste Heat Boilers (WHB)

*   **Description:** Recover heat from hot exhaust gases from other processes (e.g., gas turbines, industrial furnaces, chemical processes) to generate steam.
*   **Working Principle:** Hot exhaust gases flow over boiler tubes containing water, transferring heat and producing steam.
*   **Advantages:** Improves overall thermal efficiency by utilizing waste heat, reduces thermal pollution.
*   **Applications:** Combined cycle power plants (HRSGs - Heat Recovery Steam Generators), industrial plants.

**References:**

*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Section 3.5, "Classification of Boilers based on Fuel." This will elaborate on the specific designs for different fuel types.
*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017).** *Power Plant Technology*. Might discuss the combustion aspects and furnace design for different fuels.

**Practice Question 2:**
What are the primary advantages of using pulverized coal firing in large thermal power plants compared to grate firing?

**Answer 2:**
Pulverized coal firing offers several advantages for large thermal power plants:
*   **Higher Efficiency:** Fine coal particles burn more completely and rapidly, leading to better heat transfer and higher overall boiler efficiency.
*   **Better Combustion Control:** The combustion process is more easily controlled, allowing for optimization of air-fuel ratios.
*   **Higher Steam Temperature and Pressure:** Suitable for generating higher superheated steam temperatures and pressures required for modern turbines.
*   **Flexibility with Coal Types:** Can handle a wider range of coal types, including lower-grade coals, after proper pulverization.
*   **Reduced Smoke and Soot:** More complete combustion results in less smoke and soot emissions.

#### 2.4. Classification Based on Steam Pressure

The operating pressure of the steam generated is a critical design parameter.

##### 2.4.1. Low-Pressure Boilers

*   **Operating Pressure:** Typically below 1.7 bar (absolute).
*   **Applications:** Heating systems, small industrial processes.

##### 2.4.2. Medium-Pressure Boilers

*   **Operating Pressure:** Typically between 1.7 bar and 17 bar.
*   **Applications:** Many industrial processes, smaller power plants.

##### 2.4.3. High-Pressure Boilers

*   **Operating Pressure:** Typically above 17 bar, extending to very high pressures.
*   **Applications:** Large thermal power plants, where high-pressure steam is required for efficient turbine operation.

##### 2.4.4. Super High-Pressure / Critical Pressure Boilers

*   **Operating Pressure:** Above 221 bar (critical pressure of water).
*   **Description:** In supercritical boilers, the distinction between water and steam disappears. The working fluid is a single phase with properties of both. These boilers are designed for extremely high efficiencies.
*   **Advantages:** Highest thermal efficiency, elimination of boiler feed pump work (as there's no phase change), higher turbine efficiency.
*   **Disadvantages:** Very complex design, high cost, requires very pure feedwater.
*   **Important Point:** Supercritical and ultra-supercritical steam cycles are used in modern high-efficiency thermal power plants.

**References:**

*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Chapter 3 will likely detail these pressure classifications and their implications for boiler design and cycle efficiency.

#### 2.5. Classification Based on Steam Temperature

Steam temperature is crucial for turbine efficiency. Superheating steam significantly increases the plant's thermal efficiency.

##### 2.5.1. Saturated Steam Boilers

*   **Description:** Produce steam at the saturation temperature corresponding to the boiler pressure. The steam contains no superheat.
*   **Applications:** Older power plants or specific industrial processes where superheated steam is not required.

##### 2.5.2. Superheated Steam Boilers

*   **Description:** Produce steam at a temperature higher than the saturation temperature for the given pressure. This is achieved by passing saturated steam through a **superheater**, which is a section of tubes exposed to hot flue gases.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Increases the thermal efficiency of the Rankine cycle.
    *   Reduces moisture content in the turbine exhaust, preventing blade erosion.
*   **Applications:** All modern thermal power plants.

##### 2.5.3. Reheated Steam Boilers

*   **Description:** In addition to superheating, steam is passed through a **reheater** after partially expanding in the high-pressure turbine stages. It is then reheated in the boiler and sent to the low-pressure turbine stages.
*   **Advantages:** Further increases thermal efficiency, reduces exhaust moisture in the turbine.
*   **Applications:** Large modern thermal power plants.

**Important Point:** Superheating and reheating are critical for maximizing the efficiency of thermal power plants by improving the Rankine cycle performance (CO2).

**References:**

*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017).** *Power Plant Technology*. Will likely discuss the thermodynamics of superheating and reheating and their impact on cycle efficiency.
*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Chapter 3 will cover superheaters and reheaters as integral parts of boiler systems.

#### 2.6. Classification Based on Furnace Type

The furnace is where the combustion of fuel takes place.

##### 2.6.1. External Furnace Boilers

*   **Description:** The furnace is a separate unit built around the boiler.
*   **Examples:** Lancashire boiler, Cornish boiler.
*   **Applications:** Older designs, smaller capacity boilers.

##### 2.6.2. Internal Furnace Boilers

*   **Description:** The furnace is located within the boiler shell or casing.
*   **Examples:** Scotch marine boiler, Cochran boiler.
*   **Applications:** Compact designs, often used in marine applications and smaller industrial units.

#### 2.7. Classification Based on Circulation Method (Revisited for Water-Tube)

Within water-tube boilers, circulation can be natural or forced.

##### 2.7.1. Natural Circulation Boilers

*   **Description:** Circulation of water and steam mixture is driven by density differences between the colder water in the downcomers and the hotter, less dense steam-water mixture in the riser tubes. This relies on gravity.
*   **Limitations:** Effective up to moderate pressures (around 200 bar) because at higher pressures, the density difference between water and steam reduces, making natural circulation less effective.

##### 2.7.2. Forced Circulation Boilers

*   **Description:** A pump is used to circulate water through the boiler tubes, ensuring positive flow regardless of pressure.
*   **Advantages:** Allows for higher operating pressures and capacities, more compact design, better heat transfer control.
*   **Examples:** Loeffler boiler, Benson boiler, La-Mont boiler.
*   **Applications:** High-pressure power plants.

**Important Point:** Forced circulation is essential for supercritical and ultra-supercritical boiler designs where natural circulation is insufficient.

**References:**

*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Discusses circulation methods in detail within the water-tube boiler sections.

#### 2.8. Classification Based on Combustion Method

This relates to how the fuel and air are mixed and combusted.

##### 2.8.1. Stokers

*   **Description:** Mechanical devices that feed fuel onto a grate and control the airflow for combustion.
*   **Types:** Spreader stokers, chain grates, traveling grates, underfeed stokers.
*   **Applications:** Primarily for solid fuels (coal).

##### 2.8.2. Pulverized Fuel Firing

*   **Description:** Coal is ground into a fine powder and blown into the furnace with air, burning in suspension.
*   **Advantages:** High efficiency, quick response, suitable for large capacities.
*   **Applications:** Most modern thermal power plants.

##### 2.8.3. Oil/Gas Firing

*   **Description:** Fuel is atomized and mixed with air using burners.
*   **Advantages:** Clean combustion, easy control, high efficiency.
*   **Applications:** Power plants and industrial boilers.

##### 2.8.4. Fluidized Bed Combustion (FBC)

*   **Description:** Fuel particles are suspended in a vigorously agitated bed of inert material (like sand or ash) and air. This creates a "fluidized" state.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Ability to burn low-grade fuels (high ash, high sulfur content).
    *   Improved combustion efficiency.
    *   Reduced SOx and NOx emissions due to in-situ sulfur capture (using limestone or dolomite in the bed) and lower combustion temperatures.
*   **Applications:** Industrial boilers, power generation, especially where low-grade fuels are available or environmental regulations are strict.
*   **Important Point:** FBC is an environmentally friendly combustion technology.

**References:**

*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Chapter 3 will cover different firing methods, including stokers and pulverized fuel firing. Fluidized Bed Combustion might be covered in later chapters related to advanced combustion technologies or environmental aspects.
*   **Gupta, M. K. (2012).** *Power Plant Engineering*. May offer insights into various combustion techniques and their impact on boiler design and performance.

**Practice Question 3:**
Explain the principle of Fluidized Bed Combustion (FBC) and discuss its environmental advantages.

**Answer 3:**
**Princ of FBC:**
In Fluidized Bed Combustion, fuel particles are mixed with an inert bed material (such as sand, ash, or alumina) and air. Air is blown upwards through a perforated grate at high velocity, causing the bed material and fuel particles to behave like a fluid. This "fluidized" state ensures excellent mixing of fuel and air, leading to highly efficient and uniform combustion.

**Environmental Advantages of FBC:**
*   **Reduced SOx Emissions:** Limestone or dolomite can be added to the fluidized bed. These materials react with sulfur dioxide (SO2) produced during combustion to form solid calcium sulfate (CaSO4), effectively capturing sulfur in the bed. This is known as in-situ desulfurization.
*   **Reduced NOx Emissions:** The combustion temperatures in FBC are typically lower (800-950°C) compared to conventional boilers. Lower temperatures suppress the formation of thermal NOx (NOx formed from the reaction of nitrogen and oxygen at high temperatures).
*   **Fuel Flexibility:** FBC can efficiently burn a wide range of fuels, including low-grade coals, lignite, biomass, and industrial wastes, which are often more environmentally problematic to burn in conventional boilers.
*   **Reduced Ash Disposal Issues:** The ash generated is often in a granular form, which can be easier to handle and dispose of, or sometimes can be utilized in other applications.

### 3. Components of a Modern Steam Generator (Water-Tube Boiler)

Modern high-capacity steam generators in thermal power plants are complex systems. Understanding their key components is crucial for understanding the overall plant layout (CO1).

*   **Furnace/Combustion Chamber:** Where fuel is burned. Designed for efficient combustion and heat transfer.
*   **Boiler Tubes:** Carry water and steam. This includes:
    *   **Water Walls (Water-tube furnace walls):** Tubes forming the furnace walls, absorbing radiant heat directly from the flame.
    *   **Convection Tubes:** Arranged in banks to absorb heat from hot flue gases after they leave the furnace.
*   **Superheater:** Increases the temperature of saturated steam to superheated steam.
*   **Reheater:** Reheats steam after partial expansion in the high-pressure turbine.
*   **Economizer:** Heats the feedwater using the heat from exiting flue gases before it enters the boiler drum, improving efficiency.
*   **Air Preheater:** Heats the combustion air using the heat from exiting flue gases, improving combustion efficiency.
*   **Boiler Drum:** A large vessel at the top of the boiler where steam separates from water and from which saturated steam is drawn. It also acts as a reservoir for feedwater.
*   **Downcomers:** Large pipes that carry water from the drum to the lower headers, feeding the riser tubes.
*   **Risers:** Tubes where water and steam mixture flows upwards to the drum.
*   **Burners:** Atomize and mix fuel with air for combustion.
*   **Ash Handling System:** Removes ash produced from the combustion of solid fuels.
*   **Flue Gas Desulphurisation (FGD) and Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs):** Systems to remove pollutants from exhaust gases.

**References:**

*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Chapters on Boiler components will provide detailed descriptions and diagrams of these parts.
*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017).** *Power Plant Technology*. Will likely discuss the thermal performance and heat transfer characteristics of these components.
*   **Nagpal, G. R., Sharma, S. C. (2012).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Another valuable resource for detailed component descriptions.

### 4. Performance Parameters of Steam Generators

Understanding how to evaluate the performance of a steam generator is important for operational efficiency and economic considerations (CO5).

*   **Boiler Efficiency ($\eta_{boiler}$):** The ratio of the useful heat output (enthalpy of steam generated) to the heat input (calorific value of fuel burnt).
    *   $\eta_{boiler} = \frac{\text{Heat absorbed by water}}{\text{Heat supplied by fuel}} = \frac{m_s (h_g - h_f)}{m_f \times CV}$
        *   $m_s$: mass flow rate of steam (kg/s)
        *   $h_g$: enthalpy of steam (kJ/kg)
        *   $h_f$: enthalpy of feedwater (kJ/kg)
        *   $m_f$: mass flow rate of fuel (kg/s)
        *   $CV$: calorific value of fuel (kJ/kg)
*   **Boiler Evaporative Capacity:** The rate at which water is converted into steam, usually expressed in kg/h or tonnes/h.
*   **Equivalent Evaporation:** The amount of water evaporated per kg of fuel at specified conditions (e.g., from and at 100°C). This is used for comparing boilers operating under different conditions.
*   **Heat Rate:** The heat energy input required to produce a unit of electrical energy output. Lower heat rates indicate higher overall plant efficiency.

**Important Point:** Boiler efficiency is a key indicator of performance and significantly impacts the overall cost of power generation.

**References:**

*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Chapter 3 will include formulas and methods for calculating boiler performance.
*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017).** *Power Plant Technology*. Thermodynamics and heat transfer principles will be the basis for understanding these performance calculations.

**Practice Question 4:**
A boiler produces 50,000 kg of steam per hour at a pressure of 100 bar and a temperature of 500°C. The feedwater is supplied at 150°C. The boiler consumes 6,000 kg of coal per hour with a calorific value of 30,000 kJ/kg. Calculate the boiler efficiency.
(Assume enthalpy of steam at 100 bar, 500°C is 3373 kJ/kg and enthalpy of feedwater at 150°C is 632 kJ/kg from steam tables).

**Answer 4:**
Given:
*   $m_s = 50,000$ kg/h
*   $m_f = 6,000$ kg/h
*   $CV = 30,000$ kJ/kg
*   $h_g = 3373$ kJ/kg (enthalpy of steam)
*   $h_f = 632$ kJ/kg (enthalpy of feedwater)

Heat absorbed by water ($Q_{out}$):
$Q_{out} = m_s (h_g - h_f)$
$Q_{out} = 50,000 \text{ kg/h} \times (3373 - 632) \text{ kJ/kg}$
$Q_{out} = 50,000 \times 2741 \text{ kJ/h}$
$Q_{out} = 137,050,000 \text{ kJ/h}$

Heat supplied by fuel ($Q_{in}$):
$Q_{in} = m_f \times CV$
$Q_{in} = 6,000 \text{ kg/h} \times 30,000 \text{ kJ/kg}$
$Q_{in} = 180,000,000 \text{ kJ/h}$

Boiler Efficiency ($\eta_{boiler}$):
$\eta_{boiler} = \frac{Q_{out}}{Q_{in}} \times 100\%$
$\eta_{boiler} = \frac{137,050,000 \text{ kJ/h}}{180,000,000 \text{ kJ/h}} \times 100\%$
$\eta_{boiler} = 0.76138 \times 100\%$
$\eta_{boiler} \approx 76.14\%$

### 5. Modern Trends and Developments in Steam Generators

*   **Supercritical and Ultra-Supercritical (USC) Boilers:** Operating at pressures above the critical pressure of water (221 bar) for higher thermal efficiencies (e.g., 45-50%).
*   **Fluidized Bed Combustion (FBC):** As discussed earlier, for better fuel flexibility and reduced emissions.
*   **Combined Cycle Power Plants (CCPPs):** Utilization of Heat Recovery Steam Generators (HRSGs) to capture waste heat from gas turbines to produce steam, significantly improving overall plant efficiency.
*   **Advanced Materials:** Use of alloys capable of withstanding higher temperatures and pressures for improved performance and lifespan.
*   **Environmental Controls:** Integration of sophisticated pollution control systems (e.g., advanced ESPs, SCR for NOx reduction, FGD).

**References:**

*   **El Wakil, M. M. (2017).** *Power Plant Technology*. Likely has sections on advanced boiler technologies and modern power plant design trends.
*   **Nag, P. K. (2017).** *Power Plant Engineering*. Chapters on modern thermal power plants and emerging technologies.
