# Non-Destructive Testing: Module 2 - Magnetic Particle Testing & Eddy Current Testing (Focus on Equipment, Applications, and Magnetization)

## **Module 2: Magnetic Particle Testing (MPT) - Magnetization Techniques**

This section revisits key magnetization techniques covered in Module 2, providing a foundational understanding crucial for appreciating MPT equipment and applications.

### **1. Fundamental Principles of Magnetic Particle Testing (MPT)**

*   **Concept:** MPT is a non-destructive method used to detect surface and near-surface discontinuities in ferromagnetic materials. It relies on the principle of magnetic flux leakage.
*   **Ferromagnetism:** Only ferromagnetic materials (like iron, nickel, cobalt, and their alloys) can be effectively tested using MPT. These materials can be strongly magnetized.
*   **Flux Leakage:** When a ferromagnetic material is magnetized, its magnetic field lines are generally contained within the material. However, if a discontinuity (like a crack or inclusion) exists on or near the surface, it disrupts the uniformity of the magnetic field, causing some magnetic flux to "leak" out into the surrounding air.
*   **Particle Attraction:** Fine magnetic particles (either dry powder or suspended in a liquid carrier) are applied to the surface of the magnetized part. These particles are attracted to the areas of flux leakage, forming visible indications that reveal the presence, location, and extent of the discontinuity.

### **2. Methods of Magnetization**

To induce a magnetic field in the part under test, various magnetization methods are employed. The choice of method depends on the geometry of the part, the type of discontinuities being sought, and the sensitivity required.

#### **2.1. Head Shot Technique**

*   **Principle:** This technique involves passing a high current directly through the workpiece itself by placing the current contacts (probes) on opposite ends of the part.
*   **Magnetization:** A strong circular magnetic field is created within the part due to the current flow. This field is perpendicular to the direction of current flow.
*   **Applications:** Primarily used for detecting longitudinal discontinuities (e.g., cracks running parallel to the length of the part) such as those found in bars, shafts, and pipes.
*   **Important Considerations:**
    *   The contact points can cause localized heating and arcing, potentially damaging the surface.
    *   Proper contact surface preparation is essential to ensure good electrical conductivity and minimize damage.
    *   The technique is effective for short to medium-length parts.

#### **2.2. Cold Shot Technique (sometimes referred to as Coil Magnetization or Solenoid Method)**

*   **Principle:** The workpiece is placed inside a coil carrying a high AC or DC current. The magnetic field generated by the coil encircles the workpiece.
*   **Magnetization:** A longitudinal magnetic field is created within the part, running parallel to the axis of the coil.
*   **Applications:** Ideal for detecting transverse discontinuities (e.g., cracks perpendicular to the length of the part). Common for testing circular parts like rings, gears, and shafts.
*   **Important Considerations:**
    *   The magnetic field is uniform along the length of the coil, providing good coverage.
    *   The workpiece must fit within the coil's aperture.
    *   The sensitivity can be adjusted by varying the current and the number of turns in the coil.

#### **2.3. Central Conductor Testing**

*   **Principle:** A conductor (rod or cable) is inserted through the hollow center of a workpiece (like a pipe or ring). A high current is passed through this central conductor.
*   **Magnetization:** The current flow in the central conductor generates a circular magnetic field that passes radially outwards through the workpiece.
*   **Applications:** Specifically designed for testing hollow cylindrical parts, such as pipes, tubes, and rings, for discontinuities that lie on the inner or outer surface.
*   **Important Considerations:**
    *   This method is highly effective for internal and external surface flaws in hollow items.
    *   The central conductor must be well-insulated to prevent short circuits.
    *   The current density must be sufficient to induce the required magnetic field strength.

#### **2.4. Magnetization Using Yokes**

*   **Principle:** A portable yoke, typically with electromagnets or permanent magnets, is used to create a magnetic field that bridges two points on the surface of the workpiece.
*   **Magnetization:** The magnetic field lines are directed into the part at one pole of the yoke and emerge at the other pole, creating a localized magnetic field between them.
*   **Types:**
    *   **AC Yokes:** Provide a strong magnetic field but are less sensitive to subsurface flaws.
    *   **DC Yokes (often battery-powered):** Generate a more uniform and stronger magnetic field, making them more sensitive to both surface and slightly subsurface flaws.
*   **Applications:** Excellent for inspecting irregular shapes, large structures, and localized areas where other methods are impractical. Common for inspecting welds, castings, and forgings.
*   **Important Considerations:**
    *   The pole spacing of the yoke is critical for effective magnetization.
    *   The yoke must be placed firmly against the surface to ensure good magnetic coupling.
    *   Multiple applications with different orientations are often necessary to detect flaws in various directions.

### **Module 2 Practice Questions (Magnetization Techniques)**

1.  **Question:** For which type of discontinuity would the head shot technique be most effective?
    *   **Answer:** Longitudinal discontinuities (cracks running parallel to the length).

2.  **Question:** You need to inspect a hollow pipe for surface cracks. Which magnetization technique is most suitable?
    *   **Answer:** Central conductor testing.

3.  **Question:** What is the primary advantage of using a DC yoke over an AC yoke for Magnetic Particle Testing?
    *   **Answer:** DC yokes generate a more uniform and stronger magnetic field, leading to greater sensitivity for both surface and slightly subsurface flaws.

4.  **Question:** Explain the basic principle behind the cold shot technique.
    *   **Answer:** The workpiece is placed inside a coil, and the current flowing through the coil generates a longitudinal magnetic field that magnetizes the workpiece.

## **Module 2: Eddy Current Testing (ECT) - Equipment, Applications, and Related Concepts**

While Module 2 primarily focuses on MPT magnetization, the description provided includes a significant portion on Eddy Current Testing (ECT). This section will cover the equipment, applications, and key concepts of ECT as requested.

### **1. Fundamentals of Eddy Current Testing (ECT)**

*   **Concept:** ECT is a non-destructive method that uses electromagnetic induction to detect surface and near-surface flaws in electrically conductive materials.
*   **Principle:** A time-varying magnetic field is produced by a probe (coil) driven by an alternating current. When this probe is brought near a conductive material, the changing magnetic field induces circulating currents, known as eddy currents, within the material.
*   **Flaw Detection:** Discontinuities within the material (cracks, voids, inclusions) or changes in material properties disrupt the flow of eddy currents. This disruption causes a change in the impedance of the probe coil, which can be measured and analyzed.

### **2. Eddy Current Testing Equipment and Accessories**

ECT equipment typically consists of several key components:

#### **2.1. Eddy Current Instrument (Eddy Current Tester/Scanner)**

*   **Function:** The core of the ECT system. It generates the alternating current for the probe, processes the signals received from the probe, and displays the results.
*   **Features:**
    *   **Frequency Control:** Allows adjustment of the test frequency, which influences the depth of penetration of eddy currents and sensitivity to different flaw depths. Higher frequencies are more sensitive to surface flaws, while lower frequencies penetrate deeper.
    *   **Gain/Sensitivity Control:** Adjusts the amplification of the received signal.
    *   **Phase Control:** Allows for the manipulation of the phase of the eddy current signal to optimize the separation of signals from different sources (e.g., discontinuities vs. material conductivity).
    *   **Display:** Can be analog (meters) or digital (screens showing impedance planes, waveforms, or numerical values).
    *   **Signal Processing:** Advanced instruments may include digital signal processing (DSP) for noise reduction and enhanced flaw detection.

#### **2.2. Eddy Current Probes (Coils)**

*   **Function:** These are the transducers that generate the magnetic field and detect the changes in eddy current flow.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Absolute Probes:** Consist of a single coil whose impedance changes due to eddy currents. Used for measuring properties like conductivity or for detecting absolute defects.
    *   **Differential Probes:** Consist of two opposing coils connected in a way that their signals normally cancel out. A discontinuity causes an imbalance, producing a significant signal. Highly sensitive to localized flaws.
    *   **Reference Probes:** Used in some setups for comparison.
    *   **Array Probes:** Contain multiple coils arranged in a specific pattern, allowing for simultaneous testing of a larger area or enhanced spatial resolution.
    *   **Through-Coil Probes:** For inspecting thin conductive materials.
    *   **Bobbin Coils:** Cylindrical coils used for inspecting tubes from the inside.
    *   **Surface Probes:** Designed to be placed directly on the surface of the material.
*   **Accessories:** Probe holders, positioning guides, and calibration standards are important accessories.

#### **2.3. Calibration Standards**

*   **Function:** Essential for setting up the ECT instrument and verifying its performance. These are typically pieces of the same material as the workpiece, containing known flaws (e.g., drilled holes, slots of specific depths and lengths) or variations in conductivity.
*   **Importance:** Ensure accurate and reliable test results.

#### **2.4. Signal Processing and Data Acquisition Systems**

*   **Function:** Modern ECT systems often incorporate computers and specialized software for data recording, analysis, and visualization.
*   **Features:** Real-time display of readings, storage of test data, automated flaw sizing and characterization, and reporting.

### **3. Various Applications of Eddy Current Testing (ECT)**

ECT is a versatile NDT method with a wide range of applications, primarily focused on conductive materials.

#### **3.1. Conductivity Measurement**

*   **Principle:** The conductivity of a material directly affects the magnitude of eddy currents induced within it. Higher conductivity leads to stronger eddy currents and a change in the probe's impedance.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Alloy Sorting:** Distinguishing between different alloys that may appear similar.
    *   **Heat Treatment Verification:** Confirming that a material has undergone the correct heat treatment, as this alters its conductivity.
    *   **Material Identification:** Verifying the composition of materials.
*   **Example:** Sorting aluminum alloys or checking the proper heat treatment of aircraft components.

#### **3.2. Hardness Measurement**

*   **Principle:** Hardness in ferromagnetic materials is often related to their microstructure, which in turn affects their magnetic permeability and electrical conductivity. These properties influence eddy current behavior.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Heat Treatment Control:** Assessing the hardness of steel parts after hardening and tempering processes.
    *   **Quality Control:** Ensuring parts meet hardness specifications.
*   **Example:** Checking the hardness of gears or bearings to ensure they have the desired wear resistance.

#### **3.3. Defect Detection (Surface and Near-Surface Flaws)**

*   **Principle:** As discussed earlier, discontinuities disrupt eddy current flow, causing measurable changes in the probe's signal.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Crack Detection:** Identifying surface and subsurface cracks in metals, especially in critical components like aircraft structures, engine parts, and welds.
    *   **Corrosion Detection:** Detecting subsurface corrosion in layered structures or under coatings.
    *   **Porosity and Inclusions:** Identifying internal voids or non-metallic inclusions near the surface.
    *   **Weld Inspection:** Detecting surface and near-surface flaws in welded joints.
*   **Example:** Inspecting turbine blades for fatigue cracks or checking the surface of a railway axle for crack initiation.

#### **3.4. Coating Thickness Measurement**

*   **Principle:** The presence of a non-conductive coating on a conductive substrate, or vice-versa, affects the eddy current path. The thickness of the coating influences the magnitude of the eddy currents induced in the underlying substrate.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Paint and Plating Thickness:** Measuring the thickness of paint, plating, or other non-conductive coatings on metallic substrates.
    *   **Galvanized Coatings:** Measuring the thickness of zinc coatings on steel.
*   **Example:** Measuring the thickness of paint on an automobile body or the thickness of a chrome plating on a component.

**Important Note:** For ECT, the material being tested must be electrically conductive. The sensitivity to different types of flaws depends on the testing frequency, probe design, and material properties.

### **Module 2 Practice Questions (ECT Applications)**

1.  **Question:** A manufacturing process involves heat-treating steel parts. How can ECT be used to verify the success of this process?
    *   **Answer:** By measuring the electrical conductivity or hardness of the parts, which are affected by heat treatment.

2.  **Question:** You are inspecting an aluminum aircraft component for surface cracks. Which ECT application is most relevant?
    *   **Answer:** Defect detection.

3.  **Question:** What is the primary advantage of using a differential probe in ECT for crack detection?
    *   **Answer:** Differential probes are highly sensitive to localized flaws because their signals normally cancel out, and any disruption causes a significant signal.

4.  **Question:** An automotive manufacturer needs to ensure consistent paint thickness on their car bodies. How can ECT assist in this quality control process?
    *   **Answer:** By measuring the thickness of the non-conductive paint coating on the conductive metal substrate.

5.  **Question:** How does the test frequency affect the depth of penetration of eddy currents in ECT?
    *   **Answer:** Higher frequencies result in shallower penetration, making them more sensitive to surface flaws. Lower frequencies penetrate deeper, making them suitable for detecting subsurface flaws.

### **Highlighting Important Points to Remember:**

*   **MPT is for ferromagnetic materials only.**
*   **Magnetic flux leakage is the fundamental principle of MPT.**
*   **Magnetization techniques (head shot, cold shot, central conductor, yokes) are chosen based on part geometry and flaw orientation.**
*   **ECT relies on electromagnetic induction to create eddy currents.**
*   **ECT is applicable to electrically conductive materials.**
*   **ECT can be used for conductivity, hardness, defect detection, and coating thickness measurements.**
*   **Probe design (absolute vs. differential) significantly impacts ECT sensitivity.**
*   **Test frequency is a critical parameter in ECT for controlling penetration depth and flaw detection capability.**
*   **Calibration standards are crucial for accurate ECT results.**
