# Product Design and Innovation

## Module 1: Introduction: Classification/ Specifications of Products

### Topic: Product Mix

---

### 1. Introduction to Product Mix

The **product mix** refers to the total number of product lines and individual products that a company offers to its customers. It's a crucial aspect of a company's product strategy, as it determines the breadth and depth of its offerings. A well-defined product mix can help a company achieve its business objectives, cater to diverse customer needs, and gain a competitive advantage.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Product Line:** A group of related products under a single brand, sold by the same company, that are similar in function, target market, or price.
    *   *Example:* Apple's iPhone product line (iPhone 15, iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max).
*   **Product Item (SKU - Stock Keeping Unit):** A specific version of a product within a product line, uniquely identified by its features, size, color, etc.
    *   *Example:* iPhone 15 Pro, 256GB, Natural Titanium.
*   **Product Width:** The number of different product lines the company offers.
    *   *Example:* Samsung offers product lines in smartphones, televisions, appliances, and laptops.
*   **Product Depth:** The number of product items within each product line.
    *   *Example:* Within Samsung's smartphone product line, they offer various models, storage capacities, and color options.

**Reference Integration:**

*   **Ulrich & Eppinger (2003):** While "product mix" might not be explicitly detailed as a core chapter title, the concepts of product portfolios and platform strategies discussed in chapters related to "Product Development Strategy" implicitly touch upon managing a range of products. A well-managed product mix is essential for effective platform development and leveraging economies of scale.
*   **Otto & Wood (2000):** Their focus on "new Product Development" and "Reverse Engineering" implies the management of existing products (which form the basis of the product mix) and the introduction of new ones. Understanding the existing product mix is a prerequisite for making informed decisions about new product development.

### 2. Dimensions of Product Mix

Understanding the dimensions of a product mix helps in analyzing and managing a company's product portfolio.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Product Mix Width:** As defined above, the number of distinct product lines.
    *   *Example:* A company selling only smartphones has a width of 1. A company selling smartphones, laptops, and tablets has a width of 3.
*   **Product Mix Length:** The total number of individual product items in the company's product mix.
    *   *Example:* If a company has 3 product lines, and each line has 5 product items, the length is 3 * 5 = 15.
*   **Product Mix Depth:** The number of variations for each product within a product line. This includes different sizes, colors, features, etc.
    *   *Example:* Within a smartphone line, variations could be storage capacity (128GB, 256GB, 512GB) and color (Black, White, Blue). If there are 3 storage options and 3 color options, the depth for that product item could be considered 9 (3x3).
*   **Product Mix Consistency:** The degree to which the product lines are related in terms of end use, production requirements, distribution channels, or customer groups.
    *   *Example:* A company that manufactures both bicycles and bicycle parts has high consistency. A company selling bicycles and software has low consistency.

**Importance:**

*   **Customer Segmentation:** Different product lines can target different customer segments.
*   **Risk Diversification:** A wider product mix can reduce reliance on a single product or market.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Understanding the mix helps in allocating R&D, marketing, and production resources effectively.
*   **Market Coverage:** A well-designed mix can cover a broader spectrum of market needs.

### 3. Managing the Product Mix: Strategies and Decisions

Companies actively manage their product mix to adapt to changing market conditions, customer preferences, and competitive landscapes.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Product Line Extension:** Adding new varieties to an existing product line. This can involve new features, flavors, colors, sizes, or improved versions.
    *   *Example:* Coca-Cola introducing Diet Coke, Cherry Coke, and Coke Zero.
*   **Brand Extension:** Using an established brand name for a new product in a different category.
    *   *Example:* Virgin Group extending its brand from music to airlines, telecommunications, and finance.
*   **Product Simplification (Rationalization):** Reducing the number of product items or product lines offered to streamline operations, reduce costs, and focus on core products.
    *   *Example:* General Electric discontinuing some of its smaller appliance lines to focus on high-end refrigerators and washing machines.
*   **Product Deletion:** Discontinuing products that are no longer profitable, relevant, or strategically aligned.
    *   *Example:* Kodak discontinuing its film camera business due to the rise of digital photography.

**Reference Integration:**

*   **Ulrich & Eppinger (2003):** Chapters on "Product Development Strategy" and "Product Portfolios" will be highly relevant here. They discuss how companies decide which products to develop and how to manage a portfolio of products over their life cycles. Decisions about product line extensions and deletions are integral to managing the overall product development strategy and the product portfolio.
*   **Otto & Wood (2000):** Their emphasis on "new Product Development" directly relates to adding new items to the product mix. The principles of identifying market needs and developing solutions are the foundation for extending or altering the product mix.

### 4. Product Mix in Relation to Product Life Cycle (CO1)

The product mix strategy is heavily influenced by the **product life cycle (PLC)**. Each product within the mix will be at a different stage of its PLC, requiring different management approaches.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Product Life Cycle (PLC):** The stages a product goes through from its introduction into the market until its eventual decline or withdrawal. The typical stages are:
    1.  **Introduction:** Low sales, high costs, low profit (often negative). Focus on building awareness and trial.
    2.  **Growth:** Rapidly increasing sales, rising profits, market acceptance. Focus on market penetration and brand preference.
    3.  **Maturity:** Sales growth slows down, profits peak and may start to decline. Intense competition. Focus on market share and differentiation.
    4.  **Decline:** Sales and profits fall. Focus on harvesting, divesting, or discontinuing.

**How PLC affects Product Mix:**

*   **Introduction Stage:** Companies may launch a few core products within a new product line. The product mix might be narrow initially.
*   **Growth Stage:** Companies may extend their product lines by adding new features or variations to capitalize on growing demand. The product mix deepens.
*   **Maturity Stage:** Companies might focus on product differentiation and minor improvements to maintain market share. They might also prune less successful variations to simplify the mix. The focus is on optimizing the existing mix.
*   **Decline Stage:** Companies will strategically decide to discontinue products that are no longer profitable or strategically viable, thus reducing the product mix width or depth.

**Example:**

Consider a smartphone manufacturer.
*   A new flagship model might be in its **Introduction** phase.
*   A slightly older model with proven popularity might be in its **Growth** phase.
*   A popular, established model with many variations will be in its **Maturity** phase.
*   An older model with declining sales will be in its **Decline** phase.

The company's product mix will comprise products from all these stages, requiring different marketing, sales, and support strategies for each.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:**

*   **CO1: Determine the life cycle of a product and product development process (Knowledge Level: K2)**
    *   This section directly addresses determining the life cycle of a product and how it impacts strategic decisions like managing the product mix. Understanding where a product sits in its PLC is crucial for managing its place within the overall product mix.

### 5. Product Mix in Relation to Robust Design and Conceptual Design (CO2)

The decisions made during the conceptual design phase and the principles of robust design heavily influence the final product mix.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Conceptual Design:** The initial stage of product development where a product idea is explored and refined. This involves defining the core functionality, target market, and potential product variations.
    *   *Example:* A car manufacturer's concept for a new electric SUV might involve multiple battery sizes, seating configurations, and performance levels.
*   **Robust Design:** Designing products that perform consistently well across a wide range of variations in manufacturing processes and environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity, user error).
    *   *Example:* A digital camera designed to function reliably whether used in bright sunlight or low light, or by a novice or an expert photographer.

**How Conceptual Design and Robust Design affect Product Mix:**

*   **Conceptual Design:** The initial conceptualization of a product family or platform can directly lead to a diverse product mix. If the concept allows for modularity and easy adaptation, it facilitates creating multiple product variations for different market segments.
    *   *Example:* A platform strategy for a car manufacturer (e.g., a shared chassis and powertrain for multiple SUV models) enables a wider product mix of SUVs with different body styles, sizes, and features.
*   **Robust Design:** Products designed with robustness in mind often have fewer critical failure points and perform more reliably. This can lead to a mix of products that are perceived as high-quality and dependable, potentially reducing the need for excessive support or variations to overcome performance issues. It also means that a core robust design can be adapted with minor changes to create different product items without compromising overall quality.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:**

*   **CO2: Develop knowledge of robust design and conceptual design (Knowledge Level: K2)**
    *   This section links conceptual design to the creation of product lines and product mix strategies, and how robust design contributes to a reliable and consistent product offering within that mix.

### 6. Product Mix in Relation to Design for Manufacturing and Assembly (DFMA) (CO3)

DFMA principles impact the feasibility, cost, and speed of introducing new products and variations into the product mix.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Design for Manufacturing (DFM):** Designing products to be easily and cost-effectively manufactured. This involves considering material selection, manufacturing processes, tolerances, and minimizing the number of manufacturing steps.
    *   *Example:* Using standard fasteners instead of custom-designed ones; designing parts with simpler geometries that are easier to machine.
*   **Design for Assembly (DFA):** Designing products to be easily and cost-effectively assembled. This focuses on reducing the number of parts, minimizing the types of fasteners, and designing parts for easy handling and insertion.
    *   *Example:* Designing a product where components snap together rather than requiring screws; designing parts with clear orientations for easy assembly.

**How DFMA affects Product Mix:**

*   **Efficiency:** Products designed with DFMA are easier to manufacture and assemble, which lowers production costs. This allows companies to offer a wider range of product variations within their mix without incurring prohibitive costs.
*   **Speed to Market:** Streamlined manufacturing and assembly processes can accelerate the introduction of new products or variations into the market, enabling quicker updates to the product mix.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** By reducing manufacturing and assembly costs, DFMA makes it more feasible to offer a deeper product mix with more options.
*   **Quality:** DFMA often leads to simpler designs with fewer assembly errors, contributing to higher product quality across the mix.

**Example:**

A company designing a new modular smartphone. By applying DFA principles, they can design components that snap together easily, reducing assembly time and labor costs. This allows them to offer various configurations (different cameras, storage options, battery sizes) as part of their product mix without significantly increasing assembly complexity for each variation.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:**

*   **CO3: Introduce the concept of Design for Manufacturing and Assembly in product design (Knowledge Level: K2)**
    *   This section explains how DFMA principles enable the efficient and cost-effective creation and management of product variations within a company's product mix.

### 7. Product Mix in Relation to Value Engineering (CO4)

Value engineering is a systematic approach to improving the "value" of goods or services by examining function. Value, in this context, is defined as the ratio of function to cost.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Value Engineering (VE):** A systematic method to improve the "value" of goods or services by examining function. Value = Function / Cost. VE aims to increase value by improving function, reducing cost, or both.
    *   *Example:* Redesigning a product component to use a cheaper but equally effective material, or redesigning it to perform an additional function without increasing cost.

**How Value Engineering affects Product Mix:**

*   **Cost Reduction:** VE can identify opportunities to reduce the cost of existing products or product variations. This can make previously unprofitable product items viable, potentially expanding the product mix or allowing for more competitive pricing.
*   **Function Improvement:** VE can also lead to improvements in product functionality. These enhanced versions can be introduced as new product items or extensions within existing product lines, enriching the product mix.
*   **Product Portfolio Optimization:** By systematically evaluating the value of each product within the mix, companies can make informed decisions about which products to retain, enhance, or discontinue, thereby optimizing the overall product mix.
*   **Customer Satisfaction:** By focusing on both function and cost, VE aims to deliver better value to the customer, which can influence the perception and success of products within the mix.

**Example:**

A furniture company might use Value Engineering on a chair. They might discover that a certain support bracket can be made from a single, bent piece of metal instead of two welded pieces, reducing manufacturing cost and assembly time (improving Value = Function/Cost). This cost saving might allow them to introduce a new, slightly modified version of the chair into their product mix at a more attractive price point.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:**

*   **CO4: Use value engineering in the development of product (Knowledge Level: K2)**
    *   This section shows how value engineering can be applied to analyze and improve products within the mix, impacting their cost-effectiveness and functionality, and thus influencing strategic decisions about the product mix itself.

### 8. Product Mix in Relation to Ergonomics and Rapid Prototyping (CO5)

Ergonomics focuses on user comfort and efficiency, while rapid prototyping allows for quick validation of design concepts, both of which play a role in shaping the product mix.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Ergonomics:** The study of people's efficiency in their working or living environments. In product design, it focuses on designing products that are safe, comfortable, and easy to use.
    *   *Example:* Designing a computer mouse to fit comfortably in the hand; designing a tool with a non-slip grip and intuitive controls.
*   **Rapid Prototyping (RP):** A group of techniques used to quickly fabricate a model, a scale model, or a component of an actual part using three-dimensional computer aided design (CAD) data.
    *   *Example:* 3D printing a prototype of a new product enclosure or a new tool handle.

**How Ergonomics and Rapid Prototyping affect Product Mix:**

*   **Ergonomics:**
    *   **User-Centricity:** Incorporating ergonomic principles ensures that products are user-friendly and meet diverse user needs. This can lead to the development of multiple product variations tailored to different user groups or specific tasks, thus increasing the depth of the product mix.
    *   **Competitive Advantage:** Products with superior ergonomics can stand out in a crowded market and command premium pricing, influencing which products are prioritized in the mix.
*   **Rapid Prototyping:**
    *   **Concept Validation:** RP allows designers to quickly create and test physical prototypes of product concepts. This speeds up the decision-making process regarding which product variations are worth developing further and including in the product mix.
    *   **Iterative Improvement:** RP facilitates iterative design, allowing for rapid adjustments based on user feedback or performance testing. This is crucial for refining product variations and ensuring they meet ergonomic requirements and market expectations, thereby enhancing the quality of the product mix.
    *   **Reduced Development Time and Cost:** By enabling early identification of design flaws and facilitating quick modifications, RP reduces the overall time and cost of developing new products and variations, making it easier to manage a dynamic product mix.

**Example:**

A company designing a new power tool. They might use rapid prototyping to create several different ergonomic handle designs. Users can then test these prototypes, providing feedback on comfort and control. Based on this feedback, the most ergonomic and functional handle design can be selected and incorporated into the final product, potentially leading to a product line with variations offering different handle grips for different user preferences.

**Learning Outcome Alignment:**

*   **CO5: Incorporate ergonomics and rapid prototyping in product development (Knowledge Level: K2)**
    *   This section highlights how ergonomic considerations can lead to variations within the product mix and how rapid prototyping supports the efficient development and validation of these variations.

### 9. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Define "Product Line Extension" and provide an example.

**Answer 1:** Product Line Extension is the strategy of adding new varieties to an existing product line. This can involve introducing new features, flavors, colors, sizes, or improved versions of existing products.
*   *Example:* McDonald's introducing different types of McFlurry flavors (e.g., Oreo, Strawberry) to its existing ice cream product line.

**Question 2:** A company sells laptops, smartphones, and smartwatches. What is its product mix "width"?

**Answer 2:** The company's product mix "width" is 3, as it offers three distinct product lines: laptops, smartphones, and smartwatches.

**Question 3:** Explain how the "Maturity" stage of the Product Life Cycle might influence a company's product mix strategy.

**Answer 3:** During the Maturity stage, sales growth slows, and competition intensifies. Companies typically focus on differentiating their products and maintaining market share. This might lead to:
*   **Product line extensions:** Introducing minor variations or improvements to existing products to appeal to different market segments or fend off competitors.
*   **Product rationalization:** Eliminating less successful or low-profit product items within a line to focus resources on the best performers.
*   **Price adjustments:** To remain competitive, companies might adjust pricing for products in the maturity phase.

**Question 4:** How can Design for Manufacturing and Assembly (DFMA) help a company manage its product mix more effectively?

**Answer 4:** DFMA principles make products easier and cheaper to manufacture and assemble. This efficiency allows companies to:
*   **Offer more variations:** Lower production costs per unit enable companies to introduce a wider range of product items or options within their product lines.
*   **Reduce time-to-market for new variations:** Streamlined processes shorten the development and launch cycle for new products or updates.
*   **Maintain profitability:** Efficient production ensures that even with a diverse product mix, the overall cost structure remains manageable.

**Question 5:** A company is looking to improve the value of its product. What does "Value Engineering" suggest, and how can it impact their product mix?

**Answer 5:** Value Engineering suggests improving the ratio of Function to Cost. This can be achieved by:
*   **Improving Function:** Enhancing the performance or adding features.
*   **Reducing Cost:** Using cheaper materials, simplifying design, or streamlining processes without compromising essential functions.

Impact on Product Mix:
*   **Cost Reduction:** May make previously unprofitable product items viable, potentially expanding the mix.
*   **Function Improvement:** Can lead to new or improved product items being introduced into the mix.
*   **Portfolio Optimization:** Helps in deciding which products to invest in, modify, or discontinue, leading to a more efficient and valuable product mix.

### 10. Important Points to Remember

*   **Product mix** is the totality of a company's offerings.
*   Understanding the dimensions of **width, length, depth, and consistency** is crucial for strategic product management.
*   The **Product Life Cycle (PLC)** dictates how products are managed within the mix.
*   **Conceptual design** sets the stage for the breadth of the product mix, while **robust design** ensures reliability across variations.
*   **DFMA** enables cost-effective and efficient production, supporting a more extensive product mix.
*   **Value Engineering** focuses on maximizing **Function/Cost**, influencing product viability and optimization within the mix.
*   **Ergonomics** drives user-centric variations, and **Rapid Prototyping** accelerates the development and validation of these variations, shaping a user-friendly product mix.
