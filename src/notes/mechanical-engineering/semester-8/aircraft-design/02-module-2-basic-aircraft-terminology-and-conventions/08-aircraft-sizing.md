### 1. Introduction to Aircraft Sizing

Aircraft sizing is the process of determining the key physical and performance parameters of an aircraft based on its mission requirements. It's one of the first and most crucial steps in the conceptual design phase. The goal is to establish a baseline for the aircraft's size, weight, and performance capabilities that will satisfy the intended mission.

**Key Concept:** Aircraft sizing is an iterative process. Initial estimations are made, and then refined as more detailed analyses are performed.

**Reference:**
*   **Raymer, Chapter 4: Aircraft Sizing and Performance Estimation:** This chapter provides a thorough introduction to the principles of aircraft sizing, emphasizing the importance of mission requirements and the iterative nature of the process.

### 2. Key Parameters in Aircraft Sizing

Several interconnected parameters are considered during aircraft sizing. These can be broadly categorized as:

#### 2.1. Mission Requirements

These define *what* the aircraft needs to do. They are the primary drivers for sizing.

*   **Payload:** The amount of cargo, passengers, or weapons the aircraft must carry.
*   **Range:** The distance the aircraft needs to fly without refueling.
*   **Speed:** The required cruise speed, dash speed, or takeoff/landing speeds.
*   **Altitude:** The operating altitudes for cruise, loiter, or other mission phases.
*   **Endurance:** The duration the aircraft needs to stay airborne (e.g., for patrol or surveillance).
*   **Maneuverability:** Requirements for turning radius, climb rate, etc., especially for combat aircraft.
*   **Takeoff and Landing Performance:** Constraints on runway length, takeoff/landing speeds, and climb gradients.

**Example:** A commercial airliner's mission requirements would include passenger capacity, range between major cities, cruise speed, and comfort standards. A fighter jet's requirements would focus on payload (weapons), speed, maneuverability, and combat radius.

#### 2.2. Aircraft Performance Parameters

These are the characteristics that describe *how well* the aircraft performs its mission.

*   **Weight:**
    *   **Gross Weight (W):** The total weight of the aircraft at takeoff, including structure, engines, fuel, payload, and crew.
    *   **Empty Weight (W_e):** The weight of the aircraft without fuel, payload, or crew.
    *   **Fuel Weight (W_f):** The weight of the fuel carried.
    *   **Payload Weight (W_p):** The weight of the payload carried.
