### 1. Introduction to Vehicle Performance

Vehicle performance encompasses various aspects, including acceleration, top speed, braking, and handling. This topic focuses on **maximum speed**, which is the highest speed a vehicle can attain under ideal conditions.

**Key Concept:** Maximum speed is a critical performance metric that reflects the vehicle's ability to overcome resistive forces at high velocities.

---

### 2. Forces Resisting Vehicle Motion

To achieve maximum speed, a vehicle's propulsion system must overcome the sum of all forces that oppose its motion. These forces generally increase with speed.

*   **Aerodynamic Drag ($F_D$):** This is the most significant resistive force at high speeds. It is caused by the friction of air molecules as the vehicle moves through the air.
    *   **Formula:** $F_D = \frac{1}{2} \rho C_D A v^2$
        *   $\rho$: Air density (kg/m³)
        *   $C_D$: Drag coefficient (dimensionless, depends on vehicle shape)
        *   $A$: Frontal area of the vehicle (m²)
        *   $v$: Vehicle velocity (m/s)
    *   **Key Point:** Aerodynamic drag increases with the square of the velocity, making it a dominant factor at high speeds.
    *   **Example (Husain, 2010):** Streamlined vehicle shapes are designed to minimize $C_D$ and therefore aerodynamic drag. An SUV typically has a higher $C_D$ than a sports car.

*   **Rolling Resistance ($F_R$):** This force arises from the deformation of tires and the road surface as the wheels roll.
    *   **Formula:** $F_R = C_{rr} N$
        *   $C_{rr}$: Coefficient of rolling resistance (dimensionless, depends on tire type, inflation pressure, road surface)
        *   $N$: Normal force on the wheels (equal to vehicle weight for level ground)
    *   **Key Point:** Rolling resistance is relatively constant with speed, but can increase slightly at higher speeds due to tire deformation.
    *   **Example (Ehsani et al., 2018):** Properly inflated tires and lower rolling resistance tire compounds can reduce this force.

*   **Gradient Resistance ($F_G$):** This force opposes motion when the vehicle is moving uphill.
    *   **Formula:** $F_G = mg \sin(\theta)$
        *   $m$: Vehicle mass (kg)
        *   $g$: Acceleration due to gravity (9.81 m/s²)
        *   $\theta$: Gradient angle (degrees)
    *   **Key Point:** This force is zero on level ground. For small angles, $\sin(\theta) \approx \theta$ (in radians), so $F_G \approx mg \frac{\text{gradient percentage}}{100}$.

*   **Mechanical Losses (Internal Friction):** Friction within the powertrain components (motor, gearbox, bearings, etc.) also contributes to resistive forces. These losses are typically expressed as a percentage of the power delivered to the wheels.

---

### 3. Propulsion System Power

The maximum speed is achieved when the total power delivered by the propulsion system to the wheels is just enough to overcome the total resistive forces at that speed.

*   **Total Resistive Force ($F_{total}$):** $F_{total} = F_D + F_R + F_G$ (for a level road, $F_G=0$)
*   **Propulsive Force ($F_P$):** The force generated by the wheels to move the vehicle forward.
*   **Maximum Speed Condition:** $F_P = F_{total}$
*   **Power Required at the Wheels ($P_{wheels}$):** $P_{wheels} = F_P \times v = F_{total} \times v$
    *   Substituting the forces: $P_{wheels} = (F_D + F_R + F_G) \times v$
    *   $P_{wheels} = (\frac{1}{2} \rho C_D A v^2 + C_{rr} N + mg \sin(\theta)) \times v$
    *   **Key Point:** The power required to overcome aerodynamic drag increases with the cube of the velocity ($v^3$), while rolling resistance requires power proportional to velocity ($v^1$). This highlights why drag is dominant at high speeds.

---

### 4. Motor Power and Torque Characteristics

The **maximum speed** of a vehicle is fundamentally limited by the **maximum power** that its propulsion system can deliver to the wheels.

*   **Electric Motors:**
    *   **Key Feature:** Electric motors, particularly AC induction motors and permanent magnet synchronous motors (PMSM), can often deliver nearly constant torque over a wide speed range (until their base speed). Beyond their base speed, torque typically decreases linearly with increasing speed, while power remains relatively constant until the motor's maximum speed is reached.
    *   **Impact on Maximum Speed:** The maximum power output of the electric motor, after accounting for drivetrain efficiencies, is the primary determinant of the vehicle's maximum speed.
    *   **Example (Larminie & Lowry, 2012):** If an electric motor has a rated maximum power of 100 kW and the drivetrain efficiency is 90%, the power available at the wheels is 90 kW. This power capability, when matched against the resistive forces at various speeds, dictates the maximum achievable speed.

*   **Internal Combustion Engines (ICE):**
    *   **Key Feature:** ICEs have a specific engine speed (RPM) at which they produce their maximum power. Torque and power curves are generally more complex and variable.
    *   **Impact on Maximum Speed:** The maximum speed of an ICE vehicle is determined by the engine's peak power output and the gearing. The engine needs to be operating at or near its peak power RPM to achieve maximum road speed.

---

### 5. Role of Gearing and Transmission

The gearbox and final drive ratio are crucial for translating the motor's output (speed and torque) to the wheels and influencing the maximum speed.

*   **Gear Ratio ($G$):** The overall gear ratio (including transmission and final drive) determines the relationship between the motor's rotational speed and the wheels' rotational speed.
    *   $v = \omega_{wheels} \times R_{wheel}$
    *   $\omega_{wheels} = \omega_{motor} / G$
    *   Therefore, $v = (\omega_{motor} / G) \times R_{wheel}$
*   **Torque Multiplication:** Lower gears provide higher torque multiplication for acceleration, while higher gears allow for higher vehicle speeds at a given motor RPM.
*   **Maximum Speed Gear:** For maximum speed, the transmission is typically in its highest gear (lowest numerical ratio). At maximum speed, the motor is usually operating at or near its maximum rated RPM, and its power output is the limiting factor.
*   **Example (Ehsani et al., 2018):**
    *   Consider a vehicle with a motor capable of 10,000 RPM and a maximum power of 100 kW. Let the wheel radius be 0.3 meters.
    *   If the highest gear ratio is 5:1 and the final drive ratio is 3:1, the overall ratio ($G$) is 15:1.
    *   When the motor reaches its maximum speed (10,000 RPM), the wheels rotate at $10,000 \text{ RPM} / 15 = 666.67 \text{ RPM}$.
    *   The corresponding vehicle speed is $666.67 \text{ RPM} \times (2\pi \text{ rad/rev}) \times 0.3 \text{ m/rev} \times (1 \text{ min} / 60 \text{ sec}) \approx 20.9 \text{ m/s} \approx 75 \text{ km/h}$.
    *   **Important:** This is a simplified example. The actual maximum speed is determined when the motor's power is fully utilized to overcome the resistive forces at that velocity.

*   **Single-Speed Transmissions in EVs:** Many EVs use a single-speed transmission. In such cases, the motor's design (its torque-speed characteristics) and its maximum operating RPM are the direct determinants of maximum speed. The motor's power curve must be such that it can achieve high speeds while still providing adequate torque at lower speeds.
    *   **Challenge:** Designing a single-speed transmission that offers good acceleration and high top speed requires careful motor selection and optimization of its operating characteristics.

---

### 6. Maximum Speed Calculation - The Balance Point

Maximum speed is reached when the power output of the propulsion system at the wheels precisely equals the power required to overcome all resistive forces at that speed.

*   **Power Available ($P_{available}$):** $P_{available} = P_{motor} \times \eta_{drivetrain}$
    *   $\eta_{drivetrain}$: Overall efficiency of the transmission, differential, and axles.
*   **Power Required ($P_{required}$):** $P_{required} = F_{total} \times v = (\frac{1}{2} \rho C_D A v^2 + C_{rr} N + mg \sin(\theta)) \times v$
*   **Maximum Speed ($v_{max}$):** $P_{available} = P_{required}$
    *   This leads to an equation that needs to be solved for $v$:
        $P_{available} = (\frac{1}{2} \rho C_D A v_{max}^2 + C_{rr} N + mg \sin(\theta)) \times v_{max}$

**Illustrative Example:**

Let's assume for a simplified EV:
*   Maximum power at wheels ($P_{available}$) = 150 kW = 150,000 W
*   Air density ($\rho$) = 1.225 kg/m³
*   Drag coefficient ($C_D$) = 0.3
*   Frontal area ($A$) = 2.2 m²
*   Coefficient of rolling resistance ($C_{rr}$) = 0.012
*   Vehicle mass ($m$) = 1800 kg
*   $g$ = 9.81 m/s²
*   Level road ($\theta=0$, $F_G=0$)
*   At the estimated maximum speed, the normal force $N$ on the driven wheels is, say, 900 kg * 9.81 m/s² = 8829 N.

We need to find $v_{max}$ such that:
$150,000 = (\frac{1}{2} \times 1.225 \times 0.3 \times 2.2 \times v_{max}^2 + 0.012 \times 8829) \times v_{max}$
$150,000 = (0.40425 v_{max}^2 + 105.948) \times v_{max}$
$150,000 = 0.40425 v_{max}^3 + 105.948 v_{max}$

This is a cubic equation. Solving it (numerically or graphically) would give the maximum speed. Let's test a few values:

*   If $v_{max} = 50 \text{ m/s}$ (180 km/h):
    $P_{required} = (0.40425 \times 50^2 + 105.948) \times 50 = (0.40425 \times 2500 + 105.948) \times 50 = (1010.625 + 105.948) \times 50 = 1116.573 \times 50 = 55,828.65 \text{ W}$ (Much lower than 150 kW)

*   If $v_{max} = 70 \text{ m/s}$ (252 km/h):
    $P_{required} = (0.40425 \times 70^2 + 105.948) \times 70 = (0.40425 \times 4900 + 105.948) \times 70 = (1980.825 + 105.948) \times 70 = 2086.773 \times 70 = 146,074.11 \text{ W}$ (Close to 150 kW)

*   If $v_{max} = 71 \text{ m/s}$ (255.6 km/h):
    $P_{required} = (0.40425 \times 71^2 + 105.948) \times 71 = (0.40425 \times 5041 + 105.948) \times 71 = (2037.745 + 105.948) \times 71 = 2143.693 \times 71 = 152,202.2 \text{ W}$ (Slightly over 150 kW)

So, the maximum speed is around 70.5 m/s, which is approximately 254 km/h.

### 7. Impact of Auxiliary Loads

Auxiliary loads, such as air conditioning, heating, lights, and infotainment systems, consume power from the battery. While their impact on maximum speed is generally less significant than aerodynamic drag or propulsion system limitations, they can reduce the power available for propulsion.

*   **Power Consumption:** Auxiliary loads can consume anywhere from a few hundred watts to several kilowatts.
*   **Effect on Maximum Speed:** If a significant amount of power is diverted to auxiliary systems, the power available at the wheels ($P_{available}$) will be lower, resulting in a slightly reduced maximum speed.
*   **Example (Denton, 2020):** Running the climate control system at full power in an EV can reduce the available range and, theoretically, the maximum speed if the propulsion system is operating at its absolute limit.

### 8. Key Differences: EVs vs. ICE Vehicles for Maximum Speed

| Feature             | Electric Vehicles (EVs)                                                                | Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) Vehicles                                          |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Power Source**    | Electric Motor(s)                                                                      | Internal Combustion Engine                                                         |
| **Power Delivery**  | High torque from 0 RPM, constant power band often extends to motor's max RPM.           | Torque and power vary significantly with RPM; peak power at a specific RPM.        |
| **Transmission**    | Often single-speed; some use multi-speed transmissions.                                | Multi-speed transmissions (manual, automatic, CVT) are standard.                   |
| **Max Speed Driver**| Maximum motor power and its RPM limit, balanced against resistive forces.             | Engine's peak power output and the gearing to reach that peak power RPM at high speed. |
| **Noise/Vibration** | Generally quieter and smoother, less mechanical noise at high speeds.                 | Engine noise and vibration become more pronounced at high speeds.                  |
| **Aerodynamics**    | Design often optimized for aero efficiency to maximize range and reduce drag.          | Aerodynamic design is important, but often balanced with styling and packaging.    |

---

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   **Maximum speed is a balance between available propulsion power and resistive forces.**
*   **Aerodynamic drag ($F_D$) is the dominant resistive force at high speeds and increases with the square of velocity.**
*   **Rolling resistance ($F_R$) is less dependent on speed but significant at all speeds.**
*   **Electric motors can deliver high torque and often have a wide power band, facilitating high speeds.**
*   **The maximum power output of the electric motor, considering drivetrain efficiency, is the primary limiter of an EV's maximum speed.**
*   **Gearing (transmission and final drive) is crucial for matching the motor's speed-torque characteristics to the wheels for optimal performance across different speed regimes, including maximum speed.**
*   **Single-speed transmissions in EVs require careful motor selection to achieve both good acceleration and acceptable top speed.**
*   **Auxiliary loads reduce the power available for propulsion and can slightly impact maximum speed.**

### 10. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1 (K2):**
Which of the following forces is the most significant contributor to resistance at high vehicle speeds?
a) Rolling Resistance
b) Gradient Resistance
c) Aerodynamic Drag
d) Mechanical Friction in bearings

**Answer:** c) Aerodynamic Drag

**Question 2 (K2):**
Electric motors in EVs typically provide:
a) High torque only at high RPMs.
b) Constant torque across all RPMs.
c) High torque from low RPMs, often with a constant power region.
d) Zero torque at 0 RPM.

**Answer:** c) High torque from low RPMs, often with a constant power region.

**Question 3 (K3):**
A vehicle has a maximum power output at the wheels of 120 kW. At a speed of 60 m/s (216 km/h), the total resistive forces acting on the vehicle are equivalent to a force of 2000 N. What is the power required to maintain this speed? If the vehicle's maximum power is 120 kW, can it sustain 60 m/s?

**Calculation:**
Power Required ($P_{required}$) = Force $\times$ Velocity
$P_{required}$ = 2000 N $\times$ 60 m/s = 120,000 W = 120 kW

**Answer:** The power required to maintain 60 m/s is 120 kW. Since the vehicle's maximum power output at the wheels is 120 kW, it can *just* sustain this speed if operating at its peak power. However, to exceed it, it would need more power.

**Question 4 (K2/K3):**
Describe how the gearing in an electric vehicle affects its maximum speed. Consider both single-speed and multi-speed transmissions.

**Answer:**
*   **General Principle:** Gearing (transmission and final drive) determines the relationship between the motor's rotational speed and the wheels' rotational speed. A higher gear ratio (numerically larger) multiplies torque but reduces speed. A lower gear ratio (numerically smaller) results in less torque multiplication but allows for higher wheel speeds at a given motor RPM.
*   **Single-Speed Transmission:** In EVs with single-speed transmissions, the motor's inherent torque-speed characteristics and its maximum operating RPM are the direct determinants of maximum speed. The motor must be designed to operate efficiently at the high speeds required for top speed, while still providing adequate torque for acceleration. The gear ratio selected for the single-speed unit is a compromise between these requirements.
*   **Multi-Speed Transmission:** For maximum speed, the vehicle will be in its highest gear (lowest numerical ratio). This allows the motor to reach its maximum RPM and thus its maximum power output at the highest possible vehicle speed. The engine/motor can then operate closer to its peak power band, overcoming the rapidly increasing aerodynamic drag.

**Question 5 (Conceptual):**
Why is aerodynamic drag a more significant factor for maximum speed than rolling resistance?

**Answer:**
Aerodynamic drag is proportional to the square of the velocity ($v^2$), meaning it increases rapidly as speed increases. Rolling resistance is relatively constant with speed (or increases much more slowly). Therefore, at high speeds, the power required to overcome aerodynamic drag ($F_D \times v$, which is proportional to $v^3$) becomes much larger than the power required to overcome rolling resistance ($F_R \times v$, which is proportional to $v$). This means that as a vehicle's speed increases, aerodynamic drag requires a disproportionately larger amount of power to overcome, ultimately limiting the maximum achievable speed.
