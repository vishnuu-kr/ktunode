# Hybrid and Electric Vehicles: Module 2: Energy Storage – Battery Basics

## Topic: AIS 156 (2020) and AIS 038 Rev (2020)

This module delves into the critical aspects of energy storage in Hybrid and Electric Vehicles (HEVs), with a specific focus on the Indian standards **AIS 156 (2020)** and **AIS 038 Rev (2020)**. These standards are crucial for ensuring the safety, performance, and reliability of battery systems in electric vehicles sold in India.

---

### 1. Introduction to Automotive Industry Standards (AIS)

*   **What are AIS?**
    *   Automotive Industry Standards (AIS) are a set of standards developed by the Automotive Research Association of India (ARAI) in consultation with the government and industry stakeholders.
    *   They are primarily concerned with safety, emissions, and performance requirements for automotive vehicles and their components in India.
    *   Compliance with AIS is often mandated for vehicles to be approved for sale and use in India.

*   **Importance in HEVs/EVs:**
    *   The unique nature of high-voltage battery systems in HEVs and EVs necessitates specific safety and performance standards.
    *   AIS 156 and AIS 038 provide a framework for manufacturers to ensure their battery packs and related systems meet stringent safety requirements, protecting both occupants and the public.

---

### 2. AIS 156 (2020): Safety Requirements for Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs)

**Key Focus:** Primarily addresses the safety of the **battery pack and the electrical system** of Battery Electric Vehicles. It aims to prevent hazards arising from the high-voltage battery system.

*   **Scope:** Covers various aspects of battery pack safety, including:
    *   **Thermal Runaway Prevention:** Measures to prevent and mitigate the effects of thermal runaway within the battery pack.
    *   **Electrical Safety:** Protection against electric shock, short circuits, and overcharging.
    *   **Mechanical Integrity:** Ensuring the battery pack can withstand mechanical stresses and impacts.
    *   **Fire Safety:** Requirements related to the prevention and containment of fires originating from the battery.
    *   **Ingress Protection (IP) Ratings:** Specifying the level of protection against dust and water ingress for the battery pack and associated components.

*   **Key Requirements and Concepts:**
    *   **Battery Management System (BMS) Requirements:**
        *   **Monitoring:** Continuous monitoring of individual cell voltage, temperature, and current.
        *   **Balancing:** Ensuring uniform charging and discharging of cells to maximize pack lifespan and performance.
        *   **Protection:** Implementing safeguards against over-voltage, under-voltage, over-temperature, under-temperature, and over-current.
        *   **Fault Detection and Reporting:** Ability to detect and report system faults to the vehicle's main control unit.
        *   *(Referenced concept from textbooks like Ehsani et al. regarding BMS functionality)*
    *   **Thermal Management:**
        *   Strategies to maintain battery cells within their optimal operating temperature range (e.g., active cooling, passive cooling).
        *   Requirements for preventing excessive heat build-up during charging and discharging.
        *   *(Connects to Husain's discussions on battery thermal characteristics)*
    *   **Mechanical Strength and Durability:**
        *   Tests to ensure the battery enclosure can withstand vibrations, shocks, and impacts.
        *   Protection against physical damage that could lead to short circuits or leakage.
        *   *(Relates to the physical construction and protection of battery packs as discussed in Larminie & Lowry)*
    *   **Electrical Insulation:**
        *   Requirements for high-voltage insulation to prevent electrical leakage to the vehicle chassis or occupants.
        *   Dielectric strength tests for insulation materials.
    *   **Fire Containment:**
        *   Requirements for the battery enclosure to contain a fire for a specified duration, allowing occupants to evacuate safely.
        *   Materials used in the battery pack should exhibit certain fire-retardant properties.
    *   **Charging Safety:**
        *   Requirements for the charging interface and process to prevent hazards during charging.
        *   Interlocks to prevent charging under unsafe conditions.

*   **Example:** A BEV's battery pack enclosure must be designed to prevent the ingress of water droplets up to a certain size (e.g., IP67 rating), ensuring safety in rainy conditions. It also needs to withstand a simulated crash impact without compromising the containment of the battery cells.

### 3. AIS 038 Rev (2020): Safety Requirements for Electric Power Train of Electric Vehicles

**Key Focus:** Extends the safety considerations to the **entire electric powertrain**, including the battery pack, motor, power electronics (inverter, converter), and charging system. It addresses the safety of the high-voltage system as a whole.

*   **Scope:** While overlapping with AIS 156, AIS 038 Rev (2020) takes a broader view by incorporating:
    *   **High-Voltage System Integration:** Safety aspects related to the interconnection of the battery pack with other powertrain components.
    *   **Protection Against Electric Shock:** Comprehensive measures to protect against direct and indirect contact with live parts.
    *   **Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC):** Ensuring the electric powertrain does not interfere with other vehicle systems and is not susceptible to external electromagnetic interference.
    *   **Charging Infrastructure Interface:** Safety requirements for the connection between the vehicle and the charging station.

*   **Key Requirements and Concepts:**
    *   **System Isolation:**
        *   Ensuring adequate isolation between the high-voltage system and the vehicle chassis.
        *   Requirements for isolation monitoring systems to detect insulation failures.
    *   **Protection of Live Parts:**
        *   Physical barriers and enclosures to prevent accidental contact with high-voltage components.
        *   Service disconnects and safety interlocks to safely de-energize the high-voltage system during maintenance or in case of a crash.
        *   *(This aligns with safety practices discussed in Ehsani et al. regarding vehicle electrical architecture)*
    *   **Crash Safety Integration:**
        *   Requirements for the automatic disconnection of the high-voltage system in the event of a severe impact.
        *   Safe positioning of high-voltage components to minimize damage during a crash.
    *   **Charging Safety:**
        *   Compliance with international charging standards (e.g., IEC, ISO) for vehicle-to-grid communication and safety.
        *   Detection of charging cable faults and ensuring safe disconnection.
        *   *(Connects to Larminie & Lowry's overview of charging mechanisms and safety)*
    *   **EMC Requirements:**
        *   Tests to ensure the powertrain components do not emit excessive electromagnetic radiation that could interfere with other vehicle systems (e.g., airbags, communication systems).
        *   Ensuring the powertrain is resilient to external electromagnetic fields.

*   **Example:** AIS 038 Rev (2020) would require a BEV to have a mechanism that automatically disconnects the battery from the rest of the powertrain within milliseconds of detecting a significant collision. It also mandates that the insulation resistance between the high-voltage battery and the vehicle chassis must be above a certain threshold, even under normal operating conditions.

### 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Mandatory Compliance:** AIS 156 and AIS 038 Rev (2020) are not optional guidelines; they are regulatory standards for vehicles sold in India.
*   **Focus on Safety:** The primary objective of these standards is to enhance the safety of HEVs and EVs, particularly concerning their high-voltage battery systems.
*   **Interconnectedness:** AIS 038 Rev (2020) builds upon and expands the safety focus of AIS 156 by addressing the entire electric powertrain.
*   **Dynamic Nature:** Standards are often revised to keep pace with technological advancements and emerging safety concerns. Manufacturers must stay updated with the latest versions.
*   **Global Influence:** While specific to India, these standards often draw from and align with international safety standards (e.g., ISO, UN ECE regulations), reflecting a global commitment to EV safety.

### 6. Practice Questions & Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary objective of AIS 156 (2020)?
**Answer:** The primary objective of AIS 156 (2020) is to define safety requirements specifically for the battery pack and electrical system of Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) to prevent hazards.

**Question 2:** How does AIS 038 Rev (2020) differ from AIS 156 (2020)?
**Answer:** AIS 038 Rev (2020) has a broader scope, addressing the safety requirements for the entire electric powertrain of electric vehicles, including the battery, motor, power electronics, and charging system, whereas AIS 156 focuses more narrowly on the battery pack and its immediate electrical system.

**Question 3:** List three key safety aspects covered by AIS 156 (2020) for a battery pack.
**Answer:** Three key safety aspects covered are:
1.  Thermal runaway prevention/mitigation.
2.  Electrical safety (protection against shock, short circuits).
3.  Mechanical integrity and durability.
4.  Fire safety and containment.
5.  Ingress protection (dust and water resistance).
(Any three from the list are acceptable)

**Question 4:** Why are safety standards like AIS 156 and AIS 038 Rev (2020) crucial for the adoption of Electric Vehicles?
**Answer:** These standards are crucial because they build consumer trust and confidence in the safety of EVs, mitigating potential risks associated with high-voltage battery systems. This increased safety assurance is vital for widespread adoption.

**Question 5:** Which textbook would be most relevant for understanding the functional requirements of a Battery Management System (BMS) that are then mandated by AIS 156?
**Answer:** Textbooks like "Modern Electric, Hybrid Electric and Fuel Cell Vehicles: Fundamentals, Theory and Design" by Ehsani et al. or "Electric and Hybrid Vehicles: Design Fundamentals" by Husain would be highly relevant as they detail the core functions and importance of BMS.
