# World Class Manufacturing: Module 2 - Planning & HRD Practices in WCM: Strategy Procedure

## Introduction to Strategy Procedure in WCM

This module delves into the crucial aspect of **Strategy Procedure** within the framework of World Class Manufacturing (WCM). It highlights how a well-defined strategy is fundamental to achieving and sustaining manufacturing excellence. This section will explore the strategic imperatives that drive WCM and the systematic approach required to translate vision into actionable plans.

### 1. The Essence of Strategy in WCM

World Class Manufacturing (WCM) is not merely about adopting individual tools or techniques; it's about a holistic, integrated approach to achieving superior performance. Strategy is the guiding force that aligns all activities towards this goal.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Strategy:** A comprehensive plan designed to achieve long-term or overall aims and objectives. In WCM, it's about defining how a manufacturing organization will compete and win in the marketplace.
*   **Competitive Advantage:** What makes a company's products or services superior to its competitors. WCM aims to build and sustain competitive advantage through operational excellence.
*   **Vision:** The long-term aspirational goal of the organization.
*   **Mission:** The purpose of the organization and its overarching goals.

**Schonberger (2013) emphasizes simplicity in WCM.** This means the strategy should be clear, concise, and easily understood by everyone in the organization. A complex or convoluted strategy will hinder its implementation and effectiveness.

**Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018) highlight the importance of aligning manufacturing strategy with business strategy.** This ensures that the manufacturing function directly contributes to the overall success of the company.

### 2. Components of a WCM Strategy Procedure

A robust WCM strategy procedure involves several interconnected elements that need to be carefully considered and implemented.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Strategic Analysis:** Understanding the current state of the organization, its strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT analysis), and the competitive landscape.
*   **Goal Setting:** Defining specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) objectives that align with the WCM vision.
*   **Strategy Formulation:** Developing the broad approaches and plans to achieve the set goals. This includes identifying key areas of focus and prioritization.
*   **Strategy Implementation:** Putting the formulated strategy into action through various initiatives, resource allocation, and organizational changes.
*   **Strategy Evaluation & Control:** Monitoring the progress of the implemented strategy, measuring performance against objectives, and making necessary adjustments.

**Example:** A company might identify a weakness in its production lead time. The WCM strategy could involve implementing lean manufacturing principles (like just-in-time) and investing in new automation technology to reduce lead times and gain a competitive advantage.

**Jagdev, Brennan, & Browne (2013) discuss strategic decision-making in modern manufacturing.** Their work underscores the need for data-driven and analytical approaches to strategy formulation, considering factors like market demand, technological advancements, and supply chain dynamics.

### 3. Key Strategic Pillars of WCM

While specific strategies can vary, most WCM approaches are built upon a foundation of several core pillars.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Customer Focus:** Understanding and meeting customer needs and expectations is paramount. This involves delivering high-quality products, on-time delivery, and excellent service.
*   **Continuous Improvement (Kaizen):** A philosophy of ongoing, incremental improvements in all aspects of operations, involving everyone in the organization.
*   **Employee Involvement & Empowerment:** Recognizing that employees are the most valuable asset and empowering them to contribute to problem-solving and decision-making.
*   **Process Excellence:** Optimizing and standardizing processes to eliminate waste, reduce variation, and enhance efficiency.
*   **Technology Integration:** Leveraging appropriate technologies to enhance productivity, quality, and flexibility.
*   **Supplier Partnerships:** Building strong, collaborative relationships with suppliers to ensure a reliable and high-quality supply chain.

### 4. Developing a WCM Strategy Procedure: A Step-by-Step Approach

The development and implementation of a WCM strategy is a cyclical and iterative process.

**Steps:**

1.  **Define the Vision and Mission:** Clearly articulate what the organization aims to achieve in the long term and its fundamental purpose.
2.  **Conduct Strategic Assessment:**
    *   **Internal Analysis:** Assess current capabilities, resources, strengths, and weaknesses. (e.g., using SWOT).
    *   **External Analysis:** Analyze the market, competitors, technological trends, and customer needs. (e.g., PESTLE analysis - Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental).
3.  **Identify Strategic Gaps:** Compare the current state with the desired future state (vision) to identify areas for improvement.
4.  **Set Strategic Objectives:** Define specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) objectives that address the strategic gaps and align with the vision.
    *   *Example:* Reduce defect rate by 15% in the next 12 months.
    *   *Example:* Improve on-time delivery performance to 98% within six months.
5.  **Formulate Strategic Initiatives:** Develop specific plans and actions to achieve the strategic objectives. These initiatives should be aligned with WCM principles.
    *   *Examples:* Implement Six Sigma for quality improvement, adopt Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) for equipment reliability, implement Lean principles for waste reduction.
6.  **Develop Implementation Plan:** Outline the resources (human, financial, technological) required, timelines, responsibilities, and key performance indicators (KPIs) for each initiative.
7.  **Implement the Strategy:** Execute the plans, ensuring proper communication, training, and resource allocation.
8.  **Monitor, Evaluate, and Control:** Regularly track progress against KPIs, analyze performance data, identify deviations from the plan, and implement corrective actions. This feedback loop is crucial for continuous improvement.

**Important Point to Remember:** The strategy procedure is not a one-time event but a continuous process of planning, execution, and adaptation.

**Jagdev, Brennan, & Browne (2013)** emphasize the importance of a structured approach to strategic decision-making, which includes robust data collection and analysis at each stage of the procedure.

### 5. Tools and Methodologies Supporting WCM Strategy

Several tools and methodologies are integral to formulating and implementing a WCM strategy.

**Key Concepts & Tools:**

*   **SWOT Analysis:** Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. Used for internal and external assessment.
*   **Balanced Scorecard:** A strategic performance management tool that provides a comprehensive view of organizational performance beyond financial metrics. It typically includes four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Processes, and Learning & Growth.
*   **Benchmarking:** Comparing an organization's processes and performance metrics against best-in-class companies to identify areas for improvement.
*   **Lean Manufacturing Principles:** Focuses on eliminating waste (Muda), unevenness (Mura), and overburden (Muri). Key tools include Value Stream Mapping, 5S, Kanban, and Poka-Yoke.
*   **Six Sigma:** A data-driven methodology aimed at reducing process variation and defects. Key tools include DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control).
*   **Total Productive Maintenance (TPM):** A proactive approach to equipment maintenance that involves all employees in maximizing equipment effectiveness and minimizing breakdowns.
*   **Theory of Constraints (TOC):** A management paradigm that focuses on identifying and managing the most significant limiting factor (constraint) that affects a system's ability to achieve its goal.

**Example:** A company might use **benchmarking** to identify that its competitors have significantly shorter lead times. This insight would then feed into its **strategic assessment**, leading to the formulation of initiatives using **Lean Manufacturing principles** like Value Stream Mapping to identify and eliminate bottlenecks in its own processes.

**Sahay, Saxena, & Kumar (2018)** provide a comprehensive overview of various tools and techniques used in WCM, explaining their application in strategy implementation.

### 6. Aligning HRD Practices with WCM Strategy

Human Resource Development (HRD) plays a pivotal role in the success of a WCM strategy. Without a skilled, motivated, and engaged workforce, even the best-laid plans will falter.

**Key HRD Practices:**

*   **Skills Development & Training:** Providing employees with the necessary skills to operate new technologies, implement lean techniques, and contribute to continuous improvement.
*   **Performance Management:** Establishing clear performance expectations, providing regular feedback, and recognizing and rewarding contributions.
*   **Employee Engagement & Motivation:** Fostering a culture where employees feel valued, empowered, and motivated to contribute their best. This includes communication, involvement in decision-making, and a positive work environment.
*   **Organizational Culture:** Cultivating a culture that embraces continuous improvement, problem-solving, teamwork, and customer focus.
*   **Leadership Development:** Developing leaders who can champion WCM principles, motivate their teams, and drive change.

**CO3: To Identify the employee involvement in WCM (Knowledge Level: K1)**

**Schonberger (2013)** strongly advocates for the integration of people-focused practices within WCM. He argues that simplicity in strategy also extends to empowering employees to understand and execute their roles effectively.

**Example:** If a WCM strategy includes implementing Six Sigma, HRD practices would focus on training employees in Six Sigma methodologies, providing them with problem-solving tools, and creating a performance management system that rewards quality improvements.

### 8. Modern Trends & Terminologies in WCM Strategy

The field of manufacturing is constantly evolving, and WCM strategies must adapt to new trends.

**Key Modern Trends & Terminologies:**

*   **Industry 4.0/Smart Manufacturing:** The integration of digital technologies, automation, and data analytics into manufacturing processes.
*   **Internet of Things (IoT):** Connecting machines and sensors to collect real-time data for performance monitoring and predictive maintenance.
*   **Artificial Intelligence (AI) & Machine Learning (ML):** Used for predictive analytics, process optimization, and quality control.
*   **Digital Transformation:** The comprehensive integration of digital technology into all areas of a business, fundamentally changing how it operates and delivers value.
*   **Sustainability & Green Manufacturing:** Incorporating environmental considerations and resource efficiency into manufacturing strategies.
*   **Agile Manufacturing:** The ability to respond quickly and effectively to changes in market demand and customer requirements.
*   **Resilient Supply Chains:** Designing supply chains that can withstand disruptions and recover quickly.

**CO7: Identify various modern day trends and terminologies in WCM (Knowledge Level: K1)**

**Reference:** **Frazelle (2016)**, with his focus on Material Handling, may touch upon how modern logistics and automation technologies influence WCM strategy. **Jagdev, Brennan, & Browne (2013)** would likely discuss how strategic decision-making in modern manufacturing is influenced by these trends.

**Example:** A WCM strategy incorporating Industry 4.0 might involve using IoT sensors to monitor machine performance in real-time, applying AI for predictive maintenance to prevent breakdowns, and integrating this data into a digital twin of the factory for simulation and optimization.

## Practice Questions & Exercises

**1. Multiple Choice Questions:**

a) Which of the following is NOT a key pillar of WCM?
    i) Customer Focus
    ii) Employee Empowerment
    iii) Cost Leadership
    iv) Continuous Improvement

b) The process of comparing an organization's performance with best-in-class companies is known as:
    i) SWOT Analysis
    ii) Benchmarking
    iii) Kaizen
    iv) TPM

c) Which methodology focuses on reducing process variation and defects?
    i) Lean Manufacturing
    ii) Theory of Constraints
    iii) Six Sigma
    iv) 5S

**Answers:**
a) iii) Cost Leadership (While cost is important, it's not a primary pillar in the same way as the others. WCM aims for value, which can include lower costs, but not at the expense of quality or customer focus.)
b) ii) Benchmarking
c) iii) Six Sigma

**2. Short Answer Questions:**

a) Briefly explain the importance of aligning manufacturing strategy with business strategy in WCM. (CO2)
b) What is the role of employee involvement in a WCM strategy? (CO3)
c) List three modern trends impacting WCM strategy. (CO7)

**Answers:**

a) Aligning manufacturing strategy with business strategy ensures that the manufacturing function directly supports and contributes to the overall goals and competitive objectives of the company, such as market share growth, profitability, or customer satisfaction.

b) Employee involvement is crucial as empowered employees are closer to the processes and can identify and solve problems effectively. They are key drivers of continuous improvement, quality initiatives, and the successful implementation of WCM principles.

c) Three modern trends impacting WCM strategy are Industry 4.0/Smart Manufacturing, Artificial Intelligence (AI), and Sustainability/Green Manufacturing.

**3. Case Study Exercise:**

Imagine a small manufacturing company that produces electronic components. They are facing increasing competition and pressure to improve quality and reduce lead times.

a) Outline the initial steps this company should take to develop a WCM strategy procedure. (CO2)
b) Identify three specific WCM tools or methodologies that would be most beneficial for this company, and explain why. (CO4)
c) How can the company ensure employee involvement and buy-in for its WCM initiatives? (CO3)

**Answers (Guidance):**

a)
    *   **Vision & Mission:** Define what "world-class" means for their company (e.g., leading in quality, fastest delivery).
    *   **Strategic Assessment:** Conduct a SWOT analysis to understand their strengths (e.g., skilled workforce), weaknesses (e.g., outdated equipment), opportunities (e.g., growing market demand), and threats (e.g., new competitors). Analyze competitors.
    *   **Identify Gaps:** Determine the difference between their current state and their vision.

b)
    *   **Lean Manufacturing (Value Stream Mapping):** To identify and eliminate non-value-adding activities in their production process, thereby reducing lead times and waste.
    *   **5S Methodology:** To improve workplace organization, cleanliness, and visual management, leading to a safer and more efficient working environment.
    *   **Six Sigma (DMAIC):** To systematically identify the root causes of defects and implement solutions to improve product quality.

c)
    *   **Communication:** Clearly communicate the purpose and benefits of WCM to all employees.
    *   **Training:** Provide training on WCM principles and the tools being implemented.
    *   **Empowerment:** Create opportunities for employees to participate in problem-solving teams (e.g., Kaizen events), suggest improvements, and take ownership of their processes.
    *   **Recognition:** Recognize and reward employees for their contributions to WCM initiatives.

## Important Points to Remember

*   **Strategy is the foundation:** A clear and well-defined strategy is essential for successful WCM implementation.
*   **Simplicity is key:** Schonberger's emphasis on simplicity means strategies should be easy to understand and execute.
*   **Holistic approach:** WCM is not about isolated tools but an integrated system of practices.
*   **Customer focus drives strategy:** Understanding and meeting customer needs is paramount.
*   **Continuous improvement is ongoing:** WCM requires a mindset of perpetual progress.
*   **People are the most important asset:** HRD practices must be deeply integrated with the WCM strategy.
*   **Adaptability is crucial:** Strategies need to evolve with market changes and technological advancements.
*   **Data-driven decision-making:** Back up strategic choices with solid analysis.
