# World Class Manufacturing (WCM) - Module 3: WCM Concepts & Tools

## Topic: Automation Systems

---

### **Introduction to Automation Systems in WCM**

Automation, in the context of World Class Manufacturing (WCM), refers to the use of technology to perform tasks with minimal human intervention. It's not just about replacing labor with machines; it's about enhancing efficiency, quality, flexibility, and safety in manufacturing processes. WCM champions a strategic approach to automation, ensuring it supports the overall manufacturing objectives rather than becoming a standalone technological pursuit.

**Key Concept:** Automation in WCM is about achieving *superior performance* through intelligent application of technology, aligning with principles of simplicity, continuous improvement, and employee involvement.

**Reference:** Schonberger (2013) emphasizes the importance of simplicity and focus in WCM. While automation can be complex, its implementation in WCM should aim to simplify processes and enhance focus on value-adding activities.

---

### **1. Types of Automation Systems**

Automation can be categorized based on the level of flexibility and product variety.

*   **Fixed Automation (or Hard Automation):**
    *   **Definition:** Uses specialized equipment to automate a fixed production process. It's highly efficient for high-volume, low-variety products.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   High initial investment.
        *   High production rates.
        *   Low flexibility; difficult to change product design or volume.
        *   Minimal human intervention in the production line.
    *   **Examples:** Assembly lines for automobiles, automated bottling plants, dedicated machining centers for mass production.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** While WCM often emphasizes flexibility, fixed automation can be strategically employed in specific high-volume, stable product segments to achieve significant cost and efficiency gains.
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Sahay, Saxena, and Kumar (2018) likely discuss how different automation strategies align with different product life cycles and market demands. Fixed automation is suitable for mature products with predictable demand.

*   **Programmable Automation:**
    *   **Definition:** Allows production equipment to be reprogrammed to accommodate different product types and production volumes.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   More flexible than fixed automation.
        *   Requires time and effort to reprogram equipment.
        *   Lower production rates compared to fixed automation.
        *   Suitable for batch production.
    *   **Examples:** CNC (Computer Numerical Control) machines, robotic assembly systems that can be reprogrammed for different product variations.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** This type of automation supports WCM's need for flexibility in responding to market changes and customization demands.
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Jagdev, Brennan, and Browne (2013) in "Strategic Decision Making in Modern Manufacturing" would likely cover how to select automation levels based on strategic goals, where programmable automation offers a good balance for many modern manufacturing environments.

*   **Flexible Automation (or Soft Automation):**
    *   **Definition:** Capable of handling a variety of products with minimal changeover time. It combines features of fixed and programmable automation.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   High flexibility; can produce different product types simultaneously or with very short changeover times.
        *   High initial investment.
        *   Lower production rates than fixed automation but higher than programmable automation for mixed products.
        *   Often involves integrated systems and robotics.
    *   **Examples:** Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS), automated warehouses with robotic sorting and picking, modular production lines.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** This is highly aligned with WCM principles of adaptability, responsiveness, and the ability to cater to a wider range of customer needs.
    *   **Reference Book Insight:** Frazelle (2016) in "World Class Manufacturing and Material Handling" might discuss how flexible automation integrates with material handling systems to create agile manufacturing cells.

### **2. Key Concepts and Technologies in Automation Systems**

Understanding the core components and technologies is crucial for effective implementation of automation in WCM.

*   **Robotics:**
    *   **Definition:** Programmable mechanical devices that can perform physical tasks automatically.
    *   **Types:** Articulated robots, SCARA robots, Cartesian robots, collaborative robots (cobots).
    *   **Applications:** Welding, painting, assembly, material handling, inspection, palletizing.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Robots enhance precision, consistency, speed, and safety, reducing human exposure to hazardous environments. Cobots are particularly relevant for WCM as they facilitate human-robot collaboration, enhancing flexibility and employee involvement.
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Sahay, Saxena, and Kumar (2018) likely cover the role of robotics in improving productivity and quality.

*   **Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) & Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs):**
    *   **Definition:** AGVs follow pre-defined paths, while AMRs can navigate dynamically using sensors and AI.
    *   **Applications:** Material handling, transportation of components, inventory management within the factory.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Improve material flow, reduce bottlenecks, enhance workplace safety, and enable flexible logistics within the plant.
    *   **Reference Book Insight:** Frazelle (2016) would undoubtedly delve into the critical role of AGVs and AMRs in optimizing internal logistics and supporting agile manufacturing systems.

*   **Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs):**
    *   **Definition:** Industrial computers used for automating electromechanical processes. They are rugged and designed for industrial environments.
    *   **Applications:** Controlling machinery, conveyor systems, assembly lines, and other manufacturing processes.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Provide precise control and real-time monitoring of automated systems, enabling continuous improvement and fault detection.
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Sahay, Saxena, and Kumar (2018) might present PLCs as foundational elements for industrial automation, enabling the control of complex manufacturing operations.

*   **Sensors and Actuators:**
    *   **Definition:** Sensors detect physical conditions (e.g., temperature, pressure, position), and actuators perform an action (e.g., move a lever, open a valve).
    *   **Applications:** Monitoring process parameters, detecting product presence, controlling movement, quality inspection.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Essential for data collection for process monitoring, quality control, and enabling intelligent automation. They are the "eyes and hands" of automated systems.

*   **Artificial Intelligence (AI) & Machine Learning (ML):**
    *   **Definition:** AI enables machines to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. ML is a subset of AI that allows systems to learn from data without explicit programming.
    *   **Applications:** Predictive maintenance, quality inspection (vision systems), process optimization, demand forecasting, smart robotics.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Drive advanced automation capabilities, enabling proactive problem-solving, continuous learning, and highly adaptive manufacturing processes.
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Jagdev, Brennan, and Browne (2013) might discuss the strategic advantage of integrating AI/ML into manufacturing for competitive differentiation and enhanced decision-making.

*   **Internet of Things (IoT) in Manufacturing (Industry 4.0):**
    *   **Definition:** Connecting industrial devices, machines, and systems to the internet to enable data exchange and remote control/monitoring.
    *   **Applications:** Real-time data collection from machines, predictive maintenance alerts, remote diagnostics, smart supply chain integration.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Facilitates a connected factory, enabling greater visibility, agility, and data-driven decision-making across all WCM pillars.
    *   **Course Outcome Alignment:** CO7: Identify various modern day trends and terminologies in WCM (Knowledge Level: K1).

### **3. Benefits of Automation Systems in WCM**

Strategic implementation of automation yields significant advantages.

*   **Improved Quality and Consistency:**
    *   Automated systems perform tasks with high precision and repeatability, reducing human error and variation.
    *   **Example:** Robotic welding ensures consistent weld quality, eliminating human inconsistencies.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Directly supports WCM's focus on zero defects and high-quality products.
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Schonberger (2013) emphasizes the importance of simplicity in achieving quality. Automation, when implemented thoughtfully, can simplify quality control processes.

*   **Increased Productivity and Throughput:**
    *   Automated machines can operate continuously at higher speeds than humans, increasing output.
    *   **Example:** Automated assembly lines can produce more units per hour than manual assembly.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Essential for achieving cost competitiveness and meeting market demand efficiently.

*   **Reduced Operating Costs:**
    *   Lower labor costs, reduced material waste due to precision, and increased efficiency contribute to lower operational expenses.
    *   **Example:** Automated material handling reduces labor and potential damage during transport.
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Sahay, Saxena, and Kumar (2018) likely discuss cost reduction as a primary driver for adopting automation.

*   **Enhanced Safety and Ergonomics:**
    *   Automating dangerous, repetitive, or ergonomically challenging tasks protects workers from injuries.
    *   **Example:** Robots handling heavy lifting or working in hazardous environments.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Aligns with WCM's emphasis on employee well-being and creating a safe working environment.
    *   **Course Outcome Alignment:** CO3: To Identify the employee involvement in WCM (Knowledge Level: K1). While automation can reduce direct manual labor, it shifts employee roles towards supervision, maintenance, and higher-value tasks, often in safer conditions.

*   **Increased Flexibility and Responsiveness:**
    *   Programmable and flexible automation allows manufacturers to adapt to changing product designs, customer demands, and production volumes.
    *   **Example:** FMS can switch between producing different parts with minimal downtime.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Crucial for WCM's ability to compete in dynamic markets.

*   **Data Acquisition and Process Monitoring:**
    *   Automated systems generate vast amounts of data, providing real-time insights into process performance, quality, and efficiency.
    *   **WCM Relevance:** Enables data-driven decision-making and continuous improvement through detailed analysis.

### **4. WCM Considerations for Automation Implementation**

Simply installing automation is not enough. WCM demands a strategic and integrated approach.

*   **Alignment with WCM Pillars:**
    *   Automation should support goals related to quality, cost reduction, delivery, safety, morale, and innovation.
    *   **Example:** Implementing automated inspection to achieve zero defects (Quality pillar).

*   **Simplicity and Focus (Schonberger's Principle):**
    *   Automation should simplify processes where possible, rather than adding unnecessary complexity.
    *   Focus on automating core, value-adding activities. Avoid over-automation.
    *   **Schonberger (2013) "The Lessons of Simplicity Applied":** This is a crucial insight. Complex automation that is difficult to manage or maintain can negate WCM benefits.

*   **Employee Involvement and Skill Development:**
    *   While automation may reduce the need for manual labor, it increases the need for skilled technicians, engineers, and supervisors to operate, maintain, and program these systems.
    *   WCM requires investing in training and upskilling the workforce to manage advanced automation.
    *   **Example:** Training operators to become robot technicians or automation system supervisors.
    *   **Course Outcome Alignment:** CO3: To Identify the employee involvement in WCM (Knowledge Level: K1). Automation changes, rather than eliminates, employee involvement, requiring adaptation and skill enhancement.

*   **Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) for Automation:**
    *   Automated systems require robust maintenance strategies to ensure uptime and reliability. Implementing TPM principles for automated equipment is crucial.
    *   **Example:** Autonomous maintenance by operators on automated machinery, planned maintenance by specialists.

*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis and ROI:**
    *   Thorough evaluation of the total cost of ownership (initial investment, maintenance, training, integration) versus the expected benefits (productivity, quality, cost savings).
    *   **Textbook Insight:** Jagdev, Brennan, and Browne (2013) would stress the strategic importance of rigorous financial justification for automation investments.

*   **Integration with Existing Systems:**
    *   Automation solutions must be compatible with existing enterprise resource planning (ERP), manufacturing execution systems (MES), and other operational technologies.
    *   **Course Outcome Alignment:** CO5: Learn different concepts in Modern day Manufacturing (Knowledge Level: K1). Integration is a hallmark of modern manufacturing systems.

*   **Flexibility vs. Efficiency Trade-off:**
    *   Understanding that there's often a trade-off between the efficiency of specialized fixed automation and the flexibility of programmable/flexible systems. The choice depends on the product portfolio and market strategy.
    *   **Course Outcome Alignment:** CO4: To Examine & Categorize different tools in WCM (Knowledge Level: K2). This involves evaluating different automation types against WCM objectives.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Explain the difference between Fixed Automation and Flexible Automation, and provide an example of each in the context of a WCM philosophy.

**Answer 1:**
*   **Fixed Automation:** Uses specialized, non-programmable equipment for high-volume, low-variety production. It is efficient but inflexible. Example: An automotive assembly line with dedicated robots for spot welding specific car models. In WCM, this might be used for highly standardized, high-demand components where quality and cost are paramount, provided its inflexibility doesn't hinder adaptation to broader market shifts.
*   **Flexible Automation:** Uses programmable equipment that can be easily reprogrammed to produce a variety of products. It offers higher flexibility at a potential cost of lower unit efficiency compared to fixed automation for a single product. Example: A CNC machining center that can be programmed to create different part geometries. In WCM, this supports the ability to respond to customer customization and product variations, a key competitive advantage.

**Question 2:**
According to Schonberger's "Lessons of Simplicity Applied," what is a critical consideration when implementing automation in a WCM environment?

**Answer 2:**
The critical consideration is that automation should **simplify processes** and enhance focus on value-adding activities, rather than adding unnecessary complexity. Over-automation that leads to difficult-to-manage or maintain systems can undermine WCM goals.

**Question 3:**
How does automation support the WCM pillar of Employee Morale/Safety?

**Answer 3:**
Automation supports employee morale and safety by taking over hazardous, repetitive, or ergonomically challenging tasks. This reduces the risk of workplace injuries and allows employees to shift their focus to more engaging, skilled, and safer roles such as monitoring, programming, maintenance, and problem-solving.

**Question 4 (Scenario-based):**
A company produces a range of custom-designed furniture. They are considering implementing automation. What type of automation system would likely be most suitable, and why, considering WCM principles?

**Answer 4:**
For a company producing custom-designed furniture (implying high variety and potentially lower volumes of each specific design), **Flexible Automation** would likely be the most suitable.
*   **Reasoning in WCM context:**
    *   **Flexibility:** WCM thrives on adaptability. Flexible automation allows the company to switch between different designs and customize orders efficiently with minimal changeover times.
    *   **Responsiveness:** It enables quicker responses to market trends and individual customer requests.
    *   **Quality:** Automated cutting, CNC routing, and robotic finishing can ensure high precision and consistent quality for intricate custom designs, reducing errors associated with manual craftsmanship for complex tasks.
    *   **Employee Involvement:** While it replaces some manual tasks, it requires skilled operators to program, monitor, and maintain the automated machinery, potentially leading to more engaging roles.

**Question 5:**
What role do sensors and actuators play in achieving WCM goals related to quality and data-driven decision-making?

**Answer 5:**
Sensors and actuators are the foundational elements of intelligent automation.
*   **Quality:** Sensors provide real-time data on process parameters (e.g., temperature, pressure, dimensions, presence of defects). This allows for immediate adjustments by actuators or the control system to maintain quality standards and prevent deviations. Automated inspection systems, relying on advanced sensors, are key to achieving zero defects.
*   **Data-Driven Decision-Making:** The data collected by sensors is crucial for monitoring process performance, identifying trends, detecting anomalies, and enabling predictive maintenance. This rich dataset allows WCM practitioners to analyze performance, pinpoint areas for improvement, and make informed decisions for continuous enhancement, supporting pillars like efficiency and cost reduction.

### **6. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Automation is a Tool, Not an End Goal:** In WCM, automation must serve strategic objectives, not be pursued for its own sake.
*   **Simplicity is Key:** Implement automation that simplifies rather than complicates operations.
*   **Human-Machine Collaboration:** Embrace technologies like cobots that enhance human capabilities rather than just replace humans.
*   **Workforce Development is Crucial:** Invest in training and upskilling your workforce to manage and benefit from automation.
*   **Integration is Paramount:** Ensure new automated systems integrate seamlessly with existing infrastructure and information systems.
*   **Flexibility vs. Efficiency:** Understand the trade-offs and choose the automation type that best matches your product strategy and market demands.
*   **Data is Gold:** Leverage the data generated by automation for continuous improvement and intelligent decision-making.
*   **Maintenance Matters:** Robust maintenance strategies (like TPM) are essential for ensuring the reliability and uptime of automated systems.
