# World Class Manufacturing - Module 3: WCM Concepts & Tools
## Topic: Flexible Manufacturing

### **Introduction**

In today's dynamic and competitive manufacturing landscape, the ability to adapt to changing market demands, product variations, and customer needs is paramount. Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS) are a cornerstone of World Class Manufacturing (WCM) that enable this agility. This topic delves into the core concepts, benefits, types, and implementation considerations of flexible manufacturing, directly contributing to the WCM goal of delivering high-quality products efficiently and responsively.

### **Key Concepts & Definitions**

*   **Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS):** An integrated manufacturing system that can produce a variety of parts or products with minimal changeover time between different product types. It combines automated material handling, programmable machines, and computer control.
*   **Agility:** The ability of a manufacturing system to respond quickly and effectively to changes in product design, process, volume, and customer demand.
*   **Reconfigurability:** The capability of an FMS to be altered or rearranged to accommodate new products, processes, or production volumes.
*   **Modularity:** The design of an FMS using interchangeable components or modules that can be combined or reconfigured to create different system configurations.
*   **Changeover Time:** The time required to switch from producing one product to producing another. A key objective in FMS is to minimize this time.
*   **Batch Size:** The number of units of a particular product produced in a single production run. FMS excels at producing small batch sizes economically.

---

### **Schonberger's Perspective on Simplicity and Flexibility**

Richard J. Schonberger, in "World Class Manufacturing: The Lessons of Simplicity Applied," emphasizes that true WCM often stems from *simplifying complex processes*. While FMS might seem inherently complex, its goal is to simplify the *response* to variety. By standardizing components, modularizing production, and automating tasks, FMS aims to reduce the complexity of managing diverse product lines. Schonberger would likely view a well-implemented FMS as a system that simplifies the *transition* between products, allowing for a more consistent and predictable production flow, even with variations.

**Key takeaway from Schonberger:** Flexibility in FMS is achieved through simplification of the *transition* and *management* of variety, rather than necessarily through over-engineering.

### **Core Components of a Flexible Manufacturing System**

A typical FMS comprises the following key components:

1.  **Computer Control System:**
    *   **Central Computer:** Manages the overall operation, scheduling, routing of parts, and coordination of all components.
    *   **Machine Control Units:** Programmable controllers for individual machines (e.g., CNC machines).
    *   **Workstations:** Automated machines like CNC turning centers, milling machines, drilling machines, robotic work cells.
    *   **Material Handling System:** Automated guided vehicles (AGVs), conveyor belts, or robotic arms to transport parts between workstations.
    *   **Tool Management System:** Automated tool changers and tool storage to ensure the right tools are available at the right time.
    *   **Inspection Stations:** Automated inspection equipment for quality control.

2.  **Programmable Machines:**
    *   CNC (Computer Numerical Control) machines are the backbone of FMS. They can be programmed to perform a wide range of operations on different parts.

3.  **Automated Material Handling System (AMHS):**
    *   Crucial for transporting raw materials, work-in-progress, and finished goods between workstations, storage areas, and inspection stations without manual intervention.

4.  **Workstations:**
    *   These are the processing units where manufacturing operations take place. They can be general-purpose machines or specialized cells.

### **Types of Flexible Manufacturing Systems**

FMS can be categorized based on their scope and complexity:

1.  **Flexible Manufacturing Cells (FMCs):**
    *   A small subset of FMS, typically consisting of 2-5 machines, automated material handling, and possibly a robotic arm.
    *   Designed to produce a family of parts with similar processing requirements.
    *   **Example:** A cell with a CNC lathe, a CNC milling machine, and a robotic loader to produce a range of similar turned and milled components.

2.  **Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS) - Standalone:**
    *   A more comprehensive system with multiple workstations, an integrated material handling system, and a central computer control.
    *   Can handle a wider variety of parts and operations.
    *   **Example:** A system comprising several CNC machining centers, a turning center, an automated inspection station, and AGVs for material transport, capable of producing a diverse set of metal components.

3.  **System of Flexible Manufacturing Cells (SFMC):**
    *   A network of interconnected FMCs.
    *   Offers higher flexibility and throughput by allowing parts to be routed to different cells.
    *   **Example:** Multiple FMCs, each specializing in different types of operations (e.g., turning, milling, drilling), all linked by a common AGV system and a central scheduler.

### **Benefits of Flexible Manufacturing**

Adopting FMS aligns with WCM principles by offering significant advantages:

*   **Increased Productivity:** Automated material handling and reduced setup times lead to higher machine utilization and throughput.
*   **Reduced Lead Times:** Faster changeovers and efficient scheduling shorten the time from order to delivery.
*   **Improved Product Quality:** Consistent automated operations reduce human error, leading to higher quality and reduced scrap.
*   **Lower Work-in-Progress (WIP) Inventory:** Efficient scheduling and smaller batch sizes minimize the amount of unfinished inventory.
*   **Enhanced Flexibility and Responsiveness:** Ability to produce a wider variety of products and adapt to design changes or demand fluctuations.
*   **Reduced Labor Costs:** Automation reduces the need for manual labor for material handling and machine operation, though it requires more skilled labor for setup and maintenance.
*   **Better Resource Utilization:** Machines are utilized more effectively due to reduced downtime for setups and efficient scheduling.
*   **Cost-Effective Production of Small Batches:** FMS makes it economically viable to produce small quantities of highly varied products.

### **Implementing Flexible Manufacturing: Key Considerations**

Implementing an FMS is a strategic decision that requires careful planning and execution:

*   **Product Family Analysis:** Identifying groups of parts that share similar manufacturing processes is crucial for designing an effective FMS.
*   **Machine Selection and Tooling:** Choosing appropriate CNC machines and ensuring availability of necessary tooling is vital.
*   **Material Handling System Design:** The efficiency of the AMHS directly impacts the overall FMS performance.
*   **Computer Control and Software:** Selecting robust control systems and scheduling software is essential for managing complex operations.
*   **Workforce Training and Skill Development:** Operating and maintaining an FMS requires highly skilled personnel. This ties into CO3 (employee involvement).
*   **Integration with Other Systems:** FMS needs to be integrated with enterprise resource planning (ERP) and manufacturing execution systems (MES) for seamless operation.
*   **Maintenance and Reliability:** Proactive maintenance strategies are critical to ensure the uptime and reliability of automated components.

### **Examples and Applications**

*   **Automotive Industry:** FMS is widely used to produce various car models and their components efficiently, allowing for customization and rapid response to market trends.
*   **Aerospace Industry:** High-precision components for aircraft are often manufactured using FMS due to the need for tight tolerances and the variety of parts.
*   **Electronics Manufacturing:** Assembly of complex electronic devices can benefit from FMS's ability to handle different product configurations.
*   **Job Shops:** Small to medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) that produce a wide range of custom parts for different clients can leverage FMS to improve competitiveness.

### **Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:** Define Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS) and explain its primary objective in the context of World Class Manufacturing.
**Answer:** A Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS) is an integrated manufacturing system capable of producing a variety of parts or products with minimal changeover time between different product types. Its primary objective in WCM is to enhance agility and responsiveness to market demands, allowing for the efficient production of a diverse product mix while maintaining high quality and productivity.

**Question 2:** Differentiate between a Flexible Manufacturing Cell (FMC) and a full-scale Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS).
**Answer:** A Flexible Manufacturing Cell (FMC) is a smaller, focused system typically comprising 2-5 machines designed to produce a family of similar parts. A full-scale Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS) is a more extensive and integrated system with multiple workstations, a sophisticated automated material handling system, and advanced computer control, capable of handling a broader range of parts and operations.

**Question 3:** How does the implementation of an FMS contribute to reducing lead times in manufacturing?
**Answer:** FMS reduces lead times through several mechanisms:
    *   **Minimized Setup Times:** Automated tool changers and efficient part routing reduce the time spent preparing for a new product.
    *   **Efficient Scheduling:** Computer control optimizes production schedules, ensuring parts move smoothly through the system.
    *   **Reduced WIP:** Smaller batch sizes and continuous flow reduce the amount of work-in-progress inventory, expediting the overall production process.

**Question 4:** Discuss the role of skilled labor in operating and maintaining an FMS, aligning with Course Outcome 3 (Employee Involvement).
**Answer:** While FMS involves significant automation, skilled labor is crucial. Employees are involved in:
    *   **Programming and Setup:** Highly skilled technicians program the machines and configure the system for new products.
    *   **Monitoring and Supervision:** Operators oversee the system's operation, ensuring smooth workflow and identifying potential issues.
    *   **Maintenance and Troubleshooting:** Specialized engineers and technicians are responsible for maintaining the complex machinery and troubleshooting any breakdowns.
    *   **Process Improvement:** Skilled employees can analyze FMS performance and suggest improvements, contributing to continuous improvement goals.

**Question 5:** Considering the Indian manufacturing scenario, what are some potential challenges and benefits of adopting FMS? (Aligns with CO6)
**Answer:**
    *   **Benefits:** Increased competitiveness, ability to cater to a diverse and growing domestic market, improved quality standards, and potential for export growth.
    *   **Challenges:** High initial capital investment, the need for a skilled and trained workforce, integration with existing legacy systems, and the requirement for robust IT infrastructure.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Flexibility is Key:** FMS is all about adapting to change efficiently.
*   **Integration is Crucial:** All components of an FMS must work seamlessly together.
*   **Automation with Intelligence:** It's not just automation; it's intelligent automation managed by sophisticated computer systems.
*   **Skilled Workforce is Essential:** The human element is vital for operating, maintaining, and improving FMS.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** While the initial investment is high, the long-term benefits in terms of productivity, quality, and responsiveness can be substantial.
