# NUCLEAR ENERGY

## Module 3: COMPONENTS OF NUCLEAR REACTOR: Fermi pile – control, safety, radiation monitoring

### Topic: Nuclear Reactor Types - Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR)

---

### **1. Introduction to Pressurized Water Reactors (PWRs)**

*   **Definition:** A Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR) is a type of light-water reactor (LWR) that is the most common type of nuclear reactor used for generating electricity worldwide. It uses ordinary water (light water) as both the **neutron moderator** and the **coolant**.
*   **Core Concept:** The reactor core heats water under high pressure, preventing it from boiling. This hot, high-pressure water then flows to a steam generator, where it transfers its heat to a separate loop of water, producing steam. This steam drives a turbine, which in turn drives an electric generator.

---

### **2. Key Components of a PWR (CO1: K2)**

*   **Reactor Core:**
    *   **Fuel Rods:** Typically contain uranium dioxide ($\text{UO}_2$) pellets enriched to 3-5% Uranium-235 ($\text{U}^{235}$). These rods are encased in Zircaloy (a zirconium alloy) cladding.
    *   **Control Rods:** Made of neutron-absorbing materials (e.g., cadmium, boron, hafnium). They are inserted or withdrawn from the core to control the rate of the nuclear fission reaction.
    *   **Moderator:** Light water acts as the moderator, slowing down fast neutrons produced by fission to thermal speeds, making them more likely to cause further fission.
    *   **Coolant:** Light water also serves as the coolant, removing the heat generated by fission from the core.
*   **Reactor Vessel:** A thick-walled steel pressure vessel that houses the reactor core, control rods, and primary coolant. It is designed to withstand the high pressure and temperature of the primary coolant.
*   **Pressurizer:** A component connected to the primary coolant loop that maintains the high pressure in the system. It typically contains a water-steam interface and electric heaters to control pressure.
*   **Steam Generators:** Heat exchangers where the heat from the hot, high-pressure primary coolant is transferred to a secondary loop of water, producing steam. This separation prevents radioactive materials from reaching the turbine.
*   **Primary Coolant Loop:** The closed loop carrying the high-pressure water from the reactor core, through the steam generators, and back to the reactor vessel.
*   **Secondary Coolant Loop:** The loop carrying water to the steam generators, where it turns into steam and flows to the turbine.
*   **Turbine:** A device that converts the thermal energy of steam into rotational mechanical energy.
*   **Generator:** Converts the mechanical energy of the turbine into electrical energy.
*   **Condenser:** Cools the steam exiting the turbine, converting it back into water, which is then pumped back to the steam generator. Cooling is typically achieved using water from a river, lake, or ocean, or via cooling towers.

---

### **3. Principles of Reactor Control (CO2: K2)**

*   **Reactivity Control:** The ability to maintain a stable, critical chain reaction or to shut down the reactor is paramount.
*   **Control Rods:**
    *   **Insertion:** Absorbs neutrons, decreasing reactivity and slowing down the fission rate.
    *   **Withdrawal:** Reduces neutron absorption, increasing reactivity and accelerating the fission rate.
    *   **Emergency Shutdown (Scram):** Rapid insertion of all control rods to shut down the reactor immediately.
*   **Boron Concentration (Chemical Shim):** Boric acid can be dissolved in the primary coolant.
    *   **Increasing Boron Concentration:** Absorbs neutrons, decreasing reactivity. Used for long-term reactivity control and to compensate for fuel burnup.
    *   **Decreasing Boron Concentration (Boron Dilution):** Increases reactivity. This process is carefully controlled.
*   **Moderator Temperature Coefficient:** As the water moderator heats up, it becomes less dense. This reduces its effectiveness in moderating neutrons. In PWRs, this usually results in a negative temperature coefficient of reactivity, which is a desirable safety feature (a form of inherent safety).
*   **Fuel Temperature Coefficient (Doppler Broadening):** As the fuel heats up, the absorption of neutrons in $\text{U}^{238}$ (which capture fast neutrons before they can be moderated) increases. This also results in a negative reactivity feedback, further enhancing safety.

---

### **4. Safety Mechanisms in PWRs (CO2: K2, CO4: K2)**

*   **Negative Reactivity Coefficients:** As mentioned above, the negative temperature coefficients of the moderator and fuel provide inherent stability. An increase in temperature or power leads to a decrease in reactivity, automatically limiting the power output.
*   **Control Rods:** Provide active and rapid shutdown capability.
*   **Containment Building:** A robust, leak-tight structure (usually steel-lined reinforced concrete) surrounding the reactor vessel and primary coolant system. Its purpose is to prevent the release of radioactive materials into the environment in case of an accident.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Withstand internal pressure from steam release during an accident.
        *   Prevent release of radioactive fission products.
        *   Provide a barrier against external hazards (e.g., aircraft impact).
*   **Emergency Core Cooling Systems (ECCS):** Redundant systems designed to inject cooling water into the reactor core in case of a loss-of-coolant accident (LOCA).
    *   **High-Pressure Injection System (HPIS):** Injects borated water at high pressure.
    *   **Low-Pressure Injection System (LPIS):** Injects large volumes of borated water at lower pressure.
    *   **Accumulators:** Tanks containing borated water under nitrogen pressure, which automatically inject water when the primary system pressure drops significantly.
*   **Decay Heat Removal Systems:** Systems to remove residual heat from the core after shutdown, preventing overheating.
*   **Reactor Protection System (RPS):** An automated system that monitors reactor parameters and initiates safety actions, such as scramming the control rods or activating ECCS, if abnormal conditions are detected.

---

### **5. Radiation Monitoring (CO2: K2)**

*   **Purpose:** To detect and quantify ionizing radiation levels within the plant and in the surrounding environment, ensuring the safety of personnel and the public.
*   **Key Areas Monitored:**
    *   **Reactor Building:** To detect leaks from the primary system.
    *   **Fuel Handling Areas:** To monitor radiation from fresh and spent fuel.
    *   **Waste Treatment Areas:** To monitor radioactivity in liquid and gaseous effluents.
    *   **Personnel Dosimetry:** To measure the radiation dose received by workers.
    *   **Environmental Monitoring:** Air, water, and soil samples around the plant.
*   **Instrumentation:**
    *   **Geiger-Müller Counters:** Detect alpha, beta, and gamma radiation.
    *   **Scintillation Detectors:** Convert radiation into light pulses, which are then detected.
    *   **Ionization Chambers:** Measure radiation by detecting the ionization produced in a gas.
    *   **Thermoluminescent Dosimeters (TLDs):** Worn by personnel to record accumulated dose.
    *   **Area Radiation Monitors (ARMs):** Fixed detectors providing continuous readings of radiation levels in different plant areas.
    *   **Process Radiation Monitors (PRMs):** Monitor radiation levels in the primary and secondary coolant loops and effluent streams.

### **6. Fermi Pile (Chicago Pile-1) - Historical Context (Relevant to Control & Safety Principles)**

*   **Significance:** The Chicago Pile-1 (CP-1), led by Enrico Fermi, was the world's first artificial nuclear reactor, achieving criticality on December 2, 1942. It was a precursor to modern PWRs in demonstrating controlled nuclear fission.
*   **Construction:** A "pile" of graphite blocks and uranium "fuel" (natural uranium metal and uranium oxide) arranged in a lattice.
*   **Control:**
    *   **Cadmium Rods:** Used for initial control and shutdown. Cadmium is a strong neutron absorber.
    *   **Manual Adjustment:** Fermi and his team manually adjusted the position of cadmium rods to achieve and maintain criticality.
*   **Safety:**
    *   **Low Power:** Operated at very low power levels (initially less than a watt) to minimize heat generation and radiation hazards.
    *   **Air Cooling:** No active cooling system was present; heat was dissipated into the air.
    *   **No Containment:** Located in a squash court under the stands of Stagg Field at the University of Chicago.
    *   **Simplicity:** The relative simplicity of CP-1 allowed for direct observation of its behavior, providing fundamental insights into reactor physics and control.

### **7. Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1 (CO1: K2):** Briefly describe the role of the steam generator in a Pressurized Water Reactor.

**Answer 1:** The steam generator in a PWR is a heat exchanger. It transfers heat from the hot, high-pressure primary coolant (water) to a separate secondary loop of water, causing the secondary water to boil and produce steam. This steam then drives the turbine to generate electricity, while the primary coolant remains radioactive and contained within the primary loop.

**Question 2 (CO2: K2):** Explain how control rods are used to control the nuclear reaction rate in a PWR.

**Answer 2:** Control rods are made of materials that absorb neutrons (e.g., cadmium, boron). By inserting control rods deeper into the reactor core, more neutrons are absorbed, reducing the number of neutrons available for fission and thus slowing down the reaction rate (decreasing reactivity). Conversely, withdrawing control rods decreases neutron absorption, allowing more neutrons to cause fission and increasing the reaction rate (increasing reactivity).

**Question 3 (CO4: K2):** What is the primary purpose of the containment building in a PWR?

**Answer 3:** The primary purpose of the containment building is to prevent the release of radioactive materials into the environment in the event of an accident, such as a loss-of-coolant accident. It acts as a robust, leak-tight barrier designed to withstand internal pressure and contain any radioactive fission products.

**Question 4 (CO2: K2):** Identify two inherent safety features present in a PWR.

**Answer 4:** Two inherent safety features in a PWR are:
1.  **Negative Moderator Temperature Coefficient:** As the moderator (water) heats up, its density decreases, reducing its effectiveness in slowing down neutrons. This leads to a decrease in the fission rate.
2.  **Negative Fuel Temperature Coefficient (Doppler Broadening):** As the fuel temperature increases, the absorption of neutrons by Uranium-238 increases, reducing the number of neutrons available for fission.

**Question 5 (CO1: K2):** Name the two main types of coolant used in a PWR and their respective roles.

**Answer 5:** The two main types of coolant in a PWR are:
1.  **Light Water (Ordinary Water):** Acts as both the **neutron moderator** (slowing down neutrons) and the **primary coolant** (removing heat from the reactor core).
2.  **Steam (in the secondary loop):** Acts as the **working fluid** that drives the turbine after being heated by the primary coolant in the steam generator.

---

### **8. Important Points to Remember**

*   **PWRs use light water for both moderation and cooling.**
*   **The primary coolant is kept under high pressure to prevent boiling in the reactor core.**
*   **The separation of the primary (radioactive) and secondary (non-radioactive) coolant loops is crucial for safety.**
*   **Negative reactivity coefficients are essential for the inherent stability of PWRs.**
*   **The containment building and ECCS are vital safety systems for accident mitigation.**
*   **Radiation monitoring is continuous to protect personnel and the environment.**
*   **Enrico Fermi's Chicago Pile-1 was the foundational experiment for controlled nuclear fission, demonstrating early principles of reactor control.**
