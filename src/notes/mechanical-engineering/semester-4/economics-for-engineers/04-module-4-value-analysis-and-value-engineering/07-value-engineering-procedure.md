# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 4: Value Analysis and Value Engineering

## Topic: Value Engineering Procedure

---

**Module Overview:** This module delves into the principles and practices of Value Analysis (VA) and Value Engineering (VE). While VA focuses on improving existing products or processes, VE applies these principles during the design and development phase. Understanding these concepts is crucial for engineers to optimize cost, performance, and reliability, ultimately leading to more competitive and valuable offerings.

**Topic Focus:** This specific topic outlines the systematic procedure followed in Value Engineering projects.

---

### Learning Outcomes Covered:

*   **LO1:** Understand the systematic steps involved in a Value Engineering project.
*   **LO2:** Identify the purpose and application of each phase in the VE process.
*   **LO3:** Recognize the importance of creativity and teamwork in VE.
*   **LO4:** Appreciate how VE contributes to achieving product and project objectives.

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Value Engineering (VE):** A systematic, organized approach to providing the necessary functions in a product or system at the lowest possible cost without sacrificing quality, performance, or reliability. (Adapted from Thuesen & Fabrycky)
*   **Value:** The ratio of function to cost. Value = Function / Cost. VE aims to increase value by increasing function, decreasing cost, or both.
*   **Function:** The purpose or work performed by a product, system, or component. Functions can be primary (essential) or secondary (supportive).
*   **Cost:** All expenses incurred in the design, manufacturing, marketing, and use of a product.
*   **Performance:** The degree to which a product or system meets its intended purpose.
*   **Reliability:** The probability that a product or system will perform its intended function without failure for a specified period.
*   **Quality:** The degree to which a product or system meets specified requirements and customer expectations.

## Value Engineering Procedure

The Value Engineering procedure is a structured, multi-phase approach designed to systematically analyze products, systems, or processes to achieve the highest possible value. It is often depicted as a cyclical or iterative process, emphasizing continuous improvement. While variations exist, the most common and widely accepted procedure consists of the following key phases:

### Phase 1: Information Phase (Orientation)

*   **Purpose:** To gather all necessary information about the product, system, or process being analyzed. This phase ensures a thorough understanding of what is being worked on and its current state.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Define the Scope:** Clearly establish what is being analyzed – a specific product, a component, a process, or a system.
    *   **Gather Data:**
        *   **Design Documents:** Drawings, specifications, blueprints, CAD models.
        *   **Manufacturing Information:** Production methods, materials used, assembly sequences, tooling.
        *   **Cost Data:** Material costs, labor costs, overhead costs, tooling costs, shipping costs.
        *   **Performance Data:** Technical specifications, test results, operational data.
        *   **Customer Feedback/Requirements:** User reviews, complaints, market research.
        *   **Market Information:** Competitor products, pricing, industry standards.
        *   **Safety and Regulatory Requirements.**
    *   **Understand the Product/System:** How it works, its intended function, its history, its market position.
    *   **Identify Key Stakeholders:** Designers, manufacturers, customers, suppliers, management.
*   **Deliverables:** A comprehensive information package that serves as the foundation for subsequent phases.
*   **Emphasis:** Accuracy, completeness, and objectivity. This is not the phase for making suggestions.

*   **Reference Insight:** Geetika, Ghosh, and Chodhury emphasize the importance of understanding the "what" and "why" of the existing product or system in the context of market demand and competitive landscape. (Managerial Economics, 2015)

### Phase 2: Functional Analysis Phase (Investigation)

*   **Purpose:** To identify and analyze the functions performed by the product, system, or process and their importance and cost. This is the core of VE where the "what" is broken down into its essential "why" (function).
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Identify All Functions:** List all actions or purposes the product or its components serve.
    *   **Classify Functions:**
        *   **Primary Functions:** The main purpose for which the product or component exists (e.g., a screw's primary function is to fasten).
        *   **Secondary Functions:** Functions that support the primary function or are a consequence of the design (e.g., a screw's secondary function might be to provide a certain aesthetic appearance or ease of disassembly).
    *   **Define Functions Using Verbs and Nouns:** This ensures clarity and specificity (e.g., "Transmit Power," "Support Load," "Contain Fluid").
    *   **Quantify and Qualify Functions:** Assess the importance and necessity of each function.
    *   **Determine Function Costs:** Allocate costs to each identified function. This is often challenging but crucial for identifying areas for improvement.
    *   **Analyze Function Interrelationships:** How functions interact and affect each other.
    *   **Use Tools like Function Analysis System Technique (FAST) Diagram:** FAST diagrams visually represent the logical sequence of functions and help identify dependencies and opportunities for elimination or simplification.
*   **Deliverables:** A clear list of functions, their classification, cost allocation, and potentially a FAST diagram.
*   **Emphasis:** Focusing on *what* the product/system *does*, not *how* it does it. Separating essential functions from non-essential ones.

*   **Example:** Consider a simple metal bracket.
    *   **Primary Function:** Support component X.
    *   **Secondary Functions:** Provide alignment for component Y, offer a surface for labeling, provide a handle for installation.
    *   The VE team would analyze the cost of the bracket and try to achieve the "Support component X" function at a lower cost, potentially questioning the necessity or cost-effectiveness of the secondary functions.

*   **Reference Insight:** Park highlights that functional analysis is the cornerstone of VE, enabling the identification of unnecessary costs associated with functions that do not contribute to the product's primary purpose. (Contemporary Engineering Economics, 2001)

### Phase 3: Creative Phase (Speculation/Ideation)

*   **Purpose:** To generate a wide range of alternative solutions that can perform the identified functions, ideally at a lower cost or with improved performance.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Brainstorming:** Encourage free-thinking and the generation of as many ideas as possible without initial judgment.
    *   **Use Creative Thinking Techniques:**
        *   **Brainstorming:** Group idea generation.
        *   **Brainwriting:** Individual idea generation followed by sharing.
        *   **Reverse Brainstorming:** Identify problems and then find solutions.
        *   **Attribute Listing:** Break down the product into attributes and generate ideas for each.
        *   **SCAMPER Technique:** Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse.
    *   **Focus on Function:** Generate ideas based on performing the essential functions identified in Phase 2.
    *   **Challenge Assumptions:** Question existing designs, materials, and manufacturing processes.
    *   **Encourage "Wild" Ideas:** The most innovative solutions often come from initially unconventional suggestions.
    *   **Consider Different Materials, Manufacturing Processes, Designs, Simplifications, and Eliminations.**
*   **Deliverables:** A comprehensive list of potential alternative solutions or ideas.
*   **Emphasis:** Quantity over quality initially. Openness to all suggestions. Avoiding criticism during this phase.

*   **Reference Insight:** Thuesen and Fabrycky emphasize that this phase requires a collaborative environment where diverse perspectives can lead to innovative solutions. The focus is on "how" to achieve the desired function differently. (Engineering Economy, 1966)

### Phase 4: Evaluation Phase (Judgment/Analysis)

*   **Purpose:** To analyze, refine, and select the most promising ideas generated in the creative phase based on feasibility, cost, performance, and other criteria.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Screen Ideas:** Filter out ideas that are impractical, infeasible, or do not meet basic requirements.
    *   **Develop Promising Ideas:** Flesh out the selected ideas with more detail.
    *   **Analyze Each Viable Alternative:**
        *   **Cost Estimation:** Estimate the cost of implementing each alternative.
        *   **Performance Evaluation:** Assess how the alternative performs compared to the original.
        *   **Feasibility Assessment:** Technical feasibility, manufacturing feasibility, market acceptance.
        *   **Risk Analysis:** Identify potential risks associated with each alternative.
        *   **Life Cycle Cost Analysis:** Consider costs beyond initial production, including maintenance and disposal.
        *   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Quantify the benefits (cost savings, performance improvements) against the costs of implementation.
    *   **Rank Alternatives:** Prioritize the alternatives based on their potential value improvement.
*   **Deliverables:** A prioritized list of evaluated alternatives with supporting analysis.
*   **Emphasis:** Critical assessment, data-driven decision-making, and realistic projections.

*   **Example:** Continuing with the bracket example, if an idea was to use a plastic bracket instead of metal:
    *   **Cost Estimation:** Compare material and manufacturing costs.
    *   **Performance Evaluation:** Assess load-bearing capacity, heat resistance, and durability of plastic vs. metal.
    *   **Feasibility:** Can the plastic bracket be manufactured with the required tolerances? Is it acceptable to the end-user?
    *   **Risk Analysis:** Potential for creep or degradation of plastic over time.

*   **Reference Insight:** Paneerselvam discusses the importance of rigorous financial and technical evaluation to ensure that proposed VE solutions are not only cost-effective but also technically sound and align with organizational goals. (Engineering Economics, 2012)

### Phase 5: Development Phase (Research & Planning)

*   **Purpose:** To develop the selected alternatives into detailed proposals or action plans, ready for implementation. This phase involves detailed engineering, design, and planning.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Detailed Design & Engineering:** Create detailed drawings, specifications, and prototypes for the chosen solution.
    *   **Process Planning:** Outline the manufacturing or implementation process.
    *   **Supplier Identification & Negotiation:** If new materials or components are involved.
    *   **Testing and Validation:** Conduct rigorous testing to confirm performance, reliability, and quality.
    *   **Cost Refinement:** Develop a precise cost estimate for implementation.
    *   **Prepare a Formal Proposal:** Document the proposed change, its benefits, costs, implementation plan, and expected outcomes.
*   **Deliverables:** A detailed design, a prototype, a comprehensive implementation plan, and a formal proposal.
*   **Emphasis:** Precision, thoroughness, and preparation for action.

*   **Reference Insight:** Blank and Tarquin emphasize the need for detailed planning and design validation to ensure the successful transition from concept to implemented solution, often involving simulation and pilot testing. (Engineering Economy, 7th Edition)

### Phase 6: Presentation Phase (Approval)

*   **Purpose:** To present the VE proposal to decision-makers for approval and funding.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Prepare a Persuasive Presentation:** Clearly articulate the problem, the proposed solution, the benefits (cost savings, performance improvements, etc.), implementation costs, and expected ROI.
    *   **Address Potential Concerns:** Anticipate and prepare answers for questions or objections from stakeholders.
    *   **Highlight Value Improvement:** Quantify the value added by the proposed change.
    *   **Obtain Approval and Authorization.**
*   **Deliverables:** Approved VE proposal and authorization for implementation.
*   **Emphasis:** Clarity, conciseness, and persuasiveness. Focusing on the business case.

*   **Reference Insight:** Khan's work on the Indian Financial System underscores the importance of presenting clear financial justifications and returns on investment for any proposed project or change, which is critical for gaining management approval. (Indian Financial System, 2011)

### Phase 7: Implementation Phase (Action)

*   **Purpose:** To execute the approved VE proposal and bring the new design or process into reality.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Execute the Plan:** Implement the design changes, adopt new processes, or procure new materials.
    *   **Manage the Project:** Oversee the implementation process, ensuring it stays on schedule and within budget.
    *   **Train Personnel:** If new processes or technologies are introduced.
    *   **Monitor Progress and Resolve Issues.**
*   **Deliverables:** The implemented VE solution.
*   **Emphasis:** Efficient execution and project management.

### Phase 8: Verification Phase (Follow-up)

*   **Purpose:** To confirm that the implemented VE solution achieves the intended benefits and to capture lessons learned.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Measure Actual Results:** Compare the actual performance, costs, and savings against the projected outcomes.
    *   **Verify Functionality and Quality.**
    *   **Document Savings and Improvements:** Quantify the achieved benefits.
    *   **Conduct a Post-Implementation Review:** Analyze the success of the VE project, identify what worked well and what could be improved for future VE efforts.
    *   **Standardize the Change:** If successful, incorporate the new design or process into standard operating procedures.
*   **Deliverables:** A report on actual results, documented savings, and lessons learned.
*   **Emphasis:** Measurement, validation, and continuous improvement.

*   **Reference Insight:** Newman and Lavelle stress the importance of the verification phase as a feedback loop, ensuring that VE projects deliver tangible results and contribute to ongoing organizational learning and optimization. (Engineering Economics and Analysis, 2002)

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Teamwork is Crucial:** VE is most effective when performed by a multidisciplinary team with diverse skills and perspectives.
*   **Focus on Functions:** Always prioritize the functions that need to be performed.
*   **Cost is Everything:** Every function and every alternative should be analyzed in terms of its cost.
*   **Creativity is Key:** Encourage innovative thinking and the generation of a wide range of ideas.
*   **Challenge the Status Quo:** VE is about questioning existing methods and seeking better ways to achieve objectives.
*   **Value = Function / Cost:** The ultimate goal is to increase value by improving function or reducing cost (or both).
*   **It's a Process, Not an Event:** VE is a systematic methodology that requires discipline and adherence to its phases.

### Practice Questions:

**Q1. What is the primary objective of the Functional Analysis phase in the Value Engineering procedure?**
    a) To brainstorm new ideas.
    b) To gather all relevant data about the product.
    c) To identify and analyze the functions performed by the product and their associated costs.
    d) To develop detailed implementation plans.

**Q2. The Creative Phase of VE is characterized by:**
    a) Rigorous cost-benefit analysis.
    b) Detailed engineering design.
    c) The generation of a wide range of potential solutions.
    d) Measuring the actual performance of implemented solutions.

**Q3. Which of the following is NOT a typical activity in the Information Phase of VE?**
    a) Gathering design specifications.
    b) Estimating the cost of alternative materials.
    c) Reviewing customer feedback.
    d) Understanding manufacturing processes.

**Q4. In the context of Value Engineering, "Value" is defined as:**
    a) Cost / Function
    b) Function / Cost
    c) Performance / Cost
    d) Quality / Reliability

**Q5. The Verification Phase of VE primarily focuses on:**
    a) Obtaining management approval.
    b) Presenting the VE proposal.
    c) Implementing the approved changes.
    d) Measuring and documenting the actual benefits achieved.

---

### Answers to Practice Questions:

**A1. c)** The primary objective of the Functional Analysis phase is to identify and analyze the functions performed by the product and their associated costs.

**A2. c)** The Creative Phase of VE is characterized by the generation of a wide range of potential solutions through brainstorming and other creative techniques.

**A3. b)** Estimating the cost of alternative materials is typically part of the Evaluation or Development phases, not the initial Information Phase which focuses on gathering data about the *existing* product.

**A4. b)** In the context of Value Engineering, "Value" is defined as Function / Cost.

**A5. d)** The Verification Phase of VE primarily focuses on measuring and documenting the actual benefits achieved from the implemented changes.
