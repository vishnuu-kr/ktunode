# Machine Tools and Metrology - Module 4: Angular Measurements

## Topic: Comparators - Classification, Dial Indicators, Electrical and Electronic Comparators, Pneumatic Comparators

### 1. Introduction to Comparators

**Definition:** A comparator is a type of mechanical or electrical measuring instrument used to compare the dimensions of a workpiece with a known standard or gauge. They are primarily used for the **detection of small deviations** from a nominal size, rather than for direct measurement of absolute size.

**Purpose:**
*   To ensure interchangeability of parts in mass production.
*   To facilitate quick and accurate inspection of components on the shop floor.
*   To check for variations in size, shape, and surface finish.
*   To detect deviations from flatness, parallelism, perpendicularity, and concentricity.

**Key Concept:** Comparators are **secondary measuring instruments**. They rely on a calibrated standard (like a gauge block or a master workpiece) to establish the reference dimension.

### 2. Classification of Comparators

Comparators can be broadly classified based on their operating principle.

#### 2.1 Mechanical Comparators

These comparators use mechanical linkages (levers, gears, racks, and pinions) to magnify small linear displacements.

*   **Principle:** Amplification of the small deviation of the workpiece from the standard by a system of mechanical levers.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Simple in construction and operation.
    *   Relatively inexpensive.
    *   No external power source required (except for some types).
    *   Robust and suitable for workshop environments.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Limited magnification range.
    *   Susceptible to wear and tear of moving parts, leading to reduced accuracy over time.
    *   Inertia of moving parts can affect speed of operation.
    *   Backlash in mechanical linkages can affect accuracy.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Dial Indicators:** (Discussed in detail below)
    *   **Sigma Comparator:** Utilizes a system of levers and gears to provide high magnification.
    *   **C.E. Johansson Comparator:** Another mechanical comparator employing a lever system.

**Textbook References:**
*   *Elements of Workshop Technology Vol-II Machine Tools* by S K Hajra Choudhury Nirjhar Roy.
*   *Engineering Metrology and Measurements* by N.V. Raghavendra, l. Krishnamurthy.
*   *Metrology for Engineers* by Galyer J.F.W., Schotbolt C.R.

#### 2.2 Optical Comparators

These comparators use optical means (mirrors, prisms, light beams) for magnification and indication.

*   **Principle:** Amplification of the deviation through optical magnification and projection.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   High magnification is possible.
    *   No moving mechanical parts in contact with the workpiece, reducing wear.
    *   Can provide a visual indication on a screen.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires a light source.
    *   More complex in construction.
    *   Can be sensitive to vibrations.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Talysurf (Surface Roughness Comparator):** While primarily for surface finish, some variations can indicate dimensional deviations.
    *   **Optical Projectors (Profile Projectors):** Used for inspecting profiles and dimensions of small parts, can be used comparatively.

**Textbook References:**
*   *Engineering Metrology and Measurements* by N.V. Raghavendra, l. Krishnamurthy.
*   *Manufacturing Science* by Amitabha Ghosh Asok Kumar Mallik.

#### 2.3 Electrical and Electronic Comparators

These comparators use electrical principles (resistance, capacitance, inductance) or electronic circuits for magnification and indication.

*   **Principle:** The deviation of the workpiece causes a change in an electrical parameter (voltage, current, frequency), which is then amplified and displayed.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Very high magnification possible.
    *   Can be used for remote indication.
    *   Less sensitive to vibrations.
    *   Can be integrated with automated inspection systems.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires a power supply.
    *   More expensive.
    *   Can be sensitive to environmental factors like temperature and humidity.
    *   Requires skilled personnel for operation and maintenance.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Eddy Current Comparators:** Utilize changes in eddy currents induced in a metallic workpiece.
    *   **Capacitance Comparators:** Measure changes in capacitance between a probe and the workpiece.
    *   **Inductance Comparators:** Measure changes in inductance of a coil due to the proximity of the workpiece.
    *   **Electronic Comparators (using LVDTs, etc.):** (Discussed below)

#### 2.4 Pneumatic Comparators

These comparators utilize the principle of air flow and air pressure to measure and indicate deviations.

*   **Principle:** Based on Bernoulli's principle or air gauging principles where the back pressure or airflow through a nozzle is varied by the proximity of the workpiece.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Non-contact measurement, hence no wear on the probe or workpiece.
    *   High sensitivity and magnification.
    *   Can measure irregularly shaped surfaces and recessed features.
    *   Suitable for measuring hot or delicate parts.
    *   Can measure through coolant or oil.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires a clean and regulated supply of compressed air.
    *   Can be affected by variations in air pressure.
    *   Generally used for go/no-go gauging or detecting small variations.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Flow or Velocity Type Comparators:** Measure the velocity of air escaping from a jet.
    *   **Pressure or Back Pressure Type Comparators:** Measure the air pressure in a chamber when the jet is obstructed by the workpiece.
    *   **Solex Comparators:** A well-known example of pneumatic gauging.

### 3. Dial Indicators

**Description:** A dial indicator is a widely used mechanical comparator that provides a visual indication of small linear displacements on a graduated dial.

**Construction:**
*   **Stylus/Plunger:** A movable rod that contacts the workpiece.
*   **Rack and Pinion Mechanism:** The plunger is attached to a rack, which meshes with a pinion.
*   **Gear Train:** The pinion drives a series of gears to amplify the motion.
*   **Pointer:** The amplified motion rotates a pointer on a graduated dial.
*   **Dial:** Marked with divisions representing small increments of movement (e.g., 0.01 mm, 0.001 inch).
*   **Bezel:** Rotatable bezel for setting the zero point.
*   **Mounting Lug:** For attaching the indicator to a stand or fixture.

**Working Principle:**
1.  The stylus is brought into contact with the workpiece.
2.  When the workpiece dimension changes (deviates from the standard), the stylus moves in or out.
3.  This linear movement is converted into rotational movement by the rack and pinion mechanism.
4.  The gear train amplifies this rotation, causing the pointer to move across the dial.
5.  The scale on the dial indicates the magnitude and direction of the deviation from the set zero point.

**Types of Dial Indicators:**
*   **Standard Dial Indicators:** General-purpose indicators.
*   **Test Indicators (Dial Test Indicators):** Have a smaller contact point and a swiveling pointer, useful for measuring in confined spaces or at angles.
*   **Dovetail Mount Indicators:** Designed for mounting on machine tool slides.
*   **Back Plunger Indicators:** The plunger moves parallel to the dial face.

**Applications:**
*   Checking for run-out and concentricity of rotating parts.
*   Measuring flatness and parallelism of surfaces.
*   Setting up workpieces on machine tools.
*   Checking the alignment of machine parts.
*   Measuring the depth of slots or recesses (with attachments).

**Important Points to Remember:**
*   **Range:** The total distance the plunger can travel.
*   **Graduation:** The smallest division on the dial (e.g., 0.01mm).
*   **Resolution:** The smallest change in dimension that can be detected.
*   **Accuracy:** How close the indicated value is to the true deviation.
*   **Setting the Zero:** Requires a master gauge or a known reference.

**Textbook References:**
*   *Elements of Workshop Technology Vol-II Machine Tools* by S K Hajra Choudhury Nirjhar Roy.
*   *Engineering Metrology and Measurements* by N.V. Raghavendra, l. Krishnamurthy.
*   *Metrology for Engineers* by Galyer J.F.W., Schotbolt C.R.
*   *Practical Engineering Metrology* by Sharp K.W.B.

**Example:** To check the concentricity of a workpiece mounted on a lathe, a dial indicator is mounted on the tool post. The stylus is placed on the face of the workpiece, and as the workpiece rotates, the dial indicator shows any deviation from perfect concentricity.

### 4. Electrical and Electronic Comparators

These comparators use electrical or electronic principles to amplify and display dimensional deviations.

#### 4.1 Electrical Comparators (Electromechanical)

These often involve a stylus that alters an electrical circuit's parameters.

*   **Principle:** Typically based on the variation of resistance, capacitance, or inductance of an electrical circuit as the stylus position changes relative to the workpiece.
*   **Eddy Current Comparator:**
    *   **Principle:** An alternating magnetic field from a probe induces eddy currents in the conductive workpiece. The magnitude of eddy currents depends on the distance between the probe and the workpiece. This change in eddy current affects the impedance of the probe coil, which is measured and displayed.
    *   **Applications:** Measuring dimensions of conductive materials, especially at high speeds or in difficult environments.
*   **Capacitance Comparator:**
    *   **Principle:** The comparator utilizes a capacitor where one plate is a fixed probe and the other is the workpiece surface. The distance between them affects the capacitance. Changes in capacitance are measured and amplified.
    *   **Applications:** Measuring non-conductive materials, or where very high sensitivity is required.

**Textbook References:**
*   *Engineering Metrology and Measurements* by N.V. Raghavendra, l. Krishnamurthy.
*   *Manufacturing Science* by Amitabha Ghosh Asok Kumar Mallik.

#### 4.2 Electronic Comparators

These are more sophisticated and often use transducers to convert mechanical displacement into electrical signals that are then processed by electronic circuits.

*   **Principle:** A transducer (e.g., LVDT, strain gauge) converts the mechanical movement of the stylus into an electrical signal. This signal is then amplified, processed, and displayed on a meter, digital display, or oscilloscope.
*   **Linear Variable Differential Transformer (LVDT) Comparator:**
    *   **Working:** An LVDT consists of a primary coil and two secondary coils. The stylus is connected to a movable core. When the core moves, it changes the magnetic coupling between the primary and secondary coils, resulting in a change in the output voltage proportional to the displacement.
    *   **Advantages:** High accuracy, linearity, no friction, robust.
    *   **Applications:** Precision measurement of dimensions, surface finish checks, profile measurement.
*   **Strain Gauge Comparator:**
    *   **Working:** The stylus is attached to a beam or diaphragm that deforms under pressure. Strain gauges bonded to this deformable element change their resistance proportionally to the strain, which is then converted into an electrical signal.
    *   **Applications:** Force measurement, pressure measurement, and dimensional gauging.
*   **Digital Electronic Comparators:** Modern electronic comparators often provide direct digital readouts, making them easy to use and reducing reading errors.

**Advantages of Electronic Comparators:**
*   Very high magnification and sensitivity.
*   Precise and stable readings.
*   Can be used for remote indication.
*   Can be interfaced with computers for data logging and analysis.
*   Less affected by wear and tear.

**Disadvantages of Electronic Comparators:**
*   Require a power supply.
*   More complex and expensive than mechanical comparators.
*   Can be sensitive to environmental factors (temperature, humidity, electromagnetic interference).

**Textbook References:**

### 5. Pneumatic Comparators

These comparators use compressed air as the measuring medium.

*   **Principle:** Based on the variation in air pressure or airflow as the gap between a nozzle and the workpiece changes.
*   **Working:**
    1.  A constant supply of filtered and regulated compressed air is supplied.
    2.  The air flows through a nozzle or a set of nozzles.
    3.  When the workpiece is brought near the nozzle, it obstructs the airflow.
    4.  This obstruction causes a change in either the back pressure in the line or the velocity of the escaping air.
    5.  This change is then amplified and displayed on a gauge (e.g., a manometer or a pressure gauge calibrated in terms of dimension).
*   **Types of Pneumatic Comparators:**
    *   **Flow or Velocity Type:** The velocity of air escaping from the nozzle is measured. As the workpiece gets closer, the velocity decreases.
    *   **Pressure or Back Pressure Type:** The pressure in the chamber is measured. As the workpiece gets closer, the back pressure increases.
*   **Pneumatic Gauging Systems:** Often designed as "go/no-go" gauges or for checking tolerances. They are very effective for inspecting features like diameter, ovality, taper, and concentricity.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   **Non-contact:** Eliminates wear on the gauge and workpiece.
    *   **High Sensitivity and Magnification:** Air jets can be sensitive to very small changes in gap.
    *   **Versatility:** Can measure internal and external features, complex shapes, and recessed areas.
    *   **Dust and Coolant Resistance:** Airflow can help to clear debris from the measuring area.
    *   **Quick Response:** Generally fast in operation.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Requires a clean and regulated compressed air supply.
    *   Sensitive to changes in air pressure and temperature.
    *   Limited to measuring dimensions that can be accessed by air jets.
    *   Typically used for checking small deviations or for go/no-go gauging.

**Example:** A pneumatic bore gauge uses air jets directed radially inwards. As the gauge is inserted into a hole, the airflow is affected by the hole's diameter. The resulting change in back pressure is displayed on a gauge, indicating if the hole is within tolerance.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   Comparators detect **deviations** from a standard, not absolute measurements.
*   **Magnification** is a key characteristic of comparators, allowing detection of very small errors.
*   The choice of comparator depends on factors like **accuracy required, speed of operation, cost, and the environment**.
*   **Dial indicators** are versatile and widely used for many workshop applications.
*   **Electronic comparators** offer the highest magnification and precision but are more expensive and complex.
*   **Pneumatic comparators** are ideal for non-contact measurement and situations where debris is present.
*   Proper **setting and calibration** are crucial for accurate comparative measurements.
*   **Wear and backlash** are important considerations for mechanical comparators.
*   **Cleanliness of air supply** is critical for pneumatic comparators.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Define a comparator and explain its primary function in metrology. (CO4 - K1)

**Answer:** A comparator is a measuring instrument used to compare the dimensions of a workpiece with a known standard or gauge. Its primary function is to detect and indicate small deviations from a nominal size or a specified tolerance. It does not measure the absolute size of the workpiece.

**Question 2:** Classify comparators based on their operating principles. Briefly describe one type from each class. (CO4 - K1)

**Answer:** Comparators can be classified as:
1.  **Mechanical Comparators:** Use mechanical linkages (levers, gears) for magnification.
    *   *Example: Dial Indicator* - Uses a rack and pinion system to amplify the plunger's movement and display it on a dial.
2.  **Optical Comparators:** Use optical means (mirrors, prisms) for magnification.
    *   *Example: Optical Projector* - Magnifies and projects the profile of a workpiece onto a screen for comparison with a standard profile.
3.  **Electrical/Electronic Comparators:** Utilize electrical or electronic principles.
    *   *Example: LVDT Comparator* - An LVDT transducer converts the stylus's linear motion into a proportional electrical signal, which is amplified and displayed.
4.  **Pneumatic Comparators:** Use compressed air principles.
    *   *Example: Pneumatic Gauging System* - Measures variations in air pressure or flow caused by changes in the gap between a nozzle and the workpiece.

**Question 3:** Explain the working principle of a dial indicator and list any three of its common applications. (CO4 - K1)

**Answer:** A dial indicator works on the principle of converting the linear movement of a stylus into magnified rotational movement of a pointer on a graduated dial using a rack and pinion and gear train mechanism.
**Applications:**
1.  Checking for run-out of rotating shafts.
2.  Measuring flatness of surfaces.
3.  Setting up workpieces on machine tools (e.g., aligning a workpiece in a vise).

**Question 4:** What are the main advantages of using electronic comparators over mechanical comparators? (CO4 - K1)

**Answer:**
*   **Higher Magnification and Sensitivity:** Electronic comparators can achieve much higher levels of magnification and are more sensitive to smaller deviations.
*   **Greater Accuracy and Stability:** Readings are generally more accurate and stable, less prone to drift.
*   **Remote Indication:** The measurement can be displayed remotely from the sensing element.
*   **Interfacing Capability:** Can be easily integrated with digital displays, data loggers, and automated systems.
*   **Less Friction:** No mechanical contact parts that can cause friction and wear, leading to longer life and consistent performance.

**Question 5:** Describe how a pneumatic comparator works and mention one significant advantage it offers. (CO4 - K1)

**Answer:** A pneumatic comparator works by measuring the change in air pressure or airflow as the gap between a nozzle and the workpiece changes. Compressed air is directed through a nozzle, and the workpiece's position alters this airflow. This change is amplified and indicated on a gauge calibrated in terms of dimensional deviation.
**Significant Advantage:** **Non-contact measurement**, which prevents wear on both the measuring instrument and the workpiece.

**Question 6:** You need to inspect the concentricity of a large number of cylindrical shafts for variations in diameter up to 0.05 mm. Which type of comparator would be most suitable and why? (CO4 - K1, CO1 - K2)

**Answer:** A **dial indicator** would be a suitable choice.
**Reasoning:**
*   **Sufficient Magnification:** Dial indicators typically have graduations of 0.01 mm or 0.001 mm, which is sufficient to detect variations up to 0.05 mm.
*   **Ease of Use:** They are relatively simple to set up and operate on the shop floor.
*   **Cost-Effective:** Compared to electronic or pneumatic comparators, dial indicators are more economical for general-purpose inspection.
*   **Portability:** They can be easily mounted on stands or magnetic bases for versatile application.

**Question 7:** A comparator is used for:
(a) Measuring the absolute length of a component.
(b) Comparing the dimensions of a component with a standard.
(c) Measuring the surface roughness of a component.
(d) Measuring the angle of a component.

(CO4 - K1)

**Answer:** (b) Comparing the dimensions of a component with a standard.
