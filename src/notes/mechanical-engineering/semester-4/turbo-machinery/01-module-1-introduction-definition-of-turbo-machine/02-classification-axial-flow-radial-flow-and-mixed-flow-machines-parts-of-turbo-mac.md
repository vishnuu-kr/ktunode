## TURBO MACHINERY: Module 1 - Introduction: Definition and Classification of Turbomachines

---

### 1.1 Introduction to Turbomachines

**Definition:**

A turbomachine is a mechanical device that transfers energy between a rotor and a fluid. This energy transfer is achieved by the flow of the fluid through the rotating components of the machine. Turbomachines are broadly classified into two categories based on their function:

*   **Turbines:** These machines extract energy from a high-energy fluid and convert it into mechanical energy (usually rotational). Examples include steam turbines, gas turbines, and hydraulic turbines.
*   **Pumps/Compressors/Fans:** These machines impart energy to a fluid, increasing its pressure, velocity, or both. Examples include centrifugal pumps, axial flow compressors, and ventilating fans.

**Key Concept:** The fundamental principle behind turbomachines is the **transfer of angular momentum** from or to the fluid by the action of rotating vanes or blades.

**Reference:** Dixon, S.I. (1999). *Fluid Mechanics and Thermodynamics of Turbomachinery*. Pergamom Press. (Chapter 1 - Introduction)
**Reference:** Venkanna, B.K. (2009). *Fundamentals of Turbo Machinery*. PHI Learning Pvt. Ltd. (Chapter 1 - Introduction to Turbomachinery)

### 1.2 Classification of Turbomachines

Turbomachines can be classified based on several criteria, but the most fundamental classification is based on the **direction of fluid flow relative to the axis of rotation**.

#### 1.2.1 Axial Flow Machines

*   **Definition:** In axial flow machines, the fluid flows predominantly parallel to the axis of rotation. The fluid enters and leaves the impeller or rotor in an axial direction.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   High flow rates.
    *   Relatively low head (pressure rise) or power output per stage.
    *   Blades are typically airfoil shaped and twisted.
    *   Impellers have a disc-like structure with blades extending radially outwards.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Axial Flow Turbines:** Kaplan turbines (hydraulic), steam turbines, gas turbines.
    *   **Axial Flow Pumps/Compressors/Fans:** Propeller fans, axial flow compressors in jet engines, axial flow pumps for high-volume, low-head applications.
*   **Diagrammatic Representation:** Imagine a propeller or a jet engine compressor. The fluid moves along the central shaft.
*   **Reference:** Yahya, S.H. (1996). *Turbines, Compressors and Fans*. Tata Mc Graw Hill. (Chapter on Axial Flow Machines)
*   **Reference:** Gambini, M., & Vellini, M. (2021). *Turbomachinery – Fundamentals, Selection and Preliminary Design*. Springer. (Section on Axial Flow Machines)

#### 1.2.2 Radial Flow Machines (Centrifugal Machines)

*   **Definition:** In radial flow machines, the fluid flows predominantly in a direction perpendicular to the axis of rotation. The fluid enters axially and is discharged radially outwards (or enters radially and is discharged axially in the case of a radial turbine).
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Lower flow rates compared to axial flow machines for the same size.
    *   High head (pressure rise) or power output per stage.
    *   Impellers have vanes that extend radially from a central hub.
    *   Volute or diffuser is typically used to convert kinetic energy into pressure energy.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Radial Flow Turbines:** Francis turbines (hydraulic), Pelton turbines (impulse turbines, though flow is more tangential and radial in action).
    *   **Radial Flow Pumps/Compressors:** Centrifugal pumps (water pumps, oil pumps), centrifugal compressors (turbochargers, industrial compressors).
*   **Diagrammatic Representation:** Think of a centrifugal pump. The fluid is thrown outwards from the center by the rotating impeller.
*   **Reference:** Dixon, S.I. (1999). *Fluid Mechanics and Thermodynamics of Turbomachinery*. Pergamom Press. (Chapter on Centrifugal Machines)
*   **Reference:** Venkanna, B.K. (2009). *Fundamentals of Turbo Machinery*. PHI Learning Pvt. Ltd. (Chapter on Centrifugal Pumps and Compressors)

#### 1.2.3 Mixed Flow Machines

*   **Definition:** Mixed flow machines exhibit characteristics of both axial and radial flow machines. The fluid enters and leaves the impeller with components of velocity in both axial and radial directions. The flow path is often described as helical or spiral.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Intermediate flow rates and head compared to purely axial and radial machines.
    *   Impeller vanes are curved to guide the fluid from an axial entry to a radial or semi-radial exit.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Mixed Flow Turbines:** Francis turbines can sometimes have mixed flow characteristics, especially those designed for higher specific speeds.
    *   **Mixed Flow Pumps/Compressors:** Mixed flow pumps are used in applications requiring higher head than axial pumps but higher flow than centrifugal pumps (e.g., drainage pumps, some cooling water pumps). Mixed flow compressors are less common but can be found in certain turbocharger designs.
*   **Diagrammatic Representation:** Imagine a pump impeller where the fluid is pushed outwards but also upwards along the shaft.
*   **Reference:** Yahya, S.H. (1996). *Turbines, Compressors and Fans*. Tata Mc Graw Hill. (Chapter on Mixed Flow Machines)
*   **Reference:** Dick, E. (2022). *Fundamentals of Turbomachines*. Springer. (Discussion on flow path in turbomachines)

### 1.3 Parts of a Turbomachine

While specific components vary based on the type and application, common parts include:

*   **Rotor/Impeller/Runner:** The rotating component that contains the blades or vanes. It is the primary energy transfer element.
    *   **Axial Flow:** Impeller consists of a hub, blades, and sometimes a shroud.
    *   **Radial Flow:** Impeller typically has a closed or open design with radial vanes.
    *   **Mixed Flow:** Impeller has curved vanes that blend axial and radial flow paths.
*   **Stator/Stationary Components:** These parts guide the fluid before or after it passes through the rotor, or control the flow.
    *   **Inlet Casing/Volute/Spiral Casing:** Collects the fluid and guides it to the impeller. In radial flow machines, the volute shape converts kinetic energy to pressure energy.
    *   **Guide Vanes/Nozzles:** Stationary vanes that direct the incoming fluid onto the rotor at the optimal angle, especially crucial in turbines and some pumps/compressors.
    *   **Diffuser:** A stationary passage that increases in cross-sectional area, designed to slow down the fluid and convert its kinetic energy into pressure energy. Commonly used in centrifugal compressors and pumps.
    *   **Return Channels/Diffuser Vanes:** In some designs, these are used to recover energy from the fluid exiting the rotor.
    *   **Casing/Housing:** Encloses the rotating components and contains the fluid.
*   **Shaft:** Transmits the mechanical power to or from the rotor.
*   **Bearings:** Support the shaft and allow smooth rotation.
*   **Seals:** Prevent leakage of the fluid from the machine.

**Example (Centrifugal Pump):**
*   **Impeller:** The rotating part with vanes.
*   **Casing:** The outer shell, often designed as a volute.
*   **Shaft:** Connects the impeller to the motor.
*   **Mechanical Seal:** Prevents water leakage around the shaft.

**Reference:** Venkanna, B.K. (2009). *Fundamentals of Turbo Machinery*. PHI Learning Pvt. Ltd. (Chapters on different types of turbomachines detail their components)
**Reference:** Dixon, S.I. (1999). *Fluid Mechanics and Thermodynamics of Turbomachinery*. Pergamom Press. (Illustrations of various turbomachines and their parts)

### 1.4 Comparison with Positive Displacement Machines

Turbomachines and positive displacement (PD) machines are both fluid handling devices, but they operate on fundamentally different principles.

| Feature             | Turbomachinery                                   | Positive Displacement (PD) Machines                       |
| :------------------ | :----------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Working Principle** | Kinetic energy transfer via rotating impeller/blades to/from fluid. Continuous flow. | Traps a fixed volume of fluid and moves it from inlet to outlet. Intermittent or continuous flow depending on design. |
| **Flow Rate**       | Variable, dependent on system resistance (head). | Relatively constant for a given speed, less sensitive to system resistance. |
| **Pressure Rise**   | Head developed depends on speed and flow rate. Can be limited by system. | Can develop very high pressures, limited by structural strength and power source. |
| **Efficiency**      | Generally higher efficiency over a wider operating range. Efficiency drops significantly at off-design conditions. | High efficiency, especially at higher pressures and near design points. Can maintain good efficiency over a wider range of pressures. |
| **Flow Characteristics** | Smooth, continuous flow. | Pulsating flow (especially reciprocating types), can be smoother with rotary types. |
| **Speed Range**     | Can operate over a wide speed range, but efficiency is best near design speed. | Typically operate at lower speeds compared to turbomachines. High speeds can cause excessive wear. |
| **Priming**         | Most centrifugal pumps are not self-priming (require fluid in casing to start). Axial pumps can be self-priming. | Generally self-priming. |
| **Size/Weight**     | More compact and lighter for a given flow rate. | Can be bulkier and heavier for a given flow rate. |
| **Applications**    | High flow rates, moderate pressure. Water supply, HVAC, jet engines, power generation. | High pressure, low flow. Hydraulic systems, metering, viscous fluid transfer. |
| **Examples**        | Centrifugal pumps, axial fans, jet engines, steam turbines. | Gear pumps, piston pumps, diaphragm pumps, screw pumps, vane pumps. |

**Key Concept:** The performance of turbomachines is characterized by **performance curves** (e.g., Head-Flow, Efficiency-Flow) which show the relationship between flow rate, head, efficiency, and power. PD machines have a more linear relationship between speed and flow rate, with pressure being the primary dependent variable.

**Reference:** Venkanna, B.K. (2009). *Fundamentals of Turbo Machinery*. PHI Learning Pvt. Ltd. (Chapter comparing PD and turbomachines)
**Reference:** Dixon, S.I. (1999). *Fluid Mechanics and Thermodynamics of Turbomachinery*. Pergamom Press. (Implicitly through discussions on performance characteristics)

### 1.6 Important Points to Remember

*   **Energy Transfer:** Turbomachines transfer energy between a rotor and a fluid.
*   **Flow Direction:** The primary classification (axial, radial, mixed) is based on the fluid's path relative to the axis of rotation.
*   **Axial Flow:** High flow rate, low head/power per stage.
*   **Radial Flow:** Low flow rate, high head/power per stage.
*   **Mixed Flow:** Intermediate characteristics.
*   **PD vs. Turbo:** PD machines displace fixed volumes, leading to constant flow and pressure capabilities independent of system resistance. Turbomachines rely on fluid momentum and pressure changes, with flow and head being interdependent and influenced by system resistance.
*   **Efficiency:** Turbomachines generally offer higher efficiency over a broader operating range than PD machines, but their performance is more sensitive to operating conditions.

### 1.8 Answers to Practice Questions

**Short Answer Questions:**

1.  A turbomachine is a mechanical device that transfers energy between a rotor and a fluid through the flow of the fluid over rotating blades or vanes.
2.  **Power Consuming:** Pumps, Compressors, Fans (e.g., Centrifugal Pump). **Power Generating:** Turbines (e.g., Steam Turbine).
3.  The fluid flows predominantly parallel to the axis of rotation.
4.  The fluid flows predominantly in a direction perpendicular (radial) to the axis of rotation.
5.  Centrifugal pumps typically produce a higher head (pressure rise) but lower flow rate compared to axial flow pumps for similar sizes. Axial flow pumps provide high flow rates at lower heads.
6.  Turbomachines transfer energy via the continuous motion of fluid through rotating components, changing its kinetic and potential energy. Positive displacement machines trap and move fixed volumes of fluid.
7.  Rotor/Impeller, Casing, Shaft, Blades/Vanes, Bearings.

**Conceptual Questions:**

8.  For a large volume of water and moderate height, a **mixed flow pump** would likely be most suitable. It offers a better compromise between the high flow rates of axial pumps and the higher head capabilities of centrifugal pumps for this application. If the height is very moderate and the volume is extremely large, an axial flow pump might also be considered.
9.  For a high-head, low-flow water source, a **radial flow turbine** (like a Pelton turbine for very high heads or a Francis turbine for moderately high heads) would be most appropriate. These turbines are designed to extract significant energy from a high-pressure fluid, converting the potential energy into mechanical work efficiently, even at lower flow rates.
10. A centrifugal pump's head and flow rate are dictated by the interplay of the fluid's kinetic energy imparted by the impeller and the system's resistance. Partially closing the discharge valve increases system resistance (head), causing the operating point to shift along the pump's characteristic curve, typically resulting in a lower flow rate and higher head. A gear pump, however, traps a fixed volume of fluid with each rotation. As long as the pump and fluid are incompressible and the motor can overcome the increased resistance, the flow rate will remain largely constant regardless of the discharge pressure. The pressure will simply build up to a level that the motor can handle or the system's relief mechanisms engage.

**Problem Solving (Conceptual):**

11.
*   **Higher Head:** The **centrifugal pump (Pump A)** is likely to deliver a higher head. The radial discharge action of a centrifugal impeller is more effective at increasing the fluid's pressure (head) by converting rotational kinetic energy into pressure energy, especially when discharged through a diffuser or volute.
*   **Higher Flow Rate:** The **axial flow pump (Pump B)** is likely to deliver a higher flow rate. The axial movement of fluid through the impeller in an axial flow pump is more conducive to moving large volumes of fluid with less resistance to flow, even though the head developed per stage is lower.
