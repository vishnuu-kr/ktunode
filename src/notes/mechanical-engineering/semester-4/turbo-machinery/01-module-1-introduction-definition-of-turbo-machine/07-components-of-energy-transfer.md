# TURBO MACHINERY: Module 1: Introduction: Definition of Turbo Machine

## Topic: Components of Energy Transfer

### 1. Introduction to Turbo Machines and Energy Transfer

**Definition of Turbo Machine:** A turbo machine is a mechanical device that transfers energy between a rotor and a fluid, or vice-versa. This energy transfer is accomplished by the relative motion between the fluid and the rotating part (rotor).

**Core Concept:** The fundamental purpose of a turbo machine is to either add energy to a fluid (e.g., pumps, fans, compressors) or extract energy from a fluid (e.g., turbines).

**Knowledge Level (K3 - Application):** Understanding the components of energy transfer is crucial for applying fundamental principles of fluid mechanics and thermodynamics to analyze the performance of these machines, as stated in CO1.

### 2. Key Components Involved in Energy Transfer

Turbo machines, despite their diverse applications, share common components responsible for the energy transfer process. These can be broadly categorized:

#### 2.1. The Rotor (Impeller/Runner/Blade Assembly)

*   **Definition:** The rotating element of the turbo machine. It is the primary component where the interaction with the fluid occurs, leading to energy transfer.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Energy Addition (e.g., Pump, Compressor):** The rotor imparts kinetic energy and momentum to the fluid, increasing its pressure and velocity. This is achieved through the force exerted by the rotating blades on the fluid.
    *   **Energy Extraction (e.g., Turbine):** The fluid, possessing kinetic and/or potential energy, impinges on the rotor blades, causing them to rotate and thereby extract energy from the fluid.
*   **Key Design Features:**
    *   **Blades:** Shaped to efficiently direct the fluid flow and maximize the transfer of momentum. The curvature and angle of the blades are critical.
    *   **Hub:** The central shaft or body to which the blades are attached.
    *   **Shroud (in some designs):** An outer casing that encloses the blades, often used to prevent leakage and improve efficiency.
*   **Textbook Reference:**
    *   **Dixon, S.I. (1999), *Fluid Mechanics and Thermodynamics of Turbomachinery***: Discusses the aerodynamic design of blades and their role in momentum transfer. Chapter 3 on "Axial Flow Turbomachines" and Chapter 4 on "Radial Flow Turbomachines" detail rotor construction and blade shapes.
    *   **Venkanna, B.K. (2009), *Fundamentals of Turbo Machinery***: Explains the basic principles of how blades interact with the fluid to transfer energy. Chapter 2 on "General Concepts" covers rotor types.
    *   **Yahya, S.H. (1996), *Turbines, Compressor and Fans***: Provides detailed descriptions and illustrations of rotors in various types of turbo machines. Chapters on "Centrifugal Compressors" and "Axial Flow Compressors" are relevant.

#### 2.2. The Stator (Guide Vanes/Diffuser/Volute)

*   **Definition:** The stationary element of the turbo machine that guides the fluid flow and prepares it for interaction with the rotor, or processes the fluid after it leaves the rotor.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Energy Addition Machines (Pumps, Compressors):**
        *   **Inlet Guide Vanes:** Direct the incoming fluid into the rotor at the optimal angle to minimize shock losses and maximize efficiency.
        *   **Diffuser:** Slows down the high-velocity fluid leaving the rotor, converting kinetic energy into pressure energy. This is crucial for increasing the overall pressure rise.
        *   **Volute (or Scroll Casing):** Collects the fluid from the impeller and directs it to the discharge pipe, often with a gradual increase in cross-sectional area to reduce velocity and pressure losses.
    *   **Energy Extraction Machines (Turbines):**
        *   **Nozzle/Guide Vanes:** Direct the high-energy fluid onto the rotor blades at the correct angle to exert the maximum torque. This conversion of pressure energy to kinetic energy (in impulse turbines) or controlled flow (in reaction turbines) is vital.
*   **Textbook Reference:**
    *   **Dixon, S.I. (1999)**: Chapter 5 on "Radial Flow Machines" details the role of diffusers and volutes in centrifugal pumps and compressors. Chapter 7 on "Steam and Gas Turbines" explains the function of nozzles and guide vanes.
    *   **Venkanna, B.K. (2009)**: Chapter 3 on "Performance Characteristics" discusses how stator components influence overall performance.
    *   **Yahya, S.H. (1996)**: Chapter 4 on "Centrifugal Pumps" and Chapter 5 on "Axial Flow Pumps" describe the casing and diffuser arrangements. Chapter 10 on "Turbine Governing" touches upon the role of guide vanes.
    *   **Gambini & Vellini (2021), *Turbomachinery – Fundamentals, Selection and Preliminary Design***: Offers insights into the preliminary design considerations for stators and their impact on flow characteristics.

#### 2.3. The Casing (Housing)

*   **Definition:** The outer shell that encloses the rotor and stator components.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Containment:** Prevents leakage of the working fluid.
    *   **Structural Support:** Provides mechanical integrity to the assembly.
    *   **Flow Management:** Directs the fluid path into and out of the machine, often incorporating inlet and outlet ports.
    *   **Containment of Pressure:** Withstands the operational pressure of the fluid.
*   **Textbook Reference:**
    *   **Dixon, S.I. (1999)**: While often implicitly described, casing design is vital for sealing and flow continuity.
    *   **Venkanna, B.K. (2009)**: Chapter 1 on "Introduction to Turbomachines" mentions the casing as an integral part of the machine structure.

#### 2.4. Shaft and Bearings

*   **Definition:** The rotating shaft that connects the rotor to an external power source (motor, engine) or transmits the generated power. Bearings support the shaft and allow it to rotate smoothly.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Power Transmission:** Enables the rotor to rotate at the required speed by connecting it to a prime mover or load.
    *   **Mechanical Support:** Bearings minimize friction and radial/axial loads on the shaft, ensuring stable rotation.
*   **Importance for Energy Transfer:** While not directly involved in fluid-to-rotor energy transfer, their efficiency (low friction) directly impacts the net energy delivered or consumed by the machine.
*   **Textbook Reference:**
    *   **Venkanna, B.K. (2009)**: Chapter 1 on "Introduction to Turbomachines" introduces these mechanical components.

### 3. Mechanisms of Energy Transfer

The energy transfer in turbo machines is a complex interplay of fluid mechanics principles:

*   **Momentum Transfer:** The primary mechanism. Rotor blades exert forces on the fluid (or are acted upon by the fluid) which change the fluid's momentum.
    *   **Force = Rate of change of momentum**
    *   **Work done by fluid on rotor (Turbine) or rotor on fluid (Pump/Compressor) = Force x Distance**
*   **Work-Energy Theorem:** The work done on or by the fluid directly translates into changes in kinetic energy, potential energy (pressure), and internal energy of the fluid.
*   **Euler's Turbine Equation:** A fundamental equation relating the torque and power transferred to the fluid by the rotor to the change in angular momentum of the fluid.
    *   **Power (P) = $\rho Q (u_2 v_{u2} - u_1 v_{u1})$**
        *   $\rho$: Fluid density
        *   $Q$: Volumetric flow rate
        *   $u$: Blade tangential velocity
        *   $v_u$: Tangential component of fluid velocity (whirl velocity)
        *   Subscripts 1 and 2 refer to inlet and outlet of the rotor, respectively.

**CO1 Alignment:** Euler's equation is a direct application of fluid mechanics and thermodynamics principles to calculate the energy transfer, enabling efficiency calculations.

### 4. Types of Energy Transfer in Turbo Machines

**4.1. Energy Addition (Power Consuming Machines):**

*   **Pumps:** Add energy to a liquid to increase its pressure and/or flow rate.
    *   *Example:* Water pump in a building, oil pump in an engine.
*   **Fans:** Add energy to air or gas at low pressure rise, primarily for circulation.
    *   *Example:* Ceiling fan, cooling fan in a computer.
*   **Compressors:** Add energy to a gas to significantly increase its pressure.
    *   *Example:* Air compressor for tools, turbocharger in an engine.

**4.2. Energy Extraction (Power Generating Machines):**

*   **Turbines:** Extract energy from a moving fluid (water, steam, gas) to produce mechanical work, which is then often converted to electrical energy.
    *   *Example:* Hydroelectric turbine (Pelton, Francis, Kaplan), Steam turbine in power plants, Gas turbine in aircraft engines.

**CO3 & CO4 Alignment:** Understanding the components of energy transfer is the first step in selecting the appropriate machine for a given application, whether it's generating power (CO3) or consuming it (CO4).

### 5. Visualizing Energy Transfer: Velocity Triangles

**Definition:** Velocity triangles are graphical representations of the velocities of the fluid relative to the rotor and the absolute velocities of the fluid at the inlet and outlet of the rotor blades. They are essential for analyzing the energy transfer process.

**Key Components of a Velocity Triangle:**

*   **Absolute Velocity ($V$):** Velocity of the fluid as observed by a stationary observer.
*   **Blade Velocity ($u$):** Tangential velocity of the rotor blade.
*   **Relative Velocity ($W$):** Velocity of the fluid relative to the moving blade.

**Relationship:** $V = W + u$ (vector addition)

**CO2 Alignment:** Analyzing velocity triangles is core to understanding how flow angles and velocities change across the rotor, directly impacting performance and energy transfer.

**Example: Velocity Triangles for an Axial Flow Compressor Inlet**

*   **At the inlet (1):**
    *   $V_{u1}$ (whirl velocity) is typically zero for an ideal axial flow compressor.
    *   $V_{f1}$ (meridional/axial velocity) is the primary flow component.
    *   $W_{u1}$ (relative whirl component) and $W_{f1}$ (relative meridional component) are determined by $V_{f1}$ and the blade angle.
    *   $u_1$ is the tangential velocity of the blade at the relevant radius.

**Example: Velocity Triangles for a Centrifugal Pump Outlet**

*   **At the outlet (2):**
    *   $u_2$ (tangential velocity) is generally high.
    *   $V_{f2}$ (radial velocity) is significant.
    *   $V_{u2}$ (whirl velocity) is crucial for torque calculation using Euler's equation.
    *   $W_{f2}$ (relative radial velocity) and $W_{u2}$ (relative whirl component) relate $V_2$ and $u_2$.

**Reference Book Insight:**
*   **Dick (2022), *Fundamentals of Turbomachines***: Provides comprehensive coverage of velocity triangles for various flow types, emphasizing their role in performance prediction.

### 6. Importance of Component Design for Energy Transfer Efficiency

*   **Blade Profile:** The shape of the rotor and stator blades is critical for controlling flow, minimizing losses (friction, separation, incidence losses), and maximizing the work done per unit mass of fluid.
*   **Clearances:** Small gaps between rotating and stationary parts (e.g., impeller tip to casing) can lead to leakage, reducing efficiency.
*   **Flow Angles:** Matching the fluid velocity vector to the blade angle at the inlet and outlet (using velocity triangles) is vital to prevent shock losses and ensure smooth energy transfer.
*   **Diffuser/Volute Design:** Efficiently converting kinetic energy to pressure energy in pumps/compressors or guiding flow efficiently in turbines significantly impacts overall machine efficiency.

### 7. Practice Questions

1.  **Identify and describe the primary function of the rotor in a turbo machine.**
    *   **Answer:** The rotor is the rotating component that directly interacts with the fluid. Its function is to transfer energy to or from the fluid by changing its momentum. In pumps/compressors, it adds energy; in turbines, it extracts energy.

2.  **What is the role of the stator components (e.g., guide vanes, diffuser) in a turbo machine? Provide an example for both an energy addition and an energy extraction machine.**
    *   **Answer:** Stator components guide the fluid flow.
        *   **Energy Addition (e.g., Centrifugal Compressor):** A diffuser slows down the fluid leaving the impeller, converting kinetic energy into pressure energy. Inlet guide vanes can pre-swirl the incoming air to improve efficiency.
        *   **Energy Extraction (e.g., Steam Turbine):** Nozzles or guide vanes direct the high-velocity steam onto the rotor blades at an optimal angle to produce torque.

3.  **State Euler's Turbine Equation and define each term.**
    *   **Answer:** Power (P) = $\rho Q (u_2 v_{u2} - u_1 v_{u1})$
        *   $P$: Power transferred
        *   $\rho$: Fluid density
        *   $Q$: Volumetric flow rate
        *   $u$: Blade tangential velocity
        *   $v_u$: Tangential component of fluid velocity (whirl velocity)
        *   Subscripts 1: Inlet to rotor, 2: Outlet from rotor

4.  **Explain why velocity triangles are important in the study of turbo machinery.**
    *   **Answer:** Velocity triangles are important because they graphically represent the fluid velocities and blade velocities at the inlet and outlet of the rotor. This allows for the calculation of work done, forces on blades, and analysis of flow losses, which are critical for understanding and predicting the performance and efficiency of the turbo machine.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Energy transfer is the core function** of all turbo machines.
*   The **rotor** is the component responsible for the direct interaction with the fluid for energy transfer.
*   The **stator** components guide the fluid and condition it for optimal interaction with the rotor, or process it after leaving the rotor.
*   **Euler's Turbine Equation** is a fundamental tool for quantifying the theoretical power transfer.
*   **Velocity triangles** are essential for analyzing the fluid and blade velocities and understanding the mechanisms of energy transfer.
*   The efficiency of energy transfer is heavily dependent on the **aerodynamic design of blades**, flow angles, and the proper functioning of all components (rotor, stator, casing, shaft).
*   Understanding these components and their roles is fundamental to achieving CO1, CO2, CO3, and CO4.
