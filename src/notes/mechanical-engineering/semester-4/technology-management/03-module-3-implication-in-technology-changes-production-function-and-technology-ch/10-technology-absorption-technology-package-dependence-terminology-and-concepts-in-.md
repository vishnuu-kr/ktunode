## TECHNOLOGY MANAGEMENT: MODULE 3 - IMPLICATIONS IN TECHNOLOGY CHANGES: PRODUCTION FUNCTION AND TECHNOLOGY CHANGE

### TOPIC: TECHNOLOGY ABSORPTION

This module focuses on the critical process of **technology absorption**, which is how an organization or country successfully integrates and utilizes new technologies to improve its production processes, products, and overall competitive advantage. We will delve into the nuances of technology packages, the concept of dependence, essential terminology, the Indian experience with technology import, effective management strategies for absorption, and relevant government initiatives.

---

### 1. Technology Absorption: Technology Package & Dependence

#### 1.1 What is Technology Absorption?

*   **Definition:** Technology absorption is the process by which a firm or country acquires, adapts, integrates, and effectively utilizes a new technology to achieve desired outcomes. It involves more than just acquiring the hardware; it encompasses the skills, knowledge, organizational structures, and processes needed to make the technology work and generate value. (Rastogi, 2009; Khurana, 2012)
*   **Key Aspects:**
    *   **Acquisition:** Obtaining the technology (e.g., through import, licensing, R&D).
    *   **Adaptation:** Modifying the technology to suit local conditions, resources, and needs.
    *   **Integration:** Embedding the technology into existing production systems, organizational routines, and culture.
    *   **Utilization:** Effectively operating and maintaining the technology to achieve its intended benefits.
    *   **Mastery:** Developing the internal capabilities to innovate and improve upon the absorbed technology.

#### 1.2 Technology Package

*   **Definition:** A technology package refers to a bundle of elements that constitute a complete technological solution. It typically includes:
    *   **Hardware:** Machinery, equipment, tools, and physical infrastructure.
    *   **Software:** Operating systems, application programs, and control systems.
    *   **Know-how/Tacit Knowledge:** Skills, expertise, experience, and uncodified knowledge held by individuals and teams.
    *   **Technical Documentation:** Blueprints, manuals, operating instructions, maintenance procedures.
    *   **Organizational Practices:** New work methods, management systems, quality control procedures, and training programs.
    *   **Ancillary Services:** Installation, commissioning, maintenance, spare parts, and training support. (Narayanan, 2002)

*   **Example:** Importing a new automated manufacturing line involves not just the machines (hardware), but also the specialized software to control them, the training required for operators and maintenance staff (know-how), the manuals for operation and repair (technical documentation), and potentially new production scheduling and quality assurance procedures (organizational practices).

#### 1.3 Technology Dependence

*   **Definition:** Technology dependence occurs when a firm or country relies heavily on external sources for crucial technologies, and lacks the indigenous capabilities to develop, adapt, or maintain them independently. This can lead to a lack of control over technology diffusion, increased costs, and vulnerability to external shocks. (Rastogi, 2009)
*   **Forms of Dependence:**
    *   **Import Dependence:** Relying on imported machinery, components, or finished products.
    *   **Licensing Dependence:** Dependence on foreign firms for patents, trademarks, and proprietary know-how.
    *   **R&D Dependence:** Lack of domestic R&D capabilities to generate new technologies or adapt existing ones.
    *   **Skills Dependence:** Shortage of skilled personnel to operate, maintain, and innovate with new technologies.
*   **Consequences of Dependence:**
    *   **Economic Vulnerability:** Susceptibility to trade barriers, price fluctuations, and geopolitical pressures.
    *   **Reduced Competitiveness:** Inability to rapidly adapt to market changes or develop unique product offerings.
    *   **Drain of Foreign Exchange:** Significant outflows for licensing fees, royalties, and imported technologies.
    *   **Limited Indigenous Innovation:** Discouragement of local R&D and entrepreneurship.

*   **Highlight:** While acquiring advanced technology is often necessary, fostering indigenous capabilities is crucial to move from dependence to interdependence and eventually, self-reliance.

### 2. Terminology and Concepts in Absorption

#### 2.1 Key Concepts

*   **Technology Transfer:** The process of transmitting technology from one entity to another, often across national borders or between organizations. This can be through various mechanisms like licensing, joint ventures, FDI, or turnkey projects. (Khurana, 2012)
*   **Technology Adaptation:** The process of modifying an acquired technology to suit the specific needs, resources, and environmental conditions of the recipient. This might involve changes in materials, design, scale, or operational procedures. (Narayanan, 2002)
*   **Technology Assimilation:** The internalization of a technology within an organization's existing systems and culture, leading to improved efficiency and effectiveness. It implies the technology becomes a natural part of the operational fabric.
*   **Technology Diffusion:** The spread of technology from its point of origin to other users and organizations. This can be through formal channels (training, documentation) or informal channels (learning by doing, imitation).
*   **Absorptive Capacity:** The ability of a firm to recognize the value of new external information, assimilate it, and apply it to commercial ends. It is a function of the firm's prior knowledge and R&D intensity. (Tushman & Anderson, 2004)
    *   **Components of Absorptive Capacity:**
        *   **Prior Knowledge:** Existing technical and scientific knowledge base.
        *   **R&D Activity:** Investment in research and development.
        *   **Human Capital:** Skilled workforce and their learning capabilities.
        *   **Organizational Structure & Culture:** Environment conducive to learning and knowledge sharing.
*   **Learning Curve:** The relationship between cumulative output and the input required per unit of output. As technology is absorbed and production experience increases, costs typically decrease due to efficiency gains and learning. (Rastogi, 2009)
*   **Technological Gap:** The difference in technological capabilities between two entities (e.g., a developed vs. a developing country, or two firms in the same industry).
*   **Reverse Engineering:** The process of deconstructing a product or system to understand its design, components, and manufacturing processes, often to replicate or improve upon it.

### 3. Technology Import in India & Indian Experience

#### 3.1 Context of Technology Import in India

*   **Post-Independence Era:** India initially pursued import substitution policies, aiming to build domestic industrial capacity. However, this often resulted in the adoption of outdated technologies and limited global competitiveness.
*   **Liberalization (1991 onwards):** The economic liberalization policies significantly opened up the Indian economy, leading to a surge in technology imports through foreign direct investment (FDI), joint ventures, and direct licensing.
*   **Drivers of Technology Import:**
    *   Bridging the technological gap with developed nations.
    *   Accessing advanced manufacturing processes and product designs.
    *   Improving product quality and efficiency.
    *   Enhancing global competitiveness.
    *   Meeting growing domestic demand for sophisticated products.

#### 3.2 Indian Experience with Technology Absorption

*   **Mixed Success:** The Indian experience with technology import and absorption has been mixed, with notable successes in certain sectors and challenges in others.
*   **Success Stories:**
    *   **Automobile Sector:** Significant absorption of global automotive technologies, leading to world-class manufacturing capabilities and exports. (e.g., Maruti Suzuki's success in the 1980s through its collaboration with Suzuki).
    *   **IT and BPO Sector:** Rapid absorption of software development and business process management technologies, making India a global leader. This was often driven by indigenous innovation and adaptation rather than just direct import of mature technologies.
    *   **Pharmaceuticals:** Absorbing advanced pharmaceutical manufacturing processes and R&D methodologies.
*   **Challenges:**
    *   **Inadequate Absorptive Capacity:** Many firms lacked the necessary R&D base, skilled workforce, and organizational culture to effectively absorb and adapt imported technologies.
    *   **Focus on Acquisition over Integration:** Emphasis was sometimes placed on acquiring the technology without sufficient investment in the human and organizational elements needed for successful integration.
    *   **"Plug and Play" Mentality:** A tendency to expect imported technologies to work seamlessly without local adaptation and innovation.
    *   **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) Issues:** Concerns over IPR protection sometimes hindered the willingness of foreign firms to transfer critical "know-how."
    *   **Inadequate Backward Linkages:** Difficulty in developing domestic suppliers for critical components, leading to continued dependence on imports.
    *   **Macroeconomic Factors:** Inconsistent policy environments and infrastructure deficits in certain regions.

*   **Example:** While India imported advanced manufacturing technologies for heavy engineering, the absorption was slower compared to sectors like IT where the learning curve was steeper and the required skills were more readily available or adaptable.

#### 3.3 Knowledge Levels Alignment (Course Outcomes)

*   **K2 (Comprehension):** Analyzing the historical trends of technology import in India and the impact of liberalization.
*   **K5 (Evaluation/Synthesis):** Critically evaluating the factors contributing to the successes and failures of technology absorption in specific Indian industries, drawing on the mixed experience.
*   **CO4 (Critical Assessment):** Assessing the direct and indirect effects of technology import on India's industrial development and competitive landscape.

### 4. Managing Technology Absorption

Effective management of technology absorption is crucial for realizing the full potential of imported or newly acquired technologies. It involves a strategic and proactive approach.

#### 4.1 Key Strategies for Managing Technology Absorption

1.  **Strategic Technology Selection:**
    *   Align technology acquisition with organizational goals and market strategy.
    *   Conduct thorough due diligence on the technology's suitability, scalability, and vendor reliability.
    *   Assess the technology's potential to create a sustainable competitive advantage. (Narayanan, 2002)

2.  **Building Absorptive Capacity:**
    *   **Invest in R&D:** Foster internal R&D capabilities to understand, adapt, and improve technologies.
    *   **Human Capital Development:**
        *   **Skilled Workforce:** Recruit and train personnel with the necessary technical and managerial skills.
        *   **Continuous Learning:** Promote a culture of learning and upskilling.
        *   **Knowledge Transfer Mechanisms:** Implement effective knowledge sharing programs, mentorship, and cross-functional teams. (Rastogi, 2009)
    *   **Organizational Learning:** Create an environment that encourages experimentation, feedback, and adaptation.

3.  **Effective Technology Transfer Mechanisms:**
    *   **Negotiation:** Secure favorable terms for technology acquisition, including access to know-how and technical support.
    *   **Joint Ventures & Strategic Alliances:** Collaborate with technology providers for shared learning and risk.
    *   **Licensing Agreements:** Ensure clear terms regarding technology updates, support, and territorial rights.
    *   **Turnkey Projects:** Monitor closely to ensure effective knowledge transfer, not just equipment delivery.

4.  **Adaptation and Customization:**
    *   **Localize:** Modify the technology to suit local infrastructure, resources, and market demands.
    *   **Pilot Testing:** Conduct pilot runs to identify and resolve integration issues before full-scale deployment.
    *   **Continuous Improvement:** Establish feedback loops to continuously refine and optimize the technology's performance.

5.  **Integration into Production Systems:**
    *   **Process Re-engineering:** Redesign existing workflows to accommodate the new technology.
    *   **Supply Chain Integration:** Ensure that suppliers can meet the new technology's requirements for materials and components.
    *   **Quality Management Systems:** Integrate the new technology with existing quality control and assurance processes.

6.  **Monitoring and Evaluation:**
    *   Track key performance indicators (KPIs) related to the technology's adoption and impact.
    *   Regularly assess the effectiveness of the absorption process and identify areas for improvement.
    *   Conduct post-implementation reviews to capture lessons learned.

#### 4.2 Role of Management

*   **Vision and Leadership:** Provide clear direction and champion the technology adoption process.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Ensure adequate financial, human, and infrastructural resources are allocated.
*   **Risk Management:** Identify and mitigate potential risks associated with technology absorption.
*   **Change Management:** Effectively manage the human and organizational aspects of change to ensure smooth adoption. (Tushman & Anderson, 2004)

#### 4.3 Knowledge Levels Alignment (Course Outcomes)

*   **K2 (Comprehension):** Applying strategies for managing technology absorption in different scenarios.
*   **CO5 (Synthesize):** Synthesizing the key management approaches and strategies required for successful technology absorption, considering the production function dynamics.

### 5. Government Initiatives

Governments play a crucial role in facilitating technology absorption by creating an enabling environment and providing targeted support.

#### 5.1 Common Government Initiatives

1.  **Policy Frameworks:**
    *   **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) Protection:** Strong IPR laws encourage technology transfer and innovation by safeguarding innovators' rights.
    *   **Trade Policies:** Liberal trade policies facilitate the import of advanced technologies and components, while also promoting exports.
    *   **Industrial Policies:** Promoting specific sectors through incentives and support mechanisms.

2.  **Financial and Fiscal Incentives:**
    *   **Tax Breaks:** Incentives for R&D expenditure, technology acquisition, and adoption of new technologies.
    *   **Subsidies and Grants:** Financial support for technology development, pilot projects, and adoption by SMEs.
    *   **Venture Capital and Funding Schemes:** Government-backed funds to support technology-driven startups and SMEs.

3.  **Infrastructure Development:**
    *   **Digital Infrastructure:** High-speed internet, data centers, and digital platforms to support modern technologies.
    *   **Research Facilities:** Establishment and support of national laboratories, research institutions, and testing centers.
    *   **Industrial Parks & SEZs:** Creation of zones with shared infrastructure and favorable regulatory environments.

4.  **Human Capital Development:**
    *   **Skill Development Programs:** Government-sponsored training programs to equip the workforce with skills needed for emerging technologies.
    *   **Educational Reforms:** Integrating technology and innovation into curricula at all levels of education.
    *   **Support for Universities and Research Institutions:** Funding for research, collaboration with industry, and technology commercialization.

5.  **Facilitation and Support Services:**
    *   **Technology Information Centers:** Providing access to information on available technologies and potential partners.
    *   **Incubators and Accelerators:** Supporting startups and early-stage technology companies.
    *   **Regulatory Simplification:** Streamlining approval processes for technology imports and adoption.

#### 5.2 Examples of Indian Government Initiatives

*   **"Make in India" Initiative:** A flagship program aimed at making India a global manufacturing hub, encouraging domestic manufacturing and attracting foreign investment, which inherently involves technology transfer and absorption.
*   **Startup India Initiative:** A comprehensive ecosystem to support startups, including funding, incubation, and simplification of regulations, fostering innovation and absorption of new technologies.
*   **Digital India Program:** Focused on improving India's digital infrastructure and services, which is critical for the absorption of digital technologies.
*   **National Skill Development Mission:** Initiatives to enhance the employability of the workforce by providing skill training aligned with industry needs, including those related to new technologies.
*   **National Policy on Intellectual Property Rights:** Efforts to strengthen IPR protection to encourage innovation and technology transfer.
*   **Support for MSMEs:** Schemes like Udyam Registration and credit guarantee schemes to help Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises access finance and adopt modern technologies.

#### 5.3 Knowledge Levels Alignment (Course Outcomes)

*   **K2 (Comprehension):** Explaining the types of government initiatives that support technology absorption.
*   **CO5 (Synthesize):** Discussing how government policies and initiatives influence the process of technology absorption and India's technological advancement.
*   **CO3 (Evaluate):** Evaluating the effectiveness of specific government initiatives in promoting technology absorption and their impact on the Indian economy.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Absorption is more than acquisition:** It's about successful integration and utilization.
*   **Absorptive capacity is key:** Invest in human capital, R&D, and organizational learning.
*   **Technology dependence can be a trap:** Strive for self-reliance through indigenous capability building.
*   **Adaptation is crucial:** Imported technologies rarely work perfectly without modification.
*   **Government policies are facilitators:** They create an environment but the firm's own efforts are paramount.
*   **Continuous learning and improvement:** Technology absorption is an ongoing process.

### Practice Questions & Exercises

**Section A: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)**

1.  Which of the following is NOT typically considered a component of a technology package?
    a) Hardware
    b) Tacit Knowledge
    c) Marketing Strategy
    d) Technical Documentation
    **Answer:** c) Marketing Strategy

2.  Technology dependence can lead to:
    a) Increased bargaining power with foreign suppliers
    b) Reduced foreign exchange outflow
    c) Vulnerability to external shocks
    d) Accelerated indigenous innovation
    **Answer:** c) Vulnerability to external shocks

3.  The ability of a firm to recognize the value of new external information and apply it commercially is known as:
    a) Technology transfer
    b) Absorptive capacity
    c) Technology diffusion
    d) Reverse engineering
    **Answer:** b) Absorptive capacity

**Section B: Short Answer Questions**

1.  Define "technology absorption" and explain its importance for a developing economy.
    **Answer:** Technology absorption is the process by which an organization or country acquires, adapts, integrates, and effectively utilizes a new technology to achieve desired outcomes. It is crucial for developing economies to bridge technological gaps, enhance productivity, improve competitiveness, and foster economic growth.

2.  List three key components of a technology package.
    **Answer:** Hardware, Know-how/Tacit Knowledge, Technical Documentation, Organizational Practices, Ancillary Services.

3.  What is technology dependence, and what are two of its potential consequences?
    **Answer:** Technology dependence is relying heavily on external sources for critical technologies without possessing indigenous capabilities. Consequences include economic vulnerability, reduced competitiveness, drain of foreign exchange, or limited indigenous innovation.

**Section C: Application & Analytical Questions**

1.  Critically analyze the Indian experience with technology import in the automobile sector post-liberalization. What factors contributed to successful absorption, and what challenges were faced? (Relates to CO4, CO5)
    **Answer:** (Requires detailed discussion drawing from the Indian experience section). Key factors for success include strong collaboration with foreign partners, focused investment in manufacturing capabilities and R&D, skilled workforce development, and a competitive market environment that pushed for quality and efficiency. Challenges could include initial dependence on imported components, the need for continuous adaptation to Indian road conditions, and skill gaps in certain specialized areas.

2.  Imagine you are advising a small manufacturing firm in India that is considering importing a new CNC machining center. What steps would you recommend for managing the absorption of this technology effectively? (Relates to CO2, CO5)
    **Answer:** (Requires practical application of managing technology absorption strategies). Recommendations would include:
    *   **Strategic Selection:** Ensure the CNC machine aligns with production goals and market demand.
    *   **Vendor Assessment:** Evaluate the vendor's support, training, and spare parts availability.
    *   **Skill Development:** Train operators, programmers, and maintenance staff on the new machine.
    *   **Adaptation:** Assess if any modifications are needed for materials or environmental conditions.
    *   **Integration:** Plan how the CNC machine will integrate with existing production scheduling, quality control, and supply chain.
    *   **Pilot Run:** Conduct a trial run before full deployment.
    *   **Monitoring:** Track machine performance, output quality, and operational costs.

3.  Discuss how government initiatives like "Startup India" and "Digital India" can contribute to enhancing a country's absorptive capacity for new technologies. (Relates to CO5, CO3)
    **Answer:** "Startup India" fosters innovation by providing seed funding, incubation support, and regulatory ease, encouraging the development and adoption of new technologies by new ventures. "Digital India" improves digital infrastructure, making it easier to access, share, and utilize digital technologies and data, thus boosting absorptive capacity across various sectors. Both initiatives, by promoting innovation and digital literacy, create an environment more conducive to learning and adapting new technologies.
