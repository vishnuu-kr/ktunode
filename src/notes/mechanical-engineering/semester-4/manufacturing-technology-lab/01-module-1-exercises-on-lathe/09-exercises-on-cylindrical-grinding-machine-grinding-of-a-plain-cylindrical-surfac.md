# Manufacturing Technology Lab: Module 1 - Exercises on Cylindrical Grinding Machine

## Topic: Grinding of a Plain Cylindrical Surface

### 1. Introduction to Cylindrical Grinding

Cylindrical grinding is an essential machining process used to produce highly accurate and smooth cylindrical surfaces. It is a finishing operation that removes a small amount of material using an abrasive wheel. This process is crucial for achieving tight tolerances in terms of diameter, roundness, and surface finish, often beyond the capabilities of turning operations.

**Key Concept:** Abrasive Machining Process
**Definition:** Cylindrical grinding is a form of **abrasive machining** where a rotating workpiece is brought into contact with a rotating grinding wheel to remove material and achieve precise dimensions and a superior surface finish on cylindrical or conical surfaces.

### 2. Machine Components and Principles of Operation

Understanding the components of a cylindrical grinding machine is vital for its operation and for achieving the desired results.

**2.1. Key Components:**

*   **Grinding Wheel:** The cutting tool. Composed of abrasive grains bonded together. The choice of abrasive grain, bond type, grit size, and structure is critical for effective grinding.
    *   **Abrasive Grains:** Typically Aluminum Oxide (Al₂O₃) for ferrous materials and Silicon Carbide (SiC) for non-ferrous materials.
    *   **Bond:** The material that holds the abrasive grains together (e.g., vitrified, resinoid, rubber).
    *   **Grit Size:** Refers to the size of the abrasive particles (coarser for rapid material removal, finer for better surface finish).
    *   **Structure:** The spacing between grains (open structure for less loading, dense structure for better finish).
*   **Workpiece:** The component to be ground. Rotates and is fed against the grinding wheel.
*   **Workhead:** Holds and rotates the workpiece. Can be fixed or swivelable for grinding tapers.
*   **Tailstock:** Supports the free end of the workpiece. Centers are typically used.
*   **Table:** Supports the workhead and tailstock. It reciprocates (moves back and forth) to traverse the workpiece past the grinding wheel.
*   **Grinding Wheel Spindle:** Rotates the grinding wheel at high speeds.
*   **Wheel Dressing and Truing Devices:** Essential for maintaining the cutting efficiency and accuracy of the grinding wheel.
    *   **Dressing:** Removes loaded abrasive grains and exposes new, sharp ones.
    *   **Truing:** Reshapes the grinding wheel to a true cylindrical form after it has become worn or out of balance.

**2.2. Principles of Operation:**

*   **Workpiece Rotation:** The workpiece rotates at a relatively low speed (compared to the grinding wheel).
*   **Grinding Wheel Rotation:** The grinding wheel rotates at a very high speed.
*   **Infeed:** The grinding wheel is fed radially into the workpiece.
*   **Plunge Grinding (Plunge Feed):** The wheel is fed directly into the workpiece without traversing. Used for shoulders or short lengths.
*   **Traverse Grinding:** The workpiece reciprocates (moves back and forth) relative to the grinding wheel while the wheel is also fed radially. This is used for grinding long cylindrical surfaces.
*   **Workpiece Traverse:** The workpiece moves axially past the rotating grinding wheel. This can be achieved by moving the table or by rotating the workpiece at a slower speed and moving the wheelhead axially.

### 3. Grinding of a Plain Cylindrical Surface: Procedure and Parameters

This section details the practical steps involved in grinding a plain cylindrical surface and the key parameters that influence the outcome.

**3.1. Objectives of Grinding a Plain Cylindrical Surface:**

*   Achieve precise diameter and dimensional accuracy.
*   Obtain a high-quality surface finish (low surface roughness).
*   Improve roundness and straightness of the cylindrical feature.
*   Remove minor surface defects or slight distortions from previous operations.

**3.2. Typical Procedure:**

1.  **Workpiece Preparation:**
    *   Ensure the workpiece is clean and free from dirt or oil.
    *   If centers are used, ensure the center holes are clean, correctly drilled, and lubricated.
    *   Mount the workpiece between centers or in a chuck/collet, ensuring proper support and rigidity.
2.  **Grinding Wheel Selection and Mounting:**
    *   Select an appropriate grinding wheel based on the workpiece material, desired finish, and material removal rate (refer to Textbook: **Production Technology by HMT** for guidelines on wheel selection).
    *   Mount the grinding wheel securely on the spindle, using clean blotters and flanges.
    *   Perform a ring test to check for cracks in the wheel.
    *   Ensure the wheel is properly balanced.
3.  **Machine Setup:**
    *   **Wheel Dressing and Truing:** Dress and true the grinding wheel to ensure it is sharp, clean, and has a true cylindrical profile. This is crucial for achieving accurate dimensions and good surface finish.
    *   **Workhead and Tailstock Alignment:** Ensure the workhead and tailstock centers are aligned with each other.
    *   **Speed Settings:** Set the appropriate workhead speed (surface speed) and grinding wheel speed (peripheral speed).
        *   **Workhead Speed (N_w):** Typically calculated using: $N_w = \frac{V_w}{\pi D_w}$ where $V_w$ is the desired workpiece peripheral speed and $D_w$ is the workpiece diameter. Lower speeds are generally used for roughing and higher speeds for finishing.
        *   **Grinding Wheel Speed (N_g):** This is usually fixed by the machine spindle speed, but the peripheral speed ($V_g = \pi D_g N_g$) is important.
    *   **Depth of Cut (Infeed):** Set the infeed rate for the grinding wheel. This is the radial amount the wheel is fed into the workpiece per pass or per revolution.
    *   **Table Traverse Rate:** Set the speed at which the workpiece moves axially past the wheel.
    *   **Spark-Out Passes:** Plan for a few finishing passes with no infeed to remove any residual grinding forces and improve surface finish.
4.  **Grinding Operation:**
    *   Start the machine.
    *   Engage the workpiece rotation and the grinding wheel rotation.
    *   Feed the grinding wheel into the workpiece until the desired depth of cut is reached.
    *   Start the table traverse.
    *   Monitor the grinding process, observing the sparks. Fine, feathery sparks indicate a good grinding action; heavy, continuous sparks suggest heavy loading or incorrect wheel selection.
    *   Apply the planned number of passes and spark-out passes.
    *   Retract the grinding wheel and stop the machine.
5.  **Inspection:**
    *   Measure the diameter of the ground surface using a micrometer.
    *   Inspect the surface finish visually for any defects like grinding burns, scratches, or chatter marks.
    *   Check for roundness and straightness using appropriate measuring instruments.

**3.3. Key Grinding Parameters:**

*   **Workpiece Speed ($V_w$):** Surface speed of the workpiece. Typically in the range of 30-60 m/min for carbon steels. (Refer to **Production Technology by HMT**, Chapter on Grinding).
*   **Grinding Wheel Speed ($V_g$):** Peripheral speed of the grinding wheel. Typically in the range of 25-50 m/s. High speeds increase metal removal rate but can lead to wheel wear and potential damage.
*   **Depth of Cut (Infeed) (d):** Radial distance the wheel is fed into the workpiece per pass. It is usually a very small value, e.g., 0.01-0.05 mm for finishing.
*   **Table Traverse Rate (Feed per pass):** The axial distance the workpiece moves relative to the wheel in one pass. It is usually expressed as a fraction or multiple of the wheel width (e.g., 1/2 to 3/4 of the wheel width).
*   **Number of Passes:** The total number of times the workpiece is traversed past the grinding wheel.
*   **Spark-Out Passes:** Finishing passes with no material removal (zero infeed) to improve surface finish and accuracy.

**3.4. Coolant:**

*   A coolant is essential to:
    *   Cool the workpiece and grinding wheel, preventing thermal damage.
    *   Flush away chips and abrasive debris.
    *   Lubricate the grinding interface.
*   Common coolants include soluble oils, synthetic coolants, and straight oils.

### 4. Grinding Wheel Selection Considerations

The choice of grinding wheel is paramount for successful cylindrical grinding.

**4.1. Factors Influencing Wheel Selection:**

*   **Workpiece Material:** Harder materials generally require softer wheels with larger grit sizes for efficient cutting and to prevent loading. Softer materials benefit from harder wheels with finer grit sizes for better finish.
*   **Desired Surface Finish:** Finer grit sizes and denser wheel structures produce smoother finishes.
*   **Material Removal Rate:** Coarser grit sizes and open wheel structures generally allow for higher material removal rates.
*   **Wheel Speed:** Higher wheel speeds may require stronger bond types.
*   **Machine Rigidity:** A less rigid machine may require a softer wheel to avoid vibration and chatter.

**4.2. Common Abrasives:**

*   **Aluminum Oxide (Al₂O₃):** Most common for grinding ferrous metals (steels, cast iron).
*   **Silicon Carbide (SiC):** Used for grinding non-ferrous metals (aluminum, brass, carbide) and non-metallic materials.

**4.3. Wheel Markings:**

Grinding wheels are marked with a system that indicates their characteristics (e.g., A - Aluminum Oxide, 46 - Grit Size, J - Hardness, V - Vitrified bond). Understanding these markings is crucial for selecting the correct wheel. (Refer to **Workshop Technology Part I by W. A. J. Chapman** for detailed information on wheel marking systems).

### 5. Factors Affecting Surface Finish and Accuracy

Several factors can influence the quality of the ground surface.

*   **Wheel Sharpness:** A dull wheel will cause excessive heat, burning, and poor finish. Proper dressing is essential.
*   **Wheel Balance:** An unbalanced wheel can cause vibrations, leading to chatter marks and poor accuracy.
*   **Workpiece Rigidity:** Inadequate support can lead to deflection and vibration.
*   **Machine Rigidity:** Machine vibrations can transfer to the workpiece, causing chatter.
*   **Depth of Cut:** Excessive depth of cut increases grinding forces and heat, negatively impacting finish and accuracy.
*   **Traverse Rate:** Too fast a traverse rate can lead to a coarse surface finish.
*   **Coolant Flow:** Insufficient or improper coolant can lead to workpiece burning and reduced wheel life.
*   **Workpiece Speed:** Incorrect workpiece speed can affect the grinding action.

### 6. Safety Precautions

Safety must be prioritized when operating a grinding machine.

*   **Wear Safety Glasses or a Face Shield:** Always wear eye protection.
*   **Wear Appropriate Clothing:** Avoid loose clothing, jewelry, or anything that can get caught in the rotating machinery.
*   **Check Grinding Wheel:** Ensure the wheel is free from cracks and securely mounted. Never use a cracked wheel.
*   **Machine Guarding:** Ensure all machine guards are in place and functional.
*   **Proper Tooling:** Use correct workholders and ensure they are securely mounted.
*   **Keep the Work Area Clean:** Remove swarf and debris regularly.
*   **Never Stop a Grinding Wheel by Hand:** Use the brake mechanism if provided.
*   **Be Aware of Rotating Parts:** Keep hands and tools away from moving components.

### 8. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of dressing and truing a grinding wheel?
**Answer:** Dressing removes loaded abrasive grains and exposes new sharp ones, while truing reshapes the wheel to a true cylindrical form, ensuring efficient cutting and accuracy.

**Question 2:** List three key parameters that must be set correctly for grinding a plain cylindrical surface.
**Answer:** Three key parameters are: Workpiece speed, Grinding wheel infeed (depth of cut), and Table traverse rate.

**Question 3:** Why is a coolant used in cylindrical grinding?
**Answer:** A coolant is used to cool the workpiece and wheel, flush away debris, and lubricate the grinding interface, preventing thermal damage and improving finish.

**Question 4:** If you are grinding a very soft material like aluminum, what type of abrasive and grit size would you likely choose compared to grinding steel?
**Answer:** For aluminum, you would likely choose Silicon Carbide as the abrasive and a coarser grit size compared to grinding steel. Silicon Carbide is good for softer, non-ferrous materials, and a coarser grit helps prevent loading.

**Question 5:** What is "spark-out" in cylindrical grinding?
**Answer:** Spark-out refers to making a few passes of grinding without any infeed. This is done at the end of the grinding cycle to improve surface finish and dimensional accuracy by removing residual grinding forces and any slight imperfections.

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   **Wheel Selection is Crucial:** Always refer to guidelines for selecting the appropriate grinding wheel based on workpiece material, desired finish, and operation. (HMT textbook is a good resource).
*   **Dressing & Truing is Non-Negotiable:** An untrued or loaded wheel will lead to poor results and can damage the workpiece.
*   **Small Infeeds for Finishing:** For plain cylindrical grinding to achieve good finish and accuracy, use very small depths of cut, especially in the final passes.
*   **Coolant is Essential:** Never grind without adequate coolant flow.
*   **Safety First:** Always follow safety protocols.
*   **Observe the Sparks:** The appearance of sparks provides valuable feedback on the grinding process.
