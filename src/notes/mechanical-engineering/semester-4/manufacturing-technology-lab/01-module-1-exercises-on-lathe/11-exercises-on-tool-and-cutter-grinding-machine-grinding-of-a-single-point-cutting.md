# Manufacturing Technology Lab: Module 1 - Exercises on Lathe

## Topic: Exercises on Tool and Cutter Grinding Machine: Grinding of a Single-Point Cutting Tool

This topic focuses on understanding and performing the process of grinding a single-point cutting tool using a tool and cutter grinding machine. This is a crucial skill for maintaining and preparing cutting tools, ensuring optimal performance and the production of quality workpieces.

### 1. Introduction to Grinding and the Tool & Cutter Grinding Machine

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Grinding:** A machining process that removes material using abrasive particles (grit) held together by a binder to form a grinding wheel. It's used for finishing operations, removing hardened material, and sharpening cutting tools.
*   **Tool & Cutter Grinding Machine:** A specialized machine designed for precision grinding of various cutting tools, including single-point cutting tools, milling cutters, drills, and reamers. It offers flexibility in workpiece mounting and grinding wheel positioning.
*   **Single-Point Cutting Tool:** A cutting tool with a single cutting edge. Examples include those used on lathes, shapers, and planers.

*   **LO2:** Understanding the principles of abrasive machining and tool sharpening.

### 2. Components of a Single-Point Cutting Tool

Understanding the geometry of a single-point cutting tool is essential for proper grinding.

**Key Components and Angles:**

*   **Shank:** The main body of the tool that is held in the tool post.
*   **Head:** The part of the shank that carries the cutting edge.
*   **Cutting Edge:** The sharp edge that performs the material removal.
*   **Rake Angles:**
    *   **Back Rake Angle (α):** The angle between the top surface of the tool and a line parallel to the base, measured in a plane containing the cutting edge and perpendicular to the tool shank.
        *   *Positive Back Rake:* Reduces cutting forces, improves chip formation, suitable for softer materials.
        *   *Negative Back Rake:* Increases strength of the cutting edge, suitable for harder materials, high-speed machining.
        *   *Zero Back Rake:* A compromise, used for certain materials.
    *   **Side Rake Angle (γ):** The angle between the top surface of the tool and the base, measured in a plane parallel to the base and perpendicular to the cutting edge. Influences chip flow and side cutting edge strength.
*   **Clearance Angles:**
    *   **End Clearance Angle (β):** The angle between the flank adjacent to the nose and a plane perpendicular to the cutting stroke. Prevents rubbing of the flank on the workpiece.
    *   **Side Clearance Angle (δ):** The angle between the side flank and a plane perpendicular to the side cutting edge. Prevents rubbing of the side flank on the workpiece.
*   **Cutting Angles:**
    *   **Side Cutting Edge Angle (κ):** The angle between the side cutting edge and the axis of the tool shank. Affects chip thickness and tool life.
    *   **End Cutting Edge Angle (κ'):** The angle between the end cutting edge and a line perpendicular to the tool shank. Affects the surface finish and the force acting on the workpiece.
*   **Nose Radius (r):** The radius at the junction of the side and end cutting edges. Affects surface finish, chip breaking, and tool strength.

**Examples:**

*   A tool for machining aluminum might have a large positive back rake and side rake to facilitate smooth chip flow and reduce cutting forces.
*   A tool for machining hardened steel might have a negative back rake for edge strength and smaller clearance angles.

**Learning Outcomes Covered:**

*   **LO1:** Understanding tool geometry is crucial for setting up the grinding process.

**Textbook References:**

### 3. Types of Grinding Wheels

The choice of grinding wheel is critical for effective and efficient grinding.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Abrasive Material:** The material that does the cutting (e.g., Aluminum Oxide (Al2O3), Silicon Carbide (SiC), Diamond, Cubic Boron Nitride (CBN)).
    *   *Aluminum Oxide:* Most common for ferrous metals.
    *   *Silicon Carbide:* For non-ferrous metals, carbides, and softer materials.
*   **Grit Size:** The size of the abrasive particles (e.g., Coarse: 8-24, Medium: 30-60, Fine: 70-180, Very Fine: 220+).
    *   *Coarse grit:* Faster material removal, rougher finish.
    *   *Fine grit:* Slower material removal, smoother finish.
*   **Grade (Hardness):** The strength of the bond holding the abrasive grains to the wheel (e.g., Soft, Medium, Hard).
    *   *Soft grade:* Grains break away easily, exposing new sharp edges, good for hard materials and high stock removal.
    *   *Hard grade:* Grains are held firmly, last longer, suitable for soft materials and precision finishing.
*   **Structure:** The spacing of the abrasive grains (Dense or Open).
    *   *Dense structure:* More grains per unit volume, good for fine finishes.
    *   *Open structure:* Wider spacing, prevents loading, good for soft and ductile materials.
*   **Bonding Material:** The material that holds the abrasive grains together (e.g., Vitrified, Resinoid, Rubber, Shellac).
    *   *Vitrified bond:* Most common, strong, rigid, porous, good for general-purpose grinding.
    *   *Resinoid bond:* Flexible, good for high-speed operations, high stock removal.

**Wheel Marking System (Example: A 60 K 5 V):**

*   **A:** Abrasive material (Aluminum Oxide)
*   **60:** Grit Size (Medium)
*   **K:** Grade (Medium Hard)
*   **5:** Structure (Medium Dense)
*   **V:** Bond Type (Vitrified)

### 4. Grinding Operations for a Single-Point Cutting Tool

This section details the practical steps involved in grinding the various angles of a single-point cutting tool.

**Key Operations:**

*   **Setting up the Machine:**
    *   **Selecting and Mounting the Grinding Wheel:** Ensure the wheel is the correct type, size, and condition. Mount it securely on the spindle.
    *   **Wheel Dressing and Truing:** Dressing removes loaded grains and embedded chips, while truing restores the wheel to a concentric and flat surface. This is crucial for accurate grinding.
    *   **Workpiece Mounting:** Securely hold the single-point cutting tool in a suitable fixture or between centers on the grinding machine.
    *   **Setting Grinding Angles:** Use the machine's indexing heads or protractors to set the desired rake and clearance angles. This often involves tilting the table or the workhead.
*   **Grinding the Clearance Angles (End and Side):**
    *   Usually ground first.
    *   The tool is positioned so that the flank to be ground is presented to the grinding wheel at the desired clearance angle.
    *   Light passes are made, with frequent cooling.
*   **Grinding the Rake Angles (Back and Side):**
    *   Ground after clearance angles.
    *   The top surface of the tool is presented to the grinding wheel at the desired rake angle.
    *   Precise setting of the table or workhead is required.
    *   *Important:* Grinding the rake angle often involves grinding the flank simultaneously due to the tool's geometry.
*   **Grinding the Nose Radius:**
    *   Can be achieved using a form grinding attachment or by careful manual manipulation of the tool against the grinding wheel.
    *   A finer grit wheel is often used for a smoother nose radius.
*   **Cooling:**
    *   **Flood Cooling:** Using a stream of coolant (water-based or oil-based) to dissipate heat, prevent workpiece and wheel damage, and wash away chips.
    *   **Mist Cooling:** A fine spray of coolant.
    *   **Dry Grinding:** Used with specific wheels and materials, but generally less preferred for cutting tools due to heat generation.
*   **In-Process Inspection:** Periodically check the ground angles and dimensions using gauges or optical comparators.

**Practical Tips for Grinding:**

*   **Take light cuts:** Avoid heavy cuts to prevent overheating, wheel glazing, and tool damage.
*   **Use coolant:** Essential for most grinding operations.
*   **Keep the wheel clean and sharp:** Use a dresser frequently.
*   **Avoid "burning" the tool:** Overheating can temper the tool's hardness.
*   **Maintain consistent pressure:** Apply even pressure during grinding.
*   **Rotate the tool:** If grinding between centers, rotate the tool to distribute wear on the wheel.
*   **Allow the wheel to reach full speed:** Before engaging the workpiece.
*   **Move the workpiece across the wheel:** To ensure even grinding and prevent localized heating.

### 5. Quality Control and Inspection

Ensuring the ground tool meets specifications.

**Key Inspection Points:**

*   **Angles:** Verify that all rake and clearance angles are within the specified tolerances using protractors, sine bars, or optical comparators.
*   **Nose Radius:** Check the radius of the tool nose.
*   **Surface Finish:** Examine the ground surfaces for smoothness and absence of grinding marks, burns, or cracks.
*   **Absence of Grinding Burns:** Indicators of overheating (discoloration, microstructural changes) must be absent. This can be checked using a magnetic particle inspection or microscopic examination.
*   **Sharpness of the Cutting Edge:** Visually inspect the cutting edge for sharpness and absence of micro-chipping.

### 6. Importance and Applications

**Why is grinding single-point tools important?**

*   **Restoring Cutting Ability:** Dull tools reduce efficiency, increase cutting forces, and produce poor surface finish. Grinding sharpens them.
*   **Achieving Desired Geometry:** Correct angles are crucial for efficient material removal, chip control, and surface finish.
*   **Extending Tool Life:** Properly ground tools perform better and last longer.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Regrinding tools is more economical than constantly replacing them.
*   **Precision Machining:** For high-precision work, the cutting tool must be in perfect condition.

**Applications:**

*   **Lathes:** Grinding tools for turning, facing, and boring operations.
*   **Shapers and Planers:** Grinding tools for reciprocating cutting.
*   **Machining of various materials:** From soft metals to hardened steels and exotic alloys.

**Textbook References:**

### 7. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What are the two primary types of rake angles on a single-point cutting tool, and what is their general function?

**Answer 1:**
The two primary rake angles are:
1.  **Back Rake Angle (α):** Affects chip curl and direction of chip flow.
2.  **Side Rake Angle (γ):** Affects chip flow and the strength of the side cutting edge.

**Question 2:** Explain the difference between dressing and truing a grinding wheel.

**Answer 2:**
*   **Dressing:** The process of removing loaded grains and embedded chips from the grinding wheel surface to expose new, sharp abrasive grains. This is typically done with a single-point dresser or a multi-point dresser.
*   **Truing:** The process of restoring a grinding wheel to its original shape and concentricity. This is essential if the wheel has become out-of-round, tapered, or has a damaged periphery, and is usually done with a diamond dresser.

**Question 3:** Why is it important to use coolant when grinding a single-point cutting tool?

**Answer 3:**
Coolant is crucial for several reasons:
*   **Heat Dissipation:** Grinding generates significant heat, which can temper the hardness of the tool (causing "grinding burns") and damage the workpiece. Coolant absorbs and carries away this heat.
*   **Chip Removal:** Coolant flushes away chips and abrasive particles from the grinding zone, preventing loading of the wheel.
*   **Improved Surface Finish:** Coolant can contribute to a smoother surface finish by lubricating the grinding action and preventing thermal damage.
*   **Reduced Dust:** Coolant helps to settle dust generated during grinding.

**Question 4:** If you are grinding a tool to machine a soft ductile material like aluminum, would you typically choose a finer or coarser grit wheel, and why?

**Answer 4:**
You would typically choose a **coarser grit wheel**.
*   **Reason:** Soft ductile materials tend to load grinding wheels (i.e., chips get embedded in the wheel pores). A coarser grit wheel has wider spacing between abrasive grains, which helps to prevent loading and allows for better chip clearance, leading to more efficient grinding.

**Question 5:** What is the primary purpose of clearance angles on a single-point cutting tool?

**Answer 5:**
The primary purpose of clearance angles (end clearance and side clearance) is to **prevent the flank of the tool from rubbing against the workpiece** during the cutting operation. This rubbing would increase friction, generate heat, reduce surface finish, and cause premature tool wear.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Safety First:** Always wear safety glasses and appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) when operating any machine tool. Ensure proper guards are in place.
*   **Grinding Wheel Selection is Key:** Choose the right abrasive, grit, grade, structure, and bond for the material being ground and the desired finish.
*   **Proper Wheel Maintenance:** Regularly dress and true grinding wheels for optimal performance and accuracy.
*   **Light Cuts and Coolant:** Avoid aggressive grinding. Use light passes and plenty of coolant to prevent overheating and damage.
*   **Tool Geometry Accuracy:** Precise setting of angles is critical for the tool's cutting performance.
*   **Inspection:** Always inspect the ground tool to ensure it meets specifications.
