# DESIGN OF EARTHQUAKE RESISTANT STRUCTURES

## Module 4: Response Spectrum Method (RSM): Concept

### Topic: Evaluation of Earthquake Proneness of Building by Preliminary Inspection - Rapid Visual Screening Technique

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   Understand the purpose and importance of preliminary visual inspection for seismic vulnerability assessment.
*   Identify the key objectives of Rapid Visual Screening (RVS) techniques.
*   Recognize different RVS methodologies and their underlying principles.
*   Identify common seismic deficiencies in buildings based on visual cues.

### 1. Introduction to Preliminary Visual Inspection for Seismic Vulnerability

*   **Purpose:** To conduct an initial, quick, and cost-effective assessment of a building's potential susceptibility to earthquake damage.
*   **Importance:**
    *   **Prioritization:** Helps identify structures that are most likely to perform poorly during an earthquake, allowing for focused attention on those requiring immediate evaluation and potential retrofitting.
    *   **Resource Allocation:** Efficiently directs limited resources (time, personnel, funding) towards higher-risk structures.
    *   **Public Safety:** Provides a rapid means to alert building occupants and authorities about potential hazards.
    *   **Informed Decision-Making:** Serves as a crucial first step before more detailed and expensive engineering analyses.
*   **Contrast with Detailed Analysis:** RVS is *not* a substitute for detailed seismic analysis or design. It's a preliminary screening tool.

---

### 2. Rapid Visual Screening (RVS) Techniques: Concept and Objectives

*   **Definition:** A systematic, field-based methodology used to assess the seismic vulnerability of existing buildings through visual inspection.
*   **Key Objectives:**
    *   **Quickly identify buildings with high seismic risk.**
    *   **Categorize buildings into broad vulnerability levels (e.g., low, medium, high).**
    *   **Provide a basis for prioritizing further, more detailed seismic evaluations.**
    *   **Generate preliminary data for urban seismic risk assessment.**
    *   **Inform public awareness and emergency preparedness efforts.**

---

### 3. Common RVS Methodologies

While various RVS methodologies exist, they generally share common principles. Some prominent examples include:

*   **ATC-20 (Applied Technology Council):**
    *   **Focus:** Primarily developed for post-earthquake safety evaluation, but its methodology is adapted for pre-earthquake vulnerability assessment.
    *   **Process:** Involves a checklist-based inspection to identify structural and non-structural deficiencies.
    *   **Output:** Assigns a safety evaluation placard (Green - Inspected, Yellow - Restricted Use, Red - Unsafe). While not directly a vulnerability score, the identified deficiencies highlight potential seismic weaknesses.

*   **SEAOC-OBC (Structural Engineers Association of California - Older Building Code):**
    *   **Focus:** Developed to assess the seismic performance of unreinforced masonry (URM) buildings.
    *   **Process:** Utilizes a scoring system based on various vulnerability factors specific to URM construction.

*   **FEMA P-154 (Prioritization of Buildings for Seismic Retrofit):**
    *   **Focus:** A widely used and comprehensive RVS methodology for pre-earthquake vulnerability assessment of a broad range of building types.
    *   **Process:**
        *   **Data Collection:** Involves filling out detailed questionnaires based on visual inspection of the building's structure, non-structural elements, and occupancy.
        *   **Scoring System:** Assigns scores to various building attributes (e.g., structural system, building configuration, non-structural components, soil conditions).
        *   **Vulnerability Assessment:** Calculates a seismic performance score or vulnerability index based on the collected data.
        *   **Classification:** Categorizes buildings into risk levels (e.g., low, moderate, high).
    *   **Key Components of FEMA P-154:**
        *   **Building Identification:** Basic information about the building.
        *   **Geographic/Site Information:** Location, proximity to faults, soil type.
        *   **Structural Information:**
            *   **Structural System:** Load-bearing walls, moment frames, shear walls, etc.
            *   **Material:** Concrete, steel, masonry, wood.
            *   **Year Built:** Indicates the building codes in effect at the time.
            *   **Number of Stories:** Height influences seismic forces.
            *   **Building Configuration:** Regularity vs. Irregularity (e.g., soft story, weak story, re-entrant corners).
            *   **Connections:** How different structural elements are joined.
            *   **Lateral Force Resisting System (LFRS):** How the building resists lateral earthquake forces.
        *   **Non-Structural Information:**
            *   **Façade:** Cladding, parapets, signs.
            *   **Partitions:** Interior walls.
            *   **Ceilings:** Suspended ceilings.
            *   **Mechanical/Electrical/Plumbing (MEP) Equipment:** Anchorage of critical systems.
        *   **Occupancy Type:** Critical facilities (hospitals, schools), residential, commercial.

*   **Other RVS Approaches:** Many countries and regions have developed their own RVS tools tailored to their specific building stock and seismic hazard. The underlying principle of visual inspection and identification of vulnerabilities remains the same.

### 4. Identifying Common Seismic Deficiencies through Visual Inspection

RVS focuses on observable characteristics that are indicative of seismic weakness. Common deficiencies to look for include:

*   **Soft Story / Weak Story:** A story with significantly less lateral stiffness or strength compared to stories above or below. Often caused by large open spaces (e.g., parking garages) on the lower floors.
    *   **Visual Clues:** Large openings on the ground floor, inadequate bracing in lower stories, significantly taller first story.
*   **Irregular Configurations:**
    *   **Torsional Irregularities:** Asymmetrical plan layouts lead to twisting during earthquakes.
        *   **Visual Clues:** L-shaped, U-shaped, or T-shaped buildings; uneven distribution of mass or stiffness.
    *   **Vertical Irregularities:** Discontinuities in the LFRS or mass distribution along the height of the building.
        *   **Visual Clues:** Setbacks in upper stories, overhangs, large openings on specific floors, significant differences in floor heights.
*   **Unreinforced Masonry (URM):** Masonry walls constructed without steel reinforcement. Prone to out-of-plane failure and cracking.
    *   **Visual Clues:** Brick or block walls, visible mortar joints, absence of visible rebar. Look for cracks, spalling, and bowed walls.
*   **Inadequate Shear Walls/Bracing:** Lack of sufficient stiff elements to resist lateral forces.
    *   **Visual Clues:** Large open bays without shear walls or braced frames, inadequate connection of bracing elements.
*   **Poor Connection Details:** Weak or inadequate connections between structural elements (beams to columns, walls to diaphragms) can lead to failure.
    *   **Visual Clues:** Visible corrosion, missing bolts, cracks at connection points, inadequate welding.
*   **Non-Structural Hazards:**
    *   **Unanchored Parapets and Cornices:** Can fall and cause significant damage and injury.
        *   **Visual Clues:** Projecting elements at the roofline, visible cracks or signs of deterioration.
    *   **Unbraced Facades and Cladding:** Falling façade elements.
        *   **Visual Clues:** Loose or cracking façade materials, visible separation from the structure.
    *   **Unsecured Mezzanines and Appendages:** Falling debris.
        *   **Visual Clues:** Protruding structures not well integrated with the main building.
    *   **Unsecured Non-Structural Walls (Partitions) and Ceilings:** Falling partitions and ceiling panels.
        *   **Visual Clues:** Suspended ceilings without adequate bracing, interior partitions that appear flimsy.
    *   **Unanchored Mechanical and Electrical Equipment:** Displacement of vital systems.
        *   **Visual Clues:** Large machinery (boilers, generators, tanks) without visible anchors or bracing.

---

### 5. Role of RVS in Prioritizing Retrofitting Efforts

*   **Data-Driven Prioritization:** RVS provides a quantitative or semi-quantitative basis for ranking buildings based on their seismic risk.
*   **Resource Allocation Strategy:** Authorities can use RVS results to allocate limited funds and engineering expertise to the most vulnerable buildings first.
*   **Phased Approach:** RVS can inform a phased approach to retrofitting, starting with the highest-risk structures and gradually addressing others.
*   **Community Risk Assessment:** Aggregating RVS data for a city or region helps understand the overall seismic vulnerability of the built environment.

---

### 6. Limitations of Rapid Visual Screening

It is crucial to acknowledge the limitations of RVS:

*   **Preliminary Nature:** RVS is a screening tool, not a definitive analysis. It provides a *potential* for high seismic vulnerability.
*   **Subjectivity:** Visual inspection can involve a degree of subjectivity in assessing certain parameters.
*   **Limited Information:** RVS typically relies on readily observable features and does not involve intrusive investigation (e.g., opening up walls to inspect connections or reinforcement).
*   **Assumptions:** The scoring systems are based on generalized assumptions about structural behavior and are not tailored to the specific seismic response of every building.
*   **Incomplete Picture:** RVS may not capture all subtle seismic deficiencies or complex structural interactions.
*   **Non-Structural Focus:** While RVS considers non-structural elements, a comprehensive assessment of non-structural hazards often requires specialized expertise.

---

### 7. Important Points to Remember:

*   **RVS is a *first-pass* assessment.**
*   **Its primary goal is *prioritization* for further investigation.**
*   **Focus on *observable* structural and non-structural deficiencies.**
*   **Understanding common deficiencies like soft stories and URM construction is key.**
*   **FEMA P-154 is a widely used and comprehensive RVS framework.**
*   **RVS findings should *always* be followed up with detailed engineering analysis for high-risk structures.**

### Practice Questions and Exercises:

**Question 1:** What is the primary objective of conducting a Rapid Visual Screening (RVS) of a building?
    *   A) To design a seismic retrofitting scheme.
    *   B) To determine the exact seismic performance of the building under a specific earthquake.
    *   C) To quickly identify buildings with a high potential for seismic vulnerability and prioritize them for further evaluation.
    *   D) To provide a detailed structural analysis report.

**Answer 1:** C) To quickly identify buildings with a high potential for seismic vulnerability and prioritize them for further evaluation.

**Question 2:** List three common seismic deficiencies that can be identified through visual inspection during an RVS.
    *   A) Foundation settlement, soil liquefaction, ground rupture.
    *   B) Soft story, unreinforced masonry walls, irregular building configuration.
    *   C) Poor interior finishes, outdated plumbing, electrical wiring issues.
    *   D) Wind load resistance, snow load capacity, fire safety.

**Answer 2:** B) Soft story, unreinforced masonry walls, irregular building configuration.

**Question 3:** True or False: Rapid Visual Screening provides a complete and definitive assessment of a building's seismic safety.

**Answer 3:** False. RVS is a preliminary screening tool and not a substitute for detailed engineering analysis.

**Question 4:** Briefly explain the concept of a "soft story" and why it is considered a seismic deficiency.

**Answer 4:** A soft story is a story in a building that has significantly less lateral stiffness or strength compared to the stories above or below it. This deficiency is critical because during an earthquake, the lateral forces are concentrated in this weaker story, leading to excessive deformation and potential collapse. Visually, it can be identified by large open spaces on a lower floor (e.g., parking garages) without adequate structural bracing.

**Question 5:** Why is it important to consider non-structural elements during an RVS? Provide two examples.

**Answer 5:** It is important to consider non-structural elements because their failure during an earthquake can pose significant risks to life safety and can also lead to substantial economic losses and disruption of building function, even if the main structural system remains intact.
    *   **Example 1: Parapets and Cornices:** Unanchored parapets and cornices at the roofline can become dislodged and fall, causing injury or death to people below.
    *   **Example 2: Suspended Ceilings:** Unbraced suspended ceilings can collapse, releasing light fixtures and falling debris, which can be hazardous.
