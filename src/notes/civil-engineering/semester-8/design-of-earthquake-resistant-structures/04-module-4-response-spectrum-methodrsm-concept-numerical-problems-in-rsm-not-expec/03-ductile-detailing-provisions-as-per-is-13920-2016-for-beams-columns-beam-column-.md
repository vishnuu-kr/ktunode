# Design of Earthquake Resistant Structures

## Module 4: Response Spectrum Method (RSM)

**Topic: Ductile Detailing Provisions as per IS 13920 (2016)**

---

### Introduction to Ductile Detailing

Earthquakes cause cyclic reversals of stress and strain in structures. **Ductile detailing** is a set of design and construction practices that ensure a structure can undergo significant inelastic deformations without catastrophic failure. The primary goal is to promote **"strong column-weak beam"** behavior, where plastic hinges form in beams (which are generally more easily repaired) rather than in columns (whose failure can lead to progressive collapse).

The Indian Standard IS 13920:2016 (Ductile Design and Detailing of Reinforced Concrete Structures Subjected to Seismic Forces - Code of Practice) provides the guidelines for achieving ductile behavior in reinforced concrete structures.

### Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Ductility:** The ability of a material or structure to undergo large inelastic deformations before failure.
*   **Plastic Hinge:** A region in a structural member where plastic deformations concentrate, allowing for continued rotation after yielding.
*   **Confining Reinforcement (Confinement):** Transverse reinforcement (stirrups, ties, or hoops) that wraps around the longitudinal bars, preventing them from buckling and enhancing the concrete's compressive strength and ductility.
*   **Strong Column-Weak Beam:** A seismic design philosophy where columns are designed to be stronger and stiffer than beams at a beam-column joint, ensuring that plastic hinges form in beams.
*   **Shear Critical Section:** The section of a beam or column where shear forces are likely to cause failure before flexural yielding.
*   **Development Length:** The length of embedment required for a reinforcing bar to develop its full yield strength in concrete.

## Ductile Detailing Provisions as per IS 13920 (2016)

IS 13920 (2016) mandates specific detailing requirements for seismic zones, particularly for regions where the design seismic base shear is significant. The provisions are aimed at ensuring ductile flexural yielding in beams and columns, preventing brittle shear failures, and ensuring proper force transfer at beam-column joints.

### 1. Ductile Detailing for Beams

Beams are expected to form plastic hinges at their ends. Therefore, detailing focuses on ensuring flexural yielding at the ends and preventing shear failure in the central region and at the ends.

**1.1 Reinforcement Requirements:**

*   **Minimum Longitudinal Reinforcement:**
    *   **Top Reinforcement:** Minimum of 0.24% of the gross concrete area.
    *   **Bottom Reinforcement:** Minimum of 0.36% of the gross concrete area.
    *   *Note:* This is to ensure adequate flexural capacity and prevent premature yielding of bottom bars in negative moment regions.

*   **Maximum Longitudinal Reinforcement:**
    *   The area of tensile reinforcement should not exceed **2.5%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   The area of compression reinforcement should not exceed **1%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   *Note:* Excessive reinforcement can lead to congestion and difficulty in achieving proper concrete consolidation, as well as stress concentrations.

*   **Web Reinforcement (Stirrups):**
    *   **Spacing of Stirrups:**
        *   In the **outer plastic hinge regions** (extending to a distance of 2d or $l_b/4$, whichever is greater, from the face of the support or beam-column joint): Stirrup spacing should not exceed the *smaller* of:
            *   $d/4$ (where $d$ is the effective depth of the beam)
            *   $6d_{bl}$ (where $d_{bl}$ is the diameter of the longitudinal bar)
            *   100 mm
        *   In the **remaining portion** of the beam: Stirrup spacing should not exceed the *smaller* of:
            *   $d/2$
            *   $6d_{bl}$
            *   200 mm
    *   **Stirrup Configuration:**
        *   **First stirrup:** Should be placed at a distance not exceeding $d/4$ from the compression face or the face of the support.
        *   **Stirrup Diameter:** Not less than 6 mm or 1/4th of the diameter of the largest longitudinal bar, whichever is greater.
        *   **Hooking of Stirrups:** Stirrup legs must be hooked with a **135-degree hook** of adequate length, engaging at least 6 times the bar diameter. The hook should be extended into the core of the beam.
        *   **Sufficient Stirrups:** The quantity of stirrups in the plastic hinge region must be sufficient to provide confinement to the longitudinal bars. This is achieved by limiting the spacing and ensuring the stirrups are correctly closed.

*   **Development Length of Longitudinal Bars:**
    *   Longitudinal bars must be adequately anchored to develop their yield strength. This is critical at beam ends to ensure the plastic hinge forms correctly.
    *   The development length ($L_d$) calculation should be as per IS 456, but the anchorage must be considered for the yield strength ($f_y$) of steel, and if the bar stress is limited by detailing (e.g., if 50% of the bars are cut off), then the stress needs to be adjusted.

**1.2 Special Provisions for Beams:**

*   **Confinement at Ends:** The region within 2d or $l_b/4$ (whichever is greater) from the support face (or the joint face) is considered the plastic hinge zone. This zone requires closely spaced and well-confined stirrups.
*   **Lap Splices:**
    *   Lap splices in tension reinforcement are permitted only in the **middle-third region** of the beam (i.e., not within the plastic hinge zones).
    *   The lap length for lap splices in tension should be at least the development length or $40d_{bl}$ or $L_{e}$ (effective lap length), whichever is greater.
    *   If lap splices are unavoidable in the plastic hinge region, they must be confined by hoops or supplementary ties at the same spacing as the beam stirrups within that region.
*   **Mechanical Couplers:** Mechanical couplers can be used for splicing longitudinal bars, and their placement is not restricted to the middle third.

### 2. Ductile Detailing for Columns

Columns are designed to remain essentially elastic during the earthquake, or to develop plastic hinges only at their top and bottom ends, ideally above and below beam-column joints. The main goal is to prevent shear failure and buckling of longitudinal bars.

**2.1 Reinforcement Requirements:**

*   **Minimum Longitudinal Reinforcement:**
    *   The area of longitudinal reinforcement should not be less than **0.8%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   The area of longitudinal reinforcement should not exceed **6%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   *Note:* A minimum amount is needed for load-carrying capacity and to ensure ductile behavior. A maximum is set to avoid congestion and ensure proper concrete placement.

*   **Minimum Reinforcement Diameter:** The diameter of longitudinal bars should not be less than 12 mm.

*   **Web Reinforcement (Ties/Stirrups):**
    *   **Spacing of Ties:**
        *   **For Columns in Frame Buildings:** In the **upper and lower thirds** of the column height (i.e., in the region likely to experience plastic hinging), the spacing of ties should not exceed the *smaller* of:
            *   $d/4$ (where $d$ is the diameter of the smallest hoop/tie leg)
            *   $8 \times$ diameter of the smallest longitudinal bar
            *   $24 \times$ diameter of the tie bar
            *   100 mm
        *   In the **middle-third** of the column height, the spacing should not exceed the *smaller* of:
            *   $d/2$
            *   $8 \times$ diameter of the smallest longitudinal bar
            *   $24 \times$ diameter of the tie bar
            *   200 mm
    *   **For Columns in Buildings in Seismic Zones IV and V, or when governed by plastic hinging:** The spacing in the **entire height** of the column should be as per the upper/lower thirds requirement (i.e., the tighter spacing).
    *   **Tie Configuration:**
        *   **Hooking of Ties:** Ties must have **135-degree hooks** of adequate length, enclosing the longitudinal bars. The hook length should be at least $6d_{bl}$ and should extend into the confined core.
        *   **Cross Ties:** In columns with four or more longitudinal bars, **cross ties** are required. These should be equally distributed around the perimeter, connecting opposite longitudinal bars. They must also have 135-degree hooks.
        *   **Closed Ties:** The ties must form a closed loop to effectively confine the concrete core.
        *   **Tie Diameter:** Not less than 1/4th of the diameter of the largest longitudinal bar, but not less than 6 mm.

*   **Lap Splices:**
    *   Lap splices are **prohibited** in the regions of potential plastic hinging (top and bottom 1/3rd of column height) and within beam-column joints.
    *   If lap splices are used, they must be located in the **middle-third** of the column height.
    *   The lap length for lap splices in tension should be at least the development length or $48d_{bl}$ or $L_{e}$ (effective lap length), whichever is greater.
    *   Lap splices in compression are permitted in the middle-third, with a lap length of at least the development length in compression or $24d_{bl}$ or $L_e$, whichever is greater.
    *   **Confined Lap Splices:** If lap splices are required in regions of expected plastic hinging, they must be confined by supplementary transverse reinforcement (e.g., hoops) at the same spacing as the column ties.

**2.2 Special Provisions for Columns:**

*   **Confined Core:** The primary purpose of ties in columns is to confine the concrete core, preventing buckling of longitudinal bars under compression and enhancing the column's ductility.
*   **Axial Load Consideration:** Columns subjected to high axial loads (particularly when the axial load is close to the balanced failure load) require special attention to confinement.

### 3. Ductile Detailing for Beam-Column Joints

Beam-column joints are critical zones where forces are transferred from beams to columns. They must be designed to remain strong and avoid brittle failure modes like joint shear or joint crushing.

**3.1 Types of Joints:**

*   **Panel Zone:** The concrete region within the joint bounded by the outer faces of the intersecting members.
*   **Monolithic Joints:** Where beams and columns are cast integrally.

**3.2 Joint Shear Reinforcement (Hoops/Stirrups):**

*   **When Required:** For frame buildings in seismic zones III, IV, and V, beam-column joints must be reinforced.
*   **Details:**
    *   Hoops or confining ties with 135-degree hooks are required within the joint panel zone.
    *   The spacing of these ties should be the *smaller* of:
        *   $100 \text{ mm}$
        *   $d/4$ (effective depth of the beam)
        *   $8 \times$ diameter of the smallest longitudinal bar
    *   The diameter of these ties should not be less than 6 mm or 1/4th the diameter of the largest longitudinal bar in the beam.
*   **Anchorage of Beam Reinforcement:**
    *   **Beam Longitudinal Bars:** All longitudinal bars of the beam must extend through the joint.
        *   **Tensile Reinforcement:** Bars intended to resist tension must be anchored with a **135-degree hook** of adequate length (typically $L_d$ or $10d_{bl}$ beyond the column face, or $L_d$ into the confined region of the column).
        *   **Compression Reinforcement:** Bars intended to resist compression are usually adequately anchored by embedment.
    *   **Column Longitudinal Bars:** The longitudinal bars of the column must extend through the joint and be effectively anchored in the adjacent stories.

**3.3 Confinement of Joint Core:**

*   The joint region itself must be adequately confined by the column's transverse reinforcement (ties).

**3.4 Shear Strength of Joint:**

*   IS 13920:2016 specifies checks for the shear strength of the joint panel zone, which dictates the amount of required joint shear reinforcement.
*   The shear strength of the joint depends on the strength of the concrete and the amount of transverse reinforcement.

**3.5 Anchorage of Beam Reinforcement:**

*   **Hook Anchorage:** A 135-degree hook is generally required for tensile reinforcement passing through the joint.
*   **Development Length:** The required development length must be provided either within the joint or by extending the bar into the adjacent member and anchoring it properly.

**3.6 Beam Bar Termination:**

*   **For beam bars resisting tension:** The bars should terminate with a hook extending into the core of the column.
*   **For beam bars resisting compression:** The bars can be terminated by simple embedment.
*   **Anchorage Zone:** The effective anchorage of beam reinforcement within the column is crucial for transferring forces.

### 4. Ductile Detailing for Shear Walls

Shear walls are designed to resist lateral loads, and their ductile behavior is crucial for preventing sudden failure. They are typically governed by shear and flexural stresses.

**4.1 Reinforcement Requirements:**

*   **Vertical Reinforcement:**
    *   Minimum area of vertical reinforcement: **0.2%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   Maximum area of vertical reinforcement: **4%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   Vertical bars should be distributed uniformly along the length of the wall.
    *   **Buckling Prevention:** Vertical bars at the edges of the wall, and any bars used as compression reinforcement, must be adequately confined with **closed hoops or ties** at specified spacing, similar to columns.

*   **Horizontal Reinforcement:**
    *   Minimum area of horizontal reinforcement: **0.2%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   Maximum area of horizontal reinforcement: **4%** of the gross concrete area.
    *   Horizontal reinforcement is essential for resisting shear and limiting diagonal cracking.
    *   **Spacing of Horizontal Reinforcement:**
        *   Spacing should not exceed the *smaller* of:
            *   $2 \times$ the length of the wall in the direction perpendicular to the reinforcement.
            *   $3 \times$ the thickness of the wall.
            *   300 mm.

*   **Boundary Elements:**
    *   For walls with high ductility requirements (especially in higher seismic zones or for taller walls), **boundary elements** are often required at the ends of the wall or at openings.
    *   Boundary elements are reinforced concrete sections that are designed to provide confinement and enhance the ductility of the wall under overturning and shear forces.
    *   They are typically designed to carry the compressive stresses resulting from overturning moments and are heavily reinforced with longitudinal bars and confining ties.

**4.2 Web Reinforcement (Confinement):**

*   **Boundary Elements:** If boundary elements are provided, they require confinement similar to columns, with closely spaced hoops or ties.
*   **Web Reinforcement:** The horizontal and vertical reinforcement in the web of the shear wall acts to resist shear and prevent diagonal cracking.
*   **Confinement for Vertical Bars:** Vertical bars at the edges of the wall and any bars acting in compression must be provided with confinement reinforcement.

**4.3 Lap Splices:**

*   Lap splices in vertical reinforcement should be located in the middle-third of the wall height and confined by supplementary ties.
*   Lap splices in horizontal reinforcement should be staggered.

**4.4 Openings in Shear Walls:**

*   Openings in shear walls (e.g., for doors and windows) disrupt the load path and can concentrate stresses.
*   **Reinforcement around Openings:** Additional reinforcement is required around openings to maintain the integrity and ductility of the wall. This typically involves reinforcing the jambs (sides) of the opening with vertical bars and providing closed hoops or ties.

---

### Important Points to Remember

*   **"Strong Column-Weak Beam"** is the fundamental principle.
*   **Confinement** is key to ductility in all elements (beams, columns, joints, boundary elements of walls).
*   **135-degree hooks** for transverse reinforcement (stirrups, ties) are mandatory in critical regions.
*   **Lap splices** in tension zones must be located in regions of low stress (e.g., middle third of beams/columns) or be confined.
*   **Shear failure is brittle; flexural failure is ductile.** Ductile detailing aims to prevent shear failure before flexural yielding.
*   **Beam-column joints** are critical and require special attention to shear reinforcement and anchorage of beam bars.
*   **Shear walls** require adequate distributed reinforcement and confinement at boundaries and around openings.
*   Always refer to the **latest version of IS 13920 (2016)** for specific requirements and details.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**

Explain the concept of "strong column-weak beam" behavior in earthquake-resistant design. How does ductile detailing contribute to achieving this behavior?

**Answer:**

"Strong column-weak beam" behavior refers to designing structural elements such that plastic hinges form in beams before they form in columns. This is desirable because beams are generally easier to repair or replace than columns. Ductile detailing achieves this by:

*   **Beams:** Providing sufficient reinforcement and closely spaced stirrups in the plastic hinge zones (ends of beams) to ensure flexural yielding and prevent shear failure.
*   **Columns:** Designing columns with adequate strength and providing confining reinforcement (ties) to prevent buckling of longitudinal bars and maintain their load-carrying capacity even after significant inelastic deformation.
*   **Beam-Column Joints:** Ensuring joints are strong enough to transfer forces from beams to columns without failure, typically through adequate joint shear reinforcement.

By ensuring plastic hinges form in beams, the structure can dissipate seismic energy through controlled yielding without collapsing.

**Question 2:**

What are the specific requirements for the spacing and configuration of stirrups in the plastic hinge regions of a beam as per IS 13920 (2016)?

**Answer:**

According to IS 13920 (2016), in the plastic hinge regions of a beam (defined as a distance of 2d or $l_b/4$ from the face of the support or joint), the stirrup spacing should not exceed the *smaller* of:
*   $d/4$
*   $6d_{bl}$ (where $d_{bl}$ is the diameter of the longitudinal bar)
*   100 mm

The first stirrup should be placed at a distance not exceeding $d/4$ from the support face. Stirrups must have 135-degree hooks of adequate length to engage the longitudinal bars.

**Question 3:**

Where are lap splices generally prohibited in columns designed according to IS 13920 (2016), and what are the requirements if they are unavoidable in such regions?

**Answer:**

Lap splices are prohibited in the regions of potential plastic hinging in columns, which are the **upper and lower thirds** of the column height, and within **beam-column joints**.

If lap splices are unavoidable in regions of expected plastic hinging, they must be confined by supplementary transverse reinforcement (e.g., hoops) at the same spacing as the column ties within that region. The lap length in tension should be at least the development length or $48d_{bl}$ or $L_e$, whichever is greater.

**Question 4:**

Describe the essential ductile detailing features for beam-column joints.

**Answer:**

Essential ductile detailing features for beam-column joints include:

*   **Joint Shear Reinforcement:** Providing closed stirrups or hoops within the joint panel zone at close spacing (not exceeding $100 \text{ mm}$ or $d/4$ or $8d_{bl}$) to resist shear forces.
*   **Anchorage of Beam Reinforcement:** Ensuring that the longitudinal reinforcement of the beam is properly anchored within the column core, with tensile reinforcement typically requiring a 135-degree hook.
*   **Column Confinement:** The column's transverse reinforcement must effectively confine the joint core.
*   **Sufficient Joint Strength:** The joint must be designed to have adequate shear strength to prevent brittle shear failure.

**Question 5:**

What is the minimum and maximum percentage of vertical and horizontal reinforcement required in shear walls as per IS 13920 (2016)?

**Answer:**

As per IS 13920 (2016) for shear walls:

*   **Vertical Reinforcement:** Minimum area is **0.2%** of the gross concrete area, and the maximum is **4%** of the gross concrete area.
*   **Horizontal Reinforcement:** Minimum area is **0.2%** of the gross concrete area, and the maximum is **4%** of the gross concrete area.
