# Hydrogeology: Module 3 - Well Design Criteria
## Topic: Chemical Characteristics of Groundwater & Graphical Representation of Water Quality Data

This module focuses on understanding the chemical composition of groundwater, its implications for well design, and how to interpret this data using various graphical methods.

### 1. Chemical Characteristics of Groundwater Relevant to Well Design

Understanding the chemical composition of groundwater is crucial for designing effective and sustainable wells. Key parameters influence:

*   **Corrosion and Scaling:** Aggressive water can corrode well casing and pumps, while water with high mineral content can lead to scaling, reducing well efficiency.
*   **Water Use Suitability:** Chemistry dictates suitability for drinking, irrigation, industrial use, and other applications.
*   **Aquifer Characterization:** Chemical signatures can help identify recharge sources, flow paths, and the degree of water-rock interaction.
*   **Treatment Requirements:** Knowing the chemical makeup helps determine necessary water treatment processes.

**Key Chemical Constituents and Their Significance:**

*   **Major Ions:**
    *   **Cations:** Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$), Magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$), Sodium ($\text{Na}^{+}$), Potassium ($\text{K}^{+}$).
        *   $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$: Primarily responsible for hardness. High concentrations can lead to scaling.
        *   $\text{Na}^{+}$: Can cause dispersion of clay particles in soils, leading to reduced permeability (important for irrigation).
    *   **Anions:** Bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$), Carbonate ($\text{CO}_{3}^{2-}$), Sulfate ($\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$), Chloride ($\text{Cl}^{-}$).
        *   $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$ and $\text{CO}_{3}^{2-}$: Contribute to alkalinity and buffering capacity.
        *   $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$: Can cause corrosion in the presence of sulfate-reducing bacteria and contribute to hardness.
        *   $\text{Cl}^{-}$: Often associated with salinity and pollution. High concentrations can be detrimental to vegetation and human health.
*   **Minor and Trace Elements:** Iron ($\text{Fe}$), Manganese ($\text{Mn}$), Fluoride ($\text{F}^{-}$), Arsenic ($\text{As}$), Nitrates ($\text{NO}_{3}^{-}$), etc.
    *   $\text{Fe}$ and $\text{Mn}$: Can cause staining and clogging.
    *   $\text{NO}_{3}^{-}$: Often an indicator of agricultural or sewage contamination.
*   **Physical Parameters:**
    *   **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS):** Sum of all dissolved constituents. Indicates salinity.
    *   **pH:** Measure of acidity or alkalinity. Affects the solubility of many minerals and the corrosivity of water.
    *   **Temperature:** Influences chemical reaction rates and water density.
    *   **Electrical Conductivity (EC):** Proportional to TDS and indicates salinity.

### 2. Graphical Representation of Groundwater Quality Data

Graphical methods are powerful tools for visualizing and interpreting complex hydrochemical datasets. They allow for:

*   **Classification of Groundwater Types:** Identifying dominant ions and water genesis.
*   **Assessing Suitability:** Evaluating water quality for various uses.
*   **Identifying Hydrogeochemical Processes:** Understanding water-rock interactions, mixing, and contamination.

---

### 3. Interpretation of Hydrochemical Analysis Data

Hydrochemical analysis involves determining the concentration of various dissolved constituents in a water sample. These results are typically reported in milligrams per liter ($\text{mg/L}$) or milliequivalents per liter ($\text{meq/L}$). For plotting on diagrams, it's often necessary to convert concentrations to **milliequivalents per liter (meq/L)**.

**Conversion Formula:**

$$ \text{meq/L} = \frac{\text{Concentration (mg/L)}}{\text{Equivalent Weight}} $$

**Equivalent Weight Calculation:**

$$ \text{Equivalent Weight} = \frac{\text{Molecular Weight}}{\text{Valency}} $$

**Example:** Convert $100 \text{ mg/L}$ of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ to $\text{meq/L}$.

*   Molecular Weight of $\text{Ca} = 40.08 \text{ g/mol}$
*   Valency of $\text{Ca}^{2+} = 2$
*   Equivalent Weight of $\text{Ca}^{2+} = \frac{40.08}{2} = 20.04 \text{ g/eq}$

$$ \text{meq/L of Ca}^{2+} = \frac{100 \text{ mg/L}}{20.04 \text{ mg/meq}} \approx 4.99 \text{ meq/L} $$

**Important:** Always ensure consistency in units (e.g., $\text{mg/L}$ vs. $\text{ppm}$ which is practically the same for dilute aqueous solutions) and correct molecular weights and valencies.

### 4. Graphical Representation Techniques

#### 4.1 Hill-Piper Trilinear Diagram

*   **Purpose:** Classifies groundwater types based on the relative abundance of major cations and anions. It uses two lower triangles and one upper triangle.
*   **Principles:**
    *   **Lower Triangles:** Plot the relative percentage of cations (left triangle) and anions (right triangle) based on their concentrations in $\text{meq/L}$.
    *   **Sum of Cations = 100%:** The total of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+}$ is normalized to 100%.
    *   **Sum of Anions = 100%:** The total of $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$, $\text{Cl}^{-}$, $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$ (or $\text{CO}_{3}^{2-}$ and $\text{OH}^{-}$ if dominant) is normalized to 100%.
    *   **Upper Triangle:** The points plotted on the lower triangles are projected onto the upper triangle, where the water type is determined by the field it falls into.
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **Specific Fields:** The upper triangle is divided into fields representing dominant cation-anion combinations (e.g., $\text{Ca-HCO}_3$, $\text{Na-Cl}$).
    *   **Water Genesis:** Can indicate water origins (e.g., dilute meteoric water, evolved connate water).
    *   **Mixing:** Shows potential mixing between different water types.

**Diagram Structure (Conceptual):**

*   **Left Triangle (Cations):**
    *   Apex: $\text{Ca}^{2+}$
    *   Left Base: $\text{Mg}^{2+}$
    *   Right Base: $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+}$
*   **Right Triangle (Anions):**
    *   Apex: $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$
    *   Left Base: $\text{Cl}^{-}$
    *   Right Base: $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$
*   **Upper Triangle (Combined):**
    *   Apex: $\text{100%}$
    *   Left Base: $\text{100%}$
    *   Right Base: $\text{100%}$
    *   Fields are defined by the position of the projected point.

**Commonly Identified Water Types:**

*   **$\text{Ca-HCO}_3$:** Often indicative of recently recharged groundwater interacting with carbonate rocks.
*   **$\text{Mg-HCO}_3$:** Similar to $\text{Ca-HCO}_3$, but with higher $\text{Mg}$ influence.
*   **$\text{Ca-SO}_4$:** May indicate interaction with gypsum or sulfide oxidation.
*   **$\text{Na-Cl}$:** Typically associated with saline water, connate water, or pollution.
*   **$\text{Na-HCO}_3$:** Can result from ion exchange processes where $\text{Na}$ replaces $\text{Ca}$ and $\text{Mg}$.

**Practice Question:**

A groundwater sample has the following concentrations in $\text{meq/L}$:
$\text{Ca}^{2+} = 2.0$, $\text{Mg}^{2+} = 1.5$, $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+} = 3.0$
$\text{SO}_{4}^{2-} = 0.5$, $\text{Cl}^{-} = 1.0$, $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-} = 5.0$

Calculate the relative percentages for plotting on a Hill-Piper diagram.

**Answer:**

*   **Total Cations:** $2.0 + 1.5 + 3.0 = 6.5 \text{ meq/L}$
    *   $\text{Ca}^{2+}$%: $(2.0 / 6.5) * 100 = 30.8\%$
    *   $\text{Mg}^{2+}$%: $(1.5 / 6.5) * 100 = 23.1\%$
    *   $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+}$%: $(3.0 / 6.5) * 100 = 46.1\%$
*   **Total Anions:** $0.5 + 1.0 + 5.0 = 6.5 \text{ meq/L}$
    *   $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$%: $(0.5 / 6.5) * 100 = 7.7\%$
    *   $\text{Cl}^{-}$%: $(1.0 / 6.5) * 100 = 15.4\%$
    *   $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$%: $(5.0 / 6.5) * 100 = 76.9\%$

#### 4.2 Durov's Diagram

*   **Purpose:** A more comprehensive graphical method that plots both major ions and other characteristics like TDS, $\text{pH}$, and sometimes even specific trace elements. It provides a more detailed picture of water chemistry and genesis.
*   **Principles:**
    *   Uses a hexagonal graph paper.
    *   **Central Hexagon:** Plots the relative percentages of major cations ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+}$) and anions ($\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$, $\text{Cl}^{-}$, $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$) similar to the Hill-Piper diagram, but on a single plane.
    *   **Outer Sectors:** Plot other parameters like TDS, $\text{pH}$, and specific chemical species (e.g., $\text{Fe}$, $\text{Mn}$).
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **Water Type:** Dominant water type is indicated by the position of the plotted points within the central hexagon.
    *   **Hydrochemical Facies:** Identifies water masses and their evolution.
    *   **Contamination:** Can show the influence of pollutants.
    *   **$\text{pH}$ and TDS:** Their relationship with major ions provides insights into water-rock interaction and saturation states.

**Diagram Structure (Conceptual):**

*   The central hexagon has six vertices. Three represent cations ($\text{Ca}$, $\text{Mg}$, $\text{Na+K}$) and three represent anions ($\text{SO}_4$, $\text{Cl}$, $\text{HCO}_3$).
*   Points are plotted based on their normalized milliequivalent percentages.
*   Outer sectors are for additional parameters.

**Key Hydrochemical Processes Shown:**

*   **Dissolution:** Simple dissolution of minerals leads to characteristic water types.
*   **Ion Exchange:** Cations in the water are exchanged for cations adsorbed on clay minerals.
*   **Conmixing:** Mixing of different groundwater bodies.
*   **Evaporation:** Concentrates dissolved solids.

#### 4.3 U.S. Salinity Diagram (or Water Quality Diagram)

*   **Purpose:** Primarily used to evaluate the suitability of irrigation water. It plots the relative proportions of major cations and anions against salinity (measured as Electrical Conductivity, EC) and the Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR).
*   **Principles:**
    *   Uses two lower triangles (similar to Hill-Piper for cations and anions) but plots them on the same axis.
    *   **Cation Triangle:** Apex for $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+}$, left base for $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, right base for $\text{Mg}^{2+}$.
    *   **Anion Triangle:** Apex for $\text{Cl}^{-}$, left base for $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$, right base for $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$.
    *   **Salinity Classes (EC):** Lines representing different EC ranges ($\text{C1}$ - Low, $\text{C2}$ - Medium, $\text{C3}$ - High, $\text{C4}$ - Very High) are superimposed.
    *   **Sodium Hazard Classes (SAR):** Lines representing different SAR ranges ($\text{S1}$ - Low, $\text{S2}$ - Medium, $\text{S3}$ - High, $\text{S4}$ - Very High) are superimposed.
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **Salinity Hazard:** High EC can lead to salt accumulation in the soil, harming plant growth.
    *   **Sodium Hazard:** High SAR can degrade soil structure by dispersing clay particles, reducing infiltration and aeration.
    *   **Water Quality Classes:** The diagram classifies water into four classes (C1-S1, C1-S2, ..., C4-S4) based on a combination of salinity and sodium hazard.

**Diagram Structure (Conceptual):**

*   The x-axis represents relative anion percentages, and the y-axis represents relative cation percentages.
*   Points are plotted based on their normalized milliequivalent percentages.
*   EC classes are zones, and SAR classes are zones, often forming a grid across the diagram.

**Irrigation Water Quality Classification:**

*   **C1-S1 (Excellent):** Low salinity, low sodium. Suitable for most soils and crops.
*   **C2-S2 (Good):** Medium salinity, medium sodium. Requires good drainage and moderate management.
*   **C3-S3 (Permissible):** High salinity, high sodium. Requires excellent drainage and careful management. Generally not suitable for fine-textured soils.
*   **C4-S4 (Unsuitable):** Very high salinity, very high sodium. Unsuitable for irrigation in most cases.

**Important Considerations for Irrigation:**

*   **Soil Type:** Fine-textured soils are more susceptible to sodium effects.
*   **Climate:** Arid climates with high evaporation increase salt accumulation risk.
*   **Crop Type:** Different crops have varying tolerances to salinity and sodium.
*   **Drainage:** Adequate drainage is crucial for flushing accumulated salts.

### 5. Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR)

*   **Purpose:** A measure of the relative proportion of sodium to calcium and magnesium in irrigation water. It indicates the likelihood of sodium replacing calcium and magnesium on soil exchange sites.
*   **Definition:** SAR is calculated as:

$$ \text{SAR} = \frac{[\text{Na}^{+}]}{\sqrt{\frac{[\text{Ca}^{2+}] + [\text{Mg}^{2+}]}{2}}} $$

*   **Units:** Concentrations of ions must be in $\text{meq/L}$.
*   **Significance:** High SAR values in irrigation water can lead to:
    *   **Soil Dispersal:** Sodium ions attach to clay particles, pushing apart the soil structure.
    *   **Reduced Permeability:** Dispersed clays block pore spaces, hindering water infiltration and drainage.
    *   **Poor Aeration:** Reduced pore space limits oxygen availability to plant roots.
    *   **Increased Erosion:** Degraded soil structure is more prone to erosion.

**Example Calculation:**

A water sample has the following ionic concentrations in $\text{meq/L}$:
$\text{Na}^{+} = 3.0$, $\text{Ca}^{2+} = 2.0$, $\text{Mg}^{2+} = 1.5$

Calculate the SAR.

**Answer:**

$$ \text{SAR} = \frac{3.0}{\sqrt{\frac{2.0 + 1.5}{2}}} = \frac{3.0}{\sqrt{\frac{3.5}{2}}} = \frac{3.0}{\sqrt{1.75}} = \frac{3.0}{1.32} \approx 2.27 $$

This SAR value (2.27) falls into the low sodium hazard category.

---

### Key Points to Remember:

*   **Units are Crucial:** Always convert concentrations to $\text{meq/L}$ for plotting on most hydrochemical diagrams.
*   **Diagram Purpose:** Understand what each diagram is designed to show (water type, irrigation suitability, etc.).
*   **Combined Interpretation:** Use diagrams in conjunction with other water quality parameters (TDS, pH) for a holistic understanding.
*   **SAR & Soil Health:** High SAR is a major concern for irrigation, impacting soil structure and permeability.
*   **Well Design Impact:** Water chemistry directly influences material selection for well casing, pumps, and the need for water treatment.

### Practice Questions/Exercises:

1.  **Conversion Practice:** A water sample contains $50 \text{ mg/L}$ of $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$. Calculate its concentration in $\text{meq/L}$. (Atomic weight of S = 32.07, O = 16.00, Valency of $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-} = 2$).
2.  **Hill-Piper Plotting:** A groundwater sample yields the following results in $\text{meq/L}$: $\text{Ca}^{2+} = 3.5$, $\text{Mg}^{2+} = 1.0$, $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+} = 2.5$, $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-} = 1.0$, $\text{Cl}^{-} = 1.5$, $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-} = 4.5$. Calculate the percentages for plotting and identify the likely dominant cation and anion groups.
3.  **SAR Calculation:** Calculate the SAR for a water sample with the following ionic concentrations: $\text{Na}^{+} = 5.0 \text{ meq/L}$, $\text{Ca}^{2+} = 1.5 \text{ meq/L}$, $\text{Mg}^{2+} = 2.0 \text{ meq/L}$.
4.  **US Salinity Diagram Interpretation:** If a water sample plots in the C3-S2 region of the U.S. Salinity Diagram, what does this indicate about its suitability for irrigation in terms of salinity and sodium hazard?

---

### Answers to Practice Questions:

1.  **Conversion Practice:**
    *   Molecular Weight of $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-} = 32.07 + (4 \times 16.00) = 32.07 + 64.00 = 96.07 \text{ g/mol}$
    *   Equivalent Weight of $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-} = \frac{96.07}{2} = 48.035 \text{ g/eq}$
    *   $\text{meq/L of SO}_{4}^{2-} = \frac{50 \text{ mg/L}}{48.035 \text{ mg/meq}} \approx 1.04 \text{ meq/L}$

2.  **Hill-Piper Plotting:**
    *   **Total Cations:** $3.5 + 1.0 + 2.5 = 7.0 \text{ meq/L}$
        *   $\text{Ca}^{2+}$%: $(3.5 / 7.0) * 100 = 50\%$
        *   $\text{Mg}^{2+}$%: $(1.0 / 7.0) * 100 = 14.3\%$
        *   $\text{Na}^{+}+\text{K}^{+}$%: $(2.5 / 7.0) * 100 = 35.7\%$
    *   **Total Anions:** $1.0 + 1.5 + 4.5 = 7.0 \text{ meq/L}$
        *   $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$%: $(1.0 / 7.0) * 100 = 14.3\%$
        *   $\text{Cl}^{-}$%: $(1.5 / 7.0) * 100 = 21.4\%$
        *   $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$%: $(4.5 / 7.0) * 100 = 64.3\%$
    *   **Dominant Cation Group:** $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (50%)
    *   **Dominant Anion Group:** $\text{HCO}_{3}^{-}$ (64.3%)
    *   This suggests a $\text{Ca-HCO}_3$ type water.

3.  **SAR Calculation:**
    $$ \text{SAR} = \frac{5.0}{\sqrt{\frac{1.5 + 2.0}{2}}} = \frac{5.0}{\sqrt{\frac{3.5}{2}}} = \frac{5.0}{\sqrt{1.75}} = \frac{5.0}{1.32} \approx 3.79 $$

4.  **US Salinity Diagram Interpretation:**
    *   **C3 (High Salinity):** The water has a high concentration of dissolved salts. This means it could cause salinity problems for crops if not managed carefully, especially in soils with poor drainage or in arid climates.
    *   **S2 (Medium Sodium Hazard):** The relative proportion of sodium to calcium and magnesium is moderate. This suggests a medium risk of soil structure degradation. Good drainage and moderate management practices would be recommended.
