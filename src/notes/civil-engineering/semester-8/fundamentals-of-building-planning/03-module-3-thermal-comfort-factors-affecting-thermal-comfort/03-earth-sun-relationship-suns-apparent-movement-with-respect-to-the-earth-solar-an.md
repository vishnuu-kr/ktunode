# Fundamentals of Building Planning: Module 3 - Thermal Comfort: Factors Affecting Thermal Comfort

## Topic: Earth-Sun Relationship: Sun’s Apparent Movement with Respect to the Earth. Solar Angles

This module delves into the fundamental principles governing the interaction between the sun and the Earth, a critical aspect of understanding and achieving thermal comfort in buildings. By comprehending the sun's apparent movement and the associated solar angles, architects and planners can effectively design buildings that optimize solar heat gain in winter for warmth and minimize it in summer to prevent overheating.

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Understand the Earth's axial tilt and its role in seasonal variations.
*   Explain the apparent movement of the sun from the perspective of an observer on Earth.
*   Define and differentiate between key solar angles: altitude angle, azimuth angle, hour angle, declination angle, and angle of incidence.
*   Explain how these solar angles change throughout the day and year.
*   Understand the significance of these angles in influencing solar radiation received by a building surface.

### 1. The Earth-Sun Relationship: A Cosmic Dance

The apparent movement of the sun across the sky is not due to the sun moving around the Earth, but rather the Earth's rotation and its revolution around the sun.

#### 1.1 Earth's Axial Tilt

*   **Definition:** The Earth's axis of rotation is tilted at approximately **23.5 degrees** relative to its orbital plane around the sun (the ecliptic plane).
*   **Significance:** This axial tilt is the primary reason for the **seasons**.
    *   **Summer Solstice (around June 21st):** The Northern Hemisphere is tilted towards the sun, resulting in longer days and more direct solar radiation, leading to warmer temperatures.
    *   **Winter Solstice (around December 21st):** The Northern Hemisphere is tilted away from the sun, resulting in shorter days and less direct solar radiation, leading to colder temperatures.
    *   **Equinoxes (around March 20th and September 22nd):** Neither hemisphere is tilted towards or away from the sun. Days and nights are roughly equal in length globally, and solar radiation is more evenly distributed.

#### 1.2 Earth's Rotation and Revolution

*   **Rotation:** The Earth rotates on its axis approximately every **24 hours**. This rotation causes the **daily cycle of sunrise and sunset**, making the sun appear to rise in the east and set in the west.
*   **Revolution:** The Earth revolves around the sun approximately every **365.25 days**. This revolution, combined with the axial tilt, causes the **yearly cycle of seasons**.

### 2. Apparent Movement of the Sun

From the perspective of an observer on Earth, the sun's path across the sky changes daily and seasonally.

#### 2.1 Daily Apparent Movement

*   **Sunrise:** The sun appears on the eastern horizon.
*   **Midday:** The sun reaches its highest point in the sky.
*   **Sunset:** The sun disappears on the western horizon.
*   **Path:** The path of the sun across the sky is an arc. The shape and height of this arc depend on the latitude of the observer and the time of year.

#### 2.2 Seasonal Apparent Movement

*   **Summer:** The sun's path is higher in the sky, and it rises and sets further north (in the Northern Hemisphere) and south (in the Southern Hemisphere) of due east and west. Days are longer.
*   **Winter:** The sun's path is lower in the sky, and it rises and sets closer to due east and west. Days are shorter.
*   **Spring/Autumn Equinoxes:** The sun's path is at an intermediate height, and it rises and sets almost due east and west.

### 3. Solar Angles: Quantifying Solar Radiation

Solar angles are crucial for calculating the amount of solar radiation that strikes a surface at a particular time and location. Understanding these angles allows for precise design decisions related to shading devices, window placement, and building orientation.

#### 3.1 Altitude Angle (or Elevation Angle)

*   **Definition:** The angle between the horizontal plane and the line connecting the observer to the sun. It represents how high the sun is in the sky.
*   **Range:** 0° (on the horizon) to 90° (directly overhead).
*   **Significance:** A higher altitude angle means more direct and intense solar radiation.

#### 3.2 Azimuth Angle

*   **Definition:** The angle between the true north (or south, depending on convention) and the projection of the line connecting the observer to the sun onto the horizontal plane. It indicates the sun's horizontal direction.
*   **Convention:**
    *   **North:** 0°
    *   **East:** 90°
    *   **South:** 180°
    *   **West:** 270°
    *   (Some conventions use South as 0° and measure East as positive and West as negative.)
*   **Significance:** The azimuth angle is critical for understanding which facades of a building are exposed to direct sunlight at different times of the day.

#### 3.3 Declination Angle (δ)

*   **Definition:** The angle between the sun's rays and the plane of the Earth's equator. It varies from approximately +23.5° on the summer solstice to -23.5° on the winter solstice, and is 0° on the equinoxes.
*   **Significance:** It accounts for the Earth's axial tilt and directly influences the sun's altitude at noon and the length of the day.

#### 3.4 Hour Angle (ω)

*   **Definition:** The angle representing the time of day relative to solar noon. It is measured westward from the meridian of the observer.
*   **Values:**
    *   **Solar Noon:** 0°
    *   **1 hour before solar noon:** -15°
    *   **1 hour after solar noon:** +15°
*   **Calculation:** The Earth rotates 15° per hour (360° / 24 hours).
*   **Significance:** It directly affects the intensity of solar radiation received by a surface, as it changes the angle at which the sun's rays strike.

#### 3.5 Angle of Incidence (θ)

*   **Definition:** The angle between the sun's rays and the **normal** (a line perpendicular) to a surface. This is arguably the most important solar angle for building design.
*   **Significance:** Solar radiation intensity on a surface is **maximum when the angle of incidence is 0°** (i.e., the sun's rays are perpendicular to the surface). As the angle of incidence increases, the intensity of radiation decreases because the same amount of energy is spread over a larger area.
*   **Calculation:** This angle is derived from the altitude angle, azimuth angle, latitude, and the orientation of the surface.

### 4. How Solar Angles Influence Building Design

*   **Orientation:** Buildings should be oriented to take advantage of desirable solar gains (e.g., south-facing facades in winter for passive heating in the Northern Hemisphere) and minimize undesirable gains (e.g., east and west-facing facades in summer to avoid morning and afternoon heat gain).
*   **Shading Devices:** Overhangs, louvers, and fins can be designed based on solar angles to block high-angle summer sun while allowing low-angle winter sun to penetrate.
*   **Window Placement and Size:** The placement and size of windows on different facades are determined by the expected solar exposure at various times of the day and year.
*   **Surface Properties:** Understanding the angle of incidence helps in selecting appropriate surface materials and coatings for roofs and walls to manage solar heat absorption.

### Practice Questions:

1.  What is the primary reason for the Earth's seasons?
2.  Define the altitude angle and explain its significance for solar heat gain.
3.  If solar noon is at 12:00 PM, what is the hour angle at 2:00 PM?
4.  Explain why the angle of incidence is a critical factor in determining the solar heat received by a building facade.
5.  Describe how the apparent path of the sun differs between summer and winter in the Northern Hemisphere.

---

### Answers:

1.  The primary reason for the Earth's seasons is its axial tilt of approximately 23.5 degrees relative to its orbital plane around the sun.
2.  The altitude angle (or elevation angle) is the angle between the horizontal plane and the line connecting the observer to the sun. Its significance lies in the fact that a higher altitude angle generally corresponds to more direct and intense solar radiation, leading to greater solar heat gain.
3.  The hour angle is measured westward from solar noon, with each hour corresponding to 15 degrees. Therefore, at 2:00 PM (2 hours after solar noon), the hour angle is +30 degrees (2 hours * 15°/hour).
4.  The angle of incidence is the angle between the sun's rays and the normal to a surface. Solar radiation intensity on a surface is inversely proportional to the cosine of the angle of incidence (or directly proportional to the cosine of the angle between the sun's rays and the surface normal). When the angle of incidence is small (rays are nearly perpendicular), the radiation is concentrated over a smaller area, resulting in higher heat gain. As the angle of incidence increases, the radiation is spread over a larger area, reducing the intensity and heat gain.
5.  In the Northern Hemisphere during summer, the sun's apparent path is higher in the sky. It rises further north of due east, reaches a higher maximum altitude at noon, and sets further north of due west. Days are also longer. In winter, the sun's apparent path is lower in the sky, rising and setting closer to due east and west, and its maximum altitude at noon is lower. Days are shorter.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Earth's axial tilt (23.5°) is the cause of seasons.**
*   **Apparent solar movement is due to Earth's rotation and revolution.**
*   **Altitude angle** measures how high the sun is.
*   **Azimuth angle** indicates the sun's horizontal direction.
*   **Hour angle** tracks time relative to solar noon (15° per hour).
*   **Angle of incidence (θ)** is crucial: solar radiation is maximum when θ = 0° (rays perpendicular to surface).
*   Understanding these angles is fundamental for passive solar design and controlling thermal comfort.
