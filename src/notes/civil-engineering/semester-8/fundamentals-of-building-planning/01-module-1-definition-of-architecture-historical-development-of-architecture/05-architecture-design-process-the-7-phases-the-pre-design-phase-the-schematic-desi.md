# FUNDAMENTALS OF BUILDING PLANNING

## Module 1: Definition of Architecture – Historical Development of Architecture

### Topic: Architecture Design Process: The 7 Phases

---

### **Introduction: The Essence of Architecture**

Architecture is more than just erecting buildings; it's the art and science of designing and constructing buildings and other physical structures. It involves considering aesthetics, functionality, sustainability, and the impact on human well-being and the environment. The architecture design process is a systematic approach that transforms a client's needs and aspirations into a tangible built environment.

---

### **Learning Outcomes:**

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Identify and describe each of the seven phases of the architecture design process.
*   Explain the purpose and key activities within each phase.
*   Understand the deliverables and client involvement at each stage.
*   Recognize the importance of each phase in the overall success of a building project.
*   Differentiate between the various phases and their sequential nature.

### **The 7 Phases of the Architecture Design Process**

The architecture design process is a cyclical and iterative journey, typically broken down into seven distinct phases. While the exact naming or breakdown might vary slightly in practice, these seven phases represent the core stages from inception to completion.

#### **Phase 1: The Pre-Design Phase (also known as Programming or Conceptualization)**

This is the foundational phase where the project's vision, goals, and constraints are defined. It's about understanding *why* the building is needed and *what* it needs to achieve.

*   **Purpose:** To clearly define the project's scope, objectives, and requirements. To establish the feasibility of the project and identify potential challenges.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Client Consultation:** Extensive discussions with the client to understand their needs, desires, budget, and timeline.
    *   **Needs Assessment (Programming):** Detailing the functional requirements of the building – what activities will take place, how many people will use it, what spaces are needed, etc. This often involves creating a "program" document.
    *   **Site Analysis:** Evaluating the chosen site, considering its topography, climate, access, surrounding environment, zoning regulations, and any existing structures.
    *   **Feasibility Studies:** Assessing the financial, technical, and legal viability of the project.
    *   **Budget Definition:** Establishing a preliminary budget range for the project.
    *   **Establishing Project Team:** Identifying and engaging other consultants (e.g., engineers, landscape architects) if necessary.
*   **Deliverables:** Program document, site analysis report, preliminary budget, project brief.
*   **Client Involvement:** High. The client is heavily involved in defining the project's core aspects.
*   **Example:** A school district wants a new elementary school. In the pre-design phase, the architect meets with administrators, teachers, and parents to understand the number of students, desired classroom sizes, special education needs, administrative requirements, and the specific site characteristics.

---

#### **Phase 2: The Schematic Design Phase (SD)**

This phase translates the program and requirements into a preliminary design concept. It's about exploring ideas and developing a general layout and form.

*   **Purpose:** To develop a basic design concept and illustrate the project's scale and relationship between spaces.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Developing Conceptual Sketches:** Creating hand sketches, bubble diagrams, and basic floor plans to explore different spatial arrangements.
    *   **Preliminary Massing Studies:** Developing 3D models or sketches to understand the building's overall form and volume on the site.
    *   **Identifying Major Building Systems:** Preliminary decisions on structural systems, HVAC, and electrical systems.
    *   **Preliminary Cost Estimates:** Providing a more refined cost estimate based on the schematic design.
    *   **Client Review and Approval:** Presenting the conceptual designs to the client for feedback and approval.
*   **Deliverables:** Preliminary floor plans, elevations, sections, conceptual site plan, basic 3D models, updated cost estimate.
*   **Client Involvement:** High. Client feedback is crucial for shaping the direction of the design.
*   **Example:** For the elementary school, the architect creates several conceptual layouts showing different classroom arrangements, the placement of common areas (gym, library), and how the building sits on the site. They might present 3D massing models to illustrate the building's appearance.

---

#### **Phase 3: The Design Development Phase (DD)**

This phase refines the approved schematic design into a more detailed and coordinated plan. Technical aspects and material selections become more specific.

*   **Purpose:** To further develop the approved schematic design, detailing the building's components, systems, and materials.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Detailed Space Planning:** Refining room layouts, dimensions, and adjacencies.
    *   **Developing Structural Systems:** Working with structural engineers to finalize the structural design.
    *   **Selecting Major Building Systems:** Detailing HVAC, plumbing, and electrical systems with the relevant engineers.
    *   **Material Selection:** Choosing specific materials for finishes, facades, roofing, etc.
    *   **Updating Cost Estimates:** Providing a more accurate cost estimate based on the detailed design.
    *   **Coordination with Consultants:** Ensuring all consultant designs are integrated and coordinated.
    *   **Client Review and Approval:** Presenting the developed design for client sign-off.
*   **Deliverables:** Detailed floor plans, elevations, sections, preliminary structural drawings, MEP (Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing) system layouts, material specifications, refined cost estimate.
*   **Client Involvement:** Moderate. Client provides input on material choices and specific features.
*   **Example:** The elementary school's design development involves finalizing the exact dimensions of each classroom, specifying the type of flooring, wall finishes, window types, and the detailed layout of the HVAC ducts and electrical wiring, all coordinated with the respective engineers.

---

#### **Phase 4: The Construction Documents Phase (CD)**

This is the most technically intensive phase, where all the detailed drawings and specifications necessary for construction are produced. These documents form the legal basis for the construction contract.

*   **Purpose:** To produce a complete set of construction drawings and specifications that accurately describe the work to be performed by the contractor.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Producing Detailed Architectural Drawings:** Including final floor plans, elevations, sections, details, and schedules (e.g., door schedules, window schedules).
    *   **Producing Detailed Engineering Drawings:** Structural, MEP, civil, and other engineering disciplines provide their final construction drawings.
    *   **Developing Specifications:** Writing detailed written requirements for materials, workmanship, and quality standards.
    *   **Finalizing Cost Estimates:** Often used for bidding purposes.
    *   **Coordination and Quality Control:** Ensuring all drawings and specifications are consistent and free of errors.
    *   **Client Review:** Final review and approval of all documents before they are issued for bidding.
*   **Deliverables:** Complete set of architectural and engineering drawings (blueprints), project specifications, final cost estimate.
*   **Client Involvement:** Low to Moderate. Primarily for final review and approval.
*   **Example:** The construction documents for the elementary school will include precise drawings showing every wall, floor, ceiling, window, and door, along with detailed instructions on how to build them, the specific materials to be used (e.g., brick type, paint color, insulation R-value), and the performance requirements for all building systems.

---

#### **Phase 5: The Building Permit Phase**

Before construction can begin, the detailed construction documents must be submitted to the local authorities for review and approval.

*   **Purpose:** To obtain legal permission from the relevant authorities to proceed with construction, ensuring compliance with building codes, zoning ordinances, and other regulations.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Submitting Construction Documents:** Forwarding the complete set of drawings and specifications to the building department.
    *   **Responding to Review Comments:** Addressing any queries or required revisions from the building officials.
    *   **Obtaining the Building Permit:** Receiving the official permit to begin construction.
*   **Deliverables:** Approved building permit.
*   **Client Involvement:** Low. Typically handled by the architect or contractor.
*   **Example:** The elementary school's construction documents are submitted to the city's building department. They review the plans for fire safety compliance, structural integrity, accessibility, and adherence to zoning laws. The architect may need to make minor modifications based on their feedback before the permit is issued.

---

#### **Phase 6: The Bidding and Negotiation Phase**

Once the permit is secured, the project is put out to bid to potential contractors. The architect typically assists the client in evaluating bids and selecting a contractor.

*   **Purpose:** To solicit bids from qualified contractors, evaluate those bids, and negotiate a contract for the construction of the project.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Distributing Construction Documents:** Providing the documents to pre-qualified contractors.
    *   **Issuing Addenda:** Clarifying or modifying the construction documents based on contractor questions.
    *   **Receiving and Analyzing Bids:** Evaluating proposals based on price, experience, and proposed schedule.
    *   **Negotiating Contract Terms:** Discussing and finalizing the construction contract with the selected contractor.
    *   **Awarding the Contract:** Officially hiring the contractor.
*   **Deliverables:** Executed construction contract.
*   **Client Involvement:** High. The client makes the final decision on which contractor to hire.
*   **Example:** Several construction companies bid on building the elementary school. The architect helps the school district review each bid, interview contractors, and negotiate the final contract price and schedule.

---

#### **Phase 7: The Construction Administration Phase (CA)**

This is the final phase, where the architect oversees the construction process to ensure it adheres to the design intent and specifications.

*   **Purpose:** To observe the construction process, interpret the construction documents, and ensure the project is built according to the approved plans and specifications.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Site Visits and Observations:** Regularly visiting the construction site to monitor progress and quality.
    *   **Reviewing Shop Drawings and Submittals:** Checking the contractor's proposed materials and methods.
    *   **Responding to Requests for Information (RFIs):** Clarifying any questions from the contractor regarding the construction documents.
    *   **Issuing Change Orders:** Managing any modifications to the original design or contract.
    *   **Approving Progress Payments:** Verifying work completed and recommending payment to the contractor.
    *   **Punch List Creation:** Identifying any minor deficiencies at the end of construction.
    *   **Final Project Closeout:** Ensuring all documentation is complete and the project is finalized.
*   **Deliverables:** Certificate of Substantial Completion, project closeout documents, as-built drawings (often).
*   **Client Involvement:** Moderate. Client receives regular updates and approves significant decisions.
*   **Example:** During the construction of the elementary school, the architect visits the site weekly to check that the walls are being built correctly, the plumbing is installed as per the drawings, and the materials meet the specified quality. They review submittals for window manufacturers and approve the contractor's payment applications.

---

### **Key Concepts to Remember:**

*   **Iterative Process:** The design process is not strictly linear. Feedback and new information can cause architects to revisit earlier stages.
*   **Collaboration:** Architecture is a collaborative effort involving clients, architects, engineers, contractors, and regulatory bodies.
*   **Deliverables:** Each phase produces specific documents and outcomes that guide the next stage.
*   **Client Communication:** Continuous and clear communication with the client is vital for project success.
*   **Budget and Schedule:** These are critical constraints that influence decisions throughout all phases.
*   **Codes and Regulations:** Compliance with building codes and local ordinances is paramount at every step.

### **Practice Questions:**

1.  Which phase focuses on defining the project's fundamental needs, goals, and site conditions?
    a) Schematic Design Phase
    b) Construction Documents Phase
    c) Pre-Design Phase
    d) Construction Administration Phase

2.  What is the primary output of the Construction Documents Phase?
    a) Preliminary sketches and massing models
    b) A building permit
    c) Detailed drawings and specifications for construction
    d) A program document

3.  During which phase does the architect typically review shop drawings and respond to RFIs from the contractor?
    a) Design Development Phase
    b) Bidding and Negotiation Phase
    c) Schematic Design Phase
    d) Construction Administration Phase

4.  The phase where conceptual ideas are translated into a more defined building form and spatial relationships is known as:
    a) Pre-Design Phase
    b) Schematic Design Phase
    c) Building Permit Phase
    d) Bidding and Negotiation Phase

5.  If a client wants to understand the projected cost of a project based on initial ideas, which phase would provide the most relevant information?
    a) Construction Administration Phase
    b) Construction Documents Phase
    c) Pre-Design Phase and Schematic Design Phase
    d) Building Permit Phase

---

### **Answers to Practice Questions:**

1.  **c) Pre-Design Phase**
    *   **Explanation:** The Pre-Design Phase is dedicated to understanding the project's purpose, requirements, and site.

2.  **c) Detailed drawings and specifications for construction**
    *   **Explanation:** The Construction Documents Phase generates the complete technical documentation needed for contractors to build the project.

3.  **d) Construction Administration Phase**
    *   **Explanation:** Construction Administration involves the architect's oversight of the construction process, including reviewing contractor submissions and clarifying design issues.

4.  **b) Schematic Design Phase**
    *   **Explanation:** Schematic design is about developing the initial concepts and overall layout of the building.

5.  **c) Pre-Design Phase and Schematic Design Phase**
    *   **Explanation:** Preliminary cost estimates are developed during the Pre-Design Phase and refined in the Schematic Design Phase.

---

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **The phases are sequential but can be iterative.** Don't think of them as strictly one after the other; there's often overlap and revisiting.
*   **Clear communication is the backbone of a successful project.**
*   **Each phase builds upon the previous one.** Errors or omissions in earlier phases can be costly to fix later.
*   **Client involvement is crucial.** Their feedback and decisions shape the project at key milestones.
*   **Understanding these phases is fundamental to comprehending the entire building process.**
