# WATER AND AIR QUALITY MANAGEMENT

## Module 3: Water Resources and Quality Management in India

### Topic: GAP and National River Action Programme

---

### 1. Introduction to Water Quality Management in India

*   **Definition:** Water quality management refers to the process of controlling and improving the quality of water to make it suitable for various uses, such as drinking, irrigation, industrial processes, and supporting aquatic ecosystems.
*   **Importance in India:**
    *   **Vast Population:** India has the second-largest population in the world, leading to high demand for potable water.
    *   **Agricultural Dependence:** Agriculture, the backbone of the Indian economy, heavily relies on water resources, making water quality crucial for crop production and food security.
    *   **Diverse Water Sources:** India has a variety of water sources – rivers, lakes, groundwater, and monsoon rains – each facing different quality challenges.
    *   **Pollution Challenges:** Growing industrialization, urbanization, agricultural runoff, and untreated sewage discharge significantly impact water quality.
    *   **Health Concerns:** Contaminated water is a major cause of waterborne diseases, posing a significant public health challenge.

---

### 2. Water Availability in India: An Overview

*   **General Scenario:** Despite having significant water resources, India faces a looming water crisis due to:
    *   **Uneven Distribution:** Spatial and temporal variations in rainfall (heavy monsoon dependence).
    *   **Over-exploitation:** Excessive withdrawal of groundwater for irrigation and domestic use.
    *   **Pollution:** Deterioration of water quality in many surface and groundwater sources.
    *   **Growing Demand:** Increasing population and economic development drive up water demand.
*   **Key Water Resources:**
    *   **Surface Water:** Rivers (Ganga, Brahmaputra, Godavari, Krishna, Yamuna, etc.), lakes, reservoirs.
    *   **Groundwater:** A significant source, particularly for irrigation, but facing depletion and contamination issues.
    *   **Rainfall:** The primary source, but highly seasonal and variable.

### 3. The Ganga Action Plan (GAP)

**Learning Outcome Covered:** Understanding the objectives, scope, and achievements/limitations of the Ganga Action Plan.

*   **Background:**
    *   Launched in **1985** by the Ministry of Environment and Forests.
    *   The **first major river cleaning project** in India.
    *   Aimed at reducing pollution and improving the water quality of the river Ganga.
    *   Recognized the Ganga as a vital national resource and a symbol of Indian culture.
*   **Objectives of GAP:**
    *   To **intercept, divert, and treat** all significant polluting wastewater effluents that flow into the river Ganga.
    *   To **prevent the discharge of untreated domestic and industrial wastewater** directly into the river.
    *   To **reduce the pollution load** in the river to meet the desired water quality standards for bathing.
    *   To **promote public awareness** and participation in river conservation.
    *   To **undertake afforestation** and soil conservation measures in the Ganga basin.
    *   To **improve the sanitation conditions** in the towns and cities along the river.
*   **Phases of GAP:**
    *   **GAP Phase-I (1985-2000):** Focused on 25 Class-I cities in three states (Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar).
        *   **Key Activities:** Installation of Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs), sewerage systems, low-cost sanitation facilities, electric crematoria, and public awareness campaigns.
        *   **Scope:** Covered 26 towns.
    *   **GAP Phase-II (1993-2005):** Expanded to cover polluted stretches in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and West Bengal, including tributaries like the Yamuna and Damodar.
        *   **Key Activities:** Similar to Phase-I, with increased focus on industrial pollution control and monitoring.
        *   **Scope:** Covered 59 towns.
*   **Key Strategies and Interventions:**
    *   **Pollution Abatement:**
        *   **Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs):** Construction and operationalization of STPs to treat municipal wastewater before discharge.
        *   **Interception and Diversion (I&D) Works:** Diverting sewage from the river through sewerage systems to STPs.
        *   **Low-Cost Sanitation (LCS):** Promoting sanitary latrines to prevent open defecation into rivers.
        *   **Industrial Pollution Control:** Enforcing effluent standards and promoting cleaner production technologies for industries.
        *   **Solid Waste Management:** Improving waste disposal practices to prevent solid waste from entering the river.
    *   **Non-Point Source Pollution Control:**
        *   **Afforestation:** Planting trees in the catchment areas to reduce soil erosion.
        *   **Soil Conservation:** Implementing measures to prevent siltation of the river.
    *   **Public Awareness and Participation:**
        *   Organizing campaigns, workshops, and educational programs.
        *   Involving local communities and NGOs.
    *   **Monitoring and Evaluation:**
        *   Regular water quality monitoring.
        *   Assessment of the effectiveness of pollution control measures.
*   **Achievements of GAP:**
    *   **Significant Reduction in Pollution Load:** A considerable amount of sewage and industrial effluents were diverted and treated.
    *   **Improved Water Quality in Stretches:** In some stretches, water quality for bathing improved.
    *   **Increased Awareness:** Raised public awareness about river pollution and conservation.
    *   **Development of Infrastructure:** Created significant infrastructure for wastewater treatment and sanitation.
    *   **Foundation for Future Programs:** Paved the way for larger, more comprehensive river cleaning initiatives.
*   **Limitations and Challenges of GAP:**
    *   **Incomplete Coverage:** Not all polluting sources were fully addressed, especially non-point sources and smaller towns.
    *   **Operational Deficiencies:** Many STPs were not operated to their full capacity due to lack of funds, power, or skilled personnel.
    *   **Inadequate Funding:** Funding was often insufficient to cover the vast scale of the problem.
    *   **Low Public Participation:** Sustained public involvement remained a challenge.
    *   **Enforcement Issues:** Strict enforcement of environmental regulations for industries and municipalities was often weak.
    *   **Sustainability:** Ensuring the long-term sustainability of the treated water and the operational maintenance of infrastructure was difficult.
    *   **Unmet Water Quality Standards:** The ultimate goal of achieving bathing water quality standards was not fully met across the entire river.

### 4. National River Action Programme (NRAP)

**Learning Outcome Covered:** Understanding the rationale, objectives, scope, and key components of the National River Action Programme.

*   **Background:**
    *   Launched in **1992** as a **centrally sponsored scheme**.
    *   **Expanded upon the principles and experiences of GAP.**
    *   Aims to **restore the water quality of polluted rivers** across the country.
    *   Covers a **wider range of rivers** beyond the Ganga.
*   **Rationale for NRAP:**
    *   The problem of river pollution is **widespread** and affects many other major rivers in India.
    *   GAP provided valuable **lessons learned** that could be applied to other river basins.
    *   A **comprehensive national approach** is needed for effective river rejuvenation.
*   **Objectives of NRAP:**
    *   To **prevent the discharge of untreated sewage and other pollutants** into rivers.
    *   To **undertake pollution abatement works** in identified polluted stretches of rivers.
    *   To **promote sustainable river management practices.**
    *   To **improve water quality** to a level suitable for designated uses.
    *   To **enhance public awareness and participation** in river conservation.
    *   To **support the development of river basin management plans.**
*   **Scope of NRAP:**
    *   Initially, it covered **polluted stretches of 19 major rivers** in 14 states.
    *   The program has been expanded over time to include more rivers and tributaries.
    *   **Key Rivers Covered (Examples):** Yamuna, Gomti, Satluj, Beas, Sutlej, Cauvery, Periyar, Sabarmati, Musi, Krishna, Godavari, Narmada, etc.
*   **Key Components and Strategies of NRAP:**
    *   **Pollution Abatement Works:**
        *   **Sewerage Schemes:** Laying new sewers and repairing existing ones.
        *   **Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs):** Construction, rehabilitation, and upgrading of STPs.
        *   **Interception and Diversion (I&D) Works:** Diverting sewage to treatment facilities.
        *   **Low-Cost Sanitation (LCS):** Promoting sustainable sanitation solutions at the household level.
        *   **Solid Waste Management:** Improving collection and disposal of solid waste.
    *   **Industrial Pollution Control:**
        *   **Enforcement of Effluent Standards:** Ensuring industries comply with pollution discharge norms.
        *   **Pollution Prevention and Control (PPC) Schemes:** Encouraging industries to adopt cleaner production practices.
        *   **Common Effluent Treatment Plants (CETPs):** For clusters of small and medium-sized industries.
    *   **Environmental Upgradation:**
        *   **Riverfront development:** Beautification and public access to river banks.
        *   **Afforestation and Soil Conservation:** In the river basin.
        *   **Wetland conservation:** Protecting and restoring wetlands within the basin.
    *   **Monitoring and Evaluation:**
        *   **Water Quality Monitoring:** Regular sampling and analysis of water quality parameters.
        *   **Performance Monitoring:** Tracking the progress and effectiveness of implemented projects.
    *   **Capacity Building and Awareness:**
        *   Training programs for personnel involved in water quality management.
        *   Public awareness campaigns and community mobilization.
*   **Funding Mechanism:**
    *   It is a **centrally sponsored scheme**, with funds shared between the Central Government and the State Governments.
    *   The Central Government typically contributes a major share.
*   **Relationship with GAP:**
    *   NRAP is a **broader and more encompassing program** that builds upon the lessons learned from GAP.
    *   GAP was the **pilot project**, and NRAP is the **national-level replication and expansion**.
    *   The focus on rivers is wider in NRAP.

### 5. Current Status and Future Directions

*   **Integration and Evolution:**
    *   GAP and NRAP have been instrumental in raising awareness and building infrastructure.
    *   However, the scale of pollution requires continuous efforts and integrated approaches.
    *   The **Namami Gange Programme** (launched in 2014) has superseded GAP and is now the umbrella program for Ganga rejuvenation, integrating various aspects of pollution control, conservation, and sustainable development.
    *   NRAP continues to be implemented for other polluted rivers.
*   **Key Challenges Remaining:**
    *   **Untreated Sewage:** Huge gap between sewage generation and treatment capacity.
    *   **Industrial Pollution:** Continued discharge of untreated or partially treated industrial effluents.
    *   **Non-Point Sources:** Agricultural runoff (pesticides, fertilizers) and urban runoff remain significant challenges.
    *   **Groundwater Contamination:** Pollution of groundwater resources.
    *   **Operational Sustainability:** Ensuring the long-term operation and maintenance of infrastructure.
    *   **Enforcement of Regulations:** Weak enforcement mechanisms.
    *   **Public Participation:** Sustaining active and meaningful public involvement.
    *   **Climate Change Impacts:** Altering water availability and exacerbating pollution issues.
*   **Future Directions:**
    *   **Integrated River Basin Management:** Holistic approach considering all aspects of the river basin.
    *   **Focus on Operation & Maintenance (O&M):** Ensuring STPs and other facilities are functional.
    *   **Decentralized Wastewater Treatment:** Exploring smaller, localized treatment solutions.
    *   **Promotion of Circular Economy:** Reuse of treated wastewater and sludge management.
    *   **Strengthening Monitoring and Enforcement:** Using technology (IoT, remote sensing) for better monitoring and stricter enforcement.
    *   **Community Engagement:** Empowering local communities in river management.
    *   **Addressing Non-Point Sources:** Implementing watershed management practices.
    *   **Interlinking of Rivers:** Potential benefits and challenges need careful consideration.
    *   **Inter-sectoral Coordination:** Collaboration between different government departments and agencies.

### 6. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What was the primary objective of the Ganga Action Plan (GAP)?
    *   a) To promote tourism along the Ganga river.
    *   b) To reduce pollution and improve the water quality of the river Ganga.
    *   c) To construct dams for hydropower generation on the Ganga.
    *   d) To establish irrigation projects using Ganga water.

**Answer:** b) To reduce pollution and improve the water quality of the river Ganga.

**Question 2:** Which of the following is NOT a key component of the National River Action Programme (NRAP)?
    *   a) Construction of Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs).
    *   b) Strict regulation of industrial effluent discharge.
    *   c) Development of large-scale recreational parks along all rivers.
    *   d) Promotion of low-cost sanitation in riverine communities.

**Answer:** c) Development of large-scale recreational parks along all rivers. (While riverfront development is part of NRAP, it's not the sole or primary focus, and the emphasis is on pollution control.)

**Question 3:** Explain the difference in scope between GAP and NRAP.

**Answer:** The Ganga Action Plan (GAP) was specifically focused on cleaning and rejuvenating the Ganga river. The National River Action Programme (NRAP), on the other hand, was launched as an expansion and a national-level program to address the pollution in various other major rivers across India, building on the lessons learned from GAP.

**Question 4:** Discuss at least two limitations faced by the Ganga Action Plan.

**Answer:** Two limitations of the Ganga Action Plan include:
    1.  **Operational Deficiencies of STPs:** Many Sewage Treatment Plants were not operated at full capacity due to issues like inadequate funding for maintenance, lack of skilled manpower, or unreliable power supply.
    2.  **Incomplete Coverage:** The plan did not fully address all polluting sources, particularly non-point sources like agricultural runoff and smaller towns, leading to a significant residual pollution load.

**Question 5:** What is the successor program to the Ganga Action Plan?
    *   a) National River Rejuvenation Program
    *   b) Namami Gange Programme
    *   c) Ganga Sagar Project
    *   d) Clean Ganga Initiative

**Answer:** b) Namami Gange Programme

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **GAP (1985):** India's first major river cleaning project, focused exclusively on the Ganga.
*   **NRAP (1992):** Centrally sponsored scheme to address pollution in other major rivers across India, building on GAP's experience.
*   **Key Interventions:** Both programs focused on Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs), interception & diversion (I&D), low-cost sanitation, and industrial pollution control.
*   **Limitations:** Operational issues with STPs, funding gaps, enforcement challenges, and incomplete coverage were common problems.
*   **Namami Gange Programme:** The current umbrella initiative for Ganga rejuvenation, integrating and expanding upon the goals of GAP.
*   **River Pollution is a National Challenge:** NRAP's existence highlights the widespread nature of this problem in India.
*   **Sustainability:** The long-term operational and financial sustainability of pollution control measures remains a critical challenge.
*   **Public Participation:** Active and sustained involvement of the public is crucial for the success of any river conservation effort.
