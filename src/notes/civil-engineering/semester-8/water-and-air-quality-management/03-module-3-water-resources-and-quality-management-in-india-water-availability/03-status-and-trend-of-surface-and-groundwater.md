# WATER AND AIR QUALITY MANAGEMENT

## Module 3: Water Resources and Quality Management in India

### Topic: Status and Trend of Surface and Groundwater

This module delves into the critical aspects of water availability in India, focusing on the current status and future trends of its surface and groundwater resources.

---

### 1. Status of Surface Water Resources in India

Surface water includes water bodies like rivers, lakes, ponds, and reservoirs. India is fortunate to have a vast network of rivers, but their availability and quality face significant challenges.

#### 1.1. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Surface Water Resources:** Water that is found on the Earth's surface, such as in rivers, lakes, streams, oceans, and glaciers.
*   **River Systems:** India's major river systems are the Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna, Indus, and Brahmaputra basins, along with numerous other peninsular rivers.
*   **Droughts:** Periods of abnormally dry weather sufficiently prolonged for the lack of precipitation to cause a serious hydrological imbalance.
*   **Floods:** An overflow of a large amount of water beyond its normal confines, especially over what is normally dry land.
*   **Water Quality:** The physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of water that determine its suitability for a particular use.
*   **Pollution:** The introduction of contaminants into natural environments that cause adverse change.

#### 1.2. Current Status of Surface Water

*   **Abundant but Unevenly Distributed:** India receives substantial rainfall, but its distribution is highly uneven across regions and seasons.
    *   **Monsoon Dependency:** Over 80% of the annual rainfall occurs during the four-month monsoon season (June to September).
    *   **Regional Disparities:** Some regions, like the Brahmaputra basin, receive excess water, leading to floods, while others, like Rajasthan and parts of the Deccan plateau, face severe water scarcity.
*   **Major River Systems:**
    *   **Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna:** These are the most significant river systems, supporting a large population and agricultural activities.
    *   **Indus System:** Primarily flows through the northern part of the country, with its waters allocated to different states.
    *   **Peninsular Rivers:** Rivers like Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, and Narmada are crucial for their respective regions.
*   **Water Bodies:**
    *   **Lakes and Ponds:** Significant natural and man-made lakes and ponds exist, but many are facing degradation due to pollution and encroachment.
    *   **Reservoirs:** Numerous reservoirs have been constructed for irrigation, hydropower, and drinking water supply. Their storage capacity is vital for managing water during dry periods.
*   **Challenges:**
    *   **Pollution:** A major concern across most rivers, stemming from untreated domestic sewage, industrial effluents, agricultural runoff, and religious practices.
    *   **Droughts and Floods:** Extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and intense, impacting water availability and causing significant economic losses.
    *   **Inter-State Water Disputes:** Sharing of river waters among states is often a contentious issue.
    *   **Climate Change Impacts:** Changes in rainfall patterns, increased evaporation, and glacial melt are affecting surface water availability.

#### 1.3. Examples

*   **Ganga River:** Despite being a lifeline, the Ganga is heavily polluted by sewage and industrial waste, especially in urban centers like Kanpur and Varanasi. Efforts are underway through the Namami Gange program to revive it.
*   **Drought-prone Regions:** States like Rajasthan, Gujarat, and parts of Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka frequently experience severe droughts, impacting agriculture and livelihoods.
*   **Flood-prone Regions:** Assam, Bihar, and Uttar Pradesh are regularly hit by devastating floods due to the overflowing Brahmaputra and Ganga rivers.

### 2. Trends in Surface Water Availability

The availability of surface water in India is influenced by a dynamic interplay of climatic, hydrological, and anthropogenic factors.

#### 2.1. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Water Yield:** The amount of water that flows in a river basin after accounting for evapotranspiration and other losses.
*   **Hydrological Cycle:** The continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth.
*   **Evapotranspiration:** The sum of evaporation and plant transpiration from the Earth's land and ocean surface to the atmosphere.
*   **Monsoon Variability:** Fluctuations in the intensity and duration of the Indian monsoon.

#### 2.2. Observed Trends

*   **Increasing Variability in Rainfall:** While the overall annual rainfall might not show a drastic decline in all regions, there's a clear trend of increased variability, with more intense rainfall events and longer dry spells.
*   **Impact of Urbanization and Land-Use Change:**
    *   **Increased Runoff:** Paved surfaces in urban areas reduce infiltration, leading to higher surface runoff and increased flood risk.
    *   **Deforestation:** Reduces the water retention capacity of the soil and can lead to increased erosion and siltation of rivers.
*   **Siltation of Reservoirs:** Increased soil erosion due to deforestation and poor land management leads to silt deposition in reservoirs, reducing their storage capacity over time.
*   **Impact of Climate Change:**
    *   **Extreme Weather Events:** Increased frequency and intensity of droughts and floods.
    *   **Glacial Melt:** While initial glacial melt might increase river flows, long-term impacts could lead to reduced flows as glaciers shrink.
*   **Pollution Trends:** Despite some efforts, the overall trend of river pollution remains a significant concern, impacting the usability of available surface water.

#### 2.3. Future Projections

*   **Continued Climate Change Impacts:** Projections suggest a further increase in temperature, greater variability in monsoon patterns, and potentially altered rainfall distribution, exacerbating water scarcity and flood risks in different regions.
*   **Growing Water Demand:** Increasing population, rapid urbanization, and agricultural expansion will continue to put immense pressure on surface water resources.
*   **Need for Integrated Water Management:** The trend points towards the necessity of integrated approaches that consider all aspects of the water cycle, from rainfall to downstream usage.

#### 2.4. Examples

*   **Increased Flood Intensity:** Recent years have seen more severe floods in parts of Kerala, Uttarakhand, and Assam, often attributed to extreme rainfall events and changes in land use.
*   **Decreasing Reservoir Storage:** Many reservoirs across India are experiencing a decline in their effective storage capacity due to siltation, impacting their ability to supply water for irrigation and power generation.

### 3. Status of Groundwater Resources in India

Groundwater is a vital source of water for drinking, irrigation, and industrial use in India, often considered more reliable than surface water in drought-prone areas.

#### 3.1. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Groundwater:** Water held underground in the soil or in pores and crevices in rock.
*   **Aquifer:** An underground layer of permeable rock, sediment, or soil from which groundwater can be extracted.
*   **Recharge:** The replenishment of groundwater by infiltration of precipitation, surface water, or irrigation water.
*   **Groundwater Depletion:** The rate at which groundwater is being extracted exceeds the rate at which it is being replenished.
*   **Groundwater Quality:** The suitability of groundwater for various uses, assessed through physical, chemical, and biological parameters.
*   **Over-exploitation:** Excessive withdrawal of groundwater beyond sustainable limits.

#### 3.2. Current Status of Groundwater

*   **Major Source of Irrigation:** Groundwater is the backbone of Indian agriculture, providing irrigation for about 60% of the irrigated area.
*   **Dominant Source for Rural Water Supply:** A significant portion of rural households rely on groundwater for drinking water.
*   **Varied Availability:** Groundwater availability varies significantly across different geological formations and regions.
    *   **Alluvial Plains:** Typically have abundant groundwater.
    *   **Hard Rock Areas:** Groundwater is generally less abundant and harder to extract.
*   **Over-exploitation is Widespread:** Many parts of India, particularly in the Indo-Gangetic plains and peninsular regions, are experiencing severe groundwater over-exploitation.
    *   **Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) Reports:** CGWB categorizes assessment units (blocks/taluks/mandals) based on the stage of groundwater development and dynamic ground water resources.
        *   **Over-exploited:** Units where the annual replenishable draft exceeds 100% of the annual replenishable groundwater recharge.
        *   **Critical:** Units where the stage of groundwater development is between 90% and 100%.
        *   **Semi-critical:** Units where the stage of groundwater development is between 70% and 90%.
        *   **Safe:** Units where the stage of groundwater development is less than 70%.
*   **Groundwater Quality Issues:**
    *   **Fluorosis:** High fluoride concentration in groundwater in certain regions.
    *   **Arsenic Contamination:** Particularly in West Bengal and Bihar.
    *   **Salinity Intrusion:** In coastal areas, over-extraction can lead to saltwater entering freshwater aquifers.
    *   **Nitrate Contamination:** Due to agricultural runoff.
    *   **Heavy Metals:** From industrial pollution.

#### 3.3. Examples

*   **Punjab and Haryana:** These states are among the most severely affected by groundwater over-exploitation, with a high number of blocks categorized as over-exploited, leading to declining water tables.
*   **Western Uttar Pradesh:** Also faces significant groundwater depletion due to intensive irrigation.
*   **Tamil Nadu:** Experiences groundwater quality issues, including salinity in coastal areas.
*   **West Bengal:** A major concern is arsenic contamination in groundwater in certain districts.

### 4. Trends in Groundwater Availability

The trend in groundwater availability in India is largely characterized by declining water tables due to unsustainable extraction practices.

#### 4.1. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Water Table:** The upper surface of the zone of saturation, where the pores and fractures of the ground are saturated with water.
*   **Dynamic Groundwater Resources:** The replenishable part of groundwater resources.
*   **Recharge Zones:** Areas where groundwater is replenished.
*   **Reclamation:** The process of restoring degraded land or water resources.

#### 4.2. Observed Trends

*   **Declining Water Tables:** The most significant trend is the continuous decline in groundwater levels across large parts of the country.
    *   **CGWB Data:** Regularly shows a declining trend in average groundwater levels in many states.
*   **Increase in Over-exploited Areas:** The number of "over-exploited" and "critical" assessment units (as defined by CGWB) has been steadily increasing over the years.
*   **Increased Energy Consumption for Pumping:** Deeper water tables necessitate more powerful pumps and longer pumping hours, leading to higher energy consumption, often subsidized, creating a complex economic and environmental cycle.
*   **Impact of Reduced Surface Water Availability:** In regions where surface water is scarce or unreliable, the dependence on groundwater increases, further exacerbating depletion.
*   **Changes in Recharge Patterns:** While overall rainfall might be consistent in some areas, changes in its intensity and timing, along with increased impervious surfaces, can reduce the effective infiltration and groundwater recharge.
*   **Groundwater Quality Deterioration:** The continuous pumping and presence of pollutants lead to a decline in groundwater quality in many areas.

#### 4.3. Future Projections

*   **Continued Depletion:** If current extraction patterns persist, groundwater depletion is projected to worsen in many vulnerable regions, leading to water scarcity and potential conflicts.
*   **Increased Reliance on Treated Wastewater and Desalination:** As freshwater sources dwindle, there might be an increased need for advanced technologies like wastewater treatment and desalination, though these are costly.
*   **Importance of Sustainable Groundwater Management:** The trend strongly emphasizes the need for a paradigm shift towards sustainable groundwater management, including regulation of extraction, promotion of water-efficient agriculture, and artificial recharge.
*   **Climate Change Impacts:** Changing rainfall patterns and increased temperatures can further stress groundwater resources by altering recharge rates and increasing evapotranspiration.

#### 4.4. Examples

*   **Alarming Declines in North India:** States like Punjab and Haryana have seen their water tables drop by several meters in the last few decades.
*   **Expansion of Desertified Areas:** In arid and semi-arid regions, over-extraction of groundwater can contribute to land degradation and desertification.
*   **Impact on Livelihoods:** In areas with severe depletion, farmers face increased costs for pumping, and many are forced to abandon agriculture.

### 5. Challenges and Opportunities in Managing Surface and Groundwater Resources in India

Managing India's water resources presents a complex web of challenges, but also offers significant opportunities for improvement and innovation.

#### 5.1. Challenges

*   **Growing Water Demand:**
    *   **Population Growth:** Increasing population directly translates to higher demand for domestic, agricultural, and industrial water.
    *   **Urbanization:** Concentrated demand in urban centers puts immense pressure on local water sources.
    *   **Agricultural Needs:** Agriculture accounts for over 80% of India's water consumption, and inefficiencies in irrigation practices exacerbate the problem.
*   **Pollution of Water Bodies:**
    *   **Untreated Sewage:** A significant portion of urban and rural wastewater is discharged untreated into rivers and lakes.
    *   **Industrial Effluents:** Industries often release hazardous chemicals into water bodies.
    *   **Agricultural Runoff:** Pesticides and fertilizers from agricultural fields contaminate surface and groundwater.
*   **Inequitable Distribution and Access:**
    *   **Regional Disparities:** Uneven rainfall and water availability lead to significant disparities in access to water.
    *   **Socio-economic Disparities:** Poorer communities often have less access to safe and sufficient water.
*   **Poor Infrastructure and Management:**
    *   **Aging Infrastructure:** Many water supply and distribution systems are outdated and inefficient.
    *   **Fragmented Governance:** Water management is often handled by multiple agencies with overlapping mandates, leading to a lack of coordination.
    *   **Lack of Data and Monitoring:** Inadequate data on water availability, quality, and usage hinders effective planning and management.
*   **Climate Change Impacts:**
    *   **Increased Extremes:** More frequent and intense droughts and floods.
    *   **Unpredictable Monsoon:** Affects surface water availability and groundwater recharge.
*   **Institutional and Legal Frameworks:**
    *   **Weak Enforcement:** Existing regulations for pollution control and groundwater extraction are often not effectively enforced.
    *   **Inter-State Water Disputes:** Resolving disputes over shared river basins remains a significant challenge.

#### 5.2. Opportunities

*   **Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM):**
    *   **Holistic Approach:** Managing water resources in a coordinated manner, considering all competing uses and the environment.
    *   **Basin-level Planning:** Planning water management at the river basin level for more effective and sustainable outcomes.
*   **Water Conservation and Efficiency:**
    *   **Efficient Irrigation:** Promoting micro-irrigation techniques (drip, sprinkler) in agriculture.
    *   **Rainwater Harvesting:** Implementing rainwater harvesting structures at household, community, and institutional levels.
    *   **Reducing Non-Revenue Water (NRW):** Improving the efficiency of urban water supply systems to minimize losses.
*   **Wastewater Treatment and Reuse:**
    *   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) Norms:** Mandating treatment of wastewater before discharge.
    *   **Promoting Treated Wastewater Reuse:** Encouraging the reuse of treated wastewater for industrial, agricultural, and non-potable urban uses.
*   **Groundwater Management and Regulation:**
    *   **Regulation of Extraction:** Implementing policies and regulations for groundwater abstraction, such as mandatory registration of borewells.
    *   **Artificial Recharge:** Creating structures like check dams, percolation tanks, and recharge wells to augment groundwater recharge.
*   **Technological Advancements:**
    *   **Remote Sensing and GIS:** For monitoring water resources, mapping water stress areas, and identifying pollution sources.
    *   **Smart Water Management:** Utilizing sensors and data analytics for efficient distribution and leak detection.
*   **Public Awareness and Participation:**
    *   **Community Involvement:** Engaging local communities in water management and conservation efforts.
    *   **Education and Awareness Campaigns:** Educating the public about the importance of water conservation and responsible usage.
*   **Policy and Governance Reforms:**
    *   **Strengthening Institutions:** Empowering water management bodies and improving inter-agency coordination.
    *   **Public-Private Partnerships:** Leveraging private sector expertise and investment in water infrastructure and management.
*   **Inter-basin Water Transfer:** Exploring the feasibility of transferring water from surplus basins to deficit basins, while carefully considering environmental and social impacts.

#### 5.3. Examples

*   **Atal Bhujal Yojana:** A scheme focused on sustainable groundwater management through community participation in Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh.
*   **Namami Gange Programme:** A comprehensive initiative to clean and rejuvenate the Ganga River, focusing on sewage treatment, industrial pollution control, and riverfront development.
*   **Per Drop More Crop Scheme:** Promotes micro-irrigation to increase water use efficiency in agriculture.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Monsoon Dependency:** India's water availability is heavily reliant on the monsoon, making it vulnerable to its variability.
*   **Groundwater is Critical:** Groundwater is the lifeline for agriculture and domestic water supply for a vast population.
*   **Over-exploitation is a Major Threat:** Unchecked groundwater extraction is leading to severe depletion and quality issues.
*   **Pollution is Pervasive:** Both surface and groundwater resources are significantly impacted by pollution from various sources.
*   **Climate Change Amplifies Issues:** Climate change is exacerbating water scarcity, flood risks, and overall water resource challenges.
*   **Integrated Management is Key:** A holistic approach to managing water, considering all sources and uses, is essential for sustainability.
*   **Conservation and Efficiency are Paramount:** Emphasizing water conservation in all sectors, especially agriculture, is crucial.
*   **Policy and Implementation Matter:** Effective policies, strong enforcement, and community participation are vital for successful water management.
