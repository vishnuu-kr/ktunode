# VALUATION OF REAL PROPERTIES

## Module 2: Investment

### Topic: Method of Estimating Cost Depreciation - Numerical Examples

---

This module delves into the investment aspects of real property valuation, focusing on how depreciation affects value. This topic specifically examines methods for estimating depreciation based on the cost approach.

---

Upon completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   **Understand the concept of depreciation in real property valuation.**
*   **Identify the different types of depreciation (physical deterioration, functional obsolescence, external/economic obsolescence).**
*   **Explain the various methods used to estimate cost depreciation.**
*   **Apply numerical examples to demonstrate the calculation of depreciation using common methods.**
*   **Analyze the impact of depreciation on the depreciated cost of a property.**
*   **Recognize the limitations and considerations when estimating depreciation.**

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Depreciation:** The loss in value of an asset from any cause, other than consumption of capital, over time. In real estate, it refers to the reduction in value due to wear and tear, obsolescence, and other factors.
*   **Cost Approach:** A valuation method used to estimate the value of a property by determining the cost to replace or reproduce the property, then subtracting accrued depreciation, and finally adding the land value.
    *   **Reproduction Cost:** The cost to construct an exact replica of the subject property at current prices, using the same materials, design, and specifications.
    *   **Replacement Cost:** The cost to construct a substitute property of equal utility and desirability at current prices, using modern materials and design.
*   **Accrued Depreciation:** The total depreciation that has already occurred up to the date of valuation.
*   **Useful Life:** The estimated period over which an asset is expected to remain economically viable and productive.
*   **Economic Life:** The period over which a property is expected to remain economically profitable to its owner, considering market forces and obsolescence.

---

### Types of Depreciation:

It's crucial to understand the sources of depreciation as they influence the estimation methods.

1.  **Physical Deterioration:**
    *   **Definition:** Loss in value due to wear and tear, aging, and exposure to the elements.
    *   **Categories:**
        *   **Curable:** Items that are economically feasible to repair or replace. (e.g., worn-out plumbing fixtures, damaged flooring).
        *   **Incurable:** Items that are not economically feasible to repair or replace, or the cost of repair exceeds the expected increase in value. (e.g., a building's structural frame, general wear and tear on the foundation).

2.  **Functional Obsolescence:**
    *   **Definition:** Loss in value due to outdated design, inadequate features, or superadequacies that make the property less desirable or efficient compared to newer, more modern alternatives.
    *   **Categories:**
        *   **Curable:** Symptoms of functional obsolescence that can be corrected economically. (e.g., outdated kitchen, insufficient bathrooms, poor room layout).
        *   **Incurable:** Symptoms of functional obsolescence that cannot be corrected economically. (e.g., outdated structural design, insufficient ceiling height in older buildings).

3.  **External (Economic) Obsolescence:**
    *   **Definition:** Loss in value due to factors outside the property itself, related to its surrounding environment or market conditions.
    *   **Causes:** Negative influences from the neighborhood, undesirable zoning changes, proximity to nuisances (factories, highways), economic downturns, increased competition.
    *   **Typically Incurable:** External obsolescence is almost always incurable as the property owner has no control over these external factors.

### Methods of Estimating Cost Depreciation:

Several methods are used to quantify accrued depreciation. The choice of method often depends on the type of property, the availability of data, and the appraiser's judgment.

#### 1. The Straight-Line Method (Age-Life Method)

*   **Concept:** This is the simplest and most common method. It assumes that depreciation occurs at a constant rate over the property's total economic life.
*   **Formula:**
    *   **Annual Depreciation = (Cost New - Salvage Value) / Total Economic Life (in years)**
    *   **Accrued Depreciation = Annual Depreciation x Effective Age (in years)**
    *   **Alternatively:** **Accrued Depreciation = Cost New x (Effective Age / Total Economic Life)**
*   **Key Terms:**
    *   **Cost New:** The cost to construct the building at the time of valuation using current prices.
    *   **Salvage Value:** The estimated value of an asset at the end of its economic life. For buildings, this is often considered minimal or zero for valuation purposes, representing only the value of salvaged materials.
    *   **Effective Age:** The age of the property based on its condition and maintenance, which may differ from its chronological age.
    *   **Total Economic Life:** The total period the property is expected to remain economically useful.

*   **Example 1:**
    A building has a replacement cost new of \$500,000. It has an estimated effective age of 10 years and a total economic life of 40 years. Assume a salvage value of \$0.

    *   **Step 1: Calculate Annual Depreciation**
        Annual Depreciation = (\$500,000 - \$0) / 40 years
        Annual Depreciation = \$12,500 per year

    *   **Step 2: Calculate Accrued Depreciation**
        Accrued Depreciation = \$12,500/year x 10 years
        **Accrued Depreciation = \$125,000**

    *   **Using the alternative formula:**
        Accrued Depreciation = \$500,000 x (10 years / 40 years)
        Accrued Depreciation = \$500,000 x 0.25
        **Accrued Depreciation = \$125,000**

    *   **Depreciated Cost New:**
        Depreciated Cost New = Replacement Cost New - Accrued Depreciation
        Depreciated Cost New = \$500,000 - \$125,000
        **Depreciated Cost New = \$375,000**

*   **Example 2:**
    A warehouse was built 20 years ago with a reproduction cost new of \$1,000,000. Due to excellent maintenance, its condition is equivalent to that of a 12-year-old building. Its estimated total economic life is 50 years, with a salvage value of \$50,000.

    *   **Step 1: Calculate Annual Depreciation**
        Annual Depreciation = (\$1,000,000 - \$50,000) / 50 years
        Annual Depreciation = \$950,000 / 50 years
        Annual Depreciation = \$19,000 per year

    *   **Step 2: Calculate Accrued Depreciation**
        Accrued Depreciation = \$19,000/year x 12 years (effective age)
        **Accrued Depreciation = \$228,000**

    *   **Depreciated Cost New:**
        Depreciated Cost New = \$1,000,000 - \$228,000
        **Depreciated Cost New = \$772,000**

#### 2. The Observed Condition Method (Breakdown Method)

*   **Concept:** This method involves identifying and quantifying specific items of incurable physical deterioration and incurable functional obsolescence. Curable items are estimated separately.
*   **Process:**
    1.  **Estimate Cost New:** Determine the cost new of the subject property.
    2.  **Identify and Cost Curable Items:** List all curable physical deterioration (e.g., new roof needed, painting, etc.) and curable functional obsolescence (e.g., adding a bathroom, modernizing kitchen). Estimate the cost to cure these items. This cost represents the depreciation attributed to curable items.
    3.  **Estimate Incurable Physical Deterioration:** For the remaining structure, estimate the proportion of physical wear and tear that is incurable. This is often expressed as a percentage of the cost new of the sound structure (building minus curable items and land).
    4.  **Estimate Incurable Functional Obsolescence:** Identify and quantify losses due to incurable functional issues. This can be done by comparing the cost of modern features that are missing or the cost of superadequacies.
    5.  **Estimate External Obsolescence:** Quantify losses due to external factors, typically as a percentage of the value of the property as if it had no physical or functional deficiencies.
    6.  **Sum All Depreciation Components:** Add the costs of curable items, estimated incurable physical deterioration, incurable functional obsolescence, and external obsolescence to arrive at total accrued depreciation.

*   **Example 3:**
    Consider a building with a replacement cost new of \$700,000. The appraiser observes the following:
    *   **Curable Physical Deterioration:**
        *   Replace roof: \$30,000
        *   Repaint exterior: \$15,000
        *   Update HVAC: \$25,000
        *   **Total Curable Physical Deterioration = \$70,000**
    *   **Incurable Physical Deterioration:** The remaining structure shows wear and tear estimated at 15% of the cost of the sound structure (Cost New minus Curable Physical Deterioration).
    *   **Incurable Functional Obsolescence:** The building has poor room layouts that would cost \$50,000 to fix optimally, but the market only supports an increase in value of \$30,000 for such improvements. Therefore, the incurable functional obsolescence is \$30,000.
    *   **External Obsolescence:** The neighborhood has a declining industrial base, leading to an estimated 5% loss in value.

    *   **Step 1: Cost of Sound Structure**
        Cost of Sound Structure = Replacement Cost New - Total Curable Physical Deterioration
        Cost of Sound Structure = \$700,000 - \$70,000 = \$630,000

    *   **Step 2: Calculate Incurable Physical Deterioration**
        Incurable Physical Deterioration = 15% of \$630,000
        Incurable Physical Deterioration = 0.15 x \$630,000
        **Incurable Physical Deterioration = \$94,500**

    *   **Step 3: Calculate Total Accrued Depreciation**
        Total Accrued Depreciation = Curable Physical Deterioration + Incurable Physical Deterioration + Incurable Functional Obsolescence + External Obsolescence
        Total Accrued Depreciation = \$70,000 + \$94,500 + \$30,000 + (5% of Value as if new without these items)

        *To calculate external obsolescence, we need the value of the property *before* external obsolescence. Let's assume for this step that the "value as if new without these items" refers to the cost of the sound structure plus the cost to cure functional obsolescence.*

        Value before External Obsolescence = Cost of Sound Structure + Curable Functional Obsolescence costs (if any) + Curable Physical Deterioration
        *Revisiting the components:*
        Let's define the value basis for external obsolescence as the value of the property minus physical and functional depreciation that are inherent to the building itself. A common approach is to apply it to the property's value after accounting for physical and functional obsolescence.
        *A more precise breakdown:*
        *   Cost New: \$700,000
        *   Curable Physical Deterioration: \$70,000
        *   Incurable Physical Deterioration: \$94,500
        *   Incurable Functional Obsolescence: \$30,000

        *Total Depreciation from Physical and Functional causes:*
        \$70,000 (Curable Physical) + \$94,500 (Incurable Physical) + \$30,000 (Incurable Functional) = \$194,500

        *Value after Physical and Functional Depreciation:*
        \$700,000 - \$194,500 = \$505,500

        *Now, apply External Obsolescence to this value:*
        External Obsolescence = 5% of \$505,500 = 0.05 x \$505,500 = \$25,275

        *Total Accrued Depreciation:*
        Total Accrued Depreciation = \$70,000 (Curable Physical) + \$94,500 (Incurable Physical) + \$30,000 (Incurable Functional) + \$25,275 (External)
        **Total Accrued Depreciation = \$219,775**

    *   **Depreciated Cost New:**
        Depreciated Cost New = \$700,000 - \$219,775
        **Depreciated Cost New = \$480,225**

#### 3. The Quantity Survey Method

*   **Concept:** This is the most detailed method. It involves a detailed breakdown of the property into its component parts (labor, materials, profit). Depreciation is then estimated for each component based on its expected life and condition.
*   **Process:**
    1.  **Detailed Cost Estimate:** A complete cost estimate of the property as if new, broken down by individual components (e.g., foundation, walls, roof, plumbing, electrical, finishes).
    2.  **Assign Useful Lives:** Assign a useful life and salvage value to each component.
    3.  **Assess Condition:** Evaluate the physical condition and functional utility of each component.
    4.  **Calculate Depreciation per Component:** Apply depreciation methods (e.g., straight-line) to each component based on its effective age and remaining economic life.
    5.  **Sum Component Depreciation:** Aggregate the depreciation for all components to arrive at total accrued depreciation.
*   **Advantages:** Most accurate method, provides detailed insights into sources of depreciation.
*   **Disadvantages:** Time-consuming, requires specialized expertise, more costly to perform.
*   **Numerical Example:** This method is highly detailed and specific to individual building components, making a generalized numerical example complex to present concisely. However, the principle is to:
    *   For a "new" roof costing \$20,000 with a 20-year life and effective age of 5 years: Straight-line depreciation = (\$20,000 / 20) * 5 = \$5,000.
    *   For "new" windows costing \$15,000 with a 25-year life and effective age of 10 years: Straight-line depreciation = (\$15,000 / 25) * 10 = \$6,000.
    *   This would be done for every component, and the total depreciation summed up.

---

#### 4. Trended Physical Life Method (Less Common for Buildings, More for Equipment)

*   **Concept:** This method attempts to estimate depreciation by adjusting historical costs to current costs and then applying a depreciation factor based on the property's physical condition relative to its expected useful life. It's less commonly used for entire buildings than the straight-line or observed condition methods.

---

### Applying Depreciation in the Cost Approach:

The fundamental formula for the cost approach using depreciated cost is:

**Value = Replacement Cost New (or Reproduction Cost New) - Accrued Depreciation + Land Value**

*   **Example 4:**
    Using the results from Example 1:
    *   Replacement Cost New: \$500,000
    *   Accrued Depreciation: \$125,000
    *   Land Value: \$100,000

    *   **Estimated Value = \$500,000 - \$125,000 + \$100,000 = \$475,000**

*   **Example 5:**
    Using the results from Example 3:
    *   Replacement Cost New: \$700,000
    *   Accrued Depreciation: \$219,775
    *   Land Value: \$150,000

    *   **Estimated Value = \$700,000 - \$219,775 + \$150,000 = \$630,225**

---

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Land does not depreciate:** Land is considered to appreciate or remain stable in value, so depreciation is only applied to the building and other improvements.
*   **Effective Age vs. Chronological Age:** Effective age reflects the building's physical condition and marketability, not just its calendar age.
*   **Curable vs. Incurable:** The distinction is vital for the observed condition method. An item is curable if the cost to cure it is less than or equal to the value increase it provides.
*   **Judgment is Key:** While formulas are used, the selection of useful lives, effective ages, and depreciation rates often involves appraiser judgment based on market data and experience.
*   **Market Data:** The most reliable way to estimate depreciation is through market data analysis (paired sales analysis), though this is often done implicitly when using the cost approach and reconciling with sales comparison.
*   **Reconciliation:** In a full appraisal, the value derived from the cost approach is reconciled with values from other approaches (sales comparison, income capitalization) to arrive at a final opinion of value.

### Practice Questions:

1.  A building has a replacement cost new of \$800,000. It has an effective age of 15 years and a total economic life of 50 years. Assume a salvage value of \$40,000. Using the straight-line method, calculate the accrued depreciation and the depreciated cost new.
2.  A commercial building has a reproduction cost new of \$1,500,000. The appraiser estimates the following:
    *   Curable physical deterioration (e.g., new HVAC, interior renovations): \$120,000
    *   Incurable physical deterioration: 10% of the cost of the sound structure.
    *   Incurable functional obsolescence (outdated floor plan): Loss in value of \$80,000.
    *   External obsolescence (undesirable nearby land use): 7% loss in value.

    Calculate the total accrued depreciation and the depreciated cost new for this building, assuming the cost of the sound structure is the cost new minus curable physical deterioration.

---

### Answers to Practice Questions:

**Answer to Question 1:**

*   **Cost New:** \$800,000
*   **Salvage Value:** \$40,000
*   **Effective Age:** 15 years
*   **Total Economic Life:** 50 years

*   **Step 1: Calculate Annual Depreciation**
    Annual Depreciation = (\$800,000 - \$40,000) / 50 years
    Annual Depreciation = \$760,000 / 50 years
    Annual Depreciation = \$15,200 per year

*   **Step 2: Calculate Accrued Depreciation**
    Accrued Depreciation = \$15,200/year x 15 years
    **Accrued Depreciation = \$228,000**

*   **Step 3: Calculate Depreciated Cost New**
    Depreciated Cost New = \$800,000 - \$228,000
    **Depreciated Cost New = \$572,000**

---

**Answer to Question 2:**

*   **Reproduction Cost New:** \$1,500,000
*   **Curable Physical Deterioration:** \$120,000
*   **Incurable Functional Obsolescence:** \$80,000

*   **Step 1: Calculate Cost of Sound Structure**
    Cost of Sound Structure = \$1,500,000 - \$120,000 = \$1,380,000

*   **Step 2: Calculate Incurable Physical Deterioration**
    Incurable Physical Deterioration = 10% of \$1,380,000
    Incurable Physical Deterioration = 0.10 x \$1,380,000
    **Incurable Physical Deterioration = \$138,000**

*   **Step 3: Determine Value Basis for External Obsolescence**
    Value before External Obsolescence = Cost New - Curable Physical Deterioration - Incurable Physical Deterioration - Incurable Functional Obsolescence
    Value before External Obsolescence = \$1,500,000 - \$120,000 - \$138,000 - \$80,000
    Value before External Obsolescence = \$1,162,000

*   **Step 4: Calculate External Obsolescence**
    External Obsolescence = 7% of \$1,162,000
    External Obsolescence = 0.07 x \$1,162,000
    **External Obsolescence = \$81,340**

*   **Step 5: Calculate Total Accrued Depreciation**
    Total Accrued Depreciation = Curable Physical Deterioration + Incurable Physical Deterioration + Incurable Functional Obsolescence + External Obsolescence
    Total Accrued Depreciation = \$120,000 + \$138,000 + \$80,000 + \$81,340
    **Total Accrued Depreciation = \$419,340**

*   **Step 6: Calculate Depreciated Cost New**
    Depreciated Cost New = \$1,500,000 - \$419,340
    **Depreciated Cost New = \$1,080,660**
