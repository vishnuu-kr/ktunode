### 1. Introduction: Defining Service Scheduling and Timetabling

Both service scheduling and timetabling are critical for delivering public transportation. While closely related, they represent distinct stages in the operational planning process.

#### 1.1. Service Scheduling

*   **Definition:** Service scheduling refers to the **process of determining the frequency and capacity of transit services** offered on specific routes or corridors over a given period. It's about *how much* service to provide.
*   **Focus:**
    *   **Frequency:** How often vehicles depart.
    *   **Capacity:** How many passengers can be accommodated (number of vehicles, vehicle size).
    *   **Service Levels:** The overall quality and availability of the service (e.g., peak hour vs. off-peak, weekday vs. weekend).
    *   **Resource Allocation:** Assigning buses/trains, drivers, and other resources to meet service requirements.
*   **Key Questions Answered:**
    *   How many buses are needed on Route X during morning peak?
    *   What is the minimum service frequency required on Route Y on weekends?
    *   What type of vehicle (capacity) should be used for this service?

#### 1.2. Timetabling

*   **Definition:** Timetabling is the **process of establishing specific departure and arrival times for vehicles at designated points** (time points) along a route. It's about *when* the service runs.
*   **Focus:**
    *   **Specific Times:** Defining exact departure and arrival times for each stop or key time point.
    *   **Sequencing:** Arranging the order of departures and arrivals for vehicles on a route.
    *   **Connectivity:** Ensuring smooth transfers between different routes and modes of transport.
    *   **Reliability:** Creating schedules that are achievable and minimize delays.
*   **Key Questions Answered:**
    *   When does the first bus depart from the terminus on Route X?
    *   What are the departure times from the city center for Route Y?
    *   What time should a connecting bus arrive to allow for passenger transfers?

#### 1.3. The Relationship

*   **Hierarchical:** Service scheduling typically precedes timetabling. You first decide *how much* service is needed (scheduling), and then you specify *when* that service will operate (timetabling).
*   **Interdependent:** A well-defined schedule is the foundation for creating a realistic timetable. Conversely, the feasibility of a timetable can influence the required service frequency.
*   **Example:**
    *   **Service Scheduling:** Decide that Route 5 needs a 10-minute frequency during morning peak hours.
    *   **Timetabling:** Then, create a timetable with buses departing from the terminus at :00, :10, :20, :30, :40, :50 past the hour to achieve that 10-minute frequency.

### 2. Objectives of Service Scheduling and Timetabling

The primary goals are to meet passenger needs while operating efficiently and reliably.

#### 2.1. Meeting Passenger Demand

*   **Availability:** Service should be available when passengers need it (e.g., during commuting hours, shopping times, late-night travel).
*   **Frequency:** High demand periods require higher frequencies to avoid overcrowding and long waits.
*   **Coverage:** Service should reach the areas where people live, work, and shop.
*   **Connectivity:** Facilitating easy transfers between routes and modes.

#### 2.2. Operational Efficiency

*   **Resource Utilization:** Optimizing the use of vehicles (buses, trains) and drivers.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Balancing service levels with operational costs.
*   **Minimizing Deadhead Time:** Reducing the amount of time vehicles travel without passengers (e.g., returning to the depot).
*   **Driver Rostering:** Creating work schedules for drivers that comply with labor laws and maximize productivity.

#### 2.3. Reliability and Punctuality

*   **On-Time Performance:** Ensuring vehicles depart and arrive as scheduled.
*   **Reducing Delays:** Building in buffer times to account for unexpected issues (traffic, breakdowns).
*   **Customer Confidence:** Reliable service builds trust and encourages ridership.

#### 2.4. Network Integration

*   **Seamless Transfers:** Coordinating schedules of different routes to allow passengers to switch easily.
*   **Hub and Spoke:** Scheduling services at central hubs to align arrival and departure times.

### 3. Key Components and Considerations in Transit Scheduling

Developing effective schedules involves a multi-faceted approach.

#### 3.1. Demand Analysis

*   **Passenger Counts:** Data from fare collection systems, manual counts, or automatic passenger counters (APCs).
*   **Origin-Destination (O-D) Surveys:** Understanding where passengers are traveling from and to.
*   **Peak and Off-Peak Demand:** Recognizing variations in demand throughout the day, week, and year.
*   **Future Demand Projections:** Anticipating changes in travel patterns.

#### 3.2. Network Design and Route Planning

*   **Route Length and Speed:** Longer routes or slower speeds require more vehicles and longer running times.
*   **Stop Spacing:** The number and location of stops affect travel time.
*   **Interchanges and Transfer Points:** Locations where passengers can switch between services.

#### 3.3. Operational Constraints

*   **Vehicle Availability:** The total number of vehicles in the fleet.
*   **Driver Availability:** The number of licensed drivers and their working hours.
*   **Depot Capacity:** Where vehicles are housed and maintained.
*   **Traffic Conditions:** Real-world traffic patterns and congestion levels.
*   **Road Infrastructure:** Speed limits, road capacity, and dedicated bus lanes.
*   **Transit Signal Priority (TSP):** Systems that give priority to buses at traffic signals.
*   **Vehicle Capacity:** The number of seats and standing room on vehicles.
*   **Service Standards:** Agency policies on minimum frequency, maximum wait times, etc.
*   **Labor Regulations:** Rules governing driver work hours and rest periods.

#### 3.4. Timetabling Specifics

*   **Time Points:** Key stops or locations used as reference points for scheduling.
*   **Running Time:** The estimated time it takes a vehicle to travel between two time points, including delays.
*   **Turnaround Time:** The time required for a vehicle to complete its route, return to the origin, and prepare for its next trip.
*   **Block Time:** The total time a vehicle is assigned to a specific duty or route segment.
*   **Headways:** The interval of time between successive vehicles departing from the same stop on the same route (e.g., every 10 minutes).

#### 3.5. Service Span

*   **Operating Hours:** The total period during which service is provided on a route (e.g., 5:00 AM to 11:00 PM).
*   **First and Last Trip:** The times of the earliest and latest departures.

### 4. Relationship Between Scheduling, Timetabling, and Route Planning

These three elements are intrinsically linked and form the backbone of transit operations.

*   **Route Planning:** Defines *where* the service runs (the path, stops, and direction).
*   **Service Scheduling:** Determines *how much* service is needed along that route (frequency, capacity) based on demand and operational capacity.
*   **Timetabling:** Specifies *when* that scheduled service will operate, translating frequencies into concrete departure and arrival times.

**Example of the Flow:**

1.  **Route Planning:** A new route (Route X) is designed connecting residential area A to business district B, with specific stops.
2.  **Service Scheduling:** Demand analysis shows high demand during morning and evening peaks on Route X. The agency decides to provide a 7-minute frequency during peak hours and a 15-minute frequency during off-peak hours. They also determine that 4 buses are needed for the peak requirement.
3.  **Timetabling:** Based on the route's length, average speed, and traffic conditions, running times between key time points are calculated. A timetable is then created, ensuring buses depart at 7-minute intervals during peaks (e.g., :00, :07, :14...) and 15-minute intervals off-peak. This timetable also considers driver duty cycles and vehicle turnaround times.

### 5. Types of Transit Schedules

Different approaches exist for structuring transit operations.

#### 5.1. Headway-Based Scheduling

*   **Definition:** Service is defined by a fixed interval (headway) between vehicles. The actual departure times may vary slightly, but the goal is to maintain the frequency.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **Simplicity:** Easy for passengers to understand.
    *   **Flexibility:** Can absorb minor delays without significantly impacting the next arrival.
    *   **Good for High-Frequency Routes:** Most effective on busy routes where demand is consistent.
*   **Example:** A bus runs every 10 minutes. Passengers don't need to know the exact departure time; they just need to know it will arrive in about 10 minutes.

#### 5.2. Time-Point Scheduling

*   **Definition:** Specifies departure and arrival times only at key "time points" along a route. Intermediate stops are implicitly served within the running time between these points.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **Focus on Key Locations:** Useful for managing large networks or routes with many stops.
    *   **Requires Accurate Running Times:** Relies heavily on precise estimates of travel time between time points.
    *   **Can be Less Precise for Intermediate Stops:** Passengers at non-time-point stops may have less predictable arrival times.
*   **Example:** On Route 3, the timetable shows departure from the central station at :05 and :35, and arrival at the university at :20 and :50. The times for stops in between are not explicitly listed.

#### 5.3. Schedule-Based (Fixed-Time) Scheduling

*   **Definition:** All departure and arrival times for all stops (or a defined set of stops) are precisely specified.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **High Precision:** Offers the most detailed information to passengers.
    *   **Essential for Low-Frequency Services:** Crucial for routes with infrequent service to inform passengers exactly when to be at the stop.
    *   **Requires Careful Planning:** Sensitive to variations in running times and can lead to cascading delays if not well-managed.
    *   **Ideal for Coordinating Transfers:** Allows for precise timing of connections.
*   **Example:** A train timetable listing exact departure and arrival times for every station on the line.

#### 5.4. Mixed Approaches

*   Many transit agencies use a combination of these methods. For instance, a high-frequency route might use headway scheduling, while a lower-frequency route might use schedule-based timing. Time points are often used as a management tool across different scheduling types.

### 6. Creating Realistic and Reliable Timetables

A timetable is only useful if it's achievable and consistently met.

#### 6.1. Building in Buffer Time (Slack)

*   **Definition:** Extra time added to running times to account for unpredictable delays.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   Absorb variations in traffic, passenger boarding/alighting, and dwell times.
    *   Improve on-time performance.
    *   Reduce driver stress.
*   **Placement:** Slack can be added between time points, at terminals, or at specific busy intersections.
*   **Balancing Act:** Too much slack can lead to "bunching" (vehicles arriving in groups) and underutilization of resources. Too little slack results in poor reliability.

#### 6.2. Determining Running Times

*   **Methods:**
    *   **Field Observations:** Driving the route at different times of day to record actual travel times.
    *   **Historical Data Analysis:** Using GPS data from previous trips.
    *   **Simulation Software:** Modeling the route and traffic conditions.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   **Peak vs. Off-Peak:** Running times vary significantly due to traffic.
    *   **Vehicle Type:** Different vehicles may have different acceleration/deceleration characteristics.
    *   **Stop dwell times:** Time spent at stops for boarding and alighting.

#### 6.3. Optimizing Turnaround Times

*   **Purpose:** To ensure a vehicle has sufficient time at the end of a route to prepare for its return trip without causing delays.
*   **Components:** Driver break, vehicle inspection, passenger alighting, quick cleaning, etc.
*   **Impact:** Inefficient turnaround times can reduce service frequency.

#### 6.4. Driver Rostering and Schedule Adherence

*   **Rostering:** Creating daily or weekly work assignments for drivers that align with the timetable and adhere to labor laws.
*   **Schedule Adherence:** Drivers following the established timetable as closely as possible. This requires good training and performance monitoring.

#### 6.5. Iterative Process

*   Scheduling and timetabling are not one-off tasks. They often require continuous review and adjustment based on performance data, passenger feedback, and changing conditions.

---

### 7. Importance of Customer Information Systems (CIS)

A well-designed timetable is most effective when communicated clearly to passengers.

*   **Real-time Information:** Providing live arrival predictions (e.g., via apps, digital displays at stops).
*   **Static Timetables:** Printed timetables, PDFs online, and static displays at stations.
*   **Trip Planners:** Tools that allow passengers to plan their journeys, often integrating multiple modes and routes.
*   **Disruption Information:** Communicating service changes or delays proactively.

**Key Point:** The best timetable is useless if passengers don't know when or where to catch the service.

### Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1:**

What is the fundamental difference between service scheduling and timetabling?

**Answer 1:**
Service scheduling focuses on *how much* service is provided (frequency, capacity), while timetabling focuses on *when* that service operates, specifying exact departure and arrival times.

**Question 2:**

A transit agency decides to run a bus every 15 minutes on a particular route during off-peak hours. This decision relates to which aspect of transit operations?
a) Route planning
b) Service scheduling
c) Timetabling
d) Vehicle maintenance

**Answer 2:**
b) Service scheduling (This decision is about the frequency of service).

**Question 3:**

List three key operational constraints that a transit scheduler must consider when developing a timetable.

**Answer 3:**
Any three of the following:
*   Vehicle availability
*   Driver availability
*   Traffic conditions
*   Road infrastructure
*   Labor regulations
*   Depot capacity
*   Vehicle capacity

**Question 4:**

Imagine you are creating a timetable for a new bus route. Describe the steps you would take, starting from route planning to finalizing the timetable.

**Answer 4:**
1.  **Route Planning:** Define the route path, stops, and direction.
2.  **Demand Analysis:** Assess passenger demand throughout the day.
3.  **Service Scheduling:** Determine desired frequencies and service levels (e.g., peak vs. off-peak).
4.  **Running Time Calculation:** Estimate travel times between key points, considering traffic and stop dwell times.
5.  **Timetable Creation:** Based on scheduled frequencies and running times, assign specific departure/arrival times at time points.
6.  **Add Buffer Time (Slack):** Incorporate reasonable buffer times to improve reliability.
7.  **Driver Rostering:** Assign drivers to trips.
8.  **Review and Refine:** Check for feasibility, connectivity, and make adjustments as needed.

**Question 5 (Scenario):**

Route 12 has a scheduled headway of 5 minutes during the morning peak. The route is 20 miles long and takes an average of 60 minutes to travel. The depot is located at the terminus.

*   How many buses are likely required to maintain this 5-minute headway during the peak?
*   What is a potential problem if the running time consistently exceeds 60 minutes due to traffic?

**Answer 5:**

*   **Buses required:** To maintain a 5-minute headway on a 60-minute route, you need approximately 60 minutes / 5 minutes/bus = 12 buses operating continuously on the route. (This assumes a continuous cycle with minimal turnaround time, in reality, slightly more might be needed for buffer).
*   **Problem with exceeding running time:** If the running time consistently exceeds 60 minutes, it will disrupt the 5-minute headway. Buses will start arriving later, potentially leading to longer wait times for passengers at the origin and causing subsequent buses to bunch up as they try to catch up. This directly impacts reliability and customer satisfaction.
