# PUBLIC TRANSPORTATION SYSTEMS: Module 3 - Transit Lines and Networks: Planning Objectives

## Topic: Analysis of Metro Network Geometric Forms

This module focuses on understanding the fundamental structures and shapes of metro (rapid transit) networks and how these geometric forms influence their planning and operational effectiveness.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

1.  **Identify and describe** common geometric forms of metro networks.
2.  **Analyze the advantages and disadvantages** of each geometric form in relation to urban context and planning objectives.
3.  **Explain how geometric form influences** network coverage, connectivity, efficiency, and capacity.
4.  **Relate the choice of geometric form** to specific urban development patterns and policy goals.
5.  **Evaluate the suitability** of different geometric forms for various city sizes and types.

---

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Metro Network:** A rail-based urban public transportation system characterized by high capacity, high frequency, and grade separation from other traffic.
*   **Geometric Form:** The spatial arrangement and topology of transit lines within a metropolitan area. It dictates how lines are connected, how passengers transfer, and the overall reach of the network.
*   **Linehaul:** The primary function of a metro line, serving major travel corridors and connecting high-demand areas.
*   **Feeder Lines:** Transit services (e.g., buses, trams) that connect lower-density areas to the metro network, bringing passengers to stations.
*   **Coverage:** The extent to which a transit network serves a geographic area.
*   **Connectivity:** The ease with which passengers can travel between different parts of the network, often requiring transfers.
*   **Transfer:** The act of changing from one transit line to another.
*   **Through-running:** Trains operating on a single line without requiring passengers to transfer.
*   **Hub-and-Spoke:** A network structure where lines converge at a central point (hub) and radiate outwards (spokes).
*   **Grid Network:** A network with parallel and perpendicular lines forming a rectangular or square pattern.
*   **Radial Network:** A network where lines originate from a central business district (CBD) and extend outwards into the suburbs.
*   **Circumferential/Orbital Lines:** Lines that connect different radial lines without passing through the CBD, often forming a loop or ring around the city.
*   **Hybrid Networks:** Networks that combine elements of different basic geometric forms.

### 1. Identification and Description of Common Geometric Forms:

Metro networks typically evolve organically or are planned based on specific urban development patterns. The most common geometric forms include:

#### a) Radial Network

*   **Description:** This is one of the oldest and most common network forms. Lines emanate from a central point (usually the Central Business District - CBD) like spokes of a wheel, extending into residential areas and suburbs.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Strong focus on serving the CBD as a primary destination.
    *   High passenger flows on lines approaching the CBD during peak hours.
    *   Limited direct connectivity between suburban areas without passing through the CBD.
*   **Example:** The original Paris Métro system, many early metro lines in North American cities (e.g., early Chicago 'L' lines, portions of the New York City Subway).

#### b) Grid Network

*   **Description:** Characterized by parallel and perpendicular lines that intersect, forming a rectangular or square pattern. This form aims to provide more uniform coverage across a developed urban area.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   High degree of connectivity due to frequent intersections.
    *   Offers multiple route options for travel between different points.
    *   Can provide good coverage in areas with a grid-like street layout.
*   **Example:** Parts of the London Underground (e.g., the Piccadilly, Piccadilly and District Lines), some lines in the Montreal Metro.

#### c) Ring or Orbital Network

*   **Description:** These lines form a closed loop or a semi-circular path, typically encircling the inner city or connecting suburban centres. They are often designed to bypass the congested CBD.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Facilitates inter-suburban travel.
    *   Reduces the need to travel into the CBD for cross-town journeys.
    *   Enhances connectivity between radial lines.
*   **Example:** The Circle Line and Hammersmith & City Line in London (partially), the Ring Line of the Berlin U-Bahn.

#### d) Hybrid Networks

*   **Description:** Most modern metro networks are not purely radial or grid-based but combine elements of different forms to adapt to complex urban structures and travel patterns. This often involves radial lines complemented by orbital or cross-town connectors.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Aims to balance coverage, connectivity, and capacity.
    *   More resilient and adaptable to changing urban needs.
    *   Can be more complex to manage and plan.
*   **Example:** Tokyo Metro, the Paris Métro system which has evolved to include tangential lines, the New York City Subway, which combines radial, grid, and some orbital elements.

### 2. Advantages and Disadvantages of Each Geometric Form:

| Geometric Form   | Advantages                                                                                               | Disadvantages                                                                                                    |
| :--------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Radial**       | - Serves high-demand corridors to/from the CBD.<br>- Efficient for concentrated CBDs.<br>- Simple planning for initial development. | - Poor connectivity between suburban areas.<br>- High congestion at central transfer points.<br>- Limited coverage in non-CBD areas. |
| **Grid**         | - Excellent connectivity and route choice.<br>- Good coverage in grid-patterned cities.<br>- Reduces reliance on a single CBD. | - Can be less efficient for high-density cross-town travel if lines don't intersect at key points.<br>- May require more infrastructure and land acquisition. |
| **Ring/Orbital** | - Facilitates inter-suburban travel.<br>- Reduces CBD congestion.<br>- Improves network connectivity between radial lines. | - Can be expensive to build.<br>- May not serve all suburban areas effectively if not strategically placed.<br>- Can be less direct for travel to the CBD. |
| **Hybrid**       | - Balances coverage, connectivity, and efficiency.<br>- Adaptable to complex urban forms.<br>- Can offer multiple travel options. | - Complex to plan and integrate.<br>- Potential for operational complexity.<br>- Can be costly to develop comprehensively. |

### 3. Influence of Geometric Form on Network Performance:

The geometric form significantly impacts how a metro network functions:

#### a) Network Coverage

*   **Radial:** Good coverage along defined corridors but poor in between.
*   **Grid:** Generally good coverage across the urban fabric, especially in planned grid layouts.
*   **Ring/Orbital:** Provides good coverage for cross-suburban travel and links different radial corridors.
*   **Hybrid:** Aims for a balance of corridor and area coverage, depending on the specific integration of forms.

#### b) Connectivity

*   **Radial:** High connectivity at the centre, low connectivity between peripheral areas.
*   **Grid:** High connectivity throughout the network due to frequent intersections, offering multiple route choices.
*   **Ring/Orbital:** Excellent connectivity between different radial lines, enabling direct suburban-to-suburban travel.
*   **Hybrid:** Designed to offer improved connectivity between previously disconnected areas, aiming to reduce reliance on the CBD for transfers.

#### c) Efficiency

*   **Radial:** Efficient for trips focused on the CBD. Less efficient for non-CBD trips, requiring longer travel times or multiple transfers.
*   **Grid:** Generally efficient due to multiple route options, allowing passengers to find less congested paths. Can become less efficient if too many lines are required or if transfer points are poorly designed.
*   **Ring/Orbital:** Efficient for inter-suburban travel, reducing travel times. Less efficient if the primary destination is the CBD.
*   **Hybrid:** Aims to optimize efficiency by offering direct routes where possible and facilitating smooth transfers for common travel patterns.

#### d) Capacity

*   **Radial:** High capacity is focused on lines entering the CBD, often leading to bottlenecks at central stations.
*   **Grid:** Distributes passenger load more evenly across intersecting lines, potentially reducing peak load on any single corridor compared to a purely radial system with the same overall network length.
*   **Ring/Orbital:** Can alleviate capacity pressure on central radial lines by diverting traffic.
*   **Hybrid:** Aims to manage capacity by creating a more balanced demand across the network, using orbital and cross-town lines to decongest core radial corridors.

### 4. Relating Geometric Form to Urban Development Patterns and Policy Goals:

The choice of geometric form is not arbitrary; it's often a response to or a driver of urban development:

*   **Historical City Centres (Dense, Radial):** Early metro systems in these cities often adopted a **radial** form to connect the concentrated CBD with expanding residential areas.
    *   *Policy Goal:* To provide mass mobility for workers commuting to the CBD and to support outward urban expansion.
*   **Planned Cities or Gridded Cities:** Cities with a strong **grid** street pattern are more amenable to developing **grid** metro networks, offering consistent coverage and accessibility.
    *   *Policy Goal:* To provide equitable access to transit across a well-defined urban area.
*   **Polycentric Cities or Cities with Ring Roads:** As cities grow and develop multiple employment centres or ring road systems, **ring/orbital** lines become crucial.
    *   *Policy Goal:* To foster inter-suburban economic development, reduce congestion in the CBD, and improve accessibility for suburban residents to suburban job centres.
*   **Sprawling or Complex Urban Forms:** Large, complex, or polycentric metropolitan areas necessitate **hybrid** networks to achieve efficient coverage and connectivity.
    *   *Policy Goal:* To create a resilient and integrated transit system that supports diverse travel needs and promotes sustainable urban growth patterns.
