# Ground Improvement Techniques: Module 4 - Grouting and Injection

This module delves into the principles and applications of grouting and injection techniques used to improve the properties of soil and rock.

---

## 1. Introduction to Grouting and Injection

### 1.1. Definition and Purpose

*   **Grouting:** The process of injecting a fluid or semi-fluid material (grout) into a void, fissure, or porous medium within soil or rock.
*   **Injection:** A broader term that can encompass grouting, but also refers to the introduction of fluids for various purposes, including pressurizing, stabilizing, or creating barriers.

**Purpose of Grouting and Injection:**

*   **Strength Improvement:** To increase the shear strength and bearing capacity of the ground.
*   **Permeability Reduction:** To create impermeable barriers, control groundwater flow, and prevent seepage.
*   **Consolidation:** To fill voids, reduce settlement, and stabilize loose or collapsible soils.
*   **Water Control:** To manage groundwater during excavation or construction.
*   **Seismic Remediation:** To improve the dynamic response of the ground.
*   **Crack Sealing:** To repair fractured rock or concrete.

### 1.2. Classification of Grouting and Injection Techniques

Grouting techniques can be classified based on several criteria:

**1. Based on the Material Injected:**

*   **Cementitious Grouts:**
    *   **Cement-Slurry Grouts:** Portland cement mixed with water.
    *   **Cement-Sand Grouts:** Cement, sand, and water.
    *   **Silicate-Cement Grouts:** Combination of cement and silicate grouts for enhanced properties.
*   **Chemical Grouts:**
    *   **Acrylamide Grouts:** Polymerize to form a gel, offering excellent impermeability and flexibility.
    *   **Urethane Grouts:** React with water to form a foam or gel, used for sealing leaks and voids.
    *   **Epoxy Grouts:** Two-part systems that cure to a rigid, high-strength material, used for crack repair.
    *   **Silicate Grouts:** Can be organic (e.g., sodium silicate with organic hardeners) or inorganic (e.g., sodium silicate with mineral salts).
*   **Bituminous Grouts:** Asphalt emulsions or dispersions, used for waterproofing and sealing.
*   **Suspension Grouts:** Fine solid particles suspended in a liquid medium, typically cement-based.

**2. Based on the Injection Process:**

*   **Permeation Grouting:** Grout flows through the pores or fissures of the soil/rock without significantly disturbing the soil structure.
*   **Compaction Grouting:** High-viscosity grout is injected at high pressure to densify and displace the soil, filling voids and increasing density.
*   **Fracture Grouting (or Hydrofracturing/Jet Grouting):** Grout is injected at high pressure to create fractures in the soil/rock, allowing the grout to penetrate deeper and wider.
*   **Jet Grouting:** Uses high-pressure jets of grout (and sometimes water/air) to erode and mix the soil with grout in situ, forming cemented columns.

**3. Based on the Purpose:**

*   **Consolidation Grouting:** To improve the strength and stiffness of competent but permeable rock or fill.
*   **Water Sealing Grouting:** To reduce permeability and control groundwater flow.
*   **Stop-off Grouting:** To block the flow of water or contaminants.
*   **Stabilization Grouting:** To improve the stability of slopes or foundations.

### 1.3. Grout Constituents and Properties

*   **Binder:** The primary solid material that hardens and provides strength (e.g., cement, polymers).
*   **Water:** The solvent or carrier for the binder, affecting viscosity and setting time.
*   **Additives/Admixtures:**
    *   **Accelerators:** Speed up setting time (e.g., calcium chloride).
    *   **Retarders:** Slow down setting time (e.g., lignosulfonates).
    *   **Dispersants/Plasticizers:** Improve flowability and reduce water content.
    *   **Extenders:** Reduce cost and improve certain properties (e.g., fly ash, bentonite).
    *   **Viscosity Modifiers:** Control grout flow.
    *   **Antifoaming Agents:** Prevent excessive foaming.

**Key Grout Properties:**

*   **Viscosity:** Resistance to flow. Influences penetration capability.
*   **Setting Time:** Time taken for the grout to harden.
*   **Compressive Strength:** Ability to withstand compressive loads.
*   **Permeability:** Rate of fluid flow through the hardened grout.
*   **Rheology:** Study of the flow and deformation of matter.
*   **Stability:** Resistance to segregation and bleeding.

## 2. Permeation Grouting

### 2.1. Principle and Mechanism

*   **Principle:** Grout is injected under low to moderate pressure, flowing into and filling the pore spaces or fissures of the soil or rock.
*   **Mechanism:** The grout must have a viscosity and particle size that allows it to pass through the void network without blockage. The grout solidifies in situ, reducing permeability and/or increasing strength.

### 2.2. Types of Permeation Grouting

**1. Soil Grouting:**

*   **Fine-Grained Soils (Sands and Silts):**
    *   **Chemical Grouts:** Preferred due to their low viscosity and ability to penetrate fine pores. Examples include silicate grouts and acrylamide grouts.
    *   **Microfine Cement Grouts:** Specially processed cement with very fine particles.
    *   **Limitations:** Very fine silts and clays have pores too small for even chemical grouts, leading to blockage.
*   **Coarse-Grained Soils (Gravels):**
    *   **Cement-Slurry Grouts:** Can be used if the voids are sufficiently large.
    *   **Cement-Sand Grouts:** For larger voids.
    *   **Limitations:** High water-cement ratios might be needed for flow, potentially reducing strength.

**2. Rock Grouting:**

*   **Fissure Grouting:**
    *   **Cement Grouts:** Most common. The particle size of the cement must be smaller than the fissure width.
    *   **Pressure:** Varies depending on fissure width and desired penetration.
*   **Void Grouting:**
    *   **Cement-Sand Grouts:** Used for larger cavities and open fractures.
    *   **Pre-placed Aggregate Grouting:** Aggregate is placed in large voids, then grout is injected to fill the remaining space.

### 2.3. Grout Injection Methods for Permeation Grouting

*   **Grout Injection Holes:**
    *   **Drill holes:** Standard practice. Holes are drilled to specific depths and patterns.
    *   **Pipes:** Grout is injected through pipes or tubes (e.g., packer tubes, hose pipes) inserted into the holes.
*   **Packers:**
    *   **Inflatable Packers:** Expand to seal the borehole above the injection zone, preventing grout escape up the hole.
    *   **Mechanical Packers:** Expand mechanically to seal the borehole.
*   **Grout Delivery:**
    *   **Grout Pumps:** Various types, including piston pumps, centrifugal pumps, and diaphragm pumps, are used to deliver grout at the required pressure and flow rate.
*   **Grouting Stages/Phases:**
    *   **Stage Grouting (or Step Grouting):** Grouting is done in stages, starting from the bottom and progressively moving up as the lower zones are filled. This ensures better control and prevents grout from bypassing already grouted sections.
    *   **Grout Curtain:** A line or barrier of grouted zones designed to intercept or control groundwater flow.

### 2.4. Design Considerations for Permeation Grouting

*   **Soil/Rock Characterization:**
    *   **Grain Size Distribution (Soils):** Determines the minimum void size and thus the groutability.
    *   **Fissure/Fracture Mapping (Rocks):** Width, spacing, and orientation of discontinuities.
    *   **Permeability:** Crucial for predicting grout penetration and selecting appropriate grout.
*   **Grout Selection:** Based on void size, permeability, required strength, and environmental considerations.
*   **Injection Pressure:** Must be sufficient to overcome hydraulic resistance and fill voids but not so high as to cause hydraulic fracturing or damage the formation.
*   **Grouting Pattern:** Spacing and depth of injection holes.
*   **Grout Mix Design:** Optimizing binder content, water-cement ratio, and additives.
*   **Monitoring:** Pressure, flow rate, and sometimes grout take to assess progress and effectiveness.

## 3. Compaction Grouting

### 3.1. Principle and Mechanism

*   **Principle:** Injecting a stiff, low-slump grout (often cement-based) at high pressure into loose, granular soils.
*   **Mechanism:** The grout does not permeate the pores. Instead, the high injection pressure forces the grout into the soil, displacing and densifying the surrounding soil mass. The grout forms discrete, bulbous masses (or "lenses") that expand outwards, pushing the soil particles together.

### 3.2. Grout Mix for Compaction Grouting

*   **Stiff Consistency:** Low water-cement ratio (typically 0.35 to 0.50).
*   **Composition:** Cement, water, and sometimes fine sand or bentonite to control flow and shrinkage.
*   **Viscosity:** High, to prevent permeation.

### 3.3. Injection Process

*   **Injection Holes:** Drilled into the soil mass.
*   **Injection Ports:** Multiple ports at different elevations along the injection pipe.
*   **Injection Pressure:** High pressures are required (e.g., 200-500 psi or higher) to achieve displacement.
*   **Grout Bulbs:** The grout forms a dense, semi-rigid "bulb" as it's injected. The injection is stopped when the desired pressure is reached or the grout bulb expands to fill the surrounding soil.
*   **Grouting Pattern:** Offset pattern is common, with injection holes spaced to ensure overlap of the grout bulbs for complete densification.

### 3.4. Applications of Compaction Grouting

*   **Densification of Loose Sands:** Improving bearing capacity and reducing settlement.
*   **Stabilization of Collapsible Soils:** Preventing collapse upon wetting.
*   **Control of Differential Settlement:** Lifting settled structures by strategically injecting grout beneath them.
*   **Remediation of Sinkholes:** Filling voids and stabilizing the surrounding soil.

### 3.5. Advantages and Limitations

*   **Advantages:**
    *   Effective in granular soils.
    *   Can achieve significant ground densification.
    *   Can lift structures.
    *   Low risk of hydraulic fracturing of the ground.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Less effective in fine-grained soils (clays and silts) due to their low permeability and compressibility.
    *   Grout bulbs can be irregular in shape.
    *   Requires high injection pressures.

## 4. Jet Grouting

### 4.1. Principle and Mechanism

*   **Principle:** Uses high-velocity jets of grout (and sometimes water/air) to erode the soil in situ and mix it with the grout, forming solidified columns or panels.
*   **Mechanism:** A rotating cutting head with high-pressure nozzles is used. As the rods are withdrawn and/or rotated, the soil is eroded and mixed with the injected grout. The mixture then sets, forming a cemented soil matrix.

### 4.2. Types of Jet Grouting

The key difference lies in the number of fluid streams and their combination:

*   **Single Fluid (Type I):** High-pressure grout is injected through a single nozzle. The soil is eroded and mixed by the grout jet.
*   **Double Fluid (Type II):** Grout is injected through a central nozzle, while a high-pressure air or water shroud surrounds it. The air/water jet assists in soil erosion and slurry removal.
*   **Triple Fluid (Type III):** Grout, water, and air are injected simultaneously through separate nozzles. This offers the best control over erosion and mixing, and allows for efficient removal of spoils.

### 4.3. Grout Mix for Jet Grouting

*   **Typically Cement-Based:** Portland cement, water, and sometimes additives like bentonite or polymers for improved performance.
*   **Low Viscosity:** Required for effective spraying and mixing.

### 4.4. Equipment and Process

*   **Drilling Rig:** Capable of drilling to required depths with rotation.
*   **High-Pressure Pump:** For grout, water, and air.
*   **Grouting Unit:** Controls the flow and pressure of injected fluids.
*   **Cutting Head:** Equipped with nozzles at the tip of the drill string.
*   **Process:**
    1.  Drill to the desired depth.
    2.  Inject grout (and/or water/air) while withdrawing and/or rotating the drill string.
    3.  The speed of withdrawal and rotation, as well as the fluid pressures and flow rates, are critical parameters controlling the diameter and strength of the cemented column.

### 4.5. Applications of Jet Grouting

*   **Foundation Support:** Creating load-bearing columns.
*   **Excavation Support:** Constructing cut-off walls or retaining structures.
*   **Water Seepage Control:** Forming impermeable barriers.
*   **Tunnel Linings:** Stabilizing ground ahead of tunnel boring machines.
*   **Remediation of Contaminated Sites:** Encapsulating or solidifying contaminants.

### 4.6. Advantages and Limitations

*   **Advantages:**
    *   Forms strong, cemented columns or panels.
    *   Effective in a wide range of soil types, including soft clays and silts.
    *   Can achieve large diameters.
    *   Good for creating continuous barriers.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Can be expensive.
    *   Requires specialized equipment and skilled operators.
    *   Generates significant spoil material.
    *   Column uniformity can be challenging to control.

## 5. Grouting Equipment and Instrumentation

### 5.1. Grout Mixing Equipment

*   **Colloidal Mixers:** Essential for achieving a stable, homogeneous grout suspension. They use high shear forces to disperse the solid particles effectively.
*   **Holding Tanks:** Store mixed grout before pumping.
*   **Agitators:** Keep grout suspended in holding tanks.

### 5.2. Grout Pumping Equipment

*   **Piston Pumps:** Common for cementitious grouts, capable of generating high pressures.
*   **Diaphragm Pumps:** Suitable for both cementitious and chemical grouts, often used for lower pressures.
*   **Progressive Cavity Pumps:** Used for viscous grouts.
*   **Positive Displacement Pumps:** Generally preferred for consistent flow.

### 5.3. Grouting Accessories

*   **Hoses and Couplings:** High-pressure rated hoses.
*   **Valves:** For controlling grout flow and pressure.
*   **Manifolds:** To distribute grout to multiple injection points.
*   **Packers:** (As discussed in Permeation Grouting)

### 5.4. Instrumentation and Monitoring

*   **Pressure Gauges:** Monitor injection pressure at the pump and at the injection point.
*   **Flow Meters:** Measure the rate of grout being injected.
*   **Grout Volume Counters:** Record the total volume of grout injected.
*   **Load Cells:** Monitor the load on injection pipes.
*   **Data Acquisition Systems:** Record and log all relevant parameters for analysis.
*   **Downhole Instrumentation (less common for standard grouting):** Inclination tools, cameras, etc., for monitoring borehole conditions.

## 6. Design Considerations and Quality Control

### 6.1. Site Investigation and Characterization

*   **Geotechnical Investigation:** Boreholes, CPT, SPT, laboratory testing (grain size, Atterberg limits, permeability, shear strength).
*   **Hydrogeological Investigation:** Groundwater levels, flow direction, and pumping tests.
*   **Rock Mass Characterization:** RQD, fracture mapping, discontinuity surveys.

### 6.2. Groutability Assessment

*   **Soil Groutability:** Based on grain size distribution (e.g., grain size ratios for cement and chemical grouts).
*   **Rock Groutability:** Based on fissure width and spacing.

### 6.3. Grout Mix Design and Testing

*   **Laboratory Testing:**
    *   **Flow Cone Test:** Measures viscosity and stability.
    *   **Marsh Funnel Test:** Simpler viscosity measurement.
    *   **Setting Time Tests (Vicat apparatus).**
    *   **Compressive Strength Tests:** On grout cubes or cylinders.
    *   **Permeability Tests:** On hardened grout specimens.
    *   **Bleeding/Segregation Tests.**
*   **Field Trials:** Small-scale grouting tests to verify groutability and effectiveness.

### 6.4. Quality Control During Grouting Operations

*   **Pre-Grouting Checks:** Verify grout mix proportions, equipment calibration, and injection hole integrity.
*   **During Grouting Monitoring:**
    *   **Grout Take:** Monitor the volume of grout injected per unit length of hole or per injection stage. A sudden increase in grout take can indicate a large void or fracture. A sudden decrease might indicate blockage.
    *   **Pressure and Flow Rate:** Maintain within specified limits.
    *   **Grout Consistency:** Periodically check batch consistency.
*   **Post-Grouting Verification:**
    *   **Redrilling/Probe Drilling:** To check penetration and degree of consolidation.
    *   **Permeability Tests:** In the treated ground.
    *   **In-situ Tests:** Plate load tests, SPT, CPT, or pressuremeter tests.
    *   **Core Sampling:** For laboratory testing of the grouted material.

### 6.5. Environmental Considerations

*   **Grout Material Selection:** Use of environmentally friendly additives.
*   **Spill Containment:** Procedures to prevent grout spills.
*   **Spoil Disposal:** Proper disposal of excess grout or eroded soil.
*   **Groundwater Contamination:** Careful selection of grout materials and injection techniques to avoid contaminating potable groundwater sources.

## 7. Case Studies and Examples

*   **Tunnel Boring Machine (TBM) Breakthrough Support:** Permeation grouting used to stabilize the ground ahead of TBMs, preventing collapses.
*   **Dam Grouting:** Grouting of dam abutments and foundations to reduce seepage and improve stability.
*   **Foundation Improvement for Bridges and Buildings:** Compaction grouting to densify loose sands or lift settled structures.
*   **Cut-off Walls for Contaminated Sites:** Jet grouting to create impermeable barriers to contain pollutants.
*   **Slope Stabilization:** Grouting of fissures in rock slopes to improve shear strength.

## 9. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**

*   **Permeation Grouting:**
    *   **Principle:** Grout flows through the pores or fissures of the soil/rock.
    *   **Mechanism:** Fills voids, reduces permeability, and can increase strength by bonding particles.
    *   **Applications:** Water sealing in rock masses, stabilizing porous soils, filling voids in granular soils.
*   **Compaction Grouting:**
    *   **Princ:** Stiff grout is injected at high pressure to displace and densify soil.
    *   **Mechanism:** Forms bulbous masses that push soil particles together, increasing density. Grout does not permeate pores.
    *   **Applications:** Densifying loose sands, underpinning structures, controlling settlement.

**Answer 2:**

The primary constituents of cementitious grouts are:

1.  **Binder:** Typically Portland cement (e.g., Type I, Type II, or specialized cement like microsilica cement). This is the material that hardens and provides strength.
2.  **Water:** Acts as the carrier and activator for the cement hydration process. The water-cement ratio is critical for grout workability and strength.

**Additives** play various roles:

*   **Accelerators:** Speed up setting time (e.g., calcium chloride).
*   **Retarders:** Slow down setting time (e.g., lignosulfonates) for longer travel distances or complex operations.
*   **Dispersants/Plasticizers:** Improve fluidity and reduce the water required for a given consistency, leading to higher strength.
*   **Extenders:** Such as bentonite or fly ash, can reduce cost, improve suspension stability, and modify rheology.
*   **Antifoaming agents:** Prevent unwanted foam formation.

**Answer 3:**

The triple fluid jet grouting process involves injecting three separate streams from the drill head:

1.  **Grout:** Injected through a central nozzle.
2.  **Water:** Injected through an outer ring of nozzles, surrounding the grout jet.
3.  **Air:** Injected through an innermost ring of nozzles, often surrounding the grout jet.

**Why it's preferred:**

*   **Enhanced Erosion:** The combination of high-pressure water and air jets effectively erodes the soil more efficiently than grout alone or a water/grout combination.
*   **Improved Slurry Removal:** The air stream helps lift and carry away the eroded soil and excess water, preventing it from mixing back into the grout column and ensuring a cleaner, stronger cemented matrix.
*   **Better Control:** Allows for independent control of grout, water, and air flow rates and pressures, leading to better control over the diameter and properties of the cemented column.
*   **Reduced Contamination:** The outer shroud of water and air can help prevent grout from escaping upwards in uncontrolled ways.

**Answer 4:**

For reducing the permeability of a clean sand formation for a deep excavation, the following would be recommended:

*   **Type of Grouting:** **Permeation Grouting**. This is suitable because clean sands have pores large enough for grout to flow into and fill.
*   **Type of Grout:** **Chemical Grout** (e.g., silicate-based grout or acrylamide-based grout) or **Microfine Cement Grout**.
    *   **Chemical Grouts:** Are preferred for clean sands because they have very low viscosity and can penetrate the relatively small pore spaces between sand grains. They polymerize in situ to form a solid or gel, effectively blocking water flow.
    *   **Microfine Cement Grouts:** Specially manufactured cement with extremely fine particles can also be used if the pores are slightly larger or if a higher strength is also desired alongside permeability reduction.

**Reasoning:** Standard cement grouts might have particles too large to permeate clean sands effectively, leading to blockage rather than filling the pores. Compaction grouting would densify the sand but might not achieve the desired level of impermeability as the voids between grains would still exist, just closer together. Jet grouting could create a barrier but is often overkill and more expensive for a simple permeability reduction in sand compared to permeation grouting.

**Answer 5:**

Key quality control measures during permeation grouting operations include:

1.  **Grout Mix Verification:** Ensure the correct proportions of cement, water, and additives are used for each batch, and that mixing is thorough (colloidal mixing is essential).
2.  **Monitoring Grout Take:** Record the volume of grout injected per unit length of the borehole or per injection stage. Significant deviations from expected takes indicate potential issues (e.g., larger fractures, blockages).
3.  **Pressure and Flow Rate Control:** Maintain injection pressure within the design limits. Excessive pressure can cause hydraulic fracturing or damage the formation. Consistent flow rates are also important.
4.  **Batch Consistency Checks:** Periodically check the viscosity (e.g., flow cone test) and setting time of freshly mixed grout to ensure consistency between batches.
5.  **Observing Injection Behavior:** Note any signs of grout returning up the borehole, unexpected pressure build-up, or lack of grout take.
6.  **Hole Integrity:** Ensure the integrity of the injection holes and packers during the process.
7.  **Logging and Documentation:** Maintain detailed records of all grouting activities, including date, time, hole number, depth, injection pressures, flow rates, grout take, and any observations.
8.  **Post-Grouting Verification:** Conduct follow-up tests (e.g., redrilling, permeability tests) to confirm the effectiveness of the grouting program.

## 10. Important Points to Remember

*   **Groutability:** The ability of a grout to penetrate the void network of a soil or rock is the most critical factor in permeation grouting. This is primarily governed by the relative size of grout particles to void spaces.
*   **Grout Viscosity:** Lower viscosity generally means better penetration. Cement grouts have higher viscosity than most chemical grouts.
*   **Pressure Control:** Crucial to prevent hydraulic fracturing or damage to the formation.
*   **Compaction Grouting vs. Permeation Grouting:** Distinct mechanisms; one displaces and densifies, the other fills pores.
*   **Jet Grouting:** Forms cemented columns by eroding and mixing soil with grout, creating strong structural elements or barriers.
*   **Quality Control:** Essential throughout the process, from mix design to field execution and verification, to ensure the desired ground improvement is achieved.
*   **Site-Specific Design:** The choice of grouting technique, grout material, and design parameters must be tailored to the specific ground conditions and project requirements.
