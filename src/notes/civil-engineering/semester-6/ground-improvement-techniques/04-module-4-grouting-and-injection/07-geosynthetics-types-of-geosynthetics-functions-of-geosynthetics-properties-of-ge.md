# GROUND IMPROVEMENT TECHNIQUES: MODULE 4: GROUTING AND INJECTION

## Topic: Geosynthetics – Types, Functions, and Properties

---

### **1. Introduction to Geosynthetics**

*   **Definition:** Geosynthetics are planar products manufactured from polymeric materials used with soil, rock, earth, or any other geotechnical engineering related material as an integral part of a man-made project, structure, or system. They are synthetic materials designed to enhance the performance of soil and rock structures.
*   **Purpose:** Geosynthetics are employed in civil engineering projects to address various geotechnical challenges, including soil stabilization, drainage, filtration, separation, reinforcement, and containment.
*   **Historical Context:** The use of geosynthetics has grown significantly over the past few decades due to their effectiveness, cost-efficiency, and durability.

---

### **2. Types of Geosynthetics**

Geosynthetics are classified based on their manufacturing process, structure, and primary function. The main types are:

#### **2.1 Geotextiles**

*   **Definition:** Permeable fabrics manufactured from polymeric materials. They are the most common type of geosynthetic.
*   **Manufacturing Methods:**
    *   **Woven:** Yarns are interlaced in a regular pattern (like a loom). They generally have high tensile strength and low elongation.
        *   *Example:* Fabrics used for reinforcement in retaining walls or embankments.
    *   **Non-woven:** Fibers or filaments are bonded together by mechanical entanglement, thermal bonding, or chemical bonding. They are more flexible and have good filtration and drainage properties.
        *   *Example:* Fabrics used as filters beneath riprap or drainage layers.
    *   **Knitted:** Yarns are interlocked with needles. Less common for standard geotechnical applications but used in some specialized reinforcement products.
*   **Primary Functions:** Separation, Filtration, Drainage, Reinforcement, Protection.

#### **2.2 Geomembranes**

*   **Definition:** Impermeable or low-permeability polymeric sheets.
*   **Manufacturing:** Typically produced by extrusion.
*   **Primary Functions:** Liquid and gas containment, barrier functions.
    *   *Example:* Liners for landfills, ponds, canals, and tunnels to prevent leakage.
*   **Types:**
    *   **HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene):** High chemical resistance, UV resistance, and strength.
    *   **LLDPE (Linear Low-Density Polyethylene):** More flexible than HDPE, easier to install.
    *   **PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride):** Very flexible, used in applications requiring good conformability.

#### **2.3 Geogrids**

*   **Definition:** Polymeric structures formed into an open grid pattern with apertures of sufficient size to allow the interlock of soil or fill material.
*   **Manufacturing:** Can be made by stretching extruded sheets or by weaving/knitting/welding polymeric strands.
*   **Primary Functions:** Reinforcement.
    *   *Example:* Reinforcing soil in roads, railway tracks, and steep slopes to increase bearing capacity and stability.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Uniaxial:** Stronger in one direction (machine direction).
    *   **Biaxial:** Similar strength in both directions (machine and cross-machine directions).
    *   **Triaxial:** Designed for interlocking with aggregate in three dimensions, providing superior confinement.

#### **2.4 Geonets**

*   **Definition:** Polymeric structures formed into an open, grid-like network with apertures.
*   **Manufacturing:** Typically made by extruding polymers in a continuous process, forming interconnected filaments.
*   **Primary Functions:** Drainage. They provide a continuous pathway for fluid flow.
    *   *Example:* Drainage layers behind retaining walls or under foundations. Can be combined with geotextiles for filtration.

#### **2.5 Geocomposites**

*   **Definition:** Factory-fabricated combination of two or more geosynthetic materials (or geosynthetics and other materials) designed to perform multiple functions.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Geotextile-Geogrid:** For reinforcement and separation/filtration.
    *   **Geotextile-Geonet:** For drainage and filtration (often called a drainage geocomposite).
    *   **Geomembrane-Geotextile:** For lining and protection/filtration.
    *   **Geotextile-Geomembrane-Geotextile:** Commonly used as a barrier system.

#### **2.6 Geosynthetic Clay Liners (GCLs)**

*   **Definition:** Factory-manufactured hydraulic barriers consisting of a layer of bentonite clay sand wiched between two geotextiles or bonded to a geomembrane.
*   **Primary Function:** Hydraulic barrier.
*   **Example:** Used in landfill liners, capping systems, and containment ponds. The bentonite swells when hydrated to form a low-permeability layer.

#### **2.7 Geocells**

*   **Definition:** Three-dimensional, honeycomb-like structures formed from polymeric strips that are joined together at their edges.
*   **Primary Function:** Reinforcement and confinement of soil or aggregate.
*   **Example:** Used for slope stabilization, channel protection, load support, and erosion control. When filled with soil or aggregate, they create a stiffened mattress that distributes loads.

### **3. Functions of Geosynthetics**

Geosynthetics perform one or more of the following key functions in geotechnical engineering:

*   **Reinforcement:**
    *   **Definition:** To improve the strength and/or stability of a soil mass by introducing tensile or compressive elements.
    *   **Mechanism:** Tensile forces are transferred from the soil to the geosynthetic, increasing the overall strength of the composite material. Interlocking with the soil is crucial.
    *   *Example:* Geogrids in road bases, geotextiles in steep slopes.

*   **Separation:**
    *   **Definition:** To prevent the intermixing of two dissimilar materials, such as soil and aggregate, while allowing fluid flow.
    *   **Mechanism:** The geosynthetic acts as a physical barrier between layers.
    *   *Example:* Geotextiles between a subgrade and a base course in road construction to prevent fine soil particles from migrating into the aggregate.

*   **Filtration:**
    *   **Definition:** To allow the passage of fluids while retaining soil particles.
    *   **Mechanism:** The pore size of the geosynthetic is designed to be small enough to retain fine particles but large enough to allow water flow.
    *   *Example:* Geotextiles behind retaining walls to prevent soil erosion while allowing water to drain.

*   **Drainage:**
    *   **Definition:** To provide a pathway for fluids to move within the plane of the geosynthetic.
    *   **Mechanism:** The structure of the geosynthetic (e.g., non-woven geotextiles, geonets) allows for rapid fluid transport.
    *   *Example:* Geonets or multi-layered drainage composites behind retaining walls or under foundations.

*   **Barrier (Containment):**
    *   **Definition:** To prevent or control the movement of liquids or gases.
    *   **Mechanism:** Geomembranes and GCLs have very low permeability, acting as effective barriers.
    *   *Example:* Geomembranes in landfill liners, GCLs in capping systems.

*   **Protection:**
    *   **Definition:** To prevent or reduce localized damage to another material due to abrasion, puncture, or impact.
    *   **Mechanism:** The geosynthetic acts as a sacrificial layer or a cushion.
    *   *Example:* Geotextiles protecting geomembranes from puncture by sharp objects or rough subgrades.

*   **Erosion Control:**
    *   **Definition:** To prevent or minimize the loss of soil due to wind or water.
    *   **Mechanism:** Geosynthetics can stabilize the soil surface, provide a matrix for vegetation growth, or act as a physical barrier.
    *   *Example:* Geotextile erosion control blankets, geocells filled with soil.

### **4. Properties of Geosynthetics**

The performance of a geosynthetic is governed by its physical and mechanical properties. Key properties include:

#### **4.1 Physical Properties**

*   **Mass per Unit Area (Grams per square meter - gsm or oz/yd²):**
    *   **Definition:** The weight of the geosynthetic per unit area. It is a primary indicator of the material's thickness and robustness.
    *   **Importance:** Higher mass generally correlates with higher strength and durability.

*   **Thickness (mm or mils):**
    *   **Definition:** The perpendicular distance between the two surfaces of the geosynthetic.
    *   **Importance:** Influences compressibility, hydraulic conductivity, and ability to deform.

*   **Apparent Opening Size (AOS) or Permittivity (s⁻¹):**
    *   **Definition:**
        *   **AOS (Permeable Geosynthetics - e.g., Geotextiles):** The smallest sieve size that will be retained by the geosynthetic. It dictates filtration capability. Typically determined by sieving analysis.
        *   **Permittivity (Permeable Geosynthetics):** The rate of flow through a geosynthetic under a unit hydraulic gradient. It is a measure of the flow capacity.
    *   **Importance:** Crucial for filtration and drainage functions. AOS ensures soil retention, while permittivity ensures adequate flow.

*   **Hydraulic Conductivity (cm/s or m/s):**
    *   **Definition:** The rate at which water can pass through the geosynthetic under a given hydraulic gradient.
    *   **Importance:** Directly related to drainage and filtration performance.

*   **UV Resistance:**
    *   **Definition:** The ability of the geosynthetic to resist degradation when exposed to ultraviolet radiation from sunlight.
    *   **Importance:** Critical for applications where the geosynthetic will be exposed to sunlight during construction or for its service life.

*   **Chemical Resistance:**
    *   **Definition:** The ability of the geosynthetic to resist degradation when exposed to chemicals in the soil or leachate.
    *   **Importance:** Essential for long-term durability in aggressive environments.

#### **4.2 Mechanical Properties**

*   **Tensile Strength (kN/m or lb/in):**
    *   **Definition:** The maximum tensile stress a geosynthetic can withstand before failure.
    *   **Importance:** Key property for reinforcement applications. Often specified as:
        *   **Grab Tensile Strength:** Measured on a wide specimen.
        *   **Strip Tensile Strength:** Measured on a narrow specimen.
        *   **Tensile Modulus (Secant or Tangent):** The slope of the stress-strain curve, indicating stiffness.

*   **Elongation at Break (%):**
    *   **Definition:** The percentage increase in length of the geosynthetic at its breaking point.
    *   **Importance:** High elongation can be beneficial for conformability but may reduce stiffness for reinforcement.

*   **Puncture Resistance (N or lbf):**
    *   **Definition:** The force required to puncture the geosynthetic by a standard probe.
    *   **Importance:** Crucial for protecting underlying layers (e.g., geomembranes) from damage. Tested using methods like the Cone Penetration Test.

*   **Shear Strength (kPa or psi):**
    *   **Definition:** The ability of the geosynthetic to resist sliding failure between itself and the soil or between two geosynthetic layers.
    *   **Importance:** Important for reinforcement and stability analysis, especially in steep slopes or reinforced soil walls. Often measured as interface friction angle.

*   **Creep Resistance:**
    *   **Definition:** The ability of the geosynthetic to resist deformation under sustained tensile load over time.
    *   **Importance:** Critical for long-term reinforcement applications where permanent loads are present.

*   **Conformability:**
    *   **Definition:** The ability of the geosynthetic to conform to the irregularities of the soil surface.
    *   **Importance:** Important for ensuring good contact and stress transfer in reinforcement applications and for effective sealing in barrier applications.

### **5. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Functionality is Key:** The selection of a geosynthetic depends entirely on the specific function it needs to perform in a given project.
*   **Interlocking is Crucial for Reinforcement:** For geogrids and some geotextiles to reinforce soil effectively, good interlocking between the geosynthetic apertures and the soil/aggregate particles is essential.
*   **Proper Installation:** Correct installation practices are vital to ensure geosynthetics perform as intended and to prevent premature damage.
*   **Durability:** Consider the expected service life of the structure and the environmental conditions (chemical, biological, UV exposure) when selecting geosynthetics.
*   **Testing Standards:** Geosynthetic properties are measured according to standardized testing procedures (e.g., ASTM, ISO). Always refer to relevant standards for material specifications.
*   **Composite Functions:** Many applications benefit from geosynthetic composites, combining the advantages of different geosynthetic types.

### **6. Practice Questions & Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Which type of geosynthetic is primarily used for liquid and gas containment?
a) Geotextile
b) Geogrid
c) Geomembrane
d) Geonet

**Question 2:**
You are designing a road base. The purpose is to prevent the subgrade soil from mixing with the crushed stone aggregate, thereby maintaining the aggregate's drainage and load-bearing capacity. Which geosynthetic function is most critical here, and which type would be most suitable?

**Question 3:**
Explain the difference between AOS (Apparent Opening Size) and Permittivity. For which geosynthetic function are these properties most important?

**Question 4:**
A steep slope needs stabilization. The primary goal is to increase the shear strength of the soil mass.
a) List two types of geosynthetics commonly used for this purpose.
b) Describe the primary function of geosynthetics in this scenario.

**Question 5:**
Define the property of "Creep Resistance" for geosynthetics and explain its importance in a specific application.

---

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

**Answer 1:**
c) Geomembrane

**Answer 2:**
The critical function is **Separation**. A **woven** or **non-woven geotextile** would be most suitable, as they are permeable and will prevent intermixing while allowing water to drain.

**Answer 3:**
*   **AOS (Apparent Opening Size):** Measures the largest particle size that will *pass through* the geosynthetic. It is crucial for **filtration**, ensuring that soil particles are retained while water passes.
*   **Permittivity:** Measures the *rate of flow* through the geosynthetic. It is important for **drainage** and ensuring that water does not build up behind the geosynthetic.

**Answer 4:**
a) Two types of geosynthetics commonly used for slope stabilization (reinforcement) are:
    *   **Geogrids** (especially biaxial or triaxial geogrids)
    *   **Woven geotextiles** with high tensile strength.
b) The primary function of geosynthetics in this scenario is **Reinforcement**. They increase the shear strength and stiffness of the soil mass, improving its stability and resistance to sliding.

**Answer 5:**
**Creep Resistance** is the ability of a geosynthetic to resist gradual deformation under a sustained, constant load over an extended period.
Its importance is in **long-term reinforcement applications**, such as in reinforced soil walls or bridge abutments, where the geosynthetic will be subjected to permanent loads from the retained soil. Poor creep resistance can lead to excessive deformation and eventual failure of the structure. Geosynthetics with high creep resistance (often indicated by higher long-term design strengths) are chosen for these applications to ensure the structural integrity over the design life of the project.
