# GROUND IMPROVEMENT TECHNIQUES - MODULE 4: GROUTING AND INJECTION

## Topic: Chemicals and Materials Used in Grouting and Injection

---

### 1. Introduction to Grouting and Injection

Grouting and injection are techniques used to fill voids, cracks, and pores in soil or rock masses, or to introduce materials that can consolidate, strengthen, or waterproof the ground. This process involves pumping fluid materials (grouts) under pressure into the ground. The choice of chemicals and materials is critical and depends heavily on the specific ground conditions, the desired outcome, and environmental considerations.

---

### 2. Learning Outcomes Covered:

This module will equip you with the knowledge to:

*   Identify and describe the various categories of chemical grouts used in ground improvement.
*   Understand the properties and applications of inorganic grouts.
*   Understand the properties and applications of organic grouts.
*   Discuss the role and properties of various additives used in grouting mixtures.
*   Explain the importance of selecting the appropriate grout material based on ground conditions and project objectives.

---

### 3. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Grout:** A fluid or semi-fluid material that can be injected into the ground to fill voids, cracks, or pores. It typically consists of a binder, water, and sometimes additives.
*   **Injection:** The process of forcing grout into the ground under pressure.
*   **Permeability:** The ability of a porous material to allow fluids to pass through it. Grouting aims to reduce permeability.
*   **Cohesion:** The internal resistance of a soil to deformation or failure. Grouting can increase cohesion.
*   **Void Filling:** The primary function of grout to fill empty spaces in the ground.
*   **Consolidation:** The process of strengthening and densifying soil or rock.
*   **Waterproofing:** Preventing the ingress of water into a structure or ground mass.
*   **Setting Time:** The time taken for a grout to transition from a fluid to a solid state.
*   **Viscosity:** A measure of a fluid's resistance to flow.
*   **Gel Time:** The time taken for a chemical grout to transform from a liquid into a gel-like consistency.
*   **Strength:** The ability of the hardened grout to resist applied loads.
*   **Durability:** The ability of the hardened grout to withstand environmental conditions over time.

---

### 4. Categories of Grouting Chemicals and Materials

Grouting materials can be broadly categorized into two main types:

*   **Particulate Grouts (Suspensions):** These consist of fine solid particles suspended in a liquid medium (usually water).
    *   Examples: Cementitious grouts, bentonite grouts, lime-based grouts.
*   **Chemical Grouts (Solutions):** These are true solutions of chemicals that react and polymerize to form a solid or gel matrix.
    *   Examples: Silicates, acrylics, epoxies, polyurethanes.

---

### 5. Inorganic Grouts

Inorganic grouts are typically cement-based or mineral-based and are characterized by their strength, durability, and cost-effectiveness.

#### 5.1. Cementitious Grouts

*   **Description:** The most common type of grout, consisting of Portland cement, water, and often fine aggregates.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Portland Cement:** The binder that hydrates and forms a solid matrix. Different types of cement (e.g., Type I, Type II, Type III for rapid hardening) can be used.
    *   **Water:** The mixing medium and essential for the hydration process. The water-to-cement ratio (w/c) significantly affects strength, permeability, and fluidity.
    *   **Fine Aggregates (Sand):** Used in coarser grouts to fill larger voids and reduce shrinkage.
    *   **Additives:** (Discussed in detail in Section 7).
*   **Properties:**
    *   High strength upon hardening.
    *   Good durability.
    *   Low cost.
    *   Relatively high viscosity, limiting penetration into fine pores and cracks.
    *   Can experience shrinkage during drying.
    *   Slower setting times compared to some chemical grouts.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Filling large voids and cavities.
    *   Stabilizing rock masses and tunnels.
    *   Dam seepage control.
    *   Soil consolidation in coarser grained soils (sands and gravels).
    *   Annular space grouting around pipes and piles.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Slurry Grouting:** Cement and water mixture.
    *   **Mortar Grouting:** Cement, sand, and water.
    *   **Microfine Cement Grouting:** Using extremely fine cement particles (e.g., < 10 microns) to penetrate smaller voids than conventional cement.

#### 5.2. Silicate Grouts

*   **Description:** Based on sodium silicate (water glass) or potassium silicate solutions, which react with a hardener to form a silica gel.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Sodium Silicate Solution:** The base chemical.
    *   **Hardener/Activator:** Typically an organic compound (like esters or amides) or an inorganic salt (like calcium chloride or aluminum sulfate) that reacts with the silicate to precipitate silica.
*   **Properties:**
    *   Can achieve very low permeabilities.
    *   Relatively fast setting times.
    *   Moderate strength.
    *   Can be susceptible to chemical attack.
    *   Environmentally friendlier than some organic grouts.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Water control in granular soils.
    *   Tunnel lining waterproofing.
    *   Stabilization of sand formations.
    *   Foundation underpinning.
*   **Example:**
    *   **Two-shot silicate grouting:** The silicate solution and hardener are mixed just before injection.

#### 5.3. Other Inorganic Grouts

*   **Lime-Based Grouts:** Less common but can be used for specific applications, often for soil stabilization with fly ash or slag.
*   **Gypsum Grouts:** Rapid setting, used for temporary support.

### 6. Organic Grouts

Organic grouts are typically polymer-based and offer a wide range of properties, including very low viscosity, rapid setting, high strength, and excellent chemical resistance. They are often more expensive than inorganic grouts.

#### 6.1. Acrylamide-Based Grouts (Phosphate-Stabilized)

*   **Description:** A solution of acrylamide monomer and methylene bis-acrylamide (cross-linker) polymerized by a redox catalyst system.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Acrylamide Monomer:** The primary building block.
    *   **Methylene Bis-Acrylamide:** Acts as a cross-linker, forming a strong polymer network.
    *   **Catalyst System:** Typically a combination of an initiator (e.g., ammonium persulfate) and an accelerator (e.g., TEMED - Tetramethylethylenediamine) or a redox system.
*   **Properties:**
    *   Extremely low viscosity, allowing penetration into very fine pores and cracks.
    *   Can achieve high strength and low permeability.
    *   Can be formulated for a wide range of setting times.
    *   **Crucial Note:** Acrylamide monomer is a neurotoxin and carcinogen, requiring strict safety precautions and handling. Its use is now restricted or banned in many regions.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Waterproofing of tunnels and dams.
    *   Stabilization of fine sands and silts.
    *   Control of groundwater flow.
*   **Example:**   Grout used for sealing underground structures and controlling water in fine granular soils.

#### 6.2. Acrylic Grouts

*   **Description:** Based on acrylic monomers (like methyl methacrylate or dimethacrylate) that polymerize through a chemical reaction, often initiated by a catalyst and accelerator.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Acrylic Monomer(s):** Provide the basic resin structure.
    *   **Catalyst/Initiator:** Starts the polymerization process.
    *   **Accelerator:** Speeds up the reaction.
*   **Properties:**
    *   Low viscosity.
    *   Fast setting times.
    *   Good strength and flexibility.
    *   Excellent chemical resistance.
    *   Relatively non-toxic compared to acrylamide.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Crack injection in concrete structures.
    *   Waterproofing.
    *   Soil stabilization in granular materials.
    *   Repair of underground structures.

#### 6.3. Polyurethane Grouts

*   **Description:** Based on polyols and isocyanates, which react to form a polyurethane polymer. They can be single-component (moisture-curing) or two-component.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Isocyanates:** Highly reactive components.
    *   **Polyols:** Contain hydroxyl groups that react with isocyanates.
    *   **Catalysts:** To control reaction rate.
*   **Properties:**
    *   Can be formulated to be rigid or flexible.
    *   Excellent waterproofing properties.
    *   Good adhesion to various substrates.
    *   Low viscosity.
    *   Reaction can be controlled to achieve specific gel times.
    *   Can foam in the presence of water, creating a seal.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Water sealing in tunnels, dams, and basements.
    *   Crack injection in concrete.
    *   Soil stabilization, especially in sandy soils.
    *   Injecting into flowing water situations.
*   **Example:**
    *   **Foaming Polyurethane Grout:** Reacts with water to expand and fill voids, providing both sealing and some structural support.

#### 6.4. Epoxy Grouts

*   **Description:** Two-component systems based on epoxy resins and hardeners.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Epoxy Resin:** The base polymer.
    *   **Hardener (Curing Agent):** Reacts with the resin to initiate cross-linking.
*   **Properties:**
    *   High strength and excellent durability.
    *   Superior chemical resistance.
    *   Low viscosity is achievable.
    *   Can be formulated for various setting times.
    *   More expensive than most other grout types.
    *   Can be sensitive to moisture during mixing and application.
*   **Applications:**
    *   Structural repairs of concrete and masonry.
    *   Anchoring bolts and dowels.
    *   High-strength ground stabilization where durability is paramount.
    *   Grouting in aggressive chemical environments.

### 7. Additives Used in Grouting Mixtures

Additives are incorporated into grout formulations to modify their properties, such as viscosity, setting time, strength, durability, and workability.

#### 7.1. Accelerators

*   **Purpose:** To decrease the setting time of the grout.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Calcium Chloride (CaCl2):** Common accelerator for cementitious grouts.
    *   **Triethanolamine (TEA):** Accelerates hydration of cement.
    *   **Organic Amines (e.g., TEMED):** Used in chemical grouts to speed up polymerization.

#### 7.2. Retarders

*   **Purpose:** To increase the setting time, allowing for longer working periods or for the grout to travel further into the ground.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Sugars (e.g., sucrose):** Can act as retarders for cement.
    *   **Lignosulfonates:** By-products of wood pulping, used as retarders and plasticizers.
    *   **Certain organic acids and their salts.**

#### 7.3. Viscosity Modifiers/Thickeners

*   **Purpose:** To increase the viscosity of the grout, which can help prevent segregation of particles (in cementitious grouts) and control flow.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Bentonite:** A clay mineral that swells in water, increasing viscosity and reducing permeability.
    *   **Cellulose Ethers (e.g., CMC, HPMC):** Provide thickening and water retention.
    *   **Silica Fume:** Very fine particles that can increase viscosity and strength.

#### 7.4. Plasticizers/Superplasticizers

*   **Purpose:** To improve the fluidity of the grout at a given water content, allowing for lower w/c ratios (leading to higher strength) or improving pumpability.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Lignosulfonates.**
    *   **Sulfonated Naphthalene Formaldehyde Condensates.**
    *   **Polycarboxylates.**

#### 7.5. Anti-Settling Agents (Stabilizers)

*   **Purpose:** To prevent sedimentation of solid particles (like cement) in the grout, especially in suspension grouts.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Bentonite.**
    *   **Colloidal silica.**
    *   **Certain cellulose derivatives.**

#### 7.6. Water Reducers

*   **Purpose:** Similar to plasticizers, they allow for a reduction in water content while maintaining workability, leading to increased strength and durability.
*   **Examples:** Same as plasticizers.

#### 7.7. Air Entraining Agents

*   **Purpose:** To introduce small, stable air bubbles into the grout, which can improve freeze-thaw resistance and workability.
*   **Examples:** Surfactants that reduce surface tension.

#### 7.8. Fillers/Extenders

*   **Purpose:** To reduce cost or modify properties like strength and shrinkage.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Sand:** For coarser grouts.
    *   **Fly Ash:** Can improve workability and long-term strength, and reduce permeability.
    *   **Silica Fume:** Increases strength and reduces permeability.

### 8. Selection of Grout Materials

The selection of the most appropriate grout material is a critical step and depends on several factors:

*   **Ground Conditions:**
    *   **Soil Type/Particle Size:** Coarse-grained soils (gravels, coarse sands) can accommodate cementitious grouts. Fine-grained soils (silts, clays) and fractured rock require low-viscosity chemical grouts.
    *   **Permeability:** High permeability allows for a wider range of grouts, while low permeability necessitates very fine or low-viscosity materials.
    *   **Presence of Water:** Flowing water might require fast-setting or foaming grouts.
    *   **Chemical Environment:** Aggressive chemicals may necessitate epoxy or specific polymer grouts.
*   **Project Objectives:**
    *   **Waterproofing:** Polyurethanes, silicates, and epoxies are excellent.
    *   **Strength/Consolidation:** Cementitious grouts, epoxies.
    *   **Void Filling:** Cementitious grouts for larger voids, chemical grouts for smaller ones.
    *   **Speed of Construction:** Fast-setting chemical grouts.
*   **Economic Considerations:** Cementitious grouts are generally the most cost-effective. Chemical grouts are typically more expensive.
*   **Environmental and Health Considerations:** The toxicity of components (e.g., acrylamide) must be carefully assessed.
*   **Durability Requirements:** The long-term performance expected from the grouted zone.

Which type of grout is typically used for filling large voids and is generally the most cost-effective?
a) Acrylamide-based grout
b) Polyurethane grout
c) Cementitious grout
d) Epoxy grout

What is the primary function of methylene bis-acrylamide in acrylamide-based grouts?
a) To increase viscosity
b) To act as a cross-linker, forming the polymer network
c) To accelerate the setting time
d) To provide waterproofing

**Question 3:**
List three common additives used in grouting and explain their purpose.

**Question 4:**
You are tasked with sealing a fine sand formation that is allowing significant groundwater flow. Which type of grout would be most suitable, and why?

**Question 5:**
Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using chemical grouts compared to cementitious grouts in ground improvement.

---

### 10. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
c) Cementitious grout

**Answer 2:**
b) To act as a cross-linker, forming the polymer network

**Answer 3:**
*   **Accelerator (e.g., Calcium Chloride):** Decreases the setting time of the grout.
*   **Retarder (e.g., Sugar):** Increases the setting time, allowing for longer working periods.
*   **Viscosity Modifier (e.g., Bentonite):** Increases the viscosity to prevent particle settlement and control flow.

**Answer 4:**
A fine sand formation with significant groundwater flow would be best treated with a **low-viscosity chemical grout**, such as an **acrylamide-based grout** (though its use is now restricted due to toxicity) or a **silicate grout**, or potentially a **fast-setting polyurethane grout**. These grouts can penetrate the small pore spaces of fine sand and set quickly to seal the ground and control water flow. Cementitious grouts are generally too viscous to effectively penetrate fine sands.

**Answer 5:**

**Advantages of Chemical Grouts:**
*   **Low Viscosity:** Can penetrate very fine pores and cracks, enabling effective treatment of silts and fine sands.
*   **Tunable Properties:** Setting time, strength, and flexibility can be precisely controlled.
*   **Fast Setting:** Some chemical grouts can set very rapidly, useful in flowing water or for quick stabilization.
*   **Excellent Waterproofing:** Many chemical grouts provide superior water resistance.
*   **Good Chemical Resistance:** Certain types offer resistance to aggressive chemical environments.

**Disadvantages of Chemical Grouts:**
*   **Higher Cost:** Generally more expensive than cementitious grouts.
*   **Potential Toxicity:** Some components (like acrylamide) are hazardous and require strict handling procedures.
*   **Durability Concerns:** Some organic polymers may degrade over long periods or under specific environmental conditions, although modern formulations are very durable.
*   **Shelf Life:** Chemical components can have a limited shelf life.

**Advantages of Cementitious Grouts:**
*   **Lower Cost:** Economically viable for large-scale applications.
*   **High Strength:** Can achieve significant compressive strength upon hardening.
*   **Good Durability:** Generally durable in most soil and groundwater conditions.
*   **Readily Available:** Components are widely available.

**Disadvantages of Cementitious Grouts:**
*   **Higher Viscosity:** Limits penetration into fine pores and cracks, less effective in silts and fine sands.
*   **Slower Setting Times:** Requires longer curing periods.
*   **Shrinkage:** Can experience shrinkage during drying and setting.
*   **Limited Flexibility:** Typically brittle once set.
*   **Corrosion Potential:** Chlorides from accelerators can cause corrosion in reinforced concrete structures.

---

### 11. Important Points to Remember

*   **Grout Selection is Crucial:** The success of grouting depends heavily on choosing the right material for the specific ground conditions and project goals.
*   **Viscosity is Key for Penetration:** Lower viscosity grouts are essential for treating finer soils and narrower cracks.
*   **Additives Fine-Tune Performance:** Understand how different additives modify grout properties.
*   **Safety First:** Always be aware of the health and environmental implications of the chemicals being used, especially with organic grouts.
*   **Microfine Cements Bridge the Gap:** Microfine cement grouts offer improved penetration over conventional cement grouts, making them suitable for a wider range of applications.
*   **Water is a Common Medium, but can be an issue:** While water is used for mixing cement and is a reactant for some chemical grouts, flowing water can complicate the grouting process, requiring specific grout types.
