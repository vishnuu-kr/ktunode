# Ground Improvement Techniques - Module 4: Grouting And Injection

## Topic: Soil Nailing & Micro Pile

---

### 1. Basic Concepts

#### 1.1. Soil Nailing

*   **Definition:** Soil nailing is a ground improvement technique used for stabilizing slopes, excavations, and retaining walls. It involves installing closely spaced, passive inclusions (nails) into the soil mass. These nails are typically steel bars or rods, often grouted in place.
*   **Mechanism of Reinforcement:**
    *   **Tensile Resistance:** The nails provide tensile resistance to the soil mass, resisting outward movement and preventing failure.
    *   **Shear Resistance:** Friction between the nails and the surrounding soil contributes to shear resistance.
    *   **Soil Confinement:** The nails help to confine the soil, increasing its shear strength and stiffness.
    *   **Bridging Effect:** As the soil deforms, it tends to "bridge" across the nails, distributing stresses and improving the overall stability.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Nails:** Steel bars, rods, or even self-drilling anchors.
    *   **Grout:** Cementitious or chemical grout injected into the borehole to bond the nail to the soil.
    *   **Facing:** A surface treatment (e.g., shotcrete, precast panels) applied to the excavation face to prevent surface erosion and provide a continuous load-carrying system.
    *   **Drainage:** Often incorporated to reduce pore water pressure, which is crucial for stability.

#### 1.2. Micro Piles

*   **Definition:** Micro piles (also known as minipiles or needle piles) are small-diameter, high-strength drilled and grouted piles. They are typically used for underpinning existing structures, reinforcing foundations, and stabilizing slopes.
*   **Mechanism of Reinforcement:**
    *   **Load Transfer:** Micro piles transfer loads from the structure or unstable soil to a deeper, more competent bearing layer.
    *   **Soil Reinforcement:** When installed in a grid pattern, they can reinforce the soil mass, increasing its bearing capacity and reducing settlement.
    *   **Arching Effect:** In some configurations, they can create an arching effect within the soil mass, leading to improved load distribution.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Steel Casing (optional but common):** Provides support during drilling in unstable soils.
    *   **Reinforcing Element:** Typically a steel bar or a small-diameter steel pipe.
    *   **Grout:** Cementitious grout injected into the borehole around the reinforcing element.
    *   **Bonding:** The grout creates a bond between the reinforcing element, the surrounding soil, and the casing (if used).

### 2. Construction Sequence

#### 2.1. Soil Nailing

The construction sequence for soil nailing is typically iterative, proceeding layer by layer.

1.  **Excavation:** Excavate the soil to a specific depth, usually in lifts (e.g., 0.5m to 1.5m). The excavation is temporarily supported if necessary.
2.  **Drilling:** Drill inclined boreholes into the exposed soil face at designated locations. The angle of inclination is usually between 10° and 15° from the horizontal.
3.  **Nail Installation:** Insert the soil nail (steel bar) into the borehole.
4.  **Grouting:** Inject grout under pressure into the borehole, filling the annulus between the nail and the soil. Grouting can be done from the bottom up or simultaneously with nail withdrawal.
5.  **Facing Installation (if required):** Apply a facing system (e.g., shotcrete) to the excavated face, encompassing the nail heads.
6.  **Drainage Installation (if required):** Install drainage elements (e.g., drainage pipes) as part of the facing.
7.  **Curing:** Allow the grout to cure and gain strength.
8.  **Repeat:** Repeat steps 1-7 for subsequent layers until the desired excavation depth or slope height is achieved.
9.  **Verification Testing:** Load testing of selected nails is often performed to verify their performance.

#### 2.2. Micro Piles

The construction sequence for micro piles can vary slightly depending on the specific method and site conditions, but generally follows these steps:

1.  **Drilling:** Drill a borehole of the required diameter and depth. Various drilling methods can be used, including rotary drilling, rotary-percussive drilling, or auger drilling.
    *   *Example:* For underpinning a building, boreholes are drilled through the existing foundation or slab.
2.  **Installation of Reinforcement:** Insert the steel reinforcing element (bar or pipe) into the drilled borehole.
3.  **Grouting:** Inject cementitious grout into the borehole. This can be done under pressure, often using a tremie pipe from the bottom up to ensure complete filling and avoid segregation of the grout.
    *   *Example:* Grouting can be done through a sacrificial drill bit or a dedicated grout pipe.
4.  **Casing Removal (if used):** If a temporary casing was used, it is usually withdrawn during or after grouting, depending on the method.
5.  **Curing:** Allow the grout to cure and achieve its design strength.
6.  **Connection:** The head of the micro pile is then connected to the structure or the reinforced soil mass via a load transfer element (e.g., a steel plate and grout).
7.  **Testing:** Load testing of selected micro piles is often performed.

### 3. Areas of Application

#### 3.1. Soil Nailing

*   **Slope Stabilization:** Retaining and stabilizing natural slopes and man-made embankments.
*   **Excavation Support:** Providing temporary or permanent support for deep excavations in urban areas where space is limited.
*   **Retaining Walls:** Construction of gravity or cantilever retaining walls.
*   **Tunnel Excavations:** Supporting tunnel portals and shallow tunnels.
*   **Erosion Control:** Stabilizing areas prone to erosion.
*   **Landslide Remediation:** Repairing and stabilizing existing landslides.

#### 3.2. Micro Piles

*   **Underpinning Existing Structures:** Strengthening or extending foundations of buildings, bridges, and other structures that have settled or require increased load capacity.
*   **Foundation Repair:** Remediation of damaged or inadequate foundations.
*   **New Foundation Construction:** In situations with limited access or where large diameter piles are impractical.
*   **Slope Stabilization:** Reinforcing soil slopes, especially in confined spaces or near existing structures.
*   **Bridge Abutment Support:** Providing stable foundations for bridge abutments.
*   **Seismic Retrofitting:** Enhancing the seismic resistance of structures.

### 4. Design Considerations

#### 4.1. Soil Nailing

*   **Soil Properties:** Shear strength parameters (cohesion and friction angle), unit weight, permeability, and presence of groundwater.
*   **Nail Spacing and Inclination:** Determined based on the required factor of safety against sliding and bulging. Typically 1.5m to 2.5m spacing.
*   **Nail Length:** Calculated based on the required resistance to pull-out and bending. It often extends beyond the potential failure surface.
*   **Nail Capacity:** Determined by the bond strength between the grout and the soil, and the tensile strength of the nail itself.
*   **Facing Design:** Thickness and reinforcement of shotcrete, panel design, and connection details to nails.
*   **Drainage:** Design of weep holes, drainage layers, or subdrains to reduce pore water pressure.
*   **Groundwater:** The presence of groundwater can significantly affect nail performance and grout curing. Special considerations are needed for submerged conditions.
*   **Deformation Monitoring:** Plan for instrumentation to monitor wall deformation and nail strain during and after construction.
*   **Load Testing:** Design of pull-out tests to verify the design pull-out capacity of the nails.

#### 4.2. Micro Piles

*   **Axial Capacity:**
    *   **End Bearing:** Capacity derived from the bearing resistance at the pile tip.
    *   **Skin Friction:** Capacity derived from the bond between the grout and the surrounding soil along the pile shaft.
    *   *Design Factors:* Pile diameter, grout strength, soil properties, and the method of installation (e.g., vibrated vs. non-vibrated).
*   **Lateral Capacity:** Resistance to horizontal loads, which is critical for retaining wall applications or structures subjected to lateral forces.
*   **Structural Capacity:** The tensile and compressive strength of the steel reinforcing element and any casing.
*   **Grout Properties:** Compressive strength, flowability, and setting time.
*   **Soil Properties:** Shear strength, stiffness, and layering of the soil strata.
*   **Installation Method:** The chosen drilling and grouting method can influence the bond strength and overall performance.
*   **Group Effects:** If micro piles are installed in groups, the interaction between piles needs to be considered.
*   **Settlement Analysis:** Predicting the settlement of the structure supported by micro piles under service loads.
*   **Testing:** Design of axial and potentially lateral load tests to verify the ultimate capacity.

### 5. Merits and Demerits

#### 5.1. Soil Nailing

**Merits:**

*   **Cost-Effective:** Generally more economical than traditional retaining structures for many applications.
*   **Suitable for Limited Access:** Can be constructed in confined spaces and urban environments where conventional equipment access is difficult.
*   **Flexibility:** Adapts well to irregular excavation shapes and varying soil conditions.
*   **Rapid Construction:** Can be built relatively quickly due to the iterative process.
*   **Minimal Disturbance:** Less spoil generation and site disturbance compared to some other methods.
*   **Passive Reinforcement:** Relies on the deformation of the soil to mobilize resistance, making it inherently stable.
*   **Aesthetically Pleasing:** Can be finished with vegetation or decorative facings.

**Demerits:**

*   **Susceptible to Groundwater:** Performance can be reduced in saturated soils or areas with high groundwater tables if not properly managed.
*   **Requires Skilled Labor:** Proper installation requires experienced and trained personnel.
*   **Quality Control:** Strict quality control during drilling, grouting, and installation is essential.
*   **Limited to Certain Soil Types:** Less effective in very soft, collapsible, or highly expansive soils.
*   **Corrosion:** Corrosion of steel nails can be a concern in aggressive soil environments. Protective coatings or stainless steel may be required.
*   **Monitoring is Crucial:** Requires ongoing monitoring to ensure long-term performance.

#### 5.2. Micro Piles

**Merits:**

*   **High Load Capacity:** Can achieve significant axial and lateral load capacities relative to their small diameter.
*   **Minimal Vibration and Noise:** Installation is generally low-vibration and low-noise, making them suitable for use in sensitive areas or near existing structures.
*   **Low Headroom Applications:** Can be installed in areas with limited vertical clearance.
*   **Versatile Applications:** Effective for underpinning, new foundations, and slope stabilization.
*   **Minimal Disturbance to Existing Structures:** Can be installed with minimal disruption to ongoing operations.
*   **Can Penetrate Obstructions:** Can often be drilled through existing concrete, rubble, or other obstructions.
*   **Good in Variable Ground Conditions:** Can be designed to accommodate a range of soil types and groundwater conditions.

**Demerits:**

*   **Higher Cost per Linear Meter:** Generally more expensive per linear meter than conventional piles due to specialized equipment and materials.
*   **Slower Installation for Large Projects:** For large-scale foundation projects, the installation of numerous micro piles can be time-consuming.
*   **Quality Control:** Strict quality control of drilling, grouting, and bonding is critical for performance.
*   **Corrosion:** Steel reinforcement can be susceptible to corrosion in aggressive soil environments.
*   **Limited Bearing Layer Depth:** If a suitable bearing layer is very deep, the length of micro piles might become uneconomical.
*   **Limited Diameter for Large Loads:** For extremely high loads, conventional large-diameter piles might be more efficient.

### 6. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Explain the primary mechanism by which soil nails provide stability to an excavation.

**Answer:** Soil nails stabilize excavations by providing tensile resistance and friction along their length within the soil mass. As the soil attempts to move outwards, the nails are put into tension and shear, preventing the soil from failing. The grout creates a bond, and the facing system (if used) acts as a continuous load-carrying element, distributing the forces.

**Question 2:** What are the key components of a soil nailing system?

**Answer:** The key components are the nails (steel bars), grout (cementitious or chemical), the facing (e.g., shotcrete), and drainage elements.

**Question 3:** Describe two distinct applications where micro piles are particularly advantageous.

**Answer:**
1.  **Underpinning Existing Structures:** Micro piles are excellent for strengthening foundations of buildings that have settled due to inadequate original design or changing soil conditions. Their low vibration and small size allow installation with minimal disruption to the occupied structure.
2.  **Slope Stabilization in Confined Areas:** In urban environments or along roadways where space is limited, micro piles can be installed to reinforce soil slopes without requiring large excavation or retaining structures.

**Question 4:** What is a significant demerit of using soil nailing in areas with a high groundwater table?

**Answer:** In areas with a high groundwater table, soil nailing performance can be reduced due to reduced soil-grout bond strength and potential uplift pressures on the facing. Proper drainage measures are crucial to mitigate these effects.

**Question 5:** How does the design of a micro pile differ from the design of a conventional drilled shaft in terms of load transfer?

**Answer:** While both transfer load to the soil, micro piles rely heavily on **skin friction** along their entire length and the structural capacity of the reinforcing element. Conventional drilled shafts often derive a significant portion of their capacity from **end bearing** at the pile tip, in addition to skin friction.

---

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Soil Nailing:** Passive reinforcement for slopes and excavations, iterative construction, relies on soil-grout bond and nail tension.
*   **Micro Piles:** Small diameter, high-strength piles, often used for underpinning and foundation repair, rely on skin friction and structural capacity.
*   **Grouting:** Essential for both techniques to create the bond between the reinforcement and the soil.
*   **Quality Control:** Crucial for both methods to ensure design performance.
*   **Groundwater:** A significant factor that needs careful consideration and management in both soil nailing and micro pile design and construction.
*   **Applications:** Soil nailing is more common for mass slope stabilization and excavations, while micro piles excel in specific structural support and localized reinforcement.
