# Module 1: Construction Materials - Admixtures

## Topic: Admixtures, uses – mineral admixtures – fly ash and ground granulated blast furnace slag and chemical admixtures – plasticizers, super plasticizers, accelerators, retarders

---

### **1. Introduction to Admixtures**

*   **Definition:** Admixtures are ingredients other than cement, water, and aggregates that are added to concrete, mortar, or grout immediately before or during mixing. They are added in small quantities and are intended to modify the properties of the fresh (plastic) or hardened concrete, or both.

*   **Purpose of Admixtures:**
    *   To improve the workability of fresh concrete (e.g., easier to place and compact).
    *   To accelerate or retard the setting time of concrete.
    *   To increase strength or durability.
    *   To reduce permeability and improve resistance to chemical attack.
    *   To achieve specific performance requirements that cannot be met by plain concrete alone.
    *   To reduce costs by replacing a portion of cement or aggregates.

*   **Types of Admixtures:** Admixtures are broadly classified into two main categories:
    1.  **Mineral Admixtures (Supplementary Cementitious Materials - SCMs)**
    2.  **Chemical Admixtures**

---

### **2. Mineral Admixtures (Supplementary Cementitious Materials - SCMs)**

*   **Definition:** Mineral admixtures are finely ground inorganic materials that can contribute to the properties of concrete either by acting as a pozzolan or a cementitious material, or both. They are often by-products of industrial processes.

*   **General Uses and Benefits:**
    *   **Improved Workability:** Can increase the flowability of concrete, making it easier to mix, place, and finish.
    *   **Increased Strength:** Contribute to long-term strength development through pozzolanic or cementitious reactions.
    *   **Reduced Permeability:** Fill capillary pores, making concrete less permeable and more durable.
    *   **Enhanced Durability:** Improve resistance to sulfate attack, alkali-silica reaction (ASR), and chloride ingress.
    *   **Heat of Hydration Reduction:** Can lower the heat generated during cement hydration, reducing the risk of thermal cracking in massive structures.
    *   **Cost Reduction:** Can partially replace cement, leading to cost savings.
    *   **Environmental Benefits:** Utilizes industrial by-products, reducing waste and the need for virgin materials.

#### **2.1 Fly Ash**

*   **Source:** A by-product generated from burning pulverized coal in electric generation power plants. It is collected by electrostatic precipitators or filter banks.

*   **Types:**
    *   **Class F Fly Ash:** Low calcium content (typically less than 10% CaO). Generally a pozzolan.
    *   **Class C Fly Ash:** High calcium content (typically greater than 20% CaO). Can exhibit both pozzolanic and self-cementitious properties.

*   **Properties:**
    *   **Pozzolanic Activity:** Reacts with calcium hydroxide (a by-product of cement hydration) in the presence of water to form cementitious compounds (calcium silicate hydrates - C-S-H), contributing to strength and durability.
    *   **Fineness:** Generally finer than Portland cement.
    *   **Spherical Particles:** The particles are typically spherical, which can improve workability and reduce water demand.

*   **Uses and Benefits in Concrete:**
    *   **Improved Workability:** Spherical particles and fineness enhance flowability.
    *   **Reduced Heat of Hydration:** Particularly beneficial in mass concrete structures to prevent thermal cracking.
    *   **Increased Long-Term Strength:** Pozzolanic reaction contributes to strength gain over time.
    *   **Enhanced Durability:** Reduces permeability, improves resistance to sulfate attack and ASR.
    *   **Replacement of Portland Cement:** Typically used to replace 15-30% of Portland cement by weight, though higher percentages are possible with Class C fly ash or for specific applications.

*   **Important Points to Remember about Fly Ash:**
    *   The properties of fly ash can vary significantly depending on the type of coal used and the combustion process.
    *   Requires sufficient moisture and time for pozzolanic reaction to occur.
    *   Can cause slight retardation of initial setting and slower early strength gain compared to plain concrete.

#### **2.2 Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag (GGBS)**

*   **Source:** A by-product of the iron manufacturing process in blast furnaces. Molten blast furnace slag is rapidly quenched in water or steam, which solidifies it into granules. These granules are then ground into a fine powder.

*   **Properties:**
    *   **Latent Hydraulic Properties:** GGBS contains compounds that can react with water (hydration) and also react with activators (like calcium hydroxide from cement hydration) to form cementitious compounds. This means it can contribute to strength even without Portland cement present, though in concrete it acts synergistically with cement.
    *   **Fineness:** Ground to a fine powder, similar to or finer than Portland cement.
    *   **Color:** Generally lighter in color than Portland cement.

*   **Uses and Benefits in Concrete:**
    *   **Improved Workability:** Finer particles enhance flowability.
    *   **Reduced Permeability:** Fills pores, leading to denser concrete.
    *   **Excellent Durability:** Significantly improves resistance to sulfate attack and chloride ingress.
    *   **Reduced Heat of Hydration:** Similar to fly ash, it helps manage heat generation.
    *   **Increased Long-Term Strength:** Contributes significantly to strength development over time.
    *   **Replacement of Portland Cement:** Can be used to replace up to 70% or more of Portland cement by weight, particularly in applications requiring high durability or low heat.

*   **Important Points to Remember about GGBS:**
    *   Can cause a noticeable retardation of setting and slower early strength development, especially at higher replacement levels.
    *   The reactivity of GGBS depends on its chemical composition and fineness.
    *   Often used in combination with Portland cement for optimal performance.

### **3. Chemical Admixtures**

*   **Definition:** Chemical admixtures are liquid or powder compounds that are added to concrete in small amounts to modify specific properties of the fresh or hardened concrete. They are typically produced industrially through chemical synthesis.

*   **General Uses and Benefits:**
    *   **Workability Enhancement:** Improve flow, reduce segregation, and facilitate pumping.
    *   **Setting Time Control:** Accelerate or retard the setting process.
    *   **Strength Enhancement:** Increase compressive or flexural strength.
    *   **Durability Improvement:** Enhance resistance to freezing-thaw, chemical attack, and abrasion.
    *   **Air Entrainment:** Improve resistance to freezing and thawing cycles.
    *   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Can optimize concrete mix designs.

#### **3.1 Plasticizers (Water Reducers)**

*   **Definition:** Chemical admixtures that increase the fluidity (workability) of concrete with a constant water content, or reduce the water content required for a given workability. They are typically organic compounds, such as lignosulfonates or hydroxycarboxylic acids.

*   **Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Dispersion:** They adsorb onto the cement particles, imparting a negative charge. This causes the particles to repel each other, dispersing them more effectively.
    *   **Reduced Flocculation:** By reducing the clumping of cement particles, more water is released, increasing fluidity.

*   **Uses and Benefits:**
    *   **Improved Workability:** Allows for easier placement and consolidation, especially in heavily reinforced sections.
    *   **Reduced Water-Cement Ratio:** For a given workability, the water content can be reduced, leading to increased strength and durability.
    *   **Increased Strength:** A lower water-cement ratio directly leads to higher compressive strength.
    *   **Reduced Segregation and Bleeding:** By improving particle dispersion.

*   **Dosage:** Typically added at a rate of 0.2% to 1.0% by weight of cement.

*   **Example:** Using a plasticizer to make concrete flowable enough to be pumped to a high floor of a building or to fill intricate formwork.

#### **3.2 Superplasticizers (High-Range Water Reducers)**

*   **Definition:** Chemical admixtures that are a more powerful version of plasticizers, capable of significantly increasing the slump of concrete without adding water or significantly reducing the water content for a given slump. They are often based on polymers like sulfonated melamine formaldehyde condensates, sulfonated naphthalene formaldehyde condensates, or polycarboxylate ethers (PCEs).

*   **Mechanism of Action:** Similar to plasticizers but with much stronger dispersing action, leading to a more significant increase in fluidity. PCE-based superplasticizers offer a high degree of molecular tailored performance.

*   **Uses and Benefits:**
    *   **High Workability Concrete (Self-Consolidating Concrete - SCC):** Enables the production of concrete that can flow under its own weight without vibration, filling formwork completely.
    *   **Significant Water Reduction:** Can reduce water content by up to 30% or more while maintaining or increasing workability, leading to very high strength and durable concrete.
    *   **Improved Pumpability:** Facilitates pumping of concrete over long distances or to high elevations.
    *   **Enhanced Durability:** Achieved through lower water-cement ratios and improved particle packing.

*   **Dosage:** Typically added at a rate of 1.5% to 3.0% by weight of cement.

*   **Example:** Using superplasticizers to produce self-consolidating concrete for bridge decks or tunnel linings, where proper consolidation without vibration is critical.

#### **3.3 Accelerators**

*   **Definition:** Chemical admixtures that speed up the rate of cement hydration, causing concrete to set and gain strength more rapidly.

*   **Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Increase the rate of C₃S and C₃A hydration.**
    *   **Promote the formation of hydration products earlier.**

*   **Common Accelerators:**
    *   **Calcium Chloride (CaCl₂):** Most common and effective, but can cause corrosion of reinforcing steel. Its use is restricted in reinforced concrete.
    *   **Non-Chloride Accelerators:** Such as calcium formate, calcium nitrate, and proprietary blends. These are preferred for reinforced concrete applications.

*   **Uses and Benefits:**
    *   **Cold Weather Concreting:** To counteract the slowing effect of low temperatures on setting and strength gain.
    *   **Early Strength Development:** For rapid formwork removal, faster construction cycles, or to allow structures to be put into service quickly.
    *   **Repairs:** To accelerate the setting and hardening of concrete patches.

*   **Dosage:** Varies depending on the type and desired effect, but generally a small percentage by weight of cement.

*   **Important Points to Remember about Accelerators:**
    *   Can reduce the ultimate strength of concrete if overdosed.
    *   May increase the risk of drying shrinkage.
    *   Calcium chloride should be avoided in reinforced concrete due to corrosion risk.

#### **3.4 Retarders**

*   **Definition:** Chemical admixtures that slow down the rate of cement hydration, delaying the setting time of concrete.

*   **Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Adsorb onto cement particles and inhibit the initial hydration reactions.**
    *   **Delay the formation of hydration products.**

*   **Common Retarders:**
    *   **Hydroxycarboxylic Acids:** Such as gluconates and citrates.
    *   **Lignosulfonates:** Similar to those used in plasticizers, but often at higher dosages.
    *   **Sugars:** Though less common and can cause significant retardation.

*   **Uses and Benefits:**
    *   **Hot Weather Concreting:** To counteract the accelerating effect of high temperatures on setting time, allowing more time for placement and finishing.
    *   **Long Haul Distances:** To prevent concrete from setting before it reaches the construction site.
    *   **Complex Placements:** In situations requiring extended workability, such as large pours or heavily reinforced sections.
    *   **Cold Weather Concreting (to some extent):** Can help maintain workability for longer, although accelerators are more common for cold weather.

*   **Dosage:** Typically added at a rate of 0.1% to 1.0% by weight of cement.

*   **Important Points to Remember about Retarders:**
    *   Can reduce early strength gain.
    *   Overdosing can lead to excessive retardation and loss of strength.
    *   Should not be confused with admixtures that merely increase slump retention without significantly retarding setting.

### **4. Conclusion on Admixtures**

*   Admixtures are vital tools in modern construction engineering, allowing for the precise tailoring of concrete properties to meet specific project requirements.
*   Understanding the type, function, and dosage of admixtures is crucial for achieving desired performance, durability, and cost-effectiveness.
*   Proper selection and use of admixtures require knowledge of their chemical composition, interaction with cement, and potential side effects.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Which of the following is a mineral admixture?
(a) Superplasticizer
(b) Fly Ash
(c) Retarder
(d) Accelerator

**Answer:** (b) Fly Ash

**Question 2:**
Explain the primary mechanism by which fly ash improves concrete workability.

**Answer:** Fly ash improves workability primarily due to its spherical particle shape, which reduces interparticle friction and allows for better lubrication of the mix. Its fineness also contributes to this effect.

**Question 3:**
What is the main advantage of using GGBS in concrete, particularly in coastal environments?

**Answer:** The main advantage of GGBS in coastal environments is its significant improvement in resistance to chloride ingress and sulfate attack, which are common aggressive agents in these areas. This leads to enhanced durability and longevity of the concrete structure.

**Question 4:**
A concrete mix requires a high slump and excellent flowability to fill complex formwork without vibration. Which type of admixture would be most suitable?

**Answer:** Superplasticizers (or High-Range Water Reducers) would be most suitable for achieving a high slump and self-consolidating concrete (SCC) properties.

**Question 5:**
What is the primary concern with using calcium chloride as an accelerator in reinforced concrete?

**Answer:** The primary concern with using calcium chloride in reinforced concrete is its potential to cause corrosion of the reinforcing steel, leading to premature deterioration of the structure.

**Question 6:**
For a construction project in a hot climate where concrete needs to be transported over a long distance, which admixture would be beneficial, and why?

**Answer:** A retarder would be beneficial. It slows down the setting time of the concrete, counteracting the accelerating effect of the hot weather and ensuring that the concrete remains workable and can be placed before it hardens prematurely.

### **6. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Admixtures are NOT a substitute for good concrete practice:** Proper mix design, materials, batching, mixing, placing, and curing are still paramount.
*   **Dosage is critical:** Overdosing or underdosing can have adverse effects on concrete performance. Always follow manufacturer recommendations.
*   **Compatibility:** Ensure that admixtures are compatible with each other and with the cement being used.
*   **Testing:** It is advisable to perform trial mixes to verify the performance of admixtures in the specific concrete mix.
*   **Mineral admixtures develop strength over time:** Their full benefits, especially pozzolanic activity, may not be evident at early ages.
*   **Chemical admixtures are potent:** Small changes in dosage can lead to significant changes in performance.
