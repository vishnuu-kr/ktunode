# Introduction to Construction Engineering: Module 3 - Building Construction

## Topic: Preliminary Considerations for Shallow and Deep Foundations

---

### **1. Introduction to Foundations**

Foundations are the critical structural element that transfers the load of a building or structure to the surrounding soil or rock. They are the bedrock upon which everything else is built, and their proper design and construction are paramount for the stability and longevity of any structure.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Load Transfer:** The primary function of a foundation is to safely transfer the superimposed loads (dead load, live load, wind load, seismic load, etc.) from the superstructure to the soil or rock stratum.
*   **Bearing Capacity:** The ability of the soil or rock to support the applied loads without excessive settlement or shear failure.
*   **Settlement:** The downward movement of a foundation under load. Excessive settlement can cause structural damage and operational issues.
*   **Stability:** Ensuring the foundation remains in equilibrium under applied loads and environmental conditions.

---

### **2. Factors Influencing Foundation Selection**

The choice between shallow and deep foundations, and the specific type within each category, is dictated by a range of crucial factors. A thorough understanding of these influences is essential for an appropriate and economical foundation design.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Soil Conditions:** This is the most significant factor.
    *   **Soil Type:** Clay, sand, silt, gravel, rock. Each has different strength and compressibility characteristics.
    *   **Soil Strength (Bearing Capacity):** High bearing capacity soils can support foundations at shallower depths.
    *   **Soil Compressibility:** Highly compressible soils (e.g., soft clays, organic soils) can lead to significant settlements, often requiring deep foundations or ground improvement.
    *   **Presence of Groundwater:** High groundwater tables can reduce soil strength, increase hydrostatic pressure, and complicate excavation.
    *   **Soil Stratification:** The layering of different soil types can affect load distribution and settlement.
*   **Magnitude of Loads:** Heavier structures generally require more robust foundations, potentially necessitating deep foundations.
*   **Type of Structure:**
    *   **Buildings:** Residential, commercial, industrial – each has different load profiles and sensitivities to settlement.
    *   **Bridges, Towers, Offshore Structures:** These often have very high concentrated loads or require foundations in challenging environments.
*   **Environmental Conditions:**
    *   **Seismic Activity:** Areas prone to earthquakes require foundations designed to resist seismic forces and liquefaction potential.
    *   **Frost Depth:** In colder climates, foundations must be placed below the frost line to prevent frost heave.
    *   **Expansive Soils:** Soils that swell and shrink with moisture changes (e.g., certain clays) can cause significant foundation movement.
    *   **Corrosive Soils:** Aggressive soil conditions can degrade foundation materials, requiring protective measures.
*   **Cost and Constructability:** The economic feasibility and practicalities of constructing different foundation types are always considered.
*   **Site Accessibility:** Limited site access can influence the type of equipment that can be used for excavation and foundation installation.
*   **Future Development:** Consideration for adjacent structures and potential future construction on or near the site.

### **3. Preliminary Considerations for Shallow Foundations**

Shallow foundations are typically used when good bearing soil is available at relatively shallow depths, usually within a few meters of the ground surface. They spread the load over a wider area to reduce the pressure on the soil.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Foundations that transmit building loads to the earth at a shallow depth. The depth of embedment is generally less than the width of the foundation.
*   **Typical Applications:** Most low-to-medium rise buildings, residential structures, warehouses.
*   **Types of Shallow Foundations:**
    *   **Spread Footings (Isolated Footings):** Support individual columns.
        *   **Square Footings:** For square or rectangular columns.
        *   **Rectangular Footings:** For rectangular columns.
        *   **Circular Footings:** For circular columns.
        *   **Combined Footings:** Support two or more columns, often used when column spacing is irregular or when a footing needs to be eccentrically loaded.
    *   **Wall Footings (Strip Footings):** Support load-bearing walls.
    *   **Mat or Raft Foundations:** Large, thick slabs that cover the entire footprint of the building, supporting all columns and walls. Used when loads are heavy, soil bearing capacity is low, or to minimize differential settlement.
*   **Preliminary Design Considerations for Shallow Foundations:**
    *   **Soil Investigation:** Geotechnical report is essential to determine soil type, strength, and depth of suitable bearing strata.
    *   **Bearing Capacity of Soil:** The ultimate bearing capacity and allowable bearing pressure are critical.
    *   **Settlement Analysis:** Even with adequate bearing capacity, settlement must be checked to ensure it's within acceptable limits for the structure.
    *   **Punching Shear:** For reinforced concrete footings, checking the capacity of the concrete to resist shear stress around the loaded column.
    *   **Bending Moments:** Calculating bending moments in the footing to determine the required reinforcement.
    *   **Frost Protection:** Ensuring the base of the footing is below the local frost depth.
    *   **Drainage:** Provisions for surface and subsurface drainage around shallow foundations to prevent water accumulation.

**Example:** A two-story residential building with standard column loads on a site with stiff clay soil at 1 meter depth. Spread footings are likely a suitable and economical choice.

**Important Point to Remember:** The primary goal of shallow foundation design is to spread the load over a sufficiently large area to keep the soil pressure below the soil's allowable bearing capacity and to limit settlement to acceptable levels.

### **4. Preliminary Considerations for Deep Foundations**

Deep foundations are used when the surface soils are weak or compressible, or when heavy loads need to be transferred to stronger strata at greater depths.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Foundations that transmit loads to deeper, more competent soil or rock strata. The depth of embedment is significantly greater than the width of the foundation.
*   **Typical Applications:** High-rise buildings, bridges, structures with heavy concentrated loads, sites with poor surface soil conditions, seismic zones.
*   **Types of Deep Foundations:**
    *   **Piles:** Slender, load-carrying members installed into the ground.
        *   **By Material:** Concrete (cast-in-situ, precast), steel (H-piles, pipe piles), timber.
        *   **By Installation Method:**
            *   **Driven Piles:** Hammered or vibrated into the ground. (e.g., precast concrete piles, steel H-piles, pipe piles).
            *   **Bored Piles (Drilled Shafts/Caissons):** Created by drilling a hole and filling it with concrete and reinforcement. (e.g., continuous flight auger (CFA) piles, hand-dug caissons).
        *   **By Load Transfer Mechanism:**
            *   **End-Bearing Piles:** Transfer load primarily through their tip to a strong stratum.
            *   **Friction Piles:** Transfer load along their shaft through skin friction with the surrounding soil.
            *   **Compaction Piles:** Primarily used to compact loose granular soils.
    *   **Piers (Caissons):** Larger diameter excavations or structures that extend to significant depths, often larger than piles and sometimes open-topped during installation.
*   **Preliminary Design Considerations for Deep Foundations:**
    *   **Soil Investigation:** Essential to identify the depth and properties of competent bearing strata and the characteristics of intervening weaker soils.
    *   **Pile Capacity:**
        *   **Axial Capacity:** Determined by the ultimate bearing capacity of the pile (end bearing + shaft friction) and reduced by a factor of safety.
        *   **Lateral Capacity:** The pile's ability to resist horizontal loads.
    *   **Group Effects:** When piles are installed in groups, their individual capacities can be reduced due to overlapping stress zones in the soil.
    *   **Settlement Analysis:** Predicted settlement of pile groups.
    *   **Buckling:** For slender piles in soft soils, the potential for buckling under axial load needs to be considered.
    *   **Negative Skin Friction (Downdrag):** Occurs when consolidating soil settles around a pile, exerting a downward drag force on the pile.
    *   **Lateral Loads:** Resistance to horizontal forces from wind, seismic activity, or soil pressure.
    *   **Installation Challenges:** Noise, vibration, spoil disposal, groundwater management.
    *   **Cost:** Deep foundations are generally more expensive than shallow foundations.

**Example:** A 30-story skyscraper on a site with deep layers of soft clay and peat. Bored piles or driven piles extending to bedrock or a dense sand layer would be necessary.

**Important Point to Remember:** Deep foundations are a last resort when shallow foundations are not feasible due to poor soil conditions or heavy loads. Their design requires careful consideration of the composite behavior of the pile and surrounding soil, as well as the influence of group effects and potential negative skin friction.

### **5. Site Investigation and Geotechnical Reports**

A thorough site investigation is the cornerstone of any foundation design. This involves various methods to understand the subsurface conditions and the properties of the soil and rock. The findings are compiled into a geotechnical report.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Purpose of Site Investigation:**
    *   Determine the type, thickness, and sequence of soil and rock strata.
    *   Determine the groundwater table level and its fluctuations.
    *   Determine the strength and compressibility characteristics of the soil and rock.
    *   Identify potential geological hazards (e.g., sinkholes, landslides, existing fill).
    *   Provide recommendations for foundation type, bearing capacity, allowable settlement, and construction methods.
*   **Methods of Site Investigation:**
    *   **Desk Study:** Review of existing geological maps, aerial photographs, and previous site investigation reports.
    *   **Ground Surface Reconnaissance:** Visual inspection of the site for signs of distress, springs, or unusual ground features.
    *   **Geophysical Methods:** Non-intrusive methods like seismic surveys, resistivity surveys to infer subsurface properties.
    *   **Subsurface Exploration:**
        *   **Trial Pits/Trenches:** Excavation to shallow depths for visual inspection and sampling.
        *   **Boreholes:** Drilling to greater depths to obtain soil and rock samples.
        *   **Standard Penetration Test (SPT):** An in-situ test conducted in boreholes to measure the soil's resistance to penetration, providing an indication of its density and strength.
        *   **Cone Penetration Test (CPT):** Pushing a cone-shaped probe into the soil to measure tip resistance and sleeve friction, providing a continuous profile of soil properties.
        *   **Geophysical Logging:** In boreholes to measure properties like P-wave velocity, resistivity, etc.
*   **Geotechnical Report Contents:**
    *   Site description and purpose of investigation.
    *   Description of exploration methods used.
    *   Borehole logs, SPT/CPT data, and laboratory test results.
    *   Soil profile and groundwater conditions.
    *   Analysis of soil bearing capacity and settlement.
    *   Recommendations for foundation types, bearing pressures, allowable settlements.
    *   Recommendations for excavation, dewatering, and ground improvement if necessary.
    *   Potential design and construction considerations.

**Example:** For a new high-rise building, boreholes would be drilled to a depth significantly below the proposed foundation level. SPTs and laboratory tests (e.g., shear strength, consolidation tests) would be performed on recovered soil samples.

**Important Point to Remember:** The quality of foundation design is directly proportional to the quality of the site investigation and the thoroughness of the geotechnical report. Cutting corners on site investigation is a false economy and can lead to costly remedial work or structural failure.

### **6. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
A 5-story building is to be constructed on a site with soft clay soil extending to a depth of 15 meters, underlain by dense sand. The building loads are moderate. Briefly explain whether a shallow or deep foundation would likely be more appropriate and why.

**Answer 1:**
A deep foundation would likely be more appropriate. The presence of soft clay soil to a significant depth suggests that shallow foundations might experience excessive settlement or have insufficient bearing capacity. By extending the foundation elements (e.g., piles) through the soft clay layer to the underlying dense sand stratum, the loads can be safely transferred to a much stronger and less compressible bearing material.

**Question 2:**
List and briefly describe three key factors that influence the selection of a foundation type for a building.

**Answer 2:**
1.  **Soil Conditions:** The type, strength, compressibility, and groundwater conditions of the soil at the site. Weak or compressible soils often necessitate deep foundations, while strong soils at shallow depths permit shallow foundations.
2.  **Magnitude of Loads:** Heavier structures impose larger loads on the foundation, which may require wider footings (shallow) or deeper elements to distribute the load effectively.
3.  **Environmental Conditions:** Factors like seismic activity, frost depth, or the presence of expansive soils can dictate specific foundation design requirements and the need for deeper embedment or specialized construction methods.

**Question 3:**
What is the primary purpose of a "raft" or "mat" foundation? When might it be considered a preferable option to isolated spread footings?

**Answer 3:**
The primary purpose of a raft or mat foundation is to distribute the total load of the building over a very large area, effectively acting as a single large footing beneath the entire structure. It is often considered when:
*   The building loads are very heavy, and individual spread footings would be excessively large and close together, potentially overlapping.
*   The soil bearing capacity is low, and the large area of the raft reduces the pressure on the soil to an acceptable level.
*   To minimize differential settlement between different parts of the building, especially on variable soil conditions.

**Question 4:**
Explain the concept of "negative skin friction" and its potential impact on the design of deep foundations.

**Answer 4:**
Negative skin friction, also known as downdrag, occurs when surrounding soil settles more than the foundation element itself. This relative downward movement causes the soil to exert a downward drag (friction) along the shaft of the foundation element. This effectively adds to the load carried by the foundation. In the design of deep foundations (like piles), negative skin friction must be accounted for by either:
*   Increasing the foundation's capacity to handle the additional load.
*   Using materials resistant to corrosion or with a smooth surface to minimize adhesion.
*   Treating the soil around the pile (e.g., with bitumen coating) to reduce friction.

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Foundation is critical:** The foundation is the most important part of a building's structure. Failure here leads to failure of the entire structure.
*   **Soil dictates design:** A thorough geotechnical investigation is non-negotiable. The soil conditions are the primary driver of foundation selection.
*   **Settlement is key:** Even if the soil has adequate bearing capacity, excessive settlement can render a structure unusable.
*   **Cost vs. Performance:** While cost is a factor, it should never compromise safety and performance. Deep foundations are more expensive but necessary in challenging conditions.
*   **Understanding load transfer:** Always consider how the loads from the superstructure are being transferred to the ground.
*   **Group effects for piles:** Piles in a group behave differently than isolated piles; this must be analyzed.
*   **Environmental factors matter:** Always consider site-specific environmental conditions like seismic activity, frost, and groundwater.
