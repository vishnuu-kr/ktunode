### 1. The Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 (PLI Act)

#### 1.1. Introduction and Objectives

The PLI Act, 1991, was enacted to provide for "public liability insurance" to cover damages arising from accidents due to the handling of hazardous substances. Its primary objectives are:

*   **Compensation for Victims:** To ensure immediate relief and compensation to victims of accidents involving hazardous substances, irrespective of fault.
*   **Financial Security:** To provide a financial cushion to industries handling hazardous substances through mandatory insurance.
*   **Deterrence:** To indirectly deter hazardous industries from causing accidents by imposing financial liabilities.

#### 1.2. Key Definitions

*   **"Accident"**: Means an accident occurring as a result of the handling of any hazardous substance or due to any hazardous activity, which results in (i) death or injury to any person; or (ii) damage to any property; or (iii) damage to the environment.
*   **"Collector"**: Means a Collector of a district or any officer appointed by the Central Government to perform the functions of a Collector under this Act.
*   **"Environment"**: Means water, air, land and their interrelationship with human beings, other living creatures, plants, micro-organisms and property.
*   **"Hazardous Substance"**: Defined in **Section 2(d)** of the Act, it means a substance which, by reason of being explosive, flammable, poisonous, corrosive, toxic, bio-accumulative, radioactive or by virtue of its quantity or any other of its properties, which causes or is likely to cause, death or any injury to any person or damage to any property or to the environment, and includes:
    *   Petroleum, pesticides, explosives, chemicals, etc.
    *   Any substance specified by the Central Government by notification in the Official Gazette, having regard to the provisions of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
    *   **Example:** Benzene, Sulphuric Acid, Chlorine, Radioactive materials.
*   **"Hazardous Activity"**: Defined in **Section 2(e)**, it means any activity which involves or is connected with the production, storage, transport, import, export, or any other handling of any hazardous substance.
    *   **Example:** Operating a chemical manufacturing plant, transporting hazardous chemicals by road.
*   **"Owner"**: In relation to an enterprise, means the person who, or the authority which, has the ultimate control over the affairs of that enterprise and, where the said affairs are or are to be managed by a managing agent, director, manager or any other person, by whatever name called, the managing agent, director, manager or such other person shall be deemed to be the owner.
*   **"Public Liability Insurance Policy"**: Means an insurance policy, which is issued under and in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

#### 1.3. Mandatory Insurance and Liabilities

*   **Mandatory Insurance (Section 3):** Every "owner" carrying on a "hazardous activity" is *mandatorily* required to take out a public liability insurance policy *before* commencing such activity. This insurance must cover liability for damages caused by accidents due to handling of hazardous substances.
*   **No-Fault Liability:** The Act establishes a system of no-fault liability. This means that compensation is payable to victims *regardless of negligence or fault* on the part of the owner. The mere occurrence of an accident due to handling of hazardous substances is sufficient to trigger liability.
*   **Limits of Liability:**
    *   **Section 4:** The owner is liable to pay compensation for death or injury to any person or damage to any property or environment.
    *   **Section 6:** The Act specifies limits on the amount of compensation. For any one accident, the total amount of compensation payable shall not exceed the sum assured under the policy. However, the owner is liable to pay *in addition* to the insured amount, any further amount due to negligence.
    *   **No Maximum Limit for Death/Injury:** Interestingly, the Act does not impose a *maximum limit* on compensation for death or injury to persons. This distinguishes it from property damage.
*   **Types of Claims:** The Act provides for claims for:
    *   Death of a person.
    *   Injury to a person.
    *   Damage to property (movable or immovable).
    *   Damage to the environment.

#### 1.4. Procedure for Claims and Compensation

*   **Intimation of Accident:** The owner must give notice of any accident to the Collector and the insurance company.
*   **Role of the Collector:** The Collector plays a crucial role in assessing the damages and recommending compensation.
    *   Upon receiving notice of an accident, the Collector shall, by order, give notice to the owner of the enterprise and also to the insurer.
    *   The Collector shall, after holding an inquiry, prepare a report specifying the nature and extent of damages and recommending the amount of compensation payable.
*   **Payment of Compensation:**
    *   The insurer shall, on the basis of the Collector's report, pay the claimant such amount as is considered fit.
    *   If the claimant is not satisfied with the amount, they can approach the **Claims Tribunal**.
*   **Establishment of Claims Tribunal (Section 11):** The Act provides for the establishment of a Claims Tribunal to hear and decide claims for compensation.
    *   The Tribunal has the powers of a civil court for certain purposes.
    *   Its decisions are binding on the insurer and the claimant.
*   **Appeals:** Appeals against the orders of the Tribunal lie with the High Court.

#### 1.5. Exceptions to Liability (Section 8)

The owner shall not be liable to pay any compensation if the accident is caused:

*   **(a) Wholly by the willful refusal of the victim to obey a written law or written instructions issued by the owner for the purpose of preventing accidents.** (e.g., trespass, ignoring safety signs)
*   **(b) Wholly by the willful and fore-seeable action of a third party, other than an agent or employee of the owner, for the direct purpose of causing such accident.** (e.g., deliberate sabotage by an outsider)

**Important Point:** These exceptions are narrowly interpreted. The burden of proof for these exceptions lies with the owner.

#### 1.6. Role of the Insurance Company

*   The insurance company is the primary entity responsible for paying compensation up to the insured amount.
*   It has the right to subrogate the rights of the claimant to recover the compensation amount from the wrongdoer.

#### 1.7. Amendments and Criticisms

*   The PLI Act has been amended over the years to strengthen its provisions.
*   **Criticisms:**
    *   **Low Insurance Limits:** Initially, the insurance limits were considered too low to cover substantial damages.
    *   **Bureaucratic Delays:** The role of the Collector and the Tribunal can sometimes lead to delays in compensation.
    *   **Limited Scope:** The definition of hazardous substance/activity might not cover all potential risks.
    *   **Weak Enforcement:** Enforcement mechanisms can be a concern.

### 2. Principles of Strict and Absolute Liability

These principles are cornerstones of environmental law, holding polluters accountable for harm caused by hazardous activities.

#### 2.1. Evolution of Liability in Tort Law

*   **Fault Liability (Negligence):** Traditionally, tort law focused on proving fault or negligence to establish liability. The injured party had to demonstrate that the defendant acted carelessly.
*   **Rylands v. Fletcher (1868) - The Genesis of Strict Liability:**
    *   **Facts:** Rylands owned a mine. He contracted Fletcher to build a reservoir on his land. The reservoir was built, but due to the negligence of Fletcher's contractors, it burst and flooded Rylands' mine.
    *   **Principle:** The court held Rylands liable even though he was not negligent. The principle established was: **"If a person brings or accumulates on his land anything which, if it should escape, may cause damage to his neighbours, he keeps it there at his peril. If he does not do so, he is prima facie answerable for all the damage which is the natural consequence of its escape."**
    *   **Conditions for Strict Liability (Rylands v. Fletcher):**
        1.  **Gathering of Hazardous Things:** Bringing onto one's land something which is not naturally there.
        2.  **Non-Natural Use of Land:** The use of the land must be something out of the ordinary course of human activity.
        3.  **Escape:** The thing collected must escape from the land.
        4.  **Damage:** The escape must cause damage to a neighbour.
    *   **Defences to Strict Liability:**
        1.  **Act of God:** Natural events beyond human control (e.g., unprecedented flood, earthquake).
        2.  **Act of a Stranger:** An act of a third party over whom the defendant has no control.
        3.  **Plaintiff's Own Default:** The plaintiff's own action or negligence caused the damage.
        4.  **Statutory Authority:** If the activity causing the escape is authorized by statute.

#### 2.2. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak Case, 1987) - Introducing Absolute Liability

This landmark Indian Supreme Court judgment revolutionized the concept of liability in environmental cases.

*   **Facts:** An oleum gas leak occurred from a unit of Shriram Food and Fertiliser Industries, causing harm to people in the vicinity.
*   **The Problem with Strict Liability:** The Supreme Court observed that the existing rule of strict liability in *Rylands v. Fletcher* had several escape clauses (defences) which might not adequately protect victims of hazardous industries.
*   **The Principle of Absolute Liability:** The Supreme Court introduced the doctrine of **Absolute Liability**. This means:
    *   **No Defences:** An enterprise engaged in a hazardous or inherently dangerous activity is absolutely liable for any harm caused by such activity.
    *   **No Exceptions:** There are no exceptions or defences available to the enterprise in such cases. The enterprise is liable regardless of whether it exercised all due care and caution.
    *   **Rationale:** To protect the environment and the public from the potential dangers of hazardous industries, and to ensure that those who benefit from such activities bear the cost of any harm they cause.
    *   **Quote:** "We are of the opinion that any enterprise engaged in a hazardous or inherently dangerous activity has a solemn duty to ensure that such activity is conducted with utmost care and diligence and to see that no harm is caused to anyone, and if any harm is caused, the enterprise must be held absolutely liable to compensate for such harm."

#### 2.3. Shriram Oleum Gas Leak Case - Application of Absolute Liability

*   **Liability for Compensation:** The court directed Shriram to pay compensation to the victims of the oleum gas leak.
*   **Further Directions:** The court also issued directions regarding the relocation of the hazardous unit, safety measures, and the establishment of a social security system for victims.

#### 2.4. Additional Aspects of Absolute Liability and Environmental Law

*   **Enterprise Liability:** The liability is placed on the enterprise conducting the hazardous activity.
*   **Polluter Pays Principle:** Although not explicitly codified in the Oleum Gas Leak case, the principle of absolute liability aligns with the "polluter pays" principle, where the polluter bears the cost of environmental damage.
*   **Remedies:** Victims can seek compensation for death, injury, property damage, and environmental damage.
*   **Role of Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** PILs have been instrumental in bringing environmental issues and victims' plight to the forefront, leading to the application of these principles.

### 3. Comparison: Strict Liability vs. Absolute Liability

| Feature               | Strict Liability (*Rylands v. Fletcher*) | Absolute Liability (Indian Supreme Court) |
| :-------------------- | :--------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| **Basis of Liability**| Non-natural use of land + Escape of dangerous thing | Engaging in hazardous/inherently dangerous activity |
| **Fault Requirement** | No fault required (Strict)               | No fault required (Absolute)              |
| **Defences/Exceptions**| Act of God, Act of Stranger, Plaintiff's Default, Statutory Authority | **No defences available**                 |
| **Purpose**           | Compensate neighbours for damage from escape | Protect public and environment from hazardous activities |
| **Application**       | Narrower; specific conditions to be met  | Broader; applicable to any hazardous activity |
| **Burden of Proof**   | Plaintiff proves elements; Defendant proves defences | Plaintiff proves activity and harm; Defendant cannot escape liability |

### 4. Practical Application and Legal Framework in India

*   **PLI Act, 1991:** Provides a statutory framework for mandatory insurance and no-fault liability for handling hazardous substances, with specific provisions for compensation.
*   **Absolute Liability:** A judicial doctrine developed by the Supreme Court as a higher standard of liability for hazardous activities, offering greater protection to victims and the environment.
*   **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** This umbrella legislation allows the government to take measures to protect and improve the environment, and can be used in conjunction with other laws to address pollution and hazardous activities.
*   ** Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984):** While occurring before the PLI Act and the explicit articulation of absolute liability, the aftermath of the Bhopal disaster heavily influenced the development of environmental law in India, highlighting the need for stronger accountability for hazardous industries. The government of India's handling of the compensation claims through the Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Processing of Claims) Act, 1985, also contributed to the evolution of legal responses.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991?

**Answer:** The primary purpose of the PLI Act, 1991, is to provide for public liability insurance to cover damages arising from accidents due to the handling of hazardous substances, ensuring prompt relief and compensation to victims.

**Question 2:** Explain the concept of "no-fault liability" under the PLI Act.

**Answer:** No-fault liability under the PLI Act means that an owner of an enterprise handling hazardous substances is liable to pay compensation for damages arising from an accident, irrespective of whether negligence or fault on their part can be proven. The occurrence of the accident itself triggers liability.

**Question 3:** Differentiate between strict liability and absolute liability in the context of environmental law.

**Answer:** Strict liability (as per *Rylands v. Fletcher*) holds a person liable for damage caused by escaping dangerous things collected on their land for non-natural use, but allows for certain defences (e.g., Act of God). Absolute liability, as established by the Indian Supreme Court, imposes liability for harm caused by hazardous activities without any defences or exceptions, making the enterprise absolutely liable.

**Question 4:** What are the conditions for establishing strict liability under the rule in *Rylands v. Fletcher*?

**Answer:** The conditions are:
1.  Gathering of hazardous things on land.
2.  Non-natural use of land.
3.  Escape of the hazardous thing.
4.  Damage caused by the escape.

**Question 5:** In the case of M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak Case), what new principle of liability was introduced by the Supreme Court of India?

**Answer:** The Supreme Court introduced the principle of **Absolute Liability** in the Oleum Gas Leak Case.

**Question 6:** Can an owner escape liability under the PLI Act if an accident is caused by the willful act of a third party? Explain with reference to the relevant section.

**Answer:** Yes, under Section 8 of the PLI Act, an owner can escape liability if the accident is caused *wholly* by the willful and fore-seeable action of a third party (other than an agent or employee of the owner) for the direct purpose of causing such accident. The burden of proof for this defence lies with the owner.

**Question 7:** Imagine a chemical factory spills a highly toxic substance into a river, causing widespread fish kills and polluting the drinking water for a nearby village. Discuss the potential legal recourse for the affected villagers under Indian environmental law, referencing the principles discussed.

**Answer:** The affected villagers could seek compensation under:
1.  **The Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991:** If the factory was handling hazardous substances and had a valid insurance policy, the villagers could claim compensation for death of fish (damage to property/environment) and health impacts (injury to persons) on a no-fault basis. The Collector would assess damages, and the Claims Tribunal could be approached if unsatisfied.
2.  **The Principle of Absolute Liability:** Given that a chemical factory dealing with toxic substances is undoubtedly engaged in hazardous activity, the Supreme Court's doctrine of absolute liability would apply. The factory would be absolutely liable for the environmental damage and any harm to the villagers, irrespective of any negligence. This would allow for comprehensive compensation for all losses.
3.  **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** The government could also take action under this Act to prevent further pollution and seek restoration of the environment.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   The **PLI Act, 1991**, is a statutory law focusing on mandatory insurance and no-fault liability for handling hazardous substances.
*   **Hazardous substance** and **hazardous activity** are key defined terms under the PLI Act.
*   **Absolute liability** is a judicial doctrine in India that imposes a higher standard of accountability for hazardous activities, with **no defences**.
*   **Strict liability** (*Rylands v. Fletcher*) is a common law principle with specific elements and available defences.
*   The Indian Supreme Court has adopted **absolute liability** for environmental protection, reflecting a proactive approach to holding polluters accountable.
*   The **Bhopal Gas Tragedy** was a significant catalyst for the development of stringent environmental laws and liability principles in India.
*   **No-fault liability** in the PLI Act ensures that victims receive compensation promptly, without needing to prove the owner's negligence.
*   The **Collector** and **Claims Tribunal** are important institutions established under the PLI Act for processing and adjudicating claims.
*   The **Polluter Pays Principle** is a guiding principle in environmental law that is reinforced by the doctrines of strict and absolute liability.
