# Environmental Laws and Policy - Module 4: Hazardous Substances and Activities Legal Framework

## Topic: EPA and Rules Made Thereunder

### 1. Introduction to the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)

The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is an independent agency of the United States federal government tasked with protecting human health and the environment. It was established by President Richard Nixon in 1970 through the Reorganization Plan No. 3 of 1970, consolidating various environmental responsibilities from different federal departments.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Environmental Regulation:** The process of establishing and enforcing rules and standards to protect the environment.
*   **Federal Agency:** An administrative agency of the U.S. federal government.
*   **Independent Agency:** An agency that is not part of an executive department and operates with a degree of autonomy.

**EPA's Mission:**

*   To ensure that **all Americans** have clean air, water, and land.
*   To **reduce environmental risks** and protect public health.
*   To **protect natural resources and the environment** for future generations.
*   To **promote environmental stewardship** and sustainability.

### 2. Key Legislation Empowering the EPA

The EPA's authority to create and enforce rules stems from numerous environmental statutes enacted by Congress. For this module, we will focus on key legislation related to hazardous substances and activities.

#### 2.1. Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA)

**Description:** RCRA is the primary federal law governing the disposal of solid and hazardous waste. It aims to prevent the creation of toxic waste, ensure that waste is managed safely, and clean up existing contaminated sites.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Hazardous Waste:** Waste that exhibits certain characteristics (ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity) or is listed by the EPA as hazardous.
*   **Cradle-to-Grave Management:** RCRA's system for tracking hazardous waste from its generation through its transportation, treatment, storage, and disposal.
*   **Subtitle C:** Specifically addresses hazardous waste management.
*   **Subtitle D:** Addresses non-hazardous solid waste management.

**EPA Rules Made Under RCRA:**

*   **Hazardous Waste Identification:** Defining what constitutes hazardous waste through the **"Characteristic"** wastes (ignitable, corrosive, reactive, toxic) and **"Listed"** wastes (F-list, K-list, P-list, U-list).
    *   **Example:** Spent solvents used in dry cleaning are listed as hazardous waste (F001-F005).
*   **Hazardous Waste Generator Requirements:** Rules for entities that produce hazardous waste, classifying them as Large Quantity Generators (LQGs), Small Quantity Generators (SQGs), or Conditionally Exempt Small Quantity Generators (CESQGs), with varying management standards.
*   **Hazardous Waste Transportation:** Regulations for the manifest system (tracking document) and packaging and labeling requirements.
*   **Hazardous Waste Treatment, Storage, and Disposal Facilities (TSDFs):** Permitting requirements for facilities that handle hazardous waste, including design, operating, closure, and post-closure care standards.
*   **Land Disposal Restrictions (LDRs):** Mandates that hazardous wastes must be treated to meet specific standards before they can be landfilled.
*   **Corrective Action:** Requirements for cleaning up contamination at RCRA-regulated facilities.

#### 2.2. Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA) / Superfund

**Description:** CERCLA, commonly known as Superfund, addresses the cleanup of uncontrolled or abandoned hazardous waste sites. It provides a mechanism for holding parties responsible for the cleanup of contaminated sites.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Potentially Responsible Parties (PRPs):** Individuals or entities that may be liable for cleanup costs, including owners and operators of facilities, past owners/operators, generators of hazardous substances, and transporters who selected the site.
*   **National Priorities List (NPL):** A list of the country's most serious uncontrolled or abandoned hazardous waste sites that are eligible for Superfund-financed remedial action.
*   **Remedial Action (RA):** Cleanup activities undertaken at NPL sites.
*   **Removal Action:** Short-term cleanup activities to address imminent threats.

**EPA Rules Made Under CERCLA:**

*   **Site Identification and Assessment:** Processes for identifying and evaluating potentially contaminated sites.
*   **National Contingency Plan (NCP):** The overarching plan for responding to environmental emergencies, including hazardous substance releases. It outlines procedures for removal and remedial actions, PRP identification, and cost recovery.
*   **NPL Listing Process:** Criteria and procedures for adding sites to the NPL.
*   **Remedy Selection Process:** Guidelines for choosing appropriate cleanup technologies and strategies based on site-specific conditions and risk assessments.
*   **PRP Identification and Enforcement:** Procedures for identifying PRPs and seeking cost recovery or enforcement actions to ensure cleanup.
*   **Community Relations:** Requirements for involving affected communities in the Superfund cleanup process.

#### 2.3. Toxic Substances Control Act (TSCA)

**Description:** TSCA provides the EPA with the authority to regulate the introduction of new or already existing chemicals. It aims to protect human health and the environment from the risks posed by chemical substances.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Chemical Substances:** Elements, compounds, mixtures, and their components.
*   **Premanufacture Notification (PMN):** A requirement for manufacturers and importers of new chemical substances to notify the EPA before they are introduced into commerce.
*   **Risk Assessment:** The process of evaluating the potential adverse effects of chemical substances on human health and the environment.
*   **Significant New Use Rule (SNUR):** A rule that requires companies to notify the EPA before commencing a "significant new use" of a chemical that could increase or change the potential exposure or risk.

**EPA Rules Made Under TSCA:**

*   **Chemical Data Reporting (CDR):** Requires manufacturers and importers to report information on the production, use, and exposure of chemical substances.
*   **Inventory of Chemical Substances:** TSCA maintains a public inventory of chemical substances manufactured or processed in the U.S.
*   **New Chemical Review (PMN Process):** EPA reviews PMNs to determine if new chemicals pose an unreasonable risk and may impose restrictions or prohibitions.
*   **Existing Chemical Review:** EPA assesses risks from existing chemicals and can impose restrictions or bans on those that present an unreasonable risk.
*   **Regulation of Specific Chemicals:** TSCA has been used to regulate specific hazardous chemicals like PCBs (polychlorinated biphenyls) and asbestos.
    *   **Example:** TSCA banned the manufacture, processing, and distribution in commerce of PCBs in 1979.

#### 2.4. Emergency Planning and Community Right-to-Know Act (EPCRA)

**Description:** EPCRA requires federal, state, and local governments, Indian tribes, industry, and the public to work together to develop and implement emergency plans for responding to accidental releases of extremely hazardous substances. It also provides the public with information about potential chemical hazards in their communities.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Extremely Hazardous Substances (EHSs):** A list of chemicals that pose a significant risk to public health and the environment if released.
*   **Emergency Planning Districts:** Geographic areas designated for emergency planning.
*   **Local Emergency Planning Committees (LEPCs):** Committees responsible for developing and implementing emergency plans.
*   **Hazardous Chemical Inventory Reporting:** Requirements for facilities to report inventories of hazardous chemicals.
*   **Toxic Release Inventory (TRI):** A publicly accessible database of toxic chemical releases and other environmental activities reported by certain facilities.

**EPA Rules Made Under EPCRA:**

*   **EPCRA Section 302:** Designation of EHSs and threshold planning quantities (TPQs) for these substances.
*   **EPCRA Section 304:** Requirements for reporting releases of EHSs and hazardous substances.
*   **EPCRA Section 311 & 312:** Requirements for facilities to submit Safety Data Sheets (SDSs) or lists of hazardous chemicals and Tier I/Tier II inventory forms to state and local authorities and fire departments.
*   **EPCRA Section 313 (TRI):** Requirements for facilities in specific industries to report annual releases and other waste management activities for certain toxic chemicals.

### 3. EPA Rulemaking Process

The EPA's ability to create rules (regulations) is established by Congress through legislation. This rulemaking process is governed by the **Administrative Procedure Act (APA)**.

**Steps in the EPA Rulemaking Process:**

1.  **Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM):** EPA may issue an ANPRM to solicit public comment on whether a rulemaking is needed and what issues should be addressed.
2.  **Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM):** The EPA publishes a proposed rule in the **Federal Register**, which includes the agency's rationale, the proposed regulatory text, and a comment period (typically 30-60 days).
3.  **Public Comment Period:** Interested parties (individuals, businesses, environmental groups, etc.) can submit written comments on the proposed rule.
4.  **Public Hearings:** In some cases, the EPA may hold public hearings to gather oral testimony.
5.  **Review and Revision:** The EPA reviews all submitted comments and may revise the proposed rule based on this feedback.
6.  **Final Rule:** If the EPA decides to proceed, it publishes the final rule in the Federal Register, often with a response to significant comments. The final rule includes the regulatory text and an effective date.
7.  **Judicial Review:** Final rules can be challenged in court.

**Important Considerations:**

*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** For significant regulations, the EPA often conducts an analysis of the costs and benefits of the proposed rule.
*   **Public Participation:** The APA emphasizes public participation in the rulemaking process, ensuring transparency and accountability.

### 4. Enforcement of EPA Rules

The EPA has a variety of mechanisms to enforce its regulations.

**Enforcement Tools:**

*   **Inspections and Monitoring:** EPA or authorized state agencies conduct inspections of facilities to ensure compliance.
*   **Notices of Violation (NOVs):** Issued to facilities that are found to be in violation of regulations.
*   **Administrative Orders:** Orders issued by the EPA requiring a party to take specific actions, such as cleanup or stopping a polluting activity.
*   **Civil Penalties:** Fines levied against violators.
*   **Injunctive Relief:** Court orders requiring a party to cease or commence a specific action.
*   **Criminal Prosecution:** For willful or knowing violations, individuals and corporations can face criminal charges.

**Citizen Suits:**

Many environmental statutes, including RCRA and CERCLA, allow citizens to sue companies or the EPA itself for alleged violations of regulations or for failure to perform a non-discretionary duty.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA)?

**Answer:** The primary purpose of RCRA is to manage the disposal of solid and hazardous waste, ensuring that waste is tracked from "cradle-to-grave" and preventing environmental contamination.

**Question 2:** Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of hazardous waste as defined by RCRA?
    a) Ignitability
    b) Toxicity
    c) Volatility
    d) Corrosivity

**Answer:** c) Volatility. The characteristic wastes are ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, and toxicity.

**Question 3:** What is the role of the National Priorities List (NPL) under CERCLA?

**Answer:** The NPL identifies the most serious uncontrolled or abandoned hazardous waste sites in the United States that are eligible for cleanup under the Superfund program.

**Question 4:** Under TSCA, what is the requirement for manufacturers introducing a new chemical substance into commerce?

**Answer:** Manufacturers must submit a Premanufacture Notification (PMN) to the EPA before introducing a new chemical substance into commerce, allowing the EPA to assess its potential risks.

**Question 5:** Briefly explain the purpose of EPCRA's Toxic Release Inventory (TRI).

**Answer:** The TRI is a publicly accessible database that collects information on releases and other waste management activities of certain toxic chemicals by facilities, providing transparency about chemical hazards in communities.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   The **EPA** is the primary federal agency responsible for environmental protection in the U.S.
*   Its authority is derived from **congressional statutes**.
*   **RCRA** governs hazardous waste management from "cradle-to-grave."
*   **CERCLA (Superfund)** addresses the cleanup of contaminated sites and liability for those responsible.
*   **TSCA** regulates chemical substances throughout their lifecycle.
*   **EPCRA** focuses on emergency planning and community right-to-know about chemical hazards.
*   EPA rules are developed through a public **rulemaking process** involving public comment.
*   The EPA has **enforcement powers**, including penalties and legal actions, to ensure compliance with its rules.
*   Understanding the specific requirements and rules under each of these acts is crucial for businesses and individuals dealing with hazardous substances and activities.
