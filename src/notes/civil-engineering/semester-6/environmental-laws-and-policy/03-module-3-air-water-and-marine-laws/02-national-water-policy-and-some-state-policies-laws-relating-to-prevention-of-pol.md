# Environmental Laws and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water, and Marine Laws

## Topic: National Water Policy and State Policies: Prevention of Pollution, Access, Management, and Institutional Mechanisms

This module delves into the legal and policy framework governing water resources in India, focusing on preventing pollution, ensuring equitable access, sustainable management, and the institutional structures responsible for their implementation.

---

### 1. National Water Policy (NWP)

The National Water Policy is a cornerstone of India's water resource management. It outlines the principles and strategies for the country's water sector.

#### 1.1. Evolution of National Water Policies

*   **NWP 1987:** Focused on increasing water availability through optimal use of resources, conservation, and pollution control. Emphasized the need for a national water grid.
*   **NWP 2002:** Acknowledged water as a "deemed good" rather than a "free good." Focused on integrated water resources management (IWRM), promoting participatory approaches, and addressing the economic aspects of water.
*   **NWP 2012:** The most recent policy, it reinforces the principles of IWRM and sustainability. It emphasizes water as a **social good and an economic good**, with a strong focus on **access to clean drinking water and sanitation**.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Principles of NWP 2012

*   **Water as a Social Good and Economic Good:** Balancing human needs with economic utility.
*   **Water for Life:** Guaranteeing access to safe and adequate drinking water for all.
*   **Water Scarcity and Demand Management:** Efficient use, conservation, and addressing growing demands.
*   **Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM):** Considering water as a single resource, promoting coordinated development and management of water, land, and related resources.
*   **Participation of Stakeholders:** Involving users and stakeholders in water management decisions.
*   **Water Pricing:** Pricing water to encourage efficient use while ensuring affordability for the poor.
*   **Pollution Prevention and Control:** Treating wastewater and preventing industrial and agricultural pollution.
*   **Rainwater Harvesting:** Promoting rainwater harvesting and water conservation.
*   **Inter-State Water Disputes:** Promoting the resolution of disputes through negotiation and adjudication.
*   **Groundwater Management:** Regulating and managing groundwater extraction.
*   **Water Quality Monitoring:** Establishing robust systems for monitoring water quality.
*   **Climate Change Adaptation:** Incorporating strategies to adapt to climate change impacts on water resources.

#### 1.3. Important Highlights of NWP 2012

*   **Universal Access to Water:** Aims to ensure safe and adequate water for all, with a focus on drinking water and sanitation.
*   **Water Conservation and Demand Management:** Strong emphasis on reducing wastage and increasing efficiency in all sectors.
*   **Pollution Control:** More stringent measures for preventing and controlling water pollution.
*   **River Basin Management:** Promotes a basin-level approach to water management.
*   **Role of Women:** Recognition of the crucial role of women in water management and conservation.

### 2. State Water Policies

States have their own water policies, which are often framed within the principles of the National Water Policy but are tailored to the specific needs and challenges of the state.

#### 2.1. Why State Water Policies are Necessary

*   **State Subject:** Water is primarily a state subject under the Indian Constitution (List II, State List).
*   **Regional Variations:** Different states have unique hydrological conditions, rainfall patterns, water demand profiles, and socio-economic contexts.
*   **Specific Challenges:** States face specific issues like groundwater depletion in some regions, heavy rainfall and floods in others, interstate water sharing disputes, and specific industrial or agricultural water needs.

#### 2.2. Common Features of State Water Policies

*   **Alignment with NWP:** Generally, state policies align with the broad objectives of the NWP.
*   **Focus on Local Issues:** Address state-specific problems like groundwater over-exploitation, river pollution in specific stretches, or the management of local water bodies.
*   **Water Sector Reforms:** Often include provisions for reforms in water pricing, institutional arrangements, and participatory management.
*   **Promotion of Rainwater Harvesting:** Encouraging adoption of rainwater harvesting structures.
*   **Wastewater Reuse:** Policies to promote the reuse of treated wastewater.

#### 2.3. Examples of State Water Policies (Illustrative)

*   **Rajasthan:** Emphasis on groundwater management due to arid and semi-arid conditions, promotion of water-efficient irrigation, and drought management.
*   **Maharashtra:** Focus on watershed development, integrated water resources management, and addressing urban water supply challenges.
*   **Tamil Nadu:** Strong focus on rainwater harvesting (especially after the 2001 policy), promoting drip and sprinkler irrigation, and coastal water management.
*   **Kerala:** Emphasis on conservation of its abundant water resources, managing monsoonal rainfall, and addressing water quality issues in its numerous rivers and backwaters.

**Note:** It is crucial to consult the specific water policy of a particular state for detailed understanding.

### 3. Laws Relating to Prevention of Pollution, Access, and Management of Water

India has a robust legal framework to address water pollution, ensure access, and manage water resources.

#### 3.1. Prevention and Control of Water Pollution

*   **The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide for the prevention and control of water pollution and the maintenance or restoration of the wholesomeness of water.
    *   **Key Provisions:**
        *   **Prohibition of Discharge of Polluting Matter:** Prohibits the discharge of sewage or any polluting matter into any stream or well, or the laying of pipes for such discharge, without the consent of the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB).
        *   **Standards for Water:** Empowers SPCBs and the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) to lay down standards for water quality for different uses (e.g., drinking, bathing, agriculture).
        *   **Establishment of SPCBs and CPCB:** Creates the institutional framework for regulating water pollution.
        *   **Powers of Entry and Inspection:** Grants powers to authorized officers to inspect and take samples of water and discharge.
        *   **Penalties:** Prescribes penalties for contravention of the Act.
    *   **Amendments:** The Act has been amended to strengthen its provisions and include new types of pollutants.

*   **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:**
    *   **Purpose:** A broader legislation to protect and improve the environment.
    *   **Key Provisions related to Water:**
        *   **Power to Lay Down Standards:** Empowers the Central Government to lay down standards for quality of environment, including water quality.
        *   **Prohibition of Hazardous Wastes:** Prohibits the handling of hazardous wastes except under prescribed conditions.
        *   **Directions and Orders:** Allows the Central Government to issue directions and orders for the prevention, control, and abatement of environmental pollution.
        *   **Public Liability Insurance:** Mandates insurance for persons handling hazardous substances.

*   **The Municipal Solid Wastes (Management and Handling) Rules, 2016:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure proper management of municipal solid waste, which often contributes to water pollution through leachate.
    *   **Key Provisions:** Stipulates procedures for collection, segregation, transportation, processing, and disposal of municipal solid waste, including provisions for preventing water contamination.

*   **The Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016:**
    *   **Purpose:** To manage hazardous and other wastes, including those that can pollute water bodies.
    *   **Key Provisions:** Regulates the generation, collection, storage, treatment, transport, and disposal of hazardous wastes, with a focus on preventing environmental contamination.

*   **The Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals Rules, 1989:**
    *   **Purpose:** To prevent accidents and pollution arising from hazardous chemicals.
    *   **Key Provisions:** Mandates safety precautions and reporting for industries handling hazardous chemicals, many of which can impact water quality if released.

#### 3.2. Access to Water

*   **The Constitution of India:**
    *   **Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty):** Has been interpreted by the Supreme Court to include the "right to clean drinking water" as part of the fundamental right to life.
    *   **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP):** Articles like 48A (protection and improvement of environment and safeguarding of forests and wild life) and 39A (equal justice and free legal aid) indirectly support equitable access to resources like water.

*   **National Water Policy (NWP) 2012:**
    *   **Explicitly states:** "Drinking water is a basic human need and a fundamental right."

*   **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (Clean India Mission):** While primarily focused on sanitation, it indirectly contributes to better water access by improving hygiene and reducing water contamination.

*   **Jal Jeevan Mission (National Rural Drinking Water Programme):** Aims to provide safe and adequate drinking water to all households in rural India.

*   **Urban Water Supply Schemes:** Various schemes and policies at the national and state level focus on providing potable water to urban populations.

#### 3.3. Management of Water Resources

*   **The River Boards Act, 1956:**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide for the constitution of river boards for the regulation and development of inter-state rivers and river valleys.
    *   **Key Provisions:** Allows for the establishment of river boards to advise on matters relating to water management, conservation, and the prevention of pollution in inter-state rivers. (Limited operationalization).

*   **The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956:**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide for the adjudication of disputes relating to inter-state rivers and river valley.
    *   **Key Provisions:**
        *   **Tribunal Formation:** Empowers the Central Government to form a Tribunal to adjudicate water disputes between states.
        *   **Binding Decisions:** Decisions of the Tribunal are final and binding on the states concerned.
    *   **Examples:** Krishna Water Disputes Tribunal, Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal.

*   **Groundwater Management:**
    *   **No central legislation specifically for groundwater extraction:** This is a significant gap.
    *   **State-level Regulations:** Many states have introduced legislation or rules for groundwater extraction, such as:
        *   **No Objection Certificates (NOCs):** Requirement for NOCs for drilling borewells.
        *   **Regulation of Groundwater Abstraction:** Imposing restrictions on the depth and number of borewells.
        *   **Rainwater Harvesting Mandates:** Encouraging or mandating rainwater harvesting structures.
    *   **NWP 2012:** Emphasizes the need for regulation and management of groundwater.

*   **Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM):**
    *   Promoted by NWP 2012, it emphasizes a holistic approach to water management, considering all aspects of the water cycle and involving all stakeholders.

### 4. Institutional Mechanism

Effective implementation of water laws and policies relies on a strong institutional framework.

#### 4.1. Central Government Institutions

*   **Ministry of Jal Shakti:**
    *   **Department of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation:** Responsible for the overall policy, planning, and management of water resources, including inter-state rivers, dams, irrigation, and groundwater.
    *   **Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation:** Focuses on providing safe drinking water and sanitation facilities.

*   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):**
    *   **Role:** Apex body for pollution control in India.
    *   **Water-related functions:** Lays down water quality standards, monitors water quality, identifies polluted river stretches, and advises the Central Government on water pollution control.

*   **Central Ground Water Board (CGWB):**
    *   **Role:** National apex organization for scientific and sustainable development and management of groundwater resources.
    *   **Functions:** Conducts hydrogeological surveys, monitors groundwater levels, regulates groundwater extraction (in certain areas), and promotes rainwater harvesting.

#### 4.2. State Government Institutions

*   **State Water Resources Departments:**
    *   **Role:** Responsible for water management, irrigation, dams, canals, and inter-state water sharing within the state.

*   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):**
    *   **Role:** Implement water pollution control laws within the state.
    *   **Functions:** Grant consents to industries for discharging effluents, monitor water quality, enforce pollution control measures, and prosecute offenders.

*   **State Ground Water Departments:**
    *   **Role:** Manage and regulate groundwater resources within the state, often in coordination with CGWB.

*   **Local Authorities (Municipalities, Panchayats):**
    *   **Role:** Responsible for providing drinking water supply and sanitation in their respective jurisdictions, managing local water bodies, and implementing water conservation measures.

#### 4.3. Other Relevant Institutions

*   **River Boards:** As per the River Boards Act, 1956, for inter-state river management (limited operationalization).
*   **Water Tribunals:** Constituted under the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956, for adjudicating water disputes.
*   **NGOs and Civil Society Organizations:** Play a crucial role in advocacy, awareness generation, community participation, and project implementation.
*   **Water User Associations (WUAs):** Community-based organizations involved in the management of irrigation systems.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Explain the evolution of the National Water Policy and highlight the key differences between NWP 2002 and NWP 2012.

**Answer:**
The National Water Policy (NWP) has evolved over time to address changing water challenges.
*   **NWP 1987:** Focused on increasing water availability, optimal utilization, conservation, and pollution control.
*   **NWP 2002:** Introduced the concept of water as a "deemed good" and emphasized integrated water resources management (IWRM) and participatory approaches.
*   **NWP 2012:** Reinforces IWRM and sustainability, crucially reframes water as a **"social good and an economic good."** Key differences from NWP 2002 include a stronger emphasis on **universal access to clean drinking water and sanitation as a basic human need**, more explicit focus on **demand management**, stricter provisions for **pollution control**, and a greater recognition of the role of **women in water management**.

**Question 2:** Which central legislation provides the primary framework for the prevention and control of water pollution in India? What are its key provisions?

**Answer:**
The primary central legislation for the prevention and control of water pollution in India is **The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974.**

Key provisions include:
*   Prohibition of discharging polluting matter into streams without consent.
*   Empowerment of CPCB and SPCBs to lay down water quality standards.
*   Establishment of CPCB and SPCBs as regulatory bodies.
*   Powers of entry, inspection, and sampling by authorized officers.
*   Prescription of penalties for violations.

**Question 3:** Discuss the constitutional basis for the right to clean drinking water in India.

**Answer:**
The constitutional basis for the right to clean drinking water in India is primarily derived from **Article 21 of the Constitution of India, which guarantees the Right to Life and Personal Liberty.** The Supreme Court of India, through various landmark judgments, has interpreted this fundamental right to include the **"right to clean drinking water"** as an essential component of a dignified life. Additionally, **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)**, such as Article 48A (protection and improvement of environment), indirectly support the government's obligation to ensure access to clean water.

**Question 4:** Why are state water policies essential, and what are some common areas of focus in these policies?

**Answer:**
State water policies are essential because:
*   **Water is a State Subject:** The Constitution designates water as a state subject, allowing states to frame policies based on their specific needs.
*   **Regional Variations:** States have unique hydrological conditions, rainfall patterns, demand profiles, and socio-economic contexts that require tailored management strategies.
*   **Addressing Local Challenges:** State policies can effectively address specific regional issues like groundwater depletion, unique pollution problems, or localized water disputes.

Common areas of focus in state water policies include:
*   Groundwater management and regulation.
*   Promoting rainwater harvesting and water conservation.
*   Wastewater treatment and reuse.
*   Watershed management.
*   Improving irrigation efficiency.
*   Urban water supply and sanitation.

**Question 5 (Exercise):** Imagine you are a policy advisor to a state government facing severe groundwater depletion. Which existing laws and policies would you recommend strengthening or implementing to address this issue? Justify your recommendations.

**Answer:**
As a policy advisor, I would recommend a multi-pronged approach focusing on strengthening and implementing the following:

1.  **Groundwater Regulation Legislation:** While there's no central law, a strong state-level law is crucial. This would include:
    *   **Mandatory No Objection Certificates (NOCs)** for all new borewells, with stringent conditions based on local hydrogeological assessments.
    *   **Regulation of existing borewells** to limit extraction volumes and depths.
    *   **Prohibition of new borewells** in "over-exploited" assessment areas or with strict licensing.
    *   **Provision for rainwater harvesting (RWH) as a prerequisite** for drilling borewells or obtaining permits.

2.  **Enforcement of Rainwater Harvesting (RWH) Mandates:**
    *   **Make RWH mandatory for all new constructions** (residential, commercial, industrial) and encourage retrofitting in existing buildings.
    *   **Provide incentives** for installing effective RWH systems.
    *   **Establish clear guidelines** for the design and maintenance of RWH systems.

3.  **Promoting Water-Efficient Irrigation:**
    *   **Subsidies and support** for farmers adopting drip and sprinkler irrigation.
    *   **Awareness campaigns** on water-saving agricultural practices.
    *   **Encouraging the use of treated wastewater** for irrigation where feasible and safe.

4.  **Strengthening Enforcement Mechanisms:**
    *   **Increased capacity building and resources** for state groundwater departments and pollution control boards.
    *   **Robust monitoring and data collection** on groundwater levels and extraction.
    *   **Strict penalties** for violations of groundwater regulations.

5.  **Public Awareness and Participation:**
    *   **Community-led initiatives** for groundwater conservation and management.
    *   **Educational programs** on the importance of groundwater and sustainable practices.

**Justification:** Groundwater is a critical but finite resource. Without strong regulatory oversight and active conservation measures, its depletion will have severe consequences for agriculture, livelihoods, and drinking water availability. A combination of legal mandates, incentives, and public participation is essential for sustainable groundwater management.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Water as a Social and Economic Good:** This dual nature is central to modern water policy.
*   **IWRM:** Integrated Water Resources Management is the guiding principle for sustainable water management.
*   **The Water Act, 1974:** The foundational law for preventing water pollution.
*   **Article 21:** Enshrines the right to clean drinking water.
*   **State Subject:** Water management is largely a state responsibility, leading to diverse state policies.
*   **Institutional Framework:** The effective functioning of CPCB, SPCBs, CGWB, and state departments is crucial for implementation.
*   **Groundwater Management Gap:** The lack of a comprehensive central law for groundwater extraction is a significant challenge.
*   **Participatory Approach:** Involving stakeholders, including women, is key to effective water management.
*   **Pollution Prevention:** Proactive measures are more effective than reactive ones.
