# Environmental Laws and Policy: Module 2 - Forest, Wildlife and Biodiversity Related Laws

## Topic: Biological Diversity Act, 2002

### 1. Introduction and Objectives

The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (BDA, 2002) is a landmark piece of legislation in India aimed at conserving biological diversity, sustainably using its components, and ensuring the equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge. It was enacted to give effect to India's obligations under the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD), to which India is a party.

**Key Objectives:**

*   **Conservation of Biological Diversity:** To protect and maintain the variety of life forms in India, including species, ecosystems, and genetic diversity.
*   **Sustainable Use of Biological Resources:** To ensure that biological resources are used in a manner that does not lead to their depletion and can be sustained for future generations.
*   **Equitable Sharing of Benefits:** To ensure fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge between the people of India and the users of these resources.
*   **Regulation of Access to Biological Resources:** To control and regulate access to biological resources and associated traditional knowledge by foreign nationals and Indian citizens for commercial purposes.
*   **Protection of Traditional Knowledge:** To safeguard the traditional knowledge of local communities relating to biological resources.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Biological Diversity:** Means the variability among living organisms from all sources including, inter alia, terrestrial, marine and other aquatic ecosystems and the ecological complexes of which they are part; this includes diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems.
*   **Biological Resources:** Means plants, animals and micro-organisms, their genetic material and other factors of value contained in them, by whatever name they are referred to.
*   **Traditional Knowledge:** Means knowledge of the properties or uses of biological resources that is existing with any Indian community or people of India.
*   **Access:** Means obtaining biological resources for the purposes of research, breeding, characterization, utilization, or any other purpose, except for the purposes of commercial utilization.
*   **Commercial Utilization:** Means end-use of biological resources for the purposes of obtaining commercial benefits.
*   **Benefit Sharing:** Means sharing of benefits arising out of the use of biological resources and knowledge related thereto with the people of India.
*   **Intellectual Property Rights:** Refers to rights granted for inventions that are new, involve an inventive step and are capable of industrial application, and includes patents, plant variety protection, and geographical indications.
*   **Genetic Resources:** Means genetic material of actual or potential value.
*   **Genetic Material:** Means any material of plant, animal, microbial or other origin containing functional units of heredity.
*   **Local Communities:** Means communities of people residing in a particular geographical area, recognized by the State Government, having ownership or custodianship of biological resources, who are affected by the conservation and sustainable use of biological diversity.

### 3. Institutional Framework

The Act establishes a three-tier institutional structure for the implementation of its provisions:

**a) National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)**

*   **Establishment:** Constituted by the Central Government under Section 8 of the Act.
*   **Functions:**
    *   Advise the Central Government on matters related to conservation, sustainable use, and equitable sharing of benefits.
    *   Regulate by granting approvals or rejecting requests for commercial utilization of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge.
    *   Enter into agreements with foreign individuals, institutions, or countries for commercial utilization of biological resources.
    *   Supervise the implementation of the Act.
    *   Promote research and development in biological diversity.
    *   Develop national strategies, plans, and programmes for conservation and sustainable use.
    *   Prepare inventories of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge.
    *   Propose measures for protection of intellectual property rights.
*   **Composition:** Chairman and other members appointed by the Central Government.

**b) State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs)**

*   **Establishment:** Constituted by the respective State Governments under Section 22 of the Act.
*   **Functions:**
    *   Advise the State Government on matters related to conservation, sustainable use, and equitable sharing of benefits.
    *   Regulate by granting approvals or rejecting requests for commercial utilization of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge by persons other than citizens of India.
    *   Cooperate with the NBA and other SBBs.
    *   Conduct surveys of biological resources within their jurisdiction.
    *   Identify and notify specific biological resources and associated traditional knowledge for the purpose of conservation and sustainable use.
    *   Promote research and development.
*   **Composition:** Chairman and other members appointed by the State Government.

**c) Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)**

*   **Establishment:** Constituted by the respective State Governments at the local level (village or municipal ward) under Section 41 of the Act.
*   **Functions:**
    *   Conserve and promote conservation of biodiversity within their jurisdiction.
    *   Promote sustainable use of biological resources.
    *   Comply with the requirements of the Act and the policies of the State Government.
    *   Record the knowledge, innovations and practices of the local communities related to biological resources.
    *   Secure the sharing of benefits arising out of the use of biological resources and knowledge related thereto with the local communities.
    *   Seek advice from the SBB on matters related to conservation, sustainable use, and benefit sharing.
*   **Composition:** Typically comprises elected local representatives, traditional knowledge holders, and experts.

### 4. Regulation of Access and Benefit Sharing

The Act focuses on regulating access to India's biological resources and ensuring that benefits derived from their use are shared equitably.

**a) Access by Foreign Nationals/Institutions:**

*   Requires prior approval of the **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)** for obtaining biological resources for commercial utilization.
*   The NBA can grant access subject to terms and conditions, including benefit sharing arrangements.
*   The NBA can also enter into agreements for commercial utilization.

**b) Access by Indian Citizens/Institutions:**

*   Requires prior intimation to the **State Biodiversity Board (SBB)** for obtaining biological resources for commercial utilization.
*   The SBB can impose conditions, including benefit sharing, and can also reject the request if it is detrimental to conservation or sustainable use.
*   The SBB plays a crucial role in facilitating access and benefit sharing at the state level.

**c) Benefit Sharing:**

*   **Monetary Benefits:** This can include royalties, license fees, and other forms of payment for access to biological resources.
*   **Non-Monetary Benefits:** This can include research collaboration, technology transfer, capacity building, and sharing of intellectual property rights.
*   **Equitable Sharing:** The Act emphasizes that benefits must be shared equitably with the local communities who are the custodians of these resources and associated traditional knowledge.
*   **Role of BMCs:** BMCs are central to the benefit-sharing process, as they are responsible for documenting traditional knowledge and negotiating benefit-sharing agreements with users.

**d) Protection of Traditional Knowledge:**

*   The Act mandates the recording of traditional knowledge by BMCs.
*   This documentation helps in preventing misappropriation and ensuring that benefits reach the communities.
*   Prior informed consent (PIC) of the BMCs and relevant government authorities is required before accessing biological resources and associated traditional knowledge for commercial purposes.

### 5. Prohibitions and Penalties

The Act lays down certain prohibitions to protect biological diversity and regulate its use.

*   **Prohibition on Access:** No Indian citizen or entity shall be denied the right to access biological resources for the purpose of commercial utilization, except as provided under the Act.
*   **Prohibition on Certain Acts:**
    *   No person shall be granted any intellectual property right in or to any biological resource, or any research or information derived from it, without the knowledge of the National Biodiversity Authority.
    *   No person shall make any application for obtaining any intellectual property right in or to any biological resource, or any research or information derived from it, in India or outside India, without the previous approval of the NBA.
*   **Penalties:** The Act prescribes penalties for contravention of its provisions, including fines and imprisonment.

### 6. Key Provisions and Mechanisms

*   **National Biodiversity Fund (NBF):** Established by the Central Government to receive funds for the purpose of benefit sharing and conservation.
*   **State Biodiversity Funds:** State Governments can also establish similar funds.
*   **Reporting and Monitoring:** Provisions for regular reporting by NBA, SBBs, and BMCs to ensure effective implementation.
*   **Role of Customs and Other Authorities:** Customs and other relevant authorities are empowered to prevent the export of biological resources without the approval of the NBA.
*   **Judicial Review:** The Act also outlines procedures for appeals and judicial review of decisions made by the authorities.

### 7. Importance and Challenges

**Importance:**

*   **Catalyst for Conservation:** Provides a legal framework for the conservation of India's rich biodiversity.
*   **Empowerment of Local Communities:** Recognizes and empowers local communities as custodians of biological resources and traditional knowledge.
*   **Fair Trade in Biological Resources:** Ensures that India benefits from the commercial use of its biodiversity.
*   **Compliance with International Obligations:** Fulfills India's commitments under the CBD.

**Challenges:**

*   **Effective Implementation:** Ensuring effective implementation across all levels, especially at the grassroots by BMCs, can be challenging due to resource constraints, awareness gaps, and capacity limitations.
*   **Valuation of Biological Resources and Traditional Knowledge:** Quantifying the monetary and non-monetary value of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge for benefit sharing is complex.
*   **Documentation of Traditional Knowledge:** The process of documenting traditional knowledge is vast and requires significant effort and resources.
*   **Coordination:** Ensuring seamless coordination between the NBA, SBBs, and BMCs, as well as with other government departments, is crucial.
*   **Awareness and Capacity Building:** Raising awareness among stakeholders, including local communities, researchers, and industries, about the provisions of the Act is an ongoing challenge.
*   **Access and Benefit Sharing Agreements:** Negotiating and enforcing fair and equitable benefit-sharing agreements can be intricate.

### 8. Practice Questions and Answers

**Q1. What are the main objectives of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002?**

**Answer:** The main objectives are the conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and the equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge.

**Q2. Which authority is responsible for granting approval for commercial utilization of biological resources by foreign nationals in India?**

**Answer:** The National Biodiversity Authority (NBA).

**Q3. What is the role of Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002?**

**Answer:** BMCs are responsible for conserving and promoting biodiversity, promoting sustainable use of biological resources, recording traditional knowledge, and securing benefit sharing with local communities within their jurisdiction.

**Q4. Briefly explain the concept of "Benefit Sharing" as defined in the Act.**

**Answer:** Benefit sharing refers to the sharing of benefits arising out of the use of biological resources and knowledge related thereto with the people of India, ensuring it is fair and equitable. This can be monetary or non-monetary.

**Q5. What are the potential challenges in the implementation of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002?**

**Answer:** Potential challenges include effective implementation at the grassroots level, valuation of resources and traditional knowledge, documentation of traditional knowledge, coordination among various bodies, and awareness and capacity building.

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   **Three-Tier Structure:** Remember the roles of NBA, SBBs, and BMCs.
*   **Prior Approval:** Crucial for commercial utilization of biological resources by foreigners and notification for Indian citizens.
*   **Benefit Sharing:** The cornerstone of the Act, ensuring fairness to local communities.
*   **Traditional Knowledge:** Emphasis on its documentation and protection.
*   **Access Regulation:** The Act controls who can access and use India's biological wealth.
*   **IP Rights:** Special provisions regarding Intellectual Property Rights related to biological resources.
