# STRUCTURAL GEOLOGY - Module 4: Fractures and Faults

## Topic: Major Lineaments in Kerala and Their Possible Implications

This module focuses on understanding fractures and faults, their classification, and how these structural features manifest and impact regions like Kerala.

### 1. Introduction to Lineaments

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Lineament:** A linear or curvilinear feature on the Earth's surface that is recognized in remotely sensed imagery (e.g., satellite images, aerial photographs) and is interpreted as having a geological origin. These are surface expressions of underlying geological structures, most commonly faults or fracture zones.
*   **Fracture:** A general term for any break in a rock mass. This can include joints (no displacement) and faults (with displacement).
*   **Fault:** A fracture or zone of fractures along which there has been significant displacement of the rock blocks on either side.
*   **Remotely Sensed Data:** Data acquired from a distance, typically using sensors on satellites or aircraft. This data is crucial for identifying large-scale geological features like lineaments, which might not be easily discernible from ground-level observation.

**How Lineaments are Identified:**

*   **Visual Interpretation of Satellite Imagery:** Linear alignments of features like valleys, ridges, vegetation patterns, soil moisture variations, or drainage systems.
*   **Digital Elevation Models (DEMs):** Revealing subtle topographic features that correspond to underlying structures.
*   **Geophysical Data:** Gravity, magnetic, and radar data can highlight subsurface variations associated with lineaments.

### 2. Major Lineaments in Kerala

Kerala, situated on the southwestern margin of the Indian Peninsula, exhibits significant structural features, including several prominent lineaments. These are primarily related to the breakup of Gondwanaland and subsequent tectonic events.

**Key Lineaments Identified in Kerala:**

*   **The Periyar Lineament (Palakkad Gap Lineament):**
    *   **Description:** One of the most prominent and extensively studied lineaments in Kerala. It's a major structural discontinuity that cuts across the Western Ghats.
    *   **Geometrical Characteristics:** Primarily exhibits a WNW-ESE to E-W orientation. It is often interpreted as a major shear zone or a series of en echelon faults.
    *   **Possible Origin (Genetic Classification):**
        *   **Tectonic Origin:** Widely believed to be a result of major crustal rifting associated with the breakup of the Indian plate from Madagascar and Africa during the Mesozoic era. It likely represents a zone of weakness or a transform fault boundary.
        *   **Structural Control:** May also be influenced by pre-existing lithological boundaries or older Proterozoic structures.
    *   **Associated Features:** Often marked by a distinct topographic depression (the Palakkad Gap), localized occurrences of charnockites, breccias, and mylonites, suggesting significant tectonic activity and hydrothermal alteration.

*   **The Bhavani Lineament:**
    *   **Description:** Another significant E-W trending structural zone, located north of the Periyar Lineament.
    *   **Geometrical Characteristics:** Primarily E-W to WNW-ESE orientation.
    *   **Possible Origin (Genetic Classification):** Similar to the Periyar Lineament, it is often linked to the Gondwana rifting events. It could represent a related shear zone or fault system.
    *   **Associated Features:** May influence drainage patterns and the distribution of certain rock types.

*   **The Idukki Lineament (Central Kerala Lineament):**
    *   **Description:** A curvilinear to linear feature with a general NE-SW to N-S orientation, observed in the central parts of Kerala.
    *   **Geometrical Characteristics:** Dominantly NE-SW to N-S trending, often intersecting with E-W lineaments.
    *   **Possible Origin (Genetic Classification):**
        *   **Intraplate Tectonics:** Could be related to intraplate stresses following the breakup of Gondwanaland or later Himalayan orogeny.
        *   **Pre-existing Weaknesses:** May exploit older Proterozoic structural anisotropies within the crust.

*   **Other Minor Lineaments:** Numerous smaller lineaments with varying orientations (N-S, NE-SW, NW-SE) are also observed, often reflecting localized fracturing and joint systems within the Precambrian crystalline rocks.

**Important Note:** The precise geometric and genetic classification of lineaments can be complex and may involve multiple episodes of faulting and fracturing. Interpretation often relies on integrating various geological, geophysical, and remote sensing data.

### 3. Geometric and Genetic Classification of Fractures and Faults (Context for Lineaments)

While lineaments are observed features, they are surface manifestations of underlying fractures and faults. Understanding their classification helps in interpreting the lineaments.

**Geometric Classification (based on orientation and type of displacement):**

*   **Faults:**
    *   **Dip-Slip Faults:**
        *   **Normal Fault:** Hanging wall moves down relative to the footwall (caused by tensional stress).
        *   **Reverse Fault:** Hanging wall moves up relative to the footwall (caused by compressional stress).
        *   **Thrust Fault:** A low-angle reverse fault (often associated with mountain building).
    *   **Strike-Slip Faults:**
        *   **Dextral (Right-Lateral):** Opposite block moves to the right.
        *   **Sinistral (Left-Lateral):** Opposite block moves to the left.
    *   **Oblique-Slip Faults:** Combination of dip-slip and strike-slip movement.
*   **Joints:** Fractures with no apparent displacement.
    *   **Shear Joints:** Formed by shear stress.
    *   **Tension Joints:** Formed by tensile stress.

**Genetic Classification (based on origin and associated stress regime):**

*   **Tensional Fractures/Faults:** Formed under extensional stress regimes (e.g., rifting, basin formation). Normal faults are common.
*   **Compressional Fractures/Faults:** Formed under compressional stress regimes (e.g., mountain building). Reverse faults and thrust faults are common.
*   **Shear Fractures/Faults:** Formed under shear stress regimes (e.g., transform plate boundaries). Strike-slip faults are dominant.
*   **Volcanic Fractures:** Associated with volcanic activity (e.g., cooling joints, fissure vents).
*   **Tectonic Fractures/Faults:** Resulting from large-scale plate tectonic movements.

**Application to Kerala Lineaments:**

*   The prominent E-W trending lineaments (Periyar, Bhavani) are often interpreted as **strike-slip faults** or **shear zones** that accommodated significant horizontal displacement during Gondwana rifting, with possible components of dip-slip movement.
*   The NE-SW trending lineaments might be related to **strike-slip or oblique-slip faulting** in response to different stress regimes acting on the Indian plate.

### 4. Implications of Major Lineaments in Kerala

The presence of these significant geological structures has profound implications for the region:

*   **Groundwater Potential:**
    *   **Duality:** Lineaments act as zones of enhanced permeability due to fracturing and faulting, which can act as conduits for groundwater flow and storage.
    *   **Implication:** Areas along major lineaments are often targeted for groundwater exploration, as they can host significant aquifers, especially in hard rock terrains. However, they can also be pathways for contamination.
    *   **Example:** Many borewells in Kerala are drilled along identified lineaments to access groundwater.

*   **Seismicity and Seismic Hazard:**
    *   **Activity:** While Kerala is not considered a high seismic risk zone compared to the Himalayan region, there are instances of seismic activity.
    *   **Implication:** Major lineaments represent zones of weakness in the crust. Reactivation of these ancient faults due to regional stresses (e.g., from Himalayan uplift or Deccan Traps volcanism) can lead to seismicity.
    *   **Example:** Occasional minor earthquakes have been reported in Kerala, and their epicenters are sometimes found to be located along or proximal to major lineaments, suggesting a potential control.

*   **Mineral and Resource Exploration:**
    *   **Hydrothermal Systems:** Fault zones are often associated with hydrothermal activity, which can lead to the concentration and deposition of various minerals.
    *   **Implication:** Lineaments can be favorable zones for exploring mineral deposits, although significant mineral discoveries directly linked to Kerala's major lineaments are not widely reported. However, they might influence the distribution of economically important mineral occurrences.
    *   **Potential:** Certain lithologies associated with fault zones (like breccias) can be indicators for mineralization.

*   **Geohazards (Landslides):**
    *   **Slope Stability:** The presence of fractured rock masses along lineaments can reduce the shear strength of slopes, making them more susceptible to failure, especially in the hilly regions of Kerala.
    *   **Implication:** Areas intersecting with lineaments and experiencing high rainfall may be prone to landslides.

*   **Landscape Evolution and Drainage:**
    *   **Structural Control:** Lineaments often control major geomorphic features such as valleys, river courses, and topographic depressions.
    *   **Example:** The Palakkad Gap is a classic example of a major lineament dictating a significant topographic feature and influencing drainage patterns.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Define the term "lineament" and explain how they are typically identified using remote sensing techniques.

**Answer 1:**
A lineament is a linear or curvilinear feature on the Earth's surface that is recognized in remotely sensed imagery and is interpreted as having a geological origin, commonly representing faults or fracture zones. They are identified by visual interpretation of patterns in satellite images or aerial photographs, such as alignments of valleys, ridges, vegetation, or soil moisture variations, and can also be delineated using digital elevation models and geophysical data.

**Question 2:**
Describe the general orientation and probable origin of the Periyar Lineament in Kerala. What are some geological features often associated with it?

**Answer 2:**
The Periyar Lineament (Palakkad Gap Lineament) generally trends WNW-ESE to E-W. It is widely believed to have originated from major crustal rifting associated with the breakup of the Indian plate from Madagascar and Africa during the Mesozoic era, potentially acting as a transform fault or a major shear zone. Associated geological features include the Palakkad Gap (a topographic depression), localized occurrences of charnockites, breccias, and mylonites, indicating significant tectonic activity and alteration.

**Question 3:**
Discuss two significant implications of major lineaments in Kerala, providing specific examples where possible.

**Answer 3:**
Two significant implications are:
1.  **Groundwater Potential:** Lineaments act as zones of enhanced permeability, serving as conduits for groundwater. Many borewells in Kerala are drilled along these features to access groundwater resources.
2.  **Seismicity and Seismic Hazard:** As zones of crustal weakness, lineaments can be reactivated by regional stresses, potentially causing seismic activity. While Kerala has low seismicity, epicenters of some minor earthquakes have been observed near major lineaments.

**Question 4:**
Differentiate between the geometric and genetic classifications of faults. Provide one example of each that could be relevant to understanding the structures controlling lineaments in Kerala.

**Answer 4:**
*   **Geometric Classification:** Describes the orientation of the fault plane and the direction of slip (displacement).
    *   *Example:* A **strike-slip fault** (geometric classification) where blocks move horizontally past each other. The Periyar Lineament could be interpreted as having a significant strike-slip component.
*   **Genetic Classification:** Describes the origin of the fault and the associated stress regime that formed it.
    *   *Example:* A **tensional fault** (genetic classification) formed under extensional stress. While the major lineaments are likely related to shear, smaller fractures within them might have tensional origins. Alternatively, a **shear fault** (genetic classification) formed under shear stress is highly relevant for features like the Periyar Lineament.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Lineaments are surface expressions of subsurface geological structures**, predominantly faults and major fracture zones.
*   **Remote sensing data is crucial for identifying lineaments** due to their large scale.
*   **The Periyar Lineament (Palakkad Gap)** is a key structural feature in Kerala, likely linked to **Mesozoic rifting** and exhibiting **E-W to WNW-ESE trends**.
*   **Lineaments have practical implications** for groundwater availability, seismic hazard assessment, resource exploration, and geohazards like landslides.
*   Understanding the **geometric (orientation, slip) and genetic (stress regime, origin)** classification of fractures and faults helps interpret the nature and history of lineaments.
*   The Precambrian crystalline basement of Kerala, affected by various tectonic events, hosts these prominent structural discontinuities.
