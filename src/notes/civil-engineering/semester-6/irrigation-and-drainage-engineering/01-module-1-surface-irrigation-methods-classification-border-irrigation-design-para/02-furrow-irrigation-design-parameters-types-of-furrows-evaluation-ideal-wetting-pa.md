# IRRIGATION AND DRAINAGE ENGINEERING

## Module 1: Surface Irrigation Methods

### Topic: Furrow Irrigation

---

### 1. Introduction to Furrow Irrigation

Furrow irrigation is a method of surface irrigation where water is applied to the soil surface in small, parallel channels (furrows) dug between crop rows. Water flows down these furrows by gravity, infiltrating into the soil and reaching the plant roots. It is one of the most widely used irrigation methods globally, especially in areas with relatively flat topography and where water is readily available.

---

### 2. Classification of Surface Irrigation Methods

While this topic focuses on furrow irrigation, it's important to understand its place within the broader classification of surface irrigation:

*   **Basin Irrigation:** Land is enclosed by levees, and water is applied to flood the entire area.
*   **Border Irrigation:** Land is divided into long, narrow strips (borders) separated by levees. Water is applied at the upper end and flows down the length of the border.
*   **Furrow Irrigation:** Water is applied in small channels between crop rows.
*   **Contour Irrigation:** Irrigation is done along contour lines to reduce runoff and erosion on sloping lands.

---

### 3. Types of Furrows

The design and configuration of furrows can vary depending on the crop, soil type, and topography.

*   **On-Century Furrows:**
    *   **Description:** Furrows are laid out parallel to the direction of the slope of the field.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple to construct, efficient for moderate slopes.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can lead to erosion on steep slopes due to high water velocity. Requires careful flow control.
    *   **Example:** Commonly used in many agricultural regions with consistent slopes.

*   **Contour Furrows:**
    *   **Description:** Furrows are laid out along contour lines, meaning they are constructed perpendicular to the general slope.
    *   **Advantages:** Significantly reduces erosion by slowing down water flow. Suitable for steeper slopes.
    *   **Disadvantages:** More complex to lay out and maintain. Can lead to uneven water distribution if contours are not precisely followed.
    *   **Example:** Used in hilly or mountainous regions to prevent soil loss.

*   **Skimming Furrows:**
    *   **Description:** A variation where the furrow is not dug deep but rather a shallow channel is created along the base of the plant.
    *   **Advantages:** Minimizes soil disturbance, suitable for crops with shallow root systems.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Limited water holding capacity.
    *   **Example:** Often used for vegetables or young plants.

*   **Broad-Base Furrows:**
    *   **Description:** Wider and shallower than conventional furrows, often used in conjunction with specific tillage practices.
    *   **Advantages:** Can accommodate farm machinery more easily, less prone to collapse.
    *   **Disadvantages:** May have less soil contact per unit length for infiltration.
    *   **Example:** May be found in regions practicing conservation tillage.

### 4. Design Parameters for Furrow Irrigation

Designing an efficient furrow irrigation system involves considering several key parameters:

*   **Furrow Dimensions:**
    *   **Width:** Depends on the spacing of crop rows and the type of equipment used for cultivation. Typically ranges from 0.15 m to 0.5 m.
    *   **Depth:** Influences the volume of water the furrow can hold and the infiltration rate. Typically ranges from 0.1 m to 0.3 m.
    *   **Cross-sectional Area:** Calculated as the area of the water-filled portion of the furrow. Affects the flow capacity.

*   **Furrow Length:**
    *   **Description:** The length of the furrow from the water source to the tail end.
    *   **Impact:** Longer furrows can lead to uneven water distribution (over-irrigation at the head and under-irrigation at the tail).
    *   **Guideline:** Typically ranges from 60 m to 400 m, depending on soil type, slope, and flow rate. Sandy soils require shorter furrows than clay soils.

*   **Furrow Spacing:**
    *   **Description:** The distance between the centers of adjacent furrows. Usually dictated by crop row spacing.
    *   **Impact:** Affects the wetted area per unit length of furrow and the uniformity of irrigation. Too wide spacing can lead to dry zones between furrows.

*   **Furrow Slope:**
    *   **Description:** The gradient along the length of the furrow.
    *   **Impact:** Affects the velocity of water flow and infiltration.
        *   **Too steep:** High velocity, erosion, less infiltration time at the head.
        *   **Too flat:** Slow flow, ponding, potential waterlogging, poor advance.
    *   **Optimal Range:** Generally between 0.2% and 0.8% (2 to 8 m drop per 1000 m). Can be adjusted based on soil texture. Sandy soils can tolerate steeper slopes than clay soils.

*   **Infiltration Rate:**
    *   **Description:** The rate at which water enters the soil from the furrow.
    *   **Factors:** Soil texture, soil structure, antecedent moisture content, water depth in furrow.
    *   **Importance:** Crucial for determining the required irrigation time and depth.

*   **Flow Rate per Furrow:**
    *   **Description:** The volume of water supplied to each furrow per unit time.
    *   **Impact:** Directly influences the speed of water advance down the furrow and the total amount of water delivered.
    *   **Guideline:** Typically ranges from 0.5 L/s to 4 L/s (or 1 to 10 gallons per minute) per furrow, depending on furrow dimensions, soil type, and furrow length.

*   **Irrigation Depth/Amount:**
    *   **Description:** The total depth of water to be applied to the field to meet crop water requirements.
    *   **Calculation:** Based on evapotranspiration (ETc), effective rainfall, and soil moisture depletion.

*   **Irrigation Time (Duration):**
    *   **Description:** The period for which water is supplied to the furrows.
    *   **Determination:** Calculated based on the required irrigation depth, infiltration rate, and the wetted perimeter.

*   **Application Efficiency:**
    *   **Description:** The ratio of the water beneficially used by the crop to the total water applied.
    *   **Goal:** Maximize application efficiency to minimize water waste and improve uniformity.

### 5. Evaluation of Furrow Irrigation

Evaluating a furrow irrigation system helps identify inefficiencies and areas for improvement. Key evaluation parameters include:

*   **Advance Time:**
    *   **Definition:** The time it takes for the water to reach the end of the furrow.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured at various points along the furrow.
    *   **Significance:** A good indicator of flow rate and furrow slope. Uniform advance times suggest good flow control.

*   **Intake Opportunity Time (IOT):**
    *   **Definition:** The duration for which any given point on the furrow is exposed to flowing water.
    *   **Measurement:** Varies along the furrow. At the head end, it's equal to the irrigation duration. At the tail end, it's equal to the advance time.
    *   **Significance:** Directly affects the amount of water infiltrated.

*   **Runoff:**
    *   **Definition:** Water that flows off the end of the field without infiltrating.
    *   **Measurement:** Collected and measured.
    *   **Significance:** Represents water loss and reduces application efficiency. Can be managed by tailwater return systems or cutting off water supply before the end is reached.

*   **Deep Percolation:**
    *   **Definition:** Water that infiltrates below the root zone.
    *   **Measurement:** Difficult to measure directly but inferred from soil moisture monitoring below the root zone.
    *   **Significance:** Represents water loss and reduces irrigation efficiency.

*   **Uniformity:**
    *   **Distribution Uniformity (DU):** The ratio of the average depth of water infiltrated in the lowest 25% of the area to the average depth of water infiltrated over the entire area.
        *   **Formula:** DU = (Average depth in lowest 25%) / (Average depth over entire area) * 100%
    *   **Application Uniformity (AU):** The ratio of the average depth of water infiltrated in the lowest 80% of the area to the average depth of water infiltrated over the entire area.
        *   **Formula:** AU = (Average depth in lowest 80%) / (Average depth over entire area) * 100%
    *   **Coefficients of Uniformity (e.g., Christansen's CU):** Various statistical measures of uniformity.
    *   **Significance:** High uniformity ensures that all parts of the field receive adequate water without over-watering other areas.

*   **Application Efficiency (AE):**
    *   **Definition:** AE = (Average depth of water stored in root zone) / (Average depth of water applied) * 100%
    *   **Significance:** Overall measure of how effectively applied water is stored for crop use.

**Methods of Evaluation:**

1.  **Water Balance Method:**
    *   Measure inflow, outflow (runoff), and changes in soil moisture storage.
    *   $AE = \frac{\Delta S + ET_{crop}}{Inflow} \times 100\%$
        *   $\Delta S$: Change in soil water storage in the root zone.
        *   $ET_{crop}$: Evapotranspiration during the irrigation interval.

2.  **Advance and Recession Rate Measurements:**
    *   Measure advance time along the furrow.
    *   Measure recession time (time for water to disappear from the furrow).
    *   Use these to estimate infiltration and distribution.

3.  **Soil Moisture Monitoring:**
    *   Using tensiometers, gypsum blocks, or neutron probes to measure soil moisture content at different depths and locations.

### 6. Ideal Wetting Pattern in Furrow Irrigation

The goal of furrow irrigation is to achieve a uniform wetting pattern within the root zone of the crop, minimizing losses due to deep percolation and surface runoff.

*   **Desired Pattern:**
    *   **Uniformity across furrows:** Water should infiltrate into the soil and move laterally to wet the root zone of adjacent plants between furrows.
    *   **Adequate depth:** Water should infiltrate to the desired root zone depth (e.g., 60-90 cm for deep-rooted crops).
    *   **No ponding:** Water should infiltrate relatively quickly, avoiding prolonged ponding which can lead to waterlogging.
    *   **No excessive runoff:** Minimize water flowing off the field.
    *   **No dry zones:** Especially at the tail end of the furrow and between furrows.

*   **Factors Influencing the Wetting Pattern:**
    *   **Furrow geometry and spacing:** Tighter spacing and appropriate dimensions promote better lateral wetting.
    *   **Furrow slope:** Affects water velocity and infiltration time.
    *   **Flow rate:** Higher flow rates lead to faster advance but can increase runoff and deep percolation at the head.
    *   **Infiltration characteristics of the soil:** Sandy soils infiltrate faster, requiring shorter furrows and potentially higher flow rates, while clay soils infiltrate slower.
    *   **Irrigation duration:** Needs to be sufficient to deliver the required water depth to the tail end.
    *   **Water application method:** Continuous flow versus intermittent flow.

*   **Achieving an Ideal Pattern:**
    *   **Proper furrow design:** Optimizing dimensions, spacing, and slope.
    *   **Accurate flow control:** Using appropriate gates, siphons, or flow dividers.
    *   **Tailwater management:** Implementing tailwater return systems or surge irrigation.
    *   **Surge Irrigation:** Applying water in pulses (surges) can improve uniformity by allowing infiltration time between surges, reducing deep percolation at the head and improving advance to the tail.

### 7. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What are the primary advantages of contour furrows over on-century furrows on steep slopes?

**Answer:** Contour furrows are laid out perpendicular to the slope, slowing down water velocity and significantly reducing the risk of soil erosion compared to on-century furrows, which follow the slope and can lead to high water velocities and scouring.

---

**Question 2:** You are designing a furrow irrigation system for a sandy loam soil with a uniform slope of 0.5%. You need to deliver 50 mm of water to the root zone. If the infiltration rate is approximately 10 mm/hr and the furrow length is 200 m, what is the approximate advance time and required irrigation duration? (Assume a typical furrow dimension and flow rate for estimation purposes).

**Answer:**
*   **Required water depth:** 50 mm
*   **Infiltration rate:** 10 mm/hr
*   **Advance time:** This is complex to calculate without detailed hydraulic models. However, for sandy loam soils and a 0.5% slope, advance times for 200m furrows are typically in the range of 1-3 hours. Let's assume an average advance time of **2 hours**.
*   **Irrigation duration:** To ensure the tail end receives the required depth, the irrigation should continue until the water reaches the end and has had enough time to infiltrate. A common practice is to apply water for a duration slightly longer than the advance time to allow for infiltration at the tail. So, the irrigation duration would be approximately **2.5 to 3 hours**.
*   **Note:** A precise calculation would require knowing the furrow's hydraulic radius, Manning's roughness coefficient, and the flow rate per furrow to determine the velocity and hence advance time using Manning's equation.

---

**Question 3:** Define Distribution Uniformity (DU) and explain its importance in furrow irrigation.

**Answer:**
*   **Definition:** Distribution Uniformity (DU) is a measure of how evenly water is distributed across the irrigated area. It is calculated as the ratio of the average depth of water infiltrated in the lowest 25% of the area to the average depth of water infiltrated over the entire area, expressed as a percentage.
*   **Importance:** High DU is crucial for efficient furrow irrigation because it ensures that all parts of the field receive adequate water. Low DU indicates that some areas are receiving too much water (leading to deep percolation losses and potential waterlogging) while others are receiving too little (leading to reduced crop yield and water stress). Achieving good DU minimizes water waste and maximizes crop performance.

---

**Question 4:** What is surge irrigation, and how can it improve furrow irrigation performance?

**Answer:**
*   **Surge irrigation** is a method where water is applied to furrows in a series of short, intermittent pulses or "surges" rather than a continuous flow.
*   **Improvement:**
    *   **Improved advance:** Each surge moves further down the furrow than a continuous flow of the same volume, allowing water to reach the tail end more effectively.
    *   **Reduced deep percolation at the head:** The "off" phase of the surge allows time for infiltration, reducing the amount of water lost below the root zone at the upstream end.
    *   **Reduced runoff:** By alternating "on" and "off" periods, the total infiltration opportunity is increased, leading to less tailwater runoff.
    *   **Enhanced uniformity:** The combination of better advance and reduced losses generally leads to a more uniform distribution of water along the furrow.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Furrow irrigation is gravity-driven.** Its efficiency is heavily reliant on proper field leveling and furrow design.
*   **Soil type is critical:** Sandy soils require shorter furrows, steeper slopes (with caution for erosion), and potentially higher flow rates than clay soils.
*   **Slope management is key:** Aim for slopes that balance flow velocity (to ensure advance) with infiltration time (to maximize water uptake and minimize erosion).
*   **Uniformity is the goal:** Strive for high Distribution Uniformity (DU) and Application Uniformity (AU) to ensure all plants receive adequate water.
*   **Water management:** Proper flow rate control, irrigation duration, and tailwater management are essential for efficient furrow irrigation.
*   **Surge irrigation** is a valuable technique to overcome some of the inherent limitations of continuous furrow irrigation.
*   **Regular evaluation** of the system is necessary to identify and correct inefficiencies.
