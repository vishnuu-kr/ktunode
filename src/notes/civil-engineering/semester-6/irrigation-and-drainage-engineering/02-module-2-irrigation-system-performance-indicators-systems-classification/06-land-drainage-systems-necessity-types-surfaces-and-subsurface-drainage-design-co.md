# Irrigation and Drainage Engineering: Module 2 - Irrigation System Performance Indicators

## Topic: Land Drainage Systems

---

### 1. Necessity of Land Drainage

**Learning Outcome:** Understand the necessity and importance of land drainage for efficient agricultural production and environmental sustainability.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Waterlogging:** The condition where the soil pores are saturated with water, leaving little or no air space for plant roots. This leads to oxygen deficiency and root asphyxiation.
*   **Salinization:** The accumulation of soluble salts in the soil to levels that inhibit plant growth. This often occurs in arid and semi-arid regions with poor drainage.
*   **Soil Aeration:** The process of exchanging gases (oxygen and carbon dioxide) between the soil and the atmosphere, crucial for root respiration and microbial activity.
*   **Root Zone:** The portion of the soil that contains plant roots and is actively involved in water and nutrient uptake.
*   **Soilborne Diseases:** Fungal and bacterial diseases that thrive in waterlogged and anaerobic soil conditions.

**Why is Drainage Necessary?**

*   **Prevents Waterlogging:**
    *   **Impact:** Waterlogged soils reduce oxygen availability to plant roots, hindering respiration and nutrient uptake. This leads to stunted growth, yellowing of leaves, and ultimately plant death.
    *   **Example:** Paddy fields require a certain level of water, but prolonged flooding or inefficient drainage in other crops can cause severe damage.
*   **Controls Salinity:**
    *   **Impact:** In irrigated arid and semi-arid regions, poor drainage causes the water table to rise. As water evaporates from the soil surface, dissolved salts are left behind, accumulating in the root zone and becoming toxic to most crops.
    *   **Example:** In many parts of the Indus River basin, over-irrigation and inadequate drainage have led to widespread salinization, rendering large areas of fertile land unproductive.
*   **Improves Soil Aeration:**
    *   **Impact:** Adequate drainage removes excess water, allowing air to enter the soil pores. This is vital for the aerobic respiration of plant roots and beneficial soil microorganisms.
    *   **Example:** Well-drained soils support robust root systems and promote the decomposition of organic matter, releasing nutrients for plant uptake.
*   **Reduces Soilborne Diseases:**
    *   **Impact:** Anaerobic conditions in waterlogged soils favor the proliferation of certain fungal and bacterial pathogens that cause root rot and other diseases.
    *   **Example:** Diseases like "damping-off" in seedlings are often exacerbated by poorly drained nursery beds.
*   **Enhances Trafficability:**
    *   **Impact:** Wet soils are soft and unstable, making it difficult to operate agricultural machinery (tractors, harvesters) and leading to soil compaction.
    *   **Example:** Attempting to plow a waterlogged field can result in machinery getting stuck and causing severe soil structure damage.
*   **Facilitates Fertilization and Cultivation:**
    *   **Impact:** Drainage allows for timely application of fertilizers and permits field operations like plowing, planting, and harvesting to be carried out efficiently.
*   **Improves Soil Temperature:**
    *   **Impact:** Drained soils warm up faster in spring, allowing for earlier planting and a longer growing season. Water has a high specific heat capacity, meaning it takes longer to heat up than soil.

**Important Point to Remember:** Effective drainage is as crucial as irrigation for achieving optimal crop yields and maintaining soil health.

### 2. Types of Land Drainage Systems

**Learning Outcome:** Differentiate between surface and subsurface drainage systems and understand their respective components and applications.

**Classification of Drainage Systems:**

Drainage systems are broadly classified into two main categories:

1.  **Surface Drainage Systems:** Remove excess water from the soil surface.
2.  **Subsurface Drainage Systems:** Remove excess water from within the soil profile.

---

#### 2.1. Surface Drainage Systems

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Surface Runoff:** Water that flows over the land surface.
*   **Infiltration:** The process of water seeping into the soil.
*   **Hydraulic Gradient:** The slope of the water table or a water surface.
*   **Gravity Flow:** The movement of water due to the force of gravity, facilitated by a downward slope.

**Types of Surface Drainage:**

*   **Open Ditches/Channels:**
    *   **Description:** These are excavated channels that collect and convey surface runoff away from the fields. They can vary in size from small field ditches to large main drains.
    *   **Components:**
        *   **Field Ditches:** Smaller, shallower ditches within fields, often parallel to crop rows.
        *   **Conductor Ditches:** Larger ditches that collect water from field ditches and convey it to the main drain.
        *   **Main Drains:** The largest channels that discharge water into natural water bodies (rivers, lakes) or outfall structures.
    *   **Advantages:** Relatively simple to construct and maintain, effective for areas with mild slopes, can serve dual purpose of conveying drainage water and irrigation water.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Occupy valuable land area, can interfere with farm operations (plowing, harvesting), prone to siltation and weed growth, can be a hazard for livestock.
    *   **Example:** Contour ditches on sloping land to intercept surface runoff and prevent erosion. Parallel ditches in flat, low-lying areas to collect water.

*   **Field Surface Smoothing/Grading:**
    *   **Description:** This involves manipulating the land surface to create a smooth, gentle slope towards drainage outlets (ditches or collection points).
    *   **Methods:**
        *   **Laser Grading:** Using laser-controlled equipment to precisely level or grade the land.
        *   **Land Planing:** Using a land plane or grader to smooth out unevenness.
        *   **Broadbed-and-furrow Systems:** Creating raised beds for crops with furrows between them to collect and drain excess surface water.
    *   **Advantages:** Improves surface runoff efficiency, reduces waterlogging on the surface, can be integrated with irrigation.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires specialized equipment, can be labor-intensive if done manually, effectiveness depends on the intensity of rainfall.
    *   **Example:** In rice cultivation, creating bunds and smooth fields for controlled flooding. In row crops, using laser leveling to ensure even water distribution and drainage.

*   **Mole Drainage:**
    *   **Description:** Creating temporary, unlined channels (sounding channels) in the subsoil by pulling a bullet-shaped implement (mole plow) through the soil at a specific depth. These channels act as conduits for subsurface water to reach open ditches or other drains.
    *   **Advantages:** Relatively inexpensive, no material cost, does not occupy surface land, effective in clayey soils with good structure.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Temporary (lasts 3-7 years), only effective in cohesive soils (clays and clay loams), requires suitable soil conditions and proper installation depth.
    *   **Example:** Used in agricultural areas with clay soils to quickly remove excess water after heavy rainfall.

*   **Surface-Set Drainage (e.g., French Drains):**
    *   **Description:** Shallow trenches filled with gravel or coarse aggregate, sometimes with a perforated pipe at the bottom. These are typically used for localized drainage problems or along structures.
    *   **Advantages:** Effective for localized surface water problems.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can be labor-intensive, cost of aggregate, capacity is limited.

**Important Point to Remember:** Surface drainage is most effective in areas with moderate to high rainfall intensity and where surface water accumulation is the primary issue.

#### 2.2. Subsurface Drainage Systems

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Water Table:** The upper surface of the saturated zone in the soil.
*   **Seepage:** The slow movement of water through the soil.
*   **Drainage Coefficient:** The rate at which excess water needs to be removed from the soil, typically expressed in mm/day or cm/day.
*   **Draintile/Drainpipe:** Perforated pipes installed underground to collect and convey excess soil water.
*   **Envelope Material/Filter:** Coarse material (gravel, synthetic filter fabric) surrounding the drainpipe to prevent soil particles from entering and clogging the perforations.

**Types of Subsurface Drainage:**

*   **Tile Drainage (Perforated Pipe Drainage):**
    *   **Description:** A network of perforated pipes (traditionally clay tiles, now mostly plastic pipes like PVC or corrugated plastic) installed underground at a specific depth and spacing. These pipes collect water that infiltrates from the soil surface or seeps from deeper layers.
    *   **Components:**
        *   **Main Drains:** Larger diameter pipes that collect water from lateral drains.
        *   **Lateral Drains:** Smaller diameter pipes laid parallel to each other across the field.
        *   **Submains:** Pipes that connect laterals to mains.
        *   **Outlets:** Structures that discharge the collected water into ditches or natural watercourses.
        *   **Catch Basins/Manholes:** Structures placed at junctions of drains or at intervals to allow access for inspection and cleaning.
    *   **Advantages:** Highly effective in lowering the water table, improves soil aeration and root zone conditions, does not interfere with surface operations, long lifespan if installed properly.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High initial installation cost, requires careful design and installation, potential for clogging if filters are not used or if pipes are damaged.
    *   **Example:** Commonly used in agricultural regions with shallow water tables and high infiltration rates to improve crop productivity.

*   **Drainage Trenches (Gravel-Filled Drains):**
    *   **Description:** Trenches excavated and filled with gravel or other permeable material. Perforated pipes can be placed at the bottom of these trenches for better performance.
    *   **Advantages:** Can provide a larger drainage cross-section compared to just pipes, can be used in soils where trench sidewalls might collapse.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher cost due to the use of gravel, more labor-intensive.

*   **Blind Drains (French Drains):**
    *   **Description:** Similar to drainage trenches but without a perforated pipe. They are simply trenches filled with gravel or other permeable material, designed to intercept groundwater seepage.
    *   **Advantages:** Useful for intercepting seepage from hillsides or leaky irrigation canals.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Less efficient than pipe drainage for removing large volumes of water, prone to clogging over time if fine soil particles migrate into the gravel.

*   **Vertical Drainage (Well Drainage):**
    *   **Description:** Installation of wells that pump out excess groundwater. This is particularly useful in areas where the water table needs to be lowered over a large area or where it's not feasible to install extensive subsurface drainage networks.
    *   **Advantages:** Can effectively lower the water table over a large radius, useful in special situations.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High operational cost (pumping energy), requires a suitable aquifer, can cause land subsidence if groundwater is excessively withdrawn.

**Important Point to Remember:** Subsurface drainage is the most effective method for controlling deep waterlogging and salinity issues, leading to significant improvements in crop yields and soil health.

### 3. Design Considerations for Land Drainage Systems

**Learning Outcome:** Identify and explain the key factors influencing the design of land drainage systems, including the selection of appropriate system type and components.

**Key Design Factors:**

*   **Drainage Requirements (Drainage Coefficient):**
    *   **Description:** The amount of water that needs to be removed per unit area per unit time. This is a critical parameter for sizing the drainage network.
    *   **Factors Influencing Drainage Coefficient:**
        *   **Crop Tolerance to Waterlogging:** Different crops have varying tolerances to excess moisture.
        *   **Rainfall Intensity and Distribution:** High rainfall intensity necessitates a higher drainage rate.
        *   **Irrigation Practices:** Excessive irrigation can increase drainage needs.
        *   **Soil Permeability:** Soils with lower permeability require more extensive drainage to remove water efficiently.
        *   **Topography:** Sloping land may require different drainage strategies compared to flat land.
    *   **Determination:** Can be based on empirical data, rainfall data analysis, or specific crop requirements.

*   **Soil Characteristics:**
    *   **Description:** The physical properties of the soil significantly influence drainage design.
    *   **Key Properties:**
        *   **Permeability/Hydraulic Conductivity:** High permeability soils drain faster and may require wider spacing of subsurface drains. Low permeability soils require closer spacing or different drainage methods.
        *   **Texture and Structure:** Clay soils retain more water and are prone to compaction, requiring careful design of mole drains or closer spacing of tiles. Sandy soils drain quickly but may require filters to prevent fine particle migration into drains.
        *   **Presence of Hardpans:** Impermeable layers can impede vertical drainage and necessitate shallower drains or different installation methods.

*   **Topography and Slope:**
    *   **Description:** The land's shape and slope determine the type of drainage system and the layout of the drains.
    *   **Impact:**
        *   **Sloping Land:** Surface drainage methods (ditches, grading) and interceptor drains are often used to control surface runoff and seepage from higher areas.
        *   **Flat Land:** Subsurface drainage networks are typically required to lower the water table and remove accumulated surface water. The slope of the drains themselves is crucial for gravity flow.

*   **Depth and Spacing of Drains (Subsurface Drainage):**
    *   **Description:** These are crucial for determining the effectiveness and cost of subsurface drainage.
    *   **Factors Influencing Depth:**
        *   **Root Zone Depth:** Drains should be deep enough to adequately lower the water table below the critical root zone depth.
        *   **Soil Permeability:** Deeper drains can effectively drain a wider area in permeable soils.
        *   **Drainage Objective:** Lowering the water table for salinity control might require deeper drains than for removing surface water.
    *   **Factors Influencing Spacing:**
        *   **Soil Permeability:** Closer spacing is needed in less permeable soils.
        *   **Drainage Coefficient:** Higher drainage requirements necessitate closer spacing.
        *   **Depth of Drains:** Shallower drains generally need closer spacing.
        *   **Crop Rooting Depth:** Spacing should ensure the water table is controlled within the root zone.
    *   **Design Methods:** Various formulas and graphical methods (e.g., Djebaili's formula, Luthin's method) are used to calculate optimum spacing based on soil properties and drainage requirements.

*   **Drainage Outlet:**
    *   **Description:** The point where the drainage system discharges collected water.
    *   **Considerations:**
        *   **Elevation:** The outlet must be at a lower elevation than the drains to allow gravity flow.
        *   **Capacity:** The outlet must be able to handle the peak flow from the drainage system without backing up.
        *   **Environmental Regulations:** Discharge of drainage water must comply with environmental regulations to prevent pollution of receiving water bodies.
        *   **Non-return Valves:** May be required at outlets to prevent backflow from the receiving water body during high flood stages.

*   **Envelope/Filter Material:**
    *   **Description:** Used to prevent soil particles from entering and clogging the perforated drainpipes.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Gravel Envelope:** Coarse gravel placed around the drainpipe.
        *   **Synthetic Filter Fabric (Geotextiles):** Permeable fabrics that allow water to pass but prevent fine soil particles from entering the drain.
    *   **Selection Criteria:** Depends on soil texture, permeability, and the availability of materials. Geotextiles are often preferred due to their ease of installation and effectiveness in a wide range of soil conditions.

*   **Materials and Installation:**
    *   **Description:** The type of material used for pipes and the quality of installation are critical for the long-term performance of the drainage system.
    *   **Considerations:**
        *   **Pipe Material:** Durability, strength, cost (PVC, corrugated plastic, clay tiles).
        *   **Pipe Diameter:** Determined by the design flow rate.
        *   **Installation Precision:** Proper slope, depth, and alignment are essential.
        *   **Protection of Perforations:** Ensuring perforations are not blocked during installation.

*   **Economic Feasibility:**
    *   **Description:** The cost of the drainage system (installation and maintenance) must be justified by the expected increase in crop yield and other benefits.
    *   **Analysis:** Cost-benefit analysis is crucial to determine the economic viability of a drainage project.

**Important Point to Remember:** A well-designed drainage system is a compromise between effectiveness, cost, and long-term performance. Accurate assessment of site conditions and drainage requirements is paramount.

### 4. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Explain why land drainage is considered as important as irrigation for agricultural productivity.

**Answer:** Land drainage is crucial because it prevents waterlogging, which suffocates plant roots by reducing soil aeration. It also helps in controlling soil salinization, which is a major threat to crop yields in irrigated arid and semi-arid regions. Furthermore, proper drainage improves soil structure, enhances trafficability for farm machinery, reduces soilborne diseases, and allows for timely field operations, all contributing to optimal crop growth and higher yields.

**Question 2:**
Differentiate between open ditches and tile drainage systems, listing two advantages and two disadvantages for each.

**Answer:**

*   **Open Ditches:**
    *   **Advantages:** Simple construction, effective for surface water.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Occupy land, interfere with farm operations.
*   **Tile Drainage:**
    *   **Advantages:** Highly effective for water table control, does not interfere with surface operations.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High initial cost, requires careful installation.

**Question 3:**
A farmer in an arid region is experiencing reduced crop yields due to increasing salt accumulation in the soil, despite regular irrigation. Which type of drainage system would you recommend and why? Discuss the key design considerations for this recommended system.

**Answer:** I would recommend a **subsurface drainage system**, specifically **tile drainage**.

**Reasoning:** The problem of salt accumulation in arid regions is typically caused by a rising water table. As irrigation water evaporates from the soil surface, it leaves behind dissolved salts. If the water table is close to the surface, these salts are drawn upwards and accumulate in the root zone, leading to salinity. Subsurface drainage effectively lowers the water table, allowing excess irrigation water to leach salts below the root zone and preventing their upward movement.

**Key Design Considerations for Tile Drainage:**

1.  **Drainage Coefficient:** Determined by the amount of irrigation water applied, rainfall, and the need for leaching salts.
2.  **Soil Characteristics:** Permeability of the soil will dictate the spacing and depth of drains. In arid regions, soils might be saline or sodic, influencing their physical properties.
3.  **Depth and Spacing:** Drains need to be deep enough to intercept the rising water table and spaced to effectively lower it across the field. The depth should be sufficient to keep the water table below the critical root zone depth of the crops.
4.  **Drainage Outlet:** A stable outlet at a lower elevation is essential for gravity flow.
5.  **Envelope/Filter Material:** Crucial to prevent fine soil particles from entering and clogging the pipes, especially if the soil is sandy or silty. Geotextiles are often preferred.
6.  **Economic Feasibility:** The cost of installation must be justified by the expected increase in crop yield and the long-term benefits of salinity control.

**Question 4:**
What is the purpose of mole drainage and in what type of soil is it most effective?

**Answer:** The purpose of mole drainage is to create temporary, unlined channels in the subsoil that act as conduits for excess water to drain from the soil profile to surface drains or collection points. It is most effective in **cohesive soils**, such as **clays and clay loams**, which have sufficient strength to maintain the shape of the mole channel for a reasonable period (typically 3-7 years). It is less effective in sandy soils, as the mole channels would quickly collapse.

**Question 5:**
Explain the role of filter material in subsurface drainage systems.

**Answer:** Filter material (such as gravel or synthetic filter fabric) is placed around the perforations of subsurface drainpipes. Its primary role is to prevent fine soil particles from entering the pipes and clogging them. This ensures the continuous flow of water into the drains and maintains the long-term effectiveness of the drainage system. By allowing water to pass through while blocking soil particles, the filter material protects the hydraulic efficiency of the drains.

### 5. Important Points to Remember (Summary)

*   **Waterlogging and Salinity:** The two primary problems addressed by land drainage.
*   **Surface vs. Subsurface:** Surface drainage manages water on the land's surface, while subsurface drainage manages water within the soil profile.
*   **Open Ditches:** Effective for surface runoff, but occupy land and can impede operations.
*   **Tile Drainage:** Highly effective for water table control and salinity management, but costly to install.
*   **Mole Drainage:** A cost-effective but temporary solution for clay soils.
*   **Key Design Factors:** Drainage requirements, soil characteristics, topography, drain depth and spacing, outlet availability, and filter materials are critical for effective drainage design.
*   **Economic Viability:** Drainage projects must be economically justified by improved crop yields and land productivity.
*   **Maintenance:** All drainage systems require some level of maintenance (e.g., cleaning ditches, flushing pipes) to ensure long-term performance.
