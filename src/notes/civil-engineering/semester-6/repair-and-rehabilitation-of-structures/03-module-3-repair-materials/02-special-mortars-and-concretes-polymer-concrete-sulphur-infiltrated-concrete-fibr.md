# REPAIR AND REHABILITATION OF STRUCTURES

## Module 3: Repair Materials

---

## Topic: Special Mortars and Concretes

This module delves into various specialized mortars and concretes that are crucial for the repair and rehabilitation of existing structures. These materials offer enhanced properties compared to conventional concrete, addressing specific challenges like strength, durability, permeability, and rapid repair needs.

---

### 1. Polymer Concrete

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Polymer concrete (PC) is a composite material where a polymer resin acts as the binder instead of Portland cement. It typically consists of aggregates (sand, gravel, crushed stone) and a polymer binder (resin).
*   **Components:**
    *   **Aggregates:** Standard aggregates are used.
    *   **Polymer Binder:** Thermosetting resins (e.g., epoxy, polyester, acrylic, vinyl ester) or thermoplastic resins are commonly employed. Thermosetting resins generally offer better performance.
    *   **Monomers/Curing Agents:** Initiators or catalysts are added to initiate the polymerization process.
*   **Mechanism:** The polymer resin infiltrates the void spaces between the aggregate particles and cures through polymerization, forming a strong, monolithic matrix.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **High Strength:** Significantly higher compressive and tensile strengths than conventional concrete.
*   **Excellent Chemical Resistance:** Highly resistant to acids, alkalis, salts, and solvents.
*   **Low Water Absorption & Permeability:** Makes it ideal for corrosive environments and water-retaining structures.
*   **Rapid Curing:** Can cure at ambient temperatures, allowing for faster repairs.
*   **Good Adhesion:** Bonds well to existing concrete substrates.
*   **Good Durability:** Resists freeze-thaw cycles and abrasion.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Higher Cost:** Polymer binders are generally more expensive than cement.
*   **Limited Temperature Resistance:** Some polymers can degrade at high temperatures.
*   **Health & Safety Concerns:** Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) released during curing require proper ventilation.
*   **Shrinkage:** Some polymer systems can exhibit significant shrinkage during curing.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of industrial floors, chemical tanks, and pipelines.
*   Bridge deck overlays.
*   Repair of precast elements.
*   Encapsulation of damaged rebar.

**Example:** Epoxy-based polymer concrete is often used for repairing spalled concrete surfaces in industrial facilities due to its rapid strength gain and excellent chemical resistance.

### 2. Sulphur Infiltrated Concrete (SIC)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Sulphur concrete is a type of concrete where molten sulphur is used as a binder, replacing cement. It typically consists of aggregates and molten sulphur.
*   **Mechanism:** Aggregates are heated to remove moisture, and then molten sulphur is poured over them. As the sulphur cools and solidifies, it binds the aggregates together.
*   **Modified Versions:** Often, aggregates are first treated to create a porous concrete structure (using cement and water), which is then infiltrated with molten sulphur to fill the pores and enhance properties.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **High Early Strength:** Achieves very high strength rapidly as it cools.
*   **Excellent Resistance to Acids:** Highly resistant to many corrosive chemicals.
*   **Low Water Absorption:** Very impermeable.
*   **Non-Combustible:** Sulphur is non-combustible.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Low Temperature Resistance:** Sulphur can soften and flow at elevated temperatures (around 70-80°C).
*   **Brittleness:** Can be more brittle than Portland cement concrete.
*   **Health Hazards:** Handling molten sulphur requires extreme care due to its fumes and high temperature.
*   **Expansion on Solidification:** Sulphur expands slightly upon solidification, which can induce stresses.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of structures exposed to aggressive chemical environments, particularly acids.
*   Patching and repair of industrial floors and containment structures.

**Example:** Sulphur concrete can be used to repair concrete spillways in chemical plants where constant acid attack is a problem.

### 3. Fibre Reinforced Concrete (FRC)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Fibre reinforced concrete is a composite material in which discontinuous, discrete fibres are added to concrete to improve its mechanical properties.
*   **Types of Fibres:**
    *   **Steel Fibres:** High tensile strength, good impact resistance, crack arrestors.
    *   **Polymer (Plastic) Fibres:** Polypropylene, nylon, PVA. Good crack control, lightweight, corrosion resistant.
    *   **Glass Fibres:** Alkali-resistant glass (AR-glass) is preferred to prevent degradation by cement alkalis. Good tensile strength.
    *   **Natural Fibres:** Jute, sisal, coir. Eco-friendly, biodegradable, but lower strength and durability.
    *   **Basalt Fibres:** High tensile strength, good chemical resistance.
*   **Mechanism:** Fibres bridge the cracks that form in the concrete matrix, increasing toughness, ductility, impact resistance, and crack control. They improve the post-cracking behaviour of concrete.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Increased Toughness & Ductility:** Significantly improves the ability to absorb energy and deform without fracturing.
*   **Improved Crack Control:** Reduces the width and propagation of cracks.
*   **Enhanced Impact and Abrasion Resistance:** Better performance under impact loads and abrasive wear.
*   **Reduced Shrinkage Cracking:** Helps to mitigate drying shrinkage cracks.
*   **Improved Flexural Strength:** Can enhance the bending strength of concrete.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Workability Issues:** Can reduce workability if not properly proportioned.
*   **Fibre Balling:** Fibres can clump together (balling) during mixing, leading to poor dispersion.
*   **Cost:** Fibres add to the material cost.
*   **Corrosion (for some fibres):** Steel fibres can corrode if exposed.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Overlaying bridge decks to resist cracking from traffic loads.
*   Repair of industrial floors subject to heavy traffic and impact.
*   Shotcrete applications for tunnel linings and slope stabilization.
*   Repair of spalled areas to prevent further deterioration.

**Example:** Adding steel fibres to shotcrete used for repairing tunnel linings improves its resistance to rockfall impact and cracking.

### 4. High Strength Concrete (HSC)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** High strength concrete is concrete with a compressive strength typically exceeding 50-60 MPa (7-9 ksi). Some definitions go up to 100 MPa.
*   **Key Ingredients:**
    *   **Low Water-Binder Ratio (w/b):** Crucial for achieving high strength. Typically 0.30 to 0.40.
    *   **High Cement Content:** Usually higher than conventional concrete.
    *   **Supplementary Cementitious Materials (SCMs):** Fly ash, silica fume, ground granulated blast furnace slag (GGBFS) are essential for improving particle packing and pozzolanic reactions. Silica fume is particularly effective.
    *   **Superplasticizers:** Required to achieve adequate workability at low w/b ratios.
    *   **Well-Graded Aggregates:** Carefully selected aggregates for optimal packing.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **High Compressive Strength:** Allows for smaller structural elements and increased load-carrying capacity.
*   **Improved Durability:** Generally more resistant to chemical attack and abrasion due to low permeability.
*   **Reduced Permeability:** Lower water/cementitious material ratio leads to denser concrete.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Brittleness:** Can be more brittle than normal strength concrete.
*   **Shrinkage:** Higher cement content can lead to increased drying shrinkage.
*   **Higher Cost:** Due to specialized materials and mix design.
*   **Heat of Hydration:** Higher cement content can generate more heat during hydration, potentially causing thermal cracking.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repairing structural members requiring significant strength enhancement.
*   Patching heavily loaded areas.
*   Columns and beams in high-rise buildings requiring increased load capacity.

**Example:** Using HSC to repair a weakened concrete column in a building to restore its load-bearing capacity.

### 5. High Performance Concrete (HPC)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** High performance concrete is concrete that meets specific performance requirements beyond just strength. These requirements can include enhanced durability, reduced permeability, resistance to aggressive environments, and specific rheological properties. HPC is not solely defined by strength but by its overall performance characteristics.
*   **Key Ingredients & Design:** Similar to HSC, but with a focus on achieving specific performance criteria through:
    *   Careful selection of materials (low w/b ratio, SCMs, superplasticizers).
    *   Optimized mix design for specific properties like resistance to chloride ingress, freeze-thaw cycles, or abrasion.
    *   Often includes a higher proportion of SCMs like silica fume and fly ash.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Exceptional Durability:** Highly resistant to environmental degradation (e.g., sulfates, chlorides, carbonation).
*   **Low Permeability:** Excellent barrier properties against ingress of harmful substances.
*   **High Strength (often):** While not the sole criterion, HPC often exhibits high strength.
*   **Resistance to Chemical Attack:** Can be designed to withstand specific chemical exposures.
*   **Reduced Permeability to Gases:** Important for certain applications.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Higher Cost:** Due to specialized materials and rigorous quality control.
*   **Complexity of Mix Design:** Requires specialized knowledge and testing.
*   **Potential for Brittleness:** Similar to HSC, can exhibit brittleness.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repairing structures in marine environments (bridges, offshore structures) to resist chloride attack.
*   Repairing concrete exposed to de-icing salts.
*   Overlays for bridge decks requiring excellent durability and resistance to wear.
*   Repair of dams and spillways exposed to water flow and abrasion.

**Example:** Using HPC with a very low w/b ratio and a high percentage of silica fume for a bridge deck overlay to prevent corrosion of reinforcing steel due to de-icing salts.

### 6. Vacuum Concrete

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Vacuum concrete is a process where excess water is removed from freshly placed concrete by applying a vacuum.
*   **Mechanism:** After placing and levelling the concrete, vacuum mats (porous fabric) are placed on the surface, and a vacuum pump is used to draw out a significant portion of the mix water.
*   **Purpose:** Primarily to reduce the water-cement ratio, thereby increasing strength and durability.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Increased Strength:** By removing excess water, the w/c ratio is effectively reduced, leading to higher strength.
*   **Improved Durability:** Reduced permeability and increased abrasion resistance.
*   **Faster Setting & Hardening:** Removal of water accelerates the hydration process.
*   **Reduced Shrinkage:** Less water means less drying shrinkage.
*   **Improved Surface Finish:** Produces a denser, smoother, and harder surface.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Cost of Equipment:** Vacuum pumps and mats can be expensive.
*   **Skilled Labour Required:** The process requires trained operators.
*   **Limited Thickness:** Primarily effective for thin sections or slabs.
*   **Potential for Surface Cracking:** If vacuum is applied too quickly or for too long, it can lead to surface cracking.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of industrial floors and pavements.
*   Construction of thin concrete elements.
*   Repair of precast concrete components.
*   Surface finishing and strengthening of existing concrete slabs.

**Example:** Using the vacuum dewatering process on a concrete repair layer for a bridge deck to achieve a dense, wear-resistant surface.

### 7. Self-Compacting Concrete (SCC)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Self-compacting concrete (also known as self-consolidating concrete) is a highly flowable, non-segregating concrete that can spread into formwork, fill every void, and encapsulate reinforcement under its own weight without the need for vibration.
*   **Key Ingredients:**
    *   **High Powder Content:** Increased proportion of cementitious materials (including SCMs like fly ash and slag) to improve paste consistency.
    *   **Viscosity Modifying Admixtures (VMAs):** To prevent segregation and maintain flow.
    *   **High Range Water Reducers (Superplasticizers):** To achieve high fluidity.
    *   **Carefully Selected Aggregates:** Fine-tuned aggregate gradation and shape.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Excellent Flowability:** Can flow into complex shapes and tight spaces.
*   **High Passing Ability:** Can flow around dense reinforcement without blockages.
*   **Good Segregation Resistance:** Remains cohesive during placement.
*   **Improved Surface Finish:** Typically results in a smoother, more aesthetically pleasing surface.
*   **Reduced Noise and Vibration:** Eliminates the need for mechanical vibration during placement.
*   **Faster Construction:** Speeds up the concreting process.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Higher Material Cost:** Due to the use of specialized admixtures.
*   **Sensitivity to Mix Design:** Requires precise control over mix proportions and material properties.
*   **Potential for Excessive Bleed:** If not properly designed, can experience bleed.
*   **Surface Defects:** Can be susceptible to surface defects like dusting if curing is inadequate.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repairing congested reinforcement areas in existing structures.
*   Grouting of voids and cracks.
*   Patching spalled concrete where access is limited.
*   Pouring concrete around intricate repairs.

**Example:** Using SCC to repair a damaged concrete beam with closely spaced reinforcing bars where conventional concrete would be difficult to place without voids.

### 8. Self-Healing Concrete

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Self-healing concrete is a type of concrete that possesses the ability to autonomously repair cracks that form within it, typically micro-cracks.
*   **Mechanisms:**
    *   **Autogenous Healing:** The unhydrated cement particles within the matrix react with water and CO2 entering the cracks, forming calcium carbonate and calcium silicate hydrate, which can fill narrow cracks.
    *   **Engineered Self-Healing:** Incorporation of specific healing agents:
        *   **Encapsulated Healing Agents:** Microcapsules or vascular networks containing polymers, epoxy, or mineral precursors that rupture upon cracking and fill the crack.
        *   **Bacteria-Based Healing:** Incorporating bacteria (e.g., *Bacillus* species) and a nutrient source (e.g., calcium lactate) within the concrete. When cracks form and water enters, the bacteria become active, consume the nutrient, and precipitate calcium carbonate, sealing the crack.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Extended Service Life:** Repairs micro-cracks, preventing their propagation and protecting the reinforcement from corrosion.
*   **Reduced Maintenance Costs:** Minimizes the need for manual inspection and repair.
*   **Improved Durability:** Enhanced resistance to water ingress and aggressive substances.
*   **Sustainability:** Potentially reduces the need for new material production.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Limited Crack Width Repair:** Primarily effective for micro-cracks (typically less than 0.5-1 mm).
*   **Cost:** The incorporation of healing agents can increase the material cost.
*   **Complexity of Implementation:** Requires careful integration of healing mechanisms into the concrete mix.
*   **Long-Term Efficacy:** The long-term viability of healing agents needs further research.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of critical structures where long-term durability and reduced maintenance are paramount (e.g., bridges, tunnels, dams).
*   Water-retaining structures to maintain impermeability.
*   Structures in aggressive environments.

**Example:** Using bacteria-based self-healing concrete in a wastewater treatment plant structure to continuously seal any micro-cracks that form, preventing leakage and chemical ingress.

### 9. Geopolymer Concrete

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Geopolymer concrete is an inorganic binder that uses aluminosilicate source materials activated by alkaline silicate solutions. It is an alternative to Portland cement concrete.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Aluminosilicate Source Materials:** Industrial by-products like fly ash, GGBFS, metakaolin, or natural pozzolans.
    *   **Alkaline Activators:** Typically a combination of alkali hydroxides (e.g., NaOH) and alkali silicates (e.g., sodium silicate or potassium silicate).
*   **Mechanism:** The alkaline activators dissolve the aluminosilicate precursors, leading to a polycondensation reaction that forms a three-dimensional polymeric network (geopolymer binder).

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Low Carbon Footprint:** Significantly lower CO2 emissions compared to Portland cement production.
*   **Excellent Fire Resistance:** Retains strength at high temperatures.
*   **Good Chemical Resistance:** Resists acids and sulfates.
*   **High Early Strength:** Can achieve high strength quickly.
*   **Potential for Waste Utilization:** Uses industrial by-products.
*   **Low Drying Shrinkage:** Generally lower shrinkage than Portland cement concrete.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Availability and Variability of Source Materials:** The properties depend heavily on the source materials.
*   **Handling of Alkaline Activators:** Requires careful handling due to their corrosive nature.
*   **Long-Term Durability Data:** While promising, long-term field data is still accumulating.
*   **Workability Control:** Can be challenging to control flow and workability.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of structures exposed to fire or high temperatures.
*   Repair of chemical plants and industrial structures.
*   As a binder for repair mortars and shotcrete.
*   Sustainable repair solutions.

**Example:** Using geopolymer concrete made from fly ash for repair work in a power plant where high temperature and chemical resistance are required.

### 10. Reactive Powder Concrete (RPC)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Reactive Powder Concrete (RPC) is an ultra-high performance concrete with extremely high strength and durability, achieved by eliminating coarse aggregates and using a very dense packing of fine aggregates and mineral powders.
*   **Key Ingredients:**
    *   **Very Low Water-Binder Ratio:** Extremely low, often below 0.15.
    *   **High Volume Fraction of Cement:** Typically 600-800 kg/m³.
    *   **Fine Aggregates:** Silica sand, with strict control over particle size distribution.
    *   **Mineral Admixtures:** Silica fume (essential for densification and pozzolanic reaction), fly ash, slag.
    *   **Superplasticizers:** Crucial for workability.
    *   **Steel Fibres:** Usually incorporated for ductility and crack control.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Ultra-High Compressive Strength:** Can exceed 150-200 MPa, sometimes even higher.
*   **Very High Tensile and Flexural Strength:** Significantly higher than conventional concrete.
*   **Exceptional Durability:** Extremely low permeability and high resistance to abrasion and chemical attack.
*   **Compact and Dense Matrix:** Due to the absence of coarse aggregates and efficient particle packing.
*   **Excellent Fire Resistance:** Due to low water content.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Very High Cost:** Due to specialized materials and mix design.
*   **Difficult to Produce and Place:** Requires specialized equipment and expertise.
*   **Brittleness:** Without fibres, it can be very brittle.
*   **Limited Availability of Expertise:** Not as widely understood or practiced as other concrete types.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of highly stressed structural elements.
*   Precast repair elements requiring exceptional strength and durability.
*   Bridge deck overlays where high wear resistance and strength are needed.
*   Repair of elements subjected to severe loading conditions.

**Example:** Using RPC for repairing severely damaged bridge girders requiring a significant increase in load-carrying capacity and long-term durability.

### 11. Concrete Made with Industrial Wastes

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** This refers to concrete where industrial by-products or wastes are used as partial or complete replacements for conventional constituents like cement, aggregates, or water.
*   **Common Industrial Wastes Used:**
    *   **Fly Ash:** A by-product of coal combustion, used as a partial replacement for cement.
    *   **Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag (GGBFS):** A by-product of iron production, used as a partial replacement for cement.
    *   **Silica Fume:** A by-product of silicon metal production, used as a supplementary cementitious material (SCM).
    *   **Recycled Aggregates:** Crushed concrete or asphalt from demolition waste, used as coarse or fine aggregate.
    *   **Bottom Ash:** Another by-product of coal combustion, can be used as aggregate.
    *   **Glass Powder:** Crushed glass, can be used as aggregate or partial cement replacement.
    *   **Plastic Waste:** Shredded plastic can be incorporated as aggregate or fibres.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Environmental Benefits:** Reduces landfill waste, conserves natural resources, and lowers the carbon footprint (especially when replacing cement).
*   **Cost Savings:** Industrial wastes are often cheaper than virgin materials.
*   **Improved Properties:** SCMs like fly ash and silica fume can enhance durability, reduce permeability, and improve workability.
*   **Resource Efficiency:** Promotes a circular economy.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Variability of Waste Materials:** The quality and composition of industrial wastes can vary significantly, requiring careful quality control.
*   **Potential for Reduced Performance:** If not properly characterized and used, can lead to lower strength or durability.
*   **Processing Requirements:** Some wastes may require processing (e.g., crushing, sieving) before use.
*   **Market Acceptance:** Some applications might face resistance from specifiers or the public.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Sustainable repair mortars and concretes.
*   Mass concrete pours where cost reduction is a priority.
*   Overlays and patching materials where enhanced durability is achieved through SCMs.
*   Repair of non-critical elements using recycled aggregates.

**Example:** Using a repair mortar made with a significant proportion of fly ash and recycled concrete aggregate for patching a concrete retaining wall.

### 12. Polymer Concrete and Mortar

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** As discussed under Polymer Concrete (Section 1), this refers to concrete or mortar where a polymer resin acts as the binder instead of cement.
*   **Polymer Mortar:** Similar to polymer concrete but typically uses finer aggregates (sand) and no coarse aggregates.
*   **Types of Polymer Mortars:**
    *   **Epoxy Mortars:** High strength, excellent adhesion, good chemical resistance.
    *   **Polyester Mortars:** Good chemical resistance, fast setting.
    *   **Acrylic Mortars:** Rapid strength gain, good UV resistance.
    *   **Cement Polymer Mortars (Modified Mortars):** Portland cement is used as the primary binder, but polymer latex is added to the mix water to improve properties like adhesion, flexibility, and water resistance. These are very common in repair applications.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Excellent Adhesion:** Bonds strongly to existing concrete.
*   **High Strength:** Good compressive and tensile strengths.
*   **Chemical Resistance:** Protects against aggressive environments.
*   **Rapid Setting & Strength Gain:** Allows for fast repairs.
*   **Low Permeability:** Resists water ingress.
*   **Flexibility (Cement Polymer Mortars):** Polymer addition can impart some flexibility, reducing brittleness.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Cost:** Generally more expensive than traditional cementitious materials.
*   **Temperature Sensitivity:** Some polymers have limited temperature resistance.
*   **Shrinkage:** Can be susceptible to shrinkage.
*   **Health & Safety:** Handling of resins and hardeners requires care.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of spalled concrete surfaces, cracks, and joints.
*   Overlays for floors and decks.
*   Protective coatings.
*   Grouting and anchoring.

**Example:** Using an epoxy mortar to repair damaged concrete beams in a chemical factory, leveraging its strength and chemical resistance. A cement-polymer mortar is commonly used for patching concrete sidewalks.

### 13. Quick Setting Compounds (Rapid Hardening Cements/Mortars)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** These are cementitious materials designed to achieve high early strength within a very short period (hours or even minutes), allowing for rapid return to service.
*   **Mechanisms:**
    *   **High Early Strength Cements (e.g., Type III ASTM C150):** Finely ground cement with a higher proportion of C₃A and C₃S, leading to faster hydration.
    *   **Special Admixtures:** Accelerating admixtures (e.g., calcium chloride, nitrates, thiocyanates, aluminates) are often used.
    *   **Specialty Binders:** Expansive cements (discussed later), calcium sulfoaluminate cements.
*   **Properties & Advantages:**
    *   **Very Fast Strength Development:** Allows for rapid repair and minimal downtime.
    *   **High Early Compressive Strength:** Essential for structural repairs needing quick load transfer.
    *   **Can be Used in Cold Weather:** Faster hydration helps overcome cold temperatures.

*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **Lower Long-Term Strength:** Often sacrifice some long-term strength for early strength.
    *   **Heat of Hydration:** Generates significant heat, potentially causing thermal cracking.
    *   **Limited Working Time:** Setting time is very short.
    *   **Shrinkage:** Can be prone to shrinkage.
    *   **Corrosion (if using chloride-based accelerators):** Can accelerate corrosion of reinforcing steel.

*   **Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**
    *   Emergency repairs to infrastructure (e.g., patching roads, runways).
    *   Repair of active leaks in concrete structures.
    *   Rapid repairs in critical areas where downtime must be minimized.
    *   Anchoring of bolts and fixtures.

*   **Example:** Using a rapid-hardening mortar to patch a pothole on a busy road during off-peak hours, allowing the road to be reopened within a few hours.

### 14. Gunite and Shotcrete

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** These are pneumatically applied concrete or mortar materials, sprayed at high velocity onto a surface. They are often used for repair and rehabilitation.
*   **Shotcrete:** A general term for concrete conveyed through a hose and pneumatically projected at high speed onto a surface. It can be wet-mix or dry-mix.
    *   **Wet-Mix Shotcrete:** Cement, aggregates, and water are mixed before being pumped through the hose. A nozzleman controls the application.
    *   **Dry-Mix Shotcrete:** Cement and aggregates are mixed dry and conveyed through the hose. Water is added at the nozzle.
*   **Gunite:** A specific type of dry-mix shotcrete where only cement and sand are used (no coarse aggregate), and water is added at the nozzle. Often refers to a higher velocity application.
*   **Mechanism:** The high velocity impact compacts the material, producing a dense, low-porosity layer with excellent adhesion. Fibres (steel or synthetic) are often incorporated into shotcrete mixes.

**Properties & Advantages:**

*   **Excellent Adhesion:** Bonds very well to prepared substrates.
*   **High Strength and Durability:** Due to the high velocity compaction.
*   **Ability to Apply on Vertical and Overhead Surfaces:** Ideal for complex shapes and repairs.
*   **Fast Application:** Efficient for covering large areas.
*   **Reduced Formwork:** Eliminates the need for traditional formwork in many applications.
*   **Crack Control (with fibres):** Fibre-reinforced shotcrete improves toughness and crack resistance.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Rebound:** Some material bounces off the surface during application, leading to waste and requiring cleanup.
*   **Dust and Noise:** The process generates significant dust and noise.
*   **Skilled Nozzleman Required:** The quality of application depends heavily on the skill of the nozzleman.
*   **Limited Thickness per Pass:** For thick applications, multiple passes are required.

**Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**

*   Repair of spalled concrete on beams, columns, and walls.
*   Lining of tunnels and shafts.
*   Strengthening of existing structures.
*   Encapsulation of damaged structural elements.
*   Fireproofing of steel structures.

**Example:** Using shotcrete to repair the underside of a concrete bridge deck that has spalled due to rebar corrosion.

### 15. Expansive Cement

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Expansive cements are hydraulic cements that expand during the early hydration period. This expansion is controlled and is used to counteract the shrinkage that occurs in concrete.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Type K:** Contains anhydrous sulfate of aluminum and calcium sulfate.
    *   **Type M:** Contains calcium sulfoaluminate (ciment fondu).
    *   **Type S:** Contains a high percentage of tricalcium aluminate.
*   **Mechanism:** The expansion is primarily due to the formation of ettringite during the hydration process.
*   **Properties & Advantages:**
    *   **Controlled Expansion:** Counteracts drying shrinkage and prevents tensile stresses.
    *   **Improved Crack Control:** Reduces cracking caused by shrinkage.
    *   **Increased Bond Strength:** Can improve bond between concrete layers or between concrete and reinforcement.
    *   **Pre-stressing Applications:** Can be used to pre-stress concrete structures internally.

*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **Requires Careful Control:** The amount of expansion must be precisely controlled to avoid detrimental stresses.
    *   **Higher Cost:** Generally more expensive than ordinary Portland cement.
    *   **Limited Working Time:** Can have a shorter working time.
    *   **Specific Mix Designs Required:** Needs careful mix proportioning and curing to achieve the desired expansion.

*   **Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**
    *   Grouting and sealing of cracks to counteract shrinkage.
    *   Repair mortars to reduce shrinkage cracking and improve bond.
    *   Pre-stressing of repair sections.
    *   Mass concrete pours to minimize thermal shrinkage cracking.

*   **Example:** Using a repair mortar based on expansive cement to patch a large concrete element, ensuring the patch doesn't crack due to drying shrinkage and bonds well with the existing concrete.

### 16. Ferro Cement

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Ferrocement is a versatile construction material composed of closely spaced layers of cement mortar embedded with a mesh of small diameter steel wires or mesh.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Cement Mortar:** A rich mix of cement, sand, and water.
    *   **Reinforcement:** Typically wire mesh (e.g., chicken wire, hexagonal mesh) or woven galvanized wire mesh, sometimes combined with discrete steel wires.
    *   **Water-Cement Ratio:** Usually kept low for strength and durability.

*   **Mechanism:** The fine wire mesh distributes stress effectively and provides tensile strength to the mortar matrix. The dense mortar prevents corrosion of the mesh.

*   **Properties & Advantages:**
    *   **High Tensile Strength-to-Weight Ratio:** Strong and lightweight.
    *   **Flexibility and Crack Resistance:** Can accommodate some deformation without cracking.
    *   **Durability:** If the mortar is dense and the mesh is protected, it can be very durable.
    *   **Versatility:** Can be formed into complex shapes.
    *   **Economical:** Can be cost-effective for certain applications.

*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **Labour Intensive:** Requires skilled labour for mesh fabrication and mortar application.
    *   **Potential for Corrosion:** If the mortar cover is insufficient or cracked, the wire mesh can corrode.
    *   **Limited Fire Resistance:** The mortar cover can spall off at high temperatures.
    *   **Thickness:** Typically applied in thin layers.

*   **Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**
    *   Repair of deteriorated concrete surfaces.
    *   Shell structures, water tanks, silos.
    *   Repair of masonry walls.
    *   Protective jacketing of columns.

*   **Example:** Using ferrocement for repairing eroded concrete surfaces on a weir or for creating lightweight, durable water tanks.

### 17. Concrete Chemicals (Admixtures)

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Definition:** Concrete chemicals, more commonly known as admixtures, are ingredients added to concrete (other than cement, water, and aggregates) either before or during mixing, to modify its properties in the fresh or hardened state.
*   **Classification:**
    *   **Chemical Admixtures:** Added in small quantities (less than 5% by mass of cement).
    *   **Mineral Admixtures (SCMs):** Added in larger quantities, often as partial replacement of cement or aggregate.
*   **Key Types of Chemical Admixtures:**
    *   **Water Reducers (Plasticizers/Superplasticizers):** Reduce the amount of mixing water required for a given workability, or increase workability at a given w/c ratio.
        *   **Examples:** Lignosulfonates, sulfonated naphthalene formaldehyde, sulfonated melamine formaldehyde, polycarboxylate ethers.
    *   **Retarding Admixtures:** Slow down the rate of hydration, extending the setting time. Useful in hot weather or for long transport times.
        *   **Examples:** Lignosulfonates, carbohydrates, hydroxycarboxylic acids.
    *   **Accelerating Admixtures:** Speed up the rate of hydration, reducing setting time and increasing early strength. Useful in cold weather or for rapid repairs.
        *   **Examples:** Calcium chloride (though discouraged due to corrosion risk), calcium nitrate, triethanolamine.
    *   **Air-Entraining Admixtures:** Introduce microscopic air bubbles into the concrete matrix, improving resistance to freeze-thaw cycles and de-icing salts.
        *   **Examples:** Natural wood resins, synthetic detergents, salts of petroleum acids.
    *   **Corrosion-Inhibiting Admixtures:** Protect reinforcing steel from corrosion.
        *   **Examples:** Calcium nitrite, amines.
    *   **Shrinkage-Reducing Admixtures (SRAs):** Reduce drying shrinkage.
        *   **Examples:** Glycols, organic compounds.
    *   **Viscosity Modifying Admixtures (VMAs):** Increase the viscosity of the concrete, improving resistance to segregation and bleed. Crucial for SCC.
        *   **Examples:** Polysaccharides, cellulose derivatives.

*   **Properties & Advantages:**
    *   **Improved Workability:** Easier to place and finish.
    *   **Enhanced Durability:** Improved resistance to environmental attack, freeze-thaw.
    *   **Controlled Setting Time:** Allows for flexibility in construction schedules.
    *   **Increased Strength:** By enabling lower w/c ratios.
    *   **Special Properties:** Corrosion inhibition, shrinkage reduction.

*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **Cost:** Add to the overall cost of concrete.
    *   **Overuse:** Can lead to adverse effects if used improperly.
    *   **Compatibility Issues:** Admixtures must be compatible with each other and with the cement.
    *   **Variability:** Performance can vary with cement type and dosage.

*   **Applications in Repair & Rehabilitation:**
    *   Nearly all repair materials benefit from specific admixtures to achieve desired properties.
    *   **Repair Mortars:** Water reducers, SRAs, and sometimes polymers are used.
    *   **Shotcrete:** Accelerators are often used for quick setting.
    *   **SCC:** Superplasticizers and VMAs are essential.
    *   **HPC/HSC:** Superplasticizers and SCMs are fundamental.

*   **Example:** Adding a superplasticizer to a repair mortar to improve its flow and ensure it fills all voids in a damaged concrete element, while a small amount of accelerator might be used to speed up the repair of a spalled area on a bridge pier.

---

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Material Selection is Key:** The choice of special mortar or concrete depends entirely on the specific repair situation, the nature of the damage, the environmental conditions, and the desired performance.
*   **Performance-Based Design:** For many of these materials (HPC, SCC, RPC), the design is driven by performance requirements rather than just strength.
*   **Quality Control:** Stringent quality control of materials and workmanship is essential for the successful application of these specialized materials.
*   **Compatibility:** Ensure compatibility between repair materials and the existing substrate, as well as between different admixtures if used together.
*   **Environmental Impact:** Consider the sustainability aspects, especially when using industrial wastes or geopolymer concrete.
*   **Health and Safety:** Always adhere to safety precautions when handling chemicals, molten materials, or during high-velocity spraying.

### Practice Questions:

1.  **Which type of special concrete offers excellent resistance to acids and is often used in chemical plants?**
    *   a) Self-compacting concrete
    *   b) Sulphur infiltrated concrete
    *   c) Fibre reinforced concrete
    *   d) Geopolymer concrete
    **Answer:** b) Sulphur infiltrated concrete

2.  **What is the primary role of silica fume in High Strength Concrete (HSC) and Reactive Powder Concrete (RPC)?**
    *   a) To increase workability
    *   b) To act as a primary binder
    *   c) To improve particle packing and pozzolanic reaction, leading to densification
    *   d) To provide fire resistance
    **Answer:** c) To improve particle packing and pozzolanic reaction, leading to densification

3.  **Explain the mechanism by which bacteria-based self-healing concrete repairs cracks.**
    **Answer:** When cracks form and water enters, dormant bacteria in the concrete are activated. They consume a nutrient (like calcium lactate) and precipitate calcium carbonate (limestone), which fills the cracks.

4.  **You need to repair a severely damaged concrete beam with very congested reinforcement, making it difficult to place conventional concrete. Which special concrete would be most suitable?**
    *   a) Vacuum concrete
    *   b) Self-compacting concrete (SCC)
    *   c) Fibre reinforced concrete
    *   d) Sulphur infiltrated concrete
    **Answer:** b) Self-compacting concrete (SCC)

5.  **What are the key advantages of using Shotcrete for structural repairs?**
    **Answer:** Excellent adhesion, ability to apply on vertical/overhead surfaces, fast application, reduced formwork needs, and improved durability due to high-velocity compaction.

6.  **Which of the following materials is characterized by a very low water-binder ratio, absence of coarse aggregates, and ultra-high strength and durability?**
    *   a) Polymer concrete
    *   b) Geopolymer concrete
    *   c) Reactive Powder Concrete (RPC)
    *   d) Ferrocement
    **Answer:** c) Reactive Powder Concrete (RPC)

7.  **Discuss the role of admixtures in modern repair materials.**
    **Answer:** Admixtures play a critical role in tailoring the properties of repair materials. They can improve workability (plasticizers), control setting (accelerators/retarders), enhance durability (air-entrainers, SRAs), increase strength (plasticizers enabling lower w/c ratios), and provide specific functionalities like corrosion inhibition or viscosity modification for SCC.

8.  **Identify two common industrial wastes used in concrete production and explain their benefits.**
    **Answer:**
    *   **Fly Ash:** Used as a partial cement replacement. Benefits include reduced CO2 emissions, improved workability and durability, and lower cost.
    *   **Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag (GGBFS):** Used as a partial cement replacement. Benefits include improved durability, reduced heat of hydration, and better resistance to sulfate attack.
