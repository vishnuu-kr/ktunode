# TRAFFIC ENGINEERING AND MANAGEMENT: Module 4 - Traffic Signals

## Topic: Traffic Signals - Warrants, Pre-timed, and Traffic Actuated

---

### 1. Introduction to Traffic Signals

Traffic signals are essential control devices used to assign right-of-way to conflicting traffic movements at intersections. Their primary objective is to improve safety, efficiency, and capacity.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Intersection:** A point where two or more roadways cross.
*   **Conflicting Movements:** Traffic streams that are attempting to occupy the same space at the same time.
*   **Right-of-Way:** The legal right of a vehicle or pedestrian to proceed in preference to other traffic.

**Why Use Traffic Signals?**

*   Reduce the frequency and severity of certain types of accidents.
*   Provide for the orderly movement of vehicles and pedestrians.
*   Increase the traffic volume that can be handled at an intersection.
*   Facilitate the movement of large volumes of traffic from one major street to another.
*   Reduce delays and improve traffic flow in certain situations.

### 2. Traffic Signal Warrants

Warrants are specific conditions or criteria that justify the installation of a traffic signal at an intersection. They are used to ensure that signals are installed only when and where they are needed, as an unwarranted signal can cause more problems than it solves (e.g., increased delay, driver frustration, unnecessary stops).

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Warrant:** A minimum threshold of traffic volume, accident experience, or other conditions that indicate the need for a traffic signal.
*   **Minimum Requirements:** Warrants typically involve minimum traffic volumes on major and minor roads, pedestrian volumes, and/or accident history.

**Commonly Used Warranting Criteria (based on the Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices - MUTCD):**

*   **Warrant 1: Eight-Hour Vehicular Volume:**
    *   **Description:** This warrant considers the total vehicular volume crossing the intersection during an eight-hour period on a typical weekday. It involves comparing the observed volume with pre-defined curves that represent various combinations of major-street and minor-street volumes.
    *   **Conditions:** Requires specific minimum volumes for the major street and the minor street, often expressed as hourly rates or total volumes over eight hours. There are generally two conditions (Condition A and Condition B) that can satisfy this warrant, with Condition B being less stringent.
    *   **Example:** If the total number of vehicles on the major street during an 8-hour period is X and the total number of vehicles on the minor street during the same period is Y, this warrant is met if the combination (X, Y) falls above a specified curve on a chart.

*   **Warrant 2: Four-Hour Vehicular Volume:**
    *   **Description:** Similar to Warrant 1, but focuses on a four-hour period during which traffic volumes are heaviest. This is useful for intersections with significant peak hour variations.
    *   **Conditions:** Requires minimum vehicular volumes for three hours of a 4-hour period, with at least one hour having a volume greater than the minimum specified for the peak hour.
    *   **Example:** If during the busiest four hours of the day, three of those hours exceed certain volume thresholds for both major and minor streets, this warrant might be satisfied.

*   **Warrant 3: Peak Hour Volume:**
    *   **Description:** This warrant applies to intersections where the vehicular volume is heavy only during a few hours of the day, typically during the morning and evening commute.
    *   **Conditions:** Requires high volumes on both the major and minor streets during each of three consecutive peak hours.
    *   **Example:** If during the peak 3 hours, the major street has at least 750 vehicles per hour and the minor street has at least 250 vehicles per hour, and pedestrian volumes also meet certain criteria, this warrant can be met.

*   **Warrant 4: Pedestrian Volume:**
    *   **Description:** This warrant is primarily for situations where pedestrian traffic is a significant factor.
    *   **Conditions:** Requires a minimum number of pedestrians crossing the major street during the same 8-hour period used in Warrant 1, and also a minimum number of vehicles on the minor street.
    *   **Example:** If during an 8-hour period, there are at least 100 pedestrians crossing the major street and at least 150 vehicles entering the intersection from the minor street per hour, this warrant may be satisfied.

*   **Warrant 5: School Crossing:**
    *   **Description:** Specifically designed for school crossings where child pedestrians are present.
    *   **Conditions:** Requires a minimum number of children crossing during specific hours and a certain vehicular volume on the major street.
    *   **Example:** If at least 20 children cross during the highest hour of the morning or afternoon school periods, and the major street volume is at least 300 vehicles per hour, this warrant might be met.

*   **Warrant 6: Pedestrian and Vehicular Volume Combination:**
    *   **Description:** Combines both pedestrian and vehicular volumes, recognizing that a moderate volume of both can warrant a signal.
    *   **Conditions:** Considers combinations of pedestrian and vehicular volumes, often using a percentage of the values in Warrants 1 and 4.
    *   **Example:** If the conditions for Warrant 1 are met for 70% of the required volume and Warrant 4 is met for 70% of the required pedestrian volume, this warrant could be satisfied.

*   **Warrant 7: Coordinated Signal System:**
    *   **Description:** Applies when a signal is needed to integrate an intersection into an existing or planned coordinated signal system on an arterial street.
    *   **Conditions:** The traffic volumes on the arterial are high enough that the proposed signal can be timed to provide a favorable progression of traffic along the arterial, and the delays experienced by minor street traffic would be reduced compared to an all-way stop.
    *   **Example:** If an intersection is on a major arterial where a progression system is desired to improve flow, and the intersection's volumes warrant at least a two-phase signal, this warrant may apply.

*   **Warrant 8: Intersection Volume:**
    *   **Description:** Considers the total intersection volume, acknowledging that high combined volumes can create chaotic conditions.
    *   **Conditions:** Requires high total vehicular volumes entering the intersection from all approaches during a specific period.
    *   **Example:** If the total traffic volume from all approaches entering an intersection during any 8-hour period exceeds a certain number, this warrant may be applicable.

**Important Considerations for Warrants:**

*   **Minimum Remaining Requirements:** Many warrants require that the traffic volumes also satisfy certain minimums for at least three hours of the peak four-hour period, or that specific pedestrian volumes are met.
*   **Gap Availability:** The availability of adequate gaps in traffic on the major street is crucial for minor street vehicles and pedestrians to cross safely. Signals essentially "create" gaps.
*   **Accident Experience:** High accident frequencies, particularly those involving right-angle or turning collisions, can also be a strong indicator for signal installation, even if volume warrants are not fully met. Often, a certain number of reported accidents over a specific period (e.g., 3-5 years) is considered.
*   **Exceptional Cases:** Warrants are guidelines, not absolute rules. Unusual circumstances, such as major traffic generators (stadiums, hospitals) or specific safety concerns, might justify a signal even if the standard warrants are not fully met.

### 3. Types of Traffic Signals

Traffic signals can be broadly categorized into two main types based on their operation:

#### 3.1. Pre-timed Traffic Signals

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Fixed Cycle Length:** The total time required for a complete sequence of signal indications (e.g., green, yellow, red) for all approaches.
*   **Fixed Green Splits:** The duration of the green phase for each approach is predetermined and remains the same regardless of actual traffic demand.
*   **Offset:** The time relationship between the start of the green phase at one signal and the start of the green phase at an adjacent signal along an arterial.
*   **Cycle Length Variation:** While the splits are fixed for a given phase, the overall cycle length can be adjusted manually or automatically based on time of day or overall traffic conditions.

**Operation:**

*   Operate on a fixed, repeating cycle.
*   The green time for each approach is allocated based on historical traffic data or traffic studies.
*   Simple to design and maintain.

**Advantages:**

*   Provides predictable traffic flow along arterial streets (when coordinated).
*   Can be effective in balancing traffic on arterial streets with intersecting minor streets.
*   Lower initial cost compared to actuated signals.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Can cause unnecessary delays to minor street traffic when demand is low.
*   May not be efficient when traffic volumes fluctuate significantly.
*   Less responsive to real-time traffic demand.

**When to Use:**

*   Intersections on coordinated arterial streets where predictable progression is important.
*   Intersections with relatively stable and balanced traffic volumes throughout the day.
*   Situations where the cost of actuated equipment is prohibitive.

**Example:**

An intersection where the major street always gets 40 seconds of green, the minor street gets 20 seconds, and this cycle repeats every 60 seconds, regardless of whether there are cars waiting on the minor street.

#### 3.2. Traffic-Actuated Traffic Signals

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Traffic Detection:** Sensors (loops, video detection, radar) detect the presence and/or volume of vehicles and pedestrians on each approach.
*   **Demand-Responsive Operation:** Signal timing is adjusted in real-time based on the detected traffic demand.
*   **Cycle Length Variation:** The cycle length is not fixed and can vary from cycle to cycle.
*   **Green Splits Variation:** The duration of the green phase for each approach is variable.

**Operation:**

*   Detectors are installed on approaches to sense the presence or passage of vehicles and pedestrians.
*   The signal controller uses this information to adjust the green time, skip phases, or extend green phases.

**Types of Actuated Signals:**

*   **Semi-Actuated:** Detectors are only placed on the minor street approaches. The major street receives a minimum green time, and the signal only "happens" when demand is detected on the minor street.
    *   **Example:** On a busy arterial with a quiet side street, the arterial gets continuous green until a car is detected on the side street, at which point the signal will change to serve the side street.
*   **Fully-Actuated:** Detectors are placed on all approaches (major, minor, and pedestrian buttons). The signal provides green time to approaches that demand it and can skip phases if there is no demand.
    *   **Example:** At a complex intersection, the signal will only give green time to an approach if a car or pedestrian is detected, and the duration of that green time will depend on how many vehicles or pedestrians are present.

**Advantages:**

*   More efficient in reducing delays, especially at intersections with fluctuating traffic volumes.
*   Minimizes unnecessary stops and delays for approaches with low demand.
*   More responsive to pedestrian requests.
*   Can improve overall intersection capacity by maximizing green time utilization.

**Disadvantages:**

*   Higher initial cost due to detectors and more complex controllers.
*   Requires more maintenance for detection systems.
*   Can be more difficult to coordinate along an arterial unless advanced techniques are used.

**When to Use:**

*   Intersections with significant fluctuations in traffic volumes throughout the day.
*   Intersections where minor street demand is sporadic.
*   Intersections with substantial pedestrian activity.
*   Situations where minimizing delays is a high priority.

**Example:**

An intersection where the main road has constant traffic, but the side road only has a few cars per hour. An actuated signal would keep the main road green for longer periods and only change to the side road when a car is detected there, thus not unnecessarily stopping traffic on the main road.

### 4. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
What is the primary purpose of traffic signal warrants?

**Answer 1:**
The primary purpose of traffic signal warrants is to ensure that traffic signals are installed only at intersections where they are justified by traffic conditions, safety considerations, or other specific needs, and not installed where they would cause more problems than they solve.

**Question 2:**
Name two common warranting criteria based on vehicular volume.

**Answer 2:**
Two common warranting criteria based on vehicular volume are:
1.  Eight-Hour Vehicular Volume
2.  Four-Hour Vehicular Volume

**Question 3:**
A traffic signal that operates on a fixed cycle length and predetermined green splits for each approach is known as:
a) Traffic-Actuated
b) Pre-timed
c) Semi-Actuated
d) Fully-Actuated

**Answer 3:**
b) Pre-timed

**Question 4:**
What is a key advantage of traffic-actuated signals over pre-timed signals?

**Answer 4:**
A key advantage of traffic-actuated signals is their ability to respond to real-time traffic demand, leading to more efficient operation, reduced delays, and minimized unnecessary stops, especially at intersections with fluctuating volumes.

**Question 5:**
Describe the operational difference between a semi-actuated and a fully-actuated signal.

**Answer 5:**
*   **Semi-actuated signals** have detectors only on the minor street approaches. The major street receives a minimum green time, and the signal only serves the minor street when demand is detected there.
*   **Fully-actuated signals** have detectors on all approaches (major, minor, and pedestrian buttons). The signal provides green time to any approach that demands it and can skip phases if there is no demand, offering a more responsive control strategy.

**Question 6:**
If an intersection meets the criteria for the "Pedestrian Volume" warrant, what is a significant factor being considered?

**Answer 6:**
If an intersection meets the criteria for the "Pedestrian Volume" warrant, a significant factor being considered is the number of pedestrians crossing the major street and the vehicular volume on the minor street.

### 5. Key Points to Remember

*   **Warrants are essential guidelines:** They prevent the unnecessary installation of signals, which can lead to increased delays and driver frustration.
*   **Volume is a primary factor:** Most warrants consider vehicular volumes on major and minor streets, as well as pedestrian volumes.
*   **Accident history is also critical:** High accident frequencies can justify a signal even if volume warrants are not fully met.
*   **Pre-timed signals offer predictability:** Good for coordinated arterials, but less efficient with fluctuating demand.
*   **Actuated signals offer efficiency:** They adapt to real-time traffic, reducing delays, but have higher initial costs.
*   **Semi-actuated vs. Fully-actuated:** The difference lies in the placement of detectors and the resulting control strategy.
*   **Signal installation is a decision:** It requires careful consideration of all warranting criteria and local traffic conditions.
