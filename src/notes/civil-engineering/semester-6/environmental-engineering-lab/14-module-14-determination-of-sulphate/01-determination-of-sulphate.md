# ENVIRONMENTAL ENGINEERING LAB: Module 14 - Determination of Sulphate

## 1. Introduction to Sulphate Determination

### 1.1. Significance of Sulphate in Water and Wastewater

*   **Natural Occurrence:** Sulphates are naturally occurring ions found in rocks, soil, and minerals.
*   **Sources of Sulphate:**
    *   **Natural:** Dissolution of gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) and other sulphate-containing minerals.
    *   **Anthropogenic:**
        *   Industrial discharges (chemical manufacturing, pulp and paper mills, tanneries).
        *   Agricultural runoff (fertilizers).
        *   Wastewater treatment plant effluents.
        *   Detergents.
        *   Sewage.
*   **Impacts of Sulphate:**
    *   **Taste and Odor:** High concentrations (above 250-400 mg/L) can impart a bitter taste and laxative effect.
    *   **Corrosion:** In the presence of anaerobic bacteria, sulphates can be reduced to hydrogen sulphide (H₂S), which is corrosive to concrete and metals (e.g., in sewer systems).
    *   **Scale Formation:** Can contribute to scaling in boilers and cooling towers.
    *   **Impact on Biological Processes:** In high concentrations, sulphates can be toxic to some aquatic organisms and can interfere with biological wastewater treatment processes.

### 1.2. Learning Outcomes for Sulphate Determination

Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   Understand the principles behind the gravimetric and turbidimetric methods for sulphate determination.
*   Perform the laboratory procedure for the gravimetric determination of sulphate.
*   Perform the laboratory procedure for the turbidimetric determination of sulphate.
*   Calculate the concentration of sulphate in a given water or wastewater sample using both methods.
*   Identify potential sources of error in each method and discuss strategies for minimizing them.
*   Compare and contrast the gravimetric and turbidimetric methods for sulphate determination.

## 2. Gravimetric Determination of Sulphate

### 2.1. Principle of the Gravimetric Method

The gravimetric method involves the quantitative precipitation of sulphate ions from a sample as barium sulphate (BaSO₄) by the addition of a soluble barium salt (typically barium chloride, BaCl₂). The precipitate is then filtered, washed, dried, and weighed.

**Reaction:**

$$
SO_4^{2-} (aq) + Ba^{2+} (aq) \rightarrow BaSO_4 (s)
$$

### 2.2. Procedure for Gravimetric Determination

1.  **Sample Preparation:**
    *   Take a known volume of filtered and acidified water sample (pH 4.5-5.0 using HCl). Acidification is crucial to prevent precipitation of other barium salts, such as barium carbonate or barium phosphate.
    *   If the sample is highly colored or turbid, it may require preliminary treatment (e.g., filtration, coagulation, or flocculation).

2.  **Precipitation:**
    *   Heat the sample to near boiling.
    *   Slowly add a solution of barium chloride (BaCl₂) to the hot sample while stirring vigorously. The addition should be slow to promote the formation of larger, more easily filtered crystals of BaSO₄.
    *   Add the BaCl₂ solution dropwise until a slight excess is ensured (indicated by no further precipitation).

3.  **Digestion:**
    *   Cover the beaker and allow the precipitate to digest for at least 1-2 hours, or overnight if possible. Digestion allows the fine precipitate particles to grow into larger crystals, improving filterability and purity.

4.  **Filtration:**
    *   Filter the solution using a pre-weighed filter paper (e.g., Whatman No. 42 or a Gooch crucible with filter pulp).
    *   Wash the beaker and the precipitate several times with a dilute solution of hydrochloric acid (HCl, ~0.01 M) to remove any adsorbed BaCl₂ or other soluble impurities.
    *   Wash the precipitate with distilled water until the washings are free of chloride ions (tested with silver nitrate solution).

5.  **Drying and Ignition:**
    *   Transfer the filter paper with the precipitate to a crucible.
    *   Dry the precipitate in an oven at approximately 105-110°C until a constant weight is achieved.
    *   Ignite the precipitate in a muffle furnace at a temperature of 600-800°C to char the filter paper and dehydrate the BaSO₄.

6.  **Weighing:**
    *   Allow the crucible to cool in a desiccator and weigh it to obtain the weight of pure BaSO₄.

### 2.3. Calculations for Gravimetric Determination

*   **Formula:**

    $$
    \text{Sulphate (mg/L)} = \frac{\text{Weight of } BaSO_4 (\text{g}) \times \text{Molecular Weight of } SO_4^{2-} (\text{g/mol})}{\text{Molecular Weight of } BaSO_4 (\text{g/mol}) \times \text{Volume of Sample} (\text{L})} \times 1000 (\text{mg/g})
    $$

*   **Molecular Weights:**
    *   Molecular Weight of SO₄²⁻ = 96.06 g/mol
    *   Molecular Weight of BaSO₄ = 233.39 g/mol

*   **Simplified Calculation:**

    $$
    \text{Sulphate (mg/L)} = \frac{\text{Weight of } BaSO_4 (\text{g}) \times 96.06 \text{ g/mol}}{233.39 \text{ g/mol} \times \text{Volume of Sample} (\text{L})} \times 1000
    $$

    $$
    \text{Sulphate (mg/L)} = \frac{\text{Weight of } BaSO_4 (\text{g}) \times 0.4116}{\text{Volume of Sample} (\text{L})} \times 1000
    $$

    $$
    \text{Sulphate (mg/L)} = \text{Weight of } BaSO_4 (\text{g}) \times \frac{411.6}{\text{Volume of Sample} (\text{L})}
    $$

    **Note:** If the sample volume was measured in mL, then:

    $$
    \text{Sulphate (mg/L)} = \frac{\text{Weight of } BaSO_4 (\text{g}) \times 411.6}{\text{Volume of Sample} (\text{mL})} \times 1000
    $$

### 2.4. Advantages and Disadvantages of the Gravimetric Method

*   **Advantages:**
    *   **Accuracy and Precision:** Generally considered the most accurate and precise method if performed correctly.
    *   **Independence from Color/Turbidity:** Not affected by the color or turbidity of the sample.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **Time-Consuming:** Requires significant time for precipitation, digestion, filtration, drying, and ignition.
    *   **Labor-Intensive:** Requires careful handling of precipitates and multiple steps.
    *   **Interferences:**
        *   **Silica:** Precipitates as BaSO₄ if present in large amounts and the pH is not maintained properly.
        *   **Nitrates:** May interfere at high concentrations due to solubility of BaSO₄.
        *   **Heavy Metals:** Can precipitate as hydroxides if the pH is too high.
        *   **Chloride and Fluoride:** Can form slightly soluble barium salts, requiring thorough washing.

## 3. Turbidimetric Determination of Sulphate

### 3.1. Principle of the Turbidimetric Method

The turbidimetric method is a colorimetric or nephelometric method. Sulphate ions are precipitated with barium chloride (BaCl₂) in an acidic solution, forming a fine suspension of barium sulphate (BaSO₄) particles. The turbidity (cloudiness) of this suspension is proportional to the sulphate concentration. The turbidity is measured using a spectrophotometer or a turbidimeter at a specific wavelength (typically 400-600 nm).

**Reaction:**

$$
SO_4^{2-} (aq) + Ba^{2+} (aq) \rightarrow BaSO_4 (s)
$$

**Important:** The formation of uniform and stable BaSO₄ particles is crucial for accurate results. This is achieved by adding:

*   **Acid:** HCl to maintain a pH of 4.5-5.0.
*   **Wetting Agent/Stabilizer:** Such as glycerol or Gum Acacia, to prevent particle agglomeration and settle out.
*   **Specific Precipitating Conditions:** Controlled addition of BaCl₂ and consistent stirring/mixing.

### 3.2. Procedure for Turbidimetric Determination

1.  **Sample Preparation:**
    *   Take a known volume of filtered, clear water sample.
    *   If the sample is colored or turbid, it needs to be pre-treated.

2.  **Reagent Addition:**
    *   Add a specific volume of hydrochloric acid (HCl) to the sample to adjust the pH to 4.5-5.0.
    *   Add a measured amount of wetting agent (e.g., Gum Acacia solution).
    *   Add a measured amount of barium chloride (BaCl₂) crystals or solution. The BaCl₂ should be added all at once or in a consistent manner.

3.  **Precipitation and Reaction Time:**
    *   Mix the sample vigorously for a specified time (e.g., 1 minute) to ensure uniform precipitation.
    *   Allow the suspension to stand for a specified period (e.g., 5-10 minutes) to allow for particle development and stabilization.

4.  **Measurement of Turbidity:**
    *   Transfer the sample to a cuvette or sample cell.
    *   Measure the absorbance or light transmittance using a spectrophotometer or turbidimeter at a pre-determined wavelength.
    *   The instrument must be calibrated using a standard sulphate solution.

5.  **Calibration Curve:**
    *   Prepare a series of standard sulphate solutions of known concentrations.
    *   Treat each standard solution using the same procedure as the sample (reagent addition, mixing, reaction time).
    *   Measure the absorbance/turbidity of each standard.
    *   Plot a calibration curve with sulphate concentration on the x-axis and absorbance/turbidity on the y-axis.

### 3.3. Calculations for Turbidimetric Determination

*   **Using a Calibration Curve:**
    *   Determine the absorbance/turbidity of the unknown sample.
    *   Locate this value on the y-axis of the calibration curve and find the corresponding sulphate concentration on the x-axis.

*   **Using an Equation (if the curve is linear):**
    *   If the calibration curve is linear, it can be represented by an equation of the form: $Absorbance = m \times Concentration + c$, where $m$ is the slope and $c$ is the y-intercept.
    *   Once the absorbance of the sample is measured, the concentration can be calculated by rearranging the equation: $Concentration = (Absorbance - c) / m$.

### 3.4. Advantages and Disadvantages of the Turbidimetric Method

*   **Advantages:**
    *   **Rapid:** Significantly faster than the gravimetric method.
    *   **Less Labor-Intensive:** Requires less manual manipulation.
    *   **Suitable for Routine Analysis:** Good for frequent testing.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   **Affected by Color and Turbidity:** Sample color or initial turbidity can interfere with the measurement, requiring pre-treatment.
    *   **Particle Size Dependency:** Results are highly dependent on the size, uniformity, and stability of the BaSO₄ precipitate. Variations in reagent addition, mixing, and standing time can lead to significant errors.
    *   **Limited Concentration Range:** The method is most accurate for concentrations within the range of the calibration curve.
    *   **Interferences:**
        *   **Suspended Solids:** Can contribute to turbidity.
        *   **Silica:** Can form turbidity.
        *   **Barium Compounds:** If present in the sample, they can interfere.
        *   **Organic Matter:** Can influence precipitate formation.

## 4. Comparison of Gravimetric and Turbidimetric Methods

| Feature             | Gravimetric Method                                      | Turbidimetric Method                                       |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Principle**       | Quantitative precipitation and weighing of BaSO₄        | Measurement of turbidity of BaSO₄ suspension               |
| **Accuracy**        | High, considered definitive                             | Good, but sensitive to procedural variations               |
| **Precision**       | High                                                    | Moderate, depends on calibration and technique             |
| **Time Required**   | Long (hours to overnight)                               | Short (minutes)                                            |
| **Labor Intensive** | High                                                    | Low                                                        |
| **Equipment**       | Balance, oven, muffle furnace, filters, glassware       | Spectrophotometer/turbidimeter, glassware, reagents        |
| **Interferences**   | Silica, Nitrates, Heavy Metals, Chloride, Fluoride      | Color, Turbidity, Suspended Solids, Silica, Organic Matter |
| **Suitability**     | Reference method, for high accuracy needs               | Routine analysis, rapid screening                          |
| **Cost**            | Higher initial equipment cost, but reagent cost is low  | Lower initial equipment cost, but reagents are specific      |

## 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **pH Control:** Maintaining the correct pH (4.5-5.0) is critical for both methods to ensure only sulphate precipitates as BaSO₄ and to control particle formation.
*   **Reagent Purity:** Use high-purity reagents for both methods.
*   **Controlled Conditions:** For the turbidimetric method, consistent and controlled addition of reagents, mixing, and reaction times are paramount.
*   **Calibration:** Proper calibration of the spectrophotometer/turbidimeter with reliable standards is essential for the turbidimetric method.
*   **Sample Preparation:** Always filter samples if they are turbid or contain suspended solids. Pre-treat colored samples if necessary for the turbidimetric method.
*   **Gravimetric Considerations:** Ensure complete precipitation and thorough washing of the BaSO₄ precipitate to remove soluble impurities. Proper ignition at the correct temperature is crucial for obtaining pure BaSO₄.
*   **Turbidimetric Considerations:** Avoid formation of very coarse or very fine precipitates, as they can affect light scattering and measurement. The wetting agent plays a key role in stabilizing the suspension.

## 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1 (Gravimetric):**
A 250 mL sample of wastewater was analyzed for sulphate using the gravimetric method. After precipitation, filtration, drying, and ignition, the weight of BaSO₄ obtained was 0.2550 g. Calculate the concentration of sulphate in the wastewater sample in mg/L.

**Question 2 (Turbidimetric):**
You have prepared a calibration curve for sulphate determination using the turbidimetric method. The curve is linear and represented by the equation: Absorbance = 0.0125 * Concentration (mg/L) + 0.010. A water sample was analyzed and showed an absorbance of 0.360. Calculate the concentration of sulphate in the sample.

**Question 3 (Conceptual):**
Explain why it is important to acidify the sample before precipitating sulphate as barium sulphate in the gravimetric method.

**Question 4 (Conceptual):**
Discuss two significant differences between the gravimetric and turbidimetric methods for sulphate determination.

**Question 5 (Practical Application):**
Which method would you choose for routine monitoring of sulphate levels in a large number of water samples, and why?

### Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
*   Weight of BaSO₄ = 0.2550 g
*   Volume of Sample = 250 mL = 0.250 L
*   Formula: Sulphate (mg/L) = (Weight of BaSO₄ (g) × 411.6) / Volume of Sample (mL)
*   Sulphate (mg/L) = (0.2550 g × 411.6) / 250 mL
*   Sulphate (mg/L) = 104.958 / 250
*   **Sulphate (mg/L) = 419.8 mg/L**

**Answer 2:**
*   Absorbance = 0.360
*   Equation: Absorbance = 0.0125 * Concentration + 0.010
*   Rearranging for Concentration: Concentration = (Absorbance - 0.010) / 0.0125
*   Concentration = (0.360 - 0.010) / 0.0125
*   Concentration = 0.350 / 0.0125
*   **Concentration = 28 mg/L**

**Answer 3:**
Acidification to a pH of 4.5-5.0 (using HCl) is crucial to prevent the precipitation of other barium salts, such as barium carbonate (BaCO₃) or barium phosphate (Ba₃(PO₄)₂), which can form in neutral or alkaline conditions. These would co-precipitate with BaSO₄ and lead to an overestimation of the sulphate concentration.

**Answer 4:**
Two significant differences:
1.  **Method of Measurement:** The gravimetric method involves physically weighing the precipitate (BaSO₄), whereas the turbidimetric method measures the cloudiness (turbidity) of a BaSO₄ suspension.
2.  **Time and Labor:** The gravimetric method is time-consuming and labor-intensive due to multiple steps like digestion, drying, and ignition. The turbidimetric method is much faster and requires less manual effort.

**Answer 5:**
For routine monitoring of sulphate levels in a large number of water samples, the **turbidimetric method** would be the preferred choice. This is because it is significantly faster and less labor-intensive compared to the gravimetric method, making it more efficient for high-throughput analysis. While the gravimetric method offers higher accuracy, the speed and ease of the turbidimetric method make it suitable for routine monitoring where quick results are often desired. Proper calibration and consistent adherence to the procedure are necessary to ensure acceptable accuracy.
