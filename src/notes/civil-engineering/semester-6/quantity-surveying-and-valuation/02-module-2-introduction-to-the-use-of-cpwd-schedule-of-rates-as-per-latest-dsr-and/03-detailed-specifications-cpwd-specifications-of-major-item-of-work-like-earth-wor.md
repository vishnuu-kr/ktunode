# Quantity Surveying and Valuation: Module 2 - CPWD Specifications & Rate Analysis

## Module 2: Introduction to the use of CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR) and Analysis of Rate (DAR)

This module introduces the fundamental concepts of using the Central Public Works Department (CPWD) Schedule of Rates (DSR) and the principles of analyzing rates for various building works. Understanding these is crucial for accurate cost estimation, tender preparation, and project management.

---

### Topic: Detailed Specifications (CPWD Specifications) of Major Items of Work

This section delves into the detailed specifications as provided by the CPWD for common and significant building construction activities. These specifications ensure quality, uniformity, and a standardized approach to construction across government projects.

---

### 1. Earthwork Excavation in Foundation

**Learning Outcome:** Understand the detailed specifications for earthwork excavation in foundations as per CPWD.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Earthwork:** The process of moving soil or rock from one location to another.
*   **Excavation:** The process of removing earth to create a space for foundations, trenches, etc.
*   **Foundation:** The lowest part of a building or structure, which transmits its load to the ground.
*   **Lead:** The average horizontal distance over which the excavated material is to be transported.
*   **Lift:** The average vertical distance over which the excavated material is to be transported.
*   **Batter:** The angle at which the sides of an excavation are sloped back to prevent collapse.
*   **Shoring:** Temporary support provided to the sides of an excavation to prevent collapse.
*   **Timbering:** The use of timber or other materials to support the sides of an excavation.
*   **Dewatering:** The process of removing groundwater from an excavation.

**CPWD Specifications for Earthwork Excavation:**

*   **General:**
    *   Excavation shall be carried out to the lines, levels, and dimensions shown on the drawings or as directed by the Engineer-in-Charge.
    *   The excavated material shall be classified and disposed of as per the directions of the Engineer-in-Charge.
    *   All necessary precautions shall be taken to prevent damage to adjacent structures, services, and the public.
*   **Batter Slopes:**
    *   Unless otherwise specified or shown on the drawings, excavation in earth shall be carried out with a batter slope of 1 horizontal to 1 vertical (1:1) for depths up to 1.5m.
    *   For depths exceeding 1.5m, the batter shall be increased or vertical sides shall be supported by shoring and timbering as per the Engineer-in-Charge's direction.
    *   Specific batter slopes for different soil types (e.g., sand, clay) may be specified.
*   **Shoring and Timbering:**
    *   If the stability of the excavation sides cannot be maintained by battering, or if required by the Engineer-in-Charge, shoring and timbering shall be provided.
    *   The design and installation of shoring shall be adequate to prevent collapse and ensure safety.
    *   Materials used for shoring shall be of suitable quality and strength.
*   **Dewatering:**
    *   If groundwater is encountered, dewatering measures such as pumps, sumps, or well points shall be employed to keep the excavation dry and workable.
    *   The method of dewatering shall be approved by the Engineer-in-Charge.
*   **Disposal of Excavated Material:**
    *   Excavated material shall be carefully sorted and segregated.
    *   Approved material (e.g., for backfilling, landscaping) shall be stacked at designated locations.
    *   Unsuitable material shall be removed from the site and disposed of as directed.
    *   The lead and lift for the disposal of excavated material will be specified in the Schedule of Rates.
*   **Measurement:**
    *   Excavated work shall be measured in cubic meters (cum).
    *   Measurements shall be taken in net quantities after deducting any structure embedded in the excavation, like foundation concrete.
    *   The depth of excavation for measurement purposes shall not exceed the depth shown on the drawings or directed by the Engineer-in-Charge.

**Example:**

Excavating foundation trenches for a building. If the trench is 1.5m deep in a stable soil, a 1:1 batter might be specified. If the depth increases to 3m, the sides would need to be battered more steeply or supported with timbering. Groundwater presence would necessitate dewatering.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Always refer to the specific drawings and Engineer-in-Charge's instructions.
*   Safety is paramount in excavation work.
*   Proper classification and disposal of excavated material are crucial for cost and environmental management.

### 2. Masonry

**Learning Outcome:** Understand the detailed specifications for masonry work as per CPWD.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Masonry:** The construction of structures from individual units laid in and bound together by mortar.
*   **Units:** Bricks, stone blocks, concrete blocks, fly ash bricks, etc.
*   **Mortar:** A paste used to bind masonry units together, typically made of cement, sand, and water.
*   **Bond:** The pattern in which masonry units are laid to ensure structural integrity.
*   **Facing:** The exposed surface of a masonry wall.
*   **Backing:** The material behind the facing in a masonry wall.
*   **Pointing:** The process of finishing the mortar joints in a masonry wall.
*   **Coursed Masonry:** Masonry where units are laid in uniform courses with regular horizontal joints.
*   **Uncoursed Masonry:** Masonry where units are laid without strict uniformity of size or regularity of courses.

**CPWD Specifications for Masonry:**

*   **General:**
    *   All masonry shall be built true to line, level, and plumb, to the dimensions and patterns shown on the drawings.
    *   Masonry shall be laid in fresh mortar.
    *   All bricks/stones shall be properly wetted before laying in cement mortar, but not saturated.
*   **Materials:**
    *   **Bricks:** Shall be of first quality, well-burnt, uniformly shaped and colored, with sharp edges and flat surfaces. They should meet specified compressive strength and water absorption requirements.
    *   **Stones:** Shall be sound, durable, free from cracks and flaws, and of the specified type and size. Dressing of stones shall be as per drawings.
    *   **Mortar:** Mix proportion (e.g., 1:4 cement:sand, 1:6 cement:sand) shall be as specified. Sand shall be clean and free from silt, clay, and organic matter.
*   **Laying of Bricks/Stones:**
    *   **Bond:** Shall be in English Bond, Flemish Bond, or as specified. All joints shall be fully filled with mortar.
    *   **Joints:** Thickness of mortar joints shall be uniform, typically 10mm for brickwork. Walls shall be built in courses, with proper staggering of joints.
    *   **Plinth Protection:** Shall be provided at the base of walls as per specifications.
    *   **Through Stones/Bonders:** Shall be provided at specified intervals in the thickness of the wall to tie the facing and backing together.
*   **Stone Masonry:**
    *   **Dressing:** Stones shall be dressed as per specifications (e.g., ashlar, rubble facing).
    *   **Bedding and Joints:** Mortar bedding shall be provided, and joints shall be fully filled.
    *   **Through Stones:** Used to bind facing and backing.
*   **Finishing:**
    *   **Pointing:** Mortar joints shall be finished neatly by pointing with cement mortar of specified mix.
    *   **Washing/Racking:** For rubble masonry, joints may be racked out and filled with mortar or the wall washed with neat cement.
*   **Measurement:**
    *   Masonry work shall be measured in cubic meters (cum) for the volume of the wall.
    *   Deductions shall be made for openings (doors, windows) exceeding 0.5 sq m. No deduction for smaller openings or embedded metal parts.
    *   Extra for specific types of masonry (e.g., arch work, honeycombed brickwork) shall be measured and paid as per specifications.
    *   Plinth protection shall be measured in square meters (sq m).

**Example:**

Constructing a load-bearing brick wall using 1:6 cement mortar. Bricks are soaked before laying. English Bond is used. Mortar joints are 10mm thick and later pointed with 1:3 cement mortar.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Ensure the quality of bricks/stones and mortar.
*   Maintain plumbness and levelness throughout the work.
*   Proper bonding is crucial for structural stability.
*   Thorough filling of joints is essential to prevent water ingress.

### 3. Reinforced Cement Concrete (RCC)

**Learning Outcome:** Understand the detailed specifications for Reinforced Cement Concrete (RCC) work as per CPWD.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Reinforced Cement Concrete (RCC):** Concrete that is strengthened by embedding steel reinforcing bars (rebars) within it.
*   **Concrete Mix:** Proportions of cement, fine aggregate (sand), coarse aggregate (gravel/crushed stone), and water.
*   **Workability:** The ease with which concrete can be mixed, placed, compacted, and finished without segregation. Measured by slump test.
*   **Grade of Concrete:** Denotes the characteristic compressive strength of concrete after 28 days of curing (e.g., M20, M25).
*   **Reinforcement Steel (Rebars):** Steel bars of specified diameter and grade (e.g., Fe 500) used for tensile strength.
*   **Formwork/Shuttering:** Temporary molds into which wet concrete is poured and compacted, which supports the concrete while it hardens.
*   **Compaction:** The process of reducing the void space in concrete to increase its density and strength, typically done using vibrators.
*   **Curing:** The process of maintaining moisture content and temperature in concrete for a specified period after placement to ensure proper hydration and strength development.

**CPWD Specifications for RCC Work:**

*   **General:**
    *   All RCC work shall be executed strictly as per the drawings and the Engineer-in-Charge's instructions.
    *   Materials used shall conform to relevant IS codes and CPWD specifications.
*   **Materials:**
    *   **Cement:** Shall be Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) of specified grade (e.g., 43 grade) or Portland Pozzolana Cement (PPC) or Portland Slag Cement (PSC), as specified.
    *   **Aggregates:**
        *   **Fine Aggregate (Sand):** Clean, hard, durable, free from impurities. Grading shall be as per IS 383.
        *   **Coarse Aggregate (Gravel/Crushed Stone):** Clean, hard, durable, of specified size and shape, free from dust and organic matter. Grading shall be as per IS 383.
    *   **Reinforcement Steel:** Shall be of specified grade (e.g., Fe 500) and conform to IS 1786. Steel shall be free from rust, oil, paint, and other coatings that may impair bond.
    *   **Water:** Shall be clean and free from deleterious substances.
*   **Concrete Mix Design:**
    *   The grade of concrete (e.g., M20, M25) shall be as specified in the drawings.
    *   Concrete mix proportions shall be determined based on the required grade and workability (slump) as per IS 456 and CPWD guidelines.
*   **Proportioning and Mixing:**
    *   Cement, aggregates, and water shall be measured accurately by weight or volume as specified.
    *   Mixing shall be done in a mechanical mixer unless hand mixing is permitted for small quantities.
    *   The mixing time shall be adequate to ensure uniform distribution of all constituents.
*   **Placing and Compaction:**
    *   Concrete shall be placed as near as possible to its final position.
    *   It shall be compacted thoroughly by means of vibrators to remove entrapped air and ensure density and homogeneity.
    *   Care shall be taken to avoid segregation during placing and compaction.
*   **Formwork:**
    *   Formwork shall be made of suitable material (timber, steel, plastic), be watertight, and securely propped and braced to withstand the pressure of wet concrete.
    *   The surface of formwork in contact with concrete shall be oiled or treated to prevent sticking.
    *   Formwork shall not be removed until the concrete has gained sufficient strength.
*   **Curing:**
    *   Curing shall commence as soon as the concrete has set and shall continue for at least 7 days (or longer, as specified, especially in hot and dry weather).
    *   Methods of curing include water curing (ponding, spraying, wet coverings) or other approved methods.
*   **Measurement:**
    *   RCC work shall be measured in cubic meters (cum).
    *   No deduction shall be made for reinforcement steel or for any voids not exceeding 0.5% of the concrete volume.
    *   Extra for specific finishes (e.g., plastering on RCC surface) or for specific admixtures shall be measured and paid separately.

**Example:**

Casting an RCC beam for a building. The specified concrete grade is M25. Fe 500 steel bars of various diameters are used. The concrete is mixed in a transit mixer, placed using a concrete pump, and compacted with needle vibrators. The formwork is erected, and after casting, the beam is kept wet for 7 days for curing.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Adherence to the specified concrete grade and mix proportions is critical for strength.
*   Proper compaction is essential to avoid honeycombing and ensure durability.
*   Effective curing is vital for achieving the designed strength and durability.
*   Reinforcement placement and cover must be as per drawings.

### 4. Finishing of Building Work

**Learning Outcome:** Understand the detailed specifications for various finishing works in buildings as per CPWD.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Finishing:** The final treatment applied to surfaces of a building to provide aesthetic appeal, protection, and desired functional properties.
*   **Plastering:** Applying a coat of mortar to surfaces to create a smooth and even finish.
*   **Painting:** Applying coats of paint to surfaces for protection and decoration.
*   **Flooring:** Laying a durable and aesthetic surface on the floor.
*   **Skirting:** A decorative and protective band at the base of walls where they meet the floor.
*   **False Ceiling:** A secondary ceiling suspended below the structural ceiling.
*   **Distempering:** Applying a water-based paint for interior decoration.

**CPWD Specifications for Finishing Works:**

*   **Plastering:**
    *   **General:** Surfaces to be plastered shall be clean, sound, and free from loose particles and dust. Rough surfaces should be hacked to provide a key.
    *   **Materials:** Cement, sand, and water shall be of specified quality and proportions (e.g., 1:4 cement:sand for internal walls, 1:5 for external walls).
    *   **Application:** Plaster shall be applied in one or more coats to a uniform thickness, as specified (typically 12mm for internal, 15mm for external). The finished surface shall be true to plumb, level, and smooth.
    *   **Finishing:** The final coat shall be finished smooth, trowel-finished, or punned as specified.
    *   **Curing:** Plastered surfaces shall be kept wet for at least 7 days.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured in square meters (sq m). Deductions for openings exceeding 0.5 sq m. Extra for specific finishes like rough cast, pebble dash.
*   **Painting:**
    *   **General:** All surfaces to be painted shall be clean, dry, and free from grease, dirt, and loose paint.
    *   **Materials:** Shall be of specified type (e.g., oil bound distemper, emulsion paint, enamel paint) and quality.
    *   **Preparation:** Surfaces shall be prepared by scraping, brushing, and applying primer/undercoat as specified.
    *   **Application:** Paint shall be applied in the number of coats specified, in uniform thickness, and with smooth finish, using brushes or rollers. Each coat shall be allowed to dry thoroughly before the next coat is applied.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured in square meters (sq m). Extra for specific surfaces like railings, gratings, textured finishes.
*   **Flooring:**
    *   **General:** The base for flooring shall be clean and properly prepared.
    *   **Materials:** Specified materials like cement concrete, tiles (ceramic, vitrified), stone, marble, wood, etc.
    *   **Laying:** Tiles/stones shall be laid in a specified mortar bed, with uniform joints. Patterns, if any, shall be followed as per drawings.
    *   **Finishing:** Surface shall be smooth, level, and free from grout stains.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured in square meters (sq m). Extra for skirting, special patterns, or edge treatments.
*   **Skirting:**
    *   **General:** Skirting shall be fixed to the wall at the base of the room.
    *   **Materials:** Cement plaster, tiles, stone, etc.
    *   **Fixing:** Shall be fixed true to line and level.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured in running meters (rm).
*   **False Ceiling:**
    *   **General:** The supporting framework and panels shall be fixed as per manufacturer's instructions and drawings.
    *   **Materials:** Gypsum boards, mineral fiber tiles, metal tiles, etc., with appropriate framing systems.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured in square meters (sq m).

**Example:**

*   **Plastering:** Internal wall plastered with 1:5 cement mortar to 12mm thickness, trowel finished smooth, and cured for 7 days.
*   **Painting:** An exterior wall painted with two coats of weather-resistant emulsion paint over a primer coat.
*   **Flooring:** Vitrified tiles laid on a cement mortar bed in a hall.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Surface preparation is critical for the performance of finishing works.
*   Use of specified materials and correct mix proportions is essential.
*   Proper curing of plastered surfaces is important for durability.
*   Adherence to patterns and finishes shown in drawings ensures the desired aesthetic outcome.

### Answers to Practice Questions

**1. Multiple Choice Answers:**

*   a) (i) 1 horizontal to 1 vertical
*   b) (iii) Batter slopes or shoring/timbering
*   c) (iii) 7 days
*   d) (iii) Cubic meters (cum)
*   e) (ii) The area of the opening is deducted

**2. Short Answer Questions (Example Answers):**

*   **Key considerations for material selection in CPWD masonry work:** Quality of bricks/stones (strength, water absorption, shape), quality of sand (cleanliness, grading), and cement (grade, type).
*   **Importance of compaction in RCC work:** Compaction removes entrapped air voids, increases the density and strength of concrete, improves durability, and enhances the bond between concrete and reinforcement.
*   **Common methods of curing for concrete:** Water curing (ponding, spraying, wet coverings like hessian sacks) and steam curing (less common in general building construction).
*   **Surface preparation for painting:** Cleaning the surface of dust, dirt, grease, and loose paint; scraping old paint; filling cracks; sanding; and applying primer or undercoat.
*   **Purpose of "through stones" in stone masonry:** Through stones (or bonders) are used to tie the facing and backing of a stone wall together, ensuring the stability and monolithic action of the wall.

**3. Practical Exercise Answer:**

*   **Measures for excavation sides:** Given the depth of 2.0 meters and loose sand, vertical sides are highly unlikely to be stable. CPWD specifications would require either:
    *   **Batter slopes:** A significantly flatter batter than 1:1, perhaps 1:2 or even flatter, to prevent collapse.
    *   **Shoring and Timbering:** If battering is not feasible due to space constraints or if the sand is particularly unstable, extensive shoring and timbering using planks and props would be necessary.
*   **Potential issues in loose sand:**
    *   **Collapse:** The sides are prone to sliding and collapsing, posing a significant safety hazard.
    *   **Infiltration of sand:** Sand can continuously fall into the excavation, making it difficult to work and requiring continuous removal.
    *   **Dewatering:** If the water table is high, groundwater can saturate the sand, exacerbating its instability.
*   **Measurement of excavated material:** The excavated material (loose sand) would typically be measured in **cubic meters (cum)**. For billing purposes, it might be converted to "in situ" or "bank" measure based on standard factors, but the initial measurement is usually taken in loose volume after excavation. The lead and lift for disposal would be as per the Schedule of Rates.
