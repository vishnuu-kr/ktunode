# QUANTITY SURVEYING AND VALUATION - MODULE 3: DETAILED ESTIMATE

## TOPIC: BOQ PREPARATION OF A SINGLE-STORIED RCC BUILDING WORK

---

### 1. LEARNING OUTCOMES

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Understand the purpose and importance of a Bill of Quantities (BOQ) in construction.
*   Identify the different components and sections of a typical BOQ for a single-storied RCC building.
*   Apply standard methods of measurement (SMM) for various building trades.
*   Calculate quantities accurately for different building elements.
*   Properly format and present a BOQ for a single-storied RCC building.
*   Understand the role of the BOQ in tendering, cost control, and contractual administration.

---

### 2. KEY CONCEPTS AND DEFINITIONS

*   **Bill of Quantities (BOQ):** A document prepared by a quantity surveyor that lists all the work items to be carried out in a construction project, with their quantities measured in standard units. It serves as a basis for tendering and for valuing the work done during the construction phase.
*   **Quantity Surveyor (QS):** A professional who manages all costs relating to construction projects, from initial calculations to the final figures. They ensure that projects are completed within the budget.
*   **Single-Storied RCC Building:** A building with only one floor above ground level, where the structural frame and often other elements like walls and slabs are constructed using Reinforced Cement Concrete (RCC).
*   **Standard Methods of Measurement (SMM):** Prescribed rules and guidelines for measuring building and civil engineering works, ensuring consistency and uniformity in the preparation of BOQs. Examples include the Standard Method of Measurement of Building Works (e.g., SMM7 in the UK, or relevant national standards).
*   **Tender:** A formal offer to carry out the construction work for a specified price.
*   **Rate:** The cost per unit of a particular item of work, including labor, materials, plant, and overheads.
*   **Preliminaries:** Items of work that are necessary for the execution of the contract but cannot be directly attributed to specific work sections. Examples include site establishment, insurance, and project management.
*   **Works:** The actual construction activities and elements of the building.

---

### 3. PURPOSE AND IMPORTANCE OF A BOQ

*   **Tendering:** Provides a standardized basis for contractors to price the work, allowing for fair comparison of tenders.
*   **Cost Control:** Acts as a baseline for tracking and controlling project expenditure during construction.
*   **Contractual Administration:** Forms part of the construction contract and is used for interim payments, variations, and final account settlement.
*   **Resource Planning:** Helps in planning and procuring materials, labor, and plant.
*   **Risk Management:** Clearly defines the scope of work, reducing potential disputes and claims.
*   **Valuation of Work:** Essential for assessing the value of work executed for progress payments.

---

### 4. COMPONENTS OF A TYPICAL BOQ FOR A SINGLE-STORIED RCC BUILDING

A BOQ is typically structured into several main sections, starting with Preliminaries and followed by various work sections.

**A. Preliminaries:**

*   **General Conditions:** Contractual requirements, insurance, bonds.
*   **Site Establishment:** Site office, temporary fencing, welfare facilities, site security.
*   **Temporary Works:** Formwork, scaffolding (if not measured elsewhere), temporary lighting, water supply.
*   **Design and Supervision:** Fees for design, site supervision, testing.
*   **Health and Safety:** Implementation of safety plans, provisions for safety.
*   **Environmental Management:** Measures for pollution control, waste management.
*   **Commissioning and Handover:** Testing of services, final cleaning, as-built drawings.

**B. Works Sections (Commonly):**

*   **Substructure:**
    *   Earthwork (Excavation for foundations, footing, trenches)
    *   Concrete works (Plain Cement Concrete (PCC) for foundation beds, RCC footings)
    *   Damp Proof Course (DPC)
    *   Plinth filling (Sand, gravel, etc.)
*   **Superstructure:**
    *   **RCC Works:**
        *   Columns
        *   Beams
        *   Slabs (Roof slab, floor slab if applicable)
        *   Lintels
        *   Staircases (if any)
        *   Chajjas (Sunshades)
    *   **Masonry Works:**
        *   Brickwork/Blockwork in walls (in cement mortar)
        *   Plastering (Internal and external)
        *   Pointing (if applicable)
    *   **Doors and Windows:**
        *   Frames (Wood, Steel, RCC)
        *   Shutters (Wood, Steel, Glass)
        *   Hardware
    *   **Finishing:**
        *   Flooring (Tiles, Concrete, etc.)
        *   Skirting
        *   Painting (Internal and External)
        *   Distempering
    *   **Roofing:**
        *   Waterproofing (if applicable)
        *   Roof finishes (e.g., China chips, tiles)
    *   **Sanitary and Plumbing Works:**
        *   Pipes, fittings, fixtures
        *   Soil, waste, and vent pipes
        *   Water supply pipes
        *   Sanitary appliances (WC, wash basins)
        *   Gratings, manholes
    *   **Electrical Works:**
        *   Wiring, conduits, junction boxes
        *   Switches, sockets, light fittings
        *   Fans, exhaust fans
        *   Distribution boards
*   **External Works:**
    *   Compound wall
    *   Paving
    *   Drainage

### 5. METHODS OF MEASUREMENT AND QUANTITY CALCULATION

Accurate quantity take-off is crucial. This involves breaking down each building element into measurable components and applying standard units of measurement.

**General Principles of Measurement:**

*   **Dimensioning:** All measurements are taken in length (L), breadth (B), and height/depth (H) or thickness (T).
*   **Units:** Standard units are used (e.g., cubic meters (m³), square meters (m²), linear meters (m), numbers (Nos.)).
*   **Deductions:** Deductions for openings (doors, windows) are typically made in wall sections. The method of deduction can vary based on SMM, but generally, deductions are made if the opening exceeds a certain size (e.g., 0.5 m²).
*   **Nettings:** Items are measured "net" unless otherwise specified, meaning deductions are made for openings.
*   **Naked Measurement:** Items are measured as they are without accounting for finishes.
*   **Gross Measurement:** Items are measured with finishes included.
*   **Rule of Thumb for Deductions:** For masonry and plaster, if an opening is less than 0.3 m², it's often not deducted. If it's between 0.3 m² and 1.0 m², it's deducted and measured in m². If it's greater than 1.0 m², it's deducted and measured in m². Specific SMM should be consulted.

### 6. EXAMPLES OF QUANTITY CALCULATION (Hypothetical Single-Storied Building)

Let's assume a simple rectangular single-storied RCC building with the following approximate dimensions:
*   Length: 10m
*   Width: 8m
*   Height of walls (plinth to soffit of roof slab): 3m
*   Wall thickness: 0.23m (230mm)
*   Roof slab thickness: 0.125m (125mm)
*   Footing dimensions: 1.5m x 1.5m x 0.4m (LxWxH)
*   Columns: 0.3m x 0.3m x 3m (LxWxH)
*   Beam dimensions: 0.23m x 0.45m (WxH)
*   Roof slab area: 10m x 8m = 80 m²

**A. Earthwork in Excavation for Footings:**

*   Assume footing trenches are 0.5m wide and 0.4m deep.
*   Perimeter of building walls = 2*(10 + 8) = 36m
*   Number of columns = 4 (at corners)
*   Footing excavation length: Assume external dimensions for footing trenches.
*   Total length of footing excavation: (10m + 8m + 10m + 8m) (external wall perimeter) + length around columns.
*   Simpler approach: Excavate for foundation trenches around the perimeter and pits for columns.
    *   For foundation trenches (under walls): Length = 36m, Width = 0.5m, Depth = 0.4m.
        *   Volume = 36m * 0.5m * 0.4m = 7.2 m³
    *   For column pits: Assume 1.5m x 1.5m area. Need to be precise about column locations and overlap with wall trenches. For simplicity, let's assume dedicated pits for columns: 4 pits * 1.5m * 1.5m * 0.4m = 3.6 m³.
*   **Total Excavation:** 7.2 m³ + 3.6 m³ = 10.8 m³ (This is a simplified example, actual calculations require detailed drawings).

**B. RCC Footings:**

*   Assume footings are 1.5m x 1.5m x 0.4m.
*   Volume per footing = 1.5m * 1.5m * 0.4m = 0.9 m³
*   Number of footings = 4
*   **Total RCC for Footings:** 4 * 0.9 m³ = 3.6 m³

**C. RCC Columns:**

*   Assume columns are 0.3m x 0.3m x 3m.
*   Volume per column = 0.3m * 0.3m * 3m = 0.27 m³
*   Number of columns = 4
*   **Total RCC for Columns:** 4 * 0.27 m³ = 1.08 m³

**D. RCC Beams (Roof Level):**

*   Assume beams run along the perimeter walls and possibly some internal beams.
*   Let's assume perimeter beams: Length = 2*(10m + 8m) = 36m.
*   Beam dimensions: 0.23m x 0.45m.
*   Volume of perimeter beams = 36m * 0.23m * 0.45m = 3.726 m³
*   **Total RCC for Beams:** 3.726 m³

**E. RCC Roof Slab:**

*   Area of roof slab = 10m * 8m = 80 m²
*   Thickness of slab = 0.125m
*   Volume of roof slab = 80 m² * 0.125m = 10 m³
*   **Total RCC for Slab:** 10 m³

**F. Brickwork in Walls:**

*   **Gross Area of Walls:**
    *   Perimeter = 36m
    *   Height = 3m
    *   Gross area = 36m * 3m = 108 m²
*   **Deductions for Openings:**
    *   Assume 2 doors: 1m x 2.1m each = 2 * 2.1 m² = 4.2 m²
    *   Assume 4 windows: 1.2m x 1.5m each = 4 * 1.8 m² = 7.2 m²
    *   Total openings = 4.2 m² + 7.2 m² = 11.4 m²
*   **Net Area of Walls:** 108 m² - 11.4 m² = 96.6 m²
*   **Volume of Brickwork:** Net Area * Thickness = 96.6 m² * 0.23m = 22.218 m³
*   **Total Brickwork:** 22.22 m³ (rounded)

**G. Plastering (Internal & External):**

*   **External Plaster:**
    *   Assume plaster thickness of 12mm (0.012m).
    *   Area to be plastered: Net wall area + area of plastered soffits of beams and slab.
    *   Area of soffits: Perimeter beams length * beam width + slab area.
        *   Beams soffit area = 36m * 0.23m = 8.28 m²
        *   Slab soffit area = 80 m²
    *   Total external plastered area = 96.6 m² (walls) + 8.28 m² (beam soffits) + 80 m² (slab soffit) = 184.88 m²
    *   Volume of external plaster = 184.88 m² * 0.012m = 2.218 m³
*   **Internal Plaster:**
    *   Assume internal walls are plastered on both sides, and internal soffits of beams and slab.
    *   Internal wall area = Same as net wall area, assuming internal walls are also 0.23m thick and plastered on both sides. For simplicity, let's consider the same net area.
    *   Internal plastered area = 96.6 m² (walls, both sides) + 8.28 m² (beam soffits) + 80 m² (slab soffit) = 264.88 m²
    *   Volume of internal plaster = 264.88 m² * 0.012m = 3.178 m³
*   **Total Plaster:** 2.22 m³ (external) + 3.18 m³ (internal) = 5.40 m³ (This calculation needs refinement based on specific wall types and finishes).

**H. Reinforcement Steel:**

*   Quantities of steel are usually estimated as a percentage of the concrete volume or calculated item by item based on reinforcement drawings.
*   For estimation purposes, a common approach is to assume a percentage of RCC volume (e.g., 1-2% for slabs and beams, 2-3% for columns and footings, depending on design).
*   Total RCC Volume ≈ 3.6 (footings) + 1.08 (columns) + 3.726 (beams) + 10 (slab) = 18.406 m³
*   Estimated Steel = 18.406 m³ * 1000 kg/m³ * 1.5% (approx) = 276 kg
*   **Total Reinforcement Steel:** 276 kg (This is a rough estimate; actual quantity take-off from reinforcement drawings is required).

### 7. FORMATTING A BOQ

A typical BOQ format includes:

*   **Page Number**
*   **Item Number:** Unique identifier for each item.
*   **Description of Item:** Detailed description of the work, including materials, dimensions, and specifications.
*   **Unit:** Standard unit of measurement (m³, m², m, Nos.).
*   **Quantity:** The calculated quantity for the item.
*   **Rate:** The price per unit of the item.
*   **Amount:** Quantity multiplied by Rate.

### 8. PRACTICE QUESTIONS AND EXERCISES

**Exercise 1:**

Calculate the quantity of PCC (1:5:10) for foundation beds for a single-storied RCC building.
*   Foundation trench dimensions: 0.5m wide, 0.4m deep.
*   External wall perimeter: 36m.
*   Assume no column pits are separately measured for PCC.
*   PCC bed width: 0.45m, PCC bed depth: 0.15m.

**Answer 1:**

*   Length of PCC bed = 36m
*   Width of PCC bed = 0.45m
*   Depth of PCC bed = 0.15m
*   Volume of PCC = 36m * 0.45m * 0.15m = **2.43 m³**

**Exercise 2:**

Calculate the net area of brickwork in m² for a wall section of 5m length, 3m height, with a door opening of 1m width and 2.1m height. Assume wall thickness is 0.23m.

**Answer 2:**

*   Gross area of wall = 5m * 3m = 15 m²
*   Area of door opening = 1m * 2.1m = 2.1 m²
*   Net area of brickwork = 15 m² - 2.1 m² = **12.9 m²**

**Exercise 3:**

A roof slab is 12m long, 10m wide, and 0.15m thick. Calculate the volume of concrete required for the slab.

**Answer 3:**

*   Length = 12m
*   Width = 10m
*   Thickness = 0.15m
*   Volume of slab = 12m * 10m * 0.15m = **18 m³**

### 9. IMPORTANT POINTS TO REMEMBER

*   **Accuracy:** Precision in measurement is paramount. Double-check all calculations.
*   **SMM Adherence:** Always refer to and follow the relevant Standard Method of Measurement for your region.
*   **Drawings:** The BOQ must be prepared based on detailed architectural, structural, and service drawings.
*   **Specifications:** Understand the project specifications for materials, workmanship, and finishes.
*   **Item Description:** Be clear and concise in describing each item to avoid ambiguity.
*   **Units:** Use correct and consistent units of measurement.
*   **Deductions and Additions:** Apply the correct rules for deductions (e.g., for openings) and any necessary additions.
*   **Preliminaries:** Do not neglect the importance of accurately costing preliminaries, as they can significantly impact the overall project cost.
*   **Contingency:** While not directly part of the BOQ items, understanding that a contingency is usually added to the total tender amount is important for cost management.
*   **Waste:** While rates often account for waste, understanding material wastage can help in cost control.
