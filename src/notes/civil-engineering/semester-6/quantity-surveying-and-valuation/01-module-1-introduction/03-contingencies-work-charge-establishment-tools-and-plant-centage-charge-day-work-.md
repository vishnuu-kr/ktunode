# Quantity Surveying and Valuation: Module 1 - Introduction

## Topic: Contingencies, Work-charge Establishment, Tools and Plant, Percentage Charge, Daywork, Prime Cost, Provisional Sum & Provisional Quantity, Overhead Charges, Cost Index, Contract Documents

---

### 1. Contingencies

**Key Concept:** Amounts added to an estimate to cover unforeseen or unpredictable costs that may arise during the execution of a project.

**Definition:** Contingencies are allowances for risks and uncertainties that cannot be precisely quantified at the time of estimating. They are essential for creating a realistic and financially sound project budget.

**Types of Contingencies:**

*   **General Contingencies:** A lump sum allowance for general unforeseen events that are not specific to any particular item of work.
*   **Specific Contingencies:** Allowances for specific known risks or uncertainties associated with particular elements of the project (e.g., difficult ground conditions, potential for material price fluctuations).

**Purpose:**

*   To absorb minor variations and unexpected costs.
*   To prevent the project from exceeding its budget due to unforeseen circumstances.
*   To provide a buffer for minor design changes or site conditions.

**How they are applied:**

*   Usually expressed as a percentage of the estimated cost of the work.
*   The percentage varies depending on the complexity, nature, and perceived risks of the project. Higher risk projects will have higher contingency percentages.

**Example:**

If a preliminary estimate for a building is $1,000,000 and a 5% contingency is applied, an additional $50,000 is added to the budget to cover unforeseen costs.

**Important Point to Remember:** Contingencies should not be used to cover poor estimating practices or deliberate omissions.

### 2. Work-Charge Establishment

**Key Concept:** Costs incurred for labor and supervision directly employed by the client or owner for specific tasks, often outside the main contractor's scope.

**Definition:** Work-charge establishment refers to the costs associated with employing a workforce and supervisory staff on a temporary basis to carry out work that is not part of a formal contract with a main contractor. This often includes site supervision, management, and direct labor employed by the client.

**Purpose:**

*   To manage and execute specific project tasks that are best handled by the client's own personnel.
*   To provide direct supervision and control over certain aspects of the project.
*   To facilitate works that are required before or after the main construction phase.

**Examples:**

*   Client's engineers and site supervisors.
*   Directly employed laborers for minor works or site preparation.
*   Security personnel for the construction site.
*   Temporary site offices and facilities for the client's team.

**How it's calculated:**

*   Includes wages, benefits, and associated overheads for the work-charged personnel.
*   May also include costs for materials and equipment used by the work-charged team.

**Important Point to Remember:** Costs are often directly allocated to the project and tracked separately from contractor costs.

### 3. Tools and Plant

**Key Concept:** The costs associated with providing and maintaining the machinery, equipment, and tools necessary for carrying out construction work.

**Definition:** Tools and Plant refer to all the equipment, machinery, vehicles, and tools required for the execution of construction activities. This includes both owned and hired equipment.

**Categories:**

*   **Small Tools:** Hand tools (hammers, saws, trowels), measuring tapes, spirit levels, etc.
*   **Powered Tools:** Drills, grinders, concrete mixers, welding machines, etc.
*   **Heavy Plant:** Excavators, cranes, bulldozers, concrete pumps, scaffolding, site hoardings, etc.

**Costing Methods:**

*   **Ownership Cost:** Depreciation, interest on capital, insurance, maintenance, repairs.
*   **Hiring Cost:** Rental charges for the period of use.
*   **Operating Cost:** Fuel, lubricants, operator wages, consumables.

**How it's applied in estimates:**

*   **Direct Allocation:** For specific items of plant used on particular tasks (e.g., crane hire for lifting structural elements).
*   **Percentage of Labour/Work:** A percentage is added to labour costs to cover the use of small tools and general plant.
*   **Lump Sum:** For specific projects where a particular set of plant is required.

**Example:**

The cost of hiring a concrete pump for a day, including operator and fuel, would be an item under "Tools and Plant."

**Important Point to Remember:** The efficient selection and utilization of tools and plant significantly impact project costs and productivity.

### 4. Percentage Charge (Centage Charge)

**Key Concept:** A charge added to the cost of labor, materials, or specific items of work to cover a portion of overheads, profit, or other indirect costs.

**Definition:** A percentage charge is a common method of adding a specific percentage to a cost base (e.g., the cost of materials, the cost of labor, or the value of a subcontract) to cover a range of expenses. It's often used in situations where direct costing of overheads is difficult or impractical.

**Purpose:**

*   To cover a portion of general overheads.
*   To include a contractor's profit margin.
*   To simplify the costing process for certain items.

**Examples:**

*   **Percentage on Materials:** A contractor might add 10% to the cost of purchased materials to cover handling, storage, and a small proportion of overheads.
*   **Percentage on Subcontractors:** A main contractor may add a percentage to the value of subcontract work to cover their administration, supervision, and profit on that portion of the work.
*   **Percentage on Labor:** Less common, but could be used to cover general supervision for labor-intensive tasks.

**Important Point to Remember:** The percentage applied should be reasonable and justifiable based on the nature of the work and the contractor's cost structure.

### 5. Daywork

**Key Concept:** A method of pricing work where the contractor is reimbursed for the actual cost of labor, materials, and plant used, plus an allowance for overheads and profit.

**Definition:** Daywork is a method of valuing work when the exact quantities or nature of the work cannot be ascertained at the time of measurement or when the work is of an indeterminate nature. The contractor is paid for the time spent by their workforce and the materials consumed.

**When it's used:**

*   When the scope of work is unclear or subject to frequent changes.
*   For minor works or repairs where detailed measurement is uneconomical.
*   When instructed by the Engineer or Architect to carry out work for which no rate exists.

**How it's calculated:**

*   **Labor:** Actual wages paid to the workmen, plus an allowance for employer's contributions and profit.
*   **Materials:** Actual cost of materials used, plus an allowance for wastage and profit.
*   **Plant:** Hiring charges of plant used, plus an allowance for running costs and profit.
*   **Overheads & Profit:** A percentage added to the total cost of labor, materials, and plant.

**Daywork Schedule:** Many contracts include a pre-agreed daywork schedule that specifies the rates for different trades, materials, and plant hire, along with the applicable percentage allowances for profit and overheads.

**Example:**

If a plumber spends 4 hours fixing a leaking pipe and the agreed daywork rate for a plumber is $50/hour, the labor cost would be $200. This would then have additions for materials, plant (if any), and profit/overheads.

**Important Point to Remember:** Daywork is generally discouraged for significant portions of work as it can lead to increased costs and less control. It should be used judiciously and with proper supervision.

### 6. Prime Cost (PC) Items

**Key Concept:** Items of work or materials where the cost is uncertain or the client wishes to select them at a later stage. The contractor is reimbursed the actual cost of the item plus an agreed percentage for profit and attendance.

**Definition:** A Prime Cost (PC) item is a specific item of work or supply for which the contractor is reimbursed the actual cost incurred in providing or carrying out that item, plus an agreed percentage for overheads, profit, and attendance.

**Purpose:**

*   To allow the client to choose specific high-value finishes or fittings (e.g., light fittings, sanitary ware, kitchen cabinets) at a later stage.
*   When the exact specification or supplier of an item is not finalized at the time of tender.
*   To provide flexibility in design or material selection.

**How it's handled:**

*   The contractor provides an estimated cost for the PC item in their tender.
*   The client then selects the actual item and supplier.
*   The contractor purchases the item and is reimbursed the actual cost paid to the supplier.
*   An agreed percentage (e.g., 10-20%) is added to the actual cost to cover the contractor's overheads, profit, and attendance (e.g., delivery, storage, installation).

**Example:**

A tender might include a PC Sum of $500 for a particular type of light fitting. The client then selects a light fitting that costs $700. The contractor will be paid $700 (cost of item) + $140 (20% PC sum allowance) = $840 for that item.

**Important Point to Remember:** The contractor should not add their percentage to the cost of items supplied by a nominated subcontractor unless explicitly agreed.

### 7. Provisional Sum (PS) & Provisional Quantity

**Key Concept:** An allowance or quantity included in the contract documents to cover work or materials that cannot be precisely defined or quantified at the time of tender.

**Definition:**

*   **Provisional Sum (PS):** A sum of money included in the contract to cover the cost of work or materials that are not fully detailed in the contract documents at the time of tender. It is essentially a placeholder for an item whose cost is not known.
*   **Provisional Quantity:** An estimated quantity of an item of work that is included in the Bills of Quantities (BoQ) because the exact quantity cannot be determined at the time of tender.

**Purpose:**

*   To allow the contract to proceed even when some elements are not fully defined.
*   To account for work that is subject to future decisions or site conditions.
*   To ensure that the project can be completed without major delays waiting for full details.

**How they are handled:**

*   **Provisional Sum:** The contractor includes the PS in their tender. When the detailed specification for the work is issued, the PS is either used to pay for the work done (if the cost matches the PS) or adjusted based on the actual cost of the work performed. The contractor is typically entitled to an addition for profit and attendance on the PS.
*   **Provisional Quantity:** The BoQ will indicate that the quantity is provisional. The contractor will be paid based on the actual quantity of work executed, measured on site, at the rate provided in the tender. Adjustments may be made for overheads and profit on the actual quantities.

**Example:**

*   **PS:** A sum of $10,000 might be included for "extra excavation and disposal of unforeseen rock" if the extent of rock is unknown.
*   **Provisional Quantity:** A BoQ might list "1,000 m³ (provisional) of concrete supply and place" for a foundation if the exact volume is not yet finalized.

**Important Point to Remember:** Both PS and provisional quantities require careful management and measurement on site to ensure fair valuation. The contract conditions will specify how these are to be treated.

### 8. Overhead Charges

**Key Concept:** Indirect costs incurred by a contractor that are not directly attributable to a specific project or work item, but are necessary for the overall operation of the business.

**Definition:** Overhead charges are the costs of running a business that are not directly associated with the production of a specific product or the execution of a particular construction task. These costs are essential for the business to function but need to be allocated across all projects.

**Types of Overhead Charges:**

*   **Direct Overheads (Site Overheads):** Costs incurred on the project site but not directly attributable to a specific work item.
    *   Site management and supervision (site manager, foremen).
    *   Site office expenses (rent, utilities, stationery).
    *   Temporary site facilities (toilets, first aid).
    *   Site security.
    *   Insurance for the project.
*   **Indirect Overheads (Head Office Overheads):** Costs incurred at the contractor's head office to support all projects.
    *   Salaries of directors, administrative staff, estimators, accountants.
    *   Office rent, utilities, maintenance.
    *   Marketing and business development.
    *   Professional fees (legal, accounting).
    *   Depreciation of head office assets.

**Purpose:**

*   To cover the cost of running the business.
*   To ensure the long-term sustainability of the company.

**How they are applied:**

*   **Percentage of Tender Price:** A common method where a percentage of the total contract value is added to cover head office overheads.
*   **Percentage of Direct Costs:** Overheads can be allocated as a percentage of direct labor, materials, or a combination of both.
*   **Specific Allocation:** For direct site overheads, they are often directly measured and included in the project cost.

**Example:**

A contractor's annual head office costs might be $500,000. If they undertake $5,000,000 worth of work in a year, their head office overhead rate could be considered 10% of turnover. This 10% would be factored into their pricing for each project.

**Important Point to Remember:** Accurate estimation of overheads is crucial for a contractor's profitability. They must be allocated fairly across all projects.

### 9. Cost Index

**Key Concept:** A numerical value used to adjust historical cost data to reflect current price levels, accounting for inflation and changes in material, labor, and plant costs.

**Definition:** A cost index is a statistical measure that tracks the change in the cost of a particular item or a basket of goods and services over time. In construction, cost indices are used to update estimated costs from different periods to a common base period, typically the current period.

**Purpose:**

*   To update historical cost data for use in current estimates.
*   To forecast future costs by projecting trends.
*   To compare costs of similar projects carried out at different times.
*   To measure the rate of inflation in the construction industry.

**How it's used:**

The formula for updating a cost is:

**Current Cost = Historical Cost × (Cost Index for Current Period / Cost Index for Historical Period)**

**Types of Cost Indices:**

*   **General Construction Cost Index:** Measures the overall change in construction costs.
*   **Specific Indices:** For specific materials (e.g., steel, cement), labor rates, or types of construction.

**Example:**

If a project in 2018 cost $1,000,000 and the construction cost index for 2018 was 120, and the index for the current year (2023) is 150, the estimated cost for a similar project today would be:

$1,000,000 \times (150 / 120) = $1,250,000$

This indicates that costs have increased by 25% since 2018.

**Important Point to Remember:** Cost indices provide a general indication of price changes. Specific market conditions for particular materials or labor can cause deviations.

### 10. Contract Documents (Brief Description Only)

**Key Concept:** The legally binding set of documents that define the rights, responsibilities, and obligations of the parties involved in a construction project.

**Definition:** Contract documents form the legal agreement between the client (employer) and the contractor. They clearly outline what is to be built, how it is to be built, the quality standards, the price, and the timeframe.

**Key Components (Brief Description):**

*   **Agreement/Contract Form:** The formal document signed by both parties, stating the key terms and referencing all other contract documents.
*   **Conditions of Contract:** The standard or bespoke clauses that govern the execution of the works, including terms related to payment, variations, delays, defects liability, termination, dispute resolution, etc. (e.g., FIDIC, NEC, JCT forms).
*   **Specifications:** Detailed written descriptions of the quality of materials, workmanship, and standards required for the project.
*   **Drawings:** Visual representations of the project, showing dimensions, layouts, details, and structural elements.
*   **Bills of Quantities (BoQ):** A priced document listing the quantities of materials and labor required for the project, used for tendering and valuation.
*   **Schedule of Rates:** A list of unit rates for various items of work, often used in cost-plus or remeasurement contracts.
*   **Tender Documents:** Documents submitted by potential contractors during the bidding process.
*   **Letters of Acceptance:** Formal acceptance of a tender.

**Purpose:**

*   To establish a clear and legally enforceable agreement.
*   To define the scope of work.
*   To set out the payment terms and procedures.
*   To manage risks and responsibilities.
*   To provide a basis for resolving disputes.

**Important Point to Remember:** All contract documents are considered integral parts of the agreement, and any inconsistencies must be resolved according to the contract's hierarchy of documents.

### Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Explain the purpose of contingencies in project cost estimation.
**Answer:** Contingencies are included in project estimates to cover unforeseen costs that may arise due to uncertainties, risks, or minor variations. They help to ensure that the project budget is realistic and can absorb unexpected expenses without derailing the project financially.

**Question 2:** What is the difference between Prime Cost (PC) items and Provisional Sums (PS)?
**Answer:**
*   **PC Items:** Relate to specific components or finishes that the client wishes to select later (e.g., light fittings). The contractor is reimbursed the actual cost of the selected item plus a percentage for profit and attendance.
*   **Provisional Sums (PS):** Are sums of money included for work that is not fully defined at tender. The PS is a placeholder, and the actual cost of the work done will be measured and valued, with the contractor typically entitled to profit and attendance.

**Question 3:** A contractor is hired to carry out urgent repairs to a damaged wall. The exact extent of damage is unknown. What pricing method is most likely to be used, and why?
**Answer:** Daywork is the most likely method. This is because the exact quantities of labor, materials, and time required are uncertain. Daywork allows the contractor to be reimbursed for the actual resources used, plus profit and overheads, ensuring fair payment for indeterminate work.

**Question 4:** Identify two types of overhead charges and provide an example for each.
**Answer:**
*   **Direct Overheads (Site Overheads):** Example: Salary of the site manager.
*   **Indirect Overheads (Head Office Overheads):** Example: Rent for the company's head office.

**Question 5:** If a construction cost index was 150 in 2020 and 180 in 2023, and a project cost $500,000 in 2020, what would be the estimated cost of a similar project in 2023, assuming only inflation is considered?
**Answer:**
Estimated Cost (2023) = Historical Cost (2020) × (Cost Index (2023) / Cost Index (2020))
Estimated Cost (2023) = $500,000 \times (180 / 150)$
Estimated Cost (2023) = $500,000 \times 1.20$
Estimated Cost (2023) = $600,000$

### Summary of Key Takeaways

*   **Contingencies** provide a buffer for unforeseen costs, crucial for realistic budgeting.
*   **Work-charge establishment** covers client-employed personnel on site.
*   **Tools and Plant** encompass all machinery and equipment costs.
*   **Percentage Charge** is a simplified way to add overheads/profit to specific costs.
*   **Daywork** is used for indeterminate work, reimbursing actual costs plus profit.
*   **Prime Cost (PC) Items** allow client selection of materials/fittings later.
*   **Provisional Sums (PS)** and **Provisional Quantities** cater to work not fully defined at tender.
*   **Overhead Charges** are indirect business costs necessary for operations.
*   **Cost Indices** adjust historical costs for inflation.
*   **Contract Documents** legally define project scope, terms, and responsibilities.
