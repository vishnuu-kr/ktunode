# QUANTITY SURVEYING AND VALUATION - Module 1: Introduction

## Topic: Introduction to Quantity Surveying & Basic Principles, Role/Responsibility of a Quantity Surveyor at Various Stages of Construction

---

### **1. Introduction to Quantity Surveying**

Quantity Surveying is a profession within the construction industry that deals with the management of costs and contracts. It involves the measurement and valuation of construction work, as well as the administration of building contracts.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Cost Management:** The primary objective of quantity surveying is to ensure that construction projects are completed within budget and to the required quality standards.
*   **Contract Administration:** Quantity Surveyors play a crucial role in ensuring that construction projects are carried out according to the terms and conditions of the contract.
*   **Resource Management:** This includes managing materials, labor, and plant to optimize efficiency and minimize waste.
*   **Risk Management:** Identifying and mitigating potential cost and contractual risks.

---

### **2. Basic Principles of Quantity Surveying**

The practice of quantity surveying is built upon a foundation of core principles that guide its operations.

**Key Principles:**

*   **Accuracy and Precision:** All measurements, calculations, and valuations must be carried out with the utmost accuracy. Even small errors can have significant financial implications.
    *   **Example:** Inaccurate measurement of concrete volume can lead to over-ordering or under-ordering, resulting in wasted materials or delays and additional costs.
*   **Objectivity and Impartiality:** Quantity Surveyors must provide professional advice and valuations without bias towards any party involved in the project (client, contractor, designer).
    *   **Example:** A Quantity Surveyor must assess the value of variations impartially, considering the cost of labor, materials, and profit regardless of who requests the variation.
*   **Thoroughness and Completeness:** All aspects of the project, from initial design to final account, must be considered and accounted for.
    *   **Example:** When preparing a bill of quantities, a QS must ensure all elements of the construction are measured and described, including excavation, foundation work, structural elements, finishes, and services.
*   **Adherence to Standards and Codes:** Quantity Surveyors must follow established measurement standards (e.g., SMM, NRM) and relevant building codes and regulations.
    *   **Example:** Using the correct rules of measurement for brickwork (e.g., how to measure openings) is crucial for accurate billing.
*   **Efficiency and Economy:** Striving to achieve the project objectives in the most cost-effective manner.
    *   **Example:** Suggesting alternative materials or construction methods that achieve the same functional outcome at a lower cost, while maintaining quality.
*   **Fairness and Equity:** Ensuring that all parties are treated fairly in terms of payment and contractual obligations.
    *   **Example:** Fairly valuing provisional sums and prime cost sums based on actual costs incurred plus agreed markups.

### **3. Role and Responsibilities of a Quantity Surveyor at Various Stages of Construction**

The Quantity Surveyor's involvement spans the entire lifecycle of a construction project, from inception to completion and beyond.

#### **3.1. Pre-Construction Stage (Feasibility & Design)**

This is the conceptual and planning phase where the project's viability and design are developed.

*   **Feasibility Studies & Cost Planning:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Estimating the likely cost of the project based on preliminary designs and specifications. This helps the client determine if the project is financially viable.
    *   **Activities:** Developing cost plans, advising on the cost implications of design choices, identifying potential cost-saving measures.
    *   **Example:** Providing a cost estimate for a proposed office building based on building type, size, and basic finishes to determine if it fits within the client's budget.
*   **Cost Estimating:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Preparing more detailed cost estimates as the design progresses.
    *   **Activities:** Developing elemental cost estimates, unit cost estimates.
*   **Procurement Advice:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Advising the client on the most suitable procurement route and contract strategy.
    *   **Activities:** Recommending contract types (e.g., lump sum, cost reimbursement), advising on tendering procedures.
    *   **Example:** Recommending a design-and-build contract for a project where speed is critical and the client wants to transfer design risk to the contractor.
*   **Preparation of Bills of Quantities (BoQ) / Schedule of Rates / Tender Documentation:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Measuring and describing all the work required for the project according to standard measurement rules. This forms a crucial part of the tender package.
    *   **Activities:** Detailing quantities of materials, labor, and plant needed for each construction element.
    *   **Example:** In a BoQ, specifying the volume of concrete required for foundations, the number of bricks for walls, the area of plastering, and the length of pipework.
*   **Tender Evaluation:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Analyzing tenders received from contractors to ensure they are technically compliant and financially sound.
    *   **Activities:** Checking for completeness, comparing prices, identifying anomalies, preparing tender analysis reports.
    *   **Example:** Comparing the price of concrete work from different contractors, ensuring they have priced the same quantities and specifications.

#### **3.2. Construction Stage (Execution)**

This is the phase where the physical building work takes place.

*   **Contract Administration:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Managing the financial and contractual aspects of the project during construction.
    *   **Activities:** Issuing instructions, managing variations, assessing interim payment applications, administering the contract.
*   **Valuation of Work Done (Interim Valuations):**
    *   **Responsibility:** Assessing the value of work completed by the contractor at regular intervals (usually monthly) for interim payments.
    *   **Activities:** Measuring work in situ, valuing materials on site, applying percentages for retention.
    *   **Example:** Valuing the concrete poured, walls built, and finishes applied in a given month.
*   **Management of Variations:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Assessing the cost and time implications of any changes or additions to the original contract scope.
    *   **Activities:** Analyzing contractor's claims for variations, negotiating rates, issuing variation orders.
    *   **Example:** Valuing the extra cost of using a different type of brick specified by the architect during construction.
*   **Claims Management:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Evaluating and negotiating contractor's claims for extensions of time and additional costs arising from unforeseen events or client-caused delays.
    *   **Activities:** Reviewing substantiation for claims, negotiating settlement.
*   **Cost Control and Monitoring:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Tracking project expenditure against the budget and reporting on cost performance.
    *   **Activities:** Updating cost plans, forecasting final costs, identifying potential cost overruns.
    *   **Example:** Alerting the client if the cost of the building services is exceeding the planned budget.
*   **Management of Site Records:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Maintaining accurate records of all financial and contractual matters on site.
    *   **Activities:** Keeping records of site instructions, variations, payment certificates, correspondence.

#### **3.3. Post-Construction Stage (Completion & Finalization)**

This phase involves the handover of the project and the final settlement of accounts.

*   **Final Account Preparation and Negotiation:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Agreeing the final contract sum with the contractor, taking into account all interim payments, variations, and claims.
    *   **Activities:** Reconciling all financial records, negotiating final figures.
    *   **Example:** Agreeing the final payment to the contractor after all works are completed and certified, including all approved variations.
*   **Defects Liability Period Administration:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Managing the contractor's obligations during the defects liability period (the period after completion where the contractor is responsible for rectifying defects).
    *   **Activities:** Inspecting the works for defects, instructing the contractor to rectify them.
*   **Dispute Resolution:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Providing expert advice and evidence in case of disputes between the client and contractor, potentially involving arbitration or litigation.
    *   **Activities:** Preparing reports, attending meetings, giving expert testimony.
*   **Post-Project Review:**
    *   **Responsibility:** Analyzing the project's cost performance to inform future projects.
    *   **Activities:** Preparing cost analyses, identifying lessons learned.

### **5. Practice Questions & Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Explain the fundamental importance of **accuracy and precision** in the work of a Quantity Surveyor. Provide a brief example to illustrate your answer.

**Question 2:**
Describe the key responsibilities of a Quantity Surveyor during the **pre-construction stage** of a building project.

**Question 3:**
What is the primary role of a Quantity Surveyor during the **construction stage**? List at least three specific responsibilities.

**Question 4:**
Why is the **final account negotiation** a critical activity for a Quantity Surveyor in the post-construction phase?

---

### **6. Answers to Practice Questions**

**Answer 1:**
Accuracy and precision are fundamental because even minor errors in measurement or calculation can lead to significant financial discrepancies. This could result in cost overruns for the client, underpayment to the contractor, or disputes.
**Example:** If a QS incorrectly measures the area of wall plastering by underestimating the length of a wall by just one meter, it could lead to a minor undercharge on the bill of quantities. Over many such errors, or if the error is larger, it could lead to substantial financial loss or gain, impacting project profitability and client satisfaction.

**Answer 2:**
During the pre-construction stage, a Quantity Surveyor's key responsibilities include:
*   **Feasibility Studies & Cost Planning:** Estimating project costs to assess financial viability.
*   **Procurement Advice:** Recommending the best contract and procurement strategy.
*   **Preparation of Tender Documentation:** Measuring and describing all works in Bills of Quantities or similar documents.
*   **Tender Evaluation:** Analyzing received tenders for compliance and financial soundness.

**Answer 3:**
During the construction stage, the primary role of a Quantity Surveyor is **contract administration and financial management**. Specific responsibilities include:
*   Valuing work done for interim payments.
*   Managing variations to the contract.
*   Assessing contractor's claims.
*   Controlling project costs against the budget.
*   Maintaining site records.

**Answer 4:**
The final account negotiation is critical because it represents the **final agreed cost of the project**. It ensures all financial aspects are resolved, including payments for work done, variations, and claims. A fair and accurate final account prevents future disputes, provides closure for the project, and allows for final financial reporting and budget reconciliation. It is the culmination of the QS's cost management efforts throughout the project.

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **QS is a cost professional:** The core function is to manage and control costs.
*   **Confidentiality:** QS information is confidential and should not be disclosed without authorization.
*   **Continuous learning:** The construction industry and regulations are constantly evolving, requiring QS to stay updated.
*   **Communication is key:** Effective communication with clients, contractors, designers, and other stakeholders is vital.
*   **Ethical conduct:** Professional integrity and ethical behavior are paramount.
*   **Measurement Standards:** Familiarity with relevant measurement standards (e.g., SMM, NRM) is essential.
