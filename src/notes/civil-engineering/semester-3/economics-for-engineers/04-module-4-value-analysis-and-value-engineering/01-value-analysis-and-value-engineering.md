## Module 4: Value Analysis and Value Engineering

### Topic: Value Analysis and Value Engineering

---

### **1. Introduction to Value Analysis (VA) and Value Engineering (VE)**

**1.1 What is Value?**

*   **Definition:** Value is the ratio of function to cost.
    *   **Value = Function / Cost**
*   **Key takeaway:** To improve value, we can either:
    *   Increase the function while keeping cost the same.
    *   Decrease the cost while keeping function the same.
    *   Increase the function and decrease the cost simultaneously.
    *   Increase the function disproportionately more than the cost increases.

**1.2 Value Analysis (VA)**

*   **Definition:** VA is a systematic, organized approach to providing the necessary functions in a product, process, or service at the lowest possible cost without sacrificing quality, performance, or reliability.
*   **Focus:** VA is typically applied to **existing products, processes, or services** that are already in production or use. It aims to improve their value through redesign or modification.
*   **When it's used:**
    *   During the product's life cycle, after it has been developed and is in production.
    *   When there are cost reduction targets or opportunities identified.
    *   When customer feedback suggests areas for improvement.

**1.3 Value Engineering (VE)**

*   **Definition:** VE is a systematic, organized approach to providing the necessary functions in a product, process, or service at the lowest possible cost, **throughout its entire life cycle**, from conception to disposal, without sacrificing quality, performance, or reliability.
*   **Focus:** VE is applied **early in the design and development phase** of a new product, process, or service. It aims to incorporate value-driven decisions from the outset.
*   **When it's used:**
    *   During the conceptualization and design stages.
    *   To prevent problems and optimize design before significant resources are committed.
    *   To ensure the most cost-effective and functional design is chosen.

**1.4 Relationship between VA and VE**

*   Both VA and VE share the same fundamental goal: **maximizing value by optimizing the function-to-cost ratio.**
*   The primary difference is the **timing of application**:
    *   **VE:** Applied during the design/development phase (proactive).
    *   **VA:** Applied to existing products/processes (reactive or continuous improvement).
*   Think of VE as "designing for value" and VA as "improving value of what exists."

### **2. The Value Analysis/Engineering Process (The FAST System)**

The most common methodology for conducting VA/VE is the **Function Analysis System Technique (FAST)**. The FAST system is a structured approach that helps identify and analyze functions, their interrelationships, and opportunities for cost reduction. It typically involves the following phases:

**Phase 1: Information Gathering**

*   **Objective:** To thoroughly understand the product, process, or service.
*   **Activities:**
    *   Gather all relevant documentation (blueprints, specifications, cost data, customer feedback, market research).
    *   Understand the product's purpose and how it is used.
    *   Identify all costs associated with the product/process.
    *   Understand the target market and customer requirements.
    *   Interview key personnel (designers, manufacturers, marketers, customers).

**Phase 2: Function Analysis**

*   **Objective:** To identify and define the essential and secondary functions of the product/process.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Function:** What a product, part, or process *does*. It's a verb-noun statement.
    *   **Basic Function:** The primary purpose for which the product/process exists. Without this function, the product/process would not serve its intended purpose. (e.g., "Transmit Torque" for a gear).
    *   **Secondary Function:** Functions that support the basic function or are necessary for the product's operation, but are not its primary purpose. (e.g., "Support Shaft" for a gear).
    *   **Esteem Function:** Functions that add to the aesthetic appeal or user satisfaction, but are not essential for performance. (e.g., "Provide Shiny Appearance" for a chrome-plated part).
*   **FAST Diagramming:**
    *   A visual tool used to map out the relationships between functions.
    *   **How it works:**
        *   Start with the primary or basic function.
        *   Arrange functions chronologically or logically based on their relationships.
        *   Ask "How?" to move forward (e.g., How does "Transmit Torque"? By "Rotating").
        *   Ask "Why?" to move backward (e.g., Why transmit torque? To "Move Vehicle").
        *   The diagram typically progresses from "How" (immediate actions) to "Why" (ultimate purpose).
    *   **Purpose of FAST:**
        *   Clearly defines all functions.
        *   Identifies dependencies between functions.
        *   Helps distinguish between basic, secondary, and esteem functions.
        *   Highlights areas where functions might be redundant or can be combined.

**Phase 3: Creative Thinking / Brainstorming**

*   **Objective:** To generate as many alternative ways as possible to perform the identified functions, with a focus on reducing cost or improving function.
*   **Techniques:**
    *   Brainstorming sessions with a multidisciplinary team.
    *   Attribute listing.
    *   Morphological analysis.
    *   Random stimulation.
*   **Key Principle:** Quantity over quality in this phase. No idea is too wild or impractical at this stage.

**Phase 4: Evaluation and Selection**

*   **Objective:** To evaluate the generated ideas and select the most promising ones for further development.
*   **Criteria for Evaluation:**
    *   **Cost Savings:** How much cost can be reduced?
    *   **Functionality:** Does the alternative maintain or improve the required function?
    *   **Quality/Performance:** Does it meet or exceed quality standards?
    *   **Reliability:** Is the alternative reliable?
    *   **Manufacturability/Feasibility:** Can it be easily produced or implemented?
    *   **Market Acceptance:** Will customers accept the change?
    *   **Technical Risk:** What are the risks involved in implementing the change?
*   **Methods:**
    *   Cost-benefit analysis.
    *   Scoring models.
    *   Expert judgment.

**Phase 5: Development and Refinement**

*   **Objective:** To develop the selected ideas into detailed proposals.
*   **Activities:**
    *   Detailed design changes.
    *   Material selection.
    *   Process modifications.
    *   Prototyping and testing.
    *   Cost estimation for the proposed changes.
    *   Preparation of justification and recommendations.

**Phase 6: Presentation and Implementation**

*   **Objective:** To present the proposals to decision-makers and implement the approved changes.
*   **Activities:**
    *   Formal presentation of the findings and recommendations.
    *   Obtaining approval for implementation.
    *   Executing the approved changes (e.g., updating drawings, revising manufacturing processes).
    *   Monitoring the implementation and its impact.

**Phase 7: Follow-up and Verification**

*   **Objective:** To verify that the implemented changes have achieved the desired results (cost savings, improved function, etc.).
*   **Activities:**
    *   Tracking actual costs and comparing them to estimates.
    *   Monitoring product performance and quality.
    *   Gathering feedback on the changes.
    *   Documenting lessons learned.

### **3. Key Concepts and Techniques in VA/VE**

**3.1 Function Definition (Verb-Noun)**

*   **Importance:** Precise function definition is crucial for effective analysis.
*   **Rules:**
    *   Always use a verb and a noun (e.g., "Support Weight," "Conduct Electricity," "Limit Rotation").
    *   Avoid ambiguous or imprecise terms.
*   **Example:** Instead of "Hold," use "Support Weight" or "Contain Fluid."

**3.2 Cost Analysis**

*   **Objective:** To understand where the costs are incurred and identify areas for reduction.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Cost Breakdown:** Analyzing costs by component, operation, material, labor, overhead.
    *   **Life Cycle Costing:** Considering all costs associated with a product from inception to disposal.
    *   **Target Costing:** Setting a cost target based on market price and desired profit margin.

**3.3 Brainstorming Techniques**

*   **Brainstorming:** Free association of ideas by a group.
*   **Brainwriting:** Individuals write down ideas before sharing, reducing groupthink.
*   **Reverse Brainstorming:** Asking "How can we make this product fail?" to identify potential problems and then reverse those to find solutions.
*   **Attribute Listing:** Listing all attributes of a product and then thinking of ways to improve each attribute.

**3.4 Standard VA/VE Questions**

These questions are used throughout the process to stimulate thinking:

*   What is the function?
*   What is the cost?
*   What is the total cost?
*   What is the cost of the function?
*   What is the value of the function?
*   What else can do this function?
*   Can we use a standard part?
*   Can we use a less expensive material?
*   Can we simplify the design?
*   Can we combine parts?
*   Can we eliminate parts?
*   Can we use a different manufacturing process?
*   Can we change the design to make it easier to manufacture?
*   Can we improve the reliability or performance?
*   Will this change be accepted by the customer?

### **4. Benefits of Value Analysis and Value Engineering**

*   **Cost Reduction:** The most prominent benefit, leading to increased profitability and competitiveness.
*   **Improved Functionality and Performance:** Alternatives may offer better performance or new features.
*   **Enhanced Product Quality and Reliability:** Focusing on functions can lead to more robust designs.
*   **Streamlined Manufacturing Processes:** Simplification and standardization can reduce production complexity.
*   **Increased Customer Satisfaction:** Products that better meet customer needs at a lower price.
*   **Competitive Advantage:** Offering superior value in the marketplace.
*   **Innovation:** Encourages creative problem-solving and new approaches.
*   **Reduced Waste:** Identifying and eliminating unnecessary costs and materials.

### **5. Examples of VA/VE in Engineering**

*   **Automotive Industry:**
    *   **Example:** Redesigning a car seat mechanism to reduce the number of parts and assembly time, while maintaining comfort and safety. This could involve using fewer screws, integrating components, or using a different fastening method.
    *   **Outcome:** Lower manufacturing cost, reduced weight, faster assembly.
*   **Electronics Industry:**
    *   **Example:** Simplifying the circuit board design for a consumer electronic device by using a higher integration chip that performs the functions of multiple smaller chips.
    *   **Outcome:** Reduced component count, smaller product size, potentially lower assembly cost, and improved reliability.
*   **Construction Industry:**
    *   **Example:** Value engineering a building's facade to use a more cost-effective cladding material that provides the same aesthetic appeal and weather resistance as the original proposed material.
    *   **Outcome:** Significant cost savings for the project without compromising the building's appearance or performance.
*   **Packaging Industry:**
    *   **Example:** Analyzing the packaging for a food product to reduce the amount of plastic used, change the material to a more recyclable option, or redesign the shape for more efficient shipping.
    *   **Outcome:** Reduced material cost, improved environmental impact, and more efficient logistics.

### **6. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Define Value in the context of Value Analysis and Value Engineering.
**Answer:**
Value is defined as the ratio of Function to Cost. Value = Function / Cost. This means that for a given cost, a higher function is better value, and for a given function, a lower cost is better value.

---

**Question 2:**
What is the fundamental difference between Value Analysis (VA) and Value Engineering (VE)?
**Answer:**
The fundamental difference lies in the timing of their application. Value Engineering (VE) is applied proactively during the design and development phase of a new product or process, aiming to build value in from the start. Value Analysis (VA) is applied reactively or as a continuous improvement activity to existing products, processes, or services that are already in production or use, aiming to improve their existing value.

---

**Question 3:**
List the main phases of the Value Analysis/Engineering process (FAST).
**Answer:**
The main phases are:
1.  Information Gathering
2.  Function Analysis
3.  Creative Thinking / Brainstorming
4.  Evaluation and Selection
5.  Development and Refinement
6.  Presentation and Implementation
7.  Follow-up and Verification

**Question 5 (Scenario-based):**
A company manufactures a plastic component for an appliance. The current component costs $2.50 to produce and performs its intended function adequately. A Value Analysis team has identified that 30% of the cost is due to a complex internal ribbing structure that adds rigidity but might be over-engineered. The team proposes a redesign that eliminates half of the ribbing, reducing material cost by $0.50 and manufacturing time by 10%. The redesigned component is tested and found to still meet all functional and quality requirements.

Calculate the original value and the new value of the component, assuming the "functionality score" remains the same. What is the percentage improvement in value?

**Answer:**
Let's assign an arbitrary "functionality score" of 10 to the original component.

*   **Original Value:**
    *   Functionality Score = 10
    *   Cost = $2.50
    *   Original Value = Functionality / Cost = 10 / $2.50 = 4

*   **New Value:**
    *   Functionality Score = 10 (remains the same)
    *   New Cost = $2.50 - $0.50 = $2.00
    *   New Value = Functionality / Cost = 10 / $2.00 = 5

*   **Percentage Improvement in Value:**
    *   Improvement = New Value - Original Value = 5 - 4 = 1
    *   Percentage Improvement = (Improvement / Original Value) * 100
    *   Percentage Improvement = (1 / 4) * 100 = 25%

The component has shown a 25% improvement in value due to the cost reduction.

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Value is Function/Cost:** Always keep this ratio in mind.
*   **Focus on Functions, Not Parts:** Analyze what a part *does*, not just what it *is*.
*   **Multidisciplinary Teams are Key:** Diverse perspectives lead to better solutions.
*   **Brainstorming is Crucial:** Generate many ideas before evaluating.
*   **VE is Proactive, VA is Reactive:** Apply VE early in design; use VA for existing items.
*   **Don't Sacrifice Quality for Cost:** The goal is to maintain or improve function and quality while reducing cost.
*   **Customer Needs Drive Value:** Understand what the customer truly needs and values.
*   **Systematic Approach:** Follow a structured process like FAST for best results.
