# SURVEY LAB - Module 13: Study of Instruments

## Topic: Automatic Level

---

### 1. Introduction to Automatic Levels

**What is an Automatic Level?**

An automatic level, also known as a self-leveling level or auto-level, is a surveying instrument used to establish a horizontal line of sight. It automatically corrects for any slight tilt or inclination of the instrument, ensuring that the line of sight remains perfectly horizontal, regardless of minor disturbances. This makes it significantly faster and more efficient for leveling tasks compared to older dumpy levels that required manual leveling with leveling screws.

**Purpose of Automatic Levels in Surveying:**

*   **Establishing Horizontal Lines:** The primary function is to provide a stable, horizontal line of sight for precise vertical measurements.
*   **Determining Elevation Differences:** Used to measure the difference in elevation between two points.
*   **Setting Out Gradients:** Can be used to set out specific slopes or gradients for construction projects.
*   **Controlling Building Work:** Essential for ensuring that foundations, floors, and walls are level and at the correct elevations.

### 2. Key Concepts and Principles

**The Automatic Leveling Mechanism (Compensator):**

The core of an automatic level is its **compensator**. This is a device that automatically brings the line of sight to a horizontal position.

*   **Types of Compensators:**
    *   **Pendulum Compensator (Most Common):** This system uses a pendulum suspended by a prism or mirror. As the instrument tilts, gravity acts on the pendulum, and the prism/mirror shifts to deflect the line of sight back to a horizontal position.
        *   **Prism/Mirror System:** A prism or mirror is attached to the pendulum. Light from the telescope passes through or reflects off this prism/mirror.
        *   **Damping:** To prevent oscillations of the pendulum due to vibrations or wind, a damping mechanism is employed. This can be:
            *   **Air Damping:** Uses air resistance to slow down the pendulum.
            *   **Magnetic Damping:** Uses magnetic forces to slow down the pendulum.
    *   **Other Compensator Types (Less Common in Modern Levels):**
        *   **Fluid-filled compensators:** Utilize liquid viscosity to dampen movement.

*   **Compensator Range:** The automatic level can only compensate for a certain range of tilt (e.g., ±15 arcminutes or ±0.25 degrees). If the instrument is tilted beyond this range, the leveling will not be automatic, and the instrument may indicate an error or provide an incorrect reading.

**Line of Sight:**

*   The imaginary line projected through the telescope of the surveying instrument.
*   In an automatic level, this line is always horizontal due to the compensator.

**Stadia Method:**

Most automatic levels incorporate stadia hairs within the reticle (crosshairs). These are used for distance measurement and height of instrument (HI) calculations:

*   **Stadia Hairs:** Two horizontal lines above and below the central horizontal crosshair.
*   **Stadia Interval Factor:** The ratio between the distance measured by the stadia hairs and the actual distance. For most modern levels, this factor is 100.
    *   **Formula:** Distance = (Stadia Interval) x (Stadia Interval Factor)
    *   **Stadia Interval:** The difference between the readings on the leveling staff at the upper and lower stadia hairs.

**Leveling Staff (Rod):**

*   A graduated rod used to measure vertical distances.
*   Readings are taken by the surveyor using the automatic level.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Invar Staffs:** Made of Invar (a nickel-iron alloy) which has a very low coefficient of thermal expansion, making them highly accurate, especially for precise leveling.
    *   **Wooden or Fiberglass Staffs:** More common for general surveying work.

### 3. Components of an Automatic Level

*   **Telescope:** Magnifies the view of the leveling staff and contains the reticle.
    *   **Objective Lens:** Gathers light and forms an image.
    *   **Eyepiece:** Allows the surveyor to view the magnified image and the reticle.
    *   **Focusing Knob:** Adjusts the sharpness of the image.
*   **Reticle (Crosshairs):** The aiming mark within the telescope.
    *   **Horizontal Crosshair:** Used for aiming at the staff.
    *   **Stadia Hairs:** Used for distance measurement.
*   **Compensator Unit:** Houses the pendulum and damping mechanism.
*   **Leveling Screws:** Used for initial coarse leveling of the instrument, allowing the compensator to function.
*   **Circular Bubble (Level Vial):** A small circular bubble used for initial approximate leveling.
*   **Mirror for Circular Bubble:** Allows the surveyor to see the circular bubble while looking through the eyepiece.
*   **Tripod:** A three-legged stand that supports the instrument, providing stability.
*   **Carrying Case:** For protection and transport.

### 4. Working Principles and Procedures

**Basic Principle of Leveling with an Automatic Level:**

1.  **Set up the Automatic Level:** Mount the instrument on a stable tripod over a point where readings are needed.
2.  **Initial Leveling:** Use the leveling screws and the circular bubble to bring the instrument to an approximately level position. The automatic compensator will then take over and ensure the line of sight is truly horizontal.
3.  **Sight the Leveling Staff:** Direct the telescope towards the leveling staff held vertically at a known or unknown point.
4.  **Take Readings:** Read the staff through the eyepiece. The reading on the horizontal crosshair indicates the height of the line of sight above the base of the staff.

**Common Leveling Methods:**

*   **Differential Leveling:** Used to determine the difference in elevation between two points. This is the most common application.
    *   **Setup:**
        *   Set up the automatic level on a tripod approximately midway between two points (A and B).
        *   Ensure the instrument is stable and the circular bubble is centered.
    *   **Procedure:**
        1.  **Backsight (BS):** Have a leveling staff held vertically at point A (known or arbitrary benchmark). Sight the staff and record the reading on the horizontal crosshair. This is the backsight reading.
        2.  **Foresight (FS):** Have the leveling staff held vertically at point B (unknown elevation). Sight the staff and record the reading on the horizontal crosshair. This is the foresight reading.
        3.  **Change Point:** If the distance to point B is too great for a single setup, or if the line of sight is obstructed, a "change point" is established. The staff is placed at the last foresight point (B), and the instrument is moved to a new location. A backsight is taken on the staff at B from the new instrument position.
    *   **Calculations:**
        *   **Elevation of Instrument (HI) = Elevation of Previous Point + Backsight Reading**
        *   **Elevation of New Point = HI - Foresight Reading**
        *   **Elevation Difference = Backsight Reading - Foresight Reading** (if taken from the same setup)

*   **Profile Leveling:** Used to determine elevations along a line, such as the centerline of a road or pipeline. This involves taking a series of readings along the profile line at regular intervals.

*   **Cross-Sectional Leveling:** Used to determine elevations across a line, such as the width of a road or embankment.

### 5. Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages:**

*   **Speed and Efficiency:** The automatic leveling mechanism significantly reduces the time spent on manual leveling compared to dumpy levels.
*   **Ease of Use:** Simpler to operate once set up, as it requires less attention to the leveling screws during observation.
*   **Accuracy:** Capable of providing high accuracy for most standard surveying tasks when used correctly.
*   **Reduced Observer Error:** Minimizes the risk of errors caused by improper manual leveling.

**Limitations:**

*   **Compensator Range:** Cannot compensate for tilts exceeding its specified range. If the instrument is tilted too much, the readings will be inaccurate.
*   **Vibrations and Disturbances:** Sensitive to vibrations (e.g., from nearby traffic or wind), which can affect the compensator's performance and cause readings to fluctuate.
*   **Cost:** Generally more expensive than manual leveling instruments.
*   **Maintenance:** The complex internal mechanism (compensator) may require more specialized maintenance.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**

What is the primary function of the compensator in an automatic level?

**Answer:** The primary function of the compensator is to automatically correct for any slight tilt of the instrument, ensuring that the line of sight remains perfectly horizontal.

**Question 2:**

An automatic level has a compensator range of ±15 arcminutes. If the instrument is tilted by 20 arcminutes, what can be expected regarding the accuracy of the readings?

**Answer:** If the instrument is tilted by 20 arcminutes, which is beyond the compensator's range of ±15 arcminutes, the readings will likely be inaccurate because the automatic leveling mechanism will not be able to fully correct the tilt.

**Question 3:**

You are performing differential leveling. Your first setup is over point X. You take a backsight on a benchmark (BM) with elevation 150.00 m and read 1.250 m on the staff. You then take a foresight on point Y and read 0.850 m on the staff. What is the elevation of point Y?

**Answer:**

*   Height of Instrument (HI) = Elevation of BM + Backsight Reading
*   HI = 150.00 m + 1.250 m = 151.250 m

*   Elevation of Point Y = HI - Foresight Reading
*   Elevation of Point Y = 151.250 m - 0.850 m = 150.400 m

**Question 4:**

If the upper stadia hair reads 1.500 m and the lower stadia hair reads 0.900 m on a leveling staff, and the stadia interval factor is 100, what is the horizontal distance to the staff?

**Answer:**

*   Stadia Interval = Upper Stadia Reading - Lower Stadia Reading
*   Stadia Interval = 1.500 m - 0.900 m = 0.600 m

*   Distance = Stadia Interval x Stadia Interval Factor
*   Distance = 0.600 m x 100 = 60.00 m

**Question 5:**

List two advantages of using an automatic level over a manual leveling instrument.

**Answer:**
1.  **Speed and Efficiency:** Significantly faster due to automatic leveling.
2.  **Ease of Use:** Requires less effort in maintaining level sight during observations.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Always check the compensator range.** Never tilt the instrument beyond this limit.
*   **Ensure the instrument is stable on the tripod.** Vibrations are the enemy of accurate leveling.
*   **Use the circular bubble for initial coarse leveling.** The compensator handles the fine adjustments.
*   **Keep the leveling staff plumb (vertical).** This is crucial for accurate readings, regardless of the instrument.
*   **For precise work, use an Invar staff.**
*   **Understand the difference between backsights and foresights.**
*   **When calculating elevations, always use the HI (Height of Instrument) properly.**
*   **Regularly clean the lenses and the compensator unit.**
