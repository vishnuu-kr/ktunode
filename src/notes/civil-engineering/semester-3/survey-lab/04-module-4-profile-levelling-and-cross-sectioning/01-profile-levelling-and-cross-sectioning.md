# SURVEY LAB: Module 4 - Profile Levelling and Cross-sectioning

This module delves into two fundamental surveying techniques used for determining elevations along lines and across areas, crucial for engineering design and construction.

## 1. Profile Levelling

### 1.1 Introduction to Profile Levelling

**Profile levelling** is a surveying method used to determine the elevations of points along a straight line or a curve. It establishes the vertical profile of a surface, typically for linear features like roads, railways, pipelines, or canals.

### 1.2 Learning Outcomes for Profile Levelling

*   **Understand the purpose and applications of profile levelling:**
    *   **Purpose:** To establish the longitudinal profile (elevation vs. distance) of a proposed or existing feature.
    *   **Applications:**
        *   **Design of linear infrastructure:** Determining the required cut and fill for roads, railways, drainage systems.
        *   **Earthwork calculations:** Estimating the volume of material to be excavated or filled.
        *   **Hydraulic studies:** Analyzing flow characteristics in open channels.
        *   **Utility planning:** Laying out pipelines and cables.
*   **Identify and explain the components of a level and levelling staff:**
    *   **Level (Dumpy Level/Automatic Level):**
        *   **Telescope:** Magnifies distant objects.
        *   **Objective Lens:** Focuses light onto the objective.
        *   **Eyepiece:** Allows the observer to view the crosshairs and staff reading.
        *   **Crosshairs:** Fixed lines within the telescope that define the line of sight.
        *   **Bubble Vial (Spirit Level):** Indicates if the instrument is level.
        *   **Levelling Screws:** Used to bring the instrument to a level position.
        *   **Focusing Knob:** Adjusts the clarity of the image.
    *   **Levelling Staff (Levelling Rod):**
        *   A graduated, extendable rod held vertically by a staff person.
        *   **Graduations:** Markings indicating feet/meters and their subdivisions.
        *   **E-sight (Fortin Staff):** Common type with numerical markings for easy reading.
        *   **Target:** A movable vane for sighting by the instrument operator, especially at longer distances.
*   **Describe the procedures and fieldwork for profile levelling:**
    *   **Setup:**
        1.  **Choose a suitable instrument station:** The instrument should be placed at a location that allows clear sights to both forward and backward points on the profile line.
        2.  **Level the instrument:** Using the levelling screws and bubble vial, ensure the instrument is perfectly horizontal.
    *   **Fieldwork (Balancing Sight Lengths is Crucial):**
        1.  **First Point of Known Elevation (BM/TP):**
            *   Set up the level.
            *   Take a **Backsight (BS)** reading on a Bench Mark (BM) or Turning Point (TP) of known elevation. This establishes the **Height of Instrument (HI)**.
            *   **HI = Elevation of BM/TP + BS**
        2.  **Sight along the Profile Line:**
            *   Move to subsequent points along the profile line.
            *   Have the staff person hold the staff vertically at each desired point (chainage markers).
            *   Take **Foresight (FS)** readings to these points.
            *   **Elevation of Point = HI - FS**
        3.  **Turning Points (TP):**
            *   When the next point on the profile is too far for a clear sight or when the instrument needs to be moved to maintain balanced sight lengths, a Turning Point is used.
            *   **TP Procedure:**
                *   From the current instrument station, take an FS reading on the TP.
                *   Move the instrument to a new station, ensuring it's level.
                *   Take a BS reading on the *same* TP from the new instrument station.
                *   The BS reading on the TP from the new station is used to re-establish the HI for the new station: **New HI = Elevation of TP + BS (on TP from new station)**.
        4.  **Continue Sightings:** Repeat the process of taking FS readings to points along the profile and using TPs as needed, always ensuring balanced sight lengths to minimize errors.
    *   **Balanced Sight Lengths:** The distance of the backsight should be as close as possible to the distance of the foresight. This cancels out collimation errors and earth curvature/refraction errors.
*   **Explain the importance of turning points and the procedure for setting them:**
    *   **Importance:** To extend levelling operations beyond the reach of a single instrument setup and to change instrument stations while maintaining continuity of elevation.
    *   **Procedure:** As described above, involves taking an FS to the TP from the first station and a BS from the TP from the new station.
*   **Differentiate between backsights, foresights, and intermediate sights:**
    *   **Backsight (BS):** The first reading taken from a new instrument setup. It is always taken on a point of known or previously determined elevation (BM or TP). Used to calculate the Height of Instrument (HI).
    *   **Foresight (FS):** The last reading taken from an instrument setup before moving it. It is taken on a point whose elevation is to be determined or on a TP. Used to calculate the elevation of the sighted point.
    *   **Intermediate Sight (IS):** Any reading taken between the BS and FS from a single instrument setup. These are taken on points whose elevations are needed along the line of survey.
*   **Calculate elevations of points from levelling data:**
    *   **Height of Instrument (HI):** The elevation of the line of sight of the level.
    *   **Elevation of a Point:** `HI - Staff Reading (FS or IS)`
*   **Perform checks and identify sources of error in profile levelling:**
    *   **Checks:**
        *   **Sum of BS = Sum of FS:** This is a crucial check. The sum of all backsights taken should equal the sum of all foresights taken *if* the levelling starts and ends on the same BM or if the difference in elevation calculated by the sums matches the known difference.
        *   **Sum of BS - Sum of FS = Difference in Elevation:** This is the fundamental principle. The total rise is the sum of BS readings, and the total fall is the sum of FS readings.
        *   **Closed Loop Levelling:** Levelling between two known points and returning to the starting point.
    *   **Sources of Error:**
        *   **Instrumental Errors:**
            *   **Collimation Error:** The line of sight is not perfectly horizontal. Minimized by balancing sight lengths.
            *   **Bulb Out of Adjustment:** The bubble vial is not sensitive enough or is incorrectly calibrated.
            *   **Staff Errors:** Graduations not uniform, staff not held perfectly vertical.
        *   **Fieldwork Errors:**
            *   **Staff Not Vertical:** Leads to a reading that is higher than the true vertical reading.
            *   **Inaccurate Turning Point:** The TP is not stable or is disturbed between the FS and BS.
            *   **Unbalanced Sight Lengths:** Collimation error is not fully eliminated.
            *   **Parallax:** Not focusing the eyepiece correctly.
            *   **Reading Errors:** Misreading the staff (e.g., reading 1.500 instead of 1.510).
        *   **Environmental Errors:**
            *   **Earth Curvature and Refraction:** Affects longer sights. Minimized by balancing sight lengths.
            *   **Wind:** Causes the staff to wobble.
            *   **Heat Haze:** Distorts the image.

### 1.3 Key Concepts and Definitions for Profile Levelling

*   **Bench Mark (BM):** A permanent or semi-permanent point of known elevation.
*   **Turning Point (TP):** A temporary point established to allow the instrument to be moved.
*   **Height of Instrument (HI):** The elevation of the plane of collimation (the horizontal line of sight) of the levelling instrument.
*   **Chainage:** The horizontal distance from the starting point of a survey line, usually measured in meters or feet.
*   **Reduced Level (RL):** The elevation of a point relative to a datum (e.g., mean sea level).
*   **Datum:** A reference surface or line from which elevations are measured.

### 1.4 Example: Profile Levelling Fieldwork

**Scenario:** Determine the profile of a proposed pipeline route.

**Data:**

*   BM at Station 0+000 has an RL of 105.500 m.
*   Instrument is set up at Station 1.

| Station       | BS (m)   | IS (m)   | FS (m)   | HI (m)   | RL (m)   | Remarks              |
| :------------ | :------- | :------- | :------- | :------- | :------- | :------------------- |
| 0+000 (BM)    | 1.250    |          |          |          |          |                      |
| *(Inst. 1)*   |          |          |          | 106.750  |          | HI = 105.500 + 1.250 |
| 0+020         |          | 1.500    |          |          | 105.250  |                      |
| 0+040         |          | 1.780    |          |          | 104.970  |                      |
| 0+050 (TP1)   |          |          | 1.950    |          | 104.800  | FS to TP1            |
| *(Inst. 2)*   | 1.100    |          |          |          |          |                      |
| TP1 (0+050)   | 1.100    |          |          | 105.900  | 104.800  | BS from TP1; HI = 104.800 + 1.100 |
| 0+070         |          | 1.300    |          |          | 104.600  |                      |
| 0+085         |          | 1.550    |          |          | 104.350  |                      |
| 0+100 (TP2)   |          |          | 1.820    |          | 104.080  | FS to TP2            |
| *(Inst. 3)*   | 1.200    |          |          |          |          |                      |
| TP2 (0+100)   | 1.200    |          |          | 105.280  | 104.080  | BS from TP2; HI = 104.080 + 1.200 |
| 0+120         |          | 1.450    |          |          | 103.830  |                      |
| 0+135 (End)   |          |          | 1.600    |          | 103.680  | FS to end point      |

**Check:**

*   Sum of BS = 1.250 + 1.100 + 1.200 = 3.550 m
*   Sum of FS = 1.950 + 1.820 + 1.600 = 5.370 m
*   Difference = Sum of BS - Sum of FS = 3.550 - 5.370 = -1.820 m

*   Calculated RL of End Point = RL of BM + (Sum of BS - Sum of FS)
*   Calculated RL of End Point = 105.500 + (-1.820) = 103.680 m

This matches the RL calculated from the last FS reading, indicating the fieldwork was likely performed correctly.

### 1.5 Practice Questions for Profile Levelling

1.  Define "Height of Instrument" and explain how it is calculated.
2.  What is a "Turning Point" and why is it essential in profile levelling?
3.  A levelling instrument is set up between two points A and B. A backsight of 1.450 m is taken on point A, which has an RL of 55.600 m. A foresight of 1.750 m is taken on point B. Calculate the RL of point B.
    *   **Answer:** HI = 55.600 + 1.450 = 57.050 m. RL of B = 57.050 - 1.750 = 55.300 m.
4.  In a profile levelling survey, the following readings are taken:
    | Station      | BS    | IS    | FS    |
    | :----------- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
    | BM (100.000) | 1.200 |       |       |
    | Point 1      |       | 1.500 |       |
    | Point 2 (TP) |       |       | 1.800 |
    | TP           | 1.300 |       |       |
    | Point 3      |       | 1.600 |       |
    | Point 4      |       |       | 1.900 |

    Calculate the RLs of Points 1, 2, 3, and 4.
    *   **Answer:**
        *   HI (Inst. 1) = 100.000 + 1.200 = 101.200 m
        *   RL of Point 1 = 101.200 - 1.500 = 99.700 m
        *   RL of Point 2 (TP) = 101.200 - 1.800 = 99.400 m
        *   HI (Inst. 2) = 99.400 + 1.300 = 100.700 m
        *   RL of Point 3 = 100.700 - 1.600 = 99.100 m
        *   RL of Point 4 = 100.700 - 1.900 = 98.800 m

### 1.6 Important Points to Remember for Profile Levelling

*   **Always start from a known BM.**
*   **Balance sight lengths to minimize errors.**
*   **Ensure the instrument is perfectly level before taking readings.**
*   **Hold the staff vertically.**
*   **Use turning points correctly for changing instrument stations.**
*   **Perform checks (sum of BS vs. sum of FS) to verify accuracy.**
*   **Maintain a detailed and accurate field notebook.**

## 2. Cross-sectioning

### 2.1 Introduction to Cross-sectioning

**Cross-sectioning** is a surveying technique used to determine the elevations of points across a strip of land, perpendicular to the centerline of a linear feature. It provides information about the shape of the ground surface perpendicular to the survey line.

### 2.2 Learning Outcomes for Cross-sectioning

*   **Understand the purpose and applications of cross-sectioning:**
    *   **Purpose:** To define the shape of the ground perpendicular to the centerline, essential for calculating earthwork volumes for linear projects.
    *   **Applications:**
        *   **Roads and Railways:** Determining the width and depth of cut or fill for roadbeds.
        *   **Canals and Ditches:** Designing canal cross-sections for water flow.
        *   **Embankments and Cuttings:** Calculating volumes of material needed or to be removed.
        *   **Site Grading:** Understanding ground topography for construction.
*   **Explain the principles of cross-sectioning fieldwork:**
    *   **Centerline:** A profile levelling survey is often conducted first to establish the centerline and its elevations.
    *   **Stations:** Points along the centerline are designated as stations (e.g., 0+000, 0+020, etc.).
    *   **Perpendicular Lines:** At each station, lines are run perpendicular to the centerline, extending to the limits of the area of interest (e.g., the edges of the proposed road or embankment).
    *   **Levelling:** A levelling instrument is set up and used to take staff readings at various points along these perpendicular lines.
    *   **Offsets:** The distances from the centerline to the points where staff readings are taken are called offsets. They are measured perpendicular to the centerline.
    *   **Staff Readings:** These readings, along with the corresponding offsets and the elevation of the centerline station, are recorded.
*   **Describe the process of establishing and measuring offsets and staff readings:**
    1.  **Set up the levelling instrument:** Choose an advantageous location to sight along the cross-section lines.
    2.  **Reference the centerline:** Identify the station point on the centerline. The elevation of this station is known from profile levelling.
    3.  **Measure offsets:** From the centerline station, measure distances (offsets) perpendicular to the centerline on both sides. These offsets correspond to the boundaries of the proposed work or significant changes in terrain.
    4.  **Take staff readings:** At each offset point, hold the levelling staff vertically and take a staff reading.
    5.  **Record data:** For each station, record the centerline elevation, the offset distance (left or right of centerline), and the staff reading.
    6.  **Repeat for all stations:** Continue this process for all significant stations along the centerline.
*   **Explain the importance of centerline elevations in cross-sectioning:**
    *   The centerline elevation is the **reference point** for the cross-section. It is used to determine the **Reduced Level (RL)** of all points along the cross-section.
    *   **RL of offset point = RL of centerline station + Staff reading (if taken from HI below centerline) or HI - Staff Reading (if taken with HI established above centerline).** The common method is:
        *   **HI established using a benchmark.**
        *   **RL of centerline station is known.**
        *   **RL of offset point = HI - Staff Reading on offset point.**
*   **Perform calculations for cross-section notes and determine cut and fill:**
    *   **Cross-Section Notes:** Typically recorded in a table format.
    *   **Cut:** The vertical distance between the existing ground surface and the proposed design surface when the ground is below the design.
    *   **Fill:** The vertical distance between the existing ground surface and the proposed design surface when the ground is above the design.
    *   **Calculation:**
        *   `RL of Offset Point` (calculated from staff reading and HI)
        *   `Design Elevation of Offset Point` (this is usually the same as the centerline RL for a flat cross-section, or can vary for a sloping cross-section).
        *   **Cut = Design Elevation - RL of Offset Point** (if positive)
        *   **Fill = RL of Offset Point - Design Elevation** (if positive)
*   **Differentiate between cut and fill:**
    *   **Cut:** Material to be excavated. Occurs when the natural ground is higher than the proposed design.
    *   **Fill:** Material to be added. Occurs when the natural ground is lower than the proposed design.

### 2.3 Key Concepts and Definitions for Cross-sectioning

*   **Cross-section:** A vertical plane cutting through the ground surface, perpendicular to the centerline.
*   **Offset:** The horizontal distance from the centerline to a point on the cross-section line.
*   **Centerline:** The longitudinal line of a linear engineering project.
*   **Cut:** Amount of excavation needed.
*   **Fill:** Amount of material to be added.
*   **Side Slope:** The slope of the sides of an excavation or embankment, often expressed as a ratio (e.g., 1:1, 2:1).

### 2.4 Example: Cross-sectioning Fieldwork and Calculation

**Scenario:** Determine cross-sections for a road at Station 0+040. The centerline RL at 0+040 is 104.970 m. The proposed road has a width of 10 m and a level formation.

**Fieldwork:**

*   Instrument is set up, and the HI is established.
*   At Station 0+040, the centerline staff reading is 1.780 m (from profile levelling).
*   **Offsets are measured perpendicular to the centerline:**
    *   Left of centerline:
        *   Offset 2.0 m: Staff reading = 1.600 m
        *   Offset 5.0 m (edge of formation): Staff reading = 1.400 m
    *   Right of centerline:
        *   Offset 2.0 m: Staff reading = 1.650 m
        *   Offset 5.0 m (edge of formation): Staff reading = 1.480 m

**Calculation:**

*   **Assumed HI:** Let's assume the HI was established as 106.750 m.
*   **RL of Centerline:** 104.970 m (given, and also checked: 106.750 - 1.780 = 104.970 m).
*   **Design Elevation:** For a level formation, the design elevation is the same as the centerline RL: 104.970 m.

**Cross-Section Notes Table:**

| Station   | Offset (Left) | Staff Reading | RL of Ground | Design Elevation | Cut (m) | Fill (m) | Offset (Right) | Staff Reading | RL of Ground | Design Elevation | Cut (m) | Fill (m) |
| :-------- | :------------ | :------------ | :----------- | :--------------- | :------ | :------- | :------------- | :------------ | :----------- | :--------------- | :------ | :------- |
| 0+040     | 2.0 m         | 1.600         | 105.150      | 104.970          |         | 0.180    | 2.0 m          | 1.650         | 105.100      | 104.970          |         | 0.130    |
|           | 5.0 m         | 1.400         | 105.350      | 104.970          |         | 0.380    | 5.0 m          | 1.480         | 105.270      | 104.970          |         | 0.300    |

**Calculations:**

*   **RL of Ground (Left, 2.0m):** 106.750 (HI) - 1.600 (SR) = 105.150 m
*   **Fill (Left, 2.0m):** 105.150 (RL Ground) - 104.970 (Design Elev) = 0.180 m
*   **RL of Ground (Left, 5.0m):** 106.750 (HI) - 1.400 (SR) = 105.350 m
*   **Fill (Left, 5.0m):** 105.350 (RL Ground) - 104.970 (Design Elev) = 0.380 m
*   **RL of Ground (Right, 2.0m):** 106.750 (HI) - 1.650 (SR) = 105.100 m
*   **Fill (Right, 2.0m):** 105.100 (RL Ground) - 104.970 (Design Elev) = 0.130 m
*   **RL of Ground (Right, 5.0m):** 106.750 (HI) - 1.480 (SR) = 105.270 m
*   **Fill (Right, 5.0m):** 105.270 (RL Ground) - 104.970 (Design Elev) = 0.300 m

*Note: In this example, the ground is higher than the design elevation at all measured points, so we have fill.*

### 2.5 Practice Questions for Cross-sectioning

1.  What is the primary purpose of cross-sectioning in civil engineering projects?
    *   **Answer:** To determine the shape of the ground perpendicular to a centerline, enabling the calculation of earthwork volumes and the design of cuts and fills.
2.  At a station with a centerline RL of 85.200 m, offsets are taken.
    *   On the left, at 3m offset, the staff reading is 1.500 m.
    *   On the right, at 4m offset, the staff reading is 1.700 m.
    If the HI of the instrument was 86.800 m, and the design elevation for the cross-section is 85.200 m, calculate the cut or fill at each offset.
    *   **Answer:**
        *   **Left, 3m offset:**
            *   RL of Ground = 86.800 - 1.500 = 85.300 m
            *   Fill = 85.300 (RL Ground) - 85.200 (Design) = 0.100 m
        *   **Right, 4m offset:**
            *   RL of Ground = 86.800 - 1.700 = 85.100 m
            *   Cut = 85.200 (Design) - 85.100 (RL Ground) = 0.100 m
3.  Describe the difference between an offset and a staff reading in cross-sectioning.
    *   **Answer:** An offset is the horizontal distance from the centerline to a point on the cross-section line, measured perpendicular to the centerline. A staff reading is the vertical measurement taken by the levelling instrument at that offset point.

### 2.6 Important Points to Remember for Cross-sectioning

*   **Accurate profile levelling of the centerline is a prerequisite.**
*   **Offsets must be measured accurately and perpendicularly to the centerline.**
*   **Staff must be held vertically at each offset point.**
*   **The design elevation for the cross-section is crucial for cut/fill calculations.**
*   **Cross-sectioning is typically performed at regular intervals (e.g., every 10m, 20m) or at points of significant change in ground profile.**
*   **Data should be recorded systematically in field books.**
