# Advanced Solid Mechanics: Module 1 - Elementary Concepts of Elasticity

## Topic: Mohr's Circle for 3-D State of Stress

### 1. Introduction

Mohr's Circle is a graphical method used to represent the state of stress at a point in a material. While it's commonly introduced for 2-D stress states, it can be extended to analyze 3-D stress states, providing valuable insights into principal stresses, maximum shear stresses, and stresses on inclined planes. This topic explores the extension of Mohr's Circle to three dimensions.

### 2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this topic, you will be able to:

*   **Understand the concept of principal stresses in 3-D.**
*   **Determine the principal stresses and principal directions for a given 3-D stress state.**
*   **Construct and interpret the 3-D Mohr's Circle diagrams.**
*   **Identify the maximum shear stress and the planes on which it acts in 3-D.**
*   **Determine the state of stress on any inclined plane in 3-D using Mohr's Circles.**
*   **Relate the 3-D Mohr's Circles to the concept of stress invariants.**

### 3. Key Concepts and Definitions

#### 3.1. State of Stress in 3-D

In three dimensions, the state of stress at a point is completely described by nine stress components, which can be represented by a symmetric stress tensor:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\sigma_{xx} & \tau_{xy} & \tau_{xz} \\
\tau_{yx} & \sigma_{yy} & \tau_{yz} \\
\tau_{zx} & \tau_{zy} & \sigma_{zz}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Due to symmetry ($\tau_{xy} = \tau_{yx}$, $\tau_{xz} = \tau_{zx}$, $\tau_{yz} = \tau_{zy}$), there are six independent stress components.

#### 3.2. Stress Invariants

The state of stress at a point is characterized by quantities that remain invariant under coordinate transformations. For a 3-D stress state, these are the stress invariants:

*   **First Invariant ($I_1$):** The sum of the normal stresses on the principal planes (or the sum of the diagonal elements of the stress tensor).
    $$I_1 = \sigma_1 + \sigma_2 + \sigma_3 = \sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy} + \sigma_{zz}$$
*   **Second Invariant ($I_2$):** Related to the sum of the principal stresses multiplied in pairs.
    $$I_2 = \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2\sigma_3 + \sigma_3\sigma_1$$
*   **Third Invariant ($I_3$):** The product of the principal stresses.
    $$I_3 = \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3$$

#### 3.3. Principal Stresses and Principal Directions

**Principal Stresses ($\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3$):** These are the maximum and minimum normal stresses that can occur at a point. On planes where principal stresses act, the shear stresses are zero. They are the roots of the characteristic equation:
$$ \det(\sigma_{ij} - \sigma \delta_{ij}) = 0 $$
This expands to the cubic equation:
$$ \sigma^3 - I_1 \sigma^2 + I_2 \sigma - I_3 = 0 $$
The roots of this cubic equation are the three principal stresses, conventionally ordered as $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_3$.

**Principal Directions:** These are the directions corresponding to the principal stresses. They are mutually orthogonal.

#### 3.4. Normal and Shear Stresses on an Inclined Plane

Consider a plane with a unit normal vector $\mathbf{n} = [n_x, n_y, n_z]$ at a point. The normal stress ($\sigma_n$) and shear stress vector ($\boldsymbol{\tau}$) on this plane can be expressed as:

*   **Normal Stress ($\sigma_n$):**
    $$\sigma_n = \mathbf{n}^T \mathbf{\sigma} \mathbf{n} = \sigma_{xx}n_x^2 + \sigma_{yy}n_y^2 + \sigma_{zz}n_z^2 + 2\tau_{xy}n_xn_y + 2\tau_{yz}n_yn_z + 2\tau_{zx}n_zx_z$$
*   **Shear Stress Vector ($\boldsymbol{\tau}$):**
    $$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{\sigma} \mathbf{n} - \sigma_n \mathbf{n}$$

The magnitude of the shear stress ($\tau$) on this plane is given by $|\boldsymbol{\tau}|$.

### 4. Mohr's Circles for 3-D State of Stress

In 3-D, we don't have a single circle like in 2-D. Instead, we use three principal circles, which are generated by considering pairs of principal stresses.

#### 4.1. Construction of 3-D Mohr's Circles

The three principal Mohr's circles are constructed as follows:

1.  **Define the Principal Stresses:** First, determine the three principal stresses $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3$ by solving the characteristic cubic equation. Assume $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_3$.

2.  **Draw the Principal Circles:**
    *   **Circle 1:** Represents the stress state on planes inclined relative to the $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ directions. Its center is at $(\frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}{2}, 0)$ and its radius is $R_{12} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2}$. This circle lies in the $(\sigma, \tau)$ plane.
    *   **Circle 2:** Represents the stress state on planes inclined relative to the $\sigma_2$ and $\sigma_3$ directions. Its center is at $(\frac{\sigma_2 + \sigma_3}{2}, 0)$ and its radius is $R_{23} = \frac{\sigma_2 - \sigma_3}{2}$.
    *   **Circle 3:** Represents the stress state on planes inclined relative to the $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_3$ directions. Its center is at $(\frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_3}{2}, 0)$ and its radius is $R_{13} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2}$.

**Important Note:** The three principal circles are drawn within the same $(\sigma, \tau)$ plane. The largest circle ($R_{13}$) encloses the other two.

#### 4.2. Interpretation of 3-D Mohr's Circles

*   **Principal Stresses:** The points where the circles intersect the $\sigma$-axis represent the principal stresses.
    *   $\sigma_1$: The rightmost point on the largest circle.
    *   $\sigma_2$: The rightmost point of Circle 2 and the leftmost point of Circle 1.
    *   $\sigma_3$: The leftmost point on the largest circle.

*   **Maximum Shear Stress ($\tau_{max}$):** The maximum shear stress at a point is the radius of the largest circle.
    $$\tau_{max} = R_{13} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2}$$
    This maximum shear stress occurs on planes that are oriented at $45^\circ$ to the directions of $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_3$.

*   **Stresses on Inclined Planes:** Any point within the area bounded by the three principal circles represents a possible state of $(\sigma_n, \tau)$ on an inclined plane passing through the point. Specifically:
    *   Points on Circle 1 represent stresses on planes whose normal vector has components along the $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ directions.
    *   Points on Circle 2 represent stresses on planes whose normal vector has components along the $\sigma_2$ and $\sigma_3$ directions.
    *   Points on Circle 3 represent stresses on planes whose normal vector has components along the $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_3$ directions.

*   **Out-of-Plane Shear Stress:** The "out-of-plane" shear stresses (those not lying within the principal planes) are represented by the region *between* the circles. A point falling between the circles indicates a state of stress on a plane where shear stress exists and the normal vector has components along all three principal directions.

#### 4.3. Determining Stresses on a Specific Plane

To find the normal stress $\sigma_n$ and shear stress $\tau$ on a plane defined by its normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ with respect to the principal directions, we can use the principal stresses:

Let the principal directions be $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \mathbf{e}_3$. The normal vector in this principal coordinate system is $\mathbf{n'} = [n'_1, n'_2, n'_3]$, where $n'_i = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{e}_i$.

Then,
$$ \sigma_n = \sigma_1 (n'_1)^2 + \sigma_2 (n'_2)^2 + \sigma_3 (n'_3)^2 $$
The shear stress magnitude $\tau$ can be found using the relation:
$$ \tau^2 = \sigma_n^2 - (\sigma_1 (n'_1)^2 + \sigma_2 (n'_2)^2 + \sigma_3 (n'_3)^2)^2 $$
*More directly:* The components of the shear stress vector $\boldsymbol{\tau'}$ in the principal coordinate system are:
$$ \tau'_1 = \sigma_1 n'_1 - \sigma_n n'_1 = (\sigma_1 - \sigma_n) n'_1 $$
$$ \tau'_2 = \sigma_2 n'_2 - \sigma_n n'_2 = (\sigma_2 - \sigma_n) n'_2 $$
$$ \tau'_3 = \sigma_3 n'_3 - \sigma_n n'_3 = (\sigma_3 - \sigma_n) n'_3 $$
The magnitude of the shear stress is $\tau = \sqrt{(\tau'_1)^2 + (\tau'_2)^2 + (\tau'_3)^2}$.

A point $(\sigma_n, \tau)$ on one of the Mohr's circles is related to the orientation of the plane relative to two principal directions. For example, on Circle 1 (between $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$), the normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ makes an angle $\theta$ with the $\sigma_1$ direction in the plane of the circle. The coordinates on this circle are:
$$ \sigma_n = \frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}{2} + \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2} \cos(2\theta) $$
$$ \tau = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2} \sin(2\theta) $$

### 5. Example

**Problem:** The state of stress at a point is given by the following stress tensor:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
50 & 10 & 20 \\
10 & 40 & 15 \\
20 & 15 & 30
\end{bmatrix}
\text{ MPa}
$$

Determine the principal stresses and construct the 3-D Mohr's circles. Find the maximum shear stress.

**Solution:**

1.  **Calculate Stress Invariants:**
    $I_1 = \sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy} + \sigma_{zz} = 50 + 40 + 30 = 120 \text{ MPa}$
    $I_2 = (\sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} + \sigma_{yy}\sigma_{zz} + \sigma_{zz}\sigma_{xx}) - (\tau_{xy}^2 + \tau_{yz}^2 + \tau_{zx}^2)$
    $I_2 = (50 \times 40 + 40 \times 30 + 30 \times 50) - (10^2 + 15^2 + 20^2)$
    $I_2 = (2000 + 1200 + 1500) - (100 + 225 + 400)$
    $I_2 = 4700 - 725 = 3975 \text{ MPa}^2$
    $I_3 = \det(\sigma_{ij})$
    $I_3 = 50(40 \times 30 - 15^2) - 10(10 \times 30 - 15 \times 20) + 20(10 \times 15 - 40 \times 20)$
    $I_3 = 50(1200 - 225) - 10(300 - 300) + 20(150 - 800)$
    $I_3 = 50(975) - 10(0) + 20(-650)$
    $I_3 = 48750 - 13000 = 35750 \text{ MPa}^3$

2.  **Solve the Characteristic Cubic Equation:**
    $\sigma^3 - I_1 \sigma^2 + I_2 \sigma - I_3 = 0$
    $\sigma^3 - 120 \sigma^2 + 3975 \sigma - 35750 = 0$

    Solving this cubic equation (numerically or by factoring if roots are simple) gives the principal stresses:
    $\sigma_1 \approx 80.3 \text{ MPa}$
    $\sigma_2 \approx 34.7 \text{ MPa}$
    $\sigma_3 \approx 4.98 \text{ MPa}$

    Let's re-order them: $\sigma_1 \approx 80.3$, $\sigma_2 \approx 34.7$, $\sigma_3 \approx 4.98$ MPa.

3.  **Construct Mohr's Circles:**
    *   **Circle 1 ($\sigma_1, \sigma_2$):**
        Center: $(\frac{80.3 + 34.7}{2}, 0) = (57.5, 0)$
        Radius: $R_{12} = \frac{80.3 - 34.7}{2} = \frac{45.6}{2} = 22.8 \text{ MPa}$
        Intersects $\sigma$-axis at $57.5 \pm 22.8$, i.e., $34.7$ and $80.3$.

    *   **Circle 2 ($\sigma_2, \sigma_3$):**
        Center: $(\frac{34.7 + 4.98}{2}, 0) = (19.84, 0)$
        Radius: $R_{23} = \frac{34.7 - 4.98}{2} = \frac{29.72}{2} = 14.86 \text{ MPa}$
        Intersects $\sigma$-axis at $19.84 \pm 14.86$, i.e., $4.98$ and $34.7$.

    *   **Circle 3 ($\sigma_1, \sigma_3$):**
        Center: $(\frac{80.3 + 4.98}{2}, 0) = (42.64, 0)$
        Radius: $R_{13} = \frac{80.3 - 4.98}{2} = \frac{75.32}{2} = 37.66 \text{ MPa}$
        Intersects $\sigma$-axis at $42.64 \pm 37.66$, i.e., $4.98$ and $80.3$.

4.  **Maximum Shear Stress:**
    $\tau_{max} = R_{13} = 37.66 \text{ MPa}$

    **Diagram:** (A sketch of three circles would be drawn here, with the $\sigma$-axis as the horizontal axis and $\tau$-axis as the vertical axis. Circle 3 would be the largest, enclosing Circles 1 and 2.)

### 6. Practice Questions

1.  Given the following stress tensor:
    $$
    \begin{bmatrix}
    20 & 0 & 0 \\
    0 & 30 & 0 \\
    0 & 0 & 40
    \end{bmatrix}
    \text{ MPa}
    $$
    Determine the principal stresses and sketch the 3-D Mohr's circles. What is the maximum shear stress?

2.  If the principal stresses at a point are $\sigma_1 = 100$ MPa, $\sigma_2 = 50$ MPa, and $\sigma_3 = -20$ MPa, construct the 3-D Mohr's circles and determine the maximum shear stress.

3.  For the stress state in Question 2, what are the normal and shear stresses on a plane whose normal vector has direction cosines $(1/\sqrt{2}, 1/\sqrt{2}, 0)$ with respect to the principal stress directions?

### 7. Answers to Practice Questions

1.  **Principal Stresses:**
    Since the stress tensor is diagonal, the principal stresses are simply the diagonal components: $\sigma_1 = 40$ MPa, $\sigma_2 = 30$ MPa, $\sigma_3 = 20$ MPa.

    **Mohr's Circles:**
    *   Circle 1 ($\sigma_1, \sigma_2$): Center (35, 0), Radius $R_{12} = (40-30)/2 = 5$ MPa.
    *   Circle 2 ($\sigma_2, \sigma_3$): Center (25, 0), Radius $R_{23} = (30-20)/2 = 5$ MPa.
    *   Circle 3 ($\sigma_1, \sigma_3$): Center (30, 0), Radius $R_{13} = (40-20)/2 = 10$ MPa.
    *(Sketch would show three circles, with the largest enclosing the other two. Circles 1 and 2 are tangent at $\sigma=30$)*

    **Maximum Shear Stress:**
    $\tau_{max} = R_{13} = 10$ MPa.

2.  **Principal Stresses:**
    $\sigma_1 = 100$ MPa, $\sigma_2 = 50$ MPa, $\sigma_3 = -20$ MPa.

    **Mohr's Circles:**
    *   Circle 1 ($\sigma_1, \sigma_2$): Center $(\frac{100+50}{2}, 0) = (75, 0)$, Radius $R_{12} = \frac{100-50}{2} = 25$ MPa.
    *   Circle 2 ($\sigma_2, \sigma_3$): Center $(\frac{50+(-20)}{2}, 0) = (15, 0)$, Radius $R_{23} = \frac{50-(-20)}{2} = 35$ MPa.
    *   Circle 3 ($\sigma_1, \sigma_3$): Center $(\frac{100+(-20)}{2}, 0) = (40, 0)$, Radius $R_{13} = \frac{100-(-20)}{2} = 60$ MPa.
    *(Sketch would show three circles, with Circle 3 being the largest and enclosing the other two.)*

    **Maximum Shear Stress:**
    $\tau_{max} = R_{13} = 60$ MPa.

3.  **Stresses on the inclined plane:**
    The normal vector has components $(n'_1, n'_2, n'_3) = (1/\sqrt{2}, 1/\sqrt{2}, 0)$ in the principal coordinate system.
    Principal stresses are $\sigma_1 = 100$, $\sigma_2 = 50$, $\sigma_3 = -20$.

    Normal Stress ($\sigma_n$):
    $\sigma_n = \sigma_1 (n'_1)^2 + \sigma_2 (n'_2)^2 + \sigma_3 (n'_3)^2$
    $\sigma_n = 100 (1/\sqrt{2})^2 + 50 (1/\sqrt{2})^2 + (-20) (0)^2$
    $\sigma_n = 100 (1/2) + 50 (1/2) + 0 = 50 + 25 = 75$ MPa.

    Shear Stress Magnitude ($\tau$):
    The normal vector lies in the plane of the $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ principal stresses (since $n'_3=0$). This means the stress state on this plane will lie on Circle 1.
    On Circle 1, the normal stress is $\sigma_n = 75$ MPa.
    The coordinates on Circle 1 are $(\sigma_n, \tau)$.
    The center of Circle 1 is $(\frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}{2}, 0) = (75, 0)$.
    The radius of Circle 1 is $R_{12} = 25$ MPa.
    The general form of points on Circle 1 is $(\sigma_n, \tau) = (\frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}{2} + R_{12}\cos\alpha, R_{12}\sin\alpha)$.
    Here, $\sigma_n = 75$ MPa, which is the center of Circle 1. This means the point $(\sigma_n, \tau)$ is on the horizontal axis of Circle 1.
    $\sigma_n = 75 = 75 + 25 \cos\alpha \implies \cos\alpha = 0$.
    If $\cos\alpha = 0$, then $\sin\alpha = \pm 1$.
    The shear stress is $\tau = R_{12} \sin\alpha = 25 \times (\pm 1) = \pm 25$ MPa.

    Alternatively, using the shear stress components in principal directions:
    $\tau'_1 = (\sigma_1 - \sigma_n) n'_1 = (100 - 75) (1/\sqrt{2}) = 25/\sqrt{2}$
    $\tau'_2 = (\sigma_2 - \sigma_n) n'_2 = (50 - 75) (1/\sqrt{2}) = -25/\sqrt{2}$
    $\tau'_3 = (\sigma_3 - \sigma_n) n'_3 = (-20 - 75) (0) = 0$
    $\tau = \sqrt{(\tau'_1)^2 + (\tau'_2)^2 + (\tau'_3)^2} = \sqrt{(25/\sqrt{2})^2 + (-25/\sqrt{2})^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{625/2 + 625/2} = \sqrt{625} = 25$ MPa.

    **Answer:** Normal stress $\sigma_n = 75$ MPa, Shear stress $\tau = 25$ MPa.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **3-D stress requires three principal stresses.**
*   **Mohr's circles in 3-D are a set of three circles, not one.**
*   **The largest circle's radius represents the maximum shear stress in the material.**
*   **The region between the largest and smallest circles represents states of stress on planes with non-zero shear stress components in all principal directions.**
*   **Principal stresses are invariant and define the extreme values of normal stress.**
*   **The cubic equation for principal stresses is derived from the stress tensor's characteristic equation.**
*   **The sum of normal stresses on any three mutually orthogonal planes is constant (related to $I_1$).**
