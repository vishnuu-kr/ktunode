# Concrete Technology: Module 1 - Cement
## Topic: Admixtures

---

### 1. Introduction to Admixtures

Admixtures are ingredients added to concrete, other than cement, water, and aggregates, immediately before or during mixing. They are added in small quantities to modify the properties of fresh or hardened concrete, improve workability, durability, strength, or reduce cost.

**Necessity of Admixtures:**

*   **Enhancing Workability:** To achieve desired consistency and ease of placement without excessive water.
*   **Controlling Setting Time:** To accelerate or retard the setting of concrete based on project requirements.
*   **Improving Durability:** To resist aggressive environmental conditions like sulfates, chlorides, and freeze-thaw cycles.
*   **Increasing Strength:** To achieve higher compressive and flexural strengths.
*   **Reducing Permeability:** To create denser concrete with lower water absorption.
*   **Cost Reduction:** By reducing cement content or optimizing mix proportions.
*   **Special Requirements:** For specific applications like underwater concreting, self-compacting concrete, or colored concrete.

**Benefits of Admixtures:**

*   **Improved Performance:** Leads to better fresh and hardened concrete properties.
*   **Increased Durability:** Extends the service life of concrete structures.
*   **Enhanced Construction Efficiency:** Faster placement, reduced labor, and fewer defects.
*   **Cost Savings:** Can lead to lower material costs and reduced repair expenses.
*   **Achieving Desired Properties:** Enables concrete to meet specific design requirements.

---

### 2. Types of Admixtures

Admixtures are broadly classified into two main categories:

**A. Mineral Admixtures (Supplementary Cementitious Materials - SCMs)**

These are finely divided inorganic materials that react chemically with the by-products of cement hydration (primarily calcium hydroxide) to form cementitious compounds. This process is known as **pozzolanic reaction**.

**B. Chemical Admixtures**

These are materials added in small quantities to modify the properties of fresh or hardened concrete through physical or chemical actions.

---

### 3. Mineral Admixtures

Mineral admixtures are crucial for improving the performance and sustainability of concrete. They are typically added to the concrete mix in significant proportions (often 10-30% of cementitious material).

#### 3.1 Fly Ash

*   **Source:** A by-product of coal combustion in thermal power plants.
*   **Composition:** Primarily silicon dioxide (SiO₂) and aluminum oxide (Al₂O₃), with smaller amounts of iron oxide (Fe₂O₃) and calcium oxide (CaO).
*   **Types:**
    *   **Class F (Low Calcium):** SiO₂ + Al₂O₃ + Fe₂O₃ > 70%. Commonly used for general-purpose concrete, sulfate resistance, and alkali-silica reaction mitigation.
    *   **Class C (High Calcium):** SiO₂ + Al₂O₃ + Fe₂O₃ > 50%, and often contains significant CaO (>10%). Possesses some self-cementing properties in addition to pozzolanic activity.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Pozzolanic Reaction:** Reacts with calcium hydroxide (a by-product of cement hydration) to form additional cementitious compounds (C-S-H gel), contributing to long-term strength.
    *   **Improves Workability:** Due to its spherical particle shape, fly ash can act as a "ball bearing," improving the flow and reducing water demand.
    *   **Reduces Heat of Hydration:** Slows down the hydration process, reducing the peak temperature of concrete, which is beneficial for mass concrete structures.
    *   **Enhances Durability:** Reduces permeability, improves resistance to sulfate attack and alkali-silica reaction (ASR).
*   **Dosage:** Typically 15-30% of the cementitious material. Higher percentages (up to 50% or more) can be used with Class F fly ash in specific applications.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Improved workability and finishability.
    *   Higher long-term strength.
    *   Reduced permeability and increased durability.
    *   Lower heat of hydration.
    *   Mitigation of Alkali-Silica Reaction (ASR).
    *   Cost reduction by replacing a portion of cement.
    *   Environmental benefit by utilizing a waste product.

#### 3.2 Silica Fume (Microsilica)

*   **Source:** A by-product of silicon and ferrosilicon alloy production in electric arc furnaces.
*   **Composition:** Primarily amorphous silicon dioxide (SiO₂), extremely fine particles (average particle size 0.1-0.2 µm).
*   **Function:**
    *   **Pozzolanic Reaction:** Highly reactive pozzolan due to its extreme fineness and amorphous nature.
    *   **Particle Packing:** Its fine particles fill the voids between cement and aggregate particles, increasing the density and reducing the void content of the concrete matrix.
    *   **Improved Strength:** Significantly increases compressive and flexural strength.
    *   **Reduced Permeability:** Creates a much denser and less permeable concrete, leading to enhanced durability against chemical attack and chloride penetration.
    *   **Improved Abrasion Resistance:** Increases the surface hardness and resistance to abrasion.
*   **Dosage:** Typically 5-15% by weight of cement.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Exceptional increase in strength (especially compressive strength).
    *   Greatly reduced permeability and improved durability.
    *   Enhanced resistance to chemical attack and abrasion.
    *   Improved resistance to ASR.
*   **Considerations:** Can reduce workability and increase the risk of plastic shrinkage cracking if not handled properly. Requires the use of superplasticizers to maintain workability.

#### 3.3 Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag (GGBS)

*   **Source:** A by-product of iron manufacturing, produced by quenching molten iron slag rapidly with water or steam. The rapid cooling solidifies the slag into a glassy granular material, which is then ground into a fine powder.
*   **Composition:** Primarily calcium oxide (CaO), silicon dioxide (SiO₂), aluminum oxide (Al₂O₃), and magnesium oxide (MgO).
*   **Function:**
    *   **Latent Hydraulic Properties:** GGBS can react with water and calcium hydroxide to form cementitious compounds, even without the presence of cement.
    *   **Pozzolanic Activity:** Also exhibits pozzolanic activity by reacting with calcium hydroxide.
    *   **Improves Workability:** Similar to fly ash, its particle shape can enhance workability.
    *   **Reduces Heat of Hydration:** Contributes to a lower heat of hydration compared to pure Portland cement.
    *   **Enhances Durability:** Improves resistance to sulfate attack and alkali-silica reaction.
    *   **Improves Appearance:** Can lead to a lighter color concrete.
*   **Dosage:** Typically 20-70% replacement of cement.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Improved long-term strength development.
    *   Reduced permeability and enhanced durability.
    *   Good resistance to sulfate attack and ASR.
    *   Lower heat of hydration, suitable for mass concrete.
    *   Improved workability.
    *   Aesthetically pleasing lighter color.
    *   Sustainable material, utilizing industrial waste.

#### 3.4 Agro-Waste Based Pozzolans

These are materials derived from agricultural by-products that exhibit pozzolanic properties. They offer a sustainable alternative to conventional cementitious materials.

*   **Examples:**
    *   **Rice Husk Ash (RHA):** Produced by burning rice husks under controlled conditions (often with limited oxygen) to retain amorphous silica.
    *   **Bagasse Ash:** Ash obtained from burning sugarcane bagasse.
    *   **Coconut Husk Ash:** Ash from burnt coconut husks.
    *   **Fly Ash from Agricultural Waste Incineration:** Ash from burning crop residues like straw or stalks.
*   **Function:**
    *   **Pozzolanic Reaction:** React with calcium hydroxide to form additional cementitious products.
    *   **Filler Effect:** Fills the interstitial voids, improving the density of the concrete.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Sustainability and environmental friendliness by utilizing waste materials.
    *   Potential for cost reduction.
    *   Can improve strength and durability if processed correctly.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   The pozzolanic activity varies significantly based on the source, burning temperature, and processing.
    *   Requires careful characterization and quality control.
    *   May require pre-treatment for optimal performance.

### 4. Chemical Admixtures

Chemical admixtures are added in much smaller quantities (typically 0.1-2% by weight of cement) and are primarily used to modify the behavior of fresh concrete or to enhance specific hardened properties.

#### 4.1 Accelerators

*   **Function:** To speed up the rate of cement hydration and the development of early strength.
*   **Necessity:**
    *   **Cold Weather Concreting:** To counteract the slowing effect of low temperatures on hydration.
    *   **Early Formwork Removal:** To allow faster stripping of forms and quicker reuse.
    *   **Emergency Repairs:** To accelerate the setting and hardening for rapid patching.
    *   **Shotcreting:** To achieve quick setting for overhead or vertical applications.
*   **Common Types:**
    *   **Calcium Chloride (CaCl₂):** Most common and effective accelerator. However, it can cause corrosion of reinforcing steel, especially in the presence of moisture and oxygen. Its use is generally restricted in reinforced concrete.
    *   **Calcium Nitrate (Ca(NO₃)₂):** Non-corrosive alternative to calcium chloride.
    *   **Triethanolamine (TEA):** Organic compound that promotes hydration of C₃A.
*   **Dosage:** Typically 1-2% by weight of cement.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Faster setting times.
    *   Increased early strength development.
    *   Enables construction in cold weather.
    *   Reduces risk of damage from freezing.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   Over-dosing can lead to flash setting (rapid stiffening) and reduced ultimate strength.
    *   Corrosion risk with calcium chloride.

#### 4.2 Retarders

*   **Function:** To slow down the rate of cement hydration, thereby extending the setting time of concrete.
*   **Necessity:**
    *   **Hot Weather Concreting:** To counteract the accelerating effect of high temperatures on setting.
    *   **Long Haul Distances:** To keep the concrete workable during transportation.
    *   **Large Placements:** To prevent the formation of cold joints when placing concrete in layers.
    *   **Complex Formwork:** To allow sufficient time for proper placement and consolidation.
*   **Common Types:**
    *   **Hydroxycarboxylic Acids:** e.g., Gluconates.
    *   **Lignosulfonates:** By-products of wood pulp processing.
    *   **Carbohydrates:** Sugars.
*   **Dosage:** Typically 0.1-0.5% by weight of cement.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Increased workability retention.
    *   Allows for longer transportation times.
    *   Prevents cold joints in large pours.
    *   Useful in hot weather.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   Over-dosing can lead to significant retardation or even complete inhibition of setting.
    *   May slightly reduce early strength development.

#### 4.3 Plasticizers (Water Reducers)

*   **Function:** To improve the workability (fluidity) of concrete at a given water-cement ratio, or to reduce the water content while maintaining the same workability.
*   **Mechanism:** These admixtures are typically anionic surfactants. They adsorb onto cement particles, imparting a negative charge. This causes electrostatic repulsion between cement particles, dispersing them and releasing trapped water.
*   **Necessity:**
    *   **To improve workability for easier placement:** Especially in heavily reinforced sections or complex formwork.
    *   **To reduce water content:** For a given workability, reducing water leads to a higher strength and improved durability due to a lower water-cement ratio.
*   **Common Types:**
    *   **Lignosulfonates:** Traditional plasticizers.
    *   **Hydroxycarboxylic Acids.**
*   **Dosage:** Typically 0.1-0.5% by weight of cement.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Improved workability.
    *   Reduced water demand (leading to higher strength and durability).
    *   Easier consolidation.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   Can cause slight retardation of setting if not formulated carefully.

#### 4.4 Superplasticizers (High-Range Water Reducers)

*   **Function:** To significantly increase the workability (fluidity) of concrete without adding water, or to achieve a substantial reduction in water content while maintaining normal workability. They provide a much greater water reduction (12-30%) compared to conventional plasticizers.
*   **Mechanism:** Similar to plasticizers but with more potent dispersing action due to their chemical structure (e.g., polycarboxylates). They create a stronger repulsive force between cement particles.
*   **Necessity:**
    *   **To produce High-Strength Concrete:** Achieved by significant water reduction.
    *   **To produce Self-Compacting Concrete (SCC):** Highly fluid concrete that can flow under its own weight into formwork and encapsulate reinforcement.
    *   **To improve workability of very lean mixes.**
    *   **To reduce energy for pumping concrete.**
*   **Common Types:**
    *   **Sulfonated Melamine Formaldehyde (SMF):** Offer good slump retention.
    *   **Sulfonated Naphthalene Formaldehyde (SNF):** Highly effective water reducers.
    *   **Polycarboxylates (PCE):** Latest generation, highly versatile, and allow for precise control over rheology.
*   **Dosage:** Typically 0.5-2% by weight of cement.
*   **Benefits:**
    *   Significant water reduction (12-30%), leading to very high strength and durability.
    *   Production of flowing or self-compacting concrete.
    *   Improved finishability and reduced labor for placement and consolidation.
    *   Can be used to adjust slump without adding more water.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   Can cause rapid slump loss if not properly formulated or if the cement has poor slump retention properties.
    *   Requires careful mix design and quality control of cement.
    *   May slightly delay setting time.

### 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Admixtures are not a substitute for good concrete practice.** Proper mix design, batching, mixing, placing, and curing are still paramount.
*   **Compatibility:** Always check the compatibility of different admixtures with each other and with the cement being used.
*   **Dosage Control:** Adhere strictly to recommended dosages. Over-dosing or under-dosing can have detrimental effects.
*   **Testing:** It's advisable to conduct trial mixes to assess the effect of admixtures on concrete properties under specific project conditions.
*   **Mineral Admixtures:** Enhance concrete's long-term properties and sustainability. They react with calcium hydroxide to form additional cementitious compounds (pozzolanic reaction).
*   **Chemical Admixtures:** Modify fresh or hardened concrete properties in the short term or specific ways, added in small quantities.
*   **Fly Ash:** Improves workability, reduces heat of hydration, and enhances durability.
*   **Silica Fume:** Dramatically increases strength and reduces permeability due to its extreme fineness and pozzolanic activity.
*   **GGBS:** Provides latent hydraulic properties, improves durability, and reduces heat of hydration.
*   **Superplasticizers:** Crucial for high-strength concrete and self-compacting concrete due to their high water-reducing capabilities.

### 7. Answers to Practice Questions

**Multiple Choice Questions:**

1.  **b) Fly Ash** (Mineral admixtures are SCMs like fly ash, silica fume, GGBS)
2.  **c) Increase strength and reduce permeability** (Due to its fineness and pozzolanic activity)
3.  **c) Calcium Chloride** (Due to its chloride content which promotes corrosion)
4.  **c) Superplasticizers** (To achieve the high fluidity required for SCC)
5.  **b) Calcium Hydroxide** (This is the definition of a pozzolanic reaction)

**Short Answer Questions:**

6.  **Necessity of Admixtures:** Admixtures are necessary to enhance workability, control setting time, improve durability, increase strength, reduce permeability, reduce cost, and meet special requirements of concrete for various construction projects.
7.  **GGBS:**
    *   **Source:** By-product of iron manufacturing (molten iron slag quenched rapidly).
    *   **Primary Functions:** Possesses latent hydraulic properties (reacts with water and Ca(OH)₂) and pozzolanic activity. Improves workability, reduces heat of hydration, enhances durability (sulfate resistance, ASR mitigation), and contributes to long-term strength.
8.  **Plasticizer vs. Superplasticizer:**
    *   **Plasticizers:** Improve workability or reduce water by 5-10% at a given workability.
    *   **Superplasticizers:** Significantly increase workability or reduce water by 12-30% at a given workability. They are more potent and used for high-strength or flowing concrete.
    *   **Dosage:** Plasticizers: 0.1-0.5% by weight of cement. Superplasticizers: 0.5-2% by weight of cement.
9.  **Benefits of Fly Ash:** Improves workability (due to spherical particles), reduces water demand, lowers heat of hydration (beneficial for mass concrete), reduces permeability, enhances durability (sulfate and ASR resistance), provides cost savings, and utilizes industrial waste (environmental benefit).
10. **Importance of Dosage Control:** Adhering to recommended dosages is crucial because incorrect dosing can lead to detrimental effects such as flash setting (over-acceleration), excessive retardation, reduced ultimate strength, or adverse effects on durability. Each admixture has an optimal dosage range for its intended function.

**Practical Application Question:**

11. **Admixture Considerations for Bridge Deck:**
    *   **For high early strength:** An **accelerator**, such as calcium nitrate or a non-chloride accelerator, would be beneficial to speed up the hardening process and allow for faster construction operations.
    *   **For de-icing salt exposure (durability):**
        *   A **mineral admixture** like **fly ash (Class F)** or **GGBS** would be highly recommended. These reduce permeability, making the concrete more resistant to chloride ingress.
        *   If significant water reduction is needed to achieve low permeability and high strength, a **superplasticizer** would be essential. This allows for a lower water-cement ratio without sacrificing workability.
    *   **Consideration for hot weather:** If the construction occurs in hot weather, a **retarder** might also be considered to prevent premature stiffening, especially if long haul distances or complex placements are involved.

    **Recommended Admixture Combination:** A combination of a superplasticizer for water reduction and strength, a mineral admixture (fly ash or GGBS) for enhanced durability, and potentially a non-chloride accelerator for early strength development. Careful trial mixes are essential to optimize the combination and dosages.
