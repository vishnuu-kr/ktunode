### 1. Utility of Aggregates in Concrete

Aggregates constitute a significant portion of concrete (typically 60-75% by volume) and play a crucial role in its performance.

*   **Volume and Bulk:** Provide the necessary bulk to the concrete mix, reducing the amount of cement required and thus the cost.
*   **Strength and Stability:** Act as a skeleton, providing structural integrity and resisting compressive forces.
*   **Workability:** Influence the ease with which concrete can be mixed, placed, compacted, and finished.
*   **Durability:** Contribute to resistance against weathering, abrasion, and chemical attack.
*   **Thermal Properties:** Affect the thermal expansion and conductivity of concrete.
*   **Shrinkage:** Influence drying shrinkage and potential for cracking.

**Key Concept:** Aggregates are the inert granular materials that form the major constituent of concrete.

### 2. Fine and Coarse Aggregates

Aggregates are broadly classified based on their particle size.

#### 2.1 Fine Aggregates

*   **Definition:** Aggregates that pass through a 4.75 mm sieve (IS Sieve No. 4).
*   **Typical Material:** Natural sand, crushed rock fines.
*   **Function:** Fill the voids between coarse aggregate particles, improve workability, and contribute to the overall cohesive nature of the concrete mix.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Generally smaller particle size.
    *   Larger surface area to volume ratio compared to coarse aggregates.
    *   Influences the water demand of the mix.

#### 2.2 Coarse Aggregates

*   **Definition:** Aggregates that are retained on a 4.75 mm sieve (IS Sieve No. 4).
*   **Typical Material:** Crushed stone, gravel, boulders.
*   **Function:** Provide bulk, strength, and reduce shrinkage.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Larger particle size.
    *   Lower surface area to volume ratio.
    *   Influence the void content and density of the concrete.

**IS Classification (as per IS 383:1970):**

| Aggregate Type     | Size Range                               | IS Sieve Designation                                  |
| :----------------- | :--------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- |
| Fine Aggregate     | Passing 4.75 mm                          | Passes 4.75 mm, Retained on 90 µm                     |
| Coarse Aggregate   | Retained on 4.75 mm                      | Retained on 4.75 mm                                   |
| **Nominal Size of Aggregate** | The size of the sieve on which approximately 85% of the aggregate is retained. |                                                       |

### 3. Effect of Geometry & Texture

The shape and surface texture of aggregates significantly influence the properties of fresh and hardened concrete.

#### 3.1 Shape

*   **Cubical/Rounded:**
    *   **Geometry:** Compact, fewer elongated or flat particles.
    *   **Effect:** Good workability, lower void content, requires less cement paste for lubrication. Leads to denser concrete.
    *   **Example:** Well-rounded river gravel.
*   **Angular/Crushed:**
    *   **Geometry:** Sharp edges and corners, rough surface.
    *   **Effect:** Good interlocking, higher strength and stability, increased mechanical bond with cement paste. Requires more water and cement paste for workability due to higher void content and surface area.
    *   **Example:** Crushed granite or basalt.
*   **Flaky and Elongated:**
    *   **Geometry:** Particles with one dimension significantly smaller than others (flaky) or significantly larger than others (elongated).
    *   **Effect:** Poor workability, tendency to break under load, lower strength, increased void content. Undesirable in good quality concrete.
    *   **Example:** Mica schist, some types of crushed stone.

**Indian Standard Reference:** IS 383:1970 specifies limits for flaky and elongated aggregates.

#### 3.2 Texture

*   **Rough/Porous:**
    *   **Effect:** Better mechanical bond with cement paste, improved bond strength. Can absorb more water, potentially affecting workability and requiring adjustments in water content.
    *   **Example:** Pumice, certain types of crushed rock with rough surfaces.
*   **Smooth/Polished:**
    *   **Effect:** Weaker mechanical bond with cement paste, lower bond strength. Can lead to reduced overall concrete strength.
    *   **Example:** Rounded river gravel with polished surfaces.

**Key Concept:** Interlocking and bond between aggregate and cement paste are crucial for concrete strength.

### 4. Strength and Mechanical Properties of Aggregates

Aggregates must possess adequate strength and durability to withstand the stresses of mixing, placing, compaction, and service conditions.

*   **Aggregate Crushing Value (IS 2386 Part IV):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the resistance of aggregates to crushing under a gradually applied compressive load.
    *   **Procedure:** A known weight of aggregate is placed in a cylindrical mold and subjected to a specified load. The percentage of fine material passing a 2.36 mm sieve is the crushing value.
    *   **Interpretation:** Lower crushing value indicates higher strength.
    *   **Typical Values:**
        *   Surface dressing, water-bound macadam: Not more than 30%.
        *   Roads and pavements wearing coat: Not more than 20%.
        *   For concrete where high strength is not required: Not more than 45%.
*   **Aggregate Impact Value (IS 2386 Part IV):**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the resistance of aggregates to sudden shock or impact.
    *   **Procedure:** Aggregates are placed in a cylindrical cup and subjected to repeated blows from a hammer. The percentage of fine material passing a 2.36 mm sieve is the impact value.
    *   **Interpretation:** Lower impact value indicates higher resistance to impact.
    *   **Typical Values:**
        *   Roads and pavements wearing coat: Not more than 20%.
        *   For concrete where resistance to impact is not important: Not more than 30%.
*   **Los Angeles Abrasion Value (IS 2386 Part IV):**
    *   **Purpose:** To measure the resistance of aggregates to disintegration by abrasion, impact, and crushing. (While not a primary Indian Standard test on its own for all applications, it's a commonly recognized international standard for wear resistance).
    *   **Procedure:** Aggregates are placed in a rotating drum along with steel balls and rotated for a specified number of revolutions. The percentage of wear is determined.
    *   **Interpretation:** Lower abrasion value indicates better wear resistance.
*   **Hardness:** Resistance to scratching and abrasion.
*   **Toughness:** Resistance to impact and fracture.

**Key Concept:** Aggregates should be sufficiently strong and hard to resist physical breakdown during handling and service.

### 5. Moisture Content and Water Absorption

The presence of moisture on the aggregate surface and within its pores affects the water-cement ratio and workability of concrete.

#### 5.1 Moisture Content

*   **Definition:** The amount of water present in or on the aggregate particles, expressed as a percentage of the dry weight of the aggregate.
*   **States of Moisture:**
    *   **Oven-Dry (OD):** Aggregate completely dried in an oven.
    *   **Air-Dry:** Aggregate dried by atmospheric air, still contains some adsorbed moisture.
    *   **Saturated Surface-Dry (SSD):** Aggregate where all the pores are filled with water, but the surface is dry. This is a reference state for calculations.
    *   **Wet:** Aggregate with a visible film of moisture on its surface.

#### 5.2 Water Absorption

*   **Definition:** The amount of water that aggregate can absorb into its pores when immersed in water, expressed as a percentage of the dry weight of the aggregate.
*   **IS Specification (IS 2386 Part III):**
    *   **Fine Aggregate:** Absorption value shall not exceed 2%.
    *   **Coarse Aggregate:** Absorption value shall not exceed 1% (for aggregate used in plain concrete) and 0.6% (for aggregate used in reinforced concrete).

**Importance:**

*   **Water-Cement Ratio:** Excess surface moisture on aggregates effectively increases the amount of free water in the mix, leading to a higher water-cement ratio than intended. This reduces strength and durability.
*   **Workability:** Surface moisture can improve workability initially but can also lead to segregation if excessive.
*   **Batching Accuracy:** Accurate measurement of aggregate weight is crucial. If aggregates are wet, their apparent weight will be higher due to the water. Corrections are needed.

**Calculation Example (for practice):**
An aggregate sample weighs 1000 g in the wet condition. After oven drying, it weighs 950 g. The SSD weight is found to be 970 g. Calculate:
a) Moisture content
b) Water absorption

**Answers:**
a) Moisture content = ((Wet weight - Oven-dry weight) / Oven-dry weight) * 100 = ((1000 - 950) / 950) * 100 = (50 / 950) * 100 ≈ 5.26%
b) Water absorption = ((SSD weight - Oven-dry weight) / Oven-dry weight) * 100 = ((970 - 950) / 950) * 100 = (20 / 950) * 100 ≈ 2.11%

### 6. Bulking of Sand

*   **Definition:** The increase in the volume of sand due to the presence of a small amount of moisture on its surface.
*   **Mechanism:** Moisture films surrounding sand particles create surface tension forces, which hold the particles apart and trap air, causing the sand to occupy a larger volume than when it is dry or fully saturated.
*   **Effect:** If not accounted for, bulking can lead to an underestimation of the sand quantity and an overestimation of the cement content in a mix.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Correction during Batching:** The volume increase due to bulking needs to be accounted for by reducing the apparent volume of sand.
    *   **Light Compaction:** Lightly compacting the sand in the measuring container can reduce bulking.
    *   **Using SSD Sand:** Ideally, sand should be used in the Saturated Surface-Dry condition.
*   **Extent of Bulking:** Varies with the amount of moisture. It is maximum at about 4-8% moisture content and decreases as the moisture content increases further, becoming zero at the SSD condition.

**Indian Standard Reference:** IS 2386 Part III covers tests for bulking of sand.

**Key Concept:** Bulking is a phenomenon of volume expansion in sand due to surface moisture and must be corrected in concrete batching.

### 7. Deleterious Substances in Aggregates

Certain materials present in aggregates can adversely affect the properties of concrete, especially its durability and appearance.

*   **Clay and Silt:**
    *   **Effect:** Reduces bond between cement paste and aggregate, lowers strength, increases water demand, and can cause efflorescence.
    *   **Mitigation:** Washing of aggregates.
*   **Organic Impurities:**
    *   **Effect:** React with cement, retard or prevent hydration, reduce strength significantly.
    *   **Testing:** Colorimetric test for organic impurities (IS 2386 Part 2). If the color is darker than the standard, the aggregate is likely to contain harmful organic matter.
*   **Alkali-Reactive Substances (e.g., reactive silica, certain types of chert):**
    *   **Effect:** Can cause alkali-aggregate reaction (AAR), leading to expansion and cracking of concrete, especially in the presence of moisture.
    *   **Testing:** Chemical tests for potential reactivity (e.g., mortar bar expansion test, chemical immersion test as per IS 2386 Part 7).
*   **Sulphates:**
    *   **Effect:** Can cause disintegration and expansion of concrete.
    *   **Testing:** Chemical tests for sulphate content.
*   **Iron Pyrites:**
    *   **Effect:** Can cause staining and disintegration.
*   **Mica:**
    *   **Effect:** Flaky, reduces bond strength and workability.

**IS Specifications (IS 383:1970):**
Specifies maximum permissible limits for clay, silt, and deleterious materials.

**Example:** If a sand sample contains more than 3% silt, it is generally considered unsatisfactory for making concrete.

**Key Concept:** The presence of deleterious substances can severely compromise the performance and longevity of concrete structures.

### 8. Sieve Analysis of Aggregates

*   **Purpose:** To determine the particle size distribution of an aggregate sample. This is crucial for controlling the grading of aggregates, which in turn influences workability, density, and strength.
*   **Procedure:** A known weight of aggregate is passed through a series of standard IS sieves arranged in decreasing order of aperture size. The weight of aggregate retained on each sieve is measured.
*   **Key Sieves (IS 460 Part 1):** Common sieves used for aggregate analysis include 80 mm, 40 mm, 20 mm, 10 mm, 4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, 1.18 mm, 600 µm, 300 µm, 150 µm, 75 µm, and 90 µm.
*   **Data Presentation:**
    *   **Sieve Analysis Table:** Records the sieve size, weight retained on each sieve, cumulative weight retained, percentage retained on each sieve, and cumulative percentage retained.
    *   **Gradation Curve:** A graphical representation plotting cumulative percentage retained (on the y-axis) against sieve aperture size (on the x-axis, often on a logarithmic scale).

**Indian Standard Reference:** IS 2386 Part I describes the methods for sieve analysis.

**Calculation Example (for practice):**
A sample of coarse aggregate weighing 5000 g was subjected to sieve analysis. The weights retained on different sieves are as follows:
*   63 mm: 200 g
*   40 mm: 1200 g
*   20 mm: 2200 g
*   10 mm: 1000 g
*   4.75 mm: 300 g
*   Retained on 4.75 mm sieve (fines): 100 g

Calculate the cumulative percentage retained on each sieve and plot the gradation curve.

**Answer (Partial Calculation):**
| Sieve Size (mm) | Weight Retained (g) | Cumulative Weight Retained (g) | Cumulative % Retained |
| :-------------- | :------------------ | :----------------------------- | :-------------------- |
| 63              | 200                 | 200                            | (200/5000)*100 = 4.0  |
| 40              | 1200                | 1400                           | (1400/5000)*100 = 28.0 |
| 20              | 2200                | 3600                           | (3600/5000)*100 = 72.0 |
| 10              | 1000                | 4600                           | (4600/5000)*100 = 92.0 |
| 4.75            | 300                 | 4900                           | (4900/5000)*100 = 98.0 |
| Pan (<4.75)     | 100                 | 5000                           | (5000/5000)*100 = 100.0|

**(Plotting the curve requires plotting these cumulative percentages against the sieve sizes on graph paper).**

### 9. Aggregate Grading and Gradation Curves

*   **Aggregate Grading:** The process of combining aggregates of different sizes in specific proportions to achieve a desired particle size distribution.
*   **Importance of Grading:**
    *   **Workability:** Well-graded aggregates lead to a more cohesive mix and better workability.
    *   **Density:** A well-graded mix has fewer voids, resulting in a denser concrete and higher strength.
    *   **Economy:** Minimizes the amount of cement paste required to fill voids.
    *   **Durability:** Denser concrete is generally more durable.
    *   **Shrinkage:** Proper grading can help control drying shrinkage.

#### 9.1 Gradation Curves

*   **Definition:** A graphical plot showing the cumulative percentage of aggregate passing through a series of sieves against the sieve aperture size.
*   **Types of Grading:**
    *   **Single Sized Aggregate:** Aggregate retained on one sieve and passing the next larger sieve (e.g., all aggregate between 10 mm and 20 mm). Results in a steep vertical line on the graph. High void content, poor workability, and low density.
    *   **Well-Graded Aggregate:** Contains a wide range of particle sizes distributed in proportions that minimize void content. Results in a relatively smooth, continuous curve with a moderate slope. This is desirable for most concrete applications.
    *   **Gap-Graded Aggregate:** Lacks certain intermediate sizes of aggregate. Results in a curve with a steep slope in the missing size range. Can be used for specific purposes (e.g., roller-compacted concrete).
    *   **All-in-Aggregate:** A combination of fine and coarse aggregates.

**Indian Standard Reference:** IS 383:1970 provides specifications for grading of fine and coarse aggregates. It often specifies a range of permissible percentages passing each sieve, resulting in a "grading zone" rather than a single curve.

**Interpreting Gradation Curves:**
*   A curve closer to the top-left corner indicates a finer aggregate (more material passing smaller sieves).
*   A curve towards the bottom-right indicates a coarser aggregate.
*   A curve with a more gentle slope generally indicates better grading.

**Example:** For nominal size of coarse aggregate 20 mm, IS 383:1970 specifies limits for cumulative percentage passing various sieves.

### 10. Aggregate Testing as per Indian Standards

This section summarizes the key tests and their respective IS codes.

| Property Tested                | Indian Standard        | Purpose                                                                    |
| :----------------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sieve Analysis**             | IS 2386 Part I         | To determine particle size distribution.                                   |
| **Specific Gravity and Density** | IS 2386 Part III       | To determine the weight-volume relationship. Essential for mix design.   |
| **Water Absorption**           | IS 2386 Part III       | To determine the amount of water aggregates can absorb.                    |
| **Moisture Content**           | IS 2386 Part III       | To determine the amount of water present in aggregates.                    |
| **Bulking of Sand**            | IS 2386 Part III       | To measure the volume increase of sand due to moisture.                    |
| **Aggregate Crushing Value**   | IS 2386 Part IV        | To assess resistance to crushing under compressive load.                     |
| **Aggregate Impact Value**     | IS 2386 Part IV        | To assess resistance to sudden shock or impact.                              |
| **Hardness (Moh's Scale)**     | (General knowledge)    | Resistance to scratching.                                                  |
| **Toughness**                  | (General knowledge)    | Resistance to impact.                                                      |
| **Shape (Flakiness & Elongation)** | IS 2386 Part I      | To assess the proportion of flaky and elongated particles.               |
| **Cleanliness (Clay, Silt)**   | IS 2386 Part II        | To determine the amount of silt and clay content.                          |
| **Organic Impurities**         | IS 2386 Part II        | To detect the presence of harmful organic matter.                          |
| **Alkali-Aggregate Reactivity**| IS 2386 Part VII       | To assess the potential for alkali-aggregate reaction.                     |
| **Soundness**                  | IS 2386 Part V         | To assess the resistance of aggregates to disintegration due to weathering. |
| **Adhesion of Bitumen**        | IS 2386 Part VI        | Primarily for bituminous roads, but related to surface properties.       |

**Important Note:** Always refer to the latest versions of the relevant Indian Standards for detailed procedures and specifications.

### Practice Questions and Exercises

1.  **Question:** Briefly explain the role of aggregates in concrete.
    **Answer:** Aggregates provide bulk, strength, stability, and influence workability and durability in concrete, making up a significant volume of the mix.

2.  **Question:** Differentiate between fine and coarse aggregates.
    **Answer:** Fine aggregates pass a 4.75 mm sieve (e.g., sand), while coarse aggregates are retained on a 4.75 mm sieve (e.g., gravel, crushed stone). Fine aggregates fill voids and improve workability, while coarse aggregates provide bulk and strength.

3.  **Question:** How does the shape of an aggregate affect concrete properties? Give an example of a desirable shape and an undesirable shape.
    **Answer:** Cubical or rounded aggregates provide good workability and dense concrete due to lower void content. Angular aggregates offer better interlocking and bond, leading to higher strength. Flaky and elongated particles lead to poor workability and reduced strength. Example: Rounded gravel (desirable), flaky mica (undesirable).

4.  **Question:** What is the significance of aggregate crushing value, and what is a typical acceptable value for concrete used in pavements?
    **Answer:** Aggregate crushing value indicates the resistance to crushing under load. For concrete used in pavements, it should ideally be not more than 20%.

5.  **Question:** Explain the phenomenon of bulking of sand.
    **Answer:** Bulking is the increase in volume of sand due to the presence of a small amount of moisture, which creates surface tension forces holding particles apart.

6.  **Question:** What is the danger of using aggregates containing organic impurities? How is this tested?
    **Answer:** Organic impurities can retard or prevent cement hydration, significantly reducing concrete strength. This is tested using the colorimetric test for organic impurities (IS 2386 Part II).

7.  **Question:** A batch of concrete is made with 1000 kg of sand, which is found to have 6% moisture content. If the sand needs to be at SSD condition (which has 2% water absorption), what is the effective dry weight of sand in the batch? (Assume the moisture content is surface moisture).
    **Answer:**
    Let the total weight of sand be W = 1000 kg.
    Moisture content = 6% = 0.06.
    Weight of water = 1000 * 0.06 = 60 kg.
    Weight of dry aggregate = 1000 - 60 = 940 kg.
    This 940 kg is the oven-dry weight.
    SSD weight = Oven-dry weight * (1 + Absorption/100) = 940 * (1 + 2/100) = 940 * 1.02 = 958.8 kg.
    The question asks for the "effective dry weight" which can be interpreted as the oven-dry weight if the moisture is in excess of SSD, or the weight to be added if it's below SSD. Assuming the 1000 kg is the weight as batched and contains 6% moisture. The *dry weight* of this sand is 940 kg.

    Let's re-interpret the question to mean "If the sand batch weighed 1000 kg and contained 6% moisture, what is the actual *dry* weight of the sand solids present?".
    Dry weight = 1000 kg / (1 + 0.06) = 1000 / 1.06 ≈ 943.4 kg.
    The effective dry weight of sand solids is approximately 943.4 kg.

8.  **Question:** What information can be obtained from a sieve analysis, and why is it important for concrete mix design?
    **Answer:** Sieve analysis provides the particle size distribution of aggregates. This is crucial for mix design to select appropriate aggregate combinations that result in a well-graded mix, leading to optimal workability, density, strength, and economy.

9.  **Question:** What is the difference between a well-graded aggregate and a single-sized aggregate, as shown on a gradation curve?
    **Answer:** A well-graded aggregate has a wide range of particle sizes, resulting in a relatively smooth curve covering a significant portion of the sieve sizes. A single-sized aggregate has particles within a narrow size range, showing a steep vertical line on the graph between two sieve sizes.

10. **Question:** List three important properties of aggregates and their corresponding Indian Standard test codes.
    **Answer:**
    *   Sieve Analysis (IS 2386 Part I)
    *   Aggregate Impact Value (IS 2386 Part IV)
    *   Water Absorption (IS 2386 Part III)

### Important Points to Remember:

*   Aggregates are the backbone of concrete, providing bulk and strength.
*   Shape, texture, size, and cleanliness of aggregates critically influence concrete properties.
*   Adequate strength, hardness, and resistance to weathering are essential aggregate properties.
*   Moisture content and water absorption must be carefully managed to maintain the correct water-cement ratio.
*   Bulking of sand needs correction during batching.
*   Deleterious substances can severely compromise concrete durability.
*   Sieve analysis is fundamental for determining particle size distribution and achieving proper aggregate grading.
*   Well-graded aggregates are preferred for better workability, density, and economy.
*   Always refer to relevant Indian Standards for accurate testing procedures and specifications.
