# ARCHITECTURAL ENGINEERING: Module 1 - Definition of Architecture & Historical Development

## Topic: Architecture Design Process: The 7 Phases

This module introduces the fundamental definition and historical evolution of architecture, laying the groundwork for understanding the intricate process of architectural design. We will delve into the systematic journey from initial concept to the completed building, exploring the seven distinct phases involved.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

1.  **Define Architecture:** Articulate a comprehensive understanding of what constitutes architecture, encompassing its artistic, functional, and societal dimensions.
2.  **Trace Historical Development:** Outline the significant periods and influences that have shaped architectural styles and practices throughout history.
3.  **Identify and Describe the 7 Phases of the Architecture Design Process:** List and explain the purpose and key activities of each of the seven phases: Pre-Design, Schematic Design, Design Development, Construction Documents, Building Permit, Bidding and Negotiation, and Construction Administration.
4.  **Understand the Role of Each Phase:** Explain the specific contributions and deliverables expected at each stage of the design process.
5.  **Recognize the Interdependencies of Phases:** Understand how each phase builds upon the previous one and informs subsequent stages.

### 1. Definition of Architecture

Architecture is much more than just designing buildings. It's a multifaceted discipline that blends art, science, and technology to create spaces that shape human experience and impact society.

*   **Key Concepts & Definitions:**
    *   **Art:** Architecture is an art form that involves aesthetic considerations, the creation of beauty, and the expression of ideas and emotions through form, space, and materiality.
    *   **Science:** It relies on scientific principles of physics, structural engineering, materials science, and environmental control (HVAC, lighting, acoustics) to ensure safety, functionality, and sustainability.
    *   **Technology:** Architecture harnesses technological advancements for design, analysis, construction, and building performance.
    *   **Functionality:** Buildings must serve their intended purpose efficiently and comfortably, accommodating human activities and needs.
    *   **Aesthetics:** The visual appeal and sensory experience of a built environment, encompassing form, proportion, color, texture, and light.
    *   **Context:** Architecture must respond to its physical, social, cultural, and historical surroundings.
    *   **Sustainability:** Designing buildings that minimize negative environmental impact, conserve resources, and promote occupant well-being.
    *   **User Experience:** Creating spaces that are enjoyable, accessible, and conducive to the activities and interactions of the people who inhabit them.

*   **Key Quote:**
    *   "Architecture is the thoughtful making of spaces." - Louis Kahn
    *   "Form ever follows function." - Louis Sullivan

*   **Example:** The **Sydney Opera House** is a prime example of architecture as art, with its iconic sails creating a powerful aesthetic statement. However, it also incorporates significant scientific and technological considerations for its complex structure and acoustic performance.

### 2. Historical Development of Architecture

Architecture has evolved dramatically over millennia, reflecting human ingenuity, cultural values, technological advancements, and environmental adaptations.

*   **Key Concepts & Definitions:**
    *   **Prehistoric Architecture:** Early shelters and megalithic structures (e.g., Stonehenge) demonstrating rudimentary building techniques and communal practices.
    *   **Ancient Civilizations (Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, Rome):**
        *   **Mesopotamia:** Ziggurats, use of mud brick, development of arches and vaults.
        *   **Egypt:** Pyramids, temples, monumental scale, hieroglyphics, post-and-lintel system.
        *   **Greece:** Temples (Parthenon), emphasis on proportion, harmony, classical orders (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian), democracy influencing public spaces.
        *   **Rome:** Aqueducts, Colosseum, Pantheon, concrete, arches, vaults, domes, engineering prowess, development of urban planning.
    *   **Medieval Architecture (Byzantine, Romanesque, Gothic):**
        *   **Byzantine:** Hagia Sophia, domes, mosaics, centralized plan.
        *   **Romanesque:** Thick walls, rounded arches, barrel vaults, smaller windows, fortress-like appearance.
        *   **Gothic:** Cathedrals (Notre Dame), pointed arches, ribbed vaults, flying buttresses, stained glass, emphasis on verticality and light.
    *   **Renaissance Architecture:** Revival of classical principles, symmetry, proportion, domes, humanism, influence of architects like Brunelleschi and Palladio.
    *   **Baroque Architecture:** Grandeur, drama, ornamentation, curved forms, elaborate facades, dramatic use of light and shadow.
    *   **Neoclassical Architecture:** Return to classical ideals, simplicity, order, symmetry, often influenced by Enlightenment values.
    *   **Modern Architecture (Late 19th - Mid 20th Century):** Reaction against historical styles, emphasis on functionality, new materials (steel, glass, reinforced concrete), minimalist forms, pioneers like Le Corbusier, Mies van der Rohe, Frank Lloyd Wright.
        *   **Bauhaus:** A significant school influencing modern design with its integration of art, craft, and technology.
    *   **Postmodern Architecture:** Reaction against Modernism, embracing eclecticism, historical references, ornamentation, often playful and ironic.
    *   **Contemporary Architecture:** Current trends, diverse styles, emphasis on sustainability, digital design tools, parametricism, contextuality.

*   **Examples:**
    *   **Ancient Greek:** The **Parthenon** in Athens, embodying classical ideals of balance and proportion.
    *   **Roman:** The **Pantheon** in Rome, a marvel of engineering with its massive concrete dome.
    *   **Gothic:** **Notre Dame Cathedral** in Paris, showcasing pointed arches, flying buttresses, and elaborate stained glass.
    *   **Modern:** **Villa Savoye** by Le Corbusier, a paradigm of modern residential design with its "five points of architecture."
    *   **Contemporary:** The **Guggenheim Museum Bilbao** by Frank Gehry, a sculptural masterpiece using advanced digital design and construction techniques.

*   **Highlight:** Understanding historical precedents provides valuable lessons in construction methods, material use, spatial organization, and the cultural context of building.

### 3. The Architecture Design Process: The 7 Phases

The architectural design process is a structured journey from a client's initial idea to a completed building. While it's often depicted linearly, it's an iterative and collaborative process involving various stakeholders.

**The 7 Phases:**

1.  **Pre-Design Phase (Programming / Project Initiation)**
2.  **Schematic Design Phase**
3.  **Design Development Phase**
4.  **Construction Documents Phase**
5.  **Building Permit Phase**
6.  **Bidding and Negotiation Phase**
7.  **Construction Administration Phase**

---

#### Phase 1: Pre-Design Phase (Programming / Project Initiation)

This foundational phase is about understanding the client's needs, goals, and the project's feasibility. It sets the stage for everything that follows.

*   **Purpose:** To define the project's scope, objectives, budget, and site constraints. To establish a clear understanding of what needs to be designed and why.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Client Consultation:** In-depth discussions with the client to understand their vision, needs, functional requirements, and desired aesthetic.
    *   **Program Development:** Creating a detailed document outlining all the spaces required, their size, relationships, and specific functions (e.g., number of offices, seating capacity, circulation needs).
    *   **Site Analysis:** Evaluating the chosen site, considering factors like:
        *   Topography and soil conditions
        *   Climate (sun path, wind, rainfall)
        *   Existing utilities and infrastructure
        *   Zoning regulations and building codes
        *   Surrounding context and views
        *   Environmental impact and sustainability considerations
    *   **Feasibility Studies:** Assessing the technical, economic, and legal viability of the project. Can it be built within the budget? Are there any major legal hurdles?
    *   **Budget Definition:** Establishing a preliminary budget for design, construction, and related costs.
    *   **Team Assembly:** Identifying and engaging other consultants (structural engineers, mechanical engineers, landscape architects, etc.).
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   Program document
    *   Site analysis report
    *   Preliminary budget
    *   Project brief or executive summary
*   **Example:** For a new community library, the Pre-Design phase would involve meeting with library staff, community members, and the city council to understand the desired collection size, number of reading spaces, computer labs, meeting rooms, and accessibility requirements. Site analysis would assess available land, access routes, and zoning.

*   **Highlight:** A thorough Pre-Design phase is crucial for project success, preventing costly changes later in the process.

---

#### Phase 2: Schematic Design Phase (SD)

This phase translates the programmatic requirements into conceptual design solutions. It explores different ways to meet the project's goals.

*   **Purpose:** To develop a clear conceptual design that addresses the programmatic requirements and establishes the overall form, massing, and spatial relationships of the building.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Developing Design Concepts:** Exploring multiple design ideas and approaches through sketches, diagrams, and simple models.
    *   **Spatial Layouts:** Creating preliminary floor plans, elevations, and sections to illustrate the organization of spaces.
    *   **Massing Studies:** Developing three-dimensional models to understand the building's overall form and its relationship to the site.
    *   **Preliminary Material Selection:** Considering potential exterior and interior materials.
    *   **Initial Structural and MEP (Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing) Concepts:** Early coordination with engineers to ensure structural and system feasibility.
    *   **Budget Refinement:** Updating the budget based on the developed concepts.
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   Schematic drawings (floor plans, elevations, sections)
    *   3D massing models
    *   Conceptual site plan
    *   Preliminary outline specifications
    *   Revised budget estimate
*   **Example:** For the library, Schematic Design might explore options for a single-story building versus a multi-story structure, the placement of the entrance, the arrangement of reading areas with views, and the overall architectural style.

*   **Highlight:** This is where the "look and feel" of the building begins to take shape. Client approval of the schematic design is critical before proceeding.

---

#### Phase 3: Design Development Phase (DD)

This phase refines the approved schematic design, adding detail and further developing the building's systems and materials.

*   **Purpose:** To further develop the design, detailing the building's components, systems, and materials, ensuring that all elements are coordinated and meet project requirements.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Detailed Floor Plans, Elevations, and Sections:** Refining drawings with more precise dimensions and details.
    *   **Development of Building Systems:** Coordinating and detailing structural systems, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems with engineers.
    *   **Material Specification:** Selecting specific materials for finishes, structure, and building envelope, considering performance and aesthetics.
    *   **Door and Window Schedules:** Developing preliminary schedules for these components.
    *   **Life Safety Systems Design:** Incorporating fire suppression, alarm systems, and emergency egress.
    *   **Cost Estimating:** Developing a more accurate cost estimate based on the detailed design.
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   More detailed architectural drawings (plans, elevations, sections, details)
    *   Preliminary structural, mechanical, and electrical drawings
    *   Specifications for major materials and systems
    *   Updated cost estimate
*   **Example:** For the library, Design Development would specify the types of shelving, seating, lighting fixtures, HVAC system, and the precise construction of the building envelope (e.g., type of glazing, insulation).

*   **Highlight:** The DD phase is crucial for coordinating all building systems and ensuring the design is technically sound and within budget.

---

#### Phase 4: Construction Documents Phase (CD)

This is the phase where the detailed plans and specifications are finalized, providing all the necessary information for bidding and construction.

*   **Purpose:** To produce a complete set of technical drawings and specifications that clearly and precisely communicate the design intent to contractors for bidding and construction.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Finalizing Architectural Drawings:** Producing fully detailed plans, elevations, sections, details, and schedules.
    *   **Finalizing Engineering Drawings:** Completing structural, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing drawings.
    *   **Writing Specifications:** Preparing detailed written documents that describe the quality of materials, workmanship, and installation requirements.
    *   **Coordination of All Disciplines:** Ensuring all architectural and engineering documents are fully coordinated.
    *   **Value Engineering (Optional):** Reviewing the design for cost-saving opportunities without compromising quality or function.
    *   **Obtaining Owner Approval:** Presenting the complete construction documents to the owner for final approval.
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   Complete set of architectural and engineering construction drawings (blueprints)
    *   Project manual (including specifications, contract forms, conditions of the contract)
*   **Example:** The Construction Documents for the library would include precise dimensions for every wall, detailed instructions for installing the roofing system, specifications for the carpet in the children's area, and diagrams for the electrical wiring and plumbing layout.

*   **Highlight:** These documents are legally binding and are the primary reference for contractors during construction. Accuracy and completeness are paramount.

---

#### Phase 5: Building Permit Phase

This phase involves submitting the construction documents to the relevant authorities for review and approval.

*   **Purpose:** To obtain legal permission from the local building department to proceed with construction, ensuring compliance with all applicable building codes, zoning ordinances, and safety regulations.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Submitting Construction Documents:** Filing the complete set of drawings and specifications with the local building department.
    *   **Plan Review:** Building officials review the documents to ensure they meet all regulatory requirements.
    *   **Addressing Comments and Revisions:** Making necessary revisions to the documents based on feedback from the building department.
    *   **Issuance of Building Permit:** Once approved, the permit is issued, allowing construction to commence.
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   Issued Building Permit
*   **Example:** The library's construction documents would be submitted to the city's planning and building department, who would check for compliance with fire codes, structural safety standards, accessibility requirements, and zoning setbacks.

*   **Highlight:** This phase can sometimes cause delays depending on the efficiency of the permitting process and the complexity of the project.

---

#### Phase 6: Bidding and Negotiation Phase

In this phase, contractors are invited to submit bids for the construction of the project based on the construction documents.

*   **Purpose:** To select a qualified contractor who can build the project according to the construction documents, within the allocated budget, and in a timely manner.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Contractor Prequalification (Optional):** Assessing the experience and financial stability of potential contractors.
    *   **Issuing Bid Documents:** Providing the construction documents and specifications to prospective bidders.
    *   **Receiving Bids:** Contractors submit sealed bids with their proposed cost and schedule.
    *   **Bid Analysis:** Evaluating the bids for completeness, accuracy, and compliance.
    *   **Contractor Selection:** Choosing the winning bidder based on price, experience, and proposed methodology.
    *   **Contract Negotiation:** Discussing and finalizing the contract terms with the selected contractor.
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   Awarded Construction Contract
*   **Example:** After the library's permit is secured, architectural firms would invite construction companies to bid. The bids would be opened, analyzed, and a general contractor would be selected to build the library.

*   **Highlight:** This phase requires careful consideration to ensure the chosen contractor is capable of delivering the project as intended.

---

#### Phase 7: Construction Administration Phase (CA)

This is the final phase, where the architect oversees the construction process to ensure it aligns with the design intent and specifications.

*   **Purpose:** To ensure that the construction is carried out in accordance with the design documents, specifications, and contract, and to act as the owner's representative on the construction site.
*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Site Visits and Observations:** Regularly visiting the construction site to observe progress and the quality of work.
    *   **Reviewing Submittals:** Evaluating shop drawings, material samples, and product data submitted by the contractor.
    *   **Responding to Requests for Information (RFIs):** Answering questions from the contractor regarding the construction documents.
    *   **Issuing Construction Change Directives and Change Orders:** Documenting and authorizing any modifications to the original design or contract.
    *   **Reviewing Contractor Payment Applications:** Verifying the work completed for progress payments.
    *   **Punch List Preparation:** Creating a list of deficient items that need to be corrected before final completion.
    *   **Project Closeout:** Ensuring all project documentation, including as-built drawings and warranties, is submitted.
*   **Deliverables:**
    *   Completed project
    *   As-built drawings
    *   Warranties and operation manuals
*   **Example:** Throughout the construction of the library, the architect would visit the site to ensure the walls are built correctly, the plumbing is installed as specified, and the finishes match the approved samples. They would also review the contractor's requests for payment and address any issues that arise.

*   **Highlight:** The architect's role during Construction Administration is crucial for ensuring the quality and integrity of the built project.

### Practice Questions & Exercises:

**Question 1:**
Which phase of the architectural design process focuses on defining the project's scope, objectives, and budget?
a) Schematic Design
b) Design Development
c) Pre-Design
d) Construction Documents

**Answer:** c) Pre-Design

---

**Question 2:**
The creation of detailed architectural and engineering drawings and specifications that contractors will use for bidding and construction occurs in which phase?
a) Bidding and Negotiation
b) Construction Administration
c) Schematic Design
d) Construction Documents

**Answer:** d) Construction Documents

---

**Question 3:**
Name the seven phases of the architectural design process in their typical chronological order.

**Answer:**
1.  Pre-Design
2.  Schematic Design
3.  Design Development
4.  Construction Documents
5.  Building Permit
6.  Bidding and Negotiation
7.  Construction Administration

---

**Question 4:**
What is the primary purpose of the Schematic Design phase?
a) To select a contractor.
b) To develop conceptual design solutions that address the programmatic requirements.
c) To obtain a building permit.
d) To administer the construction contract.

**Answer:** b) To develop conceptual design solutions that address the programmatic requirements.

---

**Question 5:**
Imagine you are designing a new school. Describe one key activity that would occur during the Design Development phase for this project.

**Answer (Example):**
During the Design Development phase for a school, a key activity would be the detailed design of the classroom layouts, including specifying the types of desks, chairs, smart boards, lighting levels, and the HVAC system for each classroom to ensure an optimal learning environment.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Iterative Nature:** The design process is not strictly linear; there is often overlap and iteration between phases. Feedback from later stages can necessitate revisiting earlier decisions.
*   **Collaboration:** Architectural design is a highly collaborative effort involving the client, architect, engineers, consultants, contractors, and regulatory bodies.
*   **Client Involvement:** The client's active participation and timely decision-making are critical at every stage.
*   **Budget Management:** The budget is a constant consideration throughout the entire process, influencing design decisions and material choices.
*   **Code Compliance:** Adherence to building codes, zoning regulations, and safety standards is non-negotiable and integrated into every phase.
*   **Documentation:** Meticulous record-keeping and clear communication through drawings, specifications, and correspondence are vital for project success.
