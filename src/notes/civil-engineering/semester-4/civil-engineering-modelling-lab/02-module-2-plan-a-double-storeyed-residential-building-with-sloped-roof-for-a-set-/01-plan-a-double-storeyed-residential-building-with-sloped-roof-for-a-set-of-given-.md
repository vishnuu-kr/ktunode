# CIVIL ENGINEERING MODELLING LAB: Module 2 - Double Storey Residential Building with Sloped Roof

## Topic: Plan and Draw a Double Storeyed Residential Building with Sloped Roof for Given Requirements on a Given Plot

This module focuses on the practical application of architectural and structural design principles to create a comprehensive set of drawings for a double-storey residential building with a sloped roof. You will learn to translate client requirements and site conditions into a functional and aesthetically pleasing design.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

*   **Understand and interpret building codes and regulations relevant to residential construction.**
*   **Analyze and utilize given plot dimensions and site constraints effectively.**
*   **Develop a functional and space-efficient floor plan for a double-storey residential building, considering circulation, natural light, ventilation, and user needs.**
*   **Design a suitable sloped roof system that complements the building's architecture and provides adequate weather protection.**
*   **Generate detailed architectural drawings including:**
    *   **Site Plan:** Showing the building's location on the plot, property boundaries, setbacks, access, and surrounding features.
    *   **Floor Plans (Ground & First Floor):** Illustrating the layout of rooms, walls, doors, windows, stairs, and other architectural elements.
    *   **Sections:** Cutting through the building vertically to reveal internal construction details, floor levels, and roof structure.
    *   **Elevations:** Depicting the external appearance of the building from different sides, including window/door openings, roof profiles, and material finishes.
*   **Apply appropriate drafting conventions and standards in the preparation of drawings.**
*   **Present the design effectively and professionally.**

### 1. Key Concepts and Definitions

#### 1.1 Building Codes and Regulations

*   **Building Codes:** A set of rules and standards that specify the minimum requirements for the design, construction, and maintenance of buildings to ensure public safety, health, and welfare. These vary by region and country.
    *   *Example:* Fire safety regulations, structural integrity requirements, ventilation standards, accessibility provisions.
*   **Zoning Regulations:** Local government ordinances that dictate land use, building height, setbacks, lot coverage, and other aspects of development within specific areas.
*   **Setbacks:** The minimum distance a building must be from property lines, roads, or other structures.
*   **Floor Area Ratio (FAR):** The ratio of a building's total floor area to the size of the land on which it is built.

#### 1.2 Site Analysis and Planning

*   **Plot Dimensions:** The length, width, and area of the available land.
*   **Site Constraints:** Factors that limit design options, such as:
    *   **Topography:** The shape and slope of the land.
    *   **Soil Conditions:** The type of soil and its bearing capacity.
    *   **Orientation:** The direction the plot faces, affecting sunlight and wind exposure.
    *   **Existing Utilities:** Location of water, sewer, electricity, and gas lines.
    *   **Surrounding Environment:** Adjacent buildings, trees, noise sources.
*   **Circulation:** The movement of people and vehicles within and around the building.
    *   *Internal Circulation:* Hallways, staircases, corridors.
    *   *External Circulation:* Driveways, pathways, pedestrian access.
*   **Natural Light and Ventilation:** Designing for optimal use of sunlight and airflow to enhance comfort and reduce energy consumption.

#### 1.3 Architectural Elements

*   **Storey:** A level or floor within a building. A double-storey building has a ground floor and a first floor.
*   **Sloped Roof:** A roof with an inclined surface, designed to shed water and snow effectively. Types include:
    *   **Gable Roof:** Two sloping sides meeting at a ridge.
    *   **Hip Roof:** Four sloping sides that meet at the eaves.
    *   **Shed Roof (Lean-to):** A single sloping plane.
    *   **Mansard Roof:** A four-sided gambrel-style roof.
*   **Walls:** Vertical elements that enclose spaces.
    *   *Load-bearing Walls:* Support the weight of the floors and roof.
    *   *Non-load-bearing Walls (Partition Walls):* Divide spaces within the building.
*   **Floors:** Horizontal elements that separate storeys.
*   **Doors:** Openings in walls for access.
*   **Windows:** Openings in walls for light and ventilation.
*   **Stairs:** Connect different levels of the building.
    *   *Types:* Straight, L-shaped, U-shaped, spiral.
    *   *Components:* Treads, risers, nosing, handrail, balustrade.
*   **Foundation:** The part of the structure that transmits loads to the ground.
    *   *Types:* Strip foundation, raft foundation, pile foundation. (While not explicitly drawn in detail in this module's drawings, understanding their purpose is important).
*   **Plinth Beam:** A beam at the plinth level that helps to distribute the load from the walls and tie them together.

#### 1.4 Drawing Conventions

*   **Line Types:** Different line weights and styles to represent various features (e.g., thick lines for visible outlines, dashed lines for hidden elements).
*   **Scale:** The ratio of a drawing to the actual object. Common scales for architectural drawings include 1:50, 1:100.
*   **Dimensioning:** Adding measurements to drawings to indicate sizes and locations of elements.
*   **Hatching:** Using lines or patterns to indicate materials or cross-sections.
*   **Symbols:** Standard graphical representations for doors, windows, stairs, etc.

### 2. Planning a Double Storey Residential Building with Sloped Roof

This involves a systematic approach, translating requirements into drawings.

#### 2.1 Understanding Requirements and Plot Information

*   **Client Requirements:**
    *   Number of bedrooms, bathrooms, living areas, kitchen, dining.
    *   Specific room sizes or features.
    *   Budget considerations (influencing material choices and complexity).
    *   Family size and lifestyle.
*   **Plot Information:**
    *   Plot size and shape.
    *   Boundary lines and setbacks.
    *   Orientation (North, South, East, West).
    *   Access points (road frontage).
    *   Existing utilities or features.
    *   Local building bye-laws.

#### 2.2 Developing the Floor Plans

**2.2.1 Ground Floor Plan:**

*   **Placement:** Position the building on the plot, respecting setbacks. Consider access from the road.
*   **Room Layout:**
    *   **Entrance:** Typically leads into a living area or foyer.
    *   **Living/Drawing Room:** Usually placed near the entrance.
    *   **Dining Room:** Accessible from the living room and kitchen.
    *   **Kitchen:** Conveniently located for access from the dining area and potentially the rear.
    *   **One Bedroom/Guest Room:** Often on the ground floor for accessibility.
    *   **Bathroom/Toilet:** Accessible from common areas and the bedroom.
    *   **Staircase:** Strategically placed for efficient circulation to the first floor.
*   **Circulation:** Ensure clear and unobstructed pathways (corridors, living areas) to all rooms.
*   **Natural Light & Ventilation:** Position windows to maximize natural light and cross-ventilation. Consider the orientation (e.g., North-facing windows for consistent light, East-facing for morning sun).
*   **Plinth Level:** Define the ground floor level.

**2.2.2 First Floor Plan:**

*   **Layout:** Primarily for private spaces like bedrooms and bathrooms.
*   **Bedrooms:** Locate bedrooms for privacy and adequate light/ventilation.
*   **Bathrooms:** Position bathrooms conveniently for bedrooms.
*   **Balconies:** May be incorporated for some bedrooms.
*   **Staircase Landing:** Ensure a smooth transition from the staircase.
*   **Openings:** Place doors and windows for functional use and aesthetics.

#### 2.3 Designing the Sloped Roof

*   **Type Selection:** Choose a roof type (e.g., gable, hip) based on aesthetics, site conditions (wind, snow), and ease of construction.
*   **Pitch/Slope:** Determine the roof pitch. This is crucial for water runoff and is often specified in building codes.
*   **Ridge and Eaves:** Define the highest point (ridge) and the lowest edges (eaves) of the roof.
*   **Overhangs:** Design roof overhangs at the eaves to protect walls from rain and sun.
*   **Material:** Consider roofing materials (e.g., tiles, shingles, metal sheets) which influence the visual appearance and structural requirements.

#### 2.4 Developing the Sections

Sections are crucial for understanding the building's internal construction and vertical relationships.

*   **Cutting Plane:** Imagine a vertical plane slicing through the building, typically passing through key features like staircases, living areas, and bedrooms.
*   **Revealed Elements:**
    *   **Floor Slabs/Levels:** Clearly show the height of the ground floor, first floor, and ceiling.
    *   **Walls:** Show the thickness of internal and external walls.
    *   **Staircase:** Illustrate the treads, risers, and headroom.
    *   **Doors and Windows:** Show their positions and heights from the floor.
    *   **Roof Structure:** Depict the rafters, purlins, and roofing material.
    *   **Foundation (Implied):** Indicate the plinth beam and the top of the foundation (though full foundation details might be on a separate structural drawing).
    *   **Ceiling:** Show the ceiling line.
*   **Dimensioning:** Add key vertical dimensions (e.g., floor to floor height, sill height, lintel height, ridge height).
*   **Hatching:** Use appropriate hatching patterns to indicate different materials (e.g., concrete, brickwork).

#### 2.5 Developing the Elevations

Elevations showcase the external appearance of the building.

*   **Views:** Typically North, South, East, and West elevations.
*   **Elements Shown:**
    *   **External Walls:** Overall dimensions and appearance.
    *   **Doors and Windows:** Their size, position, and style.
    *   **Roof Profile:** The shape and slope of the roof, including any dormers or other features.
    *   **Parapet Walls:** If any.
    *   **Balconies:** Their design and projection.
    *   **Material Finishes:** Indicate proposed external finishes (e.g., plaster, paint, stone cladding).
    *   **Ground Line:** Show the level of the ground relative to the building.
*   **Dimensioning:** Add key horizontal dimensions and heights.

#### 2.6 The Site Plan

The site plan shows the building's placement within its plot and its relationship to the surroundings.

*   **Plot Boundaries:** Clearly delineate the property lines with dimensions.
*   **Building Footprint:** Outline the external walls of the building at ground level.
*   **Setbacks:** Indicate the distance of the building from all property lines.
*   **Access:** Show the driveway from the road and any pedestrian paths.
*   **Surrounding Features:** Include existing roads, adjacent properties, trees, water bodies, or other relevant elements.
*   **Orientation Arrow:** Indicate the North direction.
*   **Scale:** The site plan is usually drawn at a smaller scale than the floor plans (e.g., 1:200 or 1:500).

### 3. Drawing Practice: Step-by-Step Workflow

**Assumed Scenario:** A rectangular plot of 15m x 25m with road access from one of the longer sides (25m). Requirements: Ground floor - Living room, Dining, Kitchen, One Bedroom, One Toilet/Bathroom, Staircase. First floor - Two Bedrooms, One common Bathroom, one attached Bathroom. Sloped roof.

**Steps:**

1.  **Understand the Plot and Requirements:**
    *   Plot Size: 15m x 25m.
    *   Road Access: On a 25m side.
    *   Setbacks: Assume standard setbacks (e.g., 3m front, 2m sides, 3m rear).

2.  **Draw the Site Plan:**
    *   Draw the plot boundary as a rectangle (e.g., 25 units wide, 15 units high on paper, scaled).
    *   Draw road access on one of the 25m sides.
    *   Mark setback lines inside the plot.
    *   Position the building footprint within the setbacks, considering the road access and desired orientation.
    *   Draw the driveway.
    *   Add North arrow and scale.

3.  **Draw the Ground Floor Plan:**
    *   Draw the building outline based on the planned footprint.
    *   Place the entrance from the driveway.
    *   Zone the spaces: Living room near the entrance, kitchen and dining adjacent, bedroom and toilet tucked away.
    *   Locate the staircase centrally or in a convenient position for access from both floors.
    *   Draw internal walls, doors, and windows with appropriate symbols.
    *   Add dimensions for all rooms and elements.
    *   Indicate the ground floor level (GF).

4.  **Draw the First Floor Plan:**
    *   Align it directly above the ground floor plan.
    *   The external walls will be the same, but internal partitions will differ.
    *   Place the two bedrooms and two bathrooms as per requirements.
    *   Ensure circulation to all rooms.
    *   Add doors and windows.
    *   Add dimensions.
    *   Indicate the first floor level (FF).
    *   Show the staircase landing.

5.  **Design the Sloped Roof:**
    *   Decide on a roof type (e.g., a simple gable roof).
    *   Determine the roof pitch (e.g., 30 degrees).
    *   Draw the roof outline extending beyond the walls for overhangs.
    *   Show the ridge line and eaves.

6.  **Draw the Section (e.g., through living room and a bedroom):**
    *   Imagine cutting through the building.
    *   Draw the ground line, plinth beam, ground floor slab, wall thicknesses, first floor slab, ceiling, and the sloped roof structure (rafters, purlins).
    *   Show the internal staircase, including treads and risers.
    *   Indicate heights of floor levels, doors, windows, and the ridge.
    *   Add hatching for materials.

7.  **Draw the Elevations (e.g., Front Elevation facing the road):**
    *   Project the outline of the building from the floor plans.
    *   Draw the ground line.
    *   Depict the doors and windows as they would appear externally.
    *   Draw the sloped roof profile, including overhangs.
    *   Add any external finishes or decorative elements.
    *   Add key dimensions.

### 4. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the purpose of a setback in a site plan?
**Answer:** Setbacks are mandatory distances required between a building and property lines, roads, or other structures. They ensure adequate light and ventilation, fire safety, and allow for future infrastructure or landscaping.

**Question 2:** Name three common types of sloped roofs.
**Answer:** Gable roof, Hip roof, Shed roof (Lean-to roof).

**Question 3:** In a section drawing, what does hatching typically represent?
**Answer:** Hatching in a section drawing represents different building materials (e.g., concrete, brickwork, steel, timber) and indicates the cut surfaces.

**Question 4:** If a plot has a road frontage of 20m and the building is to be placed 5m from the front boundary and 3m from the side boundaries, calculate the maximum buildable width.
**Answer:**
Buildable width = Plot width - Front setback - Rear setback.
Assuming the plot width is measured perpendicular to the road, and we're looking at the buildable width parallel to the road:
Buildable width = 20m (Plot width) - 3m (Side setback 1) - 3m (Side setback 2) = 14m.
If considering the depth:
Buildable depth = Plot depth - Front setback - Rear setback. (Need plot depth to answer this).

**Question 5:** Why is it important to consider the orientation of the building on the plot?
**Answer:** Orientation affects natural light and ventilation. Placing living areas on the north side can provide consistent, diffused light without excessive heat. East-facing rooms get morning sun, while west-facing rooms can get strong afternoon sun, which might need shading. South-facing rooms receive good daylight throughout the day.

### 5. Important Points to Remember

*   **Adhere to building codes and local regulations.**
*   **Prioritize functionality and circulation in floor plan design.**
*   **Maximize natural light and ventilation.**
*   **Ensure structural soundness by considering the roof load and wall support (even if not detailing the structure).**
*   **Maintain consistency in scale and drafting conventions across all drawings.**
*   **Clearly label all rooms, dimensions, and elements.**
*   **The site plan is the first step; it dictates where the building sits.**
*   **Sections and elevations provide crucial detail on vertical aspects and external appearance, respectively.**
*   **Accuracy in dimensions is paramount.**
*   **A neat and organized presentation enhances the understanding of your design.**
