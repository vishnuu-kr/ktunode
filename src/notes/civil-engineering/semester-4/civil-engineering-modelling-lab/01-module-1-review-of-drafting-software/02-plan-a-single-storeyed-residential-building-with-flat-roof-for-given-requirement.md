# CIVIL ENGINEERING MODELLING LAB: Module 1 - Review of Drafting Software

## Topic: Planning a Single-Storeyed Residential Building with Flat Roof and Drawing Site Plan, Plan, Section, and Elevation

This module provides a foundational understanding of drafting software by applying it to a practical scenario: designing and drawing a single-storey residential building. This process involves understanding architectural elements, spatial planning, and the representation of these in different drawing formats.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   **Understand and interpret architectural design requirements for a single-storey residential building.**
*   **Develop a conceptual layout for a residential building based on given functional and spatial needs.**
*   **Create a Site Plan illustrating the building's placement on the plot, along with relevant site features.**
*   **Draw a detailed Floor Plan showing the arrangement of rooms, walls, doors, windows, and other architectural elements.**
*   **Generate a Sectional View to depict the internal construction and vertical relationships of building components.**
*   **Produce an Elevation View to represent the external appearance of the building.**
*   **Utilize drafting software effectively to produce these architectural drawings accurately and efficiently.**

### Key Concepts and Definitions:

#### 1. Architectural Drawings:

These are graphical representations that communicate the design of a building. They are essential for construction, planning, and client visualization.

*   **Site Plan:** A drawing showing the entire property boundary, the proposed building's location on the plot, surrounding structures, roads, utility lines, and landscape features. It provides context for the building's placement.
    *   **Key Elements:** Property lines, building footprint, setbacks (minimum distance from property lines), access roads, walkways, existing trees, utilities (water, sewer, electricity), ground contours (optional but good practice).
    *   **Scale:** Typically drawn to a smaller scale (e.g., 1:100, 1:200) to encompass the entire site.

*   **Floor Plan (Plan):** A top-down view of a building, showing the layout of rooms, walls, doors, windows, stairs, and other interior features as if a horizontal cut were made through the building at sill height (usually around 1.2m or 4ft).
    *   **Key Elements:** Walls (internal and external), doors (with swing direction), windows, rooms (labeled with dimensions and purpose), fixtures (toilets, sinks, kitchen counters), stairs, dimensions (overall and room-specific), grid lines (for structural reference).
    *   **Scale:** Typically drawn to a larger scale than the site plan (e.g., 1:50, 1:100) to show detailed information.

*   **Sectional View (Section):** A drawing that shows the internal construction of a building as if it were cut by a vertical plane. It reveals the relationships between different building elements in the vertical direction.
    *   **Key Elements:** Foundation details, floor slabs, wall construction (materials, thickness), roof structure, door and window heights, ceiling heights, materials indicated by hatching patterns.
    *   **Scale:** Similar to the floor plan scale (e.g., 1:50, 1:100).
    *   **Cutting Plane Line:** Indicated on the floor plan by a heavy dashed line with arrows showing the direction of view.

*   **Elevation View:** A drawing that shows the external appearance of a building from one side. It typically depicts the finished surfaces, materials, window and door placements, and roof forms.
    *   **Key Elements:** Building facade, external materials, window and door styles, roofline, building height, ground level, architectural features (balconies, porches).
    *   **Scale:** Similar to the floor plan scale (e.g., 1:50, 1:100).
    *   **Projection:** Elevations are drawn by projecting lines from the floor plan and sections vertically.

#### 2. Single-Storeyed Residential Building:

A building with only one habitable level above ground.

#### 3. Flat Roof:

A roof that is nearly level, with a very slight slope for drainage. Unlike pitched roofs, flat roofs are often used for modern designs and can sometimes be utilized as usable space (terraces).

#### 4. Drafting Software (CAD - Computer-Aided Design):

Software used for creating, modifying, analyzing, and optimizing designs. Examples include AutoCAD, Revit, SketchUp, etc.

### Planning a Single-Storeyed Residential Building: Given Requirements (Example Scenario)

Let's assume the following requirements for a single-storey residential building:

*   **Plot Size:** 20m x 30m (600 sq m)
*   **Building Footprint:** Approximately 120 sq m
*   **Occupancy:** Family of four
*   **Rooms Required:**
    *   Living Room (spacious)
    *   Dining Area
    *   Kitchen (modern, with pantry)
    *   Master Bedroom (with attached toilet)
    *   Bedroom 2
    *   Common Toilet
    *   Verandah/Porch at the entrance
*   **Setbacks:** 3m from front and rear property lines, 2m from side property lines.
*   **Roof:** Flat concrete roof.
*   **Structure:** Load-bearing walls or framed structure with reinforced concrete columns and beams.
*   **Orientation:** Assume the plot faces north.

#### **Step 1: Conceptual Layout & Zoning (Conceptualization)**

Before drawing, mentally or on scratch paper, decide the placement of rooms based on functionality and privacy.

*   **Public Zones:** Living Room, Dining Area, Verandah should be near the entrance.
*   **Private Zones:** Bedrooms should be placed away from the main entrance and living areas.
*   **Service Areas:** Kitchen and toilets should be conveniently located.
*   **Circulation:** Ensure sufficient corridor space for movement.

**Example Layout Idea:**

*   Entrance Verandah leading into the Living Room.
*   Living Room adjacent to the Dining Area.
*   Kitchen accessible from the Dining Area, possibly with a pantry.
*   Corridor leading to Bedrooms and Common Toilet.
*   Master Bedroom with attached toilet at one end of the corridor.
*   Bedroom 2 and Common Toilet on the other side.

#### **Step 2: Drawing the Site Plan**

1.  **Draw the Plot Boundary:** Create a rectangle representing the 20m x 30m plot. Label the dimensions.
2.  **Define Setbacks:** Draw inner lines parallel to the plot boundaries at the specified setback distances (3m front/rear, 2m sides). The building footprint must be within these lines.
3.  **Place the Building Footprint:** Draw a rectangle or irregular shape representing the planned building within the setback lines. Consider orientation (e.g., placing living areas towards the front/north for light).
4.  **Add Access:** Show the entrance road/driveway and pathways leading to the building entrance.
5.  **Include Site Features:** Indicate any existing trees, boundary walls, or utility connections if provided in the requirements.
6.  **Label and Dimension:** Label the plot, building, and any other features. Dimension the plot boundaries and setbacks. Add a North arrow.
7.  **Choose a Scale:** Select an appropriate scale (e.g., 1:200).

#### **Step 3: Drawing the Floor Plan**

1.  **Establish Grid Lines (Optional but recommended for framed structures):** Draw a grid of lines for structural reference.
2.  **Draw External Walls:** Based on the chosen footprint and setbacks, draw the outer walls of the building. Define wall thicknesses (e.g., 230mm or 9-inch brick walls).
3.  **Draw Internal Walls:** Divide the internal space according to the room layout. Again, consider wall thicknesses.
4.  **Add Doors:** Draw door symbols, indicating their swing direction. Ensure adequate door widths for circulation and accessibility.
5.  **Add Windows:** Draw window symbols on external and sometimes internal walls.
6.  **Place Fixtures:** Add symbols for sanitary fixtures (toilets, sinks, washbasins) and kitchen elements (sink, counter).
7.  **Add Dimensions:** Dimension the overall building dimensions, room dimensions, wall thicknesses, and door/window openings.
8.  **Label Rooms:** Clearly label each room with its name (e.g., "Living Room," "Master Bedroom," "Kitchen").
9.  **Indicate Materials (Optional at this stage, but good practice):** You can use simple hatching for different wall types if required.
10. **Add a Title Block:** Include drawing title, scale, date, and designer's name.
11. **Choose a Scale:** Select an appropriate scale (e.g., 1:50).

#### **Step 4: Drawing the Sectional View**

1.  **Decide Cutting Plane:** Imagine a vertical plane cutting through the building. Common sections cut through living areas, stairs, or a combination of rooms and toilets. Mark the cutting plane line on the floor plan.
2.  **Draw the Foundation:** Start by drawing the foundation element (e.g., footing, plinth beam). Indicate its dimensions and material.
3.  **Draw the Floor Slab:** Show the floor slab above the foundation/plinth.
4.  **Draw Walls:** Draw the internal and external walls as they are cut by the plane. Show the wall construction layers (e.g., brickwork, plaster) and their thicknesses.
5.  **Draw Doors and Windows:** Depict the doors and windows that are intersected by the cutting plane, showing their heights and frame details.
6.  **Draw the Roof:** Show the flat concrete roof structure, including the slab, waterproofing, and parapet walls. Indicate the ceiling level.
7.  **Add Elevations/Levels:** Dimension key vertical heights such as floor level, sill height, lintel height, and roof level.
8.  **Indicate Materials:** Use hatching patterns to represent different materials (e.g., concrete, brick, plaster).
9.  **Add a Title Block:** Include the section title (e.g., "Section A-A") and scale.
10. **Choose a Scale:** Select an appropriate scale (e.g., 1:50).

#### **Step 5: Drawing the Elevation View**

1.  **Project Lines from Floor Plan:** From the floor plan, project vertical lines for the external faces of the walls.
2.  **Establish Ground Level:** Draw a horizontal line representing the ground level.
3.  **Transfer Heights from Section:** Use the heights established in the sectional view to draw the sill levels, lintel levels, and roof levels for windows and doors, and the overall building height.
4.  **Draw Windows and Doors:** Draw the external appearance of windows and doors on the elevation.
5.  **Depict External Finishes:** Indicate the proposed external wall finishes (e.g., plaster, paint, cladding) through hatching or shading.
6.  **Show the Roofline:** Draw the flat roof parapet.
7.  **Add Architectural Features:** Include any balconies, porches, or decorative elements.
8.  **Add a Title Block:** Include the elevation title (e.g., "Front Elevation," "Side Elevation") and scale.
9.  **Choose a Scale:** Select an appropriate scale (e.g., 1:50).

### Examples in Drafting Software (Conceptual Steps - Specific commands depend on software):

**Using AutoCAD as an example:**

*   **Site Plan:**
    *   Use the `LINE` command to draw the plot boundary.
    *   Use the `OFFSET` command to create setback lines.
    *   Use `RECTANGLE` or `PLINE` to draw the building footprint.
    *   Use `CIRCLE` for trees, `ARC` for curved paths.
    *   Use `TEXT` for labeling and `DIM` commands for dimensions.
*   **Floor Plan:**
    *   Use `PLINE` or `RECTANGLE` for walls.
    *   Use `OFFSET` for internal walls and door/window reveals.
    *   Use `CIRCLE` and `ARC` for door swings.
    *   Use `BLOCKS` for pre-defined door and window symbols for efficiency.
    *   Use `HATCH` for wall materials.
    *   Use `TEXT` and `DIM` for labels and dimensions.
*   **Sectional View:**
    *   Draw a cutting plane line on the floor plan using `PLINE` with `XLINE` or `LINE` and hatching for the cutting plane indicator.
    *   Project lines vertically using `XLINE` or by drawing lines from the floor plan.
    *   Use `RECTANGLE`, `PLINE`, `CIRCLE`, `ARC`, `HATCH` to draw the foundation, walls, floors, and roof.
    *   Use `DIM` for vertical dimensions.
*   **Elevation View:**
    *   Project lines from the floor plan using `XLINE`.
    *   Transfer vertical heights from the section.
    *   Use `RECTANGLE`, `CIRCLE`, `ARC`, `PLINE`, `HATCH` to draw the facade, windows, doors, and finishes.
    *   Use `DIM` for heights and widths.

### Practice Questions and Exercises:

**Question 1:**

You are given a plot of land measuring 25m x 35m. You need to design a single-storey residential building with the following:
*   Building footprint: Approx. 150 sq m
*   Rooms: Living Room, Dining Area, Kitchen, 2 Bedrooms (one master with attached toilet), 1 Common Toilet, small Sit-out.
*   Setbacks: 4m from front, 3m from rear, 2.5m from sides.
*   Roof: Flat concrete roof.

**Tasks:**

1.  **Sketch a conceptual layout** of the building on the plot, considering the setbacks and functional zoning.
2.  **Draw a Site Plan** for this scenario, including plot boundary, setbacks, building footprint, access road, and a North arrow. Assume a North-facing plot.
3.  **Draw a Floor Plan** showing the room arrangement, walls, doors (with swings), windows, and basic dimensions.
4.  **Draw a Sectional View** cutting through the living room and a bedroom, showing the foundation, floor, walls, and flat roof.
5.  **Draw the Front Elevation** of the building.

**Answer Guidance (Conceptual):**

*   **Site Plan:** A 25x35 rectangle. Inner lines at 4m (front), 3m (rear), 2.5m (sides). Building footprint placed within these. Access road on one side.
*   **Floor Plan:** Rooms arranged logically. Entrance to sit-out/living. Kitchen near dining. Bedrooms at the rear. Ensure circulation space. Proper door/window placement.
*   **Section:** Show concrete footing, plinth beam, floor slab, wall construction (e.g., 230mm brick wall with plaster), window sill and lintel heights (e.g., 0.9m sill, 2.1m lintel), ceiling height (e.g., 3m), flat roof slab with parapet.
*   **Elevation:** Show the external facade with doors, windows, and wall finishes.

**Question 2:**

What is the purpose of a sectional view in architectural drawings? Briefly explain the key elements shown in a typical sectional view of a single-storey residential building with a flat roof.

**Answer:**

The purpose of a sectional view is to reveal the internal construction and vertical relationships between building components. It shows how different elements are assembled, their materials, and their precise heights.

Key elements shown in a sectional view of a single-storey residential building with a flat roof include:

*   **Foundation:** Footings, plinth beams, and foundation walls.
*   **Floor Construction:** Floor slab, damp-proof course (DPC).
*   **Wall Construction:** Thickness, materials (e.g., brick, block), plaster layers.
*   **Door and Window Openings:** Sill height, lintel height, frame details.
*   **Roof Structure:** Flat concrete slab, insulation (if any), waterproofing layer, parapet wall.
*   **Vertical Dimensions:** Clear heights of rooms, sill heights, lintel heights.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Accuracy:** Always maintain accuracy in dimensions and proportions, especially when using drafting software.
*   **Clarity:** Drawings should be clear, legible, and easy to understand. Use appropriate line weights and styles.
*   **Consistency:** Maintain consistency in scales, line types, and symbols across all drawings.
*   **Labels and Dimensions:** All essential elements should be clearly labeled and dimensioned.
*   **North Arrow:** Always include a North arrow on the site plan and preferably on the floor plan to indicate orientation.
*   **Title Block:** Every drawing must have a title block with essential information like the drawing title, project name, scale, date, and designer.
*   **Software Proficiency:** Familiarize yourself with the basic commands and tools of your chosen drafting software.
*   **Conventions:** Adhere to standard architectural drafting conventions for symbols, line types, and hatching.
*   **Layer Management (CAD):** Use layers effectively in CAD software to organize different elements (walls, doors, windows, dimensions, text) for better control and visibility.
