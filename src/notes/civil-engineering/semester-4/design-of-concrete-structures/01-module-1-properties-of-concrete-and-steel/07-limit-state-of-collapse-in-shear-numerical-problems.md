### 1. Introduction to Shear in Concrete Structures

#### 1.1 What is Shear?

*   **Shear force** is an internal force acting perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of a structural member, tending to cause one part of the member to slide relative to another.
*   In concrete beams, shear forces typically arise from bending due to applied loads (e.g., dead load, live load).

#### 1.2 Shear Failure Modes in Concrete Beams

Concrete beams can fail in shear in several ways:

*   **Diagonal Tension (or Shear Tension) Failure:** This is the most common and critical mode of shear failure. It occurs when the shear stresses cause tensile stresses along diagonal planes, leading to the formation of inclined cracks (shear cracks). If the shear stress exceeds the tensile strength of the concrete along these diagonal planes, failure occurs.
*   **Web Crushing Failure:** This occurs when the compressive stresses in the concrete diagonal strut within the web become excessively high, leading to crushing of the concrete. This mode is less common in typical beams but can occur in deep beams or beams with inadequate web thickness.
*   **Longitudinal Shear Failure:** This occurs along the horizontal plane between the concrete and the reinforcing steel, or between different layers of concrete. It's more relevant in composite structures or precast members.

#### 1.3 Importance of Shear Design

*   Shear failure is often sudden and brittle, offering little warning before collapse.
*   Adequate shear reinforcement is crucial to prevent catastrophic failure.
*   Shear design ensures the beam can safely resist the internal shear forces generated by applied loads.

### 2. Shear Strength of Concrete Beams Without Shear Reinforcement

#### 2.1 Shear Stress Distribution in a Rectangular Beam

*   In a rectangular beam, the shear stress distribution is parabolic, with the maximum shear stress occurring at the neutral axis and zero shear stress at the top and bottom surfaces.
*   However, for design purposes, we often consider the **average shear stress** acting on the critical section.

#### 2.2 Critical Section for Shear

*   The critical section for shear is generally taken at a distance **d/2** from the face of the support or from the point where the shear force is maximum, but not closer than the face of the support.
*   However, for beams, the critical section is generally taken at a distance **d** from the face of the support or at the point of maximum shear force, whichever is more critical, but not closer than the face of the support. (Reference to relevant codes of practice is crucial here).

#### 2.3 Shear Strength Contribution of Concrete ($V_{uc}$)

*   The shear strength provided by the concrete itself, without any shear reinforcement, is denoted by $V_{uc}$.
*   This strength is primarily due to the tensile strength of concrete along diagonal tension cracks.
*   The shear strength of concrete is influenced by:
    *   **Grade of concrete (compressive strength, $f_{ck}$):** Higher strength concrete has higher shear capacity.
    *   **Percentage of longitudinal reinforcement:** Higher reinforcement can lead to a slightly higher concrete shear capacity due to aggregate interlock.
    *   **Web width ($b_w$) and effective depth ($d$).**

*   **Calculation of Concrete Shear Strength:**
    *   The nominal shear stress in concrete is calculated as:
        $\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d)$
        where:
        *   $V_u$ = Factored shear force at the section
        *   $b_w$ = Width of the web of the beam
        *   $d$ = Effective depth of the beam

    *   The shear strength of concrete ($V_{uc}$) is calculated based on the characteristic shear strength of concrete in the web. Codes provide empirical formulas based on experimental data.
    *   A common approach (based on IS 456:2000, for example) is to define the shear strength carried by concrete as $\tau_{uc}$, and then calculate $V_{uc} = \tau_{uc} \cdot b_w \cdot d$.

    *   **$\tau_{uc}$ (Nominal Shear Stress in Concrete):**
        *   This is determined from tables provided in codes based on the grade of concrete and the percentage of longitudinal tension reinforcement ($100 \cdot A_{st} / (b_w \cdot d)$).
        *   **Table 1: $\tau_{uc}$ values (Example based on IS 456:2000)**

        | $100 \cdot A_{st} / (b_w \cdot d)$ | $\tau_{uc}$ (N/mm²) |
        | :--------------------------------- | :------------------ |
        | 0.50                               | 0.30                |
        | 0.75                               | 0.35                |
        | 1.00                               | 0.40                |
        | 1.25                               | 0.45                |
        | 1.50                               | 0.50                |
        | 1.75                               | 0.55                |
        | 2.00                               | 0.58                |
        | 2.25                               | 0.60                |
        | 2.50                               | 0.62                |
        | 2.75                               | 0.64                |
        | 3.00                               | 0.65                |

        *   *Linear interpolation is used for intermediate values.*
        *   *For percentages exceeding 3.00, $\tau_{uc}$ is capped at 0.65 N/mm² (for M20 concrete).*

#### 2.4 Maximum Shear Strength ($V_{umax}$)

*   The maximum shear stress that the concrete section can withstand (even with shear reinforcement) is limited. This is to prevent web crushing.
*   This is calculated as $V_{umax} = \tau_{c,max} \cdot b_w \cdot d$.
*   $\tau_{c,max}$ is also a tabulated value in codes based on concrete grade.

    *   **Table 2: $\tau_{c,max}$ values (Example based on IS 456:2000)**

    | Concrete Grade | $\tau_{c,max}$ (N/mm²) |
    | :------------- | :--------------------- |
    | M20            | 2.8                    |
    | M25            | 3.1                    |
    | M30            | 3.5                    |
    | M35            | 3.7                    |
    | M40 and above  | 4.0                    |

#### 2.5 Conditions for Shear Design

1.  **$V_u \le 0.5 V_{uc}$ (or $\tau_v \le 0.5 \tau_{uc}$):**
    *   If the applied shear force is less than or equal to half the shear strength of the concrete, no shear reinforcement is required.
    *   However, minimum shear reinforcement (stirrups) must still be provided to hold the main bars and to control shrinkage cracks.

2.  **$0.5 V_{uc} < V_u \le V_{uc}$ (or $0.5 \tau_{uc} < \tau_v \le \tau_{uc}$):**
    *   If the applied shear force is greater than half the concrete's shear strength but not exceeding the concrete's shear strength, shear reinforcement is required.
    *   The amount of shear reinforcement required is calculated to carry the excess shear force ($V_s = V_u - V_{uc}$).

3.  **$V_u > V_{uc}$ (or $\tau_v > \tau_{uc}$):**
    *   If the applied shear force exceeds the concrete's shear strength, shear reinforcement is required to carry the entire excess shear force ($V_s = V_u - V_{uc}$).

4.  **$V_u > V_{umax}$ (or $\tau_v > \tau_{c,max}$):**
    *   If the applied shear force exceeds the maximum shear strength the section can resist, the section is deemed inadequate. The beam's dimensions or concrete grade need to be increased.

---

### 3. Minimum Shear Reinforcement

*   Even when $V_u \le 0.5 V_{uc}$, minimum shear reinforcement (stirrups) must be provided.
*   This is to:
    *   Prevent brittle failure due to sudden shear.
    *   Control cracking due to temperature and shrinkage.
    *   Assist in holding the main tension reinforcement in place.
*   The minimum shear reinforcement is determined by the following condition:
    $A_{sv} / (b_w \cdot s_v) \ge 0.4 / (0.87 \cdot f_y)$
    where:
    *   $A_{sv}$ = total area of shear reinforcement within a distance $s_v$ along the beam.
    *   $b_w$ = width of the web of the beam.
    *   $s_v$ = spacing of the shear reinforcement along the beam.
    *   $f_y$ = characteristic yield strength of shear reinforcement.

*   The minimum shear reinforcement area per unit length of beam is:
    $A_{sv,min} / s_v = 0.4 / (0.87 \cdot f_y)$

---

### 4. Design of Shear Reinforcement

#### 4.1 Calculating the Required Shear Strength from Reinforcement ($V_s$)

*   If $V_u > V_{uc}$, the shear reinforcement must provide a shear resistance of at least $V_s = V_u - V_{uc}$.

#### 4.2 Determining the Spacing of Stirrups ($s_v$)

*   The shear strength provided by the stirrups ($V_s$) is calculated as:
    $V_s = (A_{sv} \cdot f_y \cdot d) / s_v$
    where:
    *   $A_{sv}$ = total area of stirrups (e.g., for two-legged stirrups, $A_{sv} = 2 \times (\text{area of one leg})$).
    *   $f_y$ = characteristic yield strength of stirrup steel.
    *   $d$ = effective depth of the beam.
    *   $s_v$ = spacing of stirrups.

*   Rearranging to find the spacing:
    $s_v = (A_{sv} \cdot f_y \cdot d) / V_s$

#### 4.3 Constraints on Stirrup Spacing ($s_v$)

The spacing of stirrups is subject to the following practical limits:

1.  **Maximum spacing:**
    *   $s_v$ shall not exceed $0.75d$ (for stirrups designed to resist $V_u$ where $V_u \le V_{umax}$).
    *   $s_v$ shall not exceed $d$ (for minimum shear reinforcement).
    *   The spacing should also not exceed a value based on the web width (e.g., $300$ mm, as per IS 456:2000, but this is often superseded by the $0.75d$ or $d$ limits).

2.  **Minimum spacing:**
    *   The spacing should not be less than a value that ensures concrete can be placed properly and that the stirrups can be properly anchored. Typically, the minimum spacing is dictated by practical considerations and the required area of reinforcement.

#### 4.4 Selection of Stirrup Arrangement

*   Stirrups are usually provided in the form of closed loops (vertical stirrups) or inclined stirrups.
*   For vertical stirrups: $A_{sv}$ is the area of steel in two legs of the stirrup.
*   For stirrups inclined at 45 degrees: $A_{sv}$ is the area of steel in two legs of the stirrup. The shear resistance provided by inclined stirrups is calculated differently.

### 5. Numerical Problem Solving Steps

1.  **Determine the factored shear force ($V_u$)** at the critical section.
2.  **Calculate the nominal shear stress ($\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d)$).**
3.  **Determine the concrete shear strength ($\tau_{uc}$)** from code tables based on concrete grade and percentage of longitudinal steel.
4.  **Calculate the shear strength provided by concrete ($V_{uc} = \tau_{uc} \cdot b_w \cdot d$).**
5.  **Check the condition $V_u$ vs. $V_{uc}$:**
    *   If $V_u \le 0.5 V_{uc}$: Provide minimum shear reinforcement.
    *   If $0.5 V_{uc} < V_u \le V_{uc}$: Calculate $V_s = V_u - V_{uc}$.
    *   If $V_u > V_{uc}$: Calculate $V_s = V_u - V_{uc}$.
    *   If $V_u > V_{umax}$: The section is inadequate.

6.  **Check for maximum shear capacity ($V_{umax} = \tau_{c,max} \cdot b_w \cdot d$):** Ensure $V_u \le V_{umax}$.

7.  **Design Shear Reinforcement (if $V_u > V_{uc}$):**
    *   **Calculate required $V_s = V_u - V_{uc}$.**
    *   **Select stirrup size and number of legs.** (e.g., 8 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrup). Calculate the area of one leg ($A_{s1} = \pi/4 \cdot \phi^2$). $A_{sv} = \text{number of legs} \times A_{s1}$.
    *   **Calculate the required spacing ($s_v$) using $s_v = (A_{sv} \cdot f_y \cdot d) / V_s$.**
    *   **Apply spacing constraints:**
        *   Check if the calculated $s_v$ is less than or equal to the maximum allowed spacing ($0.75d$ or $d$, and any other code-specified limits).
        *   If $s_v$ is too large, either reduce $s_v$ or increase $A_{sv}$ (use more legs or larger diameter stirrups).
        *   Ensure the spacing is practical and economically viable.

8.  **Provide Minimum Shear Reinforcement (if $V_u \le 0.5 V_{uc}$ or as required by codes):**
    *   Calculate the minimum required $A_{sv}/s_v = 0.4 / (0.87 \cdot f_y)$.
    *   Choose a suitable spacing (e.g., $s_v = 100, 150, 200$ mm) such that $A_{sv}/s_v$ meets the minimum requirement. You can use the formula to check the spacing for a chosen stirrup size.

### 6. Examples

#### Example 1: Shear design for a simply supported beam

**Problem:** A simply supported rectangular concrete beam has a width $b_w = 300$ mm and an effective depth $d = 500$ mm. The factored shear force at the critical section is $V_u = 150$ kN. The grade of concrete is M25 and the grade of steel for stirrups is Fe415. The area of longitudinal tension reinforcement is $A_{st} = 1250$ mm².

**Solution:**

1.  **Given:**
    *   $b_w = 300$ mm
    *   $d = 500$ mm
    *   $V_u = 150$ kN = $150 \times 10^3$ N
    *   Concrete grade = M25 ($f_{ck} = 25$ N/mm²)
    *   Steel grade for stirrups = Fe415 ($f_y = 415$ N/mm²)
    *   $A_{st} = 1250$ mm²

2.  **Calculate nominal shear stress ($\tau_v$):**
    $\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d) = (150 \times 10^3 \text{ N}) / (300 \text{ mm} \times 500 \text{ mm})$
    $\tau_v = 1.0$ N/mm²

3.  **Calculate percentage of longitudinal reinforcement ($100 \cdot A_{st} / (b_w \cdot d)$):**
    $100 \cdot A_{st} / (b_w \cdot d) = (100 \times 1250 \text{ mm}^2) / (300 \text{ mm} \times 500 \text{ mm})$
    $100 \cdot A_{st} / (b_w \cdot d) = 100 \times 1250 / 150000 = 0.833\%$

4.  **Determine concrete shear strength ($\tau_{uc}$):**
    From IS 456:2000 Table 1 for M25 concrete and $0.833\%$ longitudinal steel (interpolate between 0.75% and 1.00%):
    *   At 0.75%, $\tau_{uc} = 0.35$ N/mm²
    *   At 1.00%, $\tau_{uc} = 0.40$ N/mm²
    *   Interpolating for 0.833%:
        $\tau_{uc} = 0.35 + (0.40 - 0.35) \times \frac{0.833 - 0.75}{1.00 - 0.75}$
        $\tau_{uc} = 0.35 + 0.05 \times \frac{0.083}{0.25} = 0.35 + 0.05 \times 0.333$
        $\tau_{uc} = 0.35 + 0.01665 = 0.367$ N/mm² (approximately 0.37 N/mm²)

5.  **Calculate shear strength provided by concrete ($V_{uc}$):**
    $V_{uc} = \tau_{uc} \cdot b_w \cdot d = 0.37 \text{ N/mm}^2 \times 300 \text{ mm} \times 500 \text{ mm}$
    $V_{uc} = 55.5 \times 10^3 \text{ N} = 55.5$ kN

6.  **Check condition $V_u$ vs. $V_{uc}$:**
    $V_u = 150$ kN
    $V_{uc} = 55.5$ kN
    Since $V_u > V_{uc}$, shear reinforcement is required.

7.  **Calculate required shear strength from reinforcement ($V_s$):**
    $V_s = V_u - V_{uc} = 150 \text{ kN} - 55.5 \text{ kN} = 94.5$ kN
    $V_s = 94.5 \times 10^3$ N

8.  **Check maximum shear capacity ($V_{umax}$):**
    For M25 concrete, $\tau_{c,max} = 2.8$ N/mm² (from IS 456:2000 Table 2).
    $V_{umax} = \tau_{c,max} \cdot b_w \cdot d = 2.8 \text{ N/mm}^2 \times 300 \text{ mm} \times 500 \text{ mm}$
    $V_{umax} = 420 \times 10^3 \text{ N} = 420$ kN
    Since $V_u (150 \text{ kN}) < V_{umax} (420 \text{ kN})$, the section is adequate for shear.

9.  **Design Shear Reinforcement:**
    *   **Select stirrup size:** Let's use 8 mm diameter stirrups (Fe415 grade).
    *   **Number of legs:** Assume 2-legged stirrups.
    *   **Area of stirrup steel ($A_{sv}$):**
        $A_{sv} = 2 \times (\pi/4 \times 8^2) = 2 \times (\pi/4 \times 64) = 2 \times 50.26 \text{ mm}^2 = 100.52 \text{ mm}^2$ (approx. 100 mm²)

    *   **Calculate required spacing ($s_v$):**
        $s_v = (A_{sv} \cdot f_y \cdot d) / V_s = (100.52 \text{ mm}^2 \times 415 \text{ N/mm}^2 \times 500 \text{ mm}) / (94.5 \times 10^3 \text{ N})$
        $s_v = (100.52 \times 415 \times 500) / 94500 = 20862900 / 94500 = 220.77$ mm

    *   **Apply spacing constraints:**
        *   Maximum allowed spacing for shear reinforcement designed to carry $V_u > V_{uc}$ is $0.75d$.
        *   Maximum $s_v = 0.75 \times 500 \text{ mm} = 375$ mm.
        *   Also, typically $s_v \le 300$ mm (as per IS 456:2000).

    *   The calculated spacing $s_v = 220.77$ mm is less than the maximum allowed spacing of 375 mm and 300 mm.

    *   **Final Design:** Provide 8 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrups at a spacing of 220 mm c/c.

#### Example 2: Minimum shear reinforcement requirement

**Problem:** A reinforced concrete beam has a width $b_w = 250$ mm and an effective depth $d = 400$ mm. The factored shear force at the critical section is $V_u = 60$ kN. The concrete grade is M20, and the longitudinal reinforcement percentage is $100 \cdot A_{st} / (b_w \cdot d) = 1.5\%$. The steel grade for stirrups is Fe415.

**Solution:**

1.  **Given:**
    *   $b_w = 250$ mm
    *   $d = 400$ mm
    *   $V_u = 60$ kN = $60 \times 10^3$ N
    *   Concrete grade = M20 ($f_{ck} = 20$ N/mm²)
    *   Longitudinal reinforcement percentage = 1.5%
    *   Steel grade for stirrups = Fe415 ($f_y = 415$ N/mm²)

2.  **Calculate nominal shear stress ($\tau_v$):**
    $\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d) = (60 \times 10^3 \text{ N}) / (250 \text{ mm} \times 400 \text{ mm})$
    $\tau_v = 0.6$ N/mm²

3.  **Determine concrete shear strength ($\tau_{uc}$):**
    From IS 456:2000 Table 1 for M20 concrete and 1.5% longitudinal steel:
    $\tau_{uc} = 0.50$ N/mm²

4.  **Calculate shear strength provided by concrete ($V_{uc}$):**
    $V_{uc} = \tau_{uc} \cdot b_w \cdot d = 0.50 \text{ N/mm}^2 \times 250 \text{ mm} \times 400 \text{ mm}$
    $V_{uc} = 50 \times 10^3 \text{ N} = 50$ kN

5.  **Check condition $V_u$ vs. $V_{uc}$:**
    $V_u = 60$ kN
    $V_{uc} = 50$ kN
    We observe that $V_u > V_{uc}$. This means shear reinforcement is needed.
    $V_s = V_u - V_{uc} = 60 \text{ kN} - 50 \text{ kN} = 10$ kN.

    *However, if $V_u$ was less than $0.5 V_{uc}$, we would still provide minimum shear reinforcement. Let's re-evaluate the problem with a slightly different $V_u$ to illustrate minimum reinforcement.*

#### Example 2 (Revised): Minimum shear reinforcement requirement

**Problem:** A reinforced concrete beam has a width $b_w = 250$ mm and an effective depth $d = 400$ mm. The factored shear force at the critical section is $V_u = 20$ kN. The concrete grade is M20, and the longitudinal reinforcement percentage is $100 \cdot A_{st} / (b_w \cdot d) = 1.5\%$. The steel grade for stirrups is Fe415.

**Solution:**

1.  **Given:**
    *   $b_w = 250$ mm
    *   $d = 400$ mm
    *   $V_u = 20$ kN = $20 \times 10^3$ N
    *   Concrete grade = M20 ($f_{ck} = 20$ N/mm²)
    *   Longitudinal reinforcement percentage = 1.5%
    *   Steel grade for stirrups = Fe415 ($f_y = 415$ N/mm²)

2.  **Calculate nominal shear stress ($\tau_v$):**
    $\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d) = (20 \times 10^3 \text{ N}) / (250 \text{ mm} \times 400 \text{ mm})$
    $\tau_v = 0.2$ N/mm²

3.  **Determine concrete shear strength ($\tau_{uc}$):**
    From IS 456:2000 Table 1 for M20 concrete and 1.5% longitudinal steel:
    $\tau_{uc} = 0.50$ N/mm²

4.  **Calculate shear strength provided by concrete ($V_{uc}$):**
    $V_{uc} = \tau_{uc} \cdot b_w \cdot d = 0.50 \text{ N/mm}^2 \times 250 \text{ mm} \times 400 \text{ mm}$
    $V_{uc} = 50 \times 10^3 \text{ N} = 50$ kN

5.  **Check condition $V_u$ vs. $V_{uc}$:**
    $V_u = 20$ kN
    $V_{uc} = 50$ kN
    We observe that $V_u = 20 \text{ kN} \le 0.5 \times V_{uc} = 0.5 \times 50 \text{ kN} = 25$ kN.
    Since $V_u \le 0.5 V_{uc}$, no shear reinforcement needs to be *designed* to carry the shear force. However, minimum shear reinforcement must be provided.

6.  **Provide Minimum Shear Reinforcement:**
    *   **Calculate the minimum required area of shear reinforcement per unit length:**
        $A_{sv,min} / s_v = 0.4 / (0.87 \cdot f_y)$
        $A_{sv,min} / s_v = 0.4 / (0.87 \times 415 \text{ N/mm}^2)$
        $A_{sv,min} / s_v = 0.4 / 361.05 = 0.001108 \text{ mm}^2/\text{mm}$

    *   **Select stirrup size and number of legs:** Let's use 8 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrups.
        $A_{sv} = 100.52 \text{ mm}^2$

    *   **Calculate the maximum allowable spacing for minimum reinforcement:**
        $s_{v,max} = A_{sv} / (A_{sv,min} / s_v)$
        $s_{v,max} = 100.52 \text{ mm}^2 / 0.001108 \text{ mm}^2/\text{mm}$
        $s_{v,max} = 90722$ mm. This is unrealistically large.

    *   **Apply spacing constraints for minimum reinforcement:** The spacing ($s_v$) should not exceed the smaller of:
        *   $d$ = 400 mm
        *   $300$ mm (as per IS 456:2000 for stirrup spacing)

    *   So, the maximum allowed spacing is 300 mm. We need to provide 8 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrups at a spacing of **300 mm c/c**. Let's verify that this spacing provides the minimum required reinforcement:
        $A_{sv} / s_v = 100.52 \text{ mm}^2 / 300 \text{ mm} = 0.335 \text{ mm}^2/\text{mm}$
        Required $A_{sv,min} / s_v = 0.001108 \text{ mm}^2/\text{mm}$.
        Since $0.335 > 0.001108$, the chosen spacing of 300 mm c/c with 8 mm, 2-legged stirrups is adequate for minimum shear reinforcement.

### 7. Practice Questions

**Question 1:**
A rectangular concrete beam is $200$ mm wide and has an effective depth of $400$ mm. The factored shear force at the critical section is $V_u = 120$ kN. The grade of concrete is M30, and the grade of steel for stirrups is Fe415. The percentage of longitudinal tension reinforcement is $1.2\%$.
*   (a) Calculate the nominal shear stress.
*   (b) Determine the shear strength provided by concrete.
*   (c) Calculate the shear force to be resisted by shear reinforcement.
*   (d) Design the shear reinforcement using 8 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrups.

**Answer 1:**
*   (a) $\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d) = (120 \times 10^3) / (200 \times 400) = 1.5$ N/mm².
*   (b) For M30 concrete and 1.2% longitudinal steel, $\tau_{uc} = 0.45$ N/mm² (from IS 456 Table 1).
    $V_{uc} = \tau_{uc} \cdot b_w \cdot d = 0.45 \times 200 \times 400 = 36 \times 10^3$ N = 36 kN.
*   (c) $V_s = V_u - V_{uc} = 120 \text{ kN} - 36 \text{ kN} = 84$ kN.
*   (d) $A_{sv}$ for 8 mm, 2-legged stirrups = $100.52$ mm².
    $s_v = (A_{sv} \cdot f_y \cdot d) / V_s = (100.52 \times 415 \times 400) / (84 \times 10^3) = 199.5$ mm.
    Maximum spacing allowed is $0.75d = 0.75 \times 400 = 300$ mm.
    **Design:** Provide 8 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrups at 190 mm c/c. (Rounding down to a practical spacing).

**Question 2:**
A reinforced concrete beam is $300$ mm wide and has an effective depth of $550$ mm. The factored shear force at the critical section is $V_u = 200$ kN. The grade of concrete is M20, and the grade of steel for stirrups is Fe415. The percentage of longitudinal tension reinforcement is $1.75\%$.
*   (a) Calculate the nominal shear stress and the concrete's shear strength.
*   (b) Determine if shear reinforcement is required and calculate the required $V_s$ if needed.
*   (c) Check if the section is adequate for maximum shear stress.
*   (d) Design the shear reinforcement using 10 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrups.

**Answer 2:**
*   (a) $\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d) = (200 \times 10^3) / (300 \times 550) = 1.21$ N/mm².
    For M20 concrete and 1.75% longitudinal steel, $\tau_{uc} = 0.55$ N/mm² (from IS 456 Table 1).
    $V_{uc} = \tau_{uc} \cdot b_w \cdot d = 0.55 \times 300 \times 550 = 90.75 \times 10^3$ N = 90.75 kN.
*   (b) Since $V_u (200 \text{ kN}) > V_{uc} (90.75 \text{ kN})$, shear reinforcement is required.
    $V_s = V_u - V_{uc} = 200 \text{ kN} - 90.75 \text{ kN} = 109.25$ kN.
*   (c) For M20 concrete, $\tau_{c,max} = 2.8$ N/mm².
    $V_{umax} = \tau_{c,max} \cdot b_w \cdot d = 2.8 \times 300 \times 550 = 462 \times 10^3$ N = 462 kN.
    Since $V_u (200 \text{ kN}) < V_{umax} (462 \text{ kN})$, the section is adequate.
*   (d) $A_{sv}$ for 10 mm, 2-legged stirrups = $2 \times (\pi/4 \times 10^2) = 2 \times 78.54 = 157.08$ mm².
    $s_v = (A_{sv} \cdot f_y \cdot d) / V_s = (157.08 \times 415 \times 550) / (109.25 \times 10^3) = 327.5$ mm.
    Maximum spacing allowed is $0.75d = 0.75 \times 550 = 412.5$ mm.
    The calculated spacing $s_v = 327.5$ mm is acceptable. However, the code also limits spacing to $300$ mm.
    **Design:** Provide 10 mm diameter, 2-legged stirrups at 325 mm c/c (or 300 mm c/c for conservatism and practical reasons).

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Critical Section:** Always identify the correct critical section for shear design (typically 'd' from the face of the support).
*   **Nominal Shear Stress:** Use the average shear stress $\tau_v = V_u / (b_w \cdot d)$.
*   **Shear Strength of Concrete ($\tau_{uc}$):** This is *not* a fixed value but depends on the concrete grade and the percentage of longitudinal steel. Always refer to code tables.
*   **Minimum Shear Reinforcement:** It's mandatory even when $V_u \le 0.5 V_{uc}$.
*   **Spacing Limits:** Strictly adhere to the maximum spacing limits for stirrups ($0.75d$ or $d$, and other code-specified limits).
*   **Web Crushing:** Ensure $V_u$ is less than $V_{umax}$ to avoid web crushing failure.
*   **Practical Spacing:** Round calculated spacing to a practical, whole number and ensure it's less than the maximum allowed.
*   **Clear Cover:** When calculating effective depth 'd' from overall depth, remember to account for clear cover and the diameter of the main longitudinal bars.
