# Soil Mechanics: Module 2 - Index Properties

## Topic: IS Classification of Soil

### Introduction to Soil Classification

Soil classification is the process of grouping soils into categories based on their engineering properties. This helps in understanding their behavior under different conditions and in selecting appropriate construction methods and materials. The Indian Standard (IS) classification system is a widely used method in India for classifying soils, particularly for civil engineering purposes.

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Understand the purpose and importance of soil classification.
*   Explain the principles behind the IS classification system.
*   Identify the major soil groups and subgroups in the IS system.
*   Perform sieve analysis and hydrometer analysis to determine particle size distribution.
*   Utilize the results of particle size distribution to classify soils according to the IS system.
*   Understand the role of plasticity characteristics in soil classification.
*   Interpret soil classification charts and symbols.
*   Apply the IS classification system to practical engineering problems.

### Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Soil:** A naturally occurring material composed of solid particles, fluids (water and air) and organic constituents.
*   **Particle Size Distribution (PSD):** The relative proportions of different particle sizes in a soil sample. This is a fundamental property influencing soil behavior.
*   **Sieve Analysis:** A method used to determine the particle size distribution of coarse-grained soils (gravels and sands). It involves passing soil through a series of sieves with progressively smaller openings.
*   **Hydrometer Analysis (Sedimentation Analysis):** A method used to determine the particle size distribution of fine-grained soils (silts and clays). It is based on Stokes' Law, which relates the settling velocity of particles in a liquid to their size.
*   **Grain Size Scale:** A scale used to categorize particle sizes, typically including boulders, cobbles, gravel, sand, silt, and clay.
*   **Plasticity:** The ability of a soil to deform under stress without fracturing. This is a characteristic of fine-grained soils.
*   **Plasticity Index (PI):** The difference between the liquid limit (LL) and the plastic limit (PL) of a soil. It indicates the range of water content over which the soil behaves plastically.
*   **Liquid Limit (LL):** The water content at which a soil changes from a plastic state to a liquid state.
*   **Plastic Limit (PL):** The water content at which a soil changes from a plastic state to a semi-solid state.
*   **Shrinkage Limit (SL):** The water content at which further reduction in water content does not cause a decrease in the volume of the soil.
*   **Coarse-Grained Soils:** Soils where more than 50% of the particles are retained on the 75-micron IS sieve. These are primarily gravels and sands.
*   **Fine-Grained Soils:** Soils where more than 50% of the particles pass the 75-micron IS sieve. These are primarily silts and clays.
*   **Organic Soils:** Soils containing a significant proportion of organic matter.

### IS Classification of Soil System

The Indian Standard (IS) classification system for soils, specifically IS 1498 (1970), is a comprehensive system that classifies soils based on their grain size distribution and plasticity characteristics. It broadly categorizes soils into two main groups:

1.  **Coarse-Grained Soils**
2.  **Fine-Grained Soils**

A third category, **Organic Soils** and **Soils of Unknown Classification**, is also included.

#### 1. Coarse-Grained Soils

These soils have more than 50% of their particles retained on the 75-micron IS sieve. They are further classified based on their particle size distribution (gradation) and the presence of fines (silt and clay).

**Subgroups:**

*   **Gravels (G):** Soils with more than 50% of coarse-grained particles retained on the 4.75 mm IS sieve.
    *   **GW (Well-graded Gravels):** Have a wide range of particle sizes, indicating good uniformity in grading. This is determined by the coefficient of uniformity ($C_u$) and coefficient of curvature ($C_c$).
        *   $C_u = D_{60}/D_{10} \ge 4$
        *   $C_c = (D_{30})^2 / (D_{10} \times D_{60})$ between 1 and 3
    *   **GP (Poorly-graded Gravels):** Have a limited range of particle sizes or are uniformly graded.
    *   **GM (Silty Gravels):** Contain a significant amount of silt.
    *   **GC (Clayey Gravels):** Contain a significant amount of clay.
*   **Sands (S):** Soils with more than 50% of coarse-grained particles passing the 4.75 mm IS sieve.
    *   **SW (Well-graded Sands):** Have a wide range of particle sizes, indicating good uniformity in grading.
        *   $C_u = D_{60}/D_{10} \ge 6$
        *   $C_c = (D_{30})^2 / (D_{10} \times D_{60})$ between 1 and 3
    *   **SP (Poorly-graded Sands):** Have a limited range of particle sizes or are uniformly graded.
    *   **SM (Silty Sands):** Contain a significant amount of silt.
    *   **SC (Clayey Sands):** Contain a significant amount of clay.

**Criteria for Fines:**

*   **D Fines (Densely Graded Fines):** If the percentage of fines (passing 75-micron sieve) is less than 5%.
*   **M Fines (Moderately Graded Fines):** If the percentage of fines is between 5% and 12%.
*   **A Fines (Abundantly Graded Fines):** If the percentage of fines is greater than 12%.

**Important Note on Fines:** For coarse-grained soils, the classification of the fines (silty or clayey) is determined by their plasticity.
*   **Silty Fines:** If the plasticity index (PI) of the fines is less than 4, or if the soil lies below the A-line on the plasticity chart.
*   **Clayey Fines:** If the plasticity index (PI) of the fines is greater than 10, or if the soil lies above the B-line on the plasticity chart.
*   **Intermediate Fines:** If the PI is between 4 and 10, or if the soil lies between the A-line and the B-line.

**Combined Groups for Coarse-Grained Soils (with significant fines):**

*   **Gravels with fines:**
    *   **GM (Silty Gravels):** Low plasticity fines.
    *   **GC (Clayey Gravels):** High plasticity fines.
    *   **GMC (Clayey-Silty Gravels):** Intermediate plasticity fines.
*   **Sands with fines:**
    *   **SM (Silty Sands):** Low plasticity fines.
    *   **SC (Clayey Sands):** High plasticity fines.
    *   **SMC (Clayey-Silty Sands):** Intermediate plasticity fines.

**Prefixes indicating plasticity of fines:**

*   **M (Silty):** When fines are silty (low plasticity).
*   **C (Clayey):** When fines are clayey (high plasticity).

**Example:** A soil with 60% gravel, 30% sand, and 10% fines. If the fines are silty (low plasticity), it would be classified as **GM** (Silty Gravel). If the fines are clayey (high plasticity), it would be classified as **GC** (Clayey Gravel).

#### 2. Fine-Grained Soils

These soils have more than 50% of their particles passing the 75-micron IS sieve. They are classified based on their plasticity characteristics (Liquid Limit and Plasticity Index) and also considering their grain size distribution (silt vs. clay).

**Broad Categories:**

*   **Clays (C):** Soils with high plasticity.
*   **Silts (M):** Soils with low to medium plasticity.
*   **Organic Soils (O):** Soils containing significant amounts of organic matter.

**Classification based on Liquid Limit (LL):**

*   **Low Compressibility Soils (LL < 35%):**
    *   **ML (Silty Soils):** Low plasticity, typically silts.
    *   **CL (Clayey Soils):** Low to medium plasticity, typically clays.
    *   **OL (Organic Silts and Clays):** Organic soils with low plasticity.
*   **High Compressibility Soils (LL > 35%):**
    *   **MH (Silty Soils):** High plasticity, typically silts.
    *   **CH (Clayey Soils):** High plasticity, typically clays.
    *   **OH (Organic Silts and Clays):** Organic soils with high plasticity.

**Further Classification using the Plasticity Chart:**

The plasticity chart, developed by Terzaghi and later modified by others, is crucial for classifying fine-grained soils. It plots the Liquid Limit (LL) on the x-axis and the Plasticity Index (PI) on the y-axis.

*   **A-Line:** A boundary line separating silts (below) from clays (above). The equation for the A-line is: $PI = 0.73(LL - 20)$.
*   **B-Line:** A boundary line separating soils of low plasticity (below) from soils of high plasticity (above). The equation for the B-line is: $PI = 0.9(LL - 8)$.

**Classification Zones on the Plasticity Chart:**

*   **ML:** Soils with LL < 35% and below the A-line.
*   **CL:** Soils with LL < 35% and above the A-line (but below the B-line, or if B-line is not applicable due to low LL).
*   **OL:** Soils with LL < 35% and within the organic soil zone (which is typically indicated on charts).
*   **MH:** Soils with LL > 35% and below the A-line.
*   **CH:** Soils with LL > 35% and above the B-line.
*   **OH:** Soils with LL > 35% and between the A-line and B-line, or in the organic soil zone.

**Important Considerations for Fine-Grained Soils:**

*   **If LL < 35% and PI < 4:** The soil is classified as ML (Silty).
*   **If LL < 35% and PI > 4:**
    *   If the soil plots below the A-line: ML (Silty).
    *   If the soil plots above the A-line: CL (Clayey).
*   **If LL > 35% and PI < 10:**
    *   If the soil plots below the A-line: MH (Silty).
    *   If the soil plots above the A-line (and below B-line): CL (Clayey) - this subdivision is less common and often grouped with CH. More typically, if LL>35 and it's above A-line, it's CH or MH.
*   **If LL > 35% and PI > 10:**
    *   If the soil plots below the A-line: MH (Silty).
    *   If the soil plots above the A-line: CH (Clayey).

**Organic Soils (OL and OH):** These soils have a characteristic smell and color. Their plasticity values may lie in the ML/CL or MH/CH ranges, but their organic content significantly affects their behavior.

#### 3. Soils of Unknown Classification

This category is used when the soil's engineering properties cannot be determined due to lack of information or when the soil is a mixture of components that do not fit neatly into the other categories.

### Sieve Analysis Procedure

**Objective:** To determine the percentage of different particle sizes in a soil sample, primarily for coarse-grained soils.

**Materials:**
*   Soil sample
*   Set of sieves with standard opening sizes (e.g., 4.75 mm, 2.36 mm, 1.18 mm, 600 µm, 300 µm, 150 µm, 75 µm)
*   Weighing balance
*   Sieve shaker
*   Brush

**Procedure:**
1.  **Oven Dry:** Dry the soil sample in an oven at 105-110°C until a constant weight is achieved.
2.  **Weigh Sample:** Record the initial dry weight of the soil sample ($W_{total}$).
3.  **Sieve Assembly:** Arrange the sieves in descending order of opening size, with the coarsest at the top and the finest at the bottom. Place a pan at the bottom to collect the finest material.
4.  **Sieving:** Place the soil sample on the top sieve and place the sieve stack in a sieve shaker. Shake for a sufficient time to ensure all particles pass through the sieve they can go through.
5.  **Weighing Each Sieve:** Carefully weigh the soil retained on each sieve, including the pan. Record these weights.
6.  **Calculations:**
    *   **Weight Retained on Each Sieve:** $W_i$
    *   **Cumulative Weight Retained:** $W_{cumulative,i} = \sum_{j=1}^{i} W_j$
    *   **Percentage Passing:** $\% \text{ Passing}_i = \frac{W_{total} - W_{cumulative,i}}{W_{total}} \times 100$

**Particle Size Designation:**
*   **Boulders:** > 300 mm
*   **Cobbles:** 75 mm to 300 mm
*   **Gravel:** 4.75 mm to 75 mm
*   **Sand:** 0.075 mm to 4.75 mm
*   **Silt & Clay (Fines):** < 0.075 mm

**Graphical Representation:** The results are plotted on a semi-logarithmic graph with particle size (on log scale) on the x-axis and percentage passing on the y-axis. This is the **particle size distribution curve**.

**Key Parameters from PSD Curve:**
*   $D_{10}$: Effective size (particle diameter corresponding to 10% passing)
*   $D_{30}$: Particle diameter corresponding to 30% passing
*   $D_{60}$: Particle diameter corresponding to 60% passing

### Hydrometer Analysis (Sedimentation Analysis) Procedure

**Objective:** To determine the percentage of finer particles (silt and clay) in a soil sample, primarily for fine-grained soils.

**Principle:** Based on Stokes' Law, which states that the terminal velocity ($v$) of a spherical particle settling in a fluid is proportional to the square of its diameter ($D^2$) and the difference in density between the particle and the fluid ($\rho_s - \rho_f$), and inversely proportional to the viscosity of the fluid ($\eta$):

$v = \frac{D^2 (\rho_s - \rho_f) g}{18 \eta}$

Where $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

For a given time ($t$) and distance ($h$) from the surface, the effective diameter ($D$) of a particle that has settled can be calculated.

**Materials:**
*   Soil sample (passed through 75-micron sieve)
*   Hydrometer (e.g., ASTM 152H)
*   1000 ml graduated cylinder
*   Stirring apparatus (dispersion rod)
*   Beakers
*   Oven
*   Distilled water
*   Deflocculating agent (e.g., Sodium Hexametaphosphate)
*   Thermometer

**Procedure:**
1.  **Soil Sample Preparation:** Take a known weight of dry soil that passes the 75-micron sieve (e.g., 50-100g).
2.  **Dispersion:** Disperse the soil sample in a beaker with distilled water and a deflocculating agent to prevent flocculation (clumping of fine particles). Stir vigorously. Allow to stand for some time (e.g., 24 hours) for complete dispersion.
3.  **Transfer to Graduated Cylinder:** Carefully transfer the dispersed soil suspension into a 1000 ml graduated cylinder. Add distilled water to bring the total volume to the 1000 ml mark.
4.  **Initial Reading:** Stir the suspension vigorously with the dispersion rod to ensure uniform distribution of particles. Immediately after stirring, remove the rod and start the stopwatch. Record the hydrometer reading at the bottom of the meniscus ($R_1$) at time $t_1 = 0$ seconds.
5.  **Subsequent Readings:** Record hydrometer readings ($R_t$) at various time intervals (e.g., 0.5 min, 1 min, 2 min, 4 min, 8 min, 15 min, 30 min, 1 hr, 2 hrs, 4 hrs, 8 hrs, 24 hrs).
6.  **Temperature Readings:** Record the temperature of the suspension at each reading.
7.  **Blank Test:** Perform a blank test with distilled water and the deflocculating agent to determine correction factors for the hydrometer and temperature.
8.  **Calculations:**
    *   **Corrected Hydrometer Reading ($R_{c}$):** $R_c = R_t + L_c - T_c$ (where $L_c$ is the meniscus correction and $T_c$ is the temperature correction).
    *   **Effective Depth ($L$):** This is the depth of the center of the hydrometer bulb from the surface of the suspension. It can be related to the hydrometer reading.
    *   **Particle Diameter ($D$):** Using Stokes' Law, with appropriate corrections for temperature and density of the soil particles and water:
        $D = K \sqrt{\frac{L}{t}}$
        Where $K$ is a constant that depends on the temperature, viscosity, and densities of soil and water.
    *   **Percentage Finer ($P_f$):**
        $P_f = \frac{W_s'}{W_s} \times 100$
        Where $W_s$ is the initial oven-dry weight of soil used for the test, and $W_s'$ is the effective weight of soil in suspension at a given time. $W_s'$ is calculated using the corrected hydrometer reading and accounting for the portion of the hydrometer that has risen above the suspension surface (for higher readings).
        $W_s' = (\frac{R_c}{G_s - 1}) \times 1000$ (for a 1000 ml cylinder, if $G_s$ is the specific gravity of soil solids).

**Graphical Representation:** Plot the percentage finer ($P_f$) against the particle diameter ($D$). This curve, along with the sieve analysis curve, provides the complete particle size distribution.

### Practice Questions

1.  **Question:** A sieve analysis of a soil sample gave the following results:
    | Sieve Size (mm) | Weight Retained (g) |
    | :-------------- | :------------------ |
    | 4.75            | 120                 |
    | 2.36            | 180                 |
    | 1.18            | 240                 |
    | 0.600           | 300                 |
    | 0.300           | 210                 |
    | 0.150           | 120                 |
    | 0.075           | 60                  |
    | Pan             | 40                  |

    The total dry weight of the sample was 1270g.
    (a) Calculate the percentage of gravel, sand, and fines.
    (b) Determine $D_{10}$, $D_{30}$, and $D_{60}$ from the particle size distribution curve (assume you can plot it).
    (c) Calculate $C_u$ and $C_c$.
    (d) Classify the soil if the fines are silty and have a PI of 3.

    **Answer:**
    (a)
    *   Weight of gravel (retained on 4.75mm): 120g
        % Gravel = (120 / 1270) * 100 = 9.45%
    *   Weight of sand (between 4.75mm and 0.075mm): 180+240+300+210+120+60 = 1110g
        % Sand = (1110 / 1270) * 100 = 87.40%
    *   Weight of fines (passing 0.075mm): 40g
        % Fines = (40 / 1270) * 100 = 3.15%

    (b) Assuming a plotted curve (for demonstration, actual values depend on precise plotting):
        Let's assume from the curve: $D_{10} = 0.12$ mm, $D_{30} = 0.35$ mm, $D_{60} = 0.80$ mm.

    (c)
    $C_u = D_{60}/D_{10} = 0.80 / 0.12 = 6.67$
    $C_c = (D_{30})^2 / (D_{10} \times D_{60}) = (0.35)^2 / (0.12 \times 0.80) = 0.1225 / 0.096 = 1.28$

    (d)
    Percentage of fines is 3.15% (less than 5%), so it's a coarse-grained soil.
    The coarse-grained fraction is predominantly sand (87.40%).
    The fines are silty (given).
    Since $C_u = 6.67 \ge 6$ and $1 \le C_c = 1.28 \le 3$, the sand is well-graded.
    Therefore, the soil is classified as **SW (Well-graded Sand)**.

2.  **Question:** A soil sample has the following properties:
    Liquid Limit (LL) = 45%
    Plastic Limit (PL) = 22%
    Percentage passing 75-micron sieve = 70%

    (a) Calculate the Plasticity Index (PI).
    (b) Determine if the soil is coarse-grained or fine-grained.
    (c) Plot the point (LL, PI) on a plasticity chart.
    (d) Classify the soil based on the plasticity chart and the percentage passing the 75-micron sieve.

    **Answer:**
    (a) PI = LL - PL = 45% - 22% = 23%

    (b) Since 70% of the soil passes the 75-micron sieve, it is a **fine-grained soil**.

    (c) Point is (45, 23).

    (d)
    *   LL = 45% (> 35%), so it's in the high compressibility range.
    *   PI = 23%.
    *   Check A-line: $PI_{A-line} = 0.73 (LL - 20) = 0.73 (45 - 20) = 0.73 \times 25 = 18.25$.
    *   Since the actual PI (23%) is greater than the A-line PI (18.25%), the soil plots **above the A-line**.
    *   Check B-line: $PI_{B-line} = 0.9 (LL - 8) = 0.9 (45 - 8) = 0.9 \times 37 = 33.3$.
    *   Since the actual PI (23%) is less than the B-line PI (33.3%), the soil plots **below the B-line**.

    The soil has LL > 35% and plots above the A-line and below the B-line. Therefore, the soil is classified as **CH (High Compressibility Inorganic Clay)**.

3.  **Question:** Differentiate between Sieve Analysis and Hydrometer Analysis.

    **Answer:**
    *   **Sieve Analysis:**
        *   **Purpose:** Determines particle size distribution for coarse-grained soils (gravels and sands).
        *   **Method:** Physical separation of particles by passing them through sieves of different mesh sizes.
        *   **Principle:** Sieves with openings of specific dimensions.
        *   **Particle Size Range:** Larger particles (typically > 0.075 mm).
    *   **Hydrometer Analysis:**
        *   **Purpose:** Determines particle size distribution for fine-grained soils (silts and clays).
        *   **Method:** Sedimentation of soil particles in a liquid suspension.
        *   **Principle:** Stokes' Law, relating settling velocity to particle size.
        *   **Particle Size Range:** Finer particles (typically < 0.075 mm).

### Important Points to Remember

*   **IS Classification System:** A two-stage process: first by grain size (coarse/fine), then by gradation/plasticity.
*   **Coarse-Grained Soils:** Classified based on grain size (gravel/sand) and gradation (well/poorly graded), and the nature of fines (silty/clayey).
*   **Fine-Grained Soils:** Classified based on Liquid Limit (LL) and Plasticity Index (PI) using the plasticity chart.
*   **Plasticity Chart:** A key tool for classifying fine-grained soils, with the A-line and B-line as crucial boundaries.
*   **Sieve Analysis:** For coarse-grained soils, uses sieves to determine particle size distribution.
*   **Hydrometer Analysis:** For fine-grained soils, uses sedimentation and Stokes' Law to determine particle size distribution.
*   **Fines Content:** The percentage of soil passing the 75-micron sieve. It dictates whether a soil is classified as coarse-grained or fine-grained.
*   **Specific Gravity ($G_s$):** Typically assumed as 2.65 for most inorganic soils in calculations, but can vary.
*   **Deflocculating Agent:** Essential in hydrometer analysis to prevent clumping of fine particles.
*   **Temperature Correction:** Crucial in hydrometer analysis due to its effect on viscosity and density of water.
