# Engineering Geology: Module 4 - Structural Geology: Attitude of Rocks - Dip and Strike

This module introduces the fundamental concepts of structural geology, focusing on how geological layers (strata) are oriented in space. Understanding the attitude of rocks is crucial for engineering projects, as it influences slope stability, groundwater flow, tunnel construction, and the feasibility of resource extraction.

## Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   Define and explain the terms "dip" and "strike" in the context of structural geology.
*   Understand the relationship between dip and strike and the orientation of geological strata.
*   Visualize and interpret the attitude of rock layers from geological maps and cross-sections.
*   Apply the concepts of dip and strike to solve basic engineering problems related to rock mass behavior.

---

## 1. Introduction to Structural Geology and Rock Attitudes

### 1.1 What is Structural Geology?

*   **Definition:** Structural geology is the branch of geology that deals with the **geometry and origin of geological structures** and the **forces and movements** that produce them.
*   **Importance in Engineering:** Understanding the deformation and arrangement of rock masses is vital for:
    *   **Site investigation:** Assessing the stability of slopes, tunnels, and foundations.
    *   **Resource exploration:** Locating and extracting minerals, oil, and gas.
    *   **Groundwater management:** Predicting flow patterns and potential contamination pathways.
    *   **Geohazard assessment:** Evaluating risks from landslides, earthquakes, and volcanic activity.

### 1.2 What are Geological Structures?

*   **Definition:** Geological structures are **features formed by deformation of rock masses**. They represent the way rocks have been folded, fractured, or otherwise displaced from their original positions.
*   **Types of Structures:**
    *   **Bedding:** The original layering in sedimentary rocks.
    *   **Foliation:** Layering or planar features in metamorphic rocks.
    *   **Faults:** Fractures along which there has been significant displacement.
    *   **Folds:** Wavelike deformations of rock layers.
    *   **Joints:** Fractures along which there has been no significant displacement.

### 1.3 Attitude of Rocks

*   **Definition:** The "attitude" of a rock layer or planar geological feature refers to its **orientation in three-dimensional space**.
*   **Key Components:** The attitude is described by two principal measurements: **dip** and **strike**.

## 2. Dip

### 2.1 Definition of Dip

*   **Dip is the angle between a horizontal plane and the inclined plane of the rock layer (or geological structure), measured downwards from the horizontal.**
*   It represents how steeply a rock layer is inclined.
*   The direction in which the dip is measured is crucial.

### 2.2 Components of Dip

*   **Dip Angle:**
    *   **Definition:** The numerical value of the angle, ranging from **0° (horizontal)** to **90° (vertical)**.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured with a clinometer or geological compass.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   A horizontal layer has a dip of 0°.
        *   A layer inclined at 45° to the horizontal has a dip of 45°.
        *   A vertical layer has a dip of 90°.

*   **Dip Direction:**
    *   **Definition:** The **compass direction (azimuth)** towards which the inclined plane points downwards.
    *   **Measurement:** Measured using a geological compass.
    *   **Relationship to Strike:** The dip direction is always **perpendicular (90°)** to the strike direction.

### 2.3 Visualizing Dip

Imagine a tilted book. The angle you would need to tilt it further downwards from a flat surface represents the dip angle. The direction you would tilt it in is the dip direction.

---

## 3. Strike

### 3.1 Definition of Strike

*   **Strike is the compass direction (azimuth) of a horizontal line that lies within an inclined plane of rock (or geological structure).**
*   It represents the trace of the intersection of the inclined plane with a horizontal plane.
*   Strike lines are always **parallel** to the orientation of the rock layer as it appears on a horizontal surface.

### 3.2 Characteristics of Strike

*   **Measurement:** Measured using a geological compass.
*   **Direction:** Expressed as a **two-point bearing** (e.g., N30°E or S60°W) or as a **single azimuth** followed by the dip direction (e.g., N30°E, dip 45°SE).
*   **Relationship to Dip:** The strike direction is always **perpendicular (90°)** to the dip direction.
*   **Persistence:** The strike direction remains constant along a perfectly planar geological feature.

### 3.3 Visualizing Strike

Imagine the edge of a tilted book. If you held a spirit level on the tilted surface, the line formed by the spirit level when it's perfectly horizontal on the surface is the strike line.

## 4. Strike and Dip Measurements

### 4.1 Notation and Convention

*   **Standard Notation:** Strike is usually expressed as a two-point bearing, where the bearing is measured in both directions (e.g., NE-SW or N30°E-S30°W).
*   **Dip Notation:** Dip is expressed as an angle followed by the dip direction (e.g., 45° SE, meaning the dip is 45 degrees towards the Southeast).
*   **Right-Hand Rule:** A common convention is that the strike direction is given first, and the dip direction is 90 degrees to the right of the strike direction. This is a useful mnemonic.

    *   **Example:** If strike is N30°E, the dip direction would be S60°E (90° clockwise from N30°E). Or, it could be N60°W (90° counter-clockwise from N30°E). You need the dip angle to specify which is correct.

### 4.2 Practical Measurement with a Geological Compass (Brunton Compass)

A geological compass is designed to measure both dip and strike simultaneously.

1.  **To measure Strike:**
    *   Hold the compass level over the rock surface.
    *   Rotate the compass until the bubble in the spirit level is centered (meaning the compass is horizontal).
    *   The needle of the compass will point to the **strike direction**. Read the bearing from the compass dial. Note this as a two-point bearing (e.g., N60°E - S60°W).

2.  **To measure Dip:**
    *   Once the strike is determined, orient the compass so that the side with the clinometer is placed on the inclined rock surface.
    *   The strike line is perpendicular to the dip direction.
    *   Hold the compass so that the edge is aligned with the strike.
    *   The **dip direction** will be perpendicular to the strike.
    *   Tilt the compass downwards in the **dip direction** until the bubble in the clinometer is centered.
    *   Read the angle on the clinometer. This is the **dip angle**.
    *   Record the dip direction (e.g., SE, NW).

### 4.3 Key Relationships to Remember

*   **Strike and Dip are Perpendicular:** Strike direction $\perp$ Dip direction.
*   **Horizontal Plane:** Dip = 0°. Strike is undefined or can be any horizontal direction.
*   **Vertical Plane:** Dip = 90°. Strike is perpendicular to the dip direction, but the dip direction is essentially along the strike line (down the vertical face).
*   **Stratigraphic Thickness vs. Apparent Thickness:**
    *   **Stratigraphic Thickness:** The true thickness of a rock layer measured perpendicular to its bedding planes.
    *   **Apparent Thickness:** The thickness of a layer measured on a vertical or inclined surface. It is only equal to the stratigraphic thickness when the measurement is taken perpendicular to the bedding plane.

## 5. Visualizing and Interpreting Attitudes on Geological Maps

### 5.1 Representation of Dip and Strike on Maps

*   **Strike and Dip Symbols:**
    *   A long line indicates the strike direction.
    *   A shorter line (or barb) extending from the strike line indicates the dip direction.
    *   Numbers adjacent to the dip barb indicate the dip angle in degrees.

    ```
        /|  <-- Dip Barb (shows dip direction)
       / |
      /  |  <-- Dip Angle (e.g., 30)
     /___|_______
    Strike Line
    ```

*   **Interpreting the Symbols:**
    *   The strike line shows the direction of a horizontal line on the rock surface.
    *   The dip barb points in the direction of the steepest downward slope.
    *   The number indicates how steep that downward slope is.

### 5.2 Rules for Reading Strike and Dip Symbols

*   **The strike line is always perpendicular to the dip direction.**
*   The dip direction points to the side with the steeper inclination.
*   On a map, if you can imagine a horizontal line along the strike line, the dip is the angle downwards from that line in the direction indicated by the barb.

### 5.3 Geological Cross-Sections

*   Geological cross-sections are vertical slices through the Earth's crust, showing the subsurface geology.
*   Strike and dip measurements are used to construct accurate cross-sections, depicting how rock layers are oriented underground.
*   A horizontal rock layer will appear as a horizontal line on a cross-section.
*   An inclined rock layer will appear as a tilted line, with the angle of tilt on the cross-section related to the true dip angle.

## 6. Applications in Engineering Geology

### 6.1 Slope Stability

*   **Discontinuity Orientation:** The orientation of bedding planes, joints, and faults (their strike and dip) significantly influences the stability of slopes.
*   **Wedge Failure:** If discontinuities form a wedge shape that dips out of a slope face, it can lead to instability.
*   **Planar Failure:** If a single discontinuity (like a bedding plane) dips parallel to the slope face, it can cause sliding.

    *   **Example:** A rock excavation for a highway cut. If the bedding planes dip at a steep angle towards the excavation face, the slope is more likely to fail.

### 6.2 Tunneling

*   **Groundwater Flow:** The dip and strike of permeable rock layers and fractures control groundwater movement within a rock mass, affecting tunnel dewatering.
*   **Stability of Tunnel Roof and Walls:** The orientation of discontinuities can influence the support required for tunnels.

    *   **Example:** In a tunnel driven perpendicular to the strike of dipping strata, the roof might be more stable than if driven parallel to the strike where a major bedding plane is exposed.

### 6.3 Foundation Design

*   **Bearing Capacity:** The orientation of rock layers can affect how loads from structures are transmitted into the ground.
*   **Settlement:** Uneven dipping of rock layers beneath a foundation can lead to differential settlement.

### 6.4 Resource Extraction

*   **Ore Body Orientation:** The strike and dip of mineralized veins or ore bodies dictate the methods and economic viability of mining.
*   **Coal Seam Extraction:** The dip of coal seams affects the design of mines and mining techniques.

## 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Instructions:** For each question, determine the correct answer based on the concepts of dip and strike.

**Question 1:**
A rock layer is observed to be perfectly horizontal. What is its dip angle?
a) 0°
b) 45°
c) 90°
d) It cannot be determined.

**Question 2:**
If the strike of a rock layer is N45°E, what are the possible dip directions?
a) N45°E and S45°W
b) S45°E and N45°W
c) N45°S and S45°N
d) N135°E and S135°W

**Question 3:**
A geological map shows a strike and dip symbol with a strike line running East-West and a dip barb pointing North with a value of 60°. What is the dip of this rock layer?
a) 60° South
b) 60° North
c) 60° East
d) 60° West

**Question 4:**
If a rock layer has a dip of 30° towards the Southeast, what is its strike direction?
a) NE-SW
b) NW-SE
c) N30°E-S30°W
d) N60°E-S60°W

**Question 5:**
Explain why understanding the dip and strike of rock layers is important for designing a highway cut.

## 8. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
a) 0°
*   **Explanation:** A horizontal layer has no inclination from the horizontal plane, hence its dip is 0°.

**Answer 2:**
b) S45°E and N45°W
*   **Explanation:** The dip direction is always perpendicular to the strike direction. If strike is N45°E, then 90° clockwise is S45°E, and 90° counter-clockwise is N45°W.

**Answer 3:**
b) 60° North
*   **Explanation:** The strike line is East-West. The dip barb points North. The value 60° is the dip angle. Therefore, the dip is 60° North.

**Answer 4:**
d) N60°E-S60°W
*   **Explanation:** If the dip is 30° SE, the dip direction is SE. The strike direction is perpendicular to SE. The strike direction is either NE or SW. To find the strike, we rotate 90° from SE. 90° counter-clockwise from SE is NE. 90° clockwise from SE is SW. So, the strike is NE-SW. More precisely, if dip is 30° SE, then the strike is 60° away from North (or South) towards East (or West). Specifically, the strike would be N60°E - S60°W.

**Answer 5:**
Understanding the dip and strike of rock layers is crucial for designing a highway cut because:
*   **Stability:** If rock layers (bedding planes, joints) dip steeply towards the excavation face, they can create unstable conditions leading to landslides or rockfalls. Engineers need to identify these orientations to design appropriate cut slopes, retaining walls, or rock bolting systems to prevent failure.
*   **Drainage:** The dip of rock layers influences groundwater flow. Understanding this helps in designing drainage systems for the cut to prevent water buildup, which can further destabilize the slopes.
*   **Excavation Method:** The orientation of rock features can influence the choice of excavation methods (e.g., blasting vs. ripping).

## 9. Important Points to Remember

*   **Dip:** The angle of inclination of a rock plane, measured downwards from the horizontal (0° to 90°).
*   **Dip Direction:** The compass direction the inclined plane faces downwards.
*   **Strike:** The compass direction of a horizontal line on the rock plane.
*   **Perpendicularity:** Strike and dip directions are always perpendicular (90° apart).
*   **Measurement Tools:** Geological compass (Brunton compass) is essential for accurate measurement.
*   **Map Symbols:** Recognize and interpret strike and dip symbols on geological maps.
*   **Engineering Significance:** Dip and strike are fundamental to assessing slope stability, tunnel design, foundation engineering, and resource exploration.
*   **True vs. Apparent Thickness:** Be aware that the measured thickness of a layer in a borehole or on a map might be apparent, not true, unless measured perpendicular to the bedding.
