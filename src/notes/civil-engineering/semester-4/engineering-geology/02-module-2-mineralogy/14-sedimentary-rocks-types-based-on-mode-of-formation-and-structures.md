# Engineering Geology: Module 2 - Mineralogy

## Topic: Sedimentary Rocks - Types Based on Mode of Formation and Structures

This module delves into sedimentary rocks, focusing on their classification based on how they form and the characteristic structures they exhibit. Understanding these aspects is crucial for engineering applications, as they influence the rock's mechanical properties, permeability, and stability.

---

### Learning Outcomes Covered:

*   **Understand the classification of sedimentary rocks based on their origin (mode of formation).**
*   **Identify and describe the various sedimentary structures and their significance in engineering geology.**
*   **Relate the mode of formation and structures of sedimentary rocks to their engineering properties.**

---

### 1. Classification of Sedimentary Rocks Based on Mode of Formation

Sedimentary rocks are formed from the accumulation and lithification (compaction and cementation) of sediments. Sediments can originate from various sources, leading to different rock types. The primary classification is based on the **nature of the parent material**.

#### 1.1. Clastic Sedimentary Rocks

*   **Definition:** Formed from the accumulation and lithification of fragments (clasts) of pre-existing rocks that have been weathered, eroded, transported, and deposited.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Weathering:** Physical and chemical breakdown of rocks.
    *   **Erosion:** Removal of weathered material by natural agents (wind, water, ice, gravity).
    *   **Transportation:** Movement of eroded material.
    *   **Deposition:** Settling of transported material when the transporting agent loses energy.
    *   **Lithification:** The process that converts unconsolidated sediments into solid sedimentary rock, involving:
        *   **Compaction:** Reduction in pore space due to the weight of overlying sediments.
        *   **Cementation:** Precipitation of minerals (e.g., calcite, silica, iron oxides) in the pore spaces, binding the clasts together.
*   **Classification based on clast size:**

    | Rock Name        | Dominant Clast Size    | Characteristics                                                                                                                                 | Examples                                                                               | Engineering Significance                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
    | :--------------- | :--------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | **Conglomerate** | Gravel-sized (>2mm)    | Rounded clasts, poorly sorted or well-sorted.                                                                                                   | Riverbed deposits, alluvial fans.                                                      | Can be strong and durable if well-cemented, but rounded clasts can lead to lower interlocking and potential for pore water pressure build-up if poorly cemented. Significant variability in strength depending on the matrix and cement.                                                                                                                          |
    | **Breccia**      | Gravel-sized (>2mm)    | Angular clasts, poorly sorted.                                                                                                                  | Fault zones (fault breccia), talus slopes (talus breccia).                           | Angular clasts provide good interlocking, potentially leading to high strength if well-cemented. However, fault breccias can be highly fractured and permeable, leading to reduced strength and increased susceptibility to weathering and collapse.                                                                                                                 |
    | **Sandstone**    | Sand-sized (0.0625-2mm) | Composed primarily of sand grains. Texture varies based on sorting and rounding of grains. Common cements: silica, calcite, iron oxides.          | Beaches, deserts, river channels, shallow marine environments.                         | Strength varies significantly with cementation and grain type. Quartz-rich sandstones (quartzarenites) are generally strong and durable. Arkoses (feldspar-rich) and lithic sandstones (rock fragment-rich) can be less stable due to the presence of less resistant minerals. High porosity can lead to permeability issues and groundwater flow. |
    | **Siltstone**    | Silt-sized (0.0039-0.0625mm) | Composed primarily of silt-sized particles. Feels gritty to the touch. Often fissile (splits into thin layers).                                | Lake beds, tidal flats, deep marine environments.                                      | Generally weaker than sandstone. Fissility can lead to anisotropy in strength and weathering. Can be susceptible to slaking (disintegration when exposed to water). Permeability is generally lower than sandstone.                                                                                                                                |
    | **Shale**        | Clay-sized (<0.0039mm) | Composed primarily of clay minerals. Typically fissile and exhibits laminations. Often exhibits plasticity when wet.                           | Lake beds, deltaic environments, deep marine environments.                             | Generally the weakest of the clastic rocks. Fissility leads to pronounced anisotropy in strength and deformation. Swelling clays can cause significant volume changes and instability in the presence of water. Low permeability, but can be fractured making it permeable.                                                                                  |

#### 1.2. Chemical Sedimentary Rocks

*   **Definition:** Formed by the precipitation of minerals from a solution, typically water.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Precipitation:** Process where dissolved ions in a solution combine to form solid minerals.
    *   **Evaporation:** Concentration of dissolved ions in water bodies, leading to supersaturation and precipitation.
*   **Types and Examples:**

    | Rock Name      | Parent Solution/Process       | Composition                                    | Examples                                                              | Engineering Significance                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
    | :------------- | :---------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
    | **Limestone**  | Precipitation of Calcium Carbonate (CaCO₃) | Primarily calcite (CaCO₃). Can be biogenic or abiogenic. | Coral reefs, shells of marine organisms, precipitation from hard water. | **Biogenic limestones** (e.g., chalk, coquina) can be weak and porous. **Oolitic limestones** (formed by precipitation around nuclei) can be relatively strong. **Crystalline limestones** can be strong. Soluble in acidic groundwater, leading to karst topography and potential ground collapse. Permeability varies greatly. |
    | **Dolomite**   | Precipitation of Calcium Magnesium Carbonate (CaMg(CO₃)₂) | Primarily dolomite (CaMg(CO₃)₂). Often formed by alteration of limestone. | Similar environments to limestone.                                    | Generally harder and less soluble than limestone. Can exhibit secondary porosity due to dissolution of dolomite crystals, leading to increased permeability. Strength is generally good.                                                                                                |
    | **Evaporites** | Evaporation of saline water   | Halite (NaCl), Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O), Anhydrite (CaSO₄). | Salt flats, arid basins.                                              | **Halite** is very soluble and weak. **Gypsum** can dehydrate and alter to anhydrite, leading to volume changes and instability. **Anhydrite** is relatively insoluble but can convert to gypsum with hydration. All are generally considered problematic for construction due to solubility and volume change potential. |
    | **Chert**      | Precipitation of Silica (SiO₂) | Cryptocrystalline silica (quartz).           | Deep marine environments, hot springs.                                | Very hard, dense, and durable. Exhibits conchoidal fracture. Can be problematic in aggregates for concrete due to potential for alkali-silica reaction if not properly managed. Low permeability.                                                                                              |

#### 1.3. Organic (Biochemical) Sedimentary Rocks

*   **Definition:** Formed from the accumulation of organic matter, or from the shells and skeletons of organisms.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Organic Matter:** Decomposed plant and animal remains.
    *   **Skeletal Material:** Calcareous or siliceous remains of organisms.
*   **Types and Examples:**

    | Rock Name        | Parent Material/Process                          | Composition                                       | Examples                                                                     | Engineering Significance                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
    | :--------------- | :----------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
    | **Coal**         | Accumulation and burial of plant material        | Primarily carbon, with varying amounts of hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen. | Swamps, bogs.                                                                | Low strength and high compressibility. Highly combustible, presenting a fire hazard. Can be problematic for excavation and tunneling due to instability and potential for spontaneous combustion. Low density.                                                                                |
    | **Limestone (Biogenic)** | Accumulation of shells, skeletal fragments, or coral | Primarily calcite (CaCO₃).                        | Coral reefs, fossiliferous limestones.                                       | As described under chemical sedimentary rocks, strength and porosity vary greatly. Presence of fossils can indicate potential discontinuities and varying material properties within the rock mass.                                                                                                  |
    | **Chalk**        | Accumulation of microscopic marine plankton      | Calcite (CaCO₃).                                  | Chalk cliffs, shallow marine environments.                                    | Very soft, porous, and compressible. Highly susceptible to weathering and erosion. Low strength. Can be problematic for foundations and excavations.                                                                                                                                  |
    | **Diatomite**    | Accumulation of siliceous shells of diatoms      | Amorphous silica (opal).                          | Fossiliferous lake beds.                                                     | Very low density and high porosity. Soft and friable. Generally poor engineering properties due to low strength and high compressibility. Can be used as a filter medium.                                                                                                                 |
