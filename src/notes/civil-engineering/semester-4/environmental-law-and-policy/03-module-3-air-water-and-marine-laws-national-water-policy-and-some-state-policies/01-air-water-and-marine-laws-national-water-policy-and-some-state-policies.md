# Environmental Law and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water, and Marine Laws: National Water Policy and State Policies

This module delves into the legal frameworks governing air, water, and marine resources in India, with a particular focus on the National Water Policy and illustrative state-level initiatives.

---

## 1. Introduction to Environmental Law and Policy

Environmental Law and Policy are crucial for regulating human activities that impact the environment and for protecting natural resources for present and future generations. This module focuses on the water sector, highlighting the importance of integrated management and the role of national and state policies.

---

## 2. Air Laws

While the primary focus of this module is water, understanding related environmental laws provides context. Air pollution is a significant environmental concern, and India has established legislation to address it.

### 2.1. The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

This Act is the primary legislation for preventing and controlling air pollution in India.

**Key Objectives:**

*   Prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution.
*   Maintaining the quality of air.

**Key Provisions:**

*   **Establishment of Boards:** Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) are empowered to:
    *   Advise governments on matters relating to air pollution.
    *   Lay down standards for air quality.
    *   Lay down standards for emission from industrial and other sources.
    *   Collect and publish information relating to air pollution.
    *   Inspect air pollution control equipment and control measures.
    *   Grant consent for industries to operate in specified areas.
*   **Powers to Boards:**
    *   To lay down air quality standards.
    *   To lay down emission standards for industries.
    *   To inspect and direct compliance.
*   **Penalties:** Penalties for contravention of provisions include fines and imprisonment.

**Important Point to Remember:** The Air Act emphasizes a "consent to operate" mechanism for industries, requiring them to obtain permission from SPCBs and adhere to prescribed emission standards.

## 3. Water Laws

India's water resources are vital for agriculture, industry, domestic use, and ecosystems. Various legal instruments govern their use and protection.

### 3.1. The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

This is the cornerstone legislation for preventing and controlling water pollution in India.

**Key Objectives:**

*   Prevention and control of water pollution.
*   Restoration and maintenance of the wholesomeness of water.

**Key Provisions:**

*   **Establishment of Boards:** CPCB and SPCBs are empowered to:
    *   Advise governments on matters relating to water pollution.
    *   Lay down standards for water quality.
    *   Lay down standards for sewage and trade effluents discharged from industries.
    *   Collect and publish information relating to water pollution.
    *   Inspect sewage or trade effluent treatment plants.
    *   Grant consent for industries to discharge effluents.
*   **Powers to Boards:**
    *   To lay down stream water quality standards.
    *   To lay down effluent standards.
    *   To inspect and direct compliance.
    *   To issue notices and directions to polluters.
*   **Prohibition of Discharges:** No person can knowingly cause or permit to be caused any water pollution.
*   **Powers of Entry and Inspection:** Authorized officers have the power to enter premises and take samples.
*   **Penalties:** Similar to the Air Act, penalties include fines and imprisonment.

**Important Point to Remember:** The Water Act mandates obtaining "consent to discharge" for industries before releasing effluents into any water body, ensuring compliance with prescribed standards.

### 3.2. Other Important Water-Related Legislation

*   **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** This umbrella legislation provides powers to the Central Government to protect and improve the environment. It empowers the government to take measures to prevent, control, and abate environmental pollution, including water pollution. It also allows for the setting up of environmental standards and the prohibition of certain activities.
*   **The Indian Easements Act, 1882:** While not directly an environmental law, it deals with rights related to water access and usage on land, which can have environmental implications.
*   **The River Boards Act, 1956:** This Act provides for the establishment of river boards for the regulation and development of inter-state rivers and river valleys.

## 4. Marine Laws

The vast coastline of India and its exclusive economic zone necessitate specific laws to protect marine ecosystems and resources.

### 4.1. The Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification

While not a standalone Act, the CRZ Notification, issued under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, is a critical tool for regulating activities in coastal areas.

**Key Objectives:**

*   Protect and conserve coastal ecosystems, including mangroves, coral reefs, beaches, and dunes.
*   Regulate developmental activities in coastal zones to prevent degradation.
*   Promote sustainable development of coastal areas.

**Key Provisions:**

*   **Classification of Coastal Areas:** Coastal stretches are classified into four categories (CRZ-I, CRZ-II, CRZ-III, CRZ-IV) with varying restrictions on development.
    *   **CRZ-I:** Ecologically sensitive areas, no new construction allowed.
    *   **CRZ-II:** Areas that have already been developed, regulated development permitted.
    *   **CRZ-III:** Relatively undisturbed areas, permitted construction within specific guidelines.
    *   **CRZ-IV:** Territorial waters, activities related to fishing and other marine activities permitted.
*   **Development Restrictions:** Specific prohibitions and regulations on activities like construction, setting up of industries, and waste disposal within CRZ areas.
*   **Coastal Zone Management Plans (CZMPs):** States are mandated to prepare and implement CZMPs.

**Important Point to Remember:** The CRZ Notification is dynamic and subject to periodic amendments, reflecting evolving environmental concerns and developmental needs.

### 4.2. Other Relevant Marine Laws

*   **The Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, Exclusive Economic Zone and Other Maritime Zones Act, 1976:** This Act defines India's maritime zones and asserts its sovereign rights over them, including for the purpose of conservation and management of marine resources.
*   **The Merchant Shipping Act, 1958:** Regulates shipping and navigation, including measures to prevent pollution from ships.

## 5. National Water Policy

India's National Water Policy serves as a guiding document for water resource development and management. It aims to ensure equitable distribution, efficient use, and sustainable management of water resources.

### 5.1. Evolution of National Water Policies

India has had several National Water Policies, with significant revisions reflecting changing priorities:

*   **National Water Policy, 1987:** Focused on increasing irrigation efficiency and improving water management.
*   **National Water Policy, 2002:** Emphasized integrated water resource management, water pricing, and conservation.
*   **National Water Policy, 2012:** The current policy, with a greater focus on holistic water management, sustainability, and public participation.

### 5.2. Key Principles and Objectives of the National Water Policy, 2012

The 2012 policy is built on the foundation of previous policies but introduces several new dimensions.

**Core Objectives:**

*   **Ensure Sustainable and Equitable Availability:** To ensure sustainable and equitable availability of water for all legitimate uses through integrated water resource management.
*   **Promote Efficient Use and Conservation:** To promote efficient use of water, conservation of water resources, and protect water bodies from pollution.
*   **Address Water Scarcity and Stress:** To address the challenges of water scarcity and water stress in various parts of the country.
*   **Facilitate Water Sector Reforms:** To facilitate necessary reforms in the water sector to improve efficiency and equity.

**Key Principles and Provisions:**

*   **Water as a Public Good:** While recognizing water as a vital resource, it's also considered a common-pool resource that needs to be managed for the benefit of all.
*   **Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM):** This is a central tenet, promoting a holistic approach to managing water resources in a basin-wide manner, considering all aspects of water use and management.
*   **Water Allocation and Prioritization:**
    *   **Drinking Water:** To be given the highest priority.
    *   **Irrigation:** To be the next priority.
    *   Other uses like industrial, ecological needs, etc.
*   **Water Conservation and Efficiency:**
    *   Promoting rainwater harvesting.
    *   Encouraging water-efficient technologies in agriculture and industry.
    *   Reducing transmission losses.
*   **Groundwater Management:**
    *   Regulation of groundwater abstraction to prevent over-exploitation.
    *   Emphasis on recharge and conservation.
*   **Water Quality and Pollution Control:**
    *   Prevention of pollution from domestic, industrial, and agricultural sources.
    *   Treatment of wastewater.
*   **Inter-State Water Disputes:** Emphasis on cooperative and timely resolution of inter-state water disputes through dialogue and adjudication.
*   **Public Participation and Stakeholder Involvement:** Encouraging the involvement of local communities, Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs), and other stakeholders in water management.
*   **Water Pricing:** Consideration of economic instruments like water pricing to promote efficient use, though with due consideration for the poor and vulnerable sections.
*   **Management of Floods and Droughts:** Developing strategies for flood control and drought mitigation.
*   **Trans-boundary Rivers:** Cooperation with neighboring countries for the management of shared water resources.

**Important Point to Remember:** The National Water Policy is a *policy document* and not a legally binding Act. However, it guides the formulation of legislation and action plans at the national and state levels.

## 6. State Water Policies and Initiatives

States in India have their own water policies and specific initiatives to address their unique water challenges. These often align with the National Water Policy but have localized approaches.

### 6.1. Examples of State-Level Policies and Initiatives

*   **Gujarat:**
    *   **Gujarat Groundwater Development and Regulation Act, 2011:** Aims to regulate groundwater extraction to prevent over-exploitation.
    *   **Water Sector Reforms:** Focus on micro-irrigation, participatory irrigation management, and watershed development.
    *   **Narmada Canal Network:** Extensive canal network for irrigation and water supply.
*   **Maharashtra:**
    *   **Maharashtra Groundwater Development and Regulation Act, 2009:** Similar to Gujarat, to regulate groundwater.
    *   **Jalyukt Shivar Abhiyan:** A flagship scheme focused on making villages drought-free through water conservation and water harvesting.
    *   **Participatory Irrigation Management (PIM):** Encouraging farmer participation in irrigation management.
*   **Rajasthan:**
    *   **Rajasthan Groundwater (Regulation and Management) Bill:** Efforts to regulate groundwater extraction in water-scarce regions.
    *   **Jal Swavalamban Abhiyan:** A campaign to conserve water and increase water availability at the village level.
    *   **Inter-linking of Rivers:** Plans and initiatives to inter-link rivers to address regional water imbalances.
*   **Tamil Nadu:**
    *   **Tamil Nadu Water Resources Consolidation Act, 1995:** Encompasses various aspects of water management.
    *   **Rainwater Harvesting (RWH) Mandate:** Mandatory RWH structures in buildings, a pioneering initiative.
    *   **Desalination Plants:** Setting up desalination plants to augment drinking water supply, especially in coastal areas.

**Important Point to Remember:** State policies are crucial for the on-ground implementation of water management strategies. They often reflect the specific climatic conditions, hydrological characteristics, and socio-economic realities of the state.

## 7. Interplay between National and State Policies

The National Water Policy provides the overarching framework, while state policies translate these principles into actionable plans and regulations tailored to local contexts. There is a continuous need for coordination and alignment between national goals and state-level implementation.

## 8. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What are the primary objectives of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974?

**Answer:** The primary objectives are the prevention and control of water pollution and the restoration and maintenance of the wholesomeness of water.

**Question 2:** Briefly explain the concept of Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) as promoted by the National Water Policy, 2012.

**Answer:** IWRM is a holistic approach to managing water resources in a basin-wide manner, considering all aspects of water use and management, including surface water, groundwater, quality, quantity, and the involvement of all stakeholders. It aims for sustainable and equitable availability of water.

**Question 3:** Name two key provisions of the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification.

**Answer:**
1.  Classification of coastal areas into different categories (CRZ-I, II, III, IV) with specific development regulations.
2.  Prohibition of certain developmental activities in ecologically sensitive zones (CRZ-I).

**Question 4:** Which state in India is particularly known for its mandatory rainwater harvesting (RWH) initiatives in buildings?

**Answer:** Tamil Nadu.

**Question 5:** What is the significance of "consent to operate" under the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981?

**Answer:** "Consent to operate" is a mechanism requiring industries to obtain permission from State Pollution Control Boards before operating. This permission is granted based on the industry's adherence to prescribed emission standards and the installation of adequate pollution control measures, ensuring control of air pollution.

## 9. Important Points to Remember (Summary)

*   **The Air Act, 1981 & The Water Act, 1974:** These are the foundational laws for pollution control in India, establishing CPCB and SPCBs and mandating consent mechanisms for industries.
*   **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** An umbrella legislation empowering the government to protect the environment and set standards.
*   **CRZ Notification:** Crucial for regulating activities in coastal areas to protect fragile ecosystems.
*   **National Water Policy, 2012:** Provides a guiding framework for sustainable and equitable water resource management, emphasizing IWRM, conservation, and public participation.
*   **State Policies:** Complement national policies with localized strategies and regulations, crucial for effective implementation.
*   **Inter-State Water Disputes:** A significant challenge that requires cooperative mechanisms for resolution.
*   **Groundwater Regulation:** Increasingly important due to over-exploitation in many regions.
*   **Pollution Control:** A continuous process requiring monitoring, enforcement, and technological advancements.
