# Environmental Law and Policy - Module 3: Air, Water and Marine Laws

## Topic: Pollution Control Boards, Groundwater and Law, Judicial Remedies and Procedures, Marine Laws of India

This module delves into critical aspects of environmental protection, focusing on the regulatory frameworks governing water resources, legal recourse for pollution, and the specific laws pertaining to India's marine environment.

---

### 1. Pollution Control Boards (PCBs)

Pollution Control Boards are crucial statutory bodies established to prevent, control, and abate environmental pollution in India.

#### 1.1. Establishment and Mandate

*   **Under which Acts?**
    *   **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:** Provides for the establishment of Central and State Pollution Control Boards.
    *   **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981:** Empowers PCBs to control air pollution as well.
    *   **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** Further strengthens the powers and functions of PCBs.
*   **Key Mandates:**
    *   Advise government on environmental matters.
    *   Lay down standards for water and air quality.
    *   Grant consent to operate for industries discharging pollutants.
    *   Monitor environmental quality.
    *   Investigate and inspect sources of pollution.
    *   Take legal action against violators.
    *   Promote research and development in pollution control.

#### 1.2. Structure and Functions

*   **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):**
    *   Apex body for environmental regulation in India.
    *   Sets national standards and guidelines.
    *   Coordinates activities of State PCBs.
    *   Undertakes nation-wide studies and surveys on pollution.
    *   Has powers to direct State PCBs.
*   **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs):**
    *   Implement environmental laws within their respective states.
    *   Grant Consent to Establish (CTE) and Consent to Operate (CTO).
    *   Monitor ambient air and water quality.
    *   Handle complaints related to pollution.
    *   Prosecute offenders.
*   **Union Territory Pollution Control Committees (UTPCCs):**
    *   Function similarly to SPCBs in Union Territories.

#### 1.3. Powers and Enforcement

*   **Inspection and Monitoring:** PCBs have the power to inspect any industry, premise, or water body suspected of causing pollution.
*   **Direction and Prohibition:** Can issue directions to cease or prevent any activity causing pollution.
*   **Consent Mechanism:** Industries must obtain Consent to Establish (CTE) before setting up operations and Consent to Operate (CTO) to continue operations, subject to compliance with environmental norms.
*   **Penalties:** Can impose penalties and initiate prosecution for non-compliance.

#### 1.4. Important Points to Remember

*   PCBs are the primary regulatory bodies for water and air pollution control in India.
*   Their functioning is governed by multiple environmental statutes.
*   The consent mechanism is a key tool for controlling industrial pollution.

### 2. Groundwater and Law

Groundwater is a vital resource, and its protection and sustainable management are critical.

#### 2.1. Importance of Groundwater

*   **Primary Drinking Water Source:** A significant portion of India's population relies on groundwater for drinking.
*   **Agriculture:** Essential for irrigation, supporting India's agrarian economy.
*   **Industrial Use:** Used in various industrial processes.
*   **Recharge of Surface Water:** Groundwater contributes to the flow of rivers and streams.

#### 2.2. Challenges in Groundwater Management

*   **Over-exploitation:** Excessive extraction for agriculture and domestic use leads to depletion of water tables.
*   **Pollution:** Contamination from industrial effluents, agricultural runoff (pesticides, fertilizers), sewage, and hazardous waste disposal.
*   **Lack of Regulation:** Historically, groundwater extraction was less regulated than surface water.

#### 2.3. Legal Framework for Groundwater

*   **The Constitution of India:**
    *   **Article 48A:** Directive Principle of State Policy to protect and improve the environment and safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country.
    *   **Article 51A(g):** Fundamental Duty of every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment.
*   **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974:** While primarily focused on surface water, it can indirectly impact groundwater through the regulation of activities that could contaminate it.
*   **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:** Provides a broad framework for protecting the environment, including groundwater, by regulating activities that cause pollution.
*   **Groundwater Regulation/Management Acts (State-specific):**
    *   Many states have enacted specific legislation to regulate groundwater extraction.
    *   **Example:** *Delhi Groundwater Regulation Act, 2009*, *Gujarat Groundwater (Development and Management) Act, 2010*, *Rajasthan Groundwater (Management and Control) Act, 2009*.
    *   These acts often involve:
        *   **Permit/NOC for extraction:** For specific purposes and volumes.
        *   **Protection of recharge zones:** Preventing activities that hinder natural recharge.
        *   **Pollution control measures:** Preventing contamination.
        *   **Declaration of over-exploited areas:** Implementing stricter controls.

#### 2.4. Judicial Pronouncements on Groundwater

*   **A.P. Pollution Control Board v. Prof. M.V. Nayudu (1999):** The Supreme Court held that groundwater is a common resource and its contamination affects the right to life.
*   **Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991):** Supreme Court reiterated that the right to life includes the right to a clean environment and clean water.
*   **M.C. Mehta v. Union of India:** Various pronouncements have emphasized the need for proper groundwater management and pollution control.

#### 2.5. Important Points to Remember

*   Groundwater is a critical but vulnerable resource.
*   Over-exploitation and pollution are major threats.
*   A combination of central environmental laws and state-specific groundwater acts governs its management.
*   Judiciary has played a significant role in asserting the right to clean groundwater.

### 3. Judicial Remedies and Procedures

When environmental laws are violated, various legal avenues are available to seek redressal and enforce compliance.

#### 3.1. Types of Remedies

*   **Injunctions:**
    *   **Prohibitory Injunction:** Restraining a party from doing a wrongful act (e.g., preventing discharge of pollutants).
    *   **Mandatory Injunction:** Compelling a party to do a specific act (e.g., installing pollution control equipment).
*   **Damages:** Monetary compensation for harm caused by pollution.
*   **Restoration/Remediation:** Ordering the polluter to restore the environment to its previous state.
*   **Penalties and Fines:** Imposed by courts or administrative bodies for contravention of environmental laws.
*   **Revocation of Licenses/Permits:** Cancellation of operating permits for persistent non-compliance.

#### 3.2. Legal Procedures and Forums

*   **Civil Courts:**
    *   Can entertain suits for injunctions and damages.
    *   Jurisdiction is generally based on the location of the cause of action.
*   **Criminal Courts:**
    *   Prosecution for offenses under environmental laws (e.g., Water Act, Air Act, EPA).
    *   Punishments can include imprisonment and fines.
*   **Pollution Control Boards (PCBs):**
    *   Can issue directions, impose penalties, and prosecute offenders.
    *   Act as quasi-judicial authorities in certain contexts.
*   **National Green Tribunal (NGT):**
    *   Established under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.
    *   Deals with all civil cases relating to environmental protection and conservation of forests and other natural resources.
    *   Has appellate jurisdiction over certain environmental orders.
    *   Aims for expeditious disposal of environmental cases.
    *   **Powers:** Similar to a civil court, can grant relief, compensation, and restitution of property.
*   **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):**
    *   A powerful tool for environmental protection in India.
    *   Allows any public-spirited citizen or organization to approach the High Courts or Supreme Court for redressal of grievances concerning environmental issues affecting the public.
    *   Lowered locus standi requirements.
    *   **Example:** *M.C. Mehta cases* are prime examples of PILs leading to significant environmental judgments.

#### 3.3. Key Principles in Environmental Litigation

*   **Precautionary Principle:** Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, lack of full scientific certainty shall not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation.
*   **Polluter Pays Principle:** The polluter should bear the costs of pollution prevention, control, and damage.
*   **Sustainable Development:** Balancing economic development with environmental protection.
*   **Public Trust Doctrine:** Natural resources like air, water, and forests are held in trust by the state for the benefit of the public.

#### 3.4. Important Points to Remember

*   A multi-pronged approach involving civil, criminal, and specialized tribunals (NGT) is available for environmental justice.
*   PIL is a vital mechanism for bringing environmental issues to court.
*   Key principles like Precautionary and Polluter Pays guide judicial decision-making.

### 4. Marine Laws of India

India, with its extensive coastline, has a legal framework to protect its marine environment and resources.

#### 4.1. Importance of Marine Environment

*   **Biodiversity Hotspot:** Coastal and marine ecosystems host immense biodiversity.
*   **Fisheries:** Supports livelihoods and food security.
*   **Coastal Protection:** Mangroves and coral reefs protect coastlines from erosion and storms.
*   **Economic Activities:** Shipping, tourism, and resource extraction.
*   **Climate Regulation:** Oceans play a role in regulating global climate.

#### 4.2. Key Indian Legislation and Frameworks

*   **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:**
    *   Provides a broad umbrella for protecting the marine environment from pollution from land-based activities, ships, and offshore installations.
    *   Allows for the notification of Coastal Regulation Zones (CRZs).
*   **Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification:**
    *   Issued under the EPA, 1986.
    *   Classifies coastal areas into different zones (CRZ-I, CRZ-II, CRZ-III, CRZ-IV) with specific regulations for each.
    *   **CRZ-I:** Ecologically sensitive areas (mangroves, coral reefs, estuaries) where no developmental activities are permitted.
    *   **CRZ-II:** Areas within municipal limits, subject to development regulations.
    *   **CRZ-III:** Areas that are relatively undisturbed, for public amenities and for defence purposes.
    *   **CRZ-IV:** Aquatic areas, including territorial waters, where certain activities are regulated.
    *   **Objective:** Regulate development activities in the coastal areas to protect the coastline and marine ecosystems.
*   **The Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, Exclusive Economic Zone and Other Maritime Zones Act, 1976:**
    *   Defines India's maritime zones and establishes India's sovereign rights over them.
    *   **Territorial Waters:** Extend up to 12 nautical miles from the baseline.
    *   **Contiguous Zone:** Extends up to 24 nautical miles from the baseline.
    *   **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** Extends up to 200 nautical miles from the baseline, where India has sovereign rights for exploration, exploitation, conservation, and management of natural resources (living and non-living) of the seabed, subsoil, and waters.
*   **The Coastal Management Authority:** Constituted by the Central Government to implement the CRZ Notification. State Coastal Zone Management Authorities (SCZMAs) also exist.
*   **Wildlife Protection Act, 1972:** Protects marine wildlife such as turtles, dolphins, and marine mammals.
*   **Fisheries Acts (Central and State):** Regulate fishing practices to prevent overfishing and promote sustainable fisheries.
*   **International Conventions:** India is a party to various international conventions related to marine pollution, such as MARPOL (International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships).

#### 4.3. Key Issues and Challenges

*   **Pollution from Land-Based Sources:** Industrial effluents, sewage, agricultural runoff entering the sea.
*   **Shipping Pollution:** Oil spills, discharge of waste from ships.
*   **Coastal Development:** Unregulated construction in CRZs, habitat destruction.
*   **Overfishing and Destructive Fishing Practices.**
*   **Impact of Climate Change:** Sea-level rise, ocean acidification, coral bleaching.

#### 4.4. Important Points to Remember

*   The CRZ Notification is a cornerstone of marine environmental protection in India.
*   India asserts sovereign rights over its EEZ for resource management and environmental protection.
*   A combination of domestic laws and adherence to international conventions is crucial for marine conservation.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Which Act primarily established the Central and State Pollution Control Boards in India?
**Answer:** The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974.

**Question 2:** Briefly explain the 'Precautionary Principle' in environmental law.
**Answer:** The Precautionary Principle states that where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, lack of full scientific certainty shall not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation.

**Question 3:** What is the significance of India's Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)?
**Answer:** India's EEZ extends up to 200 nautical miles from its baseline. Within this zone, India has sovereign rights for the exploration, exploitation, conservation, and management of natural resources, including the protection of the marine environment.

**Question 4:** What is the primary purpose of the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification?
**Answer:** The primary purpose of the CRZ Notification is to regulate developmental activities in the coastal areas to protect the sensitive coastal ecosystems, prevent pollution, and conserve natural resources.

**Question 5:** What is Public Interest Litigation (PIL) and how is it relevant to environmental law in India?
**Answer:** Public Interest Litigation (PIL) is a legal process that allows any public-spirited citizen or organization to approach the High Courts or Supreme Court to seek redressal for environmental issues affecting the public. It is highly relevant as it provides a mechanism to bring environmental violations and concerns to the notice of the judiciary, leading to judicial intervention and enforcement of environmental protection measures.

### Module Summary & Key Takeaways

*   **Pollution Control Boards (PCBs):** The backbone of pollution regulation in India, responsible for enforcing water and air quality standards, issuing consents, and monitoring environmental compliance.
*   **Groundwater:** A critical resource facing challenges of over-exploitation and pollution, requiring strict regulation through state-specific laws alongside general environmental legislation.
*   **Judicial Remedies:** A robust legal system with civil courts, criminal courts, the National Green Tribunal (NGT), and the mechanism of PIL provides various avenues for enforcing environmental protection and seeking redressal. Key principles like the Precautionary and Polluter Pays principles guide these actions.
*   **Marine Laws:** India's commitment to protecting its vast coastline and marine environment is reflected in laws like the EPA, CRZ Notification, and the Maritime Zones Act, aiming to balance development with conservation.

This module provides a foundational understanding of the legal and institutional mechanisms in place to manage and protect India's vital water and marine resources.
