# Environmental Law and Policy: Module 3 - Air, Water, and Marine Laws

## Topic: The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

This module delves into the critical legal framework governing water resources in India. Understanding the Water Act, 1974, is fundamental to appreciating the nation's efforts in preventing and controlling water pollution.

---

### **Learning Outcomes**

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   **Understand the historical context and objectives of the Water Act, 1974.**
*   **Identify the key institutions established under the Act and their respective roles.**
*   **Explain the mechanisms for preventing and controlling water pollution as outlined in the Act.**
*   **Describe the provisions related to sewage and trade effluents.**
*   **Discuss the powers and procedures for inspection, sampling, and analysis.**
*   **Analyze the penal provisions and penalties for violations of the Act.**
*   **Understand the enforcement and adjudication mechanisms under the Act.**
*   **Recognize the relationship between the Water Act, 1974, and other environmental laws.**

### **1. Introduction: Historical Context and Objectives**

*   **Background:**
    *   Rapid industrialization and urbanization in India led to increasing pollution of rivers, lakes, and other water bodies.
    *   Existing laws were fragmented and insufficient to address the complex issue of water pollution.
    *   India's commitment to international conventions and the need for a comprehensive legal framework.
*   **Enactment:** The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act was enacted in **1974**.
*   **Objectives:**
    *   To provide for the **prevention and control of water pollution**.
    *   To maintain or restore the **wholesomeness of water** for domestic, industrial, and other legitimate uses.
    *   To establish Boards for the prevention and control of water pollution.

---

### **2. Key Institutions Established Under the Act**

The Act establishes a two-tier structure for the administration of its provisions:

*   **Central Water Pollution Control Board (CWPCB):**
    *   **Establishment:** Constituted under Section 3 of the Act.
    *   **Composition:** Chairman, members representing government, scientists, and industrialists.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Advise the Central Government on matters relating to prevention and control of water pollution.
        *   Coordinate the activities of the State Boards.
        *   Lay down standards for sewage and trade effluents.
        *   Collect and disseminate information related to water pollution.
        *   Render technical assistance to State Boards.
        *   Organize mass media communication programs.
        *   Lay down standards for the quality of water.
        *   Plan and cause to be executed a nation-wide program for the prevention and control of water pollution.
*   **State Water Pollution Control Boards (SWPCBs):**
    *   **Establishment:** Constituted under Section 4 of the Act for each State.
    *   **Composition:** Similar to CWPCB, but with state-level representation.
    *   **Functions:**
        *   Implement the provisions of the Act in the State.
        *   Advise the State Government on matters related to water pollution.
        *   Lay down, modify, or annul effluents standards for sewage and trade effluents.
        *   Consent to Establish (CTE) and Consent to Operate (CTO) mechanism for industries discharging effluents.
        *   Inspect sewage or trade effluents, works, and plants.
        *   Collect and disseminate information related to water pollution in the State.
        *   Promote research and development in water pollution control.
        *   Undertake investigations and surveys of water pollution.

---

### **3. Prevention and Control of Water Pollution**

The Act employs several mechanisms to prevent and control water pollution:

*   **Prohibition of Discharge of Polluting Matter:**
    *   **Section 24:** Prohibits the discharge of any poisonous, noxious, or polluting matter into any stream or well or sewer or on land in contravention of the prescribed standards.
    *   **Key Concept:** **Stream:** Includes any river, watercourse (whether flowing or not whether perennial or not), lake or pond, whether natural or artificial, or any subterranean water.
*   **Filing of Complaints:**
    *   **Section 49:** Any person aggrieved by an order made by a State Board under the Act can file a complaint with the State Government or Central Government or any other authority authorized in this behalf.
*   **Power to Take Measures to Prevent or Mitigate Pollution:**
    *   **Section 18:** Authorizes the State Board to take measures for the prevention, control, or abatement of water pollution, including disconnecting any supply of water or power.
*   **Power to Give Notice to Close, Occupy or Regulate Certain Works or Undertakings:**
    *   **Section 32:** If any industrial plant, etc., is causing or is likely to cause water pollution, the State Board can issue a notice to the owner to discontinue the operation or take specific steps.
*   **Power to Give Directions:**
    *   **Section 19:** The State Board can issue directions to any person to take measures to prevent or control water pollution or to take specific action.
*   **Standards for Water Quality:**
    *   The CWPCB and SWPCBs are empowered to lay down standards for the quality of water in streams or wells, which are crucial for determining the extent of pollution.

---

### **4. Sewage and Trade Effluents**

*   **Definition of Sewage:**
    *   Includes human excreta, drainage, and all other substances which pollute any stream or well.
*   **Definition of Trade Effluent:**
    *   Includes any liquid, gaseous, or solid substance which is discharged from any industrial or commercial establishment or any operation or process carried out therein or any waste matter or contained in any such liquid, gaseous or solid substance.
*   **Restrictions on the Use of Streams and Sewers:**
    *   **Section 25:** No person shall, without the previous consent of the State Board, establish or take any steps to establish any industry, operation or process, or any treatment and disposal system or any extension or addition thereto, which is likely to discharge sewage or trade effluent into a stream or well or sewer or on land.
    *   **Section 26:** No person shall, without the previous consent of the State Board, **continue to discharge any sewage or trade effluent into any stream or well or sewer or on land** after the expiry of the period of the existing consent.
*   **Grant of Consent:**
    *   State Boards have the power to grant or refuse consent, and may impose conditions for consent, such as treatment of effluents to meet prescribed standards.
    *   **Consent to Establish (CTE):** Granted before setting up an industry or expanding an existing one.
    *   **Consent to Operate (CTO):** Granted after the industry is set up and effluent treatment plants are operational, allowing discharge within specified limits.

---

### **5. Inspection, Sampling, and Analysis**

*   **Powers of Entry and Inspection:**
    *   **Section 20:** Any person authorized by a State Board may, **after giving reasonable notice** to the owner or occupier, enter any land or building or premises.
    *   **Purpose:** To inspect any work or plant or appliance for the treatment of sewage or trade effluents, or for the purpose of inspecting any records, registers, documents, or any other material object relevant to the enforcement of the Act.
*   **Powers of Sampling:**
    *   **Section 21:** Any person authorized by a State Board can take samples of any **sewage or trade effluent** which is being discharged into a stream or well or sewer or on land.
    *   **Procedure for Sampling:**
        *   The person taking the sample must give notice to the person in charge of the source of discharge.
        *   The sample is divided into two parts, sealed, and signed by both parties.
        *   One part is sent to the State Board's laboratory for analysis, and the other is retained by the person in charge.
*   **Analysis of Samples:**
    *   The State Board's laboratory analyzes the samples.
    *   The report of the analysis is sent to the person in charge.
    *   The report of the analysis is **prima facie evidence** of the facts stated therein.

---

### **6. Penal Provisions and Penalties**

The Act prescribes stringent penalties for violations:

*   **Contravention of Section 24 (Prohibition of Discharge):**
    *   **Section 41:** Imprisons for a term which shall not be less than **one year and six months** but which may extend to **six years and with fine**.
    *   **Continuing Offence:** For every day during which the offence continues, an additional fine which may extend to **five thousand rupees**.
*   **Contravention of Sections 25 and 26 (Consent Provisions):**
    *   **Section 41 (2):** Imprisons for a term which shall not be less than **one year and six months** but which may extend to **six years and with fine**.
    *   **Continuing Offence:** For every day during which the offence continues, an additional fine which may extend to **five thousand rupees**.
*   **Contravention of Directions:**
    *   **Section 42:** Imprisons for a term which shall not be less than **one year and six months** but which may extend to **six years and with fine**.
    *   **Continuing Offence:** For every day during which the offence continues, an additional fine which may extend to **five thousand rupees**.
*   **Penalties for Public Servants:**
    *   **Section 43:** Penalizes public servants for intentionally obstructing any person in the discharge of duties or willfully neglecting their duties.
*   **Offences by Companies:**
    *   **Section 47:** If an offence is committed by a company, every person who, at the time of the offence, was in charge of, and responsible to, the company for the conduct of the business of the company, as well as the company, shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence.

---

### **7. Enforcement and Adjudication**

*   **Role of State Boards:** The primary responsibility for enforcement lies with the State Pollution Control Boards.
*   **Cognizance of Offences:**
    *   **Section 48:** No court shall take cognizance of any offence under the Act except on a complaint made by the State Board or any officer authorized by it.
*   **Appeals:**
    *   **Section 28:** Any person aggrieved by an order made by the State Board under Sections 25 or 26 can, within **thirty days** from the date of the order, prefer an appeal to the State Government or any authority specified by the State Government.
    *   The decision of the appellate authority is final.

---

### **8. Relationship with Other Environmental Laws**

The Water Act, 1974, is a cornerstone of environmental protection in India and interacts with other significant legislation:

*   **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986:**
    *   The Environment Act, 1986, is an umbrella legislation that provides a broader framework for environmental protection.
    *   The Water Act's provisions are often complemented and reinforced by the powers vested under the Environment Act, particularly in setting more stringent standards or taking preventive measures in case of grave environmental emergencies.
*   **The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981:**
    *   Both Acts establish similar institutional structures (Central and State Boards) and pollution control mechanisms.
    *   The Boards established under the Air Act, 1981, are also designated as the State Pollution Control Boards under the Water Act, 1974, creating synergy in their functioning.
*   **The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977:**
    *   This Act is complementary to the Water Act, 1974.
    *   It levies a cess on water consumed by certain industries and local authorities. The revenue generated from this cess is used to meet the expenses of the State Boards in carrying out their functions under the Water Act, 1974.

---

### **9. Important Points to Remember**

*   **The Water Act, 1974, is the primary legislation for preventing and controlling water pollution in India.**
*   **The Act establishes a two-tier system of Central and State Pollution Control Boards.**
*   **Consent to Establish (CTE) and Consent to Operate (CTO) are crucial mechanisms for industrial regulation.**
*   **Strict penalties are prescribed for discharging pollutants without consent or in contravention of standards.**
*   **The Act grants powers of entry, inspection, and sampling to authorized personnel.**
*   **The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977, provides funding for the State Boards.**
*   **The Act is to be read in conjunction with the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, and the Air Act, 1981.**

### **10. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:** What are the primary objectives of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974?
**Answer:** The primary objectives are to prevent and control water pollution and to maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water for various uses.

**Question 2:** Explain the difference between Consent to Establish (CTE) and Consent to Operate (CTO) under the Water Act, 1974.
**Answer:** CTE is obtained before setting up or expanding an industry, while CTO is obtained after the industry is operational and its effluent treatment plant is functioning, allowing for discharge within permissible limits.

**Question 3:** What are the powers of a State Pollution Control Board regarding the inspection and sampling of effluents?
**Answer:** Authorized officers can enter premises with prior notice, inspect works and plants, and take samples of effluents, following a specific procedure of dividing and sealing the samples.

**Question 4:** What are the penalties prescribed for the contravention of Section 24 of the Water Act, 1974 (Prohibition of Discharge)?
**Answer:** Imprisonment for a term of not less than one year and six months but extending up to six years, with a fine. For continuing offences, a daily fine of up to ₹5,000 is applicable.

**Question 5:** How does the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977, support the implementation of the Water Act, 1974?
**Answer:** The Cess Act levies a tax on water consumption by industries, and the revenue generated is used to fund the activities of the State Pollution Control Boards in their efforts to prevent and control water pollution.
