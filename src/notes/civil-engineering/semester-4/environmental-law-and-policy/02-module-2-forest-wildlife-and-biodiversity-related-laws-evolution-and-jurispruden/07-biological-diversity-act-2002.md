# Environmental Law and Policy: Module 2 - Forest, Wildlife and Biodiversity Laws

## Topic: Biological Diversity Act, 2002

### 1. Introduction and Background

*   **Context:** The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (BD Act) was enacted in India to provide for the conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and knowledge associated with them.
*   **International Mandate:** The Act is a consequence of India's ratification of the **Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)** in 1994. The CBD aims to conserve biodiversity, use its components sustainably, and ensure fair and equitable sharing of benefits from the utilization of genetic resources.
*   **Evolution:** The BD Act, 2002, marked a significant shift from earlier conservation efforts that were primarily focused on species protection. It introduced a rights-based approach concerning access to and benefit-sharing of biological resources.

### 2. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Biological Diversity:** This refers to the variety of life on Earth at all its levels, from genes to ecosystems, and includes diversity within species (genetic diversity), between species (species diversity), and of ecosystems (ecosystem diversity).
    *   **Definition under the Act:** Section 2(1) (b) "biological diversity" means the variability between living organisms from all sources including, inter alia, terrestrial, marine and other aquatic ecosystems and the ecological complexes of which they are part; this includes diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems.

*   **Biological Resources:** This includes organisms, species, populations, genetic resources, and any other biotic component with actual or potential use or value.
    *   **Definition under the Act:** Section 2(1) (c) "biological resources" means plants, animals and micro-organisms, or parts or derivatives thereof, and includes— (i) by the constitution of India or by any other law for the time being in force, which are meant for human consumption or which have pharmaceutical, nutritional, radiological, ethnobotanical or any other use; or (ii) by virtue of derivation from such organisms includes— (a) substances of biological origin, metabolic products, or by-products of biological processes; or (b) such other biological materials or substances which can be ingrediants of, or are found in, the composition of, or are the products of, biological resource; or (c) viruses, whether infective or not, bacteria, fungi, all types of flora, fauna, and their genetic material, lichens, algae, microbes, viruses, plants and animals and their parts or derivatives, etc.

*   **Genetic Resources:** Genetic material of actual or potential value.
    *   **Definition under the Act:** Section 2(1) (e) "genetic resources" means genetic material of actual or potential value.

*   **Genetic Material:** Material of heredity.
    *   **Definition under the Act:** Section 2(1) (f) "genetic material" means material of heredity.

*   **Traditional Knowledge:** Knowledge, innovations and practices of indigenous and local communities embodying traditional lifestyles relevant to the conservation and sustainable use of biological diversity.
    *   **Definition under the Act:** Section 2(1) (k) "traditional knowledge" means knowledge, innovations and practices of indigenous and local communities embodying traditional lifestyles relevant to the conservation and sustainable use of biological diversity.

*   **Benefit Sharing:** The fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge.
    *   **Definition under the Act:** Section 2(1) (a) "access" means to obtain biological resources for the purpose of research, or for commercial utilization, or for bio-survey and bio-utilization; (refer to Section 3 for access regulation)

*   **Endangered Species:** Species facing a very high risk of extinction in the wild.
    *   *(While not explicitly defined as a separate term in the Act for its core provisions, the concept of endangered species is implicitly covered under the conservation mandate of the Act and is a key focus of related wildlife laws that the BD Act complements).*

*   **Exotic Species:** Species introduced from outside the country.
    *   **Definition under the Act:** Section 2(1) (h) "exotic species" means plants, animals and micro-organisms which do not occur, or are not occurring naturally in India and includes their species.

*   **Germplasm:** Genetic material of a plant or animal.

### 3. Objectives of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002

*   **Conservation of Biological Diversity:** To protect and conserve the rich biodiversity of India.
*   **Sustainable Use:** To ensure that the use of biological resources is sustainable and does not deplete them for future generations.
*   **Equitable Benefit Sharing:** To ensure fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge with the communities and individuals who have conserved, used, or developed them.
*   **Regulation of Access:** To regulate access to biological resources and associated traditional knowledge by foreign nationals and Indian companies for commercial purposes.
*   **Protection of Traditional Knowledge:** To protect the traditional knowledge of indigenous and local communities related to biodiversity.
*   **Conservation of Wild Species and Cultivated Varieties:** To promote the conservation of wild species and cultivated varieties of plants and animals.

### 4. Key Provisions and Mechanisms

The Act establishes a three-tiered institutional framework for its implementation:

#### 4.1. National Level: National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)

*   **Establishment:** Constituted under Section 8 of the Act.
*   **Composition:** Chairman and not more than six Members appointed by the Central Government.
*   **Functions and Powers:**
    *   **Granting Approvals/Permissions:** (Section 19) Approves requests for commercial utilization of biological resources and associated knowledge by foreign nationals and NRIs.
    *   **Regulating Third Party Rights:** (Section 20) Approves requests for transferring results of research related to biological resources to third parties.
    *   **Approving Agreements:** (Section 21) Approves agreements for bio-survey and bio-utilization of biological resources.
    *   **Monetary Fund:** (Section 22) Approves payment of monetary terms and conditions for granting approvals.
    *   **National Register of Indian Biodiversity:** (Section 41) Maintains a register of Indian biological diversity.
    *   **Advisory Role:** Advises the Central Government on matters relating to conservation, sustainable use, and benefit sharing.
    *   **Investigating and Preventing Unauthorized Access:** Takes measures to prevent unauthorized access to biological resources.
    *   **Developing Guidelines:** Develops guidelines for Indian individuals, companies, and other entities for obtaining approvals from State Biodiversity Boards.
    *   **Penalties:** Imposes penalties for contravention of the Act's provisions.

#### 4.2. State Level: State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs)

*   **Establishment:** Constituted by the State Governments under Section 22A of the Act.
*   **Functions and Powers:**
    *   **Granting Approvals/Permissions:** (Section 24) Approves requests for commercial utilization of biological resources and associated knowledge by Indian citizens, organisations, and companies.
    *   **Regulating Third Party Rights:** (Section 26) Approves requests for transferring results of research related to biological resources to third parties.
    *   **Developing Guidelines:** Develops guidelines for local bodies for performing their functions.
    *   **Advisory Role:** Advises State Governments on matters relating to conservation, sustainable use, and benefit sharing.
    *   **Maintaining Registers:** Maintains registers of biological diversity and its constituents.
    *   **Assisting NBA and Local Bodies:** Assists the NBA and local bodies in carrying out their functions.

#### 4.3. Local Level: Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)

*   **Establishment:** Constituted by the State Governments at the local level (Panchayats and Municipalities) under Section 41 of the Act.
*   **Composition:** Members elected from amongst the local community, including representatives of indigenous and local communities.
*   **Functions and Powers:**
    *   **Register of Knowledge:** (Section 41(2)) Prepare and maintain a register of biological diversity and knowledge associated with biological resources occurring within their jurisdiction. This is a crucial aspect of the Act, empowering local communities.
    *   **Approvals for Access:** Grants prior approval for access to biological resources or knowledge associated with them by individuals, entities, or organizations other than local residents.
    *   **Benefit Sharing:** Ensures that benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge are shared with the community.
    *   **Conservation and Promotion:** Undertakes conservation and promotion of biological diversity.
    *   **Reporting:** Reports to the SBB on matters within their jurisdiction.

#### 4.4. Regulation of Access to Biological Resources

*   **Section 3:** Prohibits any person, except with the previous sanction of the Central Government, to obtain any biological resource for commercial purposes or to conduct bio-survey or bio-utilization of any biological resource.
*   **Section 4:** Prohibits any person, except with the previous sanction of the State Government, to obtain any biological resource for commercial purposes or to conduct bio-survey or bio-utilization of any biological resource within the territorial jurisdiction of a State.
*   **Section 5:** Prohibits any Indian citizen, statutory or registered company or any other organisation, except with the previous sanction of the State Government concerned, to obtain any biological resource for commercial purposes or to conduct bio-survey or bio-utilization of any biological resource.

#### 4.5. Protection of Traditional Knowledge

*   **Section 6:** Prohibits any person from acquiring or seeking to acquire knowledge, resources, or technologies related to biological diversity from indigenous and local communities without their prior approval or permission.
*   **Section 7:** Prohibits any person from commercializing or utilizing any biological resource or knowledge, which may be patented or which may be discovered or obtained after the commencement of this Act, without the previous sanction of the State Government.

#### 4.6. Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)

*   The Act mandates that when biological resources and associated knowledge are accessed for commercial purposes, benefits should be shared.
*   **Types of Benefits:**
    *   **Monetary Benefits:** Royalties, license fees, lump sum payments, joint ventures, etc.
    *   **Non-Monetary Benefits:** Joint research, technology transfer, capacity building, access to ex-situ conservation facilities, benefit sharing from products developed, etc.
*   The quantum and nature of benefit sharing are decided on a case-by-case basis, taking into account factors like the resource accessed, the knowledge associated, the benefit to be derived, and the contribution of the local community.

#### 4.7. Important Provisions for Protection

*   **Section 39:** Penalties for contravention of provisions related to access to biological resources and knowledge.
*   **Section 55:** Power to compound offences.
*   **Section 56:** Cognizance of offences.

### 5. Salient Features of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002

*   **Comprehensive Approach:** Covers conservation, sustainable use, and benefit sharing.
*   **Decentralized Implementation:** Establishes a three-tiered structure (NBA, SBBs, BMCs).
*   **Empowerment of Local Communities:** BMCs play a crucial role in managing local biodiversity and knowledge.
*   **Prior Informed Consent (PIC):** Required for accessing biological resources and associated knowledge.
*   **Protection of Traditional Knowledge:** Safeguards the intellectual property rights of communities.
*   **Regulatory Mechanism:** Regulates access to biological resources for commercial purposes.
*   **Benefit Sharing:** Ensures equitable sharing of benefits from the use of biodiversity.

### 6. Challenges and Criticisms

*   **Implementation Gaps:** Delays in setting up SBBs and BMCs, and lack of adequate funding and capacity building for BMCs.
*   **Defining "Commercial Utilization":** Ambiguity in defining what constitutes "commercial utilization" can lead to misinterpretations.
*   **Register of Knowledge:** The process of preparing and maintaining the Register of Knowledge by BMCs is complex and faces challenges in documenting traditional knowledge effectively.
*   **Benefit Sharing Mechanisms:** While the Act provides for benefit sharing, the practical modalities and the quantum of benefits can be contentious.
*   **Overlap with Other Laws:** Potential overlaps and conflicts with other environmental and forest laws.
*   **"Biopiracy" Concerns:** Despite the Act, concerns about biopiracy persist.
*   **Lack of Awareness:** Limited awareness about the Act among the general public and even some stakeholders.

### 7. Amendments and Rules

*   The **Biological Diversity Rules, 2004** provide the procedural framework for the implementation of the Act.
*   **Biological Diversity (Amendment) Bill, 2021** (though not yet enacted as an Act as of my last update, it indicates ongoing efforts to address implementation challenges) proposed several changes, including:
    *   Strengthening the role of BMCs.
    *   Clarifying benefit-sharing mechanisms.
    *   Addressing traditional knowledge documentation.
    *   Introducing provisions for facilitating access while ensuring compliance.

### 8. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Briefly explain the primary objectives of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

**Answer 1:** The primary objectives of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, are:
*   Conservation of biological diversity.
*   Sustainable use of its components.
*   Equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge.
*   Regulation of access to biological resources and associated knowledge.
*   Protection of traditional knowledge of indigenous and local communities.

**Question 2:** Describe the role and responsibilities of the Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

**Answer 2:** Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) are established at the local level. Their key roles and responsibilities include:
*   Preparing and maintaining a register of biological diversity and associated traditional knowledge within their jurisdiction.
*   Granting prior approval for access to biological resources or associated knowledge by non-local residents.
*   Ensuring equitable sharing of benefits from the use of these resources and knowledge with the local community.
*   Undertaking conservation and promotion of biological diversity.
*   Reporting to the State Biodiversity Boards.

**Question 3:** What is the significance of "prior informed consent" (PIC) in the context of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002?

**Answer 3:** Prior Informed Consent (PIC) is a fundamental principle of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002. It means that any individual, entity, or organization (especially foreign nationals or companies) seeking to access biological resources or associated traditional knowledge for commercial purposes must obtain explicit permission *before* accessing them. This is crucial for ensuring that access is regulated and that benefit-sharing arrangements are established beforehand.

**Question 4:** Discuss the challenges faced in the implementation of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

**Answer 4:** Key challenges in implementing the BD Act include:
*   Delays in establishing State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) and Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs).
*   Inadequate funding and capacity building for BMCs.
*   Ambiguity in defining "commercial utilization."
*   Difficulties in accurately documenting and protecting traditional knowledge in registers.
*   Practical challenges in implementing equitable benefit-sharing mechanisms.
*   Lack of widespread awareness about the Act's provisions.

**Question 5 (Scenario-based):** A pharmaceutical company from the United States wants to conduct research on certain medicinal plants found in a tribal area of Odisha to develop a new drug. They plan to collect samples and conduct laboratory analysis. What are the legal requirements under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, for this company to proceed with their research?

**Answer 5:**
1.  **Prior Sanction:** The US company, being a foreign national/entity, needs to obtain the **previous sanction of the Central Government** (through the National Biodiversity Authority - NBA) under Section 3 of the Act for accessing biological resources for commercial purposes or for bio-survey/bio-utilization.
2.  **PIC from Local Community:** Before approaching the NBA, the company would ideally need to engage with the local community in Odisha. The **Biodiversity Management Committee (BMC)** of that area, if established, would be the authority to grant approval and negotiate terms for access to biological resources and associated traditional knowledge. Their consent is paramount.
3.  **Benefit Sharing Agreement:** The company would need to enter into a benefit-sharing agreement, which could include monetary and non-monetary benefits, with the local community and potentially the State Government, as determined by the NBA and SBB.
4.  **Approval from SBB:** The State Biodiversity Board (SBB) of Odisha will also have a role in overseeing and approving such accesses within its jurisdiction, especially in consultation with the BMC.
5.  **Reporting:** The company must comply with any reporting requirements stipulated by the NBA, SBB, or BMC.

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   The BD Act, 2002, is India's primary legislation for implementing the CBD.
*   It establishes a three-tier structure: NBA, SBBs, and BMCs.
*   The Act emphasizes **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** and **Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)**.
*   **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)** are crucial for local-level implementation and protection of traditional knowledge.
*   The **Register of Knowledge** maintained by BMCs is a key tool for protecting traditional knowledge.
*   Any access to biological resources for commercial purposes requires explicit permission from the relevant authorities (Central/State Government, NBA, SBB, and BMC).
*   The Act aims to ensure that the benefits derived from biodiversity are shared equitably with the communities who conserve it.
*   Challenges in implementation remain, particularly concerning the capacity of BMCs and the clarity of benefit-sharing mechanisms.
