# Environmental Law and Policy: Module 2 - Forest, Wildlife and Biodiversity Related Laws

## Topic: Forest Rights Act, 2006

### 1. Introduction and Historical Context

The Forest Rights Act, 2006 (Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006) is a landmark piece of legislation in India aimed at rectifying historical injustices against forest-dwelling communities. It recognizes the rights of Scheduled Tribes (STs) and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFDs) to forests and forest land, which they have traditionally occupied and used for their livelihood.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Historical Injustice:** Before the Forest Rights Act, forest dwellers were often treated as encroachers and criminals under colonial forest laws, despite their long-standing dependence on forests.
*   **Colonial Forest Laws:** Laws like the Indian Forest Act, 1927, were primarily designed for resource extraction and conservation, often disregarding the rights and needs of local communities.
*   **Need for Recognition:** The Act was enacted to bring justice and equity to forest-dependent populations by formally acknowledging their rights.

### 2. Objectives of the Forest Rights Act, 2006

The Act has several core objectives:

*   **Recognition of Forest Rights:** To recognize and vest in Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers the rights which they are, or heretofore were, in possession of or have been otherwise accustomed to using in forests for purposes of habitation, livelihood, cultivation, grazing, or any other purpose.
*   **Protection of Forest Ecosystems:** To ensure that forest conservation is achieved through the active participation of the communities who depend on them, promoting sustainable use and management.
*   **Empowerment of Forest-Dwelling Communities:** To empower these communities by giving them a say in forest management and conservation.
*   **Rectification of Historical Wrongs:** To address the historical dispossession and marginalization of these communities.
*   **Sustainable Livelihoods:** To secure the livelihoods of these communities by ensuring their access to forest resources.

### 3. Definitions and Key Terms

Understanding the definitions within the Act is crucial:

*   **Forest Rights:**
    *   **Community Forest Resource Rights (CFRs):** The right to access, manage, and conserve community forest resources. This is a crucial right that allows communities to manage their local forests.
    *   **Habitat Rights:** The right to live in and use forest resources for habitation by forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers.
    *   **Individual Forest Rights:** The right to ownership and possession of land that has been cultivated by individuals or their ancestors prior to December 13, 2005. This includes rights to minor forest produce (MFP).
    *   **Right to Forest Produce:** The right to collect, use, consume, or sell minor forest produce.
*   **Forest Land:** Land identified as forest by the State Government, regardless of ownership or legal status.
*   **Minor Forest Produce (MFP):** All non-timber forest products such as bamboos, brushwood, fodder, leaves, flowers, fruits, roots, medicinal plants, honey, wax, etc.
*   **Scheduled Tribes (STs):** Communities notified as such under Article 342 of the Constitution.
*   **Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFDs):** Any member or community who has been residing in forest for not less than three consecutive years prior to December 13, 2005, and for whom forests and the lands within them have formed the principal means of livelihood.
*   **Grama Sabha:** The village assembly consisting of all adult members of the village.
*   **Forest Rights Committee (FRC):** A committee constituted by the Gram Sabha to facilitate the process of determination of forest rights.
