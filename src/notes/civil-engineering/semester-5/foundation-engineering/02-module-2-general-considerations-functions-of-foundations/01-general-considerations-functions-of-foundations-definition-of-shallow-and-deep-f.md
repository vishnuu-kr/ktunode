# FOUNDATION ENGINEERING - Module 2: General Considerations: Functions of Foundations

## 2.1 Introduction to Foundations

A foundation is the lowest part of a structure that transmits the loads from the superstructure to the underlying soil or rock strata. It acts as an interface between the building and the ground.

**Key Concept:** The primary purpose of a foundation is to ensure the stability and safety of the structure by adequately supporting and transferring all applied loads to the soil.

## 2.2 Functions of Foundations

Foundations perform several crucial functions for a structure:

*   **Load Transmission:**
    *   Transmit all vertical loads (dead loads, live loads, snow loads) from the superstructure to the soil.
    *   Transmit lateral loads (wind loads, seismic loads) to the soil.
    *   Transmit moment loads (due to eccentric loading or lateral forces) to the soil.

*   **Settlement Control:**
    *   Minimize differential settlements (uneven sinking of different parts of the structure), which can cause cracking and structural damage.
    *   Ensure that total settlements are within acceptable limits for the particular structure.

*   **Stability Enhancement:**
    *   Provide a stable base against overturning, sliding, and uplift forces.
    *   Anchor the structure to the ground, preventing it from moving due to external forces.

*   **Protection of Superstructure:**
    *   Protect the superstructure from damage caused by soil movements (e.g., frost heave, expansive soils).
    *   Provide a level base for construction.

**Important Point to Remember:** A well-designed foundation is crucial for the longevity and serviceability of any structure. Failure of a foundation can lead to catastrophic failure of the entire building.

## 2.3 Classification of Foundations

Foundations are broadly classified based on their depth and the manner in which they transfer loads to the soil. The primary classification is into **Shallow Foundations** and **Deep Foundations**.

### 2.3.1 Shallow Foundations

**Definition:** Shallow foundations are those that transmit structural loads to the soil at a relatively shallow depth below the finished ground level. There isn't a precise definition based on depth alone, but a general rule of thumb is that the *depth of embedment (Df)* is less than or equal to the *least width of the foundation (B)*.

**Df / B ≤ 1**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Shallow Depth:** The soil strata capable of supporting the loads are encountered close to the ground surface.
*   **Bearing Capacity:** The foundation relies primarily on the bearing capacity of the soil directly beneath it.
*   **Settlement:** Settlement is often a critical design consideration for shallow foundations.

**Types of Shallow Foundations:**

*   **Spread Footings (Isolated Footings):**
    *   **Description:** Individual footings supporting single columns or isolated loads. They spread the load over a wider area of soil.
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Wall Footings (Strip Footings):** Support continuous walls.
        *   **Isolated Column Footings:** Square, rectangular, or circular footings supporting single columns.
        *   **Combined Footings:** Support two or more columns, often used when column spacing is close or when a footing cannot be placed symmetrically under a single column.
        *   **Strap Footings:** Similar to combined footings but connect an eccentrically loaded footing to an interior footing with a "strap" beam.
        *   **Mat or Raft Foundations:** Large, continuous slabs that cover the entire area beneath a structure or a significant portion of it, supporting multiple columns. Used when soil bearing capacity is low or when column loads are heavy.

*   **Examples of Shallow Foundations:**
    *   A typical house with individual concrete pads under each load-bearing wall or column.
    *   A commercial building with spread footings supporting the perimeter walls and interior columns.
    *   A multi-story building on a raft foundation due to weak soil conditions at shallow depths.

**Important Point to Remember:** Shallow foundations are generally more economical than deep foundations when suitable soil conditions are available near the surface.

### 2.3.2 Deep Foundations

**Definition:** Deep foundations are those that transmit structural loads to the soil at greater depths than shallow foundations. This is typically done when the shallow soil strata are not strong enough to support the loads or when excessive settlements are expected. The *depth of embedment (Df)* is significantly greater than the *least width of the foundation (B)*.

**Df / B > 1**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Deeper Soil Strata:** Loads are transferred to stronger and stiffer soil or rock layers located at significant depths.
*   **Load Transfer Mechanisms:** Deep foundations transfer loads through two primary mechanisms:
    *   **End Bearing:** The load is transferred through the tip of the foundation element to the strong stratum at the bottom.
    *   **Skin Friction (Shaft Resistance):** The load is transferred through friction between the soil and the side surface of the foundation element along its length.

*   **Settlement:** Settlements are generally less critical for deep foundations compared to shallow foundations, as they often rest on firm ground. However, settlements can still occur and need to be considered in design.

**Types of Deep Foundations:**

*   **Piles:**
    *   **Description:** Long, slender structural elements made of concrete, steel, or timber, driven or cast in place into the ground. They are typically installed in groups.
    *   **Categorization by Material:**
        *   **Concrete Piles:** Precast or cast-in-situ.
        *   **Steel Piles:** H-piles, pipe piles, sheet piles.
        *   **Timber Piles:** Used for lighter loads and less aggressive soil conditions.
    *   **Categorization by Installation Method:**
        *   **Driven Piles:** Hammered into the ground (e.g., precast concrete, steel piles).
        *   **Bored Piles (Drilled Shafts/Caissons):** A hole is drilled, reinforcement is placed, and concrete is poured.
    *   **Categorization by Load Transfer:**
        *   **End-Bearing Piles:** Primarily transfer load through the tip to a firm stratum.
        *   **Friction Piles:** Primarily transfer load through skin friction along the shaft.
        *   **Compaction Piles:** Driven into granular soils to compact them and increase bearing capacity.

*   **Piers and Caissons:**
    *   **Description:** Larger diameter than piles, often constructed by excavating the soil within a pre-formed casing or shell, which is then filled with concrete. They are typically used for heavier loads or to resist significant lateral forces.
    *   **Caissons:** Often used for bridge foundations or structures requiring very large, robust foundations. They are typically sunk to a considerable depth.

*   **Examples of Deep Foundations:**
    *   Tall buildings, skyscrapers, and bridges often utilize piles or drilled shafts to transfer loads to bedrock or competent soil layers deep below the surface.
    *   Offshore platforms supported by piles driven deep into the seabed.
    *   Foundations in areas with very soft clay or loose sand where shallow foundations would lead to excessive settlement or bearing failure.

**Important Point to Remember:** Deep foundations are chosen when shallow soil conditions are inadequate. They are generally more expensive but provide superior load-carrying capacity and stability.

## 2.4 Selection Criteria for Foundation Types

The choice between shallow and deep foundations (and specific types within each category) depends on several factors:

*   **Soil Properties:**
    *   **Bearing Capacity:** The ability of the soil to support the applied loads without shear failure.
    *   **Settlement Characteristics:** The magnitude and pattern of settlement the soil will undergo under load.
    *   **Stratification:** The layering of different soil types and their properties with depth.
    *   **Presence of Groundwater:** Affects excavation and soil behavior.
    *   **Special Soil Conditions:** Expansive soils, collapsible soils, peat, etc.

*   **Magnitude and Type of Loads:**
    *   Heavy vertical loads may necessitate deep foundations.
    *   Significant lateral loads or moments may influence the choice.

*   **Economic Considerations:**
    *   Shallow foundations are generally more economical if suitable.
    *   The cost of excavation, materials, and installation varies significantly.

*   **Environmental and Site Conditions:**
    *   Proximity to existing structures.
    *   Noise and vibration restrictions during construction.
    *   Availability of construction equipment.
    *   Water table depth.

*   **Building Requirements:**
    *   Type of superstructure.
    *   Desired performance (e.g., settlement tolerance).

## 2.5 Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Define a shallow foundation and state the general criterion used to classify it based on its dimensions.

**Answer 1:** A shallow foundation is a foundation that transmits structural loads to the soil at a relatively shallow depth below the finished ground level. The general criterion is that the depth of embedment (Df) is less than or equal to the least width of the foundation (B), i.e., Df / B ≤ 1.

**Question 2:** List three common types of shallow foundations and briefly describe one of them.

**Answer 2:** Three common types of shallow foundations are:
1.  Spread Footings (Isolated, Combined, Strap, Mat/Raft)
2.  Wall Footings (Strip Footings)
3.  Mat or Raft Foundations

    **Description of Spread Footings:** Spread footings are individual foundations that spread the load from a column or wall over a wider area of soil. They can be square, rectangular, or circular depending on the shape of the supported element.

**Question 3:** Define a deep foundation and explain the two primary mechanisms by which it transfers loads to the soil.

**Answer 3:** A deep foundation is a foundation that transmits structural loads to the soil at greater depths, typically when shallow soil strata are inadequate. The two primary mechanisms for load transfer are:
1.  **End Bearing:** Load is transferred through the tip of the foundation element to a strong stratum at the bottom.
2.  **Skin Friction (Shaft Resistance):** Load is transferred through friction between the soil and the side surface of the foundation element along its length.

**Question 4:** Differentiate between a pile and a pier in the context of deep foundations.

**Answer 4:** Piles are long, slender structural elements (typically with a diameter/width less than 0.5m) installed into the ground to transfer loads. Piers (or drilled shafts/caissons) are larger diameter foundations (typically greater than 0.5m) that are often constructed by excavating a large diameter hole and filling it with concrete. Piers are generally used for heavier loads than piles.

**Question 5:** Under what soil conditions would you typically consider using a deep foundation instead of a shallow foundation?

**Answer 5:** You would typically consider using a deep foundation when:
*   The shallow soil layers have low bearing capacity.
*   There is a high risk of excessive settlement (total or differential) with shallow foundations.
*   The shallow soil layers are highly compressible or problematic (e.g., expansive clays, peat, loose sands).
*   Stronger soil or rock strata are located at significant depths.
*   Scour (erosion) is a concern at shallow depths (e.g., bridge foundations in rivers).

## 2.6 Key Points to Remember

*   Foundations are critical for the stability, safety, and serviceability of structures.
*   The primary functions of foundations include load transmission, settlement control, and stability enhancement.
*   Foundations are broadly classified into shallow and deep foundations based on their depth of embedment relative to their width.
*   **Shallow Foundations:** Df / B ≤ 1. Rely on bearing capacity of shallow soil. Examples: spread footings, raft foundations.
*   **Deep Foundations:** Df / B > 1. Transfer loads to deeper, stronger soil or rock strata through end bearing and/or skin friction. Examples: piles, piers, caissons.
*   The selection of a foundation type depends on soil properties, load magnitudes, economic factors, and site conditions.
