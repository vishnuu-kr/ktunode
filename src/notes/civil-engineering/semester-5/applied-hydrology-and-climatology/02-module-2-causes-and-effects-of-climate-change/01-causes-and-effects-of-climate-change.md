# Applied Hydrology and Climatology: Module 2 - Causes and Effects of Climate Change

## Topic: Causes and Effects of Climate Change

---

### **1. Introduction to Climate Change**

*   **Definition:** Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns. These shifts may be natural, but since the 1800s, human activities have been the main driver of climate change, primarily due to the burning of fossil fuels (like coal, oil, and gas), which produces heat-trapping gases.
*   **Distinction from Weather:**
    *   **Weather:** The short-term atmospheric conditions at a specific place and time (e.g., rain today, sunny tomorrow).
    *   **Climate:** The average weather patterns over a long period (typically 30 years or more) for a particular region.
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Greenhouse Effect:** The natural process where certain gases in the Earth's atmosphere trap heat from the sun, warming the planet. This is essential for life as we know it, keeping the Earth warmer than it would otherwise be.
    *   **Enhanced Greenhouse Effect:** The intensification of the natural greenhouse effect due to increased concentrations of greenhouse gases from human activities.

---

### **2. Causes of Climate Change**

#### **2.1 Natural Causes**

*   **Volcanic Eruptions:** Release greenhouse gases (like CO2) and aerosols (tiny particles) into the atmosphere. While CO2 contributes to warming, aerosols can reflect sunlight, causing temporary cooling.
    *   *Example:* The eruption of Mount Pinatubo in 1991 caused a noticeable global cooling for a couple of years due to sulfur dioxide aerosols reflecting sunlight.
*   **Variations in Earth's Orbit (Milankovitch Cycles):** Long-term changes in Earth's orbit, axial tilt, and precession affect the amount and distribution of solar radiation received. These cycles operate over tens of thousands of years and are responsible for ice ages.
    *   *Example:* The Earth's orbit is not perfectly circular; it changes from nearly circular to more elliptical over about 100,000 years.
*   **Solar Variability:** Changes in the sun's energy output can influence Earth's climate. However, these variations are relatively small and do not explain the current rapid warming trend.
    *   *Example:* Sunspot cycles, which are periods of increased or decreased solar activity.
*   **Ocean Currents:** Natural variations in ocean currents can redistribute heat around the globe, leading to regional climate shifts.
    *   *Example:* El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) events, which involve changes in sea surface temperatures in the Pacific Ocean and have widespread impacts on global weather.

#### **2.2 Anthropogenic (Human-Caused) Causes**

*   **Burning of Fossil Fuels:** The primary driver of current climate change. Combustion of coal, oil, and natural gas for energy production, transportation, and industry releases large amounts of greenhouse gases.
    *   **Key Greenhouse Gases (GHGs):**
        *   **Carbon Dioxide (CO2):** Released from burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes. It is the most significant long-lived GHG.
        *   **Methane (CH4):** Released from natural gas leaks, agriculture (livestock, rice paddies), landfills, and biomass burning. Methane is a more potent GHG than CO2 over shorter time scales.
        *   **Nitrous Oxide (N2O):** Released from agricultural practices (fertilizer use), industrial processes, and burning fossil fuels.
        *   **Fluorinated Gases (e.g., HFCs, PFCs, SF6):** Synthetic gases used in refrigerants, aerosols, and industrial processes. They are very potent GHGs and have very long atmospheric lifetimes.
*   **Deforestation:** Trees absorb CO2 from the atmosphere during photosynthesis. When forests are cleared or burned, this stored carbon is released back into the atmosphere as CO2, and the capacity of the planet to absorb CO2 is reduced.
    *   *Example:* Clearing rainforests for agriculture or logging.
*   **Industrial Processes:** Cement production, chemical manufacturing, and other industrial activities release GHGs directly or indirectly.
    *   *Example:* The calcination process in cement manufacturing releases CO2.
*   **Agriculture:** Livestock farming (enteric fermentation and manure management) produces methane. Rice cultivation in flooded paddies also releases methane. Fertilizer use contributes to nitrous oxide emissions.
*   **Land-Use Change:** Altering land surfaces (e.g., urbanization, draining wetlands) can affect local and regional climate by changing albedo (reflectivity) and evapotranspiration rates.

**Important Point to Remember:** While natural factors influence climate, the current rate and magnitude of warming are overwhelmingly attributed to human activities and the resulting increase in GHG concentrations.

### **3. Effects of Climate Change**

#### **3.1 Environmental Effects**

*   **Rising Global Temperatures:** Average global temperatures have increased significantly since the pre-industrial era. This leads to more frequent and intense heatwaves.
    *   *Example:* Record-breaking temperatures experienced in various regions during recent summers.
*   **Changes in Precipitation Patterns:** Some regions are experiencing more intense rainfall and flooding, while others face increased drought and water scarcity.
    *   *Example:* Increased frequency of extreme rainfall events in some parts of Europe and North America, while parts of the Sahel region in Africa experience prolonged droughts.
*   **Sea Level Rise:** Caused by two main factors:
    *   **Thermal Expansion:** As ocean water warms, it expands in volume.
    *   **Melting Glaciers and Ice Sheets:** Water from melting glaciers and ice sheets (Greenland, Antarctica) flows into the oceans.
    *   *Example:* Coastal communities facing increased erosion, saltwater intrusion into freshwater sources, and more frequent flooding during high tides.
*   **Ocean Acidification:** The ocean absorbs a significant portion of atmospheric CO2. When CO2 dissolves in seawater, it forms carbonic acid, leading to a decrease in pH (increased acidity).
    *   *Example:* Harm to marine organisms with shells and skeletons made of calcium carbonate (e.g., corals, shellfish), impacting marine ecosystems and fisheries.
*   **Melting Glaciers and Ice Sheets:** Glaciers and polar ice caps are melting at an accelerated rate, contributing to sea level rise and affecting freshwater availability for communities that rely on glacial meltwater.
    *   *Example:* Retreat of glaciers in the Himalayas, impacting water supplies for millions in Asia.
*   **More Frequent and Intense Extreme Weather Events:**
    *   **Heatwaves:** Longer, hotter, and more frequent.
    *   **Droughts:** More severe and prolonged in some regions.
    *   **Floods:** Increased intensity of rainfall events leading to flooding.
    *   **Storms:** Potentially more intense hurricanes/typhoons and other storm systems.
    *   *Example:* Increased intensity of Hurricane Harvey in 2017, linked to warmer ocean temperatures.
*   **Impacts on Ecosystems and Biodiversity:**
    *   **Habitat Loss and Degradation:** Changing temperatures and precipitation patterns can make habitats unsuitable for species.
    *   **Species Migration and Extinction:** Species may be forced to move to more suitable climates, or face extinction if they cannot adapt or move.
    *   **Coral Bleaching:** Caused by rising ocean temperatures, leading to the expulsion of symbiotic algae and potential death of coral reefs.
    *   *Example:* Polar bears losing habitat due to melting Arctic sea ice; shifts in bird migration patterns.

#### **3.2 Hydrological Effects**

*   **Changes in Water Availability:**
    *   **Increased Evaporation:** Higher temperatures lead to increased evaporation from land and water surfaces, potentially reducing soil moisture and water in rivers and lakes.
    *   **Changes in Snowpack and Snowmelt:** Reduced snowpack in many regions means less water is available during spring and summer melt. Earlier snowmelt can lead to water shortages later in the season.
    *   **Increased Water Demand:** Higher temperatures often lead to increased demand for irrigation and cooling.
    *   *Example:* Reduced river flows in the western United States due to less snowpack.
*   **Increased Flood Risk:** More intense rainfall events can overwhelm drainage systems and lead to flash floods and riverine flooding.
*   **Increased Drought Risk:** Changes in precipitation patterns and increased evaporation can exacerbate drought conditions.
*   **Impacts on Water Quality:**
    *   **Increased Water Temperatures:** Can reduce dissolved oxygen levels, affecting aquatic life.
    *   **Increased Sedimentation and Pollutant Transport:** Intense rainfall can increase runoff, carrying pollutants and sediment into water bodies.
    *   **Saltwater Intrusion:** Sea level rise can push saltwater further inland into coastal aquifers and estuaries.
    *   *Example:* Algal blooms in warmer, more nutrient-rich waters.

#### **3.3 Socio-Economic Effects**

*   **Impacts on Agriculture and Food Security:**
    *   Changes in growing seasons, crop yields, increased pest outbreaks, and water scarcity can threaten food production.
    *   *Example:* Reduced wheat yields in regions experiencing prolonged heat and drought.
*   **Impacts on Human Health:**
    *   **Heat-related illnesses:** Increased heatwaves lead to more heatstroke and other heat-related health problems.
    *   **Spread of Vector-borne diseases:** Warmer temperatures can expand the geographical range of disease vectors like mosquitoes (e.g., malaria, dengue fever).
    *   **Waterborne diseases:** Flooding can contaminate water supplies, increasing the risk of waterborne diseases.
    *   **Air quality:** Increased frequency of wildfires and changes in air circulation can worsen air pollution.
*   **Displacement and Migration:** Sea level rise, extreme weather events, and resource scarcity can force people to migrate from their homes.
*   **Economic Impacts:**
    *   Damage to infrastructure from extreme weather.
    *   Losses in agriculture and fisheries.
    *   Increased costs for disaster relief and adaptation measures.
    *   Impacts on tourism and recreation.
*   **Water Conflicts:** Increased scarcity of water resources in some regions may lead to competition and conflict.

### **4. Addressing Climate Change (Mitigation and Adaptation)**

*   **Mitigation:** Actions taken to reduce GHG emissions or enhance GHG sinks (e.g., planting trees).
    *   **Transition to Renewable Energy:** Solar, wind, hydro, geothermal.
    *   **Energy Efficiency:** Improving energy use in homes, transport, and industry.
    *   **Sustainable Land Management:** Reforestation, afforestation, improved agricultural practices.
    *   **Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS):** Technologies to capture CO2 from industrial sources.
*   **Adaptation:** Adjusting to actual or expected future climate.
    *   **Water Management:** Improving water use efficiency, developing drought-resistant crops, building water storage infrastructure.
    *   **Coastal Protection:** Building sea walls, restoring wetlands.
    *   **Early Warning Systems:** For extreme weather events.
    *   **Resilient Infrastructure:** Designing infrastructure to withstand changing climate conditions.

**Question 1:** Differentiate between weather and climate. Provide an example of each.

**Question 2:** List the three most significant greenhouse gases caused by human activities and their primary sources.

**Question 3:** Explain the two main mechanisms contributing to sea level rise.

**Question 4:** Discuss one hydrological impact of climate change and provide a specific example of how it might affect a region.

**Question 5:** Briefly describe the difference between climate change mitigation and adaptation.

---

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

**Answer 1:**
*   **Weather** refers to the short-term atmospheric conditions at a specific place and time. *Example: It is raining and 15°C in London today.*
*   **Climate** refers to the average weather patterns over a long period (typically 30 years or more) for a particular region. *Example: The Mediterranean climate is characterized by hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters.*

**Answer 2:**
The three most significant greenhouse gases caused by human activities are:
1.  **Carbon Dioxide (CO2):** Primary sources include the burning of fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas) for energy, transportation, and industry, as well as deforestation.
2.  **Methane (CH4):** Primary sources include agriculture (livestock, rice cultivation), natural gas leaks, landfills, and biomass burning.
3.  **Nitrous Oxide (N2O):** Primary sources include agricultural practices (fertilizer use), industrial processes, and the burning of fossil fuels.

**Answer 3:**
The two main mechanisms contributing to sea level rise are:
1.  **Thermal Expansion:** As the Earth's oceans absorb heat from the atmosphere, the water expands in volume, leading to a rise in sea level.
2.  **Melting of Glaciers and Ice Sheets:** The increased melting of land-based ice, such as glaciers and the ice sheets of Greenland and Antarctica, adds water to the oceans, contributing to sea level rise.

**Answer 4:**
One hydrological impact of climate change is **changes in water availability due to altered snowpack and snowmelt.** In mountainous regions that rely on snowmelt for water supply during drier seasons, reduced snowpack (due to warmer winters) and earlier snowmelt can lead to water shortages in the summer and autumn. For example, regions dependent on the snowpack of the Rocky Mountains in the United States may face reduced water availability for irrigation and municipal use in later months.

**Answer 5:**
*   **Climate Change Mitigation** refers to actions taken to reduce the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, either by reducing emissions at the source or by increasing the capacity of natural or artificial sinks to absorb these gases. Examples include transitioning to renewable energy sources and reforestation.
*   **Climate Change Adaptation** refers to adjusting to actual or expected future climate conditions and their effects. It involves taking steps to manage the risks of climate change impacts, such as building flood defenses or developing drought-resistant crops.

---

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Anthropogenic GHGs are the primary driver of current climate change.**
*   **The greenhouse effect is natural; the enhanced greenhouse effect is human-caused.**
*   **Climate change has far-reaching environmental, hydrological, and socio-economic consequences.**
*   **Understanding the causes and effects is crucial for developing effective mitigation and adaptation strategies.**
*   **Water resources are particularly vulnerable to climate change impacts.**
