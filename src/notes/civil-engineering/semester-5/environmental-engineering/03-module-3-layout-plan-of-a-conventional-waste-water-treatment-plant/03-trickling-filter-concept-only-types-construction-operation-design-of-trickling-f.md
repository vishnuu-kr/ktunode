# Environmental Engineering: Module 3 - Layout Plan of a Conventional Wastewater Treatment Plant

## Topic: Trickling Filter (Concept, Types, Construction, Operation, Design)

---

### **1. Concept of Trickling Filter**

**Key Concept:** A trickling filter is a **fixed-film biological wastewater treatment process**. This means that the microorganisms responsible for treating the wastewater are attached to a fixed medium within the filter, rather than being suspended freely in the wastewater like in activated sludge processes.

**Definition:** A trickling filter is a circular or rectangular bed filled with a designated inert or specially engineered medium. Wastewater is applied intermittently or continuously to the top of the bed and allowed to trickle down through the medium. As the wastewater flows, a biological slime layer (biofilm) containing aerobic bacteria, protozoa, and other microorganisms develops on the surface of the medium. These microorganisms consume the organic pollutants in the wastewater, converting them into simpler, more stable compounds, and removing them from the water.

**How it Works (Simplified):**

1.  **Distribution:** Wastewater is distributed over the filter medium.
2.  **Biofilm Formation:** Microorganisms in the wastewater attach to the medium and grow, forming a biological slime layer (biofilm).
3.  **Pollutant Removal:** As wastewater trickles down, the biofilm consumes dissolved and suspended organic matter.
4.  **Oxygen Supply:** Air circulates through the void spaces in the filter medium, providing oxygen for the aerobic microorganisms.
5.  **Sloughing:** As the biofilm grows thicker, it can become detached (slough) from the medium, and these detached biomass particles are carried away with the treated wastewater.

**Purpose:** To remove dissolved and suspended organic matter and to reduce biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) from wastewater.

### **2. Types of Trickling Filters**

Trickling filters can be classified based on several factors, primarily the **type of medium** and the **depth of the filter**.

**A. Based on Medium Type:**

*   **Standard Rate Trickling Filters (SRTF):**
    *   **Medium:** Typically uses **crushed stone, gravel, or slag** as the filter medium.
    *   **Characteristics:** Less efficient, deeper, and larger in footprint compared to high-rate filters.
    *   **Depth:** Generally 6-10 feet (1.8-3 meters) deep.
    *   **Operation:** Operated at lower hydraulic and organic loading rates.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple construction, robust.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Prone to clogging, potential for odors and fly nuisance.

*   **High Rate Trickling Filters (HRTF):**
    *   **Medium:** Uses **manufactured media** such as plastic (e.g., corrugated sheets, random plastic packing) or specially shaped ceramic or concrete blocks. These media have high surface areas and excellent void spaces.
    *   **Characteristics:** More efficient, shallower, and smaller in footprint than SRTFs.
    *   **Depth:** Generally 15-40 feet (4.5-12 meters) deep (can be single or multi-stage).
    *   **Operation:** Operated at higher hydraulic and organic loading rates.
    *   **Advantages:** Higher BOD removal efficiency, less prone to clogging, reduced odor and fly nuisance due to better ventilation.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher initial cost due to manufactured media.

**B. Based on Depth/Configuration:**

*   **Shallow-Depth Filters:**
    *   Typically 1-3 feet deep.
    *   Often used in multi-stage systems for higher efficiency.

*   **Deep-Depth Filters:**
    *   Typically 6-10 feet deep (for SRTF).
    *   Can achieve significant BOD removal in a single pass.

**C. Based on Recirculation:**

*   **Recirculating Trickling Filters:** A portion of the settled effluent from the trickling filter is recirculated back to the influent to improve performance.
    *   **Types of Recirculation:**
        *   **Complete Recirculation:** All settled effluent is recirculated.
        *   **Partial Recirculation:** A portion of the settled effluent is recirculated.
        *   **Intermediate Recirculation:** Effluent from one filter stage is recirculated to the previous stage.
    *   **Benefits of Recirculation:**
        *   Improves BOD removal efficiency.
        *   Controls hydraulic loading.
        *   Prevents drying out of the filter medium.
        *   Reduces odor and fly nuisance.

### **3. Construction of a Trickling Filter**

Trickling filters are typically constructed as circular or rectangular beds.

**A. Circular Trickling Filters:**

*   **Structure:**
    *   **Circular Containment Wall:** Usually made of concrete, brick, or stone. Designed to withstand the pressure of the filter medium and wastewater.
    *   **Underdrain System:** Located at the bottom of the filter bed. Its primary functions are to:
        *   Support the filter medium.
        *   Collect the treated wastewater.
        *   Allow for ventilation.
        *   **Types of Underdrains:**
            *   **Slabs:** Precast concrete slabs with perforations.
            *   **Channels:** Open channels formed by blocks or concrete.
            *   **Perforated Pipes:** Laid in a network.
    *   **Filter Medium:** The material filling the bed.
        *   **Stone/Gravel:** Washed, durable, and of uniform size (typically 2-4 inches).
        *   **Manufactured Media:** Plastic or ceramic structures with high surface area and void space.
    *   **Distribution System:**
        *   **Rotary Distributor (for circular filters):** A rotating arm (or multiple arms) that receives wastewater and distributes it evenly over the filter bed by centrifugal force or gravity. Driven by the force of the effluent or by a motor.
        *   **Nozzles/Sprayers:** Attached to the distributor arms to ensure even application.

**B. Rectangular Trickling Filters:**

*   **Structure:** Similar to circular filters but with rectangular containment walls.
*   **Distribution System:**
    *   **Fixed Nozzles or Sprinklers:** Wastewater is pumped through a system of pipes with nozzles that spray it over the filter bed.
    *   **Moving Distributor:** A distribution arm that travels back and forth across the width of the filter.

**Key Construction Elements:**

*   **Medium Support:** The underdrain system must be strong enough to support the weight of the medium and the saturated wastewater.
*   **Ventilation:** Adequate void spaces within the medium and an open underdrain system are crucial for aerobic decomposition.
*   **Even Distribution:** The distribution system is vital for ensuring uniform contact between wastewater and the biofilm.

### **4. Operation of a Trickling Filter**

The effective operation of a trickling filter relies on careful control of various parameters.

**A. Wastewater Distribution:**

*   **Continuous vs. Intermittent Application:**
    *   **Continuous:** Wastewater is applied at a constant rate.
    *   **Intermittent:** Wastewater is applied in cycles. This can improve ventilation and prevent anaerobic conditions.
*   **Loading Rates:**
    *   **Hydraulic Loading Rate (HLR):** Volume of wastewater applied per unit area of filter surface per day (e.g., m³/m²/day or gal/acre/day).
    *   **Organic Loading Rate (OLR):** Amount of BOD applied per unit volume of filter medium per day (e.g., kg BOD/m³/day or lb BOD/1000 ft³/day).
*   **Recirculation Ratio:** The ratio of the recirculated flow to the incoming raw wastewater flow. A higher ratio generally leads to better performance.

**B. Biological Processes:**

*   **Biofilm Development:** The biofilm grows over time. Initially, it's thin and efficient. As it thickens, the outer layers can become anaerobic or slough off.
*   **Oxygen Transfer:** Aerobic conditions are maintained by natural ventilation through the filter medium. The rate of oxygen transfer depends on the void spaces in the medium and airflow.
*   **Sloughing:** The shedding of excess biomass from the medium. This is a natural process that prevents excessive clogging and maintains filter efficiency.

**C. Operational Challenges & Solutions:**

*   **Clogging:** Can occur if the loading rates are too high, the medium is too fine, or the wastewater contains a high concentration of suspended solids.
    *   **Solutions:** Reduce loading rates, use coarser or manufactured media, pre-treat wastewater, increase recirculation.
*   **Odor Production:** Can result from anaerobic conditions within the filter.
    *   **Solutions:** Increase recirculation, improve ventilation, use HRTF with better void spaces.
*   **Filter Flies:** Can emerge from filters, especially SRTFs.
    *   **Solutions:** Intermittent application of wastewater, wetting the surface of the filter to drown the larvae, using manufactured media.
*   **Pooling:** Wastewater collecting in areas, leading to anaerobic conditions.
    *   **Solutions:** Ensure proper distribution and underdrain design.

**D. Performance Monitoring:**

*   **BOD and COD Removal:** Measured in the influent and effluent.
*   **Volatile Suspended Solids (VSS):** Indicative of biofilm thickness.
*   **pH and Dissolved Oxygen:** Monitored to ensure aerobic conditions.

### **5. Design of a Trickling Filter**

The design of a trickling filter aims to achieve the desired level of wastewater treatment at an economical cost, considering various loading rates and operational parameters. Design is often based on empirical formulas and loading criteria.

**A. Key Design Parameters:**

*   **Influent Wastewater Characteristics:** Flow rate, BOD, suspended solids, temperature.
*   **Desired Effluent Quality:** Target BOD and suspended solids concentrations.
*   **Filter Medium:** Type, size, specific surface area, void fraction.
*   **Loading Rates:** Hydraulic and Organic.
*   **Recirculation Ratio.**
*   **Filter Depth.**

**B. Design Approaches:**

Design is typically an iterative process. Several methods exist, but the **National Research Council (NRC) formula** and the **Eckenfelder formula** are common. These formulas relate the BOD removal efficiency to various design parameters.

**1. NRC Formula (for single-stage trickling filters):**

This formula estimates the **overall BOD removal efficiency ($\%$)** and is widely used for SRTFs.

$E = \frac{100}{1 + 0.033 \sqrt{\frac{Q}{V_{e}}}}$

Where:
*   $E$ = Overall BOD removal efficiency (decimal)
*   $Q$ = Recirculated hydraulic loading rate (gal/day/ft²) or (m³/m²/day)
    *   $Q = (Q_{influent} + Q_{recirculated}) / A_{filter}$
*   $V_{e}$ = Effective volume of the filter medium (ft³) or (m³)

**Key points related to the NRC formula:**

*   It is more applicable to standard rate filters.
*   For high-rate filters with manufactured media, adjustments or different formulas are often used.
*   The formula implicitly accounts for recirculation.

**2. Eckenfelder Formula (for both SRTF and HRTF):**

This formula focuses on the **specific substrate removal rate** and is often used for high-rate filters with manufactured media.

$\frac{BOD_{removed}}{BOD_{applied}} = E = \frac{1}{1 + k_s \times \frac{W}{Q}}$

Where:
*   $E$ = BOD removal efficiency (decimal)
*   $k_s$ = Specific substrate removal rate constant (units depend on W and Q)
*   $W$ = Organic loading rate (lb BOD/day/1000 ft³ of media) or (kg BOD/day/m³ of media)
*   $Q$ = Hydraulic loading rate (gal/day/ft²) or (m³/m²/day)

**Simplified form often used:**

$E = \frac{1}{1 + k_s' \frac{F}{Q}}$

Where:
*   $k_s'$ = A temperature-dependent constant (related to the medium's surface area and voidage)
*   $F$ = Organic loading rate (lb BOD/day/ft²) or (kg BOD/day/m²)
*   $Q$ = Hydraulic loading rate (gal/day/ft²) or (m³/m²/day)

**Key points related to Eckenfelder formula:**

*   It accounts for the surface area of the media.
*   The constant $k_s'$ is influenced by the type of medium.

**C. Design Steps (General):**

1.  **Determine Influent Characteristics:** Flow (average, peak), BOD, BOD/COD ratio.
2.  **Establish Effluent Requirements:** Target BOD, TSS.
3.  **Select Filter Type:** SRTF vs. HRTF, medium type. This depends on space, cost, and desired performance.
4.  **Estimate Hydraulic Loading Rate (HLR):** Based on experience, regulations, or pilot studies.
5.  **Estimate Organic Loading Rate (OLR):** Based on influent BOD and filter volume.
6.  **Calculate Required Filter Volume:** Using appropriate design formulas (e.g., NRC, Eckenfelder) to achieve the target BOD removal efficiency.
7.  **Determine Filter Dimensions:**
    *   **Diameter (for circular filters):** $A_{filter} = \frac{Q_{total}}{HLR}$
    *   **Depth:** Based on the selected medium and efficiency requirements.
8.  **Select Medium:** Choose based on performance, cost, and void space for ventilation.
9.  **Design Distributor:** Ensure even application of wastewater. Rotary distributors are common for circular filters.
10. **Design Underdrains:** To support the medium, collect effluent, and provide ventilation.
11. **Determine Recirculation Ratio:** If used, calculate the required ratio to meet loading requirements and optimize performance.
12. **Consider Site Constraints:** Space availability, elevation differences.

**Example of Design Consideration:**

*   **Problem:** Design a trickling filter to treat a wastewater with an average flow of 1 MGD (million gallons per day) and an influent BOD of 200 mg/L to achieve an effluent BOD of 20 mg/L.
*   **Solution Process:**
    1.  **Target BOD Removal:** $(200 - 20) / 200 = 0.9$ or 90%.
    2.  **Choose Medium:** Let's assume plastic media with a high surface area.
    3.  **Estimate HLR:** For HRTF with plastic media, HLR might be in the range of 500-1000 gal/acre/day. Let's assume 750 gal/acre/day.
    4.  **Calculate Required Surface Area:** 1 MGD = 1,000,000 gal/day.
        Area (acres) = 1,000,000 gal/day / 750 gal/acre/day = 1333.3 acres.
        Convert to ft²: 1 acre = 43,560 ft². Area = 1333.3 * 43,560 = 58,076,868 ft².
    5.  **Estimate Filter Depth:** With plastic media, depths can be substantial (e.g., 20-40 ft). Let's assume a depth of 25 ft.
    6.  **Calculate Filter Volume:** Volume = Area * Depth. This would yield a very large volume, indicating that either the HLR assumption needs refinement, or a single filter might not be sufficient. Often, multiple filters are used, or a higher HLR is chosen.
    7.  **Use Eckenfelder:** Calculate the required $k_s'$ or select a suitable value based on the chosen medium and temperature, and then determine the filter volume that satisfies the efficiency requirement. The calculation would involve applying the formula iteratively.

**Important Note:** Actual design involves detailed calculations using appropriate constants and considering safety factors. This example is illustrative.

### **6. Practice Questions & Answers**

**Question 1:** What is the primary function of the biological slime layer (biofilm) in a trickling filter?
    *   A. To support the filter medium.
    *   B. To break down organic pollutants.
    *   C. To distribute wastewater.
    *   D. To provide oxygen.

**Answer:** B. To break down organic pollutants.

**Question 2:** What is a significant advantage of using manufactured media in high-rate trickling filters compared to crushed stone?
    *   A. Lower initial cost.
    *   B. Higher surface area and better void spaces.
    *   C. Greater susceptibility to clogging.
    *   D. Reduced ventilation.

**Answer:** B. Higher surface area and better void spaces.

**Question 3:** Explain the purpose of the underdrain system in a trickling filter.

**Answer:** The underdrain system supports the filter medium, collects the treated wastewater, and facilitates ventilation within the filter bed.

**Question 4:** What are the two primary loading rates that are critical in the design and operation of trickling filters?

**Answer:** Hydraulic Loading Rate (HLR) and Organic Loading Rate (OLR).

**Question 5:** A trickling filter is designed to achieve 90% BOD removal. If the influent BOD is 250 mg/L, what is the expected effluent BOD concentration?

**Answer:** Effluent BOD = Influent BOD * (1 - Removal Efficiency) = 250 mg/L * (1 - 0.90) = 250 mg/L * 0.10 = 25 mg/L.

**Question 6:** Briefly describe the concept of "sloughing" in a trickling filter.

**Answer:** Sloughing is the shedding of excess biomass (biofilm) from the filter medium. This is a natural process that prevents excessive clogging and maintains the filter's efficiency.

### **7. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Fixed-Film Process:** Trickling filters are fixed-film biological treatment systems, meaning microorganisms are attached to a medium.
*   **Aerobic Treatment:** They rely on aerobic bacteria for pollutant degradation, requiring adequate oxygen supply.
*   **Biofilm is Key:** The efficiency of a trickling filter is directly related to the health and thickness of the biofilm.
*   **Types of Media:** Standard rate (stone/gravel) vs. High rate (manufactured media). HRTF generally offers higher efficiency and smaller footprint.
*   **Loading Rates Matter:** Proper control of Hydraulic and Organic Loading Rates is crucial for optimal performance and to avoid operational problems.
*   **Recirculation:** Often used to improve efficiency, control loading, and prevent issues like odors and clogging.
*   **Ventilation is Essential:** Adequate airflow through the filter medium is vital for aerobic conditions.
*   **Design Formulas:** NRC and Eckenfelder are common tools for sizing and designing trickling filters based on performance criteria.
*   **Underdrains:** Crucial for effluent collection and ventilation.
*   **Distributor:** Essential for even wastewater application over the filter surface.
