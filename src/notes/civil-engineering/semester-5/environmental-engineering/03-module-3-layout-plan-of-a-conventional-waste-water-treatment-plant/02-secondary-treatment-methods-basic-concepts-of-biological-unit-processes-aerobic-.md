# Environmental Engineering: Module 3 - Layout Plan of a Conventional Wastewater Treatment Plant

## Topic: Secondary Treatment Methods - Basic Concepts of Biological Unit Processes

This module delves into the crucial stage of secondary treatment, where biological processes are employed to remove dissolved and suspended organic matter that wasn't eliminated during primary treatment. We will focus on the fundamental concepts behind these biological unit processes, categorizing them based on the role of oxygen (aerobic vs. anaerobic) and the nature of microbial growth (attached vs. suspended).

---

### Learning Outcomes:

*   **Understand the purpose and necessity of secondary treatment in wastewater treatment plants.**
*   **Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic biological processes.**
*   **Explain the core principles of attached growth biological processes.**
*   **Explain the core principles of suspended growth biological processes.**
*   **Identify key differences and applications of various biological treatment methods.**

---

### 1. Purpose and Necessity of Secondary Treatment

**Why is Secondary Treatment Necessary?**

*   **Removal of Dissolved and Colloidal Organic Matter:** Primary treatment primarily removes settleable solids. However, a significant portion of organic pollutants remains dissolved or as fine colloids in the wastewater.
*   **Reduction of Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD):** Organic matter in wastewater is consumed by microorganisms, leading to oxygen depletion in receiving water bodies (rivers, lakes). Secondary treatment aims to significantly reduce BOD, preventing eutrophication and harm to aquatic life.
*   **Improvement of Water Quality for Discharge:** The effluent from secondary treatment is significantly cleaner and safer for discharge into the environment or for further tertiary treatment.
*   **Nutrient Removal (in some advanced secondary processes):** While not the primary focus of basic secondary treatment, some methods can contribute to nitrogen and phosphorus removal.

**Key Concept:** **Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD):** The amount of dissolved oxygen needed by aerobic biological organisms to break down organic material present in a given water sample at a certain temperature over a specific time period. It's a key indicator of organic pollution.

### 2. Aerobic vs. Anaerobic Biological Processes

Biological treatment relies on microorganisms to break down organic pollutants. The key difference lies in the requirement of oxygen for these microorganisms.

#### 2.1 Aerobic Processes

*   **Definition:** Biological processes that occur in the presence of dissolved oxygen. Aerobic microorganisms utilize organic matter as their food source and oxygen as their electron acceptor for respiration, converting organic compounds into carbon dioxide, water, and new microbial cells.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Require a continuous supply of oxygen.
    *   High efficiency in removing organic matter.
    *   Produce less odor compared to anaerobic processes.
    *   Generally faster reaction rates.
    *   Microbial population consists of bacteria, protozoa, and some fungi.
*   **General Reaction (Simplified):**
    Organic Matter + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + New Cells + Energy
*   **Examples of Aerobic Processes:**
    *   Activated Sludge Process (Suspended Growth)
    *   Trickling Filters (Attached Growth)
    *   Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs) (Attached Growth)
    *   Membrane Bioreactors (MBRs) (Suspended Growth with Membrane Separation)

#### 2.2 Anaerobic Processes

*   **Definition:** Biological processes that occur in the absence of dissolved oxygen. Anaerobic microorganisms break down organic matter through a series of complex steps (hydrolysis, acidogenesis, acetogenesis, methanogenesis) in an oxygen-free environment.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   Do not require external oxygen supply (oxygen is achieved by eliminating it from the system).
    *   Lower efficiency in removing organic matter compared to aerobic processes for the same volume/residence time.
    *   Produce biogas (primarily methane and carbon dioxide), which can be a valuable energy source.
    *   Can produce odors if not properly managed.
    *   Slower reaction rates.
    *   Microbial population consists of various groups of bacteria and archaea.
*   **General Reaction (Simplified - for methane production):**
    Organic Matter → CH₄ + CO₂ + New Cells
*   **Examples of Anaerobic Processes:**
    *   Anaerobic Digesters (typically used for sludge stabilization, but can be used for high-strength industrial wastewater)
    *   Upflow Anaerobic Sludge Blanket (UASB) Reactors
    *   Anaerobic Filters

**Important Point to Remember:** Aerobic processes are more common for municipal wastewater secondary treatment due to their higher efficiency and faster kinetics, while anaerobic processes are often employed for industrial wastewater with high organic loads or for sludge treatment.

### 3. Attached Growth Processes

In attached growth processes, microorganisms are cultivated on a fixed surface, forming a biological film or slime layer. Wastewater flows over this biofilm, and the organic pollutants are adsorbed and degraded by the attached microorganisms.

#### 3.1 Basic Concepts

*   **Biofilm:** A community of microorganisms attached to a surface, embedded in a self-produced matrix of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS).
*   **Surface Area:** The key design parameter is the surface area provided for microbial attachment. More surface area generally means more microbial biomass and higher treatment capacity.
*   **Nutrient and Oxygen Transfer:** The efficiency of nutrient and oxygen transfer from the bulk liquid to the microorganisms within the biofilm is crucial.
*   **Shear Stress:** The wastewater flow over the biofilm can exert shear forces that help to slough off old, inactive biomass and expose younger, more active microorganisms.
*   **Process Types:**
    *   **Intermittent or Continuous Flow:** Wastewater can be applied intermittently or continuously over the media.
    *   **Recirculation:** Often used to maintain moisture and improve oxygen transfer.

#### 3.2 Examples of Attached Growth Processes

*   **Trickling Filters (TFs):**
    *   **Description:** A bed of inert media (e.g., rock, gravel, plastic) over which wastewater is trickled. Microorganisms grow as a biofilm on the media. Air diffuses through the media, providing oxygen.
    *   **Mechanism:** Wastewater is sprayed or distributed over the top of the filter. As it trickles down, organic matter is absorbed by the biofilm. Oxygen diffuses from the air within the voids of the media. Sloughed-off biomass is removed in a secondary clarifier.
    *   **Types:** High-rate, low-rate, intermediate-rate, roughing filters.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple to operate, resilient to shock loads, relatively low power consumption.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can be bulky, potential for odor and fly nuisance, media clogging issues.

*   **Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs):**
    *   **Description:** A series of large, circular discs mounted on a horizontal shaft. The discs are partially submerged in the wastewater. Microorganisms grow as a biofilm on the discs. The shaft rotates slowly, alternately exposing the biofilm to wastewater (for nutrient uptake) and air (for oxygen transfer).
    *   **Mechanism:** As the discs rotate, a thin film of wastewater adheres to the biofilm. Organic matter diffuses into the biofilm and is degraded. Rotation ensures sufficient oxygen supply and removes excess biomass.
    *   **Advantages:** Compact, low energy consumption, resilient to shock loads, minimal sludge production.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Susceptible to freezing, potential for mechanical issues with the shaft, can be susceptible to filamentous bacteria overgrowth.

*   **Biodiscs / Biological Rotating Discs:** Similar in principle to RBCs but often with more specialized disc designs and arrangements.

*   **Impingement Filters / Biofilters:** Wastewater is passed through a bed of media, and microorganisms form a biofilm.

*   **Moving Bed Biofilm Reactors (MBBRs):**
    *   **Description:** Wastewater flows through a tank containing small plastic carriers (media) that provide a large surface area for biofilm growth. These carriers are kept in constant suspension by aeration or mechanical mixing.
    *   **Mechanism:** Microorganisms attach to the carriers. The carriers move freely within the reactor, exposing the biofilm to both wastewater and oxygen. Excess biomass is sloughed off naturally.
    *   **Advantages:** Compact, handles shock loads well, less sensitive to sludge settling issues, can be retrofitted into existing tanks.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Potential for media clogging, requires carriers to be retained.

**Important Point to Remember:** Attached growth processes are generally more robust and less sensitive to variations in wastewater flow and organic load compared to suspended growth processes because the microbial community is anchored.

### 4. Suspended Growth Processes

In suspended growth processes, microorganisms are maintained in a dispersed or flocculent state within the wastewater, typically in an aeration tank. These processes require aeration to supply oxygen and mixing to keep the microorganisms in suspension and in contact with the organic pollutants.

#### 4.1 Basic Concepts

*   **Flocculation:** Microorganisms aggregate into flocs (clumps), which facilitates their separation from the treated water in a subsequent settling process.
*   **Aeration:** Essential for providing dissolved oxygen for aerobic respiration and for mixing the contents of the tank.
*   **Mixed Liquor:** The mixture of wastewater and microorganisms in the aeration tank.
*   **Sludge Retention Time (SRT):** A critical design parameter representing the average time that biomass remains in the system. A longer SRT favors the growth of slower-growing microorganisms and is crucial for nitrification.
*   **Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT):** The average time wastewater spends in the reactor.

#### 4.2 Examples of Suspended Growth Processes

*   **Activated Sludge Process (ASP):**
    *   **Description:** The most common suspended growth process. Wastewater is mixed with a recycled sludge (containing active microorganisms) in an aeration tank. After aeration, the mixture (mixed liquor) flows to a secondary clarifier, where the biomass settles out. A portion of the settled sludge (return activated sludge - RAS) is recycled to the aeration tank to maintain the microbial population, and the excess sludge (waste activated sludge - WAS) is removed for further treatment.
    *   **Mechanism:** Microorganisms in the activated sludge flocculate around organic particles and consume dissolved organic matter. Aeration provides oxygen and keeps the sludge suspended. Settling in the clarifier separates the treated effluent from the biomass.
    *   **Variations:** Conventional ASP, plug flow ASP, completely mixed ASP, step aeration, extended aeration.
    *   **Advantages:** High BOD removal efficiency, flexible in design and operation, relatively compact.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Sensitive to shock loads, requires efficient sludge settling, can be energy-intensive due to aeration, prone to bulking if filamentous bacteria dominate.

*   **Sequencing Batch Reactors (SBRs):**
    *   **Description:** A single tank is used for both aeration and settling. The treatment process occurs in a time-sequenced manner: fill, react (aeration), settle, decant (discharge effluent), and idle/sludge draw-off.
    *   **Mechanism:** Wastewater, mixed liquor, and sludge are held in the same tank throughout the different operational phases.
    *   **Advantages:** Can handle variable flow and load, good for intermittent operation, can achieve nutrient removal with specific cycles, compact.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires careful timing and automation, can be sensitive to influent characteristics during the settling phase.

*   **Membrane Bioreactors (MBRs):**
    *   **Description:** Combines activated sludge treatment with membrane filtration (microfiltration or ultrafiltration) instead of a secondary clarifier.
    *   **Mechanism:** Microorganisms are suspended in an aeration tank, and the mixed liquor is then pumped through membranes to separate the treated effluent from the biomass. The high biomass concentration achievable in MBRs leads to improved effluent quality and a smaller footprint.
    *   **Advantages:** Excellent effluent quality, compact footprint, robust process.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher capital and operational costs (membrane fouling and replacement), energy-intensive for membrane operation.

*   **Oxidation Ditches:**
    *   **Description:** A type of activated sludge process that uses an elongated, oval-shaped channel (ditch) with a rotor for aeration and mixing. Typically operated at longer SRTs.
    *   **Mechanism:** Similar to activated sludge, but the long channel and continuous aeration provide a stable environment for nitrification.
    *   **Advantages:** Stable nitrification, simpler operation than some ASP configurations, resilient to shock loads.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Larger footprint than conventional ASP.

**Important Point to Remember:** Suspended growth processes require careful control of operational parameters like dissolved oxygen, mixing, and sludge wasting to maintain a healthy and efficient microbial population.

### 5. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of secondary treatment in a wastewater treatment plant?
    a) Removal of grit and screenings
    b) Removal of dissolved and colloidal organic matter and reduction of BOD
    c) Removal of nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus
    d) Sterilization of the effluent

**Answer:** b) Removal of dissolved and colloidal organic matter and reduction of BOD

**Question 2:** In which type of biological process do microorganisms grow on a fixed surface?
    a) Suspended growth process
    b) Anaerobic process
    c) Attached growth process
    d) Aerobic process

**Answer:** c) Attached growth process

**Question 3:** Which of the following is a characteristic of aerobic processes?
    a) Production of biogas (methane)
    b) Absence of dissolved oxygen
    c) High oxygen demand by microorganisms
    d) Slower reaction rates compared to anaerobic processes

**Answer:** c) High oxygen demand by microorganisms

**Question 4:** The Activated Sludge Process is an example of which type of biological treatment?
    a) Attached growth process
    b) Suspended growth process
    c) Anaerobic process
    d) Tertiary treatment process

**Answer:** b) Suspended growth process

**Question 5:** Briefly explain the role of the return activated sludge (RAS) in the Activated Sludge Process.

**Answer:** The return activated sludge (RAS) is recycled from the secondary clarifier back to the aeration tank to maintain a sufficient concentration of active microorganisms in the system. This ensures that there are enough microbes available to effectively degrade the organic pollutants in the incoming wastewater.

---

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Secondary treatment follows primary treatment.** Its main goal is to remove dissolved and colloidal organic matter.
*   **Aerobic processes require oxygen**, while **anaerobic processes occur in the absence of oxygen.**
*   **Attached growth processes** involve microorganisms growing on a surface (media), while **suspended growth processes** keep microorganisms dispersed in the water.
*   **BOD reduction** is a key objective of secondary treatment.
*   **Key suspended growth processes** include Activated Sludge, SBRs, and MBRs.
*   **Key attached growth processes** include Trickling Filters and RBCs.
*   **SRT (Sludge Retention Time)** is a critical design and operational parameter for suspended growth processes, especially for nitrification.
*   Each biological process has its own advantages, disadvantages, and typical applications.
