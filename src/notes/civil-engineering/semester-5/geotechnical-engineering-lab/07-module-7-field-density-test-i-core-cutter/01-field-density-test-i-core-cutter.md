# Geotechnical Engineering Lab: Field Density Test – Core Cutter Method

## Module 7: Field Density Test

### Topic: Field Density Test – (i) Core Cutter

---

### **1. Introduction to Field Density Testing**

*   **Purpose:** To determine the *in-situ* (in its natural state) density of soil. This is crucial for assessing the compaction of soil in the field, which directly impacts its engineering properties like strength, compressibility, and permeability.
*   **Why field density is important:**
    *   **Compaction Control:** Ensuring that soil placed and compacted in embankments, road bases, etc., meets the specified density requirements for stability and performance.
    *   **Settlement Prediction:** Estimating potential settlements of structures built on the soil.
    *   **Bearing Capacity:** Assessing the soil's ability to support loads.
    *   **Permeability:** Understanding how easily water will flow through the soil.
*   **Limitations of Lab Testing:** Laboratory tests (like Proctor Compaction) provide *maximum dry density* achievable under controlled conditions. Field density testing confirms if this achieved density is met in the actual construction.

### **2. Core Cutter Method: Principles and Procedure**

This method is suitable for relatively soft, uncohesive, or lightly cohesive soils where a cylindrical cutter can be easily driven or pushed into the soil without significant disturbance.

#### **2.1 Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Bulk Density ($\rho_{bulk}$):** The total mass of the soil per unit volume, including both solids and voids (filled with water or air).
    *   $\rho_{bulk} = \frac{\text{Mass of wet soil}}{\text{Volume of soil sample}}$
*   **Dry Density ($\rho_{dry}$):** The mass of solid soil particles per unit volume of the soil mass. This is the most commonly used parameter for assessing compaction.
    *   $\rho_{dry} = \frac{\text{Mass of dry soil}}{\text{Volume of soil sample}}$
*   **Water Content ($w$):** The ratio of the mass of water to the mass of dry soil solids, usually expressed as a percentage.
    *   $w = \frac{\text{Mass of water}}{\text{Mass of dry soil}} \times 100\%$
*   **Core Cutter:** A cylindrical metal apparatus with a sharp cutting edge used to excavate a soil sample of known volume.
*   **In-situ Density:** The density of the soil in its natural, undisturbed state.

#### **2.2 Apparatus Required**

*   **Core Cutter:** A hollow cylinder (e.g., steel) with a specified internal diameter and height. Common dimensions are provided by standards (e.g., IS 2720 Part 29).
*   **Rammer/Mallet:** A heavy hammer used to drive the core cutter into the soil.
*   **Steel Plate:** A flat plate to place under the rammer for striking.
*   **Balances:** For weighing soil samples (e.g., 0.01g sensitivity).
*   **Oven:** For drying soil samples to determine water content.
*   **Trowel/Spatula:** For trimming the soil surface and removing excess soil.
*   **Screwdriver/Chisel:** To help lift the core cutter and soil.
*   **Brush:** For cleaning.
*   **Plastic Bags/Containers:** To store soil samples.

#### **2.3 Detailed Procedure**

1.  **Site Selection and Preparation:**
    *   Choose a representative area for testing.
    *   Level the surface of the soil to be tested using a trowel or other suitable tools. The leveled surface should be smooth and flat.

2.  **Determining the Volume of the Core Cutter:**
    *   Measure the internal diameter ($D$) and internal height ($H$) of the core cutter accurately.
    *   Calculate the volume ($V$) of the core cutter using the formula for a cylinder:
        $V = \pi \times (\frac{D}{2})^2 \times H$
    *   Record this volume. This is the volume of the soil sample to be extracted.

3.  **Excavating the Soil Sample:**
    *   Place the core cutter vertically on the prepared leveled surface.
    *   Using the rammer and steel plate, carefully strike the top of the core cutter to drive it into the soil.
    *   Continue driving until the cutter is filled with soil, ensuring the top of the soil is flush with or slightly below the top rim of the cutter.
    *   *Important:* Avoid excessive hammering that could compact the soil within the cutter.

4.  **Removing the Sample:**
    *   Carefully excavate the soil around the core cutter using a trowel or spatula.
    *   Gently lift the core cutter filled with the soil sample.
    *   *If the cutter is difficult to drive, this method might not be suitable, or manual pushing might be required.*

5.  **Trimming and Cleaning:**
    *   Remove any excess soil protruding from the top or bottom of the cutter using a trowel or spatula. The soil surface should be perfectly flush with the top and bottom rims of the cutter.
    *   Clean the exterior of the core cutter.

6.  **Weighing the Sample:**
    *   Weigh the core cutter filled with the soil sample accurately using the balance. Record this as the mass of the wet soil sample plus the core cutter ($M_{cutter+wet\_soil}$).
    *   Subtract the known mass of the empty core cutter to get the mass of the wet soil sample ($M_{wet\_soil}$).
    *   $M_{wet\_soil} = M_{cutter+wet\_soil} - M_{cutter}$

7.  **Determining Water Content:**
    *   Transfer the entire wet soil sample from the core cutter into a clean container or plastic bag.
    *   Take a representative sample (e.g., 10-20% of the total sample) from the bulked sample for moisture content determination.
    *   Determine the mass of the wet soil sample for moisture determination ($M_{wet\_moisture}$).
    *   Dry this sample in a oven at 105-110°C until a constant weight is achieved.
    *   Determine the mass of the dry soil sample for moisture determination ($M_{dry\_moisture}$).
    *   Calculate the water content ($w$) using the formula:
        $w = \frac{M_{wet\_moisture} - M_{dry\_moisture}}{M_{dry\_moisture}} \times 100\%$

8.  **Calculating Field Densities:**
    *   **Bulk Density ($\rho_{bulk}$):**
        $\rho_{bulk} = \frac{M_{wet\_soil}}{V}$
        (where $V$ is the volume of the core cutter calculated in step 2)
    *   **Dry Density ($\rho_{dry}$):**
        $\rho_{dry} = \frac{\rho_{bulk}}{1 + (w/100)}$
        Alternatively:
        $\rho_{dry} = \frac{M_{dry\_soil}}{V}$
        where $M_{dry\_soil} = M_{wet\_soil} / (1 + (w/100))$

### **3. Calculations and Reporting**

#### **3.1 Formulas Summary**

*   **Volume of Core Cutter ($V$):** $V = \pi \times (\frac{D}{2})^2 \times H$
    *   $D$: Internal diameter of the core cutter
    *   $H$: Internal height of the core cutter
*   **Mass of Wet Soil ($M_{wet\_soil}$):** $M_{wet\_soil} = (M_{cutter+wet\_soil} - M_{cutter})$
*   **Water Content ($w$):** $w = \frac{M_{wet\_moisture} - M_{dry\_moisture}}{M_{dry\_moisture}} \times 100\%$
*   **Bulk Density ($\rho_{bulk}$):** $\rho_{bulk} = \frac{M_{wet\_soil}}{V}$
*   **Dry Density ($\rho_{dry}$):** $\rho_{dry} = \frac{\rho_{bulk}}{1 + (w/100)}$

#### **3.2 Units**

*   Dimensions ($D, H$) are typically in cm.
*   Volume ($V$) will be in cm³.
*   Masses ($M$) are typically in grams (g).
*   Density ($\rho$) will be in g/cm³. This can be converted to kg/m³ by multiplying by 1000.

#### **3.3 Reporting Results**

The report should include:
*   Project name and location.
*   Date of testing.
*   Soil type being tested.
*   Dimensions of the core cutter used.
*   Calculated volume of the core cutter.
*   Field readings (mass of cutter + wet soil, mass of wet soil for moisture, mass of dry soil for moisture).
*   Calculated water content ($w$).
*   Calculated bulk density ($\rho_{bulk}$).
*   Calculated dry density ($\rho_{dry}$).
*   Comparison with the specified or desired dry density.
*   Conclusion on the degree of compaction.
*   Name of the technician and supervisor.

### **4. Advantages and Disadvantages of the Core Cutter Method**

#### **4.1 Advantages**

*   **Relatively Simple:** The procedure is straightforward and requires basic equipment.
*   **Suitable for Soft Soils:** Works well for soils that are not too hard or rocky, where driving a cutter is feasible.
*   **Provides a Relatively Undisturbed Sample:** If done carefully, it minimizes disturbance to the soil structure within the sample.
*   **Direct Measurement of Volume:** The volume of the sample is precisely known from the cutter's dimensions.

#### **4.2 Disadvantages**

*   **Limited to Soft Soils:** Not suitable for hard, compacted soils, stony soils, or granular soils with large particles. The cutter cannot be driven effectively, or sample disturbance will be significant.
*   **Difficulty in Driving:** In some soils, it can be very difficult to drive the cutter to the required depth.
*   **Potential for Soil Disturbance:** Even with care, some disturbance can occur during driving and extraction, especially around the edges.
*   **Surface Preparation is Crucial:** Inaccurate leveling of the surface will lead to errors in the sample volume.
*   **Requires Manual Effort:** Driving the cutter can be physically demanding.

### **5. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
You are performing a field density test using the core cutter method. The internal dimensions of the core cutter are: Diameter = 10.2 cm, Height = 11.6 cm. The mass of the empty core cutter is 850 g.
In the field, you filled the cutter with soil and weighed it. The total mass of the cutter filled with wet soil was 2750 g.
You took a sample from this wet soil for moisture content determination.
*   Mass of wet soil sample = 150 g
*   Mass of dry soil sample (after oven drying) = 120 g

Calculate:
a) The volume of the core cutter.
b) The mass of the wet soil sample obtained in the field.
c) The water content ($w$) of the soil.
d) The bulk density of the soil in g/cm³.
e) The dry density of the soil in g/cm³.

**Answer 1:**

*   **Given:**
    *   $D = 10.2$ cm
    *   $H = 11.6$ cm
    *   $M_{cutter} = 850$ g
    *   $M_{cutter+wet\_soil} = 2750$ g
    *   $M_{wet\_moisture} = 150$ g
    *   $M_{dry\_moisture} = 120$ g

*   **Calculations:**
    a) **Volume of core cutter ($V$):**
       $V = \pi \times (\frac{D}{2})^2 \times H$
       $V = \pi \times (\frac{10.2}{2})^2 \times 11.6$
       $V = \pi \times (5.1)^2 \times 11.6$
       $V = \pi \times 26.01 \times 11.6$
       $V \approx 3.14159 \times 26.01 \times 11.6 \approx 945.0$ cm³

    b) **Mass of wet soil sample ($M_{wet\_soil}$):**
       $M_{wet\_soil} = M_{cutter+wet\_soil} - M_{cutter}$
       $M_{wet\_soil} = 2750 - 850 = 1900$ g

    c) **Water content ($w$):**
       $w = \frac{M_{wet\_moisture} - M_{dry\_moisture}}{M_{dry\_moisture}} \times 100\%$
       $w = \frac{150 - 120}{120} \times 100\%$
       $w = \frac{30}{120} \times 100\% = 0.25 \times 100\% = 25\%$

    d) **Bulk density ($\rho_{bulk}$):**
       $\rho_{bulk} = \frac{M_{wet\_soil}}{V}$
       $\rho_{bulk} = \frac{1900 \text{ g}}{945.0 \text{ cm}^3} \approx 2.01$ g/cm³

    e) **Dry density ($\rho_{dry}$):**
       $\rho_{dry} = \frac{\rho_{bulk}}{1 + (w/100)}$
       $\rho_{dry} = \frac{2.01}{1 + (25/100)}$
       $\rho_{dry} = \frac{2.01}{1 + 0.25} = \frac{2.01}{1.25} \approx 1.61$ g/cm³

**Question 2:**
What type of soil is most suitable for the core cutter method? Briefly explain why.

**Answer 2:**
The core cutter method is most suitable for **soft, uncohesive, or lightly cohesive soils**. This is because these soils are soft enough for the cylindrical cutter to be driven or pushed into them with minimal disturbance. Hard, compacted, rocky, or granular soils with large particles are not suitable as the cutter cannot be driven effectively, or the sample obtained will be highly disturbed, leading to inaccurate density results.

### **6. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Accurate Measurement:** Precision in measuring the core cutter dimensions ($D, H$) and weights is paramount for accurate density calculations.
*   **Surface Preparation:** A perfectly leveled and smooth surface is essential to ensure the extracted sample has the correct volume.
*   **Minimize Disturbance:** Drive the core cutter carefully to avoid excessive compaction or loosening of the soil within the cutter.
*   **Representative Moisture Sample:** Ensure the sub-sample taken for moisture content determination is representative of the entire bulk sample.
*   **Suitability of Soil:** Always assess if the soil type is appropriate for the core cutter method. If the soil is too hard, stony, or coarse-grained, choose an alternative field density testing method (e.g., sand replacement method).
*   **Unit Consistency:** Ensure all units are consistent throughout the calculations (e.g., all dimensions in cm, all masses in g).
