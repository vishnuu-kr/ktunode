# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2): Module 5 - Tests on Coarse and Fine Aggregates

## Topic: Water Absorption, Bulk Density, Void Ratio, Porosity, and Specific Gravity of Aggregates

This module focuses on understanding and determining key physical properties of aggregates, which are fundamental components of concrete. These properties significantly influence the workability, strength, and durability of the final concrete mix.

---

Upon completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   Understand the significance of water absorption, bulk density, void ratio, porosity, and specific gravity of aggregates in concrete mix design.
*   Perform laboratory tests to determine these properties for both coarse and fine aggregates.

*   Apply the knowledge gained to practical scenarios in concrete technology.

### 1. Introduction to Aggregates and Their Properties

Aggregates are granular materials, typically sand, gravel, and crushed stone, that form the bulk of concrete. Their physical properties are crucial for:

*   **Workability:** How easily the concrete can be mixed, placed, and compacted.
*   **Strength:** The load-bearing capacity of the hardened concrete.
*   **Durability:** The ability of concrete to resist environmental factors like freezing, thawing, and chemical attack.
*   **Economy:** Aggregates are the cheapest component of concrete, so their properties influence the overall cost.

**Key Properties Covered:**

*   Water Absorption
*   Bulk Density
*   Void Ratio
*   Porosity
*   Specific Gravity (Apparent and Bulk)

---

### 2. Water Absorption of Aggregates

**Definition:** Water absorption is the amount of water that an aggregate can absorb when immersed in water for a specified period. It is usually expressed as a percentage of the dry weight of the aggregate.

**Significance:**

*   **Moisture Content:** Affects the actual water-cement ratio in the concrete mix. High absorption can lead to a "hungry" mix if not accounted for.
*   **Surface Texture:** Porous aggregates with rough surfaces tend to have higher absorption.
*   **Adhesion:** Affects the bond between the aggregate and the cement paste.

**Test Procedure (General - specific standards may vary):**

1.  **Preparation:** Obtain a representative sample of aggregate.
2.  **Drying:** Dry the aggregate in an oven at 100-110°C until it reaches a constant weight. This gives the **oven-dry weight ($W_{od}$)**.
3.  **Soaking:** Immerse the aggregate in water for 24 hours.
4.  **Surface Drying:** After soaking, remove the aggregate from water and gently roll it in a cloth to remove surface moisture, achieving the **saturated surface-dry (SSD) condition**. The weight at this stage is the **SSD weight ($W_{ssd}$)**.
5.  **Final Drying:** Dry the aggregate again in the oven to constant weight to confirm the oven-dry weight.

**Calculation:**

Water Absorption (%) = $\frac{W_{ssd} - W_{od}}{W_{od}} \times 100$

**Example:**

*   Oven-dry weight ($W_{od}$) = 500 g
*   SSD weight ($W_{ssd}$) = 515 g

Water Absorption (%) = $\frac{515 - 500}{500} \times 100 = \frac{15}{500} \times 100 = 3\%$

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   The SSD condition is crucial for accurate specific gravity determination.
*   Aggregates with high water absorption generally require more mixing water and can lead to higher shrinkage.

### 3. Bulk Density of Aggregates

**Definition:** Bulk density is the mass of aggregates per unit volume, including the voids between the particles. It is usually expressed in kg/m³ or g/cm³.

**Significance:**

*   **Volume Estimation:** Used to estimate the volume occupied by aggregates in a concrete mix.
*   **Mix Design:** Influences the overall density of the concrete.
*   **Batching:** Essential for volumetric batching of aggregates.

**Types:**

*   **Loose Bulk Density:** When aggregate is poured into a container without compaction.
*   **Tamped/Compacted Bulk Density:** When aggregate is compacted into a container in a standard manner.

**Test Procedure (General - specific standards may vary):**

1.  **Preparation:** Use a standard container of known volume (e.g., a 10-liter bucket).
2.  **Loose Bulk Density:** Fill the container loosely with the aggregate, strike off the excess with a straight edge. Weigh the container with the aggregate.
3.  **Tamped Bulk Density:** Fill the container in layers, compacting each layer with a standard tamping rod (e.g., 30 blows per layer). Strike off the excess. Weigh the container with the aggregate.

**Calculation:**

Bulk Density = $\frac{\text{Weight of Aggregate}}{\text{Volume of Container}}$

**Example:**

*   Container Volume = 10 liters = 0.01 m³
*   Weight of aggregate (loose) = 15 kg
*   Weight of aggregate (tamped) = 17 kg

Loose Bulk Density = $\frac{15 \text{ kg}}{0.01 \text{ m}^3} = 1500 \text{ kg/m}^3$
Tamped Bulk Density = $\frac{17 \text{ kg}}{0.01 \text{ m}^3} = 1700 \text{ kg/m}^3$

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Tamped bulk density is generally higher than loose bulk density due to reduced void space.
*   The shape and grading of aggregates influence their bulk density. Well-graded aggregates tend to have higher bulk densities.

### 4. Void Ratio and Porosity of Aggregates

These two properties are closely related and describe the amount of empty space within a given volume of aggregate.

#### 4.1 Void Ratio

**Definition:** Void ratio ($e$) is the ratio of the volume of voids to the volume of solid particles in a given volume of aggregate.

**Significance:**

*   **Paste Demand:** The amount of cement paste required to fill the voids. Higher void ratio means more paste is needed.
*   **Workability:** Affects the flow and cohesiveness of the concrete.
*   **Density:** Directly influences the bulk density.

**Calculation:**

Void Ratio ($e$) = $\frac{\text{Volume of Voids}}{\text{Volume of Solids}}$

We know that:
Volume of Bulk = Volume of Solids + Volume of Voids

So, Volume of Voids = Volume of Bulk - Volume of Solids

$e = \frac{V_{bulk} - V_{solids}}{V_{solids}}$

Alternatively, if we consider a unit weight of aggregate:
$V_{solids} = \frac{W_{od}}{G_s \times \rho_w}$
$V_{bulk} = \frac{W_{bulk}}{\rho_{bulk}}$ (where $W_{bulk}$ is the bulk weight and $\rho_{bulk}$ is the bulk density)

$e = \frac{V_{bulk}}{V_{solids}} - 1$

#### 4.2 Porosity (or Voids Percentage)

**Definition:** Porosity ($n$) is the ratio of the volume of voids to the total bulk volume of the aggregate, expressed as a percentage.

**Significance:**

*   **Water Demand:** Indicates the amount of water that can be absorbed by the aggregate.
*   **Paste Content:** Affects the proportion of cement paste in the concrete.

**Calculation:**

Porosity ($n$) (%) = $\frac{\text{Volume of Voids}}{\text{Volume of Bulk}} \times 100$

We know that:
Volume of Voids = Volume of Bulk - Volume of Solids

$n = \frac{V_{bulk} - V_{solids}}{V_{bulk}} \times 100 = \left(1 - \frac{V_{solids}}{V_{bulk}}\right) \times 100$

Since $\frac{V_{bulk}}{V_{solids}} = 1 + e$, then $\frac{V_{solids}}{V_{bulk}} = \frac{1}{1+e}$.

So, $n = \left(1 - \frac{1}{1+e}\right) \times 100 = \frac{e}{1+e} \times 100$

**Relationship between Void Ratio and Porosity:**

*   If Void Ratio ($e$) is known, Porosity ($n$) = $\frac{e}{1+e} \times 100$
*   If Porosity ($n$) is known, Void Ratio ($e$) = $\frac{n}{100-n}$

**Example (using previous bulk density and assuming specific gravity):**

Let's assume:
*   Tamped Bulk Density ($\rho_{bulk}$) = 1700 kg/m³
*   Specific Gravity of Aggregate ($G_s$) = 2.65 (assume this for calculation)
*   Density of water ($\rho_w$) = 1000 kg/m³

Volume of solids per unit bulk volume:
$V_{solids} = \frac{W_{od}}{G_s \times \rho_w}$
If we consider 1 m³ of bulk volume, then $W_{bulk} = 1700$ kg.
$V_{solids} = \frac{1700 \text{ kg}}{2.65 \times 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3} = 0.6415 \text{ m}^3$

Volume of Voids = $V_{bulk} - V_{solids} = 1 \text{ m}^3 - 0.6415 \text{ m}^3 = 0.3585 \text{ m}^3$

Void Ratio ($e$) = $\frac{\text{Volume of Voids}}{\text{Volume of Solids}} = \frac{0.3585}{0.6415} = 0.559$

Porosity ($n$) = $\frac{\text{Volume of Voids}}{\text{Volume of Bulk}} \times 100 = \frac{0.3585}{1} \times 100 = 35.85\%$

Using the relationship: $n = \frac{0.559}{1+0.559} \times 100 = \frac{0.559}{1.559} \times 100 = 35.86\%$ (slight difference due to rounding)

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Void ratio and porosity are inversely related to the bulk density and specific gravity.
*   Higher void ratio/porosity means more paste is needed to fill the voids, potentially affecting concrete strength and durability.

### 5. Specific Gravity of Aggregates

**Definition:** Specific gravity is the ratio of the density of the aggregate to the density of a reference substance, usually water, at a specified temperature. It is a dimensionless quantity.

**Significance:**

*   **Strength and Durability:** Higher specific gravity often indicates denser, less porous aggregates, which are generally stronger and more durable.
*   **Mix Design:** Essential for calculating the volumetric proportions of materials in concrete.
*   **Weight-Volume Relationships:** Used to convert between mass and volume of aggregates.

**Types:**

*   **Specific Gravity (Bulk):** Ratio of the mass of a unit volume of aggregate (including voids) to the mass of an equal volume of water. This is essentially the bulk density expressed relative to water.
    *   **Bulk Specific Gravity (Dry):** Determined using oven-dry weight.
    *   **Bulk Specific Gravity (SSD):** Determined using saturated surface-dry weight.

*   **Apparent Specific Gravity:** Ratio of the mass of a unit volume of the *solid* portion of aggregate (excluding absorbed water) to the mass of an equal volume of water. This accounts only for the volume of the aggregate material itself, not its pores.

**Test Procedure (General - Specific Gravity and Absorption, IS 2386 Part III / ASTM C127 for Coarse, ASTM C128 for Fine):**

**For Coarse Aggregate:**

1.  **Preparation:** Dry a sample of aggregate in an oven to constant weight ($W_{od}$).
2.  **Soaking:** Immerse the aggregate in water for 24 hours.
3.  **Surface Drying:** Remove aggregate and bring it to the SSD condition by rolling in a cloth. Weigh it ($W_{ssd}$).
4.  **Bulk Density (SSD) Measurement:** Place the SSD aggregate in a container of known volume and weigh it ($W_{container}$). The weight of SSD aggregate in the container is $W_{ssd}$.
5.  **Specific Gravity of Bulk (SSD):**
    $G_{s, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{ssd} - W_{container}}$ (This is specific gravity of the aggregate with the absorbed water but not the water filling the voids when submerged).
    A more common definition for bulk specific gravity (SSD) is the mass of SSD aggregate divided by the mass of water that would fill the same volume as the aggregate in the SSD condition. This is equivalent to:
    $G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{\text{Weight of water filling the container of volume occupied by the SSD aggregate}}$

    The direct calculation using a pycnometer or a specific gravity container is:
    1. Dry the aggregate to constant weight ($W_{od}$).
    2. Immerse in water for 24 hours.
    3. Bring to SSD condition and weigh ($W_{ssd}$).
    4. Place the SSD aggregate in a pycnometer or specific gravity bottle (volume $V$) and fill with water. Weigh the combination ($W_{SSD\_agg} + W_{water\_filling\_remaining\_voids}$).
    5. Empty the pycnometer, fill it with water only, and weigh ($W_{water\_only}$).

    $G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{water\_only} - (W_{SSD\_agg} + W_{water\_filling\_remaining\_voids}) + W_{ssd}}$

**A simpler and more common approach:**

1.  Dry the aggregate to constant weight ($W_{od}$).
2.  Immerse in water for 24 hours.
3.  Bring to SSD condition and weigh ($W_{ssd}$).
4.  Place the SSD aggregate in a 10-litre bucket and fill the bucket with water up to the brim. Weigh the bucket with aggregate and water ($W_{total}$).
5.  The weight of water that fills the bulk volume of SSD aggregate can be calculated.

Let $V_{bulk}$ be the volume of the 10-litre bucket (0.01 m³).
Weight of water filling the bucket = $V_{bulk} \times \rho_w = 0.01 \text{ m}^3 \times 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3 = 10 \text{ kg}$

Weight of SSD aggregate = $W_{ssd}$
Weight of water filling the remaining voids = $W_{total} - W_{ssd}$

The weight of SSD aggregate that would occupy the entire bucket volume is $W_{ssd}$ when it's in SSD condition. The volume this occupies is $V_{bulk}$.

$G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{water\_filling\_the\_bulk\_volume}} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{\rho_w \times V_{bulk}}$

This is essentially the bulk density in SSD condition, divided by the density of water.

Let's consider the standard IS/ASTM approach using weights:

*   $W_{od}$: Oven-dry weight.
*   $W_{ssd}$: Saturated surface-dry weight.
*   $W_{bulk}$: Weight of SSD aggregate in a container of known volume.

$G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{\text{Mass of water occupying the same volume as the SSD aggregate}}$

Let's consider the weight of aggregate in air and in water.
1.  $W_{od}$: Oven-dry weight.
2.  $W_{ssd}$: SSD weight.
3.  $W_{imm}$: Weight of aggregate immersed in water (aggregate is submerged, but the water level is not the issue, rather the buoyant force).

$G_{s, apparent} = \frac{W_{od}}{W_{od} - W_{imm}}$
$G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{ssd} - W_{imm}}$
Water Absorption (%) = $\frac{W_{ssd} - W_{od}}{W_{od}} \times 100$

**For Fine Aggregate:**

*   Similar procedure but uses a pycnometer or specific gravity bottle of known volume ($V_{bottle}$).
*   $W_{od}$: Oven-dry weight.
*   $W_{ssd}$: SSD weight.
*   $W_{pyc}$: Weight of pycnometer filled with water.
*   $W_{pyc+SSD}$: Weight of pycnometer filled with SSD fine aggregate and then topped up with water.

$G_{s, apparent} = \frac{W_{od}}{W_{pyc} + W_{od} - W_{pyc+SSD}}$
$G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{pyc} + W_{ssd} - W_{pyc+SSD}}$
Water Absorption (%) = $\frac{W_{ssd} - W_{od}}{W_{od}} \times 100$

**Example (Coarse Aggregate):**

*   $W_{od}$ = 1000 g
*   $W_{ssd}$ = 1030 g
*   $W_{imm}$ = 600 g (Weight of aggregate submerged in water)

Water Absorption = $\frac{1030 - 1000}{1000} \times 100 = 3\%$

$G_{s, apparent} = \frac{1000}{1000 - 600} = \frac{1000}{400} = 2.50$

$G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{1030}{1030 - 600} = \frac{1030}{430} = 2.395$

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   Apparent specific gravity is always higher than bulk specific gravity because it excludes the volume of permeable pores.
*   Bulk specific gravity (SSD) is generally used in mix design for converting volumes to masses and vice-versa.
*   Always specify which type of specific gravity is being reported.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**

A sample of coarse aggregate has the following weights:
*   Oven-dry weight ($W_{od}$) = 550 g
*   SSD weight ($W_{ssd}$) = 570 g
*   Weight of aggregate immersed in water ($W_{imm}$) = 340 g

Calculate:
a) Water Absorption
b) Apparent Specific Gravity
c) Bulk Specific Gravity (SSD)

**Answer:**

a) Water Absorption = $\frac{W_{ssd} - W_{od}}{W_{od}} \times 100 = \frac{570 - 550}{550} \times 100 = \frac{20}{550} \times 100 = 3.64\%$

b) Apparent Specific Gravity ($G_{s, apparent}$) = $\frac{W_{od}}{W_{od} - W_{imm}} = \frac{550}{550 - 340} = \frac{550}{210} = 2.62$

c) Bulk Specific Gravity (SSD) ($G_{s, bulk, SSD}$) = $\frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{ssd} - W_{imm}} = \frac{570}{570 - 340} = \frac{570}{230} = 2.48$

**Question 2:**

A sample of aggregate has a bulk density (tamped) of 1650 kg/m³ and an apparent specific gravity of 2.60. Assuming the density of water is 1000 kg/m³, calculate the void ratio and porosity of the aggregate.

**Answer:**

First, we need to find the volume of solids per unit bulk volume.
If bulk density is 1650 kg/m³, it means 1 m³ of bulk aggregate has a mass of 1650 kg.

Volume of Solids ($V_{solids}$) per m³ of bulk = $\frac{\text{Mass of solids}}{\text{Density of solids}} = \frac{\text{Mass of solids}}{G_{s, apparent} \times \rho_w}$

We need the mass of solids. Let's consider the bulk weight and specific gravity.
We know:
$G_{s, apparent} = \frac{W_{od}}{W_{od} - W_{imm}}$
$G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{ssd} - W_{imm}}$
Bulk Density ($\rho_{bulk}$) = $\frac{W_{bulk}}{V_{bulk}}$

Let's use the relationship between bulk density and specific gravity.
Bulk density is related to the volume occupied by solids and voids.

We know:
$V_{bulk} = V_{solids} + V_{voids}$
$e = V_{voids} / V_{solids}$
$n = V_{voids} / V_{bulk}$

From the definition of bulk density, let's consider 1 m³ of bulk aggregate.
Mass of 1 m³ bulk aggregate = 1650 kg.
This mass is composed of solids and absorbed water (if in SSD condition, which bulk density usually implies). However, bulk density typically refers to the mass of aggregate in its natural state as it occupies the volume.

Let's use a conceptual approach:
Consider 1 m³ of bulk aggregate with a density of 1650 kg/m³.
The volume of solids in this 1 m³ can be related to the apparent specific gravity.
Assume the 1650 kg is the weight of the aggregate in SSD condition ($W_{ssd}$) occupying 1 m³ ($V_{bulk}$).
$W_{ssd} = 1650$ kg for $V_{bulk} = 1$ m³.

Volume of solids per unit bulk volume:
$V_{solids} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{G_{s, bulk, SSD} \times \rho_w}$
We need $G_{s, bulk, SSD}$.

Let's directly use the property that bulk density is the mass per unit volume including voids.
$G_{s, apparent} = 2.60$ implies the density of solid material is $2.60 \times 1000 = 2600$ kg/m³.

In 1 m³ of bulk aggregate (mass = 1650 kg), the volume of solid particles is:
$V_{solids} = \frac{\text{Mass of solid particles}}{\text{Density of solid particles}}$

We need the mass of solid particles, which is $W_{od}$.
If $W_{ssd}$ = 1650 kg, and knowing water absorption percentage (let's assume 2% for calculation purposes if not given), $W_{od} = W_{ssd} / (1 + \text{Absorption})$.
Without knowing the water absorption, it's tricky to directly calculate $V_{solids}$ from bulk density and apparent specific gravity alone.

**Revisiting the definitions:**

Bulk Density = Mass of aggregate / Total volume occupied by aggregate (including voids)
Specific Gravity (Apparent) = Density of solid aggregate material / Density of water

Let's consider 1 unit volume of aggregate (e.g., 1 m³).
Mass of this 1 m³ (bulk density) = 1650 kg.
This 1 m³ consists of:
Volume of Solids ($V_{solids}$) + Volume of Voids ($V_{voids}$) = 1 m³

The mass of solid particles is $V_{solids} \times (G_{s, apparent} \times \rho_w)$.
The bulk density is the total mass divided by the total volume.
The total mass is approximately the mass of solid particles plus the mass of absorbed water, but for bulk density, it's often considered the mass of the aggregate as it occupies the volume.

Let's use a simplified approach based on common relationships in mix design:

Density of solid particles = $G_{s, apparent} \times \rho_w = 2.60 \times 1000 = 2600$ kg/m³.

In 1 m³ of bulk aggregate (mass = 1650 kg):
The volume of solid particles within this 1 m³ is $V_{solids}$.
The mass of these solid particles is $V_{solids} \times 2600$ kg.
The volume of voids is $V_{voids} = 1 - V_{solids}$.

The bulk density definition relates the total mass to the total volume.
If we assume the 1650 kg is the mass of aggregate that *fills* 1 m³ volume:
Mass of solids ($m_{solids}$) + Mass of absorbed water ($m_{absorbed\_water}$) = 1650 kg.

This is where the SSD condition becomes important for the definition of bulk density.
Bulk density (tamped) is typically the weight of SSD aggregate per unit volume.
So, let $W_{ssd} = 1650$ kg for $V_{bulk} = 1$ m³.

Now we can relate $W_{ssd}$ to $W_{od}$ using $G_{s, apparent}$.
We know $G_{s, apparent} = \frac{W_{od}}{W_{od} - W_{imm}}$ and $G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{W_{ssd}}{W_{ssd} - W_{imm}}$.
Also, $W_{ssd} = W_{od} \times (1 + \text{Absorption})$.

Let's consider the solid volume within 1 m³ of bulk aggregate.
The mass of the solid particles within this 1 m³ is $V_{solids} \times 2600$ kg.
The volume of voids is $V_{voids} = 1 - V_{solids}$.
The mass of the bulk aggregate (1650 kg) is the mass of the solids plus the mass of the water in the pores that are filled.

The relationship can be derived from:
Bulk Density ($\rho_{bulk}$) = $G_{s, bulk, SSD} \times \rho_w \times (1 - n)$
And $G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{G_{s, apparent} \times (1 + \text{Absorption})}{(1 + G_{s, apparent} \times \text{Absorption})}$

This is getting complicated without the absorption value.

**Let's try a more direct approach using relationships:**

Consider a volume $V$.
Mass of solid particles = $V_{solids} \times G_{s, apparent} \times \rho_w$.
Mass of aggregate in SSD condition = $V_{bulk} \times G_{s, bulk, SSD} \times \rho_w$.
Mass of aggregate in oven-dry condition = $V_{solids} \times G_{s, apparent} \times \rho_w$.

Bulk Density (tamped) = 1650 kg/m³. This is usually the weight of SSD aggregate per unit volume.
So, $G_{s, bulk, SSD} = \frac{\text{Bulk Density}}{\rho_w} = \frac{1650}{1000} = 1.65$.  **This is incorrect as bulk specific gravity is usually higher than 1.65 for normal aggregates.**

Let's use the definition of specific gravity and bulk density directly.
Density of solid particles = $2600$ kg/m³.

Consider 1 m³ of bulk aggregate.
Mass of this aggregate = 1650 kg.
This mass is spread over 1 m³.
The solid particles within this 1 m³ have a volume $V_{solids}$.
The mass of these solid particles = $V_{solids} \times 2600$ kg.
The volume of voids = $1 - V_{solids}$.

The mass of the aggregate (1650 kg) is the mass of the solid particles plus the mass of water in the voids.
If we consider the aggregate in SSD condition, then the voids are filled with water.
Mass of aggregate (SSD) = Mass of solids + Mass of water in voids.
$V_{bulk} = V_{solids} + V_{voids}$
Mass = $V_{solids} \times \rho_{solids} + V_{voids} \times \rho_w$ (for SSD condition)
$1650 \text{ kg/m}^3 = V_{solids} \times 2600 \text{ kg/m}^3 + (1 - V_{solids}) \times 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$
$1650 = 2600 V_{solids} + 1000 - 1000 V_{solids}$
$1650 - 1000 = 1600 V_{solids}$
$650 = 1600 V_{solids}$
$V_{solids} = \frac{650}{1600} = 0.40625 \text{ m}^3$

Now we can calculate:
Volume of Voids ($V_{voids}$) = $1 - V_{solids} = 1 - 0.40625 = 0.59375 \text{ m}^3$

Void Ratio ($e$) = $\frac{V_{voids}}{V_{solids}} = \frac{0.59375}{0.40625} = 1.4625$

Porosity ($n$) = $\frac{V_{voids}}{V_{bulk}} \times 100 = \frac{0.59375}{1} \times 100 = 59.38\%$

**Check:**
Relationship: $n = \frac{e}{1+e} \times 100 = \frac{1.4625}{1+1.4625} \times 100 = \frac{1.4625}{2.4625} \times 100 = 59.39\%$ (Matches closely).

So, the key was to correctly interpret that the bulk density (tamped) represents the mass of SSD aggregate per unit volume.

### 7. Key Takeaways and Summary

*   **Water Absorption** indicates how much water aggregates can absorb, influencing the actual water-cement ratio.
*   **Bulk Density** is crucial for estimating volumes and for volumetric batching of concrete ingredients. Tamped density is usually higher than loose density.
*   **Void Ratio and Porosity** quantify the empty space within aggregates, dictating the amount of cement paste needed.
*   **Specific Gravity** (both apparent and bulk) relates the density of aggregate to that of water and is vital for calculating mass-volume relationships in mix design.
*   The **SSD condition** is a critical reference point for many aggregate tests, especially for determining bulk specific gravity.
*   Understanding these properties allows for proper concrete mix design, leading to desired workability, strength, and durability.
