# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2) - Module 6: Test on Bulking of Sand

## Topic: Test on Bulking of Sand

This module delves into the phenomenon of sand bulking and the practical methods used to determine its extent. Understanding sand bulking is crucial for accurate concrete mix design, as an uncorrected bulking can lead to variations in the water-cement ratio and ultimately affect the strength and durability of the hardened concrete.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

*   **Define and explain the phenomenon of bulking of sand.**
*   **Identify the factors influencing the bulking of sand.**
*   **Understand the purpose and importance of the bulking of sand test in concrete technology.**
*   **Describe the procedure for conducting the bulking of sand test.**
*   **Calculate the percentage of bulking of sand.**
*   **Interpret the results of the bulking of sand test and its implications for concrete mix design.**

### 1. Definition and Explanation of Bulking of Sand

*   **Definition:** Bulking of sand is the phenomenon where the **volume of damp sand increases** compared to its dry volume when a small amount of moisture is added.
*   **Explanation:**
    *   In its dry state, sand particles are in close contact, with minimal void space.
    *   When a small quantity of water is added, surface tension creates a **thin film of water** around each sand particle.
    *   This water film forms **capillary bridges** between adjacent sand particles.
    *   These capillary bridges hold the particles apart, creating larger void spaces and thus increasing the overall volume of the sand.
    *   As more water is added, the capillary bridges become stronger, and the particles are pushed further apart.
    *   However, if a very large amount of water is added, the water film becomes thicker and lubricates the particles, allowing them to settle closer together, and the bulking effect diminishes or even reverses.

*   **Key Concept:** The bulking effect is most pronounced at a specific moisture content, often referred to as the "optimum moisture content" for bulking. This typically occurs when the sand is damp but not saturated.

---

### 2. Factors Influencing the Bulking of Sand

The degree to which sand bulks is influenced by several factors:

*   **Moisture Content:** This is the primary factor. Bulking increases with moisture content up to a certain point, after which it decreases.
*   **Fineness of Sand:**
    *   **Finer sand generally exhibits greater bulking.** This is because finer particles have a larger surface area per unit weight, leading to more water films and stronger capillary forces.
    *   **Coarser sand exhibits less bulking.**
*   **Particle Shape and Surface Texture:**
    *   Sand with irregular shapes and rougher surfaces tends to bulk more than smooth, rounded particles. This is due to increased surface area and better adhesion of the water film.
*   **Impurities:**
    *   The presence of **silt and clay particles** can significantly increase bulking. These fine particles have a greater affinity for water and can create stronger capillary forces.
    *   Organic matter can also contribute to increased bulking.

---

### 3. Purpose and Importance of the Bulking of Sand Test

*   **Purpose:** The primary purpose of the bulking of sand test is to **quantify the increase in volume** of a given quantity of sand due to the presence of a specific percentage of moisture.
*   **Importance in Concrete Mix Design:**
    *   **Accurate Proportioning of Aggregates:** Sand is usually measured by volume in a damp, bulking state. If the bulking is not accounted for, an incorrect amount of sand will be added to the mix.
    *   **Maintaining Water-Cement Ratio:** An overestimation of dry sand due to uncorrected bulking can lead to a lower water-cement ratio than intended, potentially reducing workability and strength. Conversely, underestimating dry sand can lead to a higher water-cement ratio.
    *   **Ensuring Consistent Concrete Properties:** By determining the percentage of bulking, adjustments can be made to the quantity of sand used in the mix to ensure consistent workability and strength.
    *   **Economic Considerations:** Over-adding sand can lead to wastage of material and a less economical mix.

### 4. Procedure for Conducting the Bulking of Sand Test

The test is typically performed using a measuring cylinder or a calibrated container.

**Materials Required:**

*   Clean sand sample (representative of the material to be used)
*   Measuring cylinder (e.g., 1000 ml capacity)
*   Trowel or scoop
*   Water
*   Graduated cylinder (for measuring water)
*   Tapping rod or light hammer
*   Measuring tape or ruler

**Steps:**

1.  **Dry Volume Measurement:**
    *   Fill a clean, dry measuring cylinder with dry sand up to a specific mark (e.g., the 500 ml or 750 ml mark).
    *   Lightly tap the cylinder on a hard surface or tap the sides gently with a trowel to settle the sand. Avoid excessive compaction.
    *   Ensure the top surface of the sand is level.
    *   Record the initial volume of the dry sand as $V_{dry}$.

2.  **Adding Moisture:**
    *   Carefully add a small amount of water to the sand in the cylinder, usually around 4-5% by weight of the sand.
    *   **Method 1 (Adding water directly):** Sprinkle water gently over the surface of the sand.
    *   **Method 2 (Pre-wetting a portion):** Alternatively, a small known weight of sand can be pre-wetted to a consistent moisture content (e.g., 4-5%) and then added to the cylinder.
    *   Mix the sand and water thoroughly within the cylinder using a trowel or rod to ensure uniform distribution of moisture.

3.  **Volume Measurement after Adding Moisture:**
    *   After mixing, gently tap the cylinder on a hard surface or tap the sides to settle the damp sand.
    *   Fill the cylinder to the same initial mark (e.g., 500 ml or 750 ml) with the damp sand.
    *   Level the surface and record the volume of the damp sand as $V_{damp}$.

    **Important Note:** It's crucial to use the *same initial volume mark* for both dry and damp sand measurements to allow for a direct comparison of volumes.

---

### 5. Calculation of Percentage of Bulking of Sand

The percentage of bulking of sand is calculated using the following formula:

$$ \text{Percentage of Bulking} = \left( \frac{V_{damp} - V_{dry}}{V_{dry}} \right) \times 100 $$

Where:
*   $V_{damp}$ = Volume of damp sand in the cylinder.
*   $V_{dry}$ = Volume of dry sand in the cylinder.

**Example Calculation:**

Let's assume:
*   $V_{dry}$ = 750 ml
*   $V_{damp}$ = 975 ml

$$ \text{Percentage of Bulking} = \left( \frac{975 \text{ ml} - 750 \text{ ml}}{750 \text{ ml}} \right) \times 100 $$
$$ \text{Percentage of Bulking} = \left( \frac{225 \text{ ml}}{750 \text{ ml}} \right) \times 100 $$
$$ \text{Percentage of Bulking} = 0.3 \times 100 = 30\% $$

This means the volume of the sand has increased by 30% due to the added moisture.

---

### 6. Interpretation of Results and Implications for Concrete Mix Design

*   **Interpretation:** The calculated percentage of bulking indicates how much extra volume the sand occupies due to moisture.
*   **Implications:**
    *   **Correction Factor:** If the percentage of bulking is determined to be, say, 30%, it means that for every 750 ml of dry sand, we are actually getting 975 ml of damp sand in the measurement.
    *   **Adjusting Sand Quantity:** When proportioning concrete by volume using damp sand, we need to account for this bulking. If the mix design specifies a certain volume of dry sand, and the sand is damp, we must use a smaller *measured volume* of damp sand to achieve the required weight or volume of dry sand.
    *   **Example Scenario:**
        *   Suppose a mix design requires 1000 kg of dry sand, and the specific gravity of sand is 2.65.
        *   If the bulk density of dry sand is, say, 1600 kg/m³, then the required volume of dry sand is $1000 \text{ kg} / 1600 \text{ kg/m³} = 0.625 \text{ m³}$.
        *   If the bulking of sand is found to be 30%, then the *apparent* volume of this 1000 kg of dry sand when damp would be $0.625 \text{ m³} \times 1.30 = 0.8125 \text{ m³}$.
        *   Therefore, if we were measuring by volume and the mix design called for 0.625 m³ of *dry* sand, and our sand was bulking by 30%, we would only need to measure 0.625 m³ of the *damp* sand, as it already contains more volume than the dry sand. (This example highlights the importance of understanding whether the mix design is based on dry or damp volume).

    *   **More Practical Approach (Weight Basis):** In modern concrete production, materials are typically batched by weight, which eliminates the issue of bulking. However, for volumetric batching or when dealing with less sophisticated methods, understanding bulking is essential.
    *   **Moisture Correction:** If the sand is significantly wet (beyond the bulking moisture content), the free water content needs to be accounted for and subtracted from the total mix water to maintain the correct water-cement ratio. This is a separate but related consideration.

### Practice Questions/Exercises

**Question 1:** Define bulking of sand. What are the primary reasons for this phenomenon?

**Answer:** Bulking of sand is the increase in volume of sand when a small amount of moisture is added, compared to its dry volume. This occurs due to the formation of capillary bridges by surface tension of water between sand particles, which push them apart and create larger voids.

**Question 2:** List at least three factors that influence the degree of bulking of sand.

**Answer:**
1.  Moisture Content
2.  Fineness of Sand
3.  Particle Shape and Surface Texture
4.  Presence of Impurities (silt and clay)

**Question 3:** A bulking of sand test was conducted. The initial volume of dry sand in a measuring cylinder was 700 ml. After adding moisture and settling, the volume of damp sand was measured as 910 ml. Calculate the percentage of bulking of the sand.

**Answer:**
$V_{dry} = 700$ ml
$V_{damp} = 910$ ml
Percentage of Bulking = $(\frac{910 - 700}{700}) \times 100 = (\frac{210}{700}) \times 100 = 0.3 \times 100 = 30\%$

**Question 4:** If a concrete mix design specifies 1000 kg of dry sand, and the sand is found to be bulking by 25%, how would this affect the volumetric batching of the sand if the bulk density of dry sand is 1500 kg/m³?

**Answer:**
Required volume of dry sand = $\frac{1000 \text{ kg}}{1500 \text{ kg/m³}} = 0.667 \text{ m³}$
If the sand is bulking by 25%, it means the damp sand occupies 1.25 times the volume of dry sand.
So, for 1000 kg of dry sand, the apparent volume when damp is $0.667 \text{ m³} \times 1.25 = 0.834 \text{ m³}$.
If measuring by volume for a mix requiring 0.667 m³ of dry sand, one would need to measure 0.667 m³ of the *damp* sand. The damp sand would already contain more volume than the dry sand it represents due to bulking.

**Question 5:** Why is it important to account for sand bulking in concrete mix design?

**Answer:** It is important to account for sand bulking to ensure accurate proportioning of aggregates, maintain the intended water-cement ratio, and achieve consistent workability and strength in the hardened concrete, especially when batching by volume.

---

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Bulking is a temporary phenomenon** dependent on moisture content.
*   The **optimum moisture content for bulking** is critical. Too little or too much water reduces the bulking effect.
*   **Finer and more impure sands** exhibit greater bulking.
*   The test is usually performed at a **moisture content of around 4-5%**.
*   **Batching by weight** is the preferred method in modern construction to avoid issues related to sand bulking and moisture variations.
*   Understanding bulking is essential for **volumetric batching** and for ensuring accurate mix proportions when moisture is present.
