# CONCRETE LAB (MT-2) - Module 9: Study on Workability of Concrete

## Topic: Study on Workability of Concrete by Vee-Bee Test and Flow Test

---

### 1. Introduction to Workability

**Definition:** Workability refers to the ease with which fresh concrete can be mixed, placed, consolidated, and finished without segregation or undue bleeding. It is a crucial property that affects the quality and performance of hardened concrete.

**Importance of Workability:**
*   **Ease of Handling:** Ensures concrete can be easily transported, placed, and compacted.
*   **Proper Compaction:** Allows for efficient elimination of voids, leading to dense and strong concrete.
*   **Surface Finish:** Contributes to achieving a desired surface finish.
*   **Prevention of Segregation and Bleeding:** Good workability minimizes the separation of coarse aggregates from the cement paste and the rise of excess water to the surface.

**Factors Affecting Workability:**
*   **Water-Cement Ratio:** Higher w/c ratio generally increases workability (but can reduce strength).
*   **Aggregate Properties:**
    *   **Size and Shape:** Rounded aggregates are more workable than angular or flaky ones.
    *   **Grading:** Well-graded aggregates improve workability by filling voids efficiently.
    *   **Surface Texture:** Smooth aggregates are more workable.
*   **Admixtures:** Plasticizers and superplasticizers improve workability without increasing the water content. Retarders can maintain workability for longer periods.
*   **Cement Content:** Higher cement content can improve workability.
*   **Temperature:** Higher temperatures generally reduce workability due to faster hydration and increased evaporation.
*   **Air Entrainment:** Entrained air can improve workability and freeze-thaw resistance.

### 2. The Vee-Bee Consistometer Test

**Objective:** To determine the workability of concrete by measuring the time required for a concrete sample, under vibration, to transform from a conical shape to a cylindrical shape, indicating its fluidity.

**Principle:** The test is based on the principle of slump and vibration. A concrete sample is placed in a standardized cone and then subjected to vibration. The time it takes for the concrete to spread out and become a uniform cylinder is recorded. This time is inversely related to the workability.

**Apparatus:**
*   **Vee-Bee Consistometer:** Consists of a vibrating table, a standard slump cone, and a transparent cylindrical casing with a collar.
*   **Slump Cone:** A truncated cone made of metal, 300 mm in height, with bottom diameter of 200 mm and top diameter of 100 mm.
*   **Tamping Rod:** Standard rod for compacting concrete in the cone.
*   **Stopwatch:** For timing the test.
*   **Scale:** For measuring the spread.

**Procedure:**
1.  **Preparation:** Assemble the Vee-Bee apparatus. Place the vibrating table in a level position. Fit the slump cone inside the cylindrical casing.
2.  **Filling the Cone:** Fill the slump cone with concrete in three layers, compacting each layer with 25 strokes of the tamping rod. Ensure the top of the cone is struck off level.
3.  **Removing the Cone:** Carefully lift the slump cone vertically, leaving the concrete in the cylindrical casing.
4.  **Fitting the Collar:** Place the transparent cylindrical casing with the collar over the concrete.
5.  **Starting the Vibration:** Start the vibrating table and the stopwatch simultaneously.
6.  **Timing the Test:** Observe the concrete through the transparent casing. The test is complete when the concrete has spread out to form a uniform cylinder and all ripples have disappeared, or when the concrete has spread to the full diameter of the casing. Record the time in seconds.
7.  **Recording Results:** The time taken for the concrete to spread out is the Vee-Bee time.

**Interpretation of Results:**
*   **Short Vee-Bee Time (e.g., 5-15 seconds):** Indicates high workability (very fluid concrete).
*   **Medium Vee-Bee Time (e.g., 15-30 seconds):** Indicates medium workability.
*   **Long Vee-Bee Time (e.g., > 30 seconds):** Indicates low workability (stiff concrete).

**Advantages:**
*   Provides a measure of workability that accounts for the effect of vibration.
*   Relatively simple to perform.
*   Suitable for concrete mixes that are too stiff to slump in the standard slump test.

**Disadvantages:**
*   Not suitable for very wet or highly fluid concrete mixes as they will not hold their shape in the cone.
*   Requires specialized equipment (Vee-Bee Consistometer).

**Examples of Vee-Bee Times and Workability:**
*   **Very High Workability (Flowing):** Less than 5 seconds
*   **High Workability (Plastic):** 5-15 seconds
*   **Medium Workability (Stiff Plastic):** 15-30 seconds
*   **Low Workability (Very Stiff):** 30-45 seconds
*   **Very Low Workability (Quaking):** Greater than 45 seconds

### 3. The Flow Test (or Slump-Flow Test)

**Objective:** To measure the workability of concrete, particularly for self-compacting concrete (SCC), by assessing its spread.

**Principle:** The test is a modification of the slump test. A slump cone is filled with concrete, and the cone is lifted. The diameter to which the concrete spreads freely under its own weight is measured. This spread diameter is an indicator of workability.

**Apparatus:**
*   **Slump Cone:** Standard slump cone (as used in the slump test).
*   **Tamping Rod:** Standard rod.
*   **Flow Table (Optional but common):** A rigid metal table with concentric rings. In some variations, the cone is placed on a flat surface.
*   **Stopwatch:** For timing (in some variations, e.g., T50).
*   **Measuring Tape or Ruler:** For measuring the spread diameter.

**Procedure (Slump-Flow Test - Common Variation):**
1.  **Preparation:** Place the slump cone on a smooth, level, non-absorbent surface.
2.  **Filling the Cone:** Fill the slump cone with concrete in two layers, compacting each layer with 20 strokes of the tamping rod. Ensure the top of the cone is struck off level.
3.  **Removing the Cone:** Carefully lift the slump cone vertically, leaving the concrete on the surface.
4.  **Measuring the Spread:** Allow the concrete to spread freely under its own weight for a specific time (e.g., 5 seconds in T50 flow test, or no holding time in basic slump-flow). Measure the diameter of the concrete spread at its widest point. The average of two perpendicular measurements is often taken.

**Interpretation of Results:**
*   **Larger Spread Diameter:** Indicates higher workability and fluidity.
*   **Smaller Spread Diameter:** Indicates lower workability.

**Variations of the Flow Test:**

*   **Slump-Flow (Basic):** Measures the final diameter after the cone is lifted.
*   **T50 Slump-Flow:** Measures the diameter of the spread after 50 seconds. This is particularly useful for assessing the filling ability and viscosity of SCC.
    *   **Short T50 time (e.g., < 2 seconds):** High fluidity, potentially low viscosity.
    *   **Longer T50 time (e.g., > 2 seconds):** Higher viscosity, better resistance to segregation.

**Examples of Slump-Flow and T50 Values for SCC Classes:**
*   **Class 1 (SF1):** Spread diameter 500-550 mm
*   **Class 2 (SF2):** Spread diameter 550-600 mm
*   **Class 3 (SF3):** Spread diameter 600-660 mm

**T50 Values (for SCC viscosity):**
*   **Class 1 (T50-1):** T50 ≤ 2 seconds (high fluidity, low viscosity)
*   **Class 2 (T50-2):** 2 < T50 ≤ 4 seconds (medium fluidity, medium viscosity)
*   **Class 3 (T50-3):** T50 > 4 seconds (low fluidity, high viscosity)

**Advantages:**
*   Suitable for very fluid concrete and self-compacting concrete.
*   Provides a direct measure of the concrete's ability to spread.
*   Simple to perform with basic slump test equipment.

**Disadvantages:**
*   May not be sensitive enough for very stiff concrete mixes.
*   Does not directly assess the ease of compaction against resistance.

### 4. Comparison and Application

| Feature           | Vee-Bee Test                                  | Flow Test (Slump-Flow)                          |
| :---------------- | :-------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| **Purpose**       | Measures workability by time to form a cylinder under vibration. | Measures workability by the spread diameter.    |
| **Applicable to** | Stiff to plastic concrete.                    | Plastic to very fluid concrete, SCC.            |
| **Principle**     | Time for concrete to become cylindrical under vibration. | Diameter of free spread under its own weight. |
| **Measurement**   | Time (seconds)                                | Diameter (mm) or Time (for T50 variation)       |
| **Equipment**     | Vee-Bee Consistometer (vibrating table)       | Slump cone, tamping rod, measuring tape.        |
| **Key Benefit**   | Accounts for vibration effect.                | Measures flow and suitability for SCC.          |

**When to Use Which Test:**
*   **Vee-Bee Test:** Choose when dealing with concrete mixes that are relatively stiff and may be difficult to assess with a standard slump test alone. It's good for understanding how vibration affects the workability and ease of consolidation of these mixes.
*   **Flow Test (Slump-Flow):** Ideal for assessing the workability of concrete that is intended to flow easily, such as self-compacting concrete (SCC). It directly measures the fluidity and spreadability. The T50 variation is excellent for assessing the viscosity of SCC.

### 5. Practice Questions & Exercises

**Question 1:** Define workability and list three factors that significantly affect it.
**Answer:** Workability is the ease with which fresh concrete can be mixed, placed, consolidated, and finished without segregation or bleeding. Factors include: water-cement ratio, aggregate grading and shape, and admixtures.

**Question 2:** Explain the principle behind the Vee-Bee test. What does a shorter Vee-Bee time indicate about the concrete's workability?
**Answer:** The Vee-Bee test measures the time for concrete to transform from a cone to a cylinder under vibration. A shorter Vee-Bee time indicates higher workability (more fluid concrete).

**Question 3:** For what type of concrete mix is the Flow Test (Slump-Flow) most commonly used, and why?
**Answer:** The Flow Test is most commonly used for Self-Compacting Concrete (SCC) because it directly measures the concrete's ability to spread under its own weight, which is a key characteristic of SCC.

**Question 4:** If a concrete sample in the Vee-Bee test takes 45 seconds to spread out, would you describe its workability as high or low? Justify your answer.
**Answer:** A Vee-Bee time of 45 seconds indicates low workability. Typically, times below 30 seconds are considered medium to high workability.
