### 1. Introduction to Evaporation

#### 1.1. Definition of Evaporation

*   **Evaporation:** The process by which water changes from a liquid state to a gaseous state (water vapor) and is released into the atmosphere. It is a crucial component of the hydrologic cycle, representing a significant loss of water from various surfaces.
*   **Sublimation:** A related process where ice or snow directly transforms into water vapor without passing through the liquid phase. While distinct, it is often considered alongside evaporation when assessing water loss.

#### 1.2. Significance of Evaporation in the Hydrologic Cycle

*   **Water Balance:** Evaporation is a primary output from water bodies (lakes, reservoirs, rivers) and land surfaces, directly impacting the overall water balance of a region.
*   **Atmospheric Moisture:** It is a major source of atmospheric moisture, influencing cloud formation, precipitation patterns, and humidity.
*   **Water Resources Management:** Understanding evaporation rates is critical for:
    *   Designing and operating reservoirs and irrigation systems.
    *   Estimating water availability.
    *   Managing water losses in arid and semi-arid regions.
    *   Assessing the impact of climate change on water resources.

### 2. Factors Influencing Evaporation

The rate of evaporation is not constant and is influenced by a combination of atmospheric and surface conditions.

#### 2.1. Energy Availability

*   **Solar Radiation:** The primary energy source for evaporation. Higher solar radiation provides more energy for water molecules to escape into the atmosphere.
*   **Air Temperature:** Higher air temperatures increase the kinetic energy of water molecules, making them more likely to evaporate. Warmer air can also hold more moisture.

#### 2.2. Vapor Pressure Gradient (Saturation Deficit)

*   **Vapor Pressure of Water Surface:** The pressure exerted by water vapor at the water surface, which is dependent on temperature (higher temperature = higher vapor pressure).
*   **Vapor Pressure of Ambient Air:** The actual pressure of water vapor in the surrounding air.
*   **Saturation Deficit:** The difference between the saturation vapor pressure at the water surface temperature and the actual vapor pressure of the air. A larger saturation deficit indicates a greater potential for evaporation.
    *   **High Humidity:** Low saturation deficit, slower evaporation.
    *   **Dry Air:** High saturation deficit, faster evaporation.

#### 2.3. Wind Speed

*   **Removal of Saturated Air:** Wind removes the layer of humid air that forms above the water surface, replacing it with drier air. This maintains a steeper vapor pressure gradient, promoting higher evaporation rates.
*   **Increased Mixing:** Wind also enhances the mixing of water at the surface, bringing warmer water to the top, which further aids evaporation.

#### 2.4. Water Properties

*   **Surface Area:** Larger water surfaces have a greater potential for evaporation.
*   **Water Quality (Salinity/Dissolved Solids):** Salinity reduces the vapor pressure of water, thereby reducing the rate of evaporation compared to fresh water.
    *   **Example:** Evaporation from the Dead Sea is significantly lower than from a freshwater lake due to its high salt content.
*   **Water Depth:** While not a direct factor in the rate per unit area, very shallow water bodies can heat up faster and evaporate more quickly.

#### 2.5. Water Surface Type

*   **Open Water Surfaces (Lakes, Reservoirs):** Experience direct evaporation from the water surface.
*   **Soil Surfaces (Bare Soil, Agricultural Fields):** Evaporation occurs from the soil surface. Moisture availability in the top layers and soil structure play a significant role.
*   **Vegetation (Evapotranspiration):** The combined process of evaporation from soil and plant surfaces and transpiration from plants. This is a major component of water loss, especially in vegetated areas.

### 3. Measurement of Evaporation

Measuring evaporation is essential for accurate water resource assessment and management. Since direct measurement of evaporation from large water bodies is challenging, indirect methods and specialized instruments are used.

#### 3.1. Evaporation Pans

*   **Principle:** Evaporation pans are standardized containers filled with water, exposed to atmospheric conditions similar to larger water bodies. The rate of water level decrease due to evaporation is measured.
*   **Types of Pans:**
    *   **Class A Evaporation Pan:** A standard circular pan, 1.22 m (4 ft) in diameter and 0.25 m (10 in) deep, made of unpainted galvanized iron. It is usually placed on a wooden platform about 0.15 m (6 in) above the ground.
    *   **GGI-3000 Pan:** A cylindrical pan, 3000 cm² in surface area and 80 cm deep, with a water depth of 20 cm. Often used in Russia and other parts of the world.
    *   **Other specialized pans:** (e.g., sunken pans).
*   **Measurement Procedure:**
    1.  The pan is filled to a specific level (e.g., 5 cm below the rim).
    2.  The water level is measured daily using a fixed point gauge or a micrometer hook gauge.
    3.  Water is added or removed to maintain the desired level.
    4.  The evaporation is calculated as the change in water level, adjusted for any rainfall that occurred.
*   **Pan Coefficient (K<sub>p</sub>):** Evaporation from a pan is generally higher than from a large water body due to the heat absorbed by the pan walls and the larger surface area to volume ratio. A pan coefficient (K<sub>p</sub>) is used to convert pan evaporation to lake or reservoir evaporation:
    $E_{lake} = K_p \times E_{pan}$
    *   K<sub>p</sub> typically ranges from 0.6 to 0.8 for a Class A pan, depending on the location, climate, and surrounding environment.
*   **Associated Measurements:** Along with water level, surrounding meteorological data is recorded:
    *   Air temperature
    *   Humidity
    *   Wind speed
    *   Solar radiation

#### 3.2. Lysimeters

*   **Principle:** Lysimeters are devices used to measure evapotranspiration (ET) from vegetated soil. They consist of a container filled with soil and vegetation, which is isolated from the surrounding environment. Water balance is maintained by measuring water added (precipitation, irrigation) and water removed (drainage, change in soil moisture storage).
*   **Types of Lysimeters:**
    *   **Gravimetric Lysimeters:** Measure the change in weight of the soil-plant system, which directly corresponds to water loss.
    *   **Volumetric Lysimeters:** Measure the change in water volume in the lysimeter.
    *   **Drainage Lysimeters:** Measure the amount of water that drains out of the soil profile.
*   **Applications:** Crucial for agricultural water management, irrigation scheduling, and ecological studies.

#### 3.3. Estimation Methods (Using Meteorological Data)

These methods estimate evaporation based on readily available meteorological data, avoiding the need for direct measurements with specialized equipment.

*   **Penman Equation (and its variations):**
    *   **Principle:** A physically based method that combines the energy balance and aerodynamic components to estimate evaporation (often potential evapotranspiration, PET). It considers solar radiation, air temperature, humidity, and wind speed.
    *   **Importance:** Considered one of the most accurate methods for estimating ET.
*   **Thornthwaite Equation:**
    *   **Principle:** An empirical method that primarily relies on mean monthly temperature and considers the duration of daylight hours to estimate PET.
    *   **Limitations:** Less accurate than Penman, especially in non-ideal conditions.
*   **Blaney-Criddle Method:**
    *   **Principle:** Another empirical method based on temperature and daylight hours, often used for agricultural applications.
*   **Hargreaves-Samani Equation:**
    *   **Principle:** A simpler method that uses extraterrestrial radiation (or solar radiation) and temperature.
*   **Water Budget Method:**
    *   **Principle:** For reservoirs or lakes, evaporation can be estimated by applying the water balance equation:
        $E = P + I - O - \Delta S$
        Where:
        *   $E$ = Evaporation
        *   $P$ = Precipitation
        *   $I$ = Inflow (surface and groundwater)
        *   $O$ = Outflow (surface and groundwater)
        *   $\Delta S$ = Change in storage
    *   **Limitations:** Requires accurate measurement of all other components, which can be challenging.

#### 3.4. Remote Sensing Techniques

*   **Principle:** Utilizes satellite imagery and sensor data (e.g., thermal infrared, microwave) to estimate land surface temperature, vegetation indices, and atmospheric moisture, which can then be used in ET models.
*   **Advantages:** Provides spatial coverage over large areas.
*   **Examples:** MODIS, Landsat satellite data.

### 4. Control of Evaporation

In water-scarce regions, reducing evaporation from reservoirs and other water bodies is a significant concern for water conservation.

#### 4.1. Methods for Reducing Evaporation from Reservoirs

*   **Floating Covers:**
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Chemical Films:** Monomolecular films (e.g., cetyl alcohol, stearyl alcohol) spread on the water surface that reduce the vapor pressure gradient. They form a barrier that inhibits the escape of water molecules.
        *   **Floating Balls/Covers:** Spherical plastic balls (e.g., shade balls) or other buoyant materials that cover a significant portion of the water surface, reducing the exposed area to solar radiation and wind.
    *   **Advantages:** Can significantly reduce evaporation.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Chemical films can be affected by wind and wave action, may have environmental concerns, and require continuous replenishment. Ball covers can be costly to deploy and maintain and may interfere with wildlife.
*   **Reservoir Shape and Depth Modification:**
    *   **Deep Reservoirs:** Generally have lower evaporation rates per unit volume than shallow ones due to a smaller surface area to volume ratio.
    *   **Reservoir Shape:** Designing reservoirs with a more compact shape (minimum surface area for a given volume) can help reduce evaporation.
*   **Windbreaks:**
    *   **Purpose:** Planting trees or constructing barriers around reservoirs to reduce wind speed over the water surface.
    *   **Effectiveness:** Can reduce evaporation by decreasing the removal of saturated air. Effectiveness depends on the height, density, and orientation of the windbreaks.
*   **Groundwater Abstraction:**
    *   **Concept:** In some cases, if a reservoir is fed by groundwater, reducing groundwater levels might indirectly reduce surface water availability for evaporation. This is a more indirect and often impractical method.
*   **Covering Reservoirs:**
    *   **Feasibility:** Covering entire reservoirs with solid structures (e.g., domes) is usually prohibitively expensive for large water bodies but might be considered for smaller, critical storage.
*   **Water Quality Management:**
    *   **Salinity:** While increasing salinity would reduce evaporation, this is not a practical or desirable method for water supply reservoirs due to its impact on water usability.

#### 4.2. Considerations for Evaporation Control Measures

*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** The cost of implementing and maintaining the control measure versus the value of water saved.
*   **Environmental Impact:** Potential effects on aquatic ecosystems, wildlife, and the surrounding environment.
*   **Technical Feasibility:** Practicality of application and maintenance in the specific context.
*   **Wind and Wave Action:** The ability of the control method to withstand prevailing weather conditions.
*   **Water Quality:** Ensuring that control measures do not negatively impact water quality.

### 5. Practical Applications

*   **Reservoir Design:** Estimating water losses due to evaporation is crucial for determining the required storage capacity to meet downstream demands.
*   **Irrigation Scheduling:** Understanding evapotranspiration from agricultural fields helps farmers optimize irrigation, saving water and energy.
*   **Water Budgeting:** Accurate accounting of evaporation is vital for regional and national water resource assessments.
*   **Climate Change Impact Assessment:** Predicting how changes in temperature, wind, and precipitation might affect evaporation rates.
*   **Water Conservation:** Implementing evaporation control measures in water-scarce regions.

---

### 6. Key Points to Remember

*   **Evaporation is the transformation of liquid water to water vapor.**
*   **Key drivers of evaporation are energy (solar radiation, temperature) and the vapor pressure gradient (humidity), with wind also playing a significant role.**
*   **Evaporation pans are common measurement tools, but a pan coefficient is needed to relate pan evaporation to actual water body evaporation.**
*   **Lysimeters are used to measure evapotranspiration from vegetated land.**
*   **Empirical and physically-based equations (like Penman's) estimate evaporation using meteorological data.**
*   **Evaporation control methods aim to reduce the factors influencing evaporation, such as solar radiation, wind, or exposing more water to the atmosphere.**
*   **Floating covers and windbreaks are common strategies for reducing reservoir evaporation.**
*   **Cost-effectiveness and environmental impact are crucial considerations when choosing evaporation control measures.**

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Define evaporation and explain its role in the hydrologic cycle. List and briefly describe at least three factors that influence the rate of evaporation.

**Answer:**
Evaporation is the process by which liquid water transforms into water vapor and enters the atmosphere. It is a vital component of the hydrologic cycle, transferring water from the Earth's surface to the atmosphere, influencing precipitation and regional water balances.

Three factors influencing evaporation are:
1.  **Solar Radiation:** Provides the energy required for the phase change of water. Higher radiation leads to higher evaporation.
2.  **Air Temperature:** Warmer air increases the kinetic energy of water molecules and can hold more moisture, thus increasing evaporation.
3.  **Vapor Pressure Gradient (Saturation Deficit):** The difference between the vapor pressure at the water surface and in the air. A larger deficit (drier air) leads to higher evaporation.
4.  **Wind Speed:** Removes saturated air from the surface, maintaining a steeper vapor pressure gradient and promoting higher evaporation.

**Question 2:**
Describe the principle of operation of a Class A evaporation pan. What is a pan coefficient and why is it used?

**Answer:**
A Class A evaporation pan is a standardized cylindrical container filled with water. It measures the amount of water lost due to evaporation over a specific period by monitoring the drop in the water level. The pan is exposed to atmospheric conditions.

A pan coefficient ($K_p$) is a dimensionless factor used to correct the evaporation measured by a pan to estimate the actual evaporation from a larger water body (like a lake or reservoir). It is used because evaporation from a pan is typically higher than from a large water body due to differences in heat absorption by the pan walls and the surface-to-volume ratio. The relationship is $E_{lake} = K_p \times E_{pan}$.

**Question 3:**
A Class A evaporation pan shows an evaporation of 8 mm on a particular day. If the pan coefficient for the location is 0.7, what is the estimated evaporation from a nearby lake on the same day?

**Answer:**
Given:
*   Pan Evaporation ($E_{pan}$) = 8 mm
*   Pan Coefficient ($K_p$) = 0.7

The estimated evaporation from the lake ($E_{lake}$) is calculated using the formula:
$E_{lake} = K_p \times E_{pan}$
$E_{lake} = 0.7 \times 8 \text{ mm}$
$E_{lake} = 5.6 \text{ mm}$

So, the estimated evaporation from the nearby lake is 5.6 mm.

**Question 4:**
Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using floating balls (shade balls) to control evaporation from reservoirs.

**Answer:**
**Advantages:**
*   **Significant Evaporation Reduction:** Can reduce evaporation by 85-90% by covering a large portion of the water surface.
*   **Reduces Algal Blooms:** Shade balls can reduce sunlight penetration, which helps inhibit the growth of algae and improve water quality.
*   **Reduces Wind Effects:** The balls act as a partial wind buffer.
*   **Relatively Simple to Deploy:** Can be placed by hand or with simple machinery.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Cost:** The initial cost of purchasing and deploying a large number of balls can be substantial.
*   **Maintenance:** Balls can degrade over time due to UV radiation and may need replacement. They can also be blown out of the reservoir by strong winds.
*   **Wind and Wave Action:** High winds can dislodge the balls, and strong waves can disrupt the coverage.
*   **Environmental Impact:** Potential for plastic debris if balls break down. Can interfere with bird nesting or other wildlife that might interact with the water surface.
*   **Water Aeration:** Reduced wind interaction may affect the oxygen levels in the upper layers of the water body.

**Question 5:**
What is a lysimeter, and for what purpose is it primarily used in hydrology?

**Answer:**
A lysimeter is a device used to measure evapotranspiration (ET) from a specific area of soil with vegetation. It typically consists of a container filled with soil and plants, isolated from the surroundings, allowing for the precise measurement of water balance components.

Its primary purpose is to accurately quantify the amount of water lost through evaporation from the soil surface and transpiration from plants (combined as evapotranspiration). This information is vital for agricultural water management, irrigation scheduling, and hydrological research.
