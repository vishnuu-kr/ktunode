# Module 4: Vertical Distribution of Groundwater - Types of Wells and Tube Wells

## Introduction

This module delves into the crucial aspect of accessing groundwater: **wells**. We will explore the different types of wells, their classifications, and specifically focus on **tube wells**, which are prevalent in modern groundwater extraction. Understanding these types is essential for efficient and sustainable groundwater management.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

*   **Define** different types of wells used for groundwater extraction.
*   **Classify** wells based on various criteria (e.g., construction, yield, depth).
*   **Describe** the construction and working principles of different types of tube wells.
*   **Differentiate** between various types of tube wells based on their construction and functional characteristics.
*   **Identify** the factors influencing the choice of a particular well type for a given situation.

## 1. Types of Wells

Wells are artificial structures constructed to tap groundwater. They can be broadly classified based on several criteria:

### 1.1 Classification Based on Construction Method

This is the most common classification.

*   **Dug Wells (or Open Wells):**
    *   **Definition:** These are shallow wells, typically excavated by manual labor or simple machinery, with a large diameter. They are usually un-cased or have a loosely fitted casing.
    *   **Construction:**
        *   Excavated to the water table.
        *   Walls are often lined with bricks, stones, or concrete to prevent collapse.
        *   Diameter is usually large (1-3 meters or more).
        *   Depth is generally limited, reaching only the unconfined aquifer.
    *   **Working Principle:** Water enters the well from the surrounding aquifer through the permeable aquifer material at the bottom and sides of the well. Extraction is usually done using buckets, pumps, or other manual methods.
    *   **Yield:** Generally low to moderate.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Relatively simple and cheap to construct in shallow water table areas.
        *   Can extract large volumes of water per draw-down cycle, suitable for domestic and small-scale irrigation.
        *   Less prone to clogging by fine sediments.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Susceptible to surface contamination (pollution).
        *   Water level fluctuates significantly with seasonal changes.
        *   Lower yield compared to tube wells for larger requirements.
        *   Requires large land area.
    *   **Example:** Traditional village wells used for domestic water supply.

*   **Bored Wells (or Drilled Wells):**
    *   **Definition:** These are narrow diameter wells constructed by drilling a hole into the ground, typically reaching confined aquifers.
    *   **Construction:**
        *   Drilled using rotary, percussion, or auger methods.
        *   Diameter is relatively small (0.1 to 0.5 meters).
        *   Usually lined with a casing pipe to prevent collapse.
    *   **Working Principle:** Similar to dug wells but designed to access deeper, often confined, aquifers. Water enters through perforations in the casing at the aquifer zone.
    *   **Yield:** Can range from low to high, depending on the aquifer characteristics and well design.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Can reach deeper aquifers, often providing higher quality water.
        *   Less susceptible to surface contamination.
        *   Requires less land area than dug wells.
        *   Can be equipped with submersible pumps for efficient extraction.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   More expensive to construct than dug wells.
        *   Requires specialized drilling equipment.
        *   Clogging can be an issue if not properly screened.
    *   **Example:** Many agricultural wells and municipal water supply wells are bored wells.

*   **Driven Wells (or Jetted Wells):**
    *   **Definition:** These wells are constructed by driving a perforated pipe, equipped with a strainer at the bottom, directly into the aquifer.
    *   **Construction:**
        *   A well screen or strainer is attached to the end of a pipe.
        *   The pipe is driven into the ground using a hammer or driving cap, often with the aid of a jet of water to loosen the soil.
        *   Typically used for shallow sandy aquifers.
    *   **Working Principle:** Water enters the aquifer through the strainer at the bottom.
    *   **Yield:** Generally low to moderate.
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Relatively inexpensive and quick to install.
        *   Suitable for temporary use or shallow water sources.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Limited to shallow depths and permeable soils.
        *   Can be damaged during driving.
        *   Limited yield.
    *   **Example:** Small hand pumps installed in shallow sandy areas.

### 1.2 Classification Based on Depth

*   **Shallow Wells:**
    *   **Definition:** Wells that tap unconfined aquifers near the surface.
    *   **Depth:** Typically up to 20-30 meters.
    *   **Construction:** Often dug wells.
    *   **Water Source:** Reaches the water table of the unconfined aquifer.
    *   **Characteristics:** Water level fluctuates significantly with rainfall and abstraction. Susceptible to pollution.

*   **Deep Wells (or Tube Wells):**
    *   **Definition:** Wells that tap confined or semi-confined aquifers, or deeper zones of unconfined aquifers.
    *   **Depth:** Can range from tens of meters to several hundred meters or more.
    *   **Construction:** Typically bored or drilled wells.
    *   **Water Source:** Reaches deeper aquifers, often under pressure (artesian conditions).
    *   **Characteristics:** Water level is more stable. Less prone to surface contamination. Generally higher yield.

### 1.3 Classification Based on Yield

*   **Low Yield Wells:** Wells that produce a small quantity of water, suitable for domestic use or small gardens.
*   **Medium Yield Wells:** Wells that produce moderate amounts of water, suitable for small-scale irrigation or livestock.
*   **High Yield Wells:** Wells that produce large quantities of water, suitable for large-scale irrigation, industrial use, or municipal supply.

### 1.4 Classification Based on Position of Screen/Perforation

*   **Shallow Screen Wells:** Screen is placed only in the saturated portion of the aquifer.
*   **Staggered Screen Wells:** Multiple screens are placed at different levels within the aquifer zone.
*   **Slotted Wells:** The casing pipe is slotted in the aquifer zone instead of using a manufactured screen.

### 1.5 Classification Based on Nature of Water Supply

*   **Gravity Wells:** Water flows out due to gravity, usually from artesian wells or springs.
*   **Sump Wells:** Wells where water is lifted by a pump.

## 2. Types of Tube Wells

Tube wells are a type of bored or drilled well characterized by their narrow diameter, typically lined with casing pipe, and equipped with a strainer or perforations in the aquifer zone. They are designed for efficient abstraction of groundwater, often from deeper aquifers.

### 2.1 Classification of Tube Wells

Tube wells can be classified based on several factors, including depth, yield, construction, and water level:

#### 2.1.1 Based on Depth and Construction

*   **Shallow Tube Wells:**
    *   **Definition:** Tube wells that penetrate only the shallow aquifers, typically unconfined or semi-confined.
    *   **Depth:** Generally up to 30-40 meters.
    *   **Construction:** Usually have a single long strainer placed in the aquifer. The casing pipe extends from the ground surface to above the static water level.
    *   **Working:** Water is usually lifted using a surface pump (e.g., centrifugal pump).
    *   **Yield:** Low to moderate.
    *   **Example:** Many tube wells for domestic use or small irrigation fields.

*   **Deep Tube Wells:**
    *   **Definition:** Tube wells that penetrate deeper aquifers, often confined or semi-confined, and may have a higher potential yield.
    *   **Depth:** Can range from 40 meters to several hundred meters.
    *   **Construction:**
        *   **Staggered Type:** Multiple perforated sections or strainers are placed at different depths within the aquifer zone to tap water from various strata. This is common in aquifers with discontinuous water-bearing layers.
        *   **Single-Screen Type:** A single, long strainer is placed in a thick, homogenous aquifer.
        *   **Multiple-Screen Type:** Similar to staggered type but often with more closely spaced screens.
    *   **Working:** Typically equipped with submersible pumps due to their depth.
    *   **Yield:** Moderate to high.
    *   **Example:** Tube wells for large-scale irrigation projects, industrial use, or municipal water supply.

#### 2.1.2 Based on Yield

*   **A-Class Tube Wells:** High yield (typically > 50 LPS - Litres Per Second).
*   **B-Class Tube Wells:** Medium yield (typically 25-50 LPS).
*   **C-Class Tube Wells:** Low yield (typically < 25 LPS).
    *   *Note: Yield classifications can vary based on regional standards and purposes.*

#### 2.1.3 Based on Water Level (Presence of Artesian Conditions)

*   **Non-Artesian Tube Wells:**
    *   **Definition:** Tube wells where the water level in the aquifer is below the ground surface.
    *   **Working:** Requires a pump to lift water.
    *   **Piezometric Level:** Below ground level.

*   **Artesian Tube Wells:**
    *   **Definition:** Tube wells that tap confined aquifers where the water is under pressure. The water level (piezometric level) is above the top of the aquifer, and in some cases, above the ground surface.
    *   **Sub-Classification:**
        *   **Flowing Artesian Wells:** If the piezometric level is above the ground surface, water will flow out naturally without pumping.
        *   **Non-Flowing Artesian Wells:** If the piezometric level is below the ground surface but above the top of the aquifer, water rises in the well but requires pumping.
    *   **Working:** Can be gravity flow or pumped, depending on the piezometric head.
    *   **Example:** Many tube wells in regions with confined aquifers like Punjab and Haryana in India exhibit artesian conditions.

#### 2.1.4 Based on Construction Details (More Specific Types)

*   **Staggered Tube Wells:**
    *   **Description:** Characterized by having multiple perforated sections (or strainers) at different depths within the aquifer zone. This design is beneficial when the aquifer is composed of several water-bearing strata separated by less permeable layers.
    *   **Purpose:** To maximize water withdrawal from various permeable zones.
    *   **Example:** Used in areas where the aquifer is not homogenous and consists of several distinct water-bearing layers.

*   **Staggered-Slotted Tube Wells:**
    *   **Description:** Similar to staggered tube wells, but instead of manufactured strainers, the casing pipe itself is slotted at multiple intervals within the aquifer.
    *   **Purpose:** Cost-effective alternative to strainers, especially for less abrasive aquifer materials.
    *   **Example:** Often used where fine gravel or sand is the primary aquifer material.

*   **Straighted Tube Wells:**
    *   **Description:** These wells have a single, continuous perforated section or strainer placed in the aquifer.
    *   **Purpose:** Suitable for aquifers that are homogenous and thick.
    *   **Example:** Used in areas with uniform sand or gravel aquifers.

### 2.2 Components of a Tube Well

*   **Well Casing:** The pipe lining the borehole to prevent collapse and to support the pump and well screen.
*   **Well Screen/Perforations:** The part of the casing that is open to the aquifer, allowing water to enter the well while preventing the entry of excessive sand and gravel. These can be manufactured screens (e.g., wire-wound, louvered) or slots cut into the casing.
*   **Gravel Pack (Gravel Shroud/Gravel Pack Screen):** In some cases, a layer of carefully selected gravel is placed around the well screen. This helps to further prevent fine sediments from entering the well and improves the hydraulic efficiency.
*   **Well Head Assembly:** The structure at the ground surface that connects the casing to the pump and provides a seal against contamination.

### 2.3 Factors Influencing the Choice of Well Type

*   **Aquifer Characteristics:**
    *   **Depth of aquifer:** Determines if shallow or deep well is needed.
    *   **Thickness of aquifer:** Influences the length of the strainer/perforations.
    *   **Permeability and transmissivity:** Affects potential yield and well design.
    *   **Nature of aquifer material:** (Sand, gravel, rock) influences drilling method and screen type.
    *   **Presence of confining layers:** Determines if confined or unconfined conditions exist.
*   **Water Quality Requirements:**
    *   **Salinity and mineral content:** Deeper aquifers might have better quality water.
    *   **Risk of surface contamination:** Deeper wells are less susceptible.
*   **Required Yield:** The volume of water needed dictates the well capacity.
*   **Economic Considerations:**
    *   **Construction cost:** Dug wells are cheaper for shallow depths, while tube wells are more economical for deeper, higher yields.
    *   **Operating and maintenance costs:** Pump type, energy consumption.
*   **Availability of Technology and Expertise:** Drilling rigs, skilled labor.
*   **Land Availability:** Dug wells require more surface area.

## Key Points to Remember

*   Wells are the primary means of accessing groundwater.
*   Dug wells are characterized by their large diameter and shallow depth, while tube wells are narrow diameter and can be shallow or deep.
*   Tube wells are crucial for tapping deeper and often higher-quality aquifers.
*   The classification of tube wells (shallow/deep, artesian/non-artesian, staggered/straight) is based on their depth, water level, and construction.
*   The choice of well type depends on a careful assessment of aquifer characteristics, water requirements, and economic factors.
*   Proper well construction, including casing and screening, is vital for longevity and efficient operation.

**Question 2:**
What is the primary purpose of a "staggered tube well," and in what type of aquifer would it be most effective?

**Answer:**
The primary purpose of a staggered tube well is to maximize water withdrawal from an aquifer that consists of multiple, distinct water-bearing strata separated by less permeable layers. It would be most effective in a heterogeneous aquifer where permeable zones are discontinuous with depth.

**Question 3:**
Explain the concept of an "artesian tube well." What is the difference between a flowing and a non-flowing artesian well?

**Answer:**
An artesian tube well taps a confined aquifer where the groundwater is under pressure. This pressure is due to the recharge of the aquifer at a higher elevation.

*   **Flowing Artesian Well:** The piezometric level (the level to which water would rise in the well due to aquifer pressure) is above the ground surface. This causes water to flow out of the well naturally without the need for a pump.
*   **Non-Flowing Artesian Well:** The piezometric level is below the ground surface but still above the top of the aquifer. Water will rise in the wellbore due to pressure, but a pump is required to lift it to the surface.

**Question 4 (Conceptual Exercise):**
You are tasked with designing a well for irrigation in a rural area. The water table is at 15 meters, and you expect to need a yield of about 15 LPS. The aquifer is a uniform sand formation. Discuss what type of well you would most likely recommend and why.

**Answer:**
Considering the water table at 15 meters and the required yield of 15 LPS from a uniform sand formation, a **shallow tube well** would be the most suitable recommendation.

*   **Why a Tube Well?** The yield requirement of 15 LPS suggests a need for more efficient extraction than a typical dug well might provide, especially over sustained periods. Tube wells are generally designed for higher yields and more consistent delivery.
*   **Why Shallow?** The water table being at 15 meters indicates that a shallow aquifer can meet the demand. A shallow tube well can effectively tap this resource.
*   **Why Uniform Sand Formation?** A uniform sand formation is well-suited for a tube well equipped with a strainer. A straight-section strainer would likely be sufficient.
*   **Pumping:** Since the water level is at 15 meters, a surface centrifugal pump would be a practical choice for lifting the water.

A dug well could be an option if the yield requirement was lower and surface contamination risks were manageable, but for 15 LPS, a tube well offers better performance and reliability.
