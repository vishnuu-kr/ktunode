# HYDROLOGY AND WATER RESOURCES ENGINEERING

## Module 3: Irrigation – Necessity, Benefits and Ill Effects

### Topic: Soil-Water-Plant Relationships

---

### **Introduction**

Understanding the intricate relationship between soil, water, and plants is fundamental to effective irrigation. This topic delves into how plants extract water from the soil, the forces that govern this process, and the critical water content levels that influence plant growth and survival. This knowledge is essential for optimizing irrigation schedules, preventing water stress, and ensuring sustainable agricultural practices.

---

### **Learning Outcomes Covered:**

By the end of this topic, you will be able to:

*   **Define and explain the key components of the soil-water-plant continuum:** Soil texture, soil structure, soil water, and plant roots.
*   **Describe the different forms of water present in the soil:** Gravitational water, capillary water, and hygroscopic water.
*   **Explain the concepts of soil moisture tension and soil water potential:** Define and differentiate between them.
*   **Identify and define key soil moisture constants:** Field capacity, permanent wilting point, and available soil moisture.
*   **Describe the process of water absorption by plant roots:** Osmosis and its role in water uptake.
*   **Discuss the factors affecting soil-water uptake by plants:** Soil properties, environmental factors, and plant characteristics.
*   **Explain the concept of evapotranspiration:** Define transpiration and evaporation and their combined effect.
*   **Discuss the importance of soil moisture monitoring for irrigation management.**

### **1. Key Concepts and Definitions**

#### **1.1 Soil**

*   **Definition:** Soil is a complex mixture of mineral particles (sand, silt, clay), organic matter, water, air, and living organisms. It acts as a reservoir and a medium for water and nutrient transport to plants.

*   **Soil Texture:**
    *   **Definition:** The relative proportion of sand, silt, and clay particles in a soil.
    *   **Importance:** Affects soil's water-holding capacity, drainage, aeration, and workability.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Sandy soils:** Large particles, good drainage, low water-holding capacity.
        *   **Clay soils:** Small particles, poor drainage, high water-holding capacity.
        *   **Loam soils:** Balanced mixture, generally ideal for agriculture.

*   **Soil Structure:**
    *   **Definition:** The arrangement and aggregation of soil particles into larger units called peds.
    *   **Importance:** Influences pore space, aeration, water infiltration, and root penetration.
    *   **Good structure:** Crumbly, well-aggregated, with good pore connectivity.
    *   **Poor structure:** Compacted, massive, hindering water movement and root growth.

#### **1.2 Soil Water**

*   **Definition:** Water held within the soil pores, available for plant uptake.

*   **Forms of Soil Water:**
    *   **Gravitational Water (Free Water):**
        *   **Description:** Water held by gravity in the macropores of the soil.
        *   **Availability to Plants:** Not available to plants; it drains away readily.
        *   **Occurrence:** Present immediately after heavy rainfall or irrigation.
    *   **Capillary Water:**
        *   **Description:** Water held in the micropores of the soil by adhesive and cohesive forces (surface tension).
        *   **Availability to Plants:** This is the **primary source of water for plants**.
        *   **Movement:** Moves from areas of higher moisture content to lower moisture content.
    *   **Hygroscopic Water:**
        *   **Description:** Water molecules tightly bound to soil particles by molecular forces.
        *   **Availability to Plants:** Not available to plants; even the most vigorous roots cannot extract it.
        *   **Occurrence:** Adsorbed onto the surface of soil particles when the soil is very dry.

#### **1.3 Plant Roots**

*   **Definition:** The underground part of a plant, responsible for anchoring the plant, absorbing water and nutrients from the soil, and sometimes storing food.
*   **Role in Water Uptake:** Roots create a large surface area for absorption. Root hairs are specialized epidermal cells that significantly increase the absorptive surface area.

### **2. Soil Moisture Tension and Soil Water Potential**

These terms describe the "pull" or energy state of water in the soil, indicating how difficult it is for plants to extract it.

#### **2.1 Soil Moisture Tension (Soil Water Suction)**

*   **Definition:** The negative pressure or suction exerted by the soil particles on the water held in the pores. It quantifies the force holding water in the soil.
*   **Measurement:** Expressed in units of pressure, typically kilopascals (kPa) or centibars (cb).
*   **Relationship with Moisture Content:** As soil dries out, soil moisture tension increases (becomes more negative).

#### **2.2 Soil Water Potential ($\Psi_s$)**

*   **Definition:** A thermodynamic term representing the potential energy of water in the soil relative to pure water at atmospheric pressure. It describes the tendency of water to move.
*   **Components:**
    *   **Matric Potential ($\Psi_m$):** Energy associated with the attraction of soil particles for water (adsorption). This is the dominant component of soil water potential.
    *   **Osmotic Potential ($\Psi_o$):** Energy associated with dissolved solutes in soil water. It's usually negative, reducing the water potential.
    *   **Gravitational Potential ($\Psi_g$):** Energy associated with the height of water in the soil profile.
    *   **Pressure Potential ($\Psi_p$):** Energy associated with pressure applied to the soil water.
*   **Formula:** $\Psi_s = \Psi_m + \Psi_o + \Psi_g + \Psi_p$
*   **Water Movement:** Water moves from a region of higher (less negative) water potential to a region of lower (more negative) water potential.

**Key Relationship:** Soil moisture tension is essentially the negative of the matric potential ($\text{Tension} = -\Psi_m$).

### **3. Key Soil Moisture Constants**

These are critical thresholds of soil water content that define the availability of water to plants.

#### **3.1 Saturation Point**

*   **Definition:** The condition where all soil pores are filled with water.
*   **Soil Moisture Tension:** Usually 0 kPa (at atmospheric pressure).
*   **Availability to Plants:** Water is available, but aeration is poor, which can be detrimental to root respiration.

#### **3.2 Field Capacity (FC)**

*   **Definition:** The maximum amount of water a soil can hold against the force of gravity after free gravitational water has drained away. It represents the water content at which the soil is still well-aerated but holds a significant amount of capillary water.
*   **Soil Moisture Tension:** Typically around -10 to -33 kPa (-0.1 to -0.33 bar). The exact value varies with soil texture.
*   **Availability to Plants:** Water is readily available to plants.

#### **3.3 Permanent Wilting Point (PWP)**

*   **Definition:** The soil moisture content at which plants can no longer extract enough water to prevent permanent wilting. At this point, the soil moisture tension is so high that plant roots cannot overcome it.
*   **Soil Moisture Tension:** Typically around -1500 kPa (-15 bar).
*   **Availability to Plants:** Water is essentially unavailable to plants.

#### **3.4 Available Soil Moisture (ASM)**

*   **Definition:** The difference between the water content at Field Capacity and the water content at Permanent Wilting Point. This is the water that plants can effectively use.
*   **Formula:** $ASM = FC - PWP$
*   **Availability to Plants:** This is the water reservoir that plants draw from.

#### **3.5 Unavailable Soil Moisture**

*   **Definition:** The water held in the soil at tensions greater than the Permanent Wilting Point (hygroscopic water) and the water held by gravity that drains away (gravitational water).

#### **3.6 Readily Available Soil Moisture (RASM)**

*   **Definition:** A portion of the available soil moisture that plants can extract with minimal stress. Plants generally start experiencing stress when soil moisture drops below a certain fraction of the available moisture (e.g., 50-75% of ASM).
*   **Importance:** Irrigation should ideally occur before the soil moisture drops into the "stress zone" (below RASM).

**Example:**
A loam soil has:
*   Field Capacity (FC) = 25% by weight
*   Permanent Wilting Point (PWP) = 10% by weight

Then, Available Soil Moisture (ASM) = 25% - 10% = 15% by weight.
If the Readily Available Soil Moisture (RASM) is considered 50% of ASM, then plants will start experiencing stress when the soil moisture content drops below 10% + (0.50 * 15%) = 17.5% by weight.

### **4. Plant Water Absorption**

#### **4.1 Process of Water Absorption**

*   **Osmosis:** The primary mechanism by which water enters plant root cells.
    *   **Definition:** The movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential (e.g., soil water) to a region of lower water potential (e.g., root xylem).
    *   **Role of Solutes:** Root cells contain dissolved solutes (sugars, salts), which create a lower (more negative) water potential inside the cells compared to the soil solution, driving water uptake.

*   **Root Pressure:**
    *   **Description:** A positive pressure generated in the root xylem due to the active transport of mineral ions into the xylem, which lowers the water potential within the xylem.
    *   **Role:** Primarily responsible for pushing water up the xylem in the absence of transpiration (e.g., at night) and contributes to guttation.

*   **Transpiration Pull:**
    *   **Description:** The most significant force driving water movement in tall plants. The evaporation of water from leaf surfaces (transpiration) creates a negative pressure (tension) in the xylem, which pulls water up from the roots. This is a continuous process driven by the water potential gradient from soil to atmosphere.

#### **4.2 Factors Affecting Soil-Water Uptake by Plants**

*   **Soil Properties:**
    *   **Soil Moisture Content:** Higher moisture content generally leads to higher uptake rate.
    *   **Soil Water Tension:** As tension increases, uptake rate decreases.
    *   **Soil Texture and Structure:** Affect aeration and root penetration, influencing the ability to access water.
    *   **Soil Temperature:** Low temperatures can reduce root activity and water uptake.
    *   **Soil Aeration:** Roots require oxygen for respiration, which is essential for active water and nutrient uptake. Waterlogged soils with poor aeration inhibit uptake.

*   **Environmental Factors:**
    *   **Atmospheric Humidity:** Lower humidity increases the rate of transpiration, thus increasing the water demand.
    *   **Temperature:** Higher temperatures increase transpiration and soil evaporation.
    *   **Wind:** Can increase transpiration by removing humid air from the leaf surface.
    *   **Solar Radiation:** Drives photosynthesis and transpiration.

*   **Plant Characteristics:**
    *   **Root System Depth and Distribution:** Deeper and more extensive root systems allow access to more soil moisture.
    *   **Root Efficiency:** The ability of roots to absorb water.
    *   **Plant Species and Variety:** Different plants have varying water requirements and tolerance to water stress.
    *   **Plant Growth Stage:** Water demand is higher during active growth and reproductive stages.
    *   **Leaf Area:** Larger leaf area generally leads to higher transpiration and water demand.

---

### **5. Evapotranspiration (ET)**

*   **Definition:** The sum of evaporation from the soil surface and transpiration from plant leaves. It represents the total water loss from the land surface and vegetation to the atmosphere.

*   **Evaporation:**
    *   **Description:** The process by which liquid water changes into water vapor and escapes into the atmosphere from the soil surface.
    *   **Factors influencing Evaporation:** Soil moisture content, solar radiation, wind speed, and air humidity.

*   **Transpiration:**
    *   **Description:** The process by which water is absorbed by plant roots, transported through the plant, and evaporated from the surfaces of leaves, primarily through stomata.
    *   **Factors influencing Transpiration:** Stomatal opening and closing, leaf temperature, solar radiation, humidity, and wind.

*   **Importance for Irrigation:** Evapotranspiration is a key component in determining crop water requirements. Irrigation scheduling aims to replenish the water lost through ET to maintain optimal soil moisture levels.

---

### **6. Importance of Soil Moisture Monitoring for Irrigation Management**

*   **Preventing Water Stress:** Monitoring helps ensure plants have access to sufficient water, especially during critical growth stages, thereby preventing yield loss due to drought stress.
*   **Avoiding Waterlogging:** By knowing when the soil is reaching field capacity, over-irrigation can be prevented, which is crucial for maintaining soil aeration and preventing root diseases.
*   **Optimizing Water Use Efficiency:** Irrigating only when necessary based on soil moisture levels (rather than a fixed schedule) conserves water and reduces energy costs.
*   **Improving Crop Yield and Quality:** Consistent water availability leads to healthier plants and better quality produce.
*   **Informing Irrigation Scheduling:** Soil moisture data is a direct indicator of when and how much to irrigate.

**Methods of Soil Moisture Monitoring:**

*   **Feel Method:** A qualitative assessment of soil moisture by touch.
*   **Gravimetric Method:** Measuring the weight of soil samples before and after drying to determine water content.
*   **Volumetric Method:** Using instruments to estimate the volume of water in the soil.
*   **Tensiometers:** Measure soil moisture tension.
*   **Electrical Resistance Blocks (Gypsum Blocks):** Measure soil moisture tension based on electrical resistance.
*   **Capacitance/Dielectric Sensors (e.g., TDR, FDR):** Measure the dielectric properties of the soil, which are related to its water content.

### **Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
Define Field Capacity and Permanent Wilting Point. Explain why the water content between these two points is considered "available soil moisture."

**Answer 1:**
*   **Field Capacity (FC):** The maximum amount of water a soil can hold against gravity after free drainage. It's the point where capillary water is retained in micropores, readily available to plants.
*   **Permanent Wilting Point (PWP):** The soil moisture content at which plants can no longer absorb enough water to prevent wilting, as the soil moisture tension becomes too high for root uptake.
*   **Available Soil Moisture (ASM):** The difference (FC - PWP) represents the reservoir of water that plants can effectively extract for their physiological processes. This is the usable water in the soil profile.

**Question 2:**
Differentiate between Gravitational Water and Capillary Water in terms of their availability to plants and the forces holding them in the soil.

**Answer 2:**
*   **Gravitational Water:** Held in macropores, readily drains due to gravity. **Not available to plants**.
*   **Capillary Water:** Held in micropores by adhesive and cohesive forces (surface tension). **This is the primary water available to plants**. It moves from wetter to drier zones.

**Question 3:**
A sandy loam soil has a field capacity of 18% and a permanent wilting point of 7% (by weight). If the bulk density of the soil is 1.4 g/cm³, and you want to irrigate when the soil moisture has dropped to 40% of the available soil moisture, calculate:
a) The total available soil moisture in percentage.
b) The moisture content (in percentage) at which you should irrigate.
c) The depth of water to be applied per meter of soil depth if the target is to bring the soil moisture back to field capacity from the irrigation trigger point.

**Answer 3:**
a) **Total Available Soil Moisture (ASM):**
   ASM = FC - PWP = 18% - 7% = 11% (by weight)

b) **Moisture content at which to irrigate:**
   Water already depleted = 100% - 40% = 60% of ASM
   Water remaining at irrigation trigger point = 40% of ASM = 0.40 * 11% = 4.4% (of dry weight)
   Moisture content at irrigation trigger point = PWP + 4.4% = 7% + 4.4% = 11.4% (by weight)

c) **Depth of water to apply per meter of soil depth:**
   The amount of water to be added is the difference between FC and the moisture content at the irrigation trigger point.
   Water to be added = FC - (Moisture content at trigger point) = 18% - 11.4% = 6.6% (by weight)

   To convert this to volume and depth:
   Volumetric Water Content = Gravimetric Water Content * Bulk Density
   Volumetric Water Content = 6.6% * 1.4 g/cm³ = 0.066 * 1.4 = 0.0924 cm³/cm³

   This means for every cm³ of soil, 0.0924 cm³ of water is needed.
   So, for 1 meter (100 cm) depth of soil:
   Depth of water to apply = Volumetric Water Content * Soil Depth
   Depth of water to apply = 0.0924 cm/cm * 100 cm = 9.24 cm

   Therefore, 9.24 cm of water needs to be applied per meter of soil depth.

**Question 4:**
What is evapotranspiration, and why is it a crucial factor in irrigation planning?

**Answer 4:**
**Evapotranspiration (ET)** is the combined process of evaporation of water from the soil surface and transpiration of water from plant leaves to the atmosphere. It represents the total water lost from the land surface and vegetation.

**Crucial Factor in Irrigation Planning:** ET dictates the water requirements of crops. By estimating the ET rate, farmers can determine how much water their crops need and when to irrigate to replenish the lost moisture, ensuring optimal plant growth and preventing water stress. Accurate ET estimation helps in efficient water management and conservation.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Soil texture** dictates water-holding capacity and drainage.
*   **Capillary water** is the primary source of water for plants.
*   **Soil moisture tension** and **water potential** indicate the energy status of soil water and its availability.
*   **Field Capacity (FC)** is the upper limit of readily available water.
*   **Permanent Wilting Point (PWP)** is the lower limit of water availability for plants.
*   **Available Soil Moisture (ASM = FC - PWP)** is the total water plants can use.
*   **Osmosis, root pressure, and transpiration pull** are key mechanisms for water uptake.
*   **Evapotranspiration (ET)** represents total water loss and is essential for calculating crop water needs.
*   **Monitoring soil moisture** is vital for efficient and effective irrigation.
