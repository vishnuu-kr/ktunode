# Environmental Health and Safety: Module 3 - Safety in the Construction Industry

## Topic: Control Measures to Reduce Risk

---

### **Introduction**

The construction industry inherently involves a high degree of risk due to the nature of the work, involving heavy machinery, hazardous materials, working at heights, and dynamic site conditions. Effective risk management through the implementation of appropriate control measures is paramount to preventing accidents, injuries, illnesses, and fatalities. This module focuses on understanding and applying various control measures to mitigate these risks.

---

### **Learning Outcomes Covered**

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Identify common hazards in the construction industry.
*   Explain the hierarchy of controls and its application in construction.
*   Describe various types of control measures for different construction hazards.
*   Discuss the importance of planning, training, and supervision in implementing control measures.
*   Evaluate the effectiveness of implemented control measures.

---

### **Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Hazard:** A potential source of harm or damage to people, property, or the environment.
*   **Risk:** The likelihood that a hazard will cause harm and the severity of that harm.
    *   *Risk = Likelihood x Severity*
*   **Risk Assessment:** The process of identifying hazards, analyzing and evaluating the risks associated with those hazards, and then determining appropriate ways to eliminate or control the risks.
*   **Control Measure:** An action or a set of actions taken to eliminate or reduce a risk to an acceptable level.
*   **Hierarchy of Controls:** A framework used to systematically identify and implement the most effective methods for controlling hazards. It prioritizes control measures from most effective to least effective.

---

### **The Hierarchy of Controls in Construction**

The hierarchy of controls provides a systematic approach to risk management, emphasizing the most effective methods first.

1.  **Elimination:** Physically removing the hazard.
    *   **Definition:** The most effective control measure, involving the complete removal of the hazard from the workplace.
    *   **Construction Example:** Redesigning a building to avoid the need for working at heights, or using pre-fabricated components to reduce on-site assembly risks.
2.  **Substitution:** Replacing the hazard with a less hazardous alternative.
    *   **Definition:** Replacing a dangerous substance, process, or equipment with a safer one.
    *   **Construction Example:** Using water-based paints instead of solvent-based paints to reduce exposure to volatile organic compounds (VOCs) or replacing manual lifting of heavy materials with mechanical lifting aids.
3.  **Engineering Controls:** Isolating people from the hazard or guarding the hazard.
    *   **Definition:** Designing or modifying workplaces, equipment, or processes to reduce exposure to hazards. These controls are typically physical barriers or modifications.
    *   **Construction Examples:**
        *   **Guardrails and Safety Nets:** For working at heights.
        *   **Ventilation Systems:** To remove dust, fumes, or vapors.
        *   **Machine Guarding:** Enclosing moving parts of machinery.
        *   **Dust Suppression Systems:** Using water to control airborne dust on site.
        *   **Scaffolding with Edge Protection:** To prevent falls.
4.  **Administrative Controls:** Changing the way people work.
    *   **Definition:** Implementing procedures, policies, training, and work practices to reduce exposure to hazards.
    *   **Construction Examples:**
        *   **Safe Work Procedures (SWPs) / Method Statements:** Detailed instructions on how to perform a task safely.
        *   **Job Safety Analysis (JSA):** Breaking down a job into steps, identifying hazards, and implementing controls for each step.
        *   **Training and Education:** On hazard recognition, safe work practices, and emergency procedures.
        *   **Permit-to-Work Systems:** For high-risk activities like hot work or confined space entry.
        *   **Signage and Warning Labels:** To alert workers to hazards.
        *   **Good Housekeeping:** Keeping the work area clean and organized to prevent trips and falls.
        *   **Job Rotation:** To limit exposure duration to certain hazards.
5.  **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Protecting the worker with equipment.
    *   **Definition:** Equipment worn by workers to minimize exposure to specific hazards. It is considered the last line of defense.
    *   **Construction Examples:**
        *   **Hard Hats:** To protect from falling objects.
        *   **Safety Glasses/Goggles:** To protect eyes from debris and chemical splashes.
        *   **Safety Boots (Steel-Toe):** To protect feet from falling objects and punctures.
        *   **Hearing Protection (Earplugs/Earmuffs):** To protect against noise-induced hearing loss.
        *   **Gloves:** To protect hands from cuts, abrasions, chemicals, and vibration.
        *   **Respiratory Protection (Respirators/Masks):** To protect against dust, fumes, and vapors.
        *   **Fall Arrest Systems (Harnesses, Lanyards):** For working at heights.
        *   **High-Visibility Clothing:** To ensure workers are visible to machinery and vehicles.

### **Control Measures for Common Construction Hazards**

This section elaborates on control measures for specific common hazards found in construction:

#### **1. Working at Heights (Falls)**

*   **Hazards:** Falls from scaffolding, ladders, roofs, open edges, and through fragile surfaces.
*   **Control Measures (following Hierarchy):**
    *   **Elimination/Substitution:** Design buildings to minimize work at height where possible (e.g., using pre-fabricated balconies).
    *   **Engineering Controls:**
        *   Guardrails and toe boards on scaffolding and elevated platforms.
        *   Safety nets.
        *   Secure scaffolding and platforms.
        *   Guard openings in floors and walls.
        *   Guard fragile surfaces.
    *   **Administrative Controls:**
        *   Fall protection plans.
        *   Regular inspection of access equipment (ladders, scaffolding).
        *   Training on safe ladder use and scaffolding erection.
        *   Permit-to-work for working at height.
        *   Signage warning of fall hazards.
    *   **PPE:**
        *   Full-body safety harnesses.
        *   Shock-absorbing lanyards and retractable lifelines.
        *   Anchorage points.

#### **2. Excavations and Trenching**

*   **Hazards:** Cave-ins, falls into excavations, striking underground services, hazardous atmospheres.
*   **Control Measures:**
    *   **Elimination/Substitution:** Avoid deep excavations if possible, or use pre-cast sections.
    *   **Engineering Controls:**
        *   **Shoring:** Using timber or metal supports to prevent trench walls from collapsing.
        *   **Shielding/Trench Boxes:** Providing a protective cage for workers.
        *   **Sloping/Benching:** Cutting back the trench walls at a safe angle.
        *   Barriers and fencing around excavations.
    *   **Administrative Controls:**
        *   Competent person to oversee excavations.
        *   Daily inspection of trenches before work begins.
        *   Safe means of entry and exit (ladders, ramps).
        *   Checking for underground utilities before digging.
        *   Adequate ventilation for deep excavations.
        *   Spoil piles kept at least 2 feet from the edge.
    *   **PPE:** Hard hats, safety boots, high-visibility clothing.

#### **3. Electrical Hazards**

*   **Hazards:** Electric shock, burns, electrocution, fires from electrical faults.
*   **Control Measures:**
    *   **Elimination/Substitution:** Use battery-powered tools where feasible.
    *   **Engineering Controls:**
        *   Double insulation on electrical tools.
        *   Ground Fault Circuit Interrupters (GFCIs) or Residual Current Devices (RCDs).
        *   Properly grounded equipment.
        *   Temporary power distribution protected by circuit breakers.
        *   Cable management to prevent damage.
    *   **Administrative Controls:**
        *   **Lockout/Tagout (LOTO):** Procedures to de-energize equipment before maintenance.
        *   Qualified electricians to perform electrical work.
        *   Regular inspection of electrical cords and equipment.
        *   Safe distances from overhead power lines.
        *   Permit-to-work for electrical work.
        *   Training on electrical safety.
    *   **PPE:** Insulated gloves, rubber-soled footwear, non-conductive tools.

#### **4. Material Handling and Ergonomics**

*   **Hazards:** Musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) from manual lifting, repetitive motions, awkward postures; injuries from dropped materials.
*   **Control Measures:**
    *   **Elimination/Substitution:** Automate tasks where possible (e.g., using cranes, forklifts).
    *   **Engineering Controls:**
        *   Use of mechanical aids (hoists, conveyors, pallet jacks, forklifts).
        *   Adjustable workbenches and material stands.
        *   Ergonomically designed tools.
    *   **Administrative Controls:**
        *   Training on proper lifting techniques (bend knees, keep back straight).
        *   Reducing the weight of loads.
        *   Providing assistance for heavy lifting.
        *   Job rotation to prevent repetitive strain.
        *   Good housekeeping to prevent tripping hazards.
        *   Providing carts or dollies for moving materials.
    *   **PPE:** Gloves for grip and protection.

#### **5. Hazardous Substances (Chemicals, Dusts, Fumes)**

*   **Hazards:** Inhalation of dust (silica, wood dust), exposure to solvents, paints, adhesives, asbestos, lead, welding fumes; skin contact and ingestion.
*   **Control Measures:**
    *   **Elimination/Substitution:** Use low-VOC paints, water-based adhesives, or less hazardous materials.
    *   **Engineering Controls:**
        *   Local Exhaust Ventilation (LEV) at the source of dust or fume generation.
        *   General ventilation to dilute airborne contaminants.
        *   Enclosing processes that generate dust or fumes.
        *   Dust suppression techniques (e.g., wet cutting).
    *   **Administrative Controls:**
        *   **Safe Work Procedures (SWPs)** for handling hazardous substances.
        *   Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS)/Safety Data Sheets (SDS) readily available and understood.
        *   Training on chemical hazards and safe handling.
        *   Good hygiene practices (washing hands before eating/drinking).
        *   Designated areas for hazardous work.
        *   Waste disposal procedures.
        *   Health surveillance and monitoring.
    *   **PPE:**
        *   Appropriate respirators (e.g., N95 for dust, cartridge respirators for fumes).
        *   Chemical-resistant gloves.
        *   Safety goggles or face shields.
        *   Protective clothing.

#### **6. Noise**

*   **Hazards:** Hearing loss due to exposure to loud machinery (jackhammers, concrete saws, heavy equipment).
*   **Control Measures:**
    *   **Elimination/Substitution:** Use quieter tools or methods where possible.
    *   **Engineering Controls:**
        *   Noise enclosures for machinery.
        *   Using dampening materials.
        *   Regular maintenance of machinery to reduce operational noise.
    *   **Administrative Controls:**
        *   Implementing a hearing conservation program.
        *   Limiting exposure time to noisy environments.
        *   Creating quiet rest areas.
        *   Training on noise hazards and PPE use.
        *   Noise monitoring.
    *   **PPE:** Earplugs or earmuffs.

### **Importance of Planning, Training, and Supervision**

*   **Planning:**
    *   **Risk Assessments:** Conducted before any work commences to identify hazards and plan appropriate control measures.
    *   **Method Statements/SWPs:** Detailed plans for how tasks will be carried out safely.
    *   **Site Safety Plans:** Overall safety management for the entire project.
    *   **Emergency Preparedness Plans:** For incidents like fires, medical emergencies, or structural collapses.
*   **Training:**
    *   **Induction Training:** For all new workers on site, covering general safety rules and site-specific hazards.
    *   **Task-Specific Training:** For specific tasks like operating machinery, working at heights, or handling hazardous materials.
    *   **Toolbox Talks/Briefings:** Regular short safety discussions on site.
    *   **Competency Training:** Ensuring workers have the skills and knowledge to perform tasks safely.
*   **Supervision:**
    *   **Competent Supervision:** Supervisors must be knowledgeable about safety procedures and capable of enforcing them.
    *   **Monitoring Work:** Regularly observing workers to ensure they are following safety procedures and using controls correctly.
    *   **Intervention:** Promptly addressing unsafe behaviors or conditions.
    *   **Reinforcement:** Providing positive feedback for safe practices.

---

### **Evaluating the Effectiveness of Control Measures**

It is crucial to regularly assess whether the implemented control measures are effectively reducing risks. This can be done through:

*   **Incident/Accident Investigation:** Analyzing what went wrong and if control measures failed.
*   **Near Miss Reporting:** Investigating near misses to identify potential failures before they cause harm.
*   **Audits and Inspections:** Regular checks of work practices, equipment, and the work environment.
*   **Worker Feedback:** Encouraging workers to report concerns and suggestions regarding safety.
*   **Health Surveillance:** Monitoring workers' health for signs of exposure-related illnesses.
*   **Reviewing Risk Assessments:** Periodically updating risk assessments to reflect changes in work methods, materials, or site conditions.

---

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Risk assessment is the foundation** of all safety measures.
*   **The hierarchy of controls is paramount:** Always aim for elimination and substitution before relying on PPE.
*   **Control measures must be suitable, sufficient, and properly maintained.**
*   **Continuous improvement** is key in safety management.
*   **Worker involvement** is essential for effective safety.
*   **The employer has a legal and moral responsibility** to provide a safe working environment.
*   **Never compromise on safety for speed or cost.**

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

1.  **Hierarchy of Controls Examples:**
    *   **Elimination:** Redesigning a structure to avoid needing to work at height.
    *   **Substitution:** Using a less toxic solvent-based paint.
    *   **Engineering Controls:** Installing guardrails on scaffolding.
    *   **Administrative Controls:** Implementing a Permit-to-Work system for hot work.
    *   **PPE:** Requiring workers to wear safety glasses.

2.  **Trips and Slips Control Measures:**
    *   **Hazards:** Unsecured cables, uneven surfaces, poor lighting, spills, debris, trailing hoses.
    *   **Control Measures:**
        *   **Engineering:** Improving site lighting, installing cable ramps or covers.
        *   **Administrative:** Implementing good housekeeping practices (daily clean-ups), clear pathways, regular site inspections, providing adequate waste bins, using warning signs for wet floors.
        *   **PPE:** Requiring workers to wear safety boots with good tread.

3.  **Lockout/Tagout (LOTO):** The purpose of LOTO is to ensure that machinery and equipment are properly shut off and de-energized before maintenance or servicing work is performed. This prevents the accidental startup of machinery, which could lead to serious injury or death. It involves attaching a lock to an energy-isolating device and tagging it with information about the worker performing the maintenance, preventing anyone else from operating it.

4.  **Excavation Risks and Prevention:**
    *   **Primary Risk:** Trench collapse (cave-in), which can bury workers, leading to suffocation, crushing injuries, or fatalities.
    *   **Prevention Methods:**
        *   **Shoring:** Installing supports to prevent wall collapse.
        *   **Shielding (Trench Boxes):** Using pre-fabricated structures to protect workers within the trench.
        *   **Sloping/Benching:** Cutting back the trench walls to a safe angle.

5.  **PPE as Last Line of Defense:** PPE is the last line of defense because it protects the individual worker from hazards but does not eliminate or control the hazard at its source. If PPE fails, is worn incorrectly, or is not maintained, the worker is still exposed. The aim is to control hazards through elimination, substitution, engineering, and administrative controls first.
    *   **Common PPE:** Hard hats, safety glasses, earplugs/earmuffs, gloves, safety boots, high-visibility vests, respirators, safety harnesses.
