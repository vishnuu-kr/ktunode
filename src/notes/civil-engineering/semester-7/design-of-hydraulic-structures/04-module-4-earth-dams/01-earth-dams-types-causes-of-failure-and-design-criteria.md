## Design of Hydraulic Structures: Module 4 - Earth Dams

### Topic: Earth Dams - Types, Causes of Failure, and Design Criteria

---

### 1. Introduction to Earth Dams

**Definition:** An earth dam is a type of dam constructed primarily from compacted earth, soil, and rock. They are typically built across rivers or streams to create reservoirs for water supply, irrigation, flood control, and hydropower generation.

**Key Characteristics:**

*   **Material:** Composed of natural earth materials like soil, sand, gravel, and clay.
*   **Construction:** Built by placing and compacting earthen materials in layers.
*   **Cross-section:** Generally have a trapezoidal cross-section with a wide base for stability and a gentler upstream and downstream slope.
*   **Water-retaining element:** The core or the entire dam mass is designed to be relatively impervious to water.

**Advantages of Earth Dams:**

*   Can be constructed in locations where suitable rock material for concrete dams is scarce.
*   Adaptable to varying foundation conditions.
*   Relatively lower construction costs compared to concrete dams in many situations.
*   Can be built with locally available materials.
*   Good for flood control due to their large storage capacity and inherent flexibility.

**Disadvantages of Earth Dams:**

*   Require large volumes of material, potentially leading to significant land acquisition.
*   Susceptible to erosion by wave action and surface runoff.
*   Require careful compaction and quality control during construction.
*   Potential for seepage issues if not properly designed and constructed.
*   Can be vulnerable to internal erosion and piping failures.

### 2. Types of Earth Dams

Earth dams are classified based on their cross-sectional composition, particularly the arrangement of impervious and pervious materials.

#### 2.1 Homogeneous Earth Dams

*   **Description:** Constructed entirely of a single type of relatively impervious material (e.g., clayey soil). The entire cross-section is homogeneous.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Simpler construction.
    *   Requires a readily available supply of uniform impervious material.
    *   Relies on the low permeability of the entire dam body to prevent excessive seepage.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Simple to construct.
    *   Less prone to internal erosion if the material is well-graded and compacted.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Can be susceptible to seepage if not thick enough.
    *   Upstream slope needs protection against wave action.
*   **Example:** Many older earth dams were built as homogeneous dams.

#### 2.2 Zoned Earth Dams

*   **Description:** Consist of different zones of selected materials with varying properties. Typically includes a central impervious core and outer pervious zones (filters and shoulders).
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   **Impervious Core:** Usually made of clayey or silty soils with low permeability. Located at the center or slightly off-center.
    *   **Filter Zones:** Placed immediately adjacent to the impervious core. These are coarser materials (e.g., sand and gravel) that prevent the finer core material from being washed out by seepage. They also transition the flow from the core to the pervious shoulders.
    *   **Pervious Shells/Shoulders:** Made of coarser materials like gravel and sand. They provide stability to the dam and allow seepage to drain freely, reducing pore water pressure within the dam.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   More efficient in controlling seepage.
    *   Improved stability due to the use of heavier, coarser materials in the outer zones.
    *   Can utilize a wider range of borrow materials.
    *   More resistant to internal erosion.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   More complex construction requiring careful selection and placement of different materials.
    *   Requires more detailed geological investigation to identify suitable materials.
*   **Example:** Most modern large earth dams are zoned dams, like the Tarbela Dam in Pakistan.

#### 2.3 Diaphragm or Core Wall Type Earth Dams

*   **Description:** Feature a continuous, thin, impermeable barrier (diaphragm) within the dam cross-section, typically made of clay or concrete/asphalt. The bulk of the dam is made of pervious materials.
*   **Types of Diaphragm:**
    *   **Earth Diaphragm:** A thick core of compacted clay.
    *   **Concrete Diaphragm:** A concrete wall extending from the foundation to the crest.
    *   **Asphaltic Concrete Diaphragm:** A flexible asphaltic concrete wall.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   The impervious diaphragm is the primary water barrier.
    *   The outer shell provides the bulk and stability.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Can be built with readily available pervious materials for the main embankment.
    *   Effective in controlling seepage, especially when a robust diaphragm is used.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   The diaphragm must be continuous and well-jointed to be effective.
    *   Potential for leakage through joints in concrete or asphalt diaphragms.
    *   Requires careful construction to ensure the diaphragm is properly embedded in the foundation.
*   **Example:** Dams with a clay core wall extending through the entire embankment.

### 3. Causes of Failure in Earth Dams

Earth dams can fail due to various reasons, often related to hydraulic forces, internal instability, or external factors. Understanding these causes is crucial for proper design and safety.

#### 3.1 Seepage and Piping Failure (Internal Erosion)

*   **Description:** Seepage is the movement of water through the porous material of the dam and its foundation. Piping occurs when the seepage flow velocity is high enough to dislodge soil particles from the embankment or foundation, creating channels (pipes) through which water flows. This process can lead to the progressive collapse of the dam.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **High hydraulic gradient:** Caused by a large difference in water levels between the reservoir and downstream.
    *   **Lack of adequate filters/drainage:** Absence of filter layers that gradually transition from fine to coarse materials to reduce flow velocity and retain fine particles.
    *   **Presence of highly permeable layers in the foundation:** Water finds an easy path through permeable strata, leading to high seepage forces.
    *   **Cracks or fissures in the dam body or foundation:** These provide preferential paths for water flow.
    *   **Poor compaction:** Leads to higher permeability.
*   **Consequences:** Progressive erosion of the dam material, eventual breach.
*   **Prevention:** Proper filter design, adequate drainage, ensuring continuity of the impervious core, proper foundation treatment.

#### 3.2 Slope Instability and Landslides

*   **Description:** The slopes of the earth dam can become unstable due to the weight of the embankment, the pressure of water in the reservoir, and pore water pressure within the soil. If the shear stresses exceed the shear strength of the soil, a slope failure (landslide) occurs.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Steep slopes:** Exceeding the natural angle of repose for the soil.
    *   **Rapid drawdown:** When the reservoir level is lowered quickly, the saturated soil on the upstream slope may not have time to drain, leading to increased pore water pressure and reduced shear strength.
    *   **High pore water pressure:** Due to poor drainage or infiltration.
    *   **Earthquakes (Seismic Instability):** Ground shaking can liquefy saturated granular soils or reduce the shear strength of cohesive soils.
    *   **Erosion of toe:** The downstream toe can be eroded by water, reducing the support for the slope.
*   **Consequences:** Large portions of the embankment slide down, potentially leading to a breach.
*   **Prevention:** Designing with flatter slopes, providing adequate drainage to reduce pore water pressure, careful consideration of drawdown conditions, seismic analysis, and protection of the toe.

#### 3.3 Overtopping

*   **Description:** Occurs when the water level in the reservoir rises above the crest of the dam, and the spillway is inadequate to discharge the excess water. The water then flows over the crest of the dam, eroding the downstream slope.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Inadequate spillway capacity:** The spillway cannot handle the peak inflow during a flood event.
    *   **Extreme flood events:** Floods exceeding the design storm.
    *   **Gate malfunction:** If spillway gates are not operated correctly or fail to open.
    *   **Settlement of the dam:** Lowering the effective crest level.
*   **Consequences:** Severe erosion of the downstream slope, leading to progressive collapse and breach.
*   **Prevention:** Designing a spillway with sufficient capacity to safely pass the design flood, ensuring proper operation of spillway gates, and monitoring for excessive settlement.

#### 3.4 Foundation Failure

*   **Description:** The foundation soil or rock can fail under the load of the dam, leading to excessive settlement, differential settlement, or bearing capacity failure.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Weak foundation soils:** Low bearing capacity, compressibility, or susceptibility to liquefaction.
    *   **Differential settlement:** Uneven settlement across the dam's base, creating stress concentrations and cracks.
    *   **Permeable foundation strata:** Leading to excessive seepage and uplift pressures.
    *   **Presence of soluble materials in foundation:** Dissolution can create voids and instability.
*   **Consequences:** Structural distress, cracking, increased seepage, potential collapse.
*   **Prevention:** Thorough foundation investigation, appropriate foundation treatment (e.g., cutoff trenches, grout curtains), designing appropriate embankment slopes, and drainage.

#### 3.5 Cracking and Fissuring

*   **Description:** Cracks can develop in the dam body due to various reasons, providing pathways for water to enter and erode the internal material.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Differential settlement:** As mentioned above.
    *   **Shrinkage of clayey core material:** During drying.
    *   **Construction joints:** If not properly bonded.
    *   **Thermal stresses:** Due to temperature fluctuations.
    *   **Earthquakes:** Shaking can induce cracking.
*   **Consequences:** Increased seepage, potential for internal erosion (piping).
*   **Prevention:** Proper material selection, controlled compaction, avoiding rapid changes in moisture content, and adequate bonding of construction layers.

#### 3.6 Earthquake-Induced Failures

*   **Description:** Earthquakes can cause significant damage to earth dams through ground shaking, liquefaction of saturated granular soils, and seismic-induced landslides.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Ground motion (shaking):** Accelerations and displacements can exceed the strength of the dam materials.
    *   **Liquefaction:** Saturated, loose sands can lose their strength and behave like a liquid under seismic shaking.
    *   **Cyclic mobility:** In some soils, repeated cyclic loading can lead to large deformations.
*   **Consequences:** Slope failures, cracking, loss of shear strength, liquefaction, and potentially catastrophic collapse.
*   **Prevention:** Seismic hazard analysis, designing for seismic loads, using stable materials, ensuring adequate freeboard, implementing drainage measures, and using seismic retrofitting techniques if necessary.

### 4. Design Criteria for Earth Dams

The design of earth dams aims to ensure their safety, stability, and satisfactory performance throughout their intended lifespan. Key design considerations include:

#### 4.1 Stability Analysis (Slope Stability)

*   **Objective:** To ensure that the slopes of the dam are stable under various conditions, including static and seismic loading, and rapid drawdown.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Limit Equilibrium Methods:**
        *   **Fellenius Method (Swedish Method):** Assumes a circular slip surface.
        *   **Bishop's Simplified Method:** Similar to Fellenius but considers interslice forces.
        *   **Janbu's Simplified Method:** Accounts for non-circular slip surfaces.
        *   **Morgenstern-Price Method:** Considers the shape of the slip surface and interslice forces.
    *   **Finite Element Methods (FEM):** More advanced analysis that models the entire dam as a continuum, providing stress and strain distributions.
*   **Key Parameters:**
    *   **Shear Strength of Soil:** Determined from laboratory tests (e.g., triaxial shear test, direct shear test). Parameters include cohesion ($c$) and angle of internal friction ($\phi$).
    *   **Pore Water Pressure:** Crucial for effective stress analysis. Determined from seepage analysis.
    *   **Unit Weight of Soil:** Bulk unit weight for saturated and dry conditions.
*   **Safety Factor (FS):** The ratio of the resisting forces (or moments) to the driving forces (or moments).
    *   **For static conditions:** FS $\ge$ 1.5 (generally required).
    *   **For seismic conditions:** FS $\ge$ 1.2 (generally required).
*   **Critical Slip Surfaces:** The analysis identifies the slip surface with the lowest FS.

#### 4.2 Seepage Analysis and Control

*   **Objective:** To limit the rate of seepage through the dam and foundation to acceptable levels and to prevent internal erosion (piping).
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Graphical Methods (Flow Nets):** For two-dimensional, steady-state seepage. Used to determine seepage rate and hydraulic gradient.
    *   **Analytical Methods:** Using differential equations (e.g., Laplace's equation) for simplified geometries.
    *   **Numerical Methods (Finite Element/Finite Difference Methods):** For complex geometries and transient seepage conditions.
*   **Design Features for Seepage Control:**
    *   **Impervious Core:** Made of low-permeability material (clay, compacted silt).
    *   **Filters:** Layers of granular material (sand, gravel) designed to prevent migration of fine particles from the core and to reduce hydraulic gradients. They are graded from fine to coarse from the core outwards.
    *   **Drainage Layers/Blankets:** Typically placed at the downstream toe to intercept seepage and reduce pore water pressure in the foundation and embankment.
    *   **Cutoff Trenches/Curtain Walls:** Installed in the foundation to reduce seepage under the dam.
*   **Permissible Seepage:** The seepage rate is typically limited to what can be managed by drainage systems without causing erosion or excessive saturation of the downstream slope.
*   **Uplift Pressure:** In the foundation, seepage can cause uplift pressure that reduces the effective stress and bearing capacity. This needs to be accounted for in stability analysis.

#### 4.3 Freeboard

*   **Definition:** The vertical distance between the normal maximum water level (MWL) in the reservoir and the crest of the dam.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   To prevent overtopping by waves generated by wind.
    *   To provide a safety margin against unexpected rises in water level or operational errors.
    *   To allow for settlement of the dam.
*   **Factors Influencing Freeboard:**
    *   **Fetch:** The distance over which wind blows across the water surface.
    *   **Wind Velocity:** Higher wind speeds generate larger waves.
    *   **Reservoir Size and Depth:** Larger reservoirs can sustain larger waves.
    *   **Seismic Activity:** Additional freeboard may be required in seismic zones.
    *   **Wave Run-up:** The height the wave reaches on the upstream slope.
*   **Typical Values:** Freeboard can range from 1 to 3 meters or more, depending on the dam's size and exposure.

#### 4.4 Spillway Design

*   **Objective:** To safely pass flood flows from the reservoir over or around the dam without causing damage to the embankment.
*   **Design Flood:** The maximum flood that the spillway must be capable of passing. This is determined by hydrological studies (e.g., Probable Maximum Flood - PMF).
*   **Components:**
    *   **Approach Channel:** Guides water to the spillway.
    *   **Control Section:** Where flow is regulated (e.g., crest gate, weir).
    *   **Chute:** A sloping channel carrying water downstream.
    *   **Stilling Basin:** Dissipates the energy of the flowing water to prevent erosion downstream.
*   **Capacity:** The spillway must have sufficient discharge capacity to keep the water level below the maximum permissible level (crest of the dam + allowance for wave action).

#### 4.5 Embankment Material Properties and Compaction

*   **Material Selection:**
    *   **Impervious Core:** Clayey or silty soils with low permeability, good plasticity, and resistance to erosion.
    *   **Filters:** Well-graded granular materials (sand, gravel) with specific particle size distributions to prevent migration and provide drainage.
    *   **Shells/Shoulders:** Coarser materials like gravel, crushed rock, or sandy soils to provide stability and drainage.
*   **Compaction:**
    *   **Objective:** To increase the density and shear strength of the soil, and to reduce its permeability and compressibility.
    *   **Compaction Method:** Achieved by using rollers (sheepsfoot, vibratory, pneumatic-tired) and applying energy to the soil layers.
    *   **Compaction Control:** The degree of compaction is specified by the engineer, usually as a percentage of the Maximum Dry Density (MDD) obtained from a standard laboratory compaction test (e.g., Proctor test). Field density tests are conducted to verify compaction.
    *   **Moisture Content:** Compaction is most effective at or near the Optimum Moisture Content (OMC).

#### 4.6 Foundation Treatment

*   **Objective:** To ensure the foundation can safely support the embankment and to prevent excessive seepage through or under the dam.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Removal of Poor Material:** Excavating weak, organic, or permeable soils from the foundation.
    *   **Cutoff Trenches:** Excavating a trench along the centerline of the dam and filling it with impervious material to reduce seepage under the dam.
    *   **Grout Curtains:** Injecting grout into fissures or porous zones in rock foundations to reduce permeability.
    *   **Slurry Walls:** Constructing continuous impermeable barriers using bentonite-cement slurries.
    *   **Blanket Drains:** Placing pervious material at the foundation to reduce uplift pressure.

#### 4.7 Seismic Design

*   **Objective:** To ensure the dam remains stable and functional during and after an earthquake.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   **Seismic Hazard Analysis:** Determining the expected ground motion at the dam site.
    *   **Dynamic Analysis:** Simulating the dam's response to seismic shaking using methods like FEM.
    *   **Liquefaction Potential Assessment:** Evaluating the risk of liquefaction for saturated granular soils.
    *   **Design for Seismic Loads:** Incorporating seismic forces into stability and stress analyses.
    *   **Freeboard:** Adequate freeboard is essential to accommodate settlement and wave action during seismic events.

### 5. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:** Differentiate between a homogeneous earth dam and a zoned earth dam. List one advantage and one disadvantage of each type.

**Answer 1:**
*   **Homogeneous Earth Dam:** Constructed from a single type of soil throughout its cross-section.
    *   **Advantage:** Simpler construction.
    *   **Disadvantage:** Less efficient in controlling seepage compared to zoned dams.
*   **Zoned Earth Dam:** Composed of different zones of selected materials, typically an impervious core and pervious shells.
    *   **Advantage:** More efficient seepage control and improved stability.
    *   **Disadvantage:** More complex construction requiring careful material selection and placement.

**Question 2:** Explain the phenomenon of piping failure in earth dams. What are the key preventive measures?

**Answer 2:**
*   **Piping Failure:** Occurs when seepage flow velocity is high enough to dislodge soil particles, creating channels (pipes) that lead to progressive erosion and collapse of the dam.
*   **Preventive Measures:**
    *   Designing adequate filter layers that transition from fine to coarse materials.
    *   Ensuring proper drainage systems (e.g., downstream toe drains).
    *   Using materials with low erodibility for the core.
    *   Proper foundation treatment to prevent seepage under the dam.
    *   Maintaining the integrity of the impervious core and avoiding cracks.

**Question 3:** What is freeboard in an earth dam, and why is it important?

**Answer 3:**
*   **Freeboard:** The vertical distance between the normal maximum water level in the reservoir and the crest of the dam.
*   **Importance:**
    *   Prevents overtopping due to wave action caused by wind.
    *   Provides a safety margin against unexpected water level rises or operational errors.
    *   Accounts for potential settlement of the dam during its service life.

**Question 4:** A trial fill for an earth dam was compacted in the field. The wet unit weight was measured as 18 kN/m³ and the moisture content as 12%. If the specific gravity of soil solids is 2.65, calculate the dry unit weight and the degree of saturation. (Assume $\gamma_w = 9.81$ kN/m³)

**Answer 4:**
*   Given:
    *   Wet unit weight ($\gamma_{wet}$) = 18 kN/m³
    *   Moisture content ($w$) = 12% = 0.12
    *   Specific gravity of solids ($G_s$) = 2.65
    *   Unit weight of water ($\gamma_w$) = 9.81 kN/m³

*   **Calculate Dry Unit Weight ($\gamma_{dry}$):**
    $\gamma_{wet} = \gamma_{dry} (1 + w)$
    $18 = \gamma_{dry} (1 + 0.12)$
    $18 = \gamma_{dry} (1.12)$
    $\gamma_{dry} = 18 / 1.12 \approx 16.07$ kN/m³

*   **Calculate Degree of Saturation ($S$):**
    First, find the void ratio ($e$):
    $\gamma_{dry} = \frac{G_s \gamma_w}{1 + e}$
    $16.07 = \frac{2.65 \times 9.81}{1 + e}$
    $16.07 = \frac{25.9965}{1 + e}$
    $1 + e = \frac{25.9965}{16.07} \approx 1.618$
    $e \approx 0.618$

    Now, calculate the degree of saturation:
    $S e = w G_s$
    $S \times 0.618 = 0.12 \times 2.65$
    $S \times 0.618 = 0.318$
    $S = 0.318 / 0.618 \approx 0.515$ or 51.5%

**Question 5:** List three common types of failure that can occur in earth dams.

**Answer 5:**
1.  Piping failure (Internal Erosion)
2.  Slope Instability / Landslides
3.  Overtopping

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   **Material Suitability:** The selection of embankment materials is critical, with clayey/silty soils for the core and granular soils for filters and shells.
*   **Compaction is Key:** Proper compaction significantly influences the strength, permeability, and stability of the earth dam.
*   **Seepage Control:** Adequate drainage and filter systems are essential to prevent internal erosion and reduce pore water pressure.
*   **Slope Stability:** Slopes must be designed with appropriate angles to ensure stability under all anticipated loading conditions, including rapid drawdown and seismic events.
*   **Spillway Capacity:** The spillway must be capable of safely discharging the design flood to prevent overtopping.
*   **Foundation Integrity:** Thorough foundation investigation and treatment are crucial to prevent foundation failure and excessive seepage.
*   **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of pore water pressure, settlement, and seepage is vital for detecting potential problems early.
*   **Safety Factors:** Adequate safety factors are applied in all stability and structural analyses to ensure a margin of safety against failure.
*   **Zoned Dams:** Generally preferred for larger and more critical dams due to their superior performance in terms of seepage control and stability.
