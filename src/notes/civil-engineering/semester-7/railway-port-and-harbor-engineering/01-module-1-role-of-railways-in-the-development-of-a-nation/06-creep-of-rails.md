# Railway, Port and Harbor Engineering - Module 1: Role of Railways in the Development of a Nation

## Topic: Creep of Rails

---

### 1. Understanding Creep of Rails

**1.1 Definition:**

*   **Creep of Rails:** The longitudinal movement or displacement of railway rails in the direction of the traffic, under the action of repeated traffic loads and other forces. It's a slow, continuous shifting of the rail within its fastenings and ballast.

**1.2 Significance:**

*   Creep is a phenomenon that occurs in both conventional and high-speed rail systems.
*   It can lead to serious safety issues if not managed properly.
*   Understanding creep is crucial for maintaining track geometry and ensuring operational safety.

---

### 2. Causes of Rail Creep

**2.1 Primary Causes (Directly related to traffic):**

*   **Longitudinal Forces Exerted by Wheels:**
    *   **Starting and Stopping:** When a train starts, wheels exert a forward pushing force on the rail. When it brakes, it exerts a backward pulling force. The net effect over many operations is a tendency for the rail to move in the direction of dominant traffic flow.
    *   **Acceleration and Deceleration:** Similar to starting and stopping, these actions also generate longitudinal forces.
*   **Vertical and Lateral Forces from Wheels:**
    *   As wheels roll, they exert both vertical (downward) and lateral (sideways) forces on the railhead. These forces, combined with friction between the wheel and rail, create shear stresses that can initiate creep.
*   **Action of Brakes:**
    *   Brake shoes press against the wheel tread, generating significant friction. This friction, transmitted to the rail, can cause it to move longitudinally.

**2.2 Secondary Causes (Contributing factors):**

*   **Ballast Conditions:**
    *   **Insufficient Lateral Resistance of Ballast:** If the ballast is not well-compacted or has poor quality aggregate, it offers less resistance to the lateral forces generated by wheel loads. This allows the rail to shift sideways, which can then translate into longitudinal creep.
    *   **Poorly Shaped or Rounded Ballast:** Sharp-angled ballast particles interlock better and provide greater resistance to movement. Rounded or worn ballast provides less friction and support.
*   **Sleeper Movement:**
    *   **Longitudinal Sleepers (Tie-beams):** Sleepers, especially those made of concrete, can experience expansion and contraction due to temperature changes. They can also shift under traffic loads.
    *   **Vertical Movement of Sleepers:** If sleepers settle or tilt, it can affect the way forces are transmitted to the ballast, influencing creep.
*   **Rail Fastenings:**
    *   **Loose Clips/Fastenings:** Inadequate tightness of clips or spikes allows for more play and movement between the rail and the sleeper, facilitating creep.
    *   **Wear of Fastenings:** Worn clips or baseplates can reduce the grip on the rail.
*   **Temperature Variations:**
    *   **Rail Expansion and Contraction:** While not a direct cause of *creep* in the traffic sense, temperature changes cause the rail to expand longitudinally. If this expansion is restrained by track components (e.g., at points and crossings), it can induce stresses that can interact with traffic forces and influence creep.
*   **Grade and Curves:**
    *   **Up and Down Gradients:** On descending gradients, the weight of the train tends to pull the rails downhill. On ascending gradients, trains pushing upwards can also cause movement.
    *   **Curved Tracks:** Centrifugal forces on curves exert lateral pressure on the outer rail, contributing to sideways movement that can lead to creep.
*   **Ballast Shoulder Width and Depth:**
    *   A narrow or shallow ballast shoulder provides less lateral support to the sleepers and thus to the rails.

---

### 3. Effects of Rail Creep

**3.1 Track Geometry Disturbance:**

*   **Sleeper Distortion:** Creep can cause sleepers to skew, tilt, or displace longitudinally, disrupting the intended track geometry.
*   **Buckling of Rails:** In extreme cases, particularly on long straight stretches, accumulated creep can lead to a sudden, catastrophic failure known as track buckling (or "sun kink" if due to temperature). This occurs when longitudinal compressive forces exceed the lateral restraint provided by the sleepers and ballast.
*   **Distortion of Curves:** Creep can alter the intended radius of curves, leading to an increase in curvature, which is dangerous.
*   **Gaps at Rail Joints:** Creep can cause expansion gaps at rail joints to close or even overlap, creating unsafe conditions.

**3.2 Impact on Track Components:**

*   **Damage to Turnouts and Points:** Creep can significantly affect the precise alignment required for switches and crossings, leading to operational failures and damage.
*   **Stress on Expansion Joints:** If expansion joints are installed, creep can cause them to be compressed beyond their design limits.
*   **Wear on Fastenings:** Continuous movement puts stress on rail clips, bolts, and baseplates, leading to premature wear and potential failure.

**3.3 Safety Hazards:**

*   **Derailment:** Distorted track geometry, buckling, and damaged components can all lead to derailment.
*   **Operational Difficulties:** Trains may experience rough rides, and speed restrictions might be imposed.

### 4. Measurement of Rail Creep

*   **Purpose:** To monitor the extent of creep and identify areas requiring intervention.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Benchmarking:** Measuring the position of the rail relative to fixed points in the ground (e.g., reference posts, structures).
    *   **Reference Marks on Rails:** Creating permanent marks on the railhead or web and measuring their displacement relative to fixed points or other sections of the track.
    *   **Sleeper Monitoring:** Observing the position and orientation of sleepers relative to fixed points.
    *   **Optical Surveying and GPS:** Modern techniques offer precise measurements of track alignment.
    *   **Extensometers:** Specialized devices can be installed to continuously monitor longitudinal strain in rails.

### 5. Control and Prevention of Rail Creep

**5.1 Track Design and Construction Practices:**

*   **Adequate Ballast Section:** Maintaining a sufficiently wide and deep ballast shoulder is critical for providing lateral support to sleepers.
*   **Quality of Ballast:** Using hard, angular ballast aggregate that interlocks well.
*   **Proper Ballast Compaction:** Ensuring thorough compaction of ballast during track maintenance.
*   **Effective Drainage:** Waterlogged ballast loses its load-bearing capacity and lateral resistance.
*   **Proper Fastening Tightness:** Ensuring all rail clips and fastenings are tightened to the manufacturer's specifications and regularly maintained.
*   **Continuous Welded Rails (CWR):** CWR significantly reduces the number of joints, which are a major source of creep. However, CWR introduces thermal stresses that must be managed.

**5.2 Rail Anchors (Anti-creepers):**

*   **Definition:** Devices attached to the rail and anchored against the side of the sleeper or ballast to resist longitudinal movement.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Spring-Type Anchors:** These are typically one-piece or two-piece units that grip the rail base and are driven against the sleeper. Examples include the 'Cleveland' type or 'Pandrol' type anchors.
    *   **Rigid or Forged Anchors:** These are generally heavier and more robust, often forged from steel.
*   **Installation:**
    *   **Placement:** Anchors are usually installed alternately on one side of the rail and then the other, or on both sides of the rail at strategic intervals.
    *   **Spacing:** The spacing depends on the anticipated creep rate, traffic density, and track conditions. Typically, they are spaced every few sleepers.
    *   **Mechanism:** They work by providing a frictional or wedging force against the sleeper/ballast to counteract the longitudinal forces on the rail.
*   **Maintenance:** Regular inspection and re-tightening of anchors are necessary as they can become loose or ineffective over time.

**5.3 Other Measures:**

*   **Fish Plate Clamping:** Ensuring fish plates at rail joints are securely fastened.
*   **Ballast Shoulder Maintenance:** Re-ballasting and maintaining adequate shoulder width.
*   **Track Lubrication:** In some cases, lubricating contact points can reduce friction and hence creep, but this is usually a secondary measure.

### 6. Factors Influencing Creep Rate

*   **Traffic Density and Type:** Higher traffic density and frequent acceleration/deceleration can increase creep.
*   **Track Condition:** Poor ballast, loose fastenings, and worn components exacerbate creep.
*   **Track Geometry:** Steeper gradients and tighter curves generally experience higher creep rates.
*   **Climate:** Extreme temperature variations and heavy rainfall can influence ballast conditions.
*   **Rail Section and Weight:** Heavier rails generally offer more resistance to creep due to their mass.
*   **Sleeper Type and Spacing:** Different sleeper materials and spacing affect how loads are distributed to the ballast.

### 7. Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:** Define rail creep.
**Answer 1:** Rail creep is the longitudinal movement or displacement of railway rails in the direction of traffic, caused by repeated traffic loads and other forces.

**Question 2:** What are the primary causes of rail creep?
**Answer 2:** The primary causes are the longitudinal forces exerted by wheels during starting, stopping, acceleration, and deceleration, as well as the forces generated by brake shoes.

**Question 3:** Explain the role of ballast in rail creep.
**Answer 3:** The ballast provides lateral resistance to the sleepers. If the ballast is poorly compacted, has rounded aggregate, or a narrow shoulder, it offers less resistance to lateral forces, allowing sleepers to shift, which in turn facilitates rail creep.

**Question 4:** Describe two effects of rail creep on the track structure.
**Answer 4:**
1.  **Distortion of Track Geometry:** Creep can cause sleepers to skew or displace, leading to changes in the alignment and level of the track.
2.  **Buckling of Rails:** Accumulated longitudinal forces due to creep can, under certain conditions (e.g., high temperatures, inadequate lateral restraint), cause the rails to buckle laterally in a catastrophic failure.

**Question 5:** What is a rail anchor (anti-creeper) and how does it work?
**Answer 5:** A rail anchor is a device attached to the rail and anchored against a sleeper or the ballast. It works by providing a frictional or wedging force that resists the longitudinal movement of the rail, thereby preventing or controlling creep.

**Question 6:** How can proper fastening maintenance help prevent rail creep?
**Answer 6:** Properly tightened rail clips and fastenings provide better grip on the rail and secure it to the sleeper. This reduces play and restricts the movement of the rail relative to the sleeper, thus minimizing creep.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   **Creep is a dynamic process:** It is a continuous movement influenced by many factors.
*   **Safety is paramount:** Uncontrolled creep can lead to severe track instability and derailments.
*   **Ballast condition is key:** The lateral resistance provided by the ballast is crucial in controlling creep.
*   **Rail anchors are primary control devices:** They are essential for managing creep on many railway lines.
*   **Continuous Welded Rails (CWR) have different creep dynamics:** While reducing joint creep, they introduce thermal stresses that must be managed.
*   **Regular monitoring is vital:** Detecting and measuring creep allows for timely intervention.
*   **A combination of factors leads to creep:** It's rarely a single cause, but an interplay of traffic, track conditions, and environmental factors.
