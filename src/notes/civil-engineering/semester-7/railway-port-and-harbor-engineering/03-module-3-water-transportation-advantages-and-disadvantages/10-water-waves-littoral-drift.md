### **1. Water Waves**

Water waves are oscillatory movements on the surface of a body of water, typically the sea or ocean. They are a fundamental phenomenon in water transportation and have significant implications for coastal engineering.

#### **1.1. Definition and Characteristics**

*   **Definition:** A wave is a transfer of energy through a medium (in this case, water) without the net displacement of the medium itself. The water particles move in an orbital path, returning to their approximate original position after the wave passes.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   **Crest:** The highest point of a wave.
    *   **Trough:** The lowest point of a wave.
    *   **Wave Height (H):** The vertical distance between the crest and the trough.
    *   **Wavelength (L):** The horizontal distance between two successive crests or troughs.
    *   **Wave Period (T):** The time it takes for two successive crests or troughs to pass a fixed point.
    *   **Wave Frequency (f):** The number of waves passing a fixed point per unit of time ($f = 1/T$).
    *   **Wave Speed (C or $c_g$):** The speed at which the wave crests or troughs travel. In shallow water, $C = \sqrt{gD}$ (where $g$ is acceleration due to gravity and $D$ is water depth). In deep water, $C = \sqrt{gL/(2\pi)}$.
    *   **Wave Amplitude (A):** Half the wave height ($A = H/2$).

#### **1.2. Types of Water Waves**

Waves can be classified based on their origin, size, and behavior:

*   **Wind Waves:**
    *   **Formation:** Generated by wind blowing over the water surface. The friction between wind and water transfers energy.
    *   **Characteristics:** Vary in height, period, and direction depending on wind speed, duration, and fetch (the distance over which the wind blows).
    *   **Significance:** The most common type of wave encountered in coastal and harbor areas, significantly impacting navigation, operations, and structures.

*   **Swell Waves:**
    *   **Formation:** Wind waves that have traveled away from their generating area. They become more regular and have longer periods.
    *   **Characteristics:** Characterized by smoother, more rounded crests and are less steep than local wind waves.
    *   **Significance:** Can travel long distances, carrying significant energy into coastal zones even when local winds are calm.

*   **Tidal Waves (Tides):**
    *   **Formation:** Caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun.
    *   **Characteristics:** Very long wavelengths (spanning oceans) and periodic rises and falls of sea level.
    *   **Significance:** While not "waves" in the typical sense, they influence water depths, currents, and overall water levels, affecting ship access and port operations.

*   **Tsunamis:**
    *   **Formation:** Generated by sudden displacements of large volumes of water, often due to underwater earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides.
    *   **Characteristics:** Extremely long wavelengths and can travel across entire oceans with relatively low height in deep water, but build up significant heights and destructive power in shallow coastal areas.
    *   **Significance:** Although infrequent, they are highly destructive and pose a significant hazard.

*   **Capillary Waves (Ripples):**
    *   **Formation:** Small waves formed by very light winds. Surface tension is the dominant restoring force.
    *   **Characteristics:** Very short wavelengths (less than 1.7 cm) and are quickly dissipated.
    *   **Significance:** Primarily cosmetic, but can be the initial stage of wind wave formation.

#### **1.3. Wave Action on Structures**

*   **Forces Exerted:** Waves exert dynamic forces on coastal structures (breakwaters, piers, seawalls). These forces can be:
    *   **Impact Forces:** From breaking waves hitting the structure.
    *   **Pressure Forces:** From the water pushing against the structure.
    *   **Overtopping Forces:** When waves flow over the top of a structure.
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   **Wave Height and Period:** Crucial for determining the forces and their characteristics.
    *   **Wave Direction:** Influences the angle of impact and potential for flanking.
    *   **Wave Breaking:** Whether waves break on the structure or in front of it significantly affects the forces.
    *   **Water Depth:** Affects wave characteristics and energy approaching the structure.
    *   **Structure Type and Orientation:** Determines how the structure interacts with the waves.

### **2. Littoral Drift**

Littoral drift, also known as sediment transport or longshore drift, is the movement of sand and sediment along the coastline by the action of waves and currents. It's a continuous process that shapes beaches and the coastal environment.

#### **2.1. Definition and Causes**

*   **Definition:** The process by which waves approach a shoreline at an angle, pushing sediment up the beach (swash), and then gravity pulls the water and sediment back down the beach perpendicular to the shoreline (backwash). This zig-zag motion results in a net movement of sediment along the coast.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Waves Approaching at an Angle (Oblique Waves):** This is the primary driver of littoral drift.
    *   **Longshore Currents:** The currents generated by the oblique wave approach that flow parallel to the coastline.

#### **2.2. Components of Littoral Drift**

Littoral drift can be broadly divided into two components:

*   **Onshore Drift (Swash):**
    *   **Process:** The movement of sediment up the beach face by the wave swash.
    *   **Factors:** Wave energy, sediment size, beach slope.

*   **Offshore Drift (Backwash):**
    *   **Process:** The movement of sediment back down the beach face by gravity after the swash loses energy.
    *   **Factors:** Gravity, sediment size, beach slope.

*   **Longshore Drift (Longshore Transport):**
    *   **Process:** The net movement of sediment parallel to the coastline by longshore currents.
    *   **Factors:** Angle of wave approach, wave energy, longshore current velocity, sediment availability.

#### **2.3. Factors Influencing Littoral Drift**

*   **Wave Characteristics:**
    *   **Wave Height and Period:** Higher energy waves can move larger sediment particles and increase drift rates.
    *   **Angle of Wave Approach:** The greater the angle, the more significant the longshore drift.
*   **Sediment Characteristics:**
    *   **Grain Size and Shape:** Finer sediments are more easily transported.
    *   **Sediment Availability:** The amount of sediment available on the beach.
*   **Beach Morphology:**
    *   **Beach Slope:** Steeper beaches have a stronger backwash, potentially reducing net onshore transport, but can also generate stronger longshore currents.
    *   **Beach Width and Profile:** Influences how waves interact with the shore.
*   **Nearshore Currents:**
    *   **Longshore Currents:** As mentioned, the direct driver of longshore transport.
    *   **Rip Currents:** Can carry sediment offshore, but are less significant to the overall longshore drift process.
*   **Tidal Range and Currents:** Tidal fluctuations can influence sediment distribution and current patterns.
*   **Wind:** Can indirectly influence waves and currents.

#### **2.4. Significance in Port and Harbor Engineering**

Littoral drift has profound implications for the design, construction, and maintenance of ports and harbors:

*   **Silting of Channels and Harbors:**
    *   **Problem:** Littoral drift can carry sand and sediment into navigation channels, entrances to harbors, and berths, reducing water depth.
    *   **Consequences:** Requires regular dredging to maintain navigable depths, which is costly and time-consuming.
    *   **Example:** The constant need to dredge the entrance to a harbor to counteract the accumulation of sand transported by longshore currents.

*   **Erosion and Accretion of Beaches:**
    *   **Problem:** Structures like jetties, groins, and breakwaters can interrupt the natural flow of littoral drift.
    *   **Consequences:**
        *   **Downdrift Erosion:** Beaches downdrift of a structure that traps sand can experience significant erosion.
        *   **Updrift Accretion:** Beaches updrift can experience excessive sand accumulation.
    *   **Example:** A jetty built to protect a harbor entrance might trap sand on its updrift side, causing erosion on the downdrift side, potentially threatening nearby beaches or structures.

*   **Design of Coastal Structures:**
    *   **Breakwaters:** Must be designed to withstand wave forces and can alter littoral drift patterns, potentially leading to silting behind them.
    *   **Jetties and Groins:** Specifically built to control littoral drift by trapping sand or preventing its passage into a channel.
    *   **Seawalls:** Can reflect wave energy, potentially altering sediment transport patterns offshore.

*   **Maintenance and Dredging:**
    *   **Requirement:** Understanding littoral drift is essential for planning and scheduling dredging operations.
    *   **Strategy:** Predicting the rate and location of sediment accumulation allows for efficient dredging.

*   **Environmental Impact:**
    *   **Habitat Alteration:** Changes in sediment transport can affect coastal habitats like beaches, dunes, and intertidal zones.
    *   **Water Quality:** Dredging operations can temporarily impact water quality.

#### **2.5. Mitigation and Management of Littoral Drift**

*   **Dredging:** The removal of accumulated sediment from navigation channels and harbors.
*   **Groins:** Structures built perpendicular to the shore to trap sand from the longshore drift.
*   **Jetties:** Similar to groins, often built in pairs to stabilize a channel entrance, but typically larger.
*   **Breakwaters:** Can be used to reduce wave energy, which in turn reduces the ability of waves to move sediment, but can also cause downdrift erosion.
*   **Beach Nourishment:** Adding sand to beaches that are eroding due to altered littoral drift.
*   **Artificial Headlands:** Structures designed to redirect wave energy and currents.

### **3. Interplay between Waves and Littoral Drift in Port and Harbor Engineering**

*   **Wave Energy:** Is the primary force that initiates and sustains littoral drift. Higher energy waves are more effective at moving sediment.
*   **Wave Direction:** Determines the direction of longshore currents and, consequently, the direction of littoral drift.
*   **Wave Period:** Longer period waves can move larger sediment particles.
*   **Breaking Waves:** The zone where waves break is where most of the sediment transport occurs. The energy dissipation in this zone fuels the longshore currents.
*   **Coastal Structures:** are often designed to either:
    *   **Deflect waves** to reduce their impact on the port/harbor.
    *   **Influence littoral drift** to prevent silting or maintain beach stability.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   Water waves are a form of energy transfer, not mass transfer of water.
*   Wave height, wavelength, and period are key parameters describing wave characteristics.
*   Wind waves are the most common type encountered in ports and harbors.
*   Littoral drift is the alongshore movement of sediment driven by waves and longshore currents.
*   The oblique approach of waves is the primary cause of littoral drift.
*   Littoral drift leads to silting in navigation channels and can cause erosion or accretion of beaches near coastal structures.
*   Understanding and managing littoral drift is crucial for the economic viability and operational efficiency of ports and harbors.
*   Coastal engineering structures like jetties and groins are designed to control littoral drift.

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

1.  **Definition:** A water wave is the transfer of energy through water without net displacement of the water itself, characterized by oscillatory movement.
    **Characteristics:** Crest, trough, wave height (H), wavelength (L), wave period (T), wave speed (C).
2.  **Wind Wave:** Generated by local winds, can be irregular.
    **Swell Wave:** Wind waves that have traveled away from their generating area, are more regular and have longer periods.
3.  **Littoral Drift:** The movement of sand and sediment along the coastline by waves and currents.
    **Primary Causes:** Waves approaching the shore at an angle, generating longshore currents.
4.  **Components of Littoral Drift:**
    *   **Onshore Drift (Swash):** Movement of sediment up the beach.
    *   **Offshore Drift (Backwash):** Movement of sediment back down the beach.
    *   **(Also consider Longshore Drift as the net movement parallel to the shore).**
5.  **Impact on Navigation Channels:** Littoral drift causes sediment deposition (silting) in navigation channels, reducing water depth. This necessitates regular and costly dredging to maintain navigable depths, impacting port operations and costs.
6.  **Consequences of a Jetty:**
    *   **Updrift Accretion:** Sand accumulates on the updrift side of the jetty, potentially causing shoaling in adjacent areas or requiring sand bypass.
    *   **Downdrift Erosion:** Beaches downdrift of the jetty may experience significant erosion due to the interruption of sand supply.
7.  **Engineering Structures to Manage Littoral Drift:**
    *   **Groins:** Perpendicular structures built to trap sand from the longshore drift, nourishing beaches on the updrift side.
    *   **Jetties:** Larger structures, often in pairs, to stabilize a channel entrance and control sediment movement into it, while also allowing some sediment passage.
8.  **Engineering Solutions for Silting:**
    *   **Dredging:** Regularly remove accumulated sand from the port entrance.
    *   **Groins/Jetties:** Construct groins or a jetty on the updrift (north) side of the port entrance to trap the incoming sand before it reaches the entrance.
    *   **Sand Bypassing:** If a jetty is used, implement a sand bypassing system to transfer trapped sand from the updrift side to the downdrift side to maintain beach equilibrium.
