# FINANCE FOR ENGINEERING - MODULE 2: RATE OF RETURN METHOD – MINIMUM ATTRACTIVE RATE OF RETURN (MARR)

---

## Introduction to Rate of Return (ROR)

The Rate of Return (ROR) is a fundamental concept in finance and engineering economics used to evaluate the profitability of an investment or project. It represents the percentage return an investment generates over a specific period.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Rate of Return (ROR):** The measure of profit or loss generated by an investment over a given period, expressed as a percentage of the initial investment. It answers the question: "How much money did I make (or lose) relative to what I put in?"

*   **Simple Rate of Return:** A basic calculation that doesn't account for the time value of money. It's often calculated as:
    *   $ROR_{simple} = \frac{\text{Average Annual Profit}}{\text{Initial Investment}} \times 100\%$

*   **Annual Worth (AW):** The equivalent uniform annual amount of all revenues and costs over the life of an asset. It's a method to compare projects with different lifespans.

*   **Present Worth (PW):** The equivalent value of all future cash flows of an investment at the present time, discounted at a specific interest rate.

*   **Future Worth (FW):** The equivalent value of all cash flows of an investment at a specific future point in time, compounded at a specific interest rate.

*   **Equivalent Uniform Annual Cost (EUAC):** The annual cost equivalent to all costs incurred over the life of an asset.

*   **Equivalent Uniform Annual Benefit (EUAB):** The annual benefit equivalent to all benefits gained over the life of an asset.

## Minimum Attractive Rate of Return (MARR)

The Minimum Attractive Rate of Return (MARR) is a critical benchmark for evaluating investment opportunities. It represents the minimum acceptable rate of return an organization expects to earn on an investment, considering the risk and opportunity cost associated with that investment.

**Key Concepts & Definitions:**

*   **Minimum Attractive Rate of Return (MARR):** The minimum acceptable rate of return that a project or investment must achieve to be considered viable. It is often expressed as an interest rate (e.g., 10% per year).

*   **Opportunity Cost:** The potential benefit that is given up when one alternative is chosen over another. If an organization can earn 12% on a low-risk investment, then investing in a project with a lower ROR represents an opportunity cost of 2% (12% - ROR of the project).

*   **Cost of Capital:** The average rate of return a company must pay to its investors (both debt and equity holders) to finance its assets. This is often a significant component in determining the MARR.

*   **Risk:** The uncertainty associated with the outcome of an investment. Higher risk investments typically demand a higher MARR.

**Factors Influencing MARR:**

*   **Cost of Capital:** The company's weighted average cost of capital (WACC) is a primary determinant.
*   **Risk of the Project:** Riskier projects require a higher MARR to compensate for the potential for loss.
*   **Availability of Capital:** If capital is scarce, the MARR might be higher to prioritize the most profitable investments.
*   **Inflation:** The MARR should ideally be a "real" rate (adjusted for inflation) or the nominal MARR should account for expected inflation.
*   **Management Policy and Investment Objectives:** Corporate strategies and desired returns influence the MARR.

**How MARR is Used in Decision Making:**

The primary rule for evaluating projects using ROR methods is:

*   **If the calculated Rate of Return (ROR) of a project is GREATER THAN or EQUAL TO the MARR, the project is considered acceptable.**
*   **If the calculated Rate of Return (ROR) of a project is LESS THAN the MARR, the project is considered unacceptable.**

This rule applies whether you are comparing a single project against a benchmark or selecting the best among mutually exclusive projects.

## Methods for Calculating Rate of Return

While this module focuses on the concept of MARR and its application, it's important to acknowledge that there are different methods to *calculate* the ROR of a project. The choice of method often depends on the nature of the cash flows and the specific context.

**Common ROR Calculation Methods:**

1.  **Internal Rate of Return (IRR):** The discount rate at which the Net Present Worth (NPW) of all cash flows from a project equals zero. In simpler terms, it's the rate of return that the project is expected to generate.

    *   **Formula:** IRR is the rate 'i' such that: $NPW = \sum_{t=0}^{n} P_t (1+i)^{-t} = 0$
        where:
        *   $P_t$ = Net cash flow at time t
        *   $i$ = Interest rate
        *   $n$ = Number of periods

2.  **Modified Internal Rate of Return (MIRR):** Addresses some of the limitations of IRR, particularly the assumption that interim cash flows are reinvested at the IRR. MIRR assumes reinvestment at a specified rate (often the MARR).

3.  **Rate of Return Based on Average Annual Profit (as introduced earlier):** A simpler method that is less precise as it ignores the time value of money.

**Comparison of Projects using ROR and MARR:**

When comparing mutually exclusive projects (where choosing one means you cannot choose another), the decision rule based on MARR is crucial:

*   **Independent Projects:** If a project's ROR $\geq$ MARR, accept it. If it's $<$ MARR, reject it.
*   **Mutually Exclusive Projects:** Calculate the ROR for each project. Select the project with the *highest* ROR, provided that its ROR is $\geq$ MARR. If none of the projects meet the MARR, then no project should be selected.

**Important Note on IRR:** For projects with non-conventional cash flows (i.e., cash flows that change sign more than once), IRR might yield multiple rates or no meaningful rate. In such cases, methods like MIRR or external rate of return (ERR) might be more appropriate, or a comparison based on Net Present Worth (NPW) at the MARR is preferred.

## Examples

**Example 1: Simple Project Evaluation**

A company is considering a project that requires an initial investment of \$50,000 and is expected to generate an average annual profit of \$10,000 over its 5-year life. The company's MARR is 12%.

*   **Calculate the Simple Rate of Return:**
    $ROR_{simple} = \frac{\$10,000}{\$50,000} \times 100\% = 20\%$

*   **Decision:** Since the simple ROR (20%) is greater than the MARR (12%), this project is considered acceptable based on this simplified calculation.

**Example 2: Comparing Mutually Exclusive Projects**

Company Alpha is deciding between two mutually exclusive projects, Project A and Project B.

*   **Project A:** Initial Investment = \$100,000. Expected IRR = 15%.
*   **Project B:** Initial Investment = \$150,000. Expected IRR = 13%.
*   **Company Alpha's MARR = 10%.**

*   **Evaluation:**
    *   Project A's IRR (15%) $\geq$ MARR (10%) $\rightarrow$ Acceptable.
    *   Project B's IRR (13%) $\geq$ MARR (10%) $\rightarrow$ Acceptable.

*   **Decision:** Since both projects are acceptable, Company Alpha should choose the project with the higher IRR. Project A has a higher IRR (15% vs. 13%), so it should be selected.

**Example 3: Project Below MARR**

A project requires an initial investment of \$200,000 and is expected to have an IRR of 8%. The company's MARR is 10%.

*   **Decision:** Since the project's IRR (8%) is less than the MARR (10%), the project should be rejected. It will not generate a return attractive enough to meet the company's minimum requirements.

## Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**

The Minimum Attractive Rate of Return (MARR) is the minimum acceptable rate of return that a company expects to earn from an investment. It represents the hurdle rate for any project. Its significance lies in ensuring that only projects that can generate returns exceeding the company's cost of capital and compensating for risk are undertaken, thereby maximizing shareholder value and ensuring financial sustainability.

**Answer 2:**

Three factors that influence MARR are:
1.  **Cost of Capital:** The company's overall cost of financing (e.g., WACC).
2.  **Risk Associated with the Project:** Higher risk demands higher compensation.
3.  **Availability of Capital:** Scarcity of funds might lead to a higher MARR.
4.  **(Other valid answers include:** Inflation expectations, management policy, investment opportunities elsewhere).

**Answer 3:**

Yes, the project should be accepted. The project's Rate of Return (14%) is greater than the company's MARR (12%). This indicates that the project is expected to generate more return than the minimum acceptable threshold, making it a financially attractive investment.

**Answer 4:**

*   **Project X:** ROR (11%) $\geq$ MARR (10%). Project X should be accepted.
*   **Project Y:** ROR (9%) $<$ MARR (10%). Project Y should be rejected.
Therefore, only Project X should be accepted.

**Answer 5:**

The primary advantage of using MARR in decision-making is that it provides a clear, objective benchmark for evaluating project profitability. It ensures that investments are aligned with the company's financial goals and risk tolerance, preventing the acceptance of projects that do not offer adequate returns relative to the available alternatives or the cost of funds. It helps in prioritizing projects and making rational capital allocation decisions.

## Important Points to Remember

*   **MARR is a Threshold:** Always compare the calculated Rate of Return (ROR) of a project against the MARR.
*   **ROR > MARR = Acceptable:** The project meets or exceeds the minimum acceptable return.
*   **ROR < MARR = Unacceptable:** The project does not generate enough return to justify the investment.
*   **Mutually Exclusive Projects:** When comparing projects where only one can be chosen, select the one with the highest ROR, provided it is still greater than or equal to the MARR.
*   **Cost of Capital is a Foundation:** The company's cost of capital is a crucial component in setting the MARR.
*   **Risk Premium:** Higher risk projects generally require a higher MARR to compensate investors.
*   **IRR Limitations:** Be aware of potential issues with IRR for non-conventional cash flows. In such cases, NPW analysis at the MARR is often more reliable.
*   **MARR is not Static:** It can change based on market conditions, company performance, and economic factors.
