# AIRPORT PLANNING AND DESIGN: Module 3 - Landside Planning

## 3.1 Terminal Area Elements and Requirements

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Landside:** The portion of the airport that is accessible to the public and serves as the interface between the airport and the ground transportation network. This includes terminals, parking, access roads, and associated facilities.
*   **Terminal Building:** The primary facility for processing passengers and baggage, and for providing essential services.
*   **Functional Areas:** The different zones within a terminal building, each serving a specific purpose (e.g., check-in, security, departure gates, baggage claim).

**Elements of the Terminal Area:**

1.  **Terminal Building:**
    *   **Passenger Processing Areas:** Check-in, security screening, immigration/emigration, customs.
    *   **Passenger Waiting & Circulation Areas:** Concessions, lounges, gate waiting areas, corridors.
    *   **Baggage Handling Systems:** Check-in baggage sorting, outbound baggage handling, inbound baggage claim.
    *   **Aircraft Apron Interface:** Gate areas, jet bridges/scarfs, remote stand facilities.
    *   **Support Facilities:** Offices, operations control, utilities, maintenance.

2.  **Ancillary Facilities:**
    *   **Access Roads & Parking:** Car parking, taxi stands, bus bays, rental car facilities, ride-sharing pick-up/drop-off zones.
    *   **Airside Interface Facilities:** Baggage handling corridors, passenger walkways to aircraft.
    *   **Cargo Facilities:** Cargo terminals, warehouses, handling areas (often separate but considered landside interface).

**Requirements for Terminal Area Elements:**

*   **Capacity:** Must be able to handle projected passenger and baggage volumes efficiently.
*   **Flow:** Smooth and logical movement of passengers, baggage, and staff.
*   **Safety & Security:** Adherence to stringent security protocols and passenger safety.
*   **Accessibility:** Ease of access for all users, including those with disabilities.
*   **Comfort & Convenience:** Pleasant environment for passengers, with adequate amenities.
*   **Efficiency:** Minimizing processing times and operational costs.
*   **Flexibility:** Ability to adapt to future growth and changing operational needs.

**Examples:**

*   **Check-in counters:** Need sufficient space for queues during peak hours.
*   **Security screening checkpoints:** Require multiple lanes and waiting areas to process passengers quickly.
*   **Baggage claim carousels:** Sized based on the number of aircraft arriving simultaneously and the expected baggage volume.

## 3.2 Terminal Concepts and Types

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Terminal Concept:** The overall design philosophy and layout of a terminal building, influencing passenger flow, operational efficiency, and passenger experience.
*   **Pier/Finger Concept:** A linear pier extending from the terminal building, with aircraft parked along its sides.
*   **Satellite Concept:** A standalone terminal building connected to the main terminal by a shuttle or underground walkway.
*   **Transporter Concept:** Aircraft are moved to/from the terminal by specialized transporters.
*   **Linear Concept:** A long, narrow terminal building with aircraft parked parallel to it.
*   **Modular Concept:** Consists of multiple smaller, interconnected modules that can be added or reconfigured.

**Types of Terminals:**

1.  **Single Terminal:** One large building serving all flights.
    *   **Pros:** Centralized facilities, easier passenger navigation.
    *   **Cons:** Can become congested, potential for long walks.
    *   **Example:** Many older, smaller airports.

2.  **Multiple Terminals:** Separate terminal buildings for different airlines, carriers, or operational functions (e.g., domestic vs. international).
    *   **Pros:** Better organization, can cater to specific needs.
    *   **Cons:** Requires efficient inter-terminal transport, can be confusing for passengers.
    *   **Example:** London Heathrow Airport (Terminals 2, 3, 4, 5).

3.  **Pier/Finger Terminals:**
    *   **Pros:** Efficient apron utilization, relatively short walking distances to gates.
    *   **Cons:** Can be aesthetically unappealing, limited flexibility for gate changes.
    *   **Example:** Many airports, including parts of Amsterdam Schiphol.

4.  **Satellite Terminals:**
    *   **Pros:** Reduces congestion at the main terminal, allows for better control of passenger flow, can be expanded independently.
    *   **Cons:** Requires a reliable and efficient shuttle system, potential for longer overall travel time.
    *   **Example:** Denver International Airport (main terminal connected to three satellites via underground trains).

5.  **Transporter Terminals:**
    *   **Pros:** Efficient use of apron space, can serve aircraft that cannot reach traditional gates.
    *   **Cons:** Requires specialized vehicles, can be less comfortable for passengers.
    *   **Example:** Some European airports for specific aircraft types or remote stands.

6.  **Linear Terminals:**
    *   **Pros:** Simple design, good for smaller airports.
    *   **Cons:** Can lead to long walking distances if the terminal is extensive.
    *   **Example:** Smaller regional airports.

7.  **Modular Terminals:**
    *   **Pros:** Highly flexible and scalable, adaptable to changing demand.
    *   **Cons:** Can appear less integrated, higher initial cost for modular construction.
    *   **Example:** Newer terminals often incorporate modular design principles.

## 3.3 Passenger Requirements at Terminal Building

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Passenger Journey:** The entire process a passenger experiences from arriving at the airport to boarding the aircraft, and vice-versa.
*   **Passenger Experience (PaxEx):** The overall perception of a passenger's interaction with the airport and its services.
*   **Passenger Processing Time:** The duration it takes for a passenger to complete a specific task (e.g., check-in, security).

**Passenger Requirements:**

1.  **Ease of Access & Wayfinding:**
    *   Clear signage and directions.
    *   Intuitive layout.
    *   Easy connections to ground transportation.

2.  **Efficient Processing:**
    *   Minimal wait times at check-in, security, immigration, and baggage claim.
    *   Automated systems (self-check-in, self-bag drop).

3.  **Comfort & Convenience:**
    *   Adequate seating in waiting areas.
    *   Clean and well-maintained restrooms.
    *   Availability of amenities: food and beverage, retail, lounges, Wi-Fi.
    *   Children's play areas.
    *   Quiet zones.

4.  **Information & Communication:**
    *   Real-time flight information displays.
    *   Access to information about airport services.
    *   Customer service assistance.

5.  **Safety & Security:**
    *   Feeling of security throughout the terminal.
    *   Efficient but non-intrusive security screening.

6.  **Accessibility:**
    *   Facilities for passengers with disabilities (wheelchairs, accessible restrooms, ramps, elevators).
    *   Assistance for elderly passengers and unaccompanied minors.

**Examples:**

*   **Short queues at security:** Achieved through adequate staffing and multiple screening lanes.
*   **Clear flight information screens:** Displaying gate numbers, departure times, and delays prominently.
*   **Baggage claim services:** Efficient delivery of baggage to carousels.

**Important Point to Remember:** A positive passenger experience is crucial for an airport's reputation and commercial success.

## 3.4 Space Requirements - Design Peak Hour Demand

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Peak Hour Demand (PHD):** The maximum passenger or aircraft activity expected within a single hour. This is the critical design parameter for airport facilities.
*   **Design Hour:** The specific hour for which the airport's landside facilities are designed to handle demand. Typically, this is the 30th busiest hour of the year (PH30) or a similar high-demand period.
*   **Level of Service (LOS):** A qualitative measure describing operational conditions within a facility, based on factors like passenger density and wait times. Airports aim for specific LOS standards.
*   **Throughput:** The rate at which passengers, baggage, or aircraft can be processed.

**Calculating Space Requirements:**

Space requirements are driven by the design peak hour demand, projected passenger volumes, and desired levels of service.

**Factors Influencing Space Needs:**

1.  **Passenger Volume:** Number of departing, arriving, and connecting passengers per hour.
2.  **Passenger Mix:** Domestic vs. International, Transfer passengers.
3.  **Aircraft Mix & Size:** Determines the number of passengers arriving/departing simultaneously.
4.  **Check-in Methods:** Traditional counters vs. self-service kiosks.
5.  **Security Screening Technology:** Throughput capacity of screening equipment.
6.  **Baggage Handling System Capacity:** Rate of sorting and delivery.
7.  **Boarding Gate Requirements:** Number of aircraft at gates, gate utilization.
8.  **Concession Space:** Based on passenger dwell time and spending patterns.
9.  **Circulation Space:** Corridors, waiting areas, walkways.

**Methodology:**

*   **Forecasting:** Projecting future passenger numbers and activity levels.
*   **Peak Hour Analysis:** Identifying the peak hour of demand for departures, arrivals, and transfers.
*   **Capacity Calculations:** Determining the processing capacity of individual elements (e.g., check-in desks, security lanes).
*   **Queueing Theory:** Used to estimate wait times and required space for queues based on arrival rates and service rates.
*   **Space Norms/Standards:** Applying established guidelines for space allocation per passenger or per function.

**Example Calculation Snippet (Simplified):**

*   **Scenario:** Design peak hour demand for departing passengers is 1,000 passengers per hour.
*   **Check-in:** Assume 50% use kiosks (faster) and 50% use counters.
*   **Check-in Desk Capacity:** A traditional counter can process ~30 passengers/hour. A kiosk can process ~40 passengers/hour.
*   **Required Check-in Desks:** (500 passengers / 30 passengers/desk) + (500 passengers / 40 passengers/desk) = ~17 desks + ~13 kiosks.
*   **Space per Desk/Kiosk:** Including queueing space, circulation, and service area. Standards might suggest 15-25 sq meters per check-in position.

**Important Point to Remember:** Designing for the peak hour ensures that the airport operates efficiently and comfortably even during its busiest periods, avoiding excessive delays and passenger dissatisfaction.

## 3.5 Standards

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Airport Design Standards:** Guidelines and benchmarks for the design and performance of airport facilities, often set by international organizations or national aviation authorities.
*   **Level of Service (LOS) Standards:** Define acceptable wait times and passenger comfort levels for different airport processes.
*   **Capacity Standards:** Specify the throughput capacity required for various components.
*   **Safety & Security Standards:** Mandate specific requirements for passenger and aircraft safety.

**Key Standards & Guidelines:**

1.  **ICAO (International Civil Aviation Organization):**
    *   **Annex 14:** Aerodromes, Volume I: Aerodrome Design and Operations. Provides recommendations and requirements for airport physical characteristics, including terminal buildings and aprons.
    *   **Airport Services Manual (Doc 9137):** Offers guidance on various aspects of airport operations and design.

2.  **FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) - USA:**
    *   **Advisory Circulars (ACs):** Provide guidance on airport design and operations. Relevant ACs include those on airport terminal building design, access roads, and baggage handling.
    *   **Terminal Design Standards:** Specific requirements for gate capacity, processing areas, and passenger amenities.

3.  **Other National Authorities & Industry Best Practices:**
    *   **ACI (Airports Council International):** Develops best practices and benchmarks for airport operations and passenger experience.
    *   **Local Building Codes & Regulations:** Must also be adhered to.

**Commonly Used Standards & Benchmarks:**

*   **Check-in Counters:**
    *   **Space per Counter:** 15-25 m² (includes queue space).
    *   **Capacity:** 30-40 passengers/hour/counter (varies with automation).
*   **Security Screening Checkpoints:**
    *   **Lane Width:** Minimum 0.9m, preferably 1.2m.
    *   **Throughput:** Varies significantly with equipment (e.g., EDS/GMDs).
    *   **Queue Space:** Sufficient to accommodate peak hour demand without spilling into circulation areas.
*   **Baggage Claim Carousels:**
    *   **Length:** Typically 15-30 meters.
    *   **Space per Carousel:** Sufficient area for passenger collection and baggage recirculation.
*   **Circulation Areas (Corridors, Walkways):**
    *   **Width:** Based on peak passenger flow, often ranging from 3m to 10m or more.
    *   **Passenger Density:** Aiming for specific LOS (e.g., comfortable movement without congestion).
*   **Gate Lounges:**
    *   **Seating per Gate:** Based on aircraft capacity and expected dwell time.
    *   **Space per Passenger:** ~1.5-2.5 m² (includes circulation).
*   **Restrooms:**
    *   **Fixture Counts:** Based on passenger volume and gender split, adhering to building codes.
    *   **Accessibility:** Requirements for accessible stalls.

**Example:** A common LOS standard for security screening might aim for a maximum wait time of 10 minutes during the peak hour. This translates to a required number of screening lanes and associated space.

**Important Point to Remember:** Standards are dynamic and evolve with technology, passenger expectations, and security requirements. They provide a framework for efficient, safe, and comfortable airport operations.

## 3.6 Location Planning Concepts of Other Landside Elements

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Landside Integration:** Ensuring seamless connectivity between different landside elements and with the airside.
*   **Accessibility:** Ease of access for passengers, staff, and vehicles.
*   **Efficiency:** Minimizing travel distances and optimizing operational flows.
*   **Customer Convenience:** Providing amenities and services in logical and accessible locations.

**Location Planning for Key Landside Elements:**

1.  **Access Roads & Curbside:**
    *   **Location:** Directly in front of the terminal building for passenger drop-off/pick-up.
    *   **Concept:** Minimize walking distances from vehicles to terminal entrances. Separate lanes for different modes (private cars, taxis, buses, ride-sharing).
    *   **Considerations:** Traffic flow management, environmental impact, noise.

2.  **Parking Facilities (Short-Term & Long-Term):**
    *   **Location:** Proximity to the terminal is crucial for short-term parking. Long-term parking can be further away with shuttle services.
    *   **Concept:** Balance convenience with cost-effectiveness. Ensure clear access from roadways.
    *   **Considerations:** Capacity, pricing, accessibility for disabled parking, covered vs. uncovered.

3.  **Ground Transportation Centers (GTCs):**
    *   **Location:** Often integrated with or adjacent to the terminal building, providing a consolidated point for various transport modes.
    *   **Concept:** Facilitate easy transfer between air travel and other modes (trains, buses, taxis, rental cars).
    *   **Considerations:** Passenger flow, intermodal connectivity, customer information.

4.  **Rental Car Facilities:**
    *   **Location:** Typically located on the periphery of the airport, connected by shuttle buses or integrated within a GTC.
    *   **Concept:** Convenience for arriving passengers needing rental vehicles.
    *   **Considerations:** Shuttle frequency, branding, customer service.

5.  **Cargo Terminals:**
    *   **Location:** Often at a distance from passenger terminals to minimize interference with passenger operations, but with efficient access to runways and roads.
    *   **Concept:** Optimize logistics for freight handling, customs, and distribution.
    *   **Considerations:** Airside access, road access for trucks, security.

6.  **Airport Support Facilities (Maintenance, Operations):**
    *   **Location:** Strategically placed for efficient access to both airside and landside operations, often away from passenger areas.
    *   **Concept:** Functional efficiency and safety.
    *   **Considerations:** Noise impact, access control.

**Examples:**

*   **Centralized Parking Garages:** Directly connected to the terminal via skybridges, minimizing outdoor exposure for passengers.
*   **Integrated Ground Transportation Hubs:** Terminal 1 at Seoul Incheon International Airport integrates a train station, bus terminals, and taxi stands directly within the building structure.

**Important Point to Remember:** The location of landside elements significantly impacts passenger convenience, operational efficiency, and the overall airport experience. A well-planned landside facilitates smooth transitions between air and ground transport.

## Practice Questions and Answers

**Question 1:**
What is the primary difference between the "pier/finger" terminal concept and the "satellite" terminal concept in terms of passenger access to aircraft?

**Answer 1:**
In the **pier/finger** concept, aircraft are parked directly at the end of piers that extend from the main terminal building, allowing passengers to walk directly from the terminal to the aircraft via jet bridges. In the **satellite** concept, the aircraft are parked at a separate satellite building, and passengers are transported from the main terminal to the satellite (and vice-versa) via a shuttle system (e.g., automated people mover or bus).

---

**Question 2:**
Explain the importance of designing for "Peak Hour Demand" in airport terminal planning.

**Answer 2:**
Designing for Peak Hour Demand (PHD) is crucial because it ensures that the airport's facilities can adequately and efficiently handle the maximum expected volume of passengers and operations within a single hour. This prevents excessive wait times, overcrowding, and operational disruptions during the busiest periods, thereby maintaining a satisfactory Level of Service (LOS) and a positive passenger experience. Failure to design for PHD would lead to significant inefficiencies and passenger dissatisfaction during peak times.

---

**Question 3:**
List three key passenger requirements at a terminal building that contribute to a positive passenger experience.

**Answer 3:**
Three key passenger requirements are:
1.  **Ease of Access & Wayfinding:** Clear signage, intuitive layout, and easy connections to ground transportation.
2.  **Efficient Processing:** Minimal wait times at check-in, security, and immigration.
3.  **Comfort & Convenience:** Adequate seating, clean facilities, access to amenities like food, retail, and Wi-Fi.

---

**Question 4:**
If a new airport terminal is being designed and the projected peak hour demand for departing passengers is 1,500, what are two key landside elements that will be directly impacted by this demand in terms of space requirements?

**Answer 4:**
Two key landside elements directly impacted by the peak hour demand of 1,500 departing passengers are:
1.  **Check-in Area:** This will require sufficient counter/kiosk capacity and corresponding queueing space to process these passengers within the hour.
2.  **Security Screening Checkpoints:** This will necessitate an adequate number of screening lanes and waiting areas to process the passengers efficiently and safely.

---

**Question 5:**
Where would you typically locate rental car facilities at a large international airport, and what is the rationale behind this location?

**Answer 5:**
Rental car facilities are typically located on the periphery of the airport campus, often connected to a Ground Transportation Center (GTC) or served by dedicated shuttle buses. The rationale is to:
*   **Separate Rental Car Operations:** To keep these vehicle movements separate from the primary passenger curbside and internal airport traffic, reducing congestion.
*   **Accessibility:** While not directly at the terminal, they are made accessible to arriving passengers through clear signage and efficient shuttle services, balancing convenience with operational efficiency.
*   **Space Efficiency:** Allows for larger facilities to accommodate the fleet and customer service areas.

## Key Points to Remember

*   **Landside vs. Airside:** Understand the clear distinction and how they interface.
*   **Peak Hour Demand is King:** All space and capacity calculations are driven by this critical parameter.
*   **Passenger-Centric Design:** Prioritize passenger flow, comfort, and experience.
*   **Flexibility & Scalability:** Design for future growth and adaptability.
*   **Integration is Key:** Seamless connectivity between landside elements and between landside and airside is vital.
*   **Standards are Benchmarks:** Use ICAO, FAA, and industry best practices as guides.
*   **Location Matters:** Strategic placement of landside elements impacts efficiency and convenience.
