### **1. Introduction to Formwork in Special Conditions**

Formwork, while a fundamental aspect of concrete construction, often necessitates specialized approaches when dealing with unique structural geometries, challenging site environments, or specific project requirements. These "special conditions" demand a departure from standard formwork practices due to increased complexity in design, construction, and safety management.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Special Conditions:** Situations in concrete construction that deviate from typical flat slabs, columns, or beams, requiring tailored formwork solutions. These can include curved surfaces, large spans, high-rise structures, underwater construction, seismic zones, precast elements, and complex architectural designs.
*   **Challenges:** Increased structural loads, complex geometry, difficult access, stringent tolerances, safety risks, and the need for specialized materials and expertise.

### **2. Identifying and Explaining Special Conditions**

This section details various special conditions that necessitate specialized formwork.

#### **2.1 Curved and Complex Geometries**

**Description:** Formwork for elements with curved surfaces, such as domes, vaults, tunnels, and architectural features, presents significant challenges in achieving accurate shapes and maintaining structural integrity.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Radial Formwork:** Formwork that pivots around a central point, used for circular or conical structures.
*   **Ribbed Formwork:** Formwork constructed with ribs to support curved panels, allowing for the creation of vaulted ceilings or complex arches.
*   **In-situ Forming of Curved Surfaces:** Directly shaping formwork panels to match the desired curve, often using flexible materials or segmented panels.
*   **Pre-fabricated Curved Formwork Systems:** Modular formwork panels pre-formed to specific radii, facilitating faster erection and better accuracy.

**Examples:**

*   **Domes:** St. Peter's Basilica dome, Sydney Opera House.
*   **Tunnels:** Road tunnels, subway tunnels.
*   **Architectural Features:** Curved walls, soffits, and facades.

#### **2.2 Large Spans and Heavy Loads**

**Description:** Structures requiring long, uninterrupted spans or carrying substantial loads necessitate formwork that can withstand significant bending moments and deflection, often employing advanced structural analysis and robust support systems.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Trussed Formwork:** Incorporating trusses to provide rigidity and support over long spans.
*   **Heavy-Duty Shoring and Scaffolding:** Specialized systems designed to carry high vertical loads and resist lateral forces.
*   **Table Forms (Slab Formwork Systems):** Prefabricated units that can be erected and struck efficiently for large slabs.
*   **Cantilevered Formwork:** Formwork that extends outwards from a support, used for elements like balconies or bridge decks.

**Examples:**

*   **Bridge Decks:** Long-span bridges.
*   **Stadiums and Arenas:** Large clear-span roofs.
*   **Industrial Floors:** Heavy-duty concrete floors in factories.

#### **2.3 High-Rise Structures**

**Description:** Formwork for tall buildings requires efficient vertical movement of formwork systems, robust anchoring, and careful consideration of wind loads and climbing mechanisms.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Climbing Formwork:** Formwork that is moved vertically as the concrete structure rises.
    *   **Jump Formwork:** Formwork that is independently supported and can be "jumped" to a higher level after a pour.
    *   **Slip Formwork:** Continuous movement of formwork as concrete is poured, achieving rapid vertical construction.
    *   **Self-Climbing Formwork:** Mechanized systems that use hydraulic jacks to lift the formwork.
*   **Tunnel Formwork:** Specialized formwork for concrete core walls and shafts in high-rise buildings.
*   **Crane-Lifted Formwork:** Formwork systems that are lifted by cranes to successive levels.

**Examples:**

*   **Skyscrapers:** Burj Khalifa, Shanghai Tower.
*   **Concrete Core Walls:** Structural cores of residential and commercial buildings.
*   **Chimneys and Silos:** Tall, cylindrical structures.

#### **2.4 Tunnel and Underground Structures**

**Description:** Formwork for tunnels, subways, and underground reservoirs must accommodate often irregular excavation shapes, resist significant earth pressures, and allow for efficient movement within confined spaces.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Arch Formwork:** Specifically designed to create the curved soffit of tunnels.
*   **Traveling Formwork:** Formwork systems that can be moved along the length of a tunnel for continuous pouring.
*   **Steel Formwork:** Robust and durable for repeated use in tunnel construction.
*   **Hydraulic Systems:** Used for adjusting and moving formwork in confined and challenging environments.

**Examples:**

*   **Road and Rail Tunnels:** Channel Tunnel, Gotthard Base Tunnel.
*   **Underground Car Parks:** Parking structures below ground.
*   **Sewage and Water Conduits:** Underground pipelines.

#### **2.5 Precast Concrete Construction**

**Description:** While precast elements are formed off-site, the formwork used in precast yards requires precision, durability, and often specialized tilt-up or repetitive casting configurations.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Tilt-Up Panels:** Large concrete panels cast flat on the ground and then tilted into their vertical position. Formwork is simple but requires robust release agents and finishes.
*   **Molds for Precast Elements:** Custom-designed molds for specific precast components like beams, columns, and facade panels.
*   **Factory Production:** Formwork designed for efficient and repetitive casting in a controlled environment.

**Examples:**

*   **Precast Concrete Walls and Slabs:** Used in a wide range of buildings.
*   **Precast Bridge Girders:** Large structural components for bridges.

#### **2.6 Underwater Structures**

**Description:** Formwork for underwater concrete placement requires specialized sealing, anchoring, and methods to prevent washout of cementitious materials.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Watertight Formwork:** Formwork designed to prevent ingress of water and egress of concrete.
*   **Tremie Method:** A method for placing concrete underwater using a steel pipe to prevent segregation and washout.
*   **Grout Injection:** Used to seal formwork joints or fill voids.
*   **Floating Formwork:** Formwork that is floated into position and then sunk.

**Examples:**

*   **Bridge Piers:** Foundations for bridges in water bodies.
*   **Offshore Platforms:** Structures for oil and gas extraction.
*   **Submerged Tunnels:** Tunnels constructed below the water surface.

#### **2.7 Seismic Zone Construction**

**Description:** Formwork in seismic zones must be designed to withstand lateral forces and ensure accurate placement of reinforcement to provide ductility and prevent structural failure during earthquakes.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Ductility:** The ability of concrete members to deform significantly without fracturing.
*   **Reinforcement Detailing:** Specific arrangements of reinforcing steel to enhance seismic performance.
*   **Formwork Rigidity and Anchoring:** Ensuring formwork is robust and securely anchored to prevent movement or collapse during seismic events.
*   **Quality Control of Concrete Strength:** Crucial for seismic performance.

**Examples:**

*   **Buildings in Earthquake-Prone Regions:** California, Japan.
*   **Bridges and Overpasses:** Infrastructure designed to withstand seismic activity.

### **3. Design Considerations, Materials, and Construction Techniques**

This section delves into the specifics of how formwork is designed, what materials are used, and how it's constructed for special conditions.

#### **3.1 Design Considerations**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Load Calculations:** Comprehensive assessment of all loads, including concrete weight, fluid pressure, live loads, wind loads, seismic loads, and construction loads.
*   **Deflection Control:** Designing formwork to minimize sagging and maintain accurate shape under load.
*   **Accuracy and Tolerances:** Strict adherence to specified dimensions and geometric tolerances for complex shapes.
*   **Erection and Striking Methods:** Planning for efficient and safe assembly and disassembly of formwork, especially for climbing and moving systems.
*   **Safety:** Integrating safety features into the design, including access platforms, guardrails, and load-bearing capacity of formwork elements.
*   **Material Selection:** Choosing materials that can withstand the specific conditions (e.g., temperature, pressure, abrasion) and achieve the desired finish.
*   **Reinforcement Clearance:** Ensuring adequate space for the placement and vibration of concrete around intricate reinforcement.
*   **Vibration Effects:** Considering how concrete vibration can impact the formwork structure and fluid pressure.

#### **3.2 Materials Used**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Steel:**
    *   **Advantages:** High strength, durability, reusability, precise shapes, resistance to warping.
    *   **Applications:** Heavy-duty formwork, tunnel formwork, climbing formwork, large-span formwork.
*   **Plywood (Film-Faced or Phenolic-Coated):**
    *   **Advantages:** Versatility, good surface finish, relatively lightweight, cost-effective for many applications.
    *   **Applications:** Curved formwork (with careful bending), standard walls and slabs, architectural concrete.
*   **Aluminum:**
    *   **Advantages:** Lightweight, corrosion-resistant, reusable, good for modular systems.
    *   **Applications:** Modular formwork systems for walls and slabs, high-rise construction.
*   **Fiber-Reinforced Polymer (FRP):**
    *   **Advantages:** Lightweight, high strength-to-weight ratio, corrosion-resistant, can be molded into complex shapes.
    *   **Applications:** Specialized architectural elements, marine structures.
*   **Timber:**
    *   **Advantages:** Readily available, easy to cut and shape, cost-effective for smaller projects or temporary use.
    *   **Applications:** Residential construction, temporary bracing, some curved elements (with skilled craftsmanship).
*   **Specialty Materials:**
    *   **Rubber or PVC Membranes:** For watertight sealing.
    *   **Flexible Plastics/Composites:** For highly complex curves.

#### **3.3 Construction Techniques**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Prefabrication:** Manufacturing formwork components off-site for faster assembly on-site, improving quality control and reducing site congestion.
*   **Modular Systems:** Using interchangeable formwork panels and components to create various shapes and sizes, enhancing efficiency.
*   **Climbing and Sliding:**
    *   **Jump Forming:** Formwork is anchored to the hardened concrete and can be moved up in stages.
    *   **Slip Forming:** Continuous process where formwork moves upwards as concrete is poured, achieving rapid vertical construction of elements like silos, chimneys, or core walls.
*   **Traveling Formwork:** Formwork that moves horizontally along a structure, typically used in tunnel construction.
*   **Crane and Hoist Utilization:** Essential for lifting and positioning heavy formwork components, especially in high-rise projects.
*   **Hydraulic Systems:** Employed for lifting, adjusting, and moving formwork in climbing, slip, and tunneling operations.
*   **Sealing and Joint Treatment:** Critical for underwater and watertight formwork to prevent leakage.
*   **Temporary Bracing and Support:** Ensuring the stability of formwork during construction, especially for elements subjected to lateral forces or significant loads.

### **4. Planning, Management, and Safety Aspects**

Effective project planning, diligent management, and rigorous safety protocols are paramount for formwork in special conditions.

#### **4.1 Planning**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Detailed Formwork Drawings:** Comprehensive plans showing dimensions, materials, connections, support systems, and erection sequence.
*   **Sequencing and Scheduling:** Developing a logical order for formwork erection, concrete pouring, and striking, considering interdependencies.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Planning for personnel, equipment (cranes, hoists, welding machines), and materials.
*   **Logistics and Site Access:** Managing the delivery and movement of large or heavy formwork components, especially in confined or high-rise sites.
*   **Risk Assessment and Mitigation:** Identifying potential hazards and developing strategies to minimize risks.
*   **Communication Plan:** Establishing clear communication channels between all stakeholders (designers, contractors, site supervisors).

#### **4.2 Management**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Site Supervision:** Experienced supervisors to oversee formwork erection, concrete placement, and striking.
*   **Progress Monitoring:** Tracking the progress of formwork construction against the schedule.
*   **Cost Control:** Managing expenses related to formwork materials, labor, and equipment.
*   **Quality Assurance:** Ensuring that formwork is constructed to the required standards and specifications.
*   **Change Management:** Procedures for handling any modifications to the original formwork plan.
*   **Coordination with Other Trades:** Ensuring seamless integration with reinforcement fixing, concrete supply, and other construction activities.

#### **4.3 Safety**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Fall Prevention:** Guardrails, safety nets, scaffolding, and mandatory use of personal protective equipment (PPE) like harnesses.
*   **Load Bearing Capacity:** Ensuring that all formwork components, bracing, and supports can safely carry the intended loads.
*   **Stability During Erection and Striking:** Implementing procedures to maintain formwork stability during assembly and disassembly, especially for tall or cantilevered elements.
*   **Working at Height Regulations:** Strict adherence to regulations governing work performed at significant elevations.
*   **Material Handling:** Safe lifting and moving of heavy or awkward formwork components using appropriate equipment.
*   **Electrical Safety:** Ensuring safe use of electrical equipment on site.
*   **Fire Safety:** Awareness of fire hazards, especially with timber formwork or flammable materials.
*   **Toolbox Talks:** Regular safety briefings for site personnel.
*   **Emergency Preparedness:** Having plans in place for accidents and emergencies.

**Important Point to Remember:** Safety in special conditions is paramount due to the increased complexity and potential for higher-risk activities. A proactive and comprehensive safety management system is non-negotiable.

### **5. Quality Control and Inspection Procedures**

Ensuring the quality of formwork in special conditions is critical for the structural integrity and dimensional accuracy of the final concrete element.

#### **5.1 Quality Control (QC) Measures**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Pre-Construction Checks:**
    *   **Material Inspection:** Verifying that materials used meet specifications (e.g., strength of timber, type of plywood coating, integrity of steel components).
    *   **Fabrication Inspection:** Checking the accuracy of pre-fabricated formwork components.
    *   **Toolbox Talks:** Briefing workers on quality requirements and specific techniques.
*   **During Construction Checks:**
    *   **Dimensional Accuracy:** Regularly measuring formwork to ensure it conforms to design drawings.
    *   **Alignment and Verticality/Horizontalit:** Checking that formwork is plumb and level as per design.
    *   **Bracing and Support:** Verifying that all supports and bracing are correctly installed and securely fixed.
    *   **Formwork Joint Integrity:** Ensuring joints are properly sealed to prevent leakage, especially for watertight requirements.
    *   **Reinforcement Clearance:** Checking that adequate space is maintained for reinforcement.
    *   **Release Agent Application:** Ensuring uniform application of release agents for ease of striking and good concrete finish.
    *   **Cleanness of Formwork:** Ensuring formwork is free from debris, wood shavings, and excess mortar.
*   **Pre-Pour Inspection:**
    *   **Final Dimensional Checks:** Last-minute verification of all critical dimensions.
    *   **Reinforcement Placement:** Checking that reinforcement is correctly positioned and tied.
    *   **Embedments and Block-outs:** Verifying the correct placement of any inserts or openings.
    *   **Formwork Stability:** A final assessment of the overall stability of the erected formwork.
    *   **Cleanness:** Ensuring the formwork cavity is clean and free from foreign matter.

#### **5.2 Inspection Procedures**

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Independent Inspection:** Formwork inspections are often carried out by a dedicated quality control inspector or an independent third party.
*   **Checklists and Inspection Forms:** Using standardized forms to record all inspection points and findings.
*   **Marking and Sign-off:** Defective work should be clearly marked, and approved work should be signed off by the inspector.
*   **Hold Points:** Specific stages in the formwork construction where work cannot proceed until an inspection is successfully completed.
*   **Corrective Actions:** Documenting and tracking any necessary rework or modifications to address deficiencies.
*   **Documentation:** Maintaining comprehensive records of all inspections, test results, and remedial actions.

**Important Point to Remember:** In special conditions, more frequent and rigorous inspections are usually required compared to standard formwork. The criticality of the structure and the complexity of the formwork dictate the intensity of the QC program.

### **6. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1 (LO1, LO2):**
Describe three distinct "special conditions" that would necessitate a departure from standard formwork practices. For each condition, briefly explain why specialized formwork is required.

**Question 2 (LO3):**
When constructing formwork for a long-span bridge deck, what are the primary design considerations you would focus on, and what types of materials would be most suitable?

**Question 3 (LO4):**
Discuss the key planning and safety management considerations for implementing jump formwork in a high-rise building project.

**Question 4 (LO5):**
What are the critical checks performed during a pre-pour inspection of formwork for a complex architectural concrete feature?

**Question 5 (LO3, LO4):**

### **Answers to Practice Questions**

**Answer 1:**
1.  **Curved Geometries (e.g., Dome):** Requires formwork that can accurately replicate the specific curve, often involving flexible materials or segmented panels. Standard rectangular formwork cannot achieve this.
2.  **Large Spans (e.g., Stadium Roof):** Needs formwork systems capable of carrying significant loads over long distances without excessive deflection. This involves robust shoring, trusses, or table forms, unlike simple props for shorter spans.
3.  **High-Rise Structures (e.g., Skyscraper Core):** Requires formwork that can be efficiently moved vertically as construction progresses. This necessitates climbing or slip formwork systems, which are specialized for continuous vertical progress, unlike static formwork.

**Answer 2:**
*   **Design Considerations:**
    *   **Load Bearing Capacity:** To support the weight of fresh concrete, reinforcement, workers, and equipment.
    *   **Deflection Control:** To ensure the deck maintains its shape and meets flatness tolerances.
    *   **Lateral Stability:** To resist wind loads during construction.
    *   **Erection and Striking Efficiency:** Especially if using repetitive systems.
    *   **Connection Strength:** To ensure formwork components are securely joined.
*   **Suitable Materials:**
    *   **Steel:** For heavy-duty shoring, beams, and panels due to its strength and reusability.
    *   **Heavy-duty Plywood (Film-Faced):** For the deck surface to provide a good finish and load-carrying capacity.
    *   **Engineered Lumber/Trusses:** For supporting long spans, providing rigidity.
    *   **Specialized Heavy-Duty Scaffolding/Shoring Systems:** To provide adequate vertical support.

**Answer 3:**
*   **Planning Considerations:**
    *   **Detailed Drawings:** Showing the climbing mechanism, jacking points, anchorages, and access platforms.
    *   **Sequencing:** Defining the precise cycle for concreting, climbing, and subsequent operations.
    *   **Resource Allocation:** Ensuring availability of climbing jacks, crane access for materials, and skilled labor.
    *   **Site Logistics:** Managing the storage and movement of formwork components within a potentially congested high-rise site.
    *   **Wind Load Assessment:** Crucial for stability at height.
*   **Safety Management:**
    *   **Fall Prevention:** Mandatory use of harnesses, secure guardrails around all open edges and climbing platforms.
    *   **Load Capacity Checks:** Ensuring the climbing system and concrete structures supporting it can handle the loads.
    *   **Secure Anchoring:** Verifying that anchor points are correctly installed and load-rated.
    *   **Controlled Climbing Operations:** Only authorized and trained personnel should operate climbing jacks.
    *   **Working at Height Protocols:** Strict adherence to all site safety rules for working at height.
    *   **Toolbox Talks:** Focusing on the specific risks of climbing formwork.

**Answer 4:**
The critical checks during a pre-pour inspection for a complex architectural concrete feature include:
*   **Final Dimensional Accuracy:** Verifying all curves, angles, and overall dimensions against the design drawings.
*   **Surface Finish Requirements:** Checking for any damage to the form face that could affect the final concrete surface quality.
*   **Formwork Integrity and Sealing:** Ensuring all joints are tight to prevent grout leakage and that the formwork is structurally sound to withstand concrete pressure.
*   **Reinforcement Clearance and Position:** Confirming reinforcement is correctly placed with adequate cover and spacing within the complex geometry.
*   **Embedments and Block-outs:** Verifying that any required anchorages, conduits, or openings are precisely positioned.
*   **Cleanness of the Formwork Cavity:** Ensuring no debris, form oil residues, or foreign objects are inside the formwork.
*   **Access and Egress:** Confirming safe access for concrete placement and vibration, and clear egress.

**Answer 5:**
*   **Material Choices:**
    *   **Steel Formwork:** For the primary tunnel lining, offering durability, high reusability, and ability to form precise curves.
    *   **Rubber or PVC Membranes:** To seal joints and prevent water ingress/concrete egress.
    *   **Hydraulic Jacks/Cylinders:** For adjusting and moving the formwork.
*   **Construction Techniques:**
    *   **Traveling Formwork:** The formwork will be designed to be moved along the tunnel axis after each pour.
    *   **Arch Segmental Design:** The formwork will likely consist of several hinged segments to facilitate assembly, movement, and concrete placement.
    *   **Hydraulic Power Packs:** To operate the jacking systems for moving and adjusting the formwork.
    *   **Heavy-duty wheels or rail systems:** To support and guide the traveling formwork.
*   **Safety Precautions:**
    *   **Tunnel Ventilation:** Ensuring adequate air quality for workers.
    *   **Lighting:** Providing sufficient illumination within the tunnel.
    *   **Emergency Exits and Communication:** Clear pathways and communication systems.
    *   **Ground Support Assessment:** Ensuring the excavated tunnel walls are stable before formwork is erected.
    *   **Load Bearing of Traveling Mechanism:** Ensuring the mechanism can safely support the formwork and concrete.
    *   **Safe Striking and Erection Procedures:** Detailed plans for moving and setting up the formwork for the next pour.
    *   **PPE:** Including hard hats, safety boots, high-visibility clothing, and potentially respiratory protection.

### **Important Points to Remember Summary:**

*   **Special conditions demand specialized solutions:** Standard formwork techniques are often insufficient for complex geometries, heavy loads, high-rise structures, tunnels, and other unique scenarios.
*   **Design is paramount:** Thorough load calculations, deflection control, and accuracy are critical for the success and safety of special formwork.
*   **Material selection matters:** Choosing the right materials (steel, specialized plywood, aluminum) is crucial for durability, accuracy, and performance under specific conditions.
*   **Planning and management are key:** Effective logistics, sequencing, resource allocation, and progress monitoring are essential for timely and cost-effective completion.
*   **Safety is non-negotiable:** Increased complexity in special conditions often translates to higher risks; robust safety protocols, fall prevention, and load-bearing checks are vital.
*   **Quality control is intensified:** More frequent and rigorous inspections are required to ensure dimensional accuracy, structural integrity, and adherence to specifications.
*   **Skilled personnel are necessary:** Working with specialized formwork systems often requires experienced formworkers, engineers, and supervisors.
