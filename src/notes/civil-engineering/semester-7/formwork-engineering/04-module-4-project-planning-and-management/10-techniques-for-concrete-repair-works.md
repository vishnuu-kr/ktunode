# Formwork Engineering: Module 4 - Project Planning and Management

## Topic: Techniques for Concrete Repair Works

This module focuses on the practical aspects of managing concrete repair projects. Understanding the various techniques available is crucial for selecting the most appropriate method based on the damage, structural requirements, and site conditions.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

*   **Identify and describe common types of concrete deterioration and their causes.**
*   **Explain the principles and advantages/disadvantages of different concrete repair techniques.**
*   **Outline the key steps involved in planning and executing concrete repair works.**
*   **Understand the importance of material selection, quality control, and safety in concrete repair.**
*   **Discuss the role of formwork in concrete repair operations.**

### 1. Understanding Concrete Deterioration and Its Causes

Before selecting a repair technique, it's essential to diagnose the problem accurately.

*   **Common Types of Deterioration:**
    *   **Cracking:**
        *   **Causes:** Shrinkage (plastic, drying), thermal expansion/contraction, applied loads, settlement, alkali-silica reaction (ASR), freeze-thaw cycles.
        *   **Impact:** Reduces aesthetic appeal, compromises watertightness, allows ingress of aggressive substances.
    *   **Spalling:**
        *   **Definition:** Localized breaking or chipping of the concrete surface.
        *   **Causes:** Corrosion of reinforcement (rust expansion), freeze-thaw damage (ice expansion), impact, ASR.
        *   **Impact:** Exposes reinforcement, compromises structural integrity, creates hazards.
    *   **Chipping:**
        *   **Definition:** Similar to spalling but often at edges or corners due to impact or abrasion.
        *   **Causes:** Mechanical damage, improper handling of precast elements.
        *   **Impact:** Reduces durability, aesthetics.
    *   **Surface Wear/Abrasion:**
        *   **Causes:** Traffic (vehicular, pedestrian), exposure to abrasive materials, poor quality concrete mix.
        *   **Impact:** Reduces cover to reinforcement, weakens the surface.
    *   **Chemical Attack:**
        *   **Causes:** Exposure to acids, sulfates, chlorides, carbonation.
        *   **Impact:** Leaching of cement paste, disintegration of concrete, corrosion of reinforcement.
    *   **Corrosion of Reinforcement:**
        *   **Causes:** Ingress of chlorides (from de-icing salts or seawater), carbonation of concrete (reducing alkalinity).
        *   **Impact:** Rust formation leads to expansion, causing cracking and spalling of the concrete cover.

*   **Diagnostic Steps:**
    *   **Visual Inspection:** Identify visible signs of distress (cracks, spalling, efflorescence).
    *   **Non-Destructive Testing (NDT):**
        *   **Schmidt Hammer (Rebound Hammer):** Estimates surface hardness and compressive strength.
        *   **Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity (UPV):** Detects internal flaws, cracks, and assesses concrete quality.
        *   **Covermeter:** Locates reinforcement and measures concrete cover.
        *   **Half-Cell Potential:** Indicates the likelihood of active reinforcement corrosion.
    *   **Destructive Testing:** Core sampling and laboratory analysis for strength, chemical composition, and chloride content.

### 2. Principles and Techniques for Concrete Repair

The choice of repair technique depends on the nature and extent of the damage, the required performance of the repaired element, and economic considerations.

#### 2.1. Crack Repair Techniques

*   **Epoxy Injection:**
    *   **Principle:** Low-viscosity epoxy resin is injected under pressure into cracks to bond the concrete faces.
    *   **Advantages:** High strength, excellent adhesion, seals cracks against water and aggressive agents, can restore structural integrity.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can be expensive, requires specialized equipment, effectiveness depends on crack width and cleanliness.
    *   **Application:** Structural cracks, bonding delaminated concrete.
*   **Polyurethane Injection:**
    *   **Principle:** Flexible polyurethane resins are injected to seal cracks and prevent water ingress.
    *   **Advantages:** Flexible, good for sealing against water, can accommodate some movement.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Not typically for structural bonding.
    *   **Application:** Sealing non-structural cracks against water.
*   **Cementitious Grouting:**
    *   **Principle:** Fine cementitious materials are injected into cracks.
    *   **Advantages:** Cost-effective, good for wider cracks.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Lower strength and adhesion compared to epoxies, can be prone to shrinkage.
    *   **Application:** Non-structural cracks, filling voids.
*   **Routing and Sealing:**
    *   **Principle:** Cracks are widened and then filled with a flexible sealant (e.g., polysulfide, silicone).
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, cost-effective for cosmetic repairs.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Does not restore structural integrity, primarily for aesthetics and water-tightness.
    *   **Application:** Non-structural cracks, expansion joints.

#### 2.2. Spall/Chipping Repair Techniques

*   **Patch Repair (Mortar/Concrete):**
    *   **Principle:** Damaged concrete is removed, the substrate is prepared, and a new repair mortar or concrete is applied.
    *   **Advantages:** Relatively simple, can be done on-site.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can suffer from bond failure and shrinkage cracking if not properly executed, compatibility issues with original concrete.
    *   **Key Steps:**
        1.  **Removal of loose/damaged concrete:** To sound concrete.
        2.  **Cleaning:** Thoroughly clean the substrate.
        3.  **Bonding Agent:** Apply an epoxy or polymer-modified bonding agent.
        4.  **Placement:** Apply the repair mortar/concrete, ensuring good compaction.
        5.  **Curing:** Proper curing is essential.
*   **Polymer Concrete/Mortar Repair:**
    *   **Principle:** Repair materials use polymer binders (e.g., epoxy, acrylic, polyurethane) mixed with aggregates.
    *   **Advantages:** Excellent adhesion, high strength, good chemical resistance, low shrinkage.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Can be more expensive than cementitious repairs, require careful mixing and application.
    *   **Application:** High-performance repairs, areas requiring chemical resistance.
*   **Overlay/Resurfacing:**
    *   **Principle:** A new layer of repair material is applied over the entire surface to provide a uniform finish and protect the substrate.
    *   **Advantages:** Restores surface profile, protects against wear and environmental attack, can improve aesthetics.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires careful surface preparation, can add thickness to the element.
    *   **Application:** Worn floors, bridge decks, dam surfaces.
*   **Shotcrete/Sprayed Concrete:**
    *   **Principle:** Concrete is propelled at high velocity onto a prepared surface.
    *   **Advantages:** Rapid application, good for complex shapes and overhead work, high density and low permeability.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Requires specialized equipment and skilled operators, rebound can cause waste and affect quality.
    *   **Application:** Tunnel linings, retaining walls, repairs to large structures.
        *   **Dry-Mix Shotcrete:** Cement and aggregate are mixed, water is added at the nozzle.
        *   **Wet-Mix Shotcrete:** All ingredients are mixed before being conveyed to the nozzle.
*   **Cathodic Protection:**
    *   **Principle:** An electrochemical process that arrests the corrosion of steel reinforcement. It can be applied in conjunction with other repair methods.
    *   **Advantages:** Addresses the root cause of corrosion.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Costly, requires ongoing monitoring.
    *   **Application:** Structures with active reinforcement corrosion.

#### 2.3. Reinforcement Protection

*   **Concrete Repair with Concrete Removal and Replacement:**
    *   **Principle:** Spalled concrete is removed to expose the reinforcement, the reinforcement is cleaned (wire brushing, abrasive blasting), treated with a rust inhibitor, and then the concrete is replaced.
    *   **Advantages:** Addresses corrosion and removes damaged concrete.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Risk of disbondment if not properly executed.
*   **Priming Reinforcement:**
    *   **Principle:** After cleaning, a protective coating (e.g., epoxy-based primer) is applied to the exposed reinforcement to prevent re-bar corrosion.
    *   **Advantages:** Provides a barrier against corrosion.
    *   **Disadvantages:** An additional step, potential for coating damage during concrete placement.

### 3. Planning and Executing Concrete Repair Works

A well-structured plan is essential for successful and efficient repair.

*   **Phase 1: Assessment and Planning:**
    *   **Detailed Investigation:** Identify the cause and extent of deterioration.
    *   **Define Repair Objectives:** What is the desired outcome (structural, aesthetic, protective)?
    *   **Select Repair Technique(s):** Based on investigation and objectives.
    *   **Material Selection:** Choose appropriate repair materials compatible with the original concrete and the repair method.
    *   **Develop Work Sequence and Program:** Outline the steps, schedule, and resource allocation.
    *   **Health and Safety Plan:** Identify hazards and implement control measures.
    *   **Quality Management Plan:** Define quality control checks and acceptance criteria.
    *   **Environmental Considerations:** Manage waste and dust.

*   **Phase 2: Preparation:**
    *   **Site Mobilization:** Set up scaffolding, safety barriers, and access equipment.
    *   **Demolition/Removal:** Safely remove damaged concrete to the specified limits.
    *   **Cleaning:** Thoroughly clean the substrate of dust, debris, and contaminants.
    *   **Reinforcement Preparation:** Clean, treat (if required), and ensure reinforcement is sound.
    *   **Formwork Erection (if required):** Construct and secure formwork to contain the repair material.

*   **Phase 3: Execution:**
    *   **Application of Repair Materials:** Apply chosen repair materials according to manufacturer's instructions and project specifications.
    *   **Compaction:** Ensure proper compaction of repair materials to eliminate voids.
    *   **Finishing:** Achieve the desired surface finish.
    *   **Curing:** Implement appropriate curing methods to ensure strength development and durability.

*   **Phase 4: Post-Repair and Monitoring:**
    *   **Quality Control Checks:** Conduct inspections and tests to verify the repair quality.
    *   **Formwork Removal:** Remove formwork carefully after sufficient strength gain.
    *   **Protection:** Apply any final protective coatings or sealants.
    *   **Monitoring:** Periodically inspect the repaired area to ensure its long-term performance.

### 4. Material Selection, Quality Control, and Safety

These aspects are paramount for the success of any concrete repair project.

*   **Material Selection:**
    *   **Compatibility:** Repair materials should be compatible with the existing concrete in terms of:
        *   **Coefficient of Thermal Expansion:** To minimize differential expansion/contraction stresses.
        *   **Modulus of Elasticity:** To ensure load sharing.
        *   **Chemical Properties:** To avoid reactions that degrade the bond or original concrete.
    *   **Durability:** Select materials that can withstand the service environment.
    *   **Performance Requirements:** Choose materials that meet the structural, mechanical, and durability needs of the repair.
    *   **Manufacturer's Recommendations:** Always follow guidelines from reputable manufacturers.

*   **Quality Control (QC) and Quality Assurance (QA):**
    *   **Incoming Material Inspection:** Verify that materials meet specifications.
    *   **Mix Proportioning and Mixing:** Ensure correct ratios and thorough mixing.
    *   **Application Control:** Monitor workmanship, temperature, humidity, and application rates.
    *   **Testing:**
        *   **Freshly Mixed Material Tests:** Slump, flow, temperature.
        *   **Hardened Material Tests:** Compressive strength (cubes/cylinders), bond strength, adhesion tests, flexural strength.
    *   **Documentation:** Maintain detailed records of all inspections and tests.

*   **Health and Safety:**
    *   **Risk Assessment:** Identify potential hazards (falls from height, dust inhalation, chemical exposure, electrical hazards, mechanical hazards).
    *   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Ensure availability and proper use of safety helmets, goggles, gloves, respirators, safety footwear, and harnesses.
    *   **Scaffolding and Edge Protection:** Ensure safe working platforms and guardrails.
    *   **Dust Control:** Implement measures like water suppression or dust extraction systems, especially during concrete removal and abrasive blasting.
    *   **Chemical Handling:** Follow safety data sheets (SDS) for all chemicals.
    *   **Electrical Safety:** Ensure all electrical tools and equipment are properly earthed and maintained.
    *   **Emergency Procedures:** Have clear plans for accidents and emergencies.

### 5. The Role of Formwork in Concrete Repair

While formwork is often associated with new construction, it plays a critical role in certain concrete repair operations.

*   **Purpose of Formwork in Repairs:**
    *   **Containment:** To hold repair materials (especially in vertical, overhead, or complex configurations) in place until they gain sufficient strength and cohesion.
    *   **Shaping:** To achieve a specific profile or finish for the repaired section.
    *   **Support:** To provide temporary support during the repair process.

*   **Types of Formwork Used in Repairs:**
    *   **Traditional Timber or Plywood Formwork:** Versatile and cost-effective for many situations.
    *   **Steel Formwork:** Durable, reusable, and can create precise shapes, often used for specific profiles or repetitive elements.
    *   **Fiberglass or Composite Formwork:** Lightweight and can be molded into complex shapes, suitable for intricate repairs.
    *   **In-Situ Formwork (e.g., permanent formwork):** Sometimes a special material (like geofabric, stainless steel mesh) is used as formwork that remains in place after the repair material hardens. This is common in shotcrete or sprayed applications.

*   **Considerations for Repair Formwork:**
    *   **Design:** Must be designed to withstand the pressure of the wet repair material and any imposed loads.
    *   **Erection:** Needs to be erected accurately to ensure the correct shape and dimensions.
    *   **Fixing:** Securely fixed to the existing concrete to prevent movement or leakage.
    *   **Release Agents:** Appropriate release agents are crucial to prevent the formwork from sticking to the hardened repair material, especially with polymer-based repairs.
    *   **Removal:** Formwork should only be removed after the repair material has achieved sufficient strength to support itself and resist any remaining stresses. The timing of removal is critical and often dictated by the properties of the repair material and the application.
    *   **Sealing:** Gaps between formwork and the existing concrete must be sealed to prevent leakage of the repair material.

*   **Specific Examples:**
    *   **Repairing a damaged beam:** Formwork is erected underneath and on the sides to hold a new concrete or mortar infill.
    *   **Repairing a spalled column:** Formwork is placed around the damaged section to contain a new layer of repair material.
    *   **Repairing the underside of a concrete slab:** Formwork is essential to support the new repair material.
    *   **Shotcrete repairs to tunnel linings:** Often uses specialized steel or polymer formwork, or a geofabric liner acts as formwork.

### Practice Questions/Exercises:

1.  **Scenario:** A concrete bridge deck is showing signs of spalling and cracking due to de-icing salt ingress. The spalling is localized, exposing rusted reinforcement bars in some areas.
    *   **Question:** What are the likely causes of this deterioration?
    *   **Answer:** Corrosion of reinforcement due to chloride ingress from de-icing salts. The expansion of rust causes concrete cracking and spalling.

2.  **Scenario:** For the bridge deck mentioned above, you need to perform repairs. The primary goal is to restore structural integrity and protect the deck from further deterioration.
    *   **Question:** Describe a suitable sequence of repair steps, including the potential role of formwork.
    *   **Answer:**
        1.  **Assessment:** Identify extent of damage, test for chloride levels and reinforcement activity.
        2.  **Preparation:** Remove all spalled and deteriorated concrete to sound concrete, exposing a sufficient length of sound reinforcement.
        3.  **Reinforcement Treatment:** Clean reinforcement bars to bright metal, apply a suitable rust inhibitor or epoxy primer.
        4.  **Concrete Removal:** Carefully remove unsound concrete to create a neat edge for the repair.
        5.  **Formwork Erection:** Erect formwork where needed to contain the new repair material, especially at the edges of the deck or where material needs to be held in place for curing. This could be timber or steel formwork.
        6.  **Bonding Agent:** Apply an appropriate bonding agent (e.g., epoxy resin) to the prepared concrete substrate and reinforcement.
        7.  **Repair Material Application:** Place a compatible, high-performance repair mortar or polymer concrete, ensuring good compaction. For larger areas, shotcrete might be considered.
        8.  **Finishing:** Level and finish the repair to match the surrounding deck profile.
        9.  **Curing:** Implement effective curing for the repair material.
        10. **Optional Overlays:** Consider an overlay system (e.g., a polymer-modified concrete overlay or a protective coating) for long-term protection of the entire deck.
        11. **Formwork Removal:** Remove formwork only after the repair material has gained sufficient strength.

3.  **Question:** Differentiate between epoxy injection and cementitious grouting for crack repair. When would you choose one over the other?
    *   **Answer:**
        *   **Epoxy Injection:** Uses low-viscosity epoxy resin. Offers high strength, excellent adhesion, and seals cracks against water/chemicals. Ideal for structural crack repair and bonding delaminated concrete. More expensive.
        *   **Cementitious Grouting:** Uses fine cementitious materials. Cost-effective, good for wider cracks. Lower strength and adhesion compared to epoxies, can be prone to shrinkage. Suitable for non-structural cracks and filling voids.

4.  **Question:** What are the critical factors to consider when selecting a repair material for concrete?
    *   **Answer:** Compatibility (thermal expansion, modulus of elasticity), Durability, Performance Requirements (strength, permeability), Chemical Resistance, Shrinkage properties, and Manufacturer's recommendations.

### Important Points to Remember:

*   **Diagnose before you repair:** Understanding the cause of deterioration is crucial for selecting the correct repair method and preventing recurrence.
*   **Surface preparation is key:** The success of most repairs hinges on meticulous surface preparation, including the removal of all damaged material and thorough cleaning.
*   **Compatibility is paramount:** Repair materials must be compatible with the original concrete to avoid bond failures and differential movement.
*   **Follow manufacturer's instructions:** Repair materials have specific application requirements that must be adhered to for optimal performance.
*   **Curing is vital:** Proper curing ensures the repair material develops its designed strength and durability.
*   **Safety first:** Always prioritize safety during all stages of concrete repair work.
*   **Formwork plays a crucial role:** Don't underestimate the importance of well-designed and properly executed formwork in containing and shaping repair materials.
*   **Quality control is non-negotiable:** Implement a robust QC program to ensure the repair meets all project specifications.
