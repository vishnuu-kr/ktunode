# FORMWORK ENGINEERING - Module 2: Basic Principles of Formwork Design

## Topic: Foundations, Walls, Columns, Beams, and Slabs

---

### **Introduction**

Formwork is the temporary or permanent mould or framework into which concrete is poured and allowed to harden. It plays a crucial role in achieving the desired shape, size, and finish of concrete structures. This module focuses on the basic principles of formwork design for common structural elements.

*   **LO2: Understand the specific formwork requirements for foundations.**
*   **LO3: Understand the specific formwork requirements for walls.**

### **1. Fundamental Principles of Formwork Design (LO1)**

Formwork design is governed by several key principles to ensure safety, efficiency, and quality of the finished concrete structure.

*   **Strength:**
    *   Formwork must be strong enough to withstand all vertical and lateral loads imposed by the wet concrete, construction activities, and environmental factors.
    *   **Loads include:**
        *   **Dead Load:** Weight of concrete, formwork itself, and embedded components.
        *   **Live Load:** Weight of workers, equipment, and materials during construction.
        *   **Lateral Pressure:** Hydrostatic pressure of wet concrete, wind pressure, and pressure from bracing.
        *   **Impact Loads:** From concrete discharge and vibration.
    *   **Important Point:** Underestimation of loads is a common cause of formwork failures.
*   **Rigidity:**
    *   Formwork must be sufficiently rigid to prevent deformation or deflection under load.
    *   Excessive deflection can lead to:
        *   Inaccurate dimensions of the concrete element.
        *   Cracking of concrete due to stress concentration.
        *   Poor surface finish.
    *   **Design Consideration:** Stiffening members (e.g., battens, wales) and adequate bracing are crucial for rigidity.
*   **Stability:**
    *   Formwork must be stable against overturning, buckling, and sliding.
    *   This is achieved through proper bracing, shoring, and anchorage.
    *   **Example:** Against overturning: Ensure base plates or mudsills are appropriately sized and leveled. Against buckling: Use appropriate bracing for vertical supports.
*   **Serviceability/Workability:**
    *   Formwork should be designed for ease of erection, adjustment, and dismantling.
    *   Considerations include:
        *   **Ease of Assembly:** Using standardized components, simple connections.
        *   **Ease of Stripping:** Designing for clean release without damaging the concrete surface.
        *   **Access:** Providing adequate space for concrete placement, finishing, and inspection.
        *   **Tolerances:** Formwork must be set out to achieve the required dimensional tolerances of the concrete element.
*   **Economy:**
    *   Formwork should be designed and constructed in a cost-effective manner.
    *   This involves:
        *   **Minimizing Material Usage:** Optimizing design to reduce the amount of timber, steel, or other materials.
        *   **Reusability:** Designing for multiple reuses of formwork components.
        *   **Standardization:** Using standard sizes of formwork panels and accessories.
        *   **Labor Efficiency:** Minimizing erection and dismantling time.
*   **Safety:**
    *   This is paramount. Formwork must be designed and erected to ensure the safety of workers and the public.
    *   Key safety aspects include:
        *   Adequate support and bracing.
        *   Safe access and working platforms.
        *   Protection against falling objects.
        *   Proper removal of formwork.

### **2. Formwork for Foundations (LO2)**

Foundations are the part of the structure that transfers loads to the ground. Formwork for foundations must be robust and accurate to ensure the correct dimensions and placement of foundation elements.

*   **Types of Foundations and their Formwork:**
    *   **Strip Footings:**
        *   **Description:** Continuous concrete strip supporting load-bearing walls.
        *   **Formwork:** Typically comprises timber or steel boards (soldiers) held in place by stakes driven into the ground. The width and depth of the footing dictate the formwork dimensions.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Excavation:** Formwork is often placed directly within a trench. The trench walls can sometimes act as formwork if the soil is stable.
            *   **Leveling:** Ensuring the bottom of the formwork is level is critical for consistent footing depth.
            *   **Bracing:** Lateral bracing might be needed if trench walls are unstable.
    *   **Pad Footings (Isolated Footings):**
        *   **Description:** Square or rectangular concrete pads supporting individual columns.
        *   **Formwork:** Usually formed by four sides of timber or steel panels. The panels are held together by walings (horizontal members) and tie rods or by props from the outside.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Dimensions:** Accurate setting out of dimensions is crucial for column placement.
            *   **Bracing:** External bracing is required to support the formwork sides.
            *   **Keyways/Dowels:** Provision for dowel bars or starter bars for the column must be made.
    *   **Raft Foundations:**
        *   **Description:** A large, thick slab of concrete supporting the entire structure, used when soil bearing capacity is low.
        *   **Formwork:** Forms the perimeter of the raft and any internal upstands or drop panels. Similar principles to slab formwork apply but on a larger scale.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Containment:** Ensuring the concrete is contained within the intended footprint.
            *   **Edge Beams/Upstands:** Formwork for these features needs to be robust.
*   **General Principles for Foundation Formwork:**
    *   **Ground Bearing:** Formwork must be supported on firm ground. If the ground is soft, a timber base or concrete pour might be needed to provide a stable base.
    *   **Anchoring:** Formwork needs to be anchored to prevent movement due to the pressure of concrete.
    *   **Reinforcement Clearance:** Ensure adequate space is left for reinforcement bars.
    *   **Stripping:** Forms can often be stripped relatively soon after concrete placement, especially for footing sides, as they are not typically load-bearing in the early stages.

### **3. Formwork for Walls (LO3)**

Wall formwork encloses the vertical space where concrete is poured to form structural or non-structural walls.

*   **Types of Walls and their Formwork:**
    *   **Solid Walls:**
        *   **Description:** Traditional concrete walls.
        *   **Formwork:** Typically consists of two vertical panels (form faces) held apart by ties and braced from the outside.
        *   **Key Components:**
            *   **Form Faces:** Plywood, steel panels, or timber boards.
            *   **Walings/Wedges:** Horizontal members to tie the form faces together and support vertical members.
            *   **Tie Rods:** Steel rods passing through the wall to maintain the correct distance between form faces and resist lateral pressure.
            *   **Bracing:** To prevent the formwork from overturning or buckling.
            *   **Access Platforms:** For working at height.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Lateral Pressure:** The hydrostatic pressure of wet concrete is significant and increases with the depth of pour. This is a primary design consideration.
            *   **Tie Rod Spacing:** Determined by the pressure and strength of the form faces and walings.
            *   **Ply Strength and Sagging:** The form face material must be strong enough to span between walings without excessive deflection.
            *   **Reinforcement Clearance:** Adequate cover to reinforcement must be maintained.
            *   **Lift Height:** The height of concrete poured in one lift affects the lateral pressure. Higher lifts mean higher pressure.
    *   **Core Walls (e.g., Stairwells, Service Cores):**
        *   **Description:** Often tall, relatively thin walls forming internal cores.
        *   **Formwork:** Can be slip-formed, jump-formed, or climbing.
            *   **Jump Form:** Formwork that is advanced to a new position once the concrete has gained sufficient strength. Requires access via ladders or hoists.
            *   **Climbing Form:** Formwork that is lifted by external jacking systems as the concrete rises.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Verticality:** Maintaining plumbness is critical.
            *   **Tie Locations:** Need to be carefully placed to avoid interference with the structure or service penetrations.
            *   **Openings:** Formwork for windows, doors, or service penetrations needs to be accurately placed and braced.
*   **Important Points for Wall Formwork:**
    *   **Verticality:** Use levels and plumb bobs to ensure the formwork is vertical.
    *   **Alignment:** Faces must be in the correct plane for the finished wall.
    *   **Joints:** The construction joints between lifts must be planned and formed correctly.
    *   **Surface Finish:** The choice of form face material significantly impacts the surface finish of the concrete.

### **4. Formwork for Columns (LO4)**

Columns are vertical compression members. Formwork for columns must be strong, rigid, and square or rectangular to ensure accurate dimensions and load-bearing capacity.

*   **Types of Columns and their Formwork:**
    *   **Square/Rectangular Columns:**
        *   **Description:** Most common type.
        *   **Formwork:** Typically comprises four vertical panels (form faces) held together by yokes or ties.
        *   **Key Components:**
            *   **Form Faces:** Plywood (often special column formwork panels), steel panels, or timber.
            *   **Yokes/Ties:** Horizontal bands or straps that encircle the column formwork to resist the outward pressure of concrete. These can be steel yokes, timber battens, or steel band clamps.
            *   **Bracing:** Often not required as the yokes provide inherent stability. However, temporary bracing might be needed during erection or if the column is very tall.
            *   **Corner Bracing:** Special corner construction is needed to ensure sharp and true corners.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Pressure:** The lateral pressure is significant, especially at the base of the column. Yokes must be adequately sized and spaced.
            *   **Alignment:** Ensuring the formwork is plumb and the corners are square is critical.
            *   **Stripping:** Columns are often stripped relatively quickly as the yokes carry the load.
            *   **Reinforcement:** Starter bars from the foundation and column reinforcement must be correctly placed within the formwork.
            *   **Pouring:** Concrete is typically poured from the top.
    *   **Circular Columns:**
        *   **Description:** Used for aesthetic or structural reasons.
        *   **Formwork:** Can be made from steel (typically pre-fabricated drums) or timber (laminated veneer lumber - LVL).
        *   **Key Components:**
            *   **Form Shell:** Cylindrical steel or timber panels.
            *   **Clamps/Bands:** To hold the shell together and resist pressure.
            *   **Bracing:** May be needed for taller columns.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Achieving True Circle:** Formwork must be circular and smooth.
            *   **Tie Rods:** If steel formwork, tie rods might be used through the shell to maintain the diameter.
            *   **Stripping:** Steel cylindrical forms are generally easy to strip by releasing clamps.
*   **Important Points for Column Formwork:**
    *   **Plumbness:** Critical for the structural integrity and appearance of the column.
    *   **Corner Squareness:** For rectangular columns, precise corners are essential.
    *   **Yoke Tightness:** Yokes must be securely fastened to prevent leakage.
    *   **Access:** Ensure adequate access for pouring and vibrating the concrete.

### **5. Formwork for Beams (LO5)**

Beams are horizontal structural elements that span between supports and carry loads. Beam formwork includes the soffit (bottom) and the sides.

*   **Types of Beams and their Formwork:**
    *   **Simply Supported Beams:**
        *   **Description:** Beams resting on two supports.
        *   **Formwork:** Consists of a bottom shutter (soffit board) supported by props, and two side shutters (form faces) held in place by battens or walings and kickers.
        *   **Key Components:**
            *   **Soffit Board:** Plywood or timber boards forming the bottom of the beam.
            *   **Props:** Vertical supports to carry the weight of the soffit board and wet concrete.
            *   **Side Shutters:** Timber or plywood forming the sides of the beam.
            *   **Battens/Walings:** Horizontal members securing the side shutters.
            *   **Kickers/Cleats:** Small timber blocks at the base of the side shutters to prevent them from spreading outwards.
            *   **Corner Fillets:** Often used at corners for a chamfered or rounded finish.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Deflection of Soffit:** The soffit board must be strong enough to span between props without excessive sagging. This determines the prop spacing.
            *   **Lateral Pressure on Sides:** The height of the beam influences the lateral pressure on the side forms.
            *   **Support Bearing:** Props must be placed on stable bases.
            *   **Stripping:** Soffit formwork can be stripped after initial setting, but props may need to remain for a longer period to support the beam's self-weight and superimposed loads until sufficient strength is achieved. This is known as "propping."
    *   **Continuous Beams:**
        *   **Description:** Beams spanning over multiple supports.
        *   **Formwork:** Similar to simply supported beams but the soffit formwork may be continuous over intermediate supports.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Support to Soffit:** Adequate support is needed at intermediate piers.
            *   **Reinforcement:** The top reinforcement crossing supports needs to be accommodated by the formwork height.
    *   **Cantilever Beams:**
        *   **Description:** Beams supported at one end and projecting horizontally.
        *   **Formwork:** Similar to simply supported beams but requires strong support at the fixed end and very robust propping.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Uplift Pressure:** At the support, there can be an upward force on the formwork due to the cantilever action.
            *   **Rigidity:** The entire formwork system must be extremely rigid.
*   **Important Points for Beam Formwork:**
    *   **Soffit Support:** Crucial to prevent sagging and ensure correct beam depth.
    *   **Kickers:** Essential for holding the sides of the beam formwork in place.
    *   **Propping:** Understand the difference between formwork stripping and prop removal.
    *   **Alignment:** Ensure beams are level and in the correct position.

### **6. Formwork for Slabs (LO6)**

Slabs are horizontal structural elements supporting floors or roofs. Slab formwork forms the underside and edges of the slab.

*   **Types of Slabs and their Formwork:**
    *   **Solid Slabs (One-Way and Two-Way):**
        *   **Description:** Flat, horizontal slabs.
        *   **Formwork:** Typically consists of a soffit supported by a system of props and horizontal beams (joists and primary beams/needles).
        *   **Key Components:**
            *   **Soffit Sheeting:** Plywood or timber boards forming the bottom surface.
            *   **Joists:** Horizontal timber or steel members supporting the soffit sheeting.
            *   **Needles/Primary Beams:** Larger horizontal members supporting the joists.
            *   **Props:** Vertical supports for the needles.
            *   **Bracing:** Horizontal bracing for the props to prevent buckling.
            *   **Edge Formwork:** To contain the concrete at the edges of the slab.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Prop Spacing:** Determined by the load-carrying capacity of the soffit sheeting, joists, and needles.
            *   **Joist Spacing:** Determined by the load-carrying capacity of the soffit sheeting.
            *   **Needle Spacing:** Determined by the load-carrying capacity of the joists.
            *   **Deflection:** The entire supporting system must be designed to limit deflection.
            *   **Access:** Provision for workers to place concrete and reinforcement.
            *   **Stripping:** Similar to beams, soffit sheeting can be stripped relatively early, but props must remain until sufficient strength is achieved.
    *   **Ribbed Slabs (Waffle Slabs, T-Beams):**
        *   **Description:** Slabs with integrated beams, often formed using permanent or temporary void formers (e.g., plastic or fiberglass domes/liners).
        *   **Formwork:** The formwork supports the void formers and the concrete in the flange (top) of the T-beam or waffle unit.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Void Former Placement:** Accurate positioning and support of void formers is critical.
            *   **Formwork Strength:** Must support concrete in the ribs and flange.
            *   **Stripping:** Stripping can be more complex due to the void formers.
    *   **Flat Slabs:**
        *   **Description:** Slabs supported directly by columns, often with drop panels or mushroom heads.
        *   **Formwork:** Similar to solid slabs but requires formwork for drop panels or column heads.
        *   **Key Considerations:**
            *   **Drop Panel Formwork:** Requires robust support and accurate positioning.
            *   **Column Head Formwork:** Specialized forms are often used.
*   **Important Points for Slab Formwork:**
    *   **Uniform Support:** Ensure the entire soffit area is consistently supported.
    *   **Levelness:** The soffit must be level to achieve the correct slab thickness.
    *   **Edge Containment:** Formwork at the edges must be secure to prevent leakage.
    *   **Access and Safety:** Ensure safe working platforms and access for reinforcement and concrete.

### **7. Applying Basic Formwork Design Principles to Typical Project Scenarios (LO7)**

This involves combining the principles discussed with practical considerations.

*   **Scenario 1: Residential House Foundation (Strip Footing)**
    *   **Element:** Strip footing.
    *   **Formwork Type:** Timber boards and stakes.
    *   **Key Design Checks:**
        *   **Strength:** Are the boards thick enough to resist concrete pressure without bowing? Are stakes strong enough and adequately spaced?
        *   **Rigidity:** Is the formwork braced against the trench walls to prevent movement?
        *   **Serviceability:** Is the top of the formwork level to ensure correct footing depth?
        *   **Safety:** Are workers safe when working in the trench?
*   **Scenario 2: Small Commercial Building Column**
    *   **Element:** Concrete column (e.g., 300mm x 300mm).
    *   **Formwork Type:** Plywood panels with steel yokes.
    *   **Key Design Checks:**
        *   **Strength:** Calculate lateral pressure at the base. Determine the required strength and spacing of steel yokes to resist this pressure. Ensure plywood panel strength is adequate.
        *   **Rigidity:** Ensure yokes are tightened sufficiently.
        *   **Serviceability:** Check for plumbness and squareness of corners.
        *   **Economy:** Consider standard plywood sizes to minimize cutting.
        *   **Safety:** Access for pouring and vibration.
*   **Scenario 3: Suspended Floor Slab (Residential Apartment)**
    *   **Element:** Suspended concrete slab (e.g., 150mm thick).
    *   **Formwork Type:** Timber joists, needles, and props with plywood soffit.
    *   **Key Design Checks:**
        *   **Strength & Rigidity:**
            *   Calculate the total load (concrete weight + finishing load + live load).
            *   Determine prop spacing based on the load capacity of the needles.
            *   Determine needle spacing based on the load capacity of the joists.
            *   Determine joist spacing based on the load capacity of the soffit sheeting.
            *   Check the load capacity of the props themselves.
        *   **Serviceability:** Ensure the soffit is level to achieve the correct slab thickness.
        *   **Economy:** Optimize the spacing of props, needles, and joists for efficient material usage and rapid erection.
        *   **Safety:** Safe access for workers, fall protection.

### **Key Concepts and Definitions:**

*   **Formwork:** Temporary mould for concrete.
*   **Falsework:** The temporary structure supporting formwork.
*   **Soffit:** The underside of a beam or slab.
*   **Form Face:** The surface of the formwork that comes into contact with the concrete.
*   **Waler/Waling:** Horizontal member used to tie form faces together and support studs or soldiers.
*   **Yoke/Tie:** A member that encircles and holds formwork sides together, especially for columns.
*   **Tie Rod:** A threaded rod passing through a wall formwork to maintain spacing and resist lateral pressure.
*   **Prop:** Vertical support for soffit formwork.
*   **Kicker/Kicker Board:** A small timber member at the base of a wall or beam form to prevent spreading.
*   **Stripping:** The process of removing formwork.
*   **Propping:** The temporary support left in place after formwork is stripped.
*   **Lateral Pressure:** Outward force exerted by wet concrete.
*   **Hydrostatic Pressure:** Pressure exerted by a fluid at rest (applies to wet concrete).
*   **Deflection:** Sagging of a structural element or formwork under load.
*   **Plumb:** Perfectly vertical.
*   **Chamfer:** Beveled edge.

---

### **Important Points to Remember:**

*   **Load Calculation is Paramount:** Always accurately calculate all loads acting on the formwork.
*   **Rigidity Prevents Deflection:** Use adequate bracing and stiffeners.
*   **Stability is Non-Negotiable:** Ensure formwork cannot overturn, slide, or buckle.
*   **Formwork Design is an Engineering Task:** It requires understanding of structural mechanics and material properties.
*   **Safety First, Always:** Never compromise on safety for speed or cost.
*   **Stripping and Propping Times:** Understand the concrete strength requirements before removing formwork and props.
*   **Surface Finish:** The choice of form face material directly impacts the concrete surface quality.
*   **Connections:** Ensure all formwork connections are strong and secure.

### **Practice Questions/Exercises:**

**Question 1:**
List and briefly explain the five fundamental principles of formwork design.

**Question 2:**
What are the main loads that formwork for a tall concrete wall needs to withstand?

**Question 3:**
Why is the plumbness of column formwork so critical?

**Question 4:**
For a suspended slab supported by timber joists and props, what is the primary consideration when determining the spacing of the props?

**Question 5:**
Describe the role of 'kickers' in beam formwork.

---

### **Answers:**

**Answer 1:**
The five fundamental principles are:
1.  **Strength:** To withstand all loads without failure.
2.  **Rigidity:** To prevent deformation or deflection.
3.  **Stability:** To prevent overturning, buckling, or sliding.
4.  **Serviceability/Workability:** Ease of erection, adjustment, dismantling, and achieving dimensional accuracy.
5.  **Economy:** Cost-effectiveness through efficient design and material use.
*(Safety is also a critical overarching principle.)*

**Answer 2:**
The main loads are:
*   **Lateral pressure** from the wet concrete (hydrostatic pressure).
*   **Vertical load** from the weight of the concrete itself and the formwork.
*   **Live loads** from workers and equipment.
*   **Impact loads** during concrete placement.
*   **Wind loads** if exposed.

**Answer 3:**
The plumbness of column formwork is critical because columns are primary vertical load-bearing members. If the formwork is not plumb, the resulting column will be out of vertical alignment. This can lead to:
*   Eccentric loading on the column, causing uneven stress distribution.
*   Reduced load-carrying capacity.
*   Aesthetically unacceptable results.
*   Difficulty in constructing subsequent floors or finishes.

**Answer 4:**
The primary consideration when determining the spacing of props for a suspended slab is the **load-carrying capacity of the needles and the soffit sheeting**. The props must be able to support the combined weight of the needles, joists, soffit sheeting, and wet concrete without failure or excessive deflection.

**Answer 5:**
'Kickers' (or kickers) are small timber members fixed to the base of the side shutters of beam formwork. Their primary roles are:
*   **To provide a stable base:** They prevent the side forms from spreading outwards at the bottom under the lateral pressure of the wet concrete.
*   **To help maintain alignment:** They assist in keeping the side forms perpendicular to the soffit.
*   **To provide a clean edge:** They create a straight, defined edge at the bottom of the beam's side.
