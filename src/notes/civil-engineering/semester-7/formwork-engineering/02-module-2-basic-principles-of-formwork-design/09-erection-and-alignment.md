# FORMWORK ENGINEERING - Module 2: Basic Principles of Formwork Design
## Topic: Erection and Alignment

This module delves into the practical aspects of putting formwork into place, ensuring it's accurately positioned and remains stable throughout the concrete pouring process. Proper erection and alignment are critical for achieving the desired concrete dimensions, structural integrity, and overall project success.

---

### **1. Learning Outcomes Covered**

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   **Understand the sequence of erection for various formwork systems.**
*   **Identify and apply methods for accurate horizontal and vertical alignment of formwork.**
*   **Recognize the importance of temporary bracing and support during erection.**
*   **Describe procedures for checking and adjusting formwork during erection.**
*   **Understand the role of formwork drawings and instructions in the erection process.**
*   **Identify common challenges and safety considerations during erection and alignment.**

---

### **2. Key Concepts and Definitions**

*   **Erection:** The process of assembling and installing formwork components on site according to design specifications.
*   **Alignment:** The precise positioning of formwork in relation to a fixed datum or other structural elements, ensuring correct dimensions and geometry of the concrete element.
*   **Datum:** A reference point or line used as a basis for measurement and alignment. This could be a bench mark, survey point, or established structural member.
*   **Plumb:** Perfectly vertical.
*   **Level:** Perfectly horizontal.
*   **Setting Out:** The process of transferring design dimensions and positions from plans onto the construction site.
*   **Kickouts/Braces:** Temporary structural members used to hold formwork in its correct position and resist lateral forces during erection and concrete pouring.
*   **Wedges/Adjustable Jacks:** Devices used to fine-tune the height and level of formwork components.
*   **Formwork Drawings:** Detailed plans showing the layout, dimensions, connections, and erection sequence of formwork.
*   **Shop Drawings:** Detailed drawings prepared by the formwork supplier or manufacturer, often showing how standard components fit together.
*   **Tolerance:** The permissible deviation from the specified dimensions or positions in construction.

### **3. Erection Sequence for Common Formwork Systems**

The erection sequence is crucial for maintaining stability and ensuring the correct placement of concrete. While specific sequences vary, general principles apply:

#### **3.1. Wall Formwork (e.g., Panel Systems)**

1.  **Setting Out:** Mark the lines of the walls on the foundation or existing structure based on survey data.
2.  **Base Installation:** Install the base plates, sill plates, or sole plates that the formwork will rest upon. These must be accurately leveled.
3.  **First Course of Panels:** Erect the first row of wall panels along the setting-out lines. Ensure they are plumb and braced.
4.  **Joining Panels:** Connect adjacent panels securely using clamps, ties, or bolts as per the system's design.
5.  **Installing Walers/Chains:** Attach horizontal bracing (walers) or ties to the panels to maintain rigidity and resist concrete pressure.
6.  **Vertical Alignment:** Use plumb bobs, spirit levels, or laser levels to ensure panels are perfectly vertical.
7.  **Bracing:** Install temporary braces to support the formwork and prevent it from tipping or leaning.
8.  **Subsequent Courses:** Erect subsequent courses of panels, ensuring overlap and secure connections to the lower courses.
9.  **Tie Rods:** Install tie rods that pass through the formwork to hold the two sides together at the correct distance, resisting the outward pressure of the concrete.
10. **Inserts and Openings:** Install any required conduits, rebar chairs, or block-outs for openings as erection progresses.

#### **3.2. Slab Formwork (e.g., Timber or System Formwork)**

1.  **Setting Out:** Mark the position of supporting elements (columns, walls) and the slab edges.
2.  **Support Erection:** Erect the primary supporting structure:
    *   **Props (Shoring):** Install adjustable props at the required spacing and height.
    *   **Beams/Joists:** Place primary beams and then secondary joists or bearers on top of the props.
3.  **Leveling:** Accurately level all supporting beams and joists using wedges or adjustable jacks to achieve the correct slab soffit level. This is critical.
4.  **Decking/Plywood Installation:** Lay the decking material (plywood, proprietary panels) onto the joists, ensuring a tight fit and minimal gaps.
5.  **Edge Formwork:** Install edge forms or stop ends to contain the concrete at the perimeter of the slab.
6.  **Checking Level and Stability:** Thoroughly check the entire deck for levelness and ensure all supports are stable.

#### **3.3. Column Formwork (e.g., Steel or Timber)**

1.  **Setting Out:** Accurately mark the position and dimensions of the column on the foundation or soffit.
2.  **Base Plates/Sills:** Install base plates to provide a stable and level foundation for the column formwork.
3.  **Erecting First Side:** Position and brace one side of the column formwork accurately according to the setting out.
4.  **Subsequent Sides:** Erect the remaining sides, ensuring they are plumb and square to each other.
5.  **Joining and Clamping:** Securely join the corners of the formwork using ties, clamps, or bolts.
6.  **Tie Rods/Spreader Braces:** Install internal tie rods or spreader braces to maintain the correct column dimensions and resist concrete pressure.
7.  **Alignment Checks:** Continuously check for plumbness and squareness using levels and measuring diagonals.
8.  **Bracing:** Provide external bracing to support the formwork until the concrete has gained sufficient strength.

### **4. Methods for Accurate Horizontal and Vertical Alignment**

Accurate alignment is paramount. Deviations can lead to structural issues, aesthetic problems, and difficulties in subsequent construction phases.

#### **4.1. Vertical Alignment (Plumbness)**

*   **Spirit Levels:** Used for smaller elements or to check individual components. Larger spirit levels are available for greater accuracy.
*   **Plumb Bobs:** Traditional but effective for checking verticality. A plumb bob hung from a known true vertical line will align with the formwork.
*   **Laser Levels:** Provide a highly accurate, visible horizontal or vertical plane. Can be used to project a plumb line onto the formwork.
*   **Total Stations/Theodolites:** Used in large-scale projects for precise setting out and alignment of major formwork elements.
*   **Inclinometers/Clinometers:** Electronic devices that measure the angle of inclination, directly indicating deviation from plumb.

**Example:** For a concrete wall, you would use a spirit level or laser level against the face of the formwork panels to ensure they are perfectly vertical. Diagonal measurements of the column formwork should be equal to ensure it's square.

#### **4.2. Horizontal Alignment (Levelness)**

*   **Spirit Levels:** Used on formwork decks, beams, and ledges.
*   **Water Levels:** A simple yet effective method for establishing a level across a large area. Consists of connected transparent tubes filled with water. The water surface in all tubes will be at the same level.
*   **Dumpy Levels/Automatic Levels:** Surveying instruments used to establish precise levels over long distances.
*   **Laser Levels:** Can project a horizontal plane, allowing for easy checking of levels across the formwork.
*   **Adjustable Jacks/Wedges:** These are the *means* by which levelness is achieved. They are adjusted based on readings from leveling instruments.

**Example:** When erecting slab formwork, adjustable props are raised or lowered until the beams or joists resting on them are perfectly level, as confirmed by a spirit level or laser level.

### **5. Temporary Bracing and Support During Erection**

Temporary bracing is vital for the stability of formwork *during* the erection process and before it's fully tied together and self-supporting, or before the concrete is poured.

*   **Purpose of Bracing:**
    *   **Prevent Tipping:** Holds the formwork upright against gravity and potential wind loads.
    *   **Resist Lateral Forces:** Protects against accidental knocks, movement of workers, or pressure from partially placed concrete.
    *   **Maintain Alignment:** Keeps formwork in its correct position and prevents distortion.
    *   **Provide Stability for Workers:** Creates a safe working platform.

*   **Types of Bracing:**
    *   **Sway Bracing:** Diagonal members that resist lateral forces and prevent the formwork from racking or leaning.
    *   **Knee Braces:** Angled braces connecting vertical members to horizontal members to provide stiffness.
    *   **Push Braces:** Used to push formwork away from a fixed point to maintain alignment, often at the top of tall formwork.
    *   **Tie Rods:** While primarily for resisting concrete pressure, they also provide significant stability by holding opposing formwork faces together.
    *   **Kickouts:** Braces that extend outwards from the base of the formwork to stabilize it, particularly for wall formwork.

**Important Consideration:** Bracing must be securely anchored to the ground or a stable structure and should be removed carefully in the correct sequence after the concrete has achieved sufficient strength.

### **6. Checking and Adjusting Formwork During Erection**

Continuous checking and adjustment are essential to catch errors early and ensure accuracy.

*   **Pre-Erection Checks:**
    *   Inspect formwork components for damage, wear, and correct dimensions.
    *   Verify that all necessary accessories (ties, clamps, wedges) are available.

*   **During Erection Checks:**
    *   **Regular Plumb and Level Checks:** As described in Section 4.
    *   **Diagonal Measurements:** For rectangular or square elements (columns, beams), measure the diagonals of the opening. They should be equal to ensure the formwork is square.
    *   **Tied Distance Checks:** Ensure tie rods are set at the correct spacing to match the designed concrete section.
    *   **Propping Loads:** Ensure props are not overloaded. Distribute loads evenly.
    *   **Joint Integrity:** Check that formwork joints are tight to prevent leakage of cement paste.

*   **Adjustment Procedures:**
    *   **Loosening and Re-tightening:** Gently loosen connections, adjust alignment using levels and measuring tapes, and re-tighten securely.
    *   **Wedge/Jack Adjustment:** Fine-tune heights and levels using adjustable jacks or wedges.
    *   **Shimming:** Use thin pieces of material (plywood, plastic) to make small adjustments in level or to fill minor gaps.

**Example:** If a concrete wall formwork is found to be slightly out of plumb, the braces would be loosened, the formwork nudged into the correct vertical position, and then the braces re-tightened and checked again.

### **7. Role of Formwork Drawings and Instructions**

Formwork drawings and instructions are the blueprints for erection.

*   **Formwork Drawings:**
    *   **Layout:** Show the overall arrangement of formwork components.
    *   **Dimensions:** Specify the exact sizes and shapes of the concrete elements, and therefore the formwork.
    *   **Setting Out Points:** Indicate reference points for positioning formwork.
    *   **Support Grids:** Detail the spacing and arrangement of props, beams, and joists for slabs.
    *   **Tie Rod Locations:** Show where tie rods should be placed.
    *   **Bracing Requirements:** Indicate necessary bracing locations and types.
    *   **Construction Details:** Show how components are connected.

*   **Erection Instructions/Method Statements:**
    *   **Step-by-Step Sequence:** Provide a clear, logical order for assembling the formwork.
    *   **Safety Precautions:** Outline specific safety measures to be followed.
    *   **Materials List:** Specify the formwork components and accessories required.
    *   **Equipment Needed:** List tools and lifting equipment.
    *   **Quality Control Checks:** Detail the checks to be performed at various stages.

**Importance:** Adhering strictly to these documents is crucial. Any deviations must be approved by the engineer.

### **8. Common Challenges and Safety Considerations**

#### **8.1. Common Challenges**

*   **Inaccurate Setting Out:** Errors in transferring design dimensions to the site.
*   **Uneven Ground:** Making it difficult to establish a stable and level base for formwork.
*   **Wind Loads:** Can destabilize erected formwork, especially tall or large panel systems.
*   **Overloading of Formwork:** Placing too much concrete or construction material on inadequately supported formwork.
*   **Leakage:** Gaps in formwork allowing cement paste to escape, leading to honeycombing and reduced strength.
*   **Difficulty in Stripping:** Formwork that is too tightly fitted or improperly released can be difficult and damaging to remove.
*   **Damage to Formwork Components:** Leading to inaccurate dimensions or structural weakness.
*   **Thermal Expansion/Contraction:** Can cause slight changes in formwork dimensions, particularly with metal systems in varying temperatures.

#### **8.2. Safety Considerations**

*   **Working at Height:** Formwork is often erected at significant heights, requiring fall protection (harnesses, guardrails).
*   **Falling Objects:** Tools, materials, or formwork components can fall, posing a severe hazard. Proper site management, tool lanyards, and exclusion zones are necessary.
*   **Instability of Formwork:** Unbraced or improperly erected formwork can collapse, causing severe injury or death.
*   **Manual Handling:** Lifting and positioning heavy formwork panels can lead to musculoskeletal injuries. Use of lifting equipment is often required.
*   **Electrical Hazards:** Ensure all electrical tools and lighting are properly grounded and protected.
*   **Entrapment:** Workers can be trapped between moving parts or collapsing formwork.
*   **Sharp Edges:** Formwork materials can have sharp edges.

**Key Safety Practices:**
*   **Competent Supervision:** Ensure experienced personnel oversee erection.
*   **Regular Inspections:** Constantly check for signs of distress or instability.
*   **Clear Communication:** Ensure all workers understand their tasks and potential hazards.
*   **Use of PPE:** Hard hats, safety glasses, steel-toed boots, and appropriate gloves are mandatory.
*   **Load Management:** Never exceed the designed load capacity of formwork.
*   **Safe Access and Egress:** Ensure clear and safe pathways to and from the working area.
*   **Proper Bracing Removal:** Remove temporary bracing only after the concrete has gained sufficient strength, following a prescribed sequence.

### **9. Practice Questions and Exercises**

**Question 1:**
What is the primary purpose of temporary bracing during the erection of wall formwork?
a) To provide a smooth surface for the concrete.
b) To prevent the formwork from tipping or leaning and to resist lateral forces.
c) To ensure the formwork is level.
d) To support the weight of the wet concrete.

**Question 2:**
Which tool would be most appropriate for ensuring a long concrete wall form is perfectly vertical?
a) A small spirit level.
b) A plumb bob.
c) A laser level.
d) A measuring tape.

**Question 3:**
When erecting slab formwork, what is the critical step to ensure the final concrete slab has the correct thickness and is not sloped unintentionally?
a) Tightly fitting the plywood deck.
b) Ensuring the primary support props are accurately leveled.
c) Securing the edge forms properly.
d) Using plenty of nails to fix the joists.

**Question 4:**
If the diagonal measurements of a column formwork opening are found to be unequal during erection, what is the likely issue, and how would you correct it?

**Question 5:**
List three common safety hazards associated with formwork erection and alignment.

---

### **10. Answers to Practice Questions**

**Answer 1:**
b) To prevent the formwork from tipping or leaning and to resist lateral forces.
*   *Explanation:* While levelness is important (c), bracing's primary role during erection is stability against tipping and lateral movement. Supporting wet concrete (d) is the role of the formwork structure itself, not temporary bracing during erection.

**Answer 2:**
c) A laser level.
*   *Explanation:* For a long wall, a laser level provides a consistent, accurate vertical reference along the entire length, which is more practical and precise than repeatedly using a plumb bob or small spirit level.

**Answer 3:**
b) Ensuring the primary support props are accurately leveled.
*   *Explanation:* The levelness of the props directly dictates the levelness of the beams, joists, and ultimately the formwork deck, which defines the underside of the concrete slab.

**Answer 4:**
*   **Issue:** The column formwork is not square. The unequal diagonals indicate that the angles are not 90 degrees.
*   **Correction:** Loosen the ties or clamps holding the formwork sides together. Gently adjust the position of the sides until the diagonal measurements are equal. Re-tighten the ties/clamps and re-check the diagonals. Ensure the formwork is also plumb in both directions.

**Answer 5:**
Three common safety hazards:
1.  **Instability/Collapse of Formwork:** Due to inadequate bracing, overloading, or improper erection.
2.  **Falling Objects:** Tools, materials, or formwork components falling from height.
3.  **Working at Height:** Risk of falls from elevated formwork structures.
4.  **Manual Handling Injuries:** From lifting and moving heavy formwork components.
5.  **Entrapment:** Being caught between moving parts or collapsing elements.

### **11. Important Points to Remember**

*   **Accuracy is Paramount:** Formwork erection and alignment are not just about holding concrete; they define the finished shape and dimensions of the concrete structure.
*   **Sequence Matters:** Always follow a logical erection sequence to maintain stability and prevent damage.
*   **Brace Adequately:** Temporary bracing is critical for safety and stability during erection, especially in windy conditions or on uneven ground.
*   **Continuous Checks:** Regularly check for plumbness, levelness, and squareness throughout the erection process.
*   **Understand the Drawings:** Formwork drawings are your guide; adhere to them strictly.
*   **Safety First:** Always prioritize safety. Work at height, falling objects, and formwork instability are major risks.
*   **Competent Workforce:** Ensure that the team erecting the formwork is experienced and well-trained.
*   **Adjust and Correct:** Don't hesitate to adjust or correct any deviations found during erection. It's much easier and safer to fix errors early.
*   **Tie Rods are Critical:** Properly installed and spaced tie rods are essential for maintaining the correct dimensions and resisting concrete pressure.
*   **Cleanliness:** Keep the formwork and the erection area clean to prevent tripping hazards and ensure accurate measurements.
