# ADVANCED GEOTECHNICAL INVESTIGATION

## Module 1: Planning an Investigation Programme

### Topic: Planning an Investigation Programme

---

## 1. Introduction to Geotechnical Investigation Planning

### 1.1. Purpose and Importance of Geotechnical Investigation Planning

*   **Purpose:** To define the necessary information for the design and construction of a project, ensuring safety, economic viability, and environmental sustainability.
*   **Importance:**
    *   **Risk Mitigation:** Identifies potential geotechnical hazards (e.g., landslides, settlement, bearing capacity failure) early on, allowing for design modifications.
    *   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Prevents costly over-design or unforeseen construction problems. A well-planned investigation minimizes unnecessary testing.
    *   **Informed Design:** Provides reliable data for foundation design, slope stability analysis, earthwork design, and other geotechnical aspects.
    *   **Regulatory Compliance:** Meets legal and ethical requirements for site investigation.
    *   **Environmental Protection:** Assesses potential environmental impacts of soil and groundwater conditions.

### 1.2. Key Stages in Planning an Investigation Programme

The planning process is iterative and involves several interconnected stages:

*   **Stage 1: Preliminary Investigation & Information Gathering**
*   **Stage 2: Defining Project Requirements & Geotechnical Objectives**
*   **Stage 3: Developing the Investigation Strategy**
*   **Stage 4: Detailed Planning & Design of Investigation Tasks**
*   **Stage 5: Implementation, Monitoring & Reporting**

## 2. Stage 1: Preliminary Investigation & Information Gathering

### 2.1. Review of Existing Information

*   **Objective:** To gain a preliminary understanding of the site geology, topography, and potential geotechnical issues.
*   **Sources of Information:**
    *   **Geological Maps:** Provide regional geological context, stratigraphy, and known fault lines.
        *   *Example:* Consulting a national geological survey map to identify potential bedrock types and surficial deposits.
    *   **Topographical Maps:** Show landforms, slopes, drainage patterns, and existing structures.
        *   *Example:* Identifying steep slopes or water bodies that might indicate instability.
    *   **Aerial Photographs & Satellite Imagery:** Reveal surface features, vegetation patterns, land use, and signs of past ground movement.
        *   *Example:* Spotting unusual patterns in vegetation or subtle depressions that could indicate subsidence.
    *   **Previous Geotechnical Reports:** Investigations for adjacent or nearby sites can provide valuable insights.
        *   *Example:* A report for a neighboring building might indicate the presence of soft clays or a high water table.
    *   **Utility Maps & Records:** Information on underground services (pipes, cables) to avoid during intrusive investigations.
        *   *Example:* Locating existing sewer lines to prevent damage.
    *   **Hydrological Data:** Rainfall records, groundwater levels, and surface water flow can impact site conditions.
        *   *Example:* Understanding seasonal variations in groundwater table.
    *   **Local Knowledge:** Discussions with residents or local authorities can reveal undocumented issues.

### 2.2. Reconnaissance Visit (Site Walkover)

*   **Objective:** A visual inspection of the site to confirm and supplement existing information and to identify new potential issues.
*   **Key Observations:**
    *   **Topography and Slopes:** Steepness, evidence of erosion, landslides, or settlements.
    *   **Vegetation:** Type and health of vegetation can indicate soil moisture content and stability.
    *   **Surface Features:** Existing cracks, depressions, humps, or exposed bedrock.
    *   **Drainage:** Presence of surface water, evidence of ponding, or poor drainage.
    *   **Existing Structures:** Condition of nearby buildings, roads, or retaining walls.
    *   **Evidence of Contamination:** Stains, unusual odors, or discarded materials.
    *   **Access and Constraints:** Identifying areas that are difficult to access or have environmental sensitivities.

## 3. Stage 2: Defining Project Requirements & Geotechnical Objectives

### 3.1. Understanding the Project Scope and Design Requirements

*   **Key Questions:**
    *   What is the nature of the proposed structure(s)? (e.g., building, bridge, dam, road, tunnel)
    *   What are the design loads? (e.g., vertical, lateral, seismic)
    *   What are the expected performance criteria? (e.g., allowable settlement, bearing capacity, slope stability factors of safety)
    *   What is the construction methodology? (e.g., excavation, piling, dewatering)
    *   What are the project timeline and budget?

### 3.2. Identifying Geotechnical Issues and Risks

*   **Based on preliminary information and project understanding, identify potential problems:**
    *   **Bearing Capacity:** Insufficient strength of soil/rock to support foundation loads.
    *   **Settlement:** Excessive vertical movement of foundations under load.
        *   *Types:* Immediate, consolidation, differential.
    *   **Slope Stability:** Risk of landslides or collapses in natural or man-made slopes.
    *   **Liquefaction:** Loss of soil strength in saturated granular soils under seismic loading.
    *   **Expansive or Collapsible Soils:** Soils that change volume significantly with moisture content.
    *   **Groundwater Issues:** High water table, perched water, seepage, or artesian conditions.
    *   **Contaminated Land:** Presence of pollutants affecting soil properties or posing environmental risks.
    *   **Seismic Hazards:** Site-specific seismic amplification, fault crossings.
    *   **Frost Susceptibility:** Soils that are prone to heave due to freezing.
    *   **Subsidence:** Settlement due to natural or man-induced causes (e.g., mining, groundwater extraction).

### 3.3. Formulating Geotechnical Objectives

*   **Translate project requirements and identified issues into specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) investigation objectives.**
*   **Examples:**
    *   Determine the bearing capacity of the soil at foundation depth for a proposed multi-storey building.
    *   Assess the settlement potential of soft clay layers beneath a proposed highway embankment.
    *   Evaluate the stability of a proposed cut slope for a residential development.
    *   Determine the depth to bedrock and its quality for the design of bridge abutments.
    *   Characterize the groundwater regime and its potential impact on basement construction.
    *   Identify potential liquefaction hazards for seismic design of a port facility.

## 4. Stage 3: Developing the Investigation Strategy

### 4.1. Phased Approach to Investigation

*   **Rationale:** A multi-phase approach allows for flexibility and optimization of testing based on initial findings. It also helps manage costs.
*   **Typical Phases:**
    *   **Phase 1: Desk Study & Reconnaissance:** (Covered in Stage 1)
    *   **Phase 2: Initial Ground Investigation:** Characterize general ground conditions, identify key soil/rock types, and assess major geotechnical risks. This phase often involves a limited number of boreholes and in-situ tests.
    *   **Phase 3: Detailed Ground Investigation:** Target specific areas of concern identified in Phase 2. This may involve more boreholes, specialized in-situ tests, sampling for laboratory testing, and potentially geophysical surveys or trial pits.
    *   **Phase 4: Specific Design Investigations:** Focused investigations directly addressing critical design parameters (e.g., extensive testing for a deep foundation design, detailed stability analysis for a critical slope).
    *   **Phase 5: Construction Monitoring & Verification:** Ongoing testing during construction to ensure compliance with design assumptions and to address unexpected conditions.

### 4.2. Selecting Appropriate Investigation Methods

The choice of methods depends on the geotechnical objectives, site conditions, project type, and budget.

*   **Non-Intrusive Methods (Geophysical Surveys):**
    *   **Purpose:** To provide a broad overview of subsurface conditions, identify anomalies, and guide the placement of intrusive investigations.
    *   **Methods:**
        *   **Seismic Refraction/Reflection:** Determine depth to bedrock, identify soil layers, and detect anomalies.
        *   **Electrical Resistivity:** Differentiate soil types, identify groundwater levels, and locate buried utilities.
        *   **Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR):** Detect shallow subsurface features, utilities, and voids.
        *   **Magnetometry:** Locate buried ferrous objects (e.g., pipes, old foundations).
    *   *Example:* Using seismic refraction to estimate the depth to competent rock before planning borehole locations for a bridge foundation.

*   **Intrusive Methods:**
    *   **Trial Pits (Excavations):**
        *   **Purpose:** To visually inspect shallow soil profiles, obtain disturbed and undisturbed samples, and conduct simple in-situ tests (e.g., Vane Shear Test).
        *   **Advantages:** Relatively inexpensive, good for shallow depths, allows direct visual observation.
        *   **Disadvantages:** Limited to shallow depths (typically < 5m), can be affected by groundwater.
        *   *Example:* Excavating trial pits to assess the suitability of soil for shallow foundations or to investigate the extent of fill material.
    *   **Boreholes:**
        *   **Purpose:** To penetrate to greater depths, obtain detailed logs of soil/rock strata, collect undisturbed samples, and perform in-situ tests.
        *   **Types of Drilling:** Rotary drilling, cable percussion drilling, auger drilling.
        *   **Standard Penetration Test (SPT):**
            *   **Procedure:** Driving a Standard Penetration Test split-spoon sampler into the soil using a hammer of standard weight and fall.
            *   **Measurement:** Number of blows required to drive the sampler 300mm (after an initial seating drive). This is the N-value.
            *   **Purpose:** Provides an indication of soil density (sands) or consistency (clays), and allows for collection of disturbed samples.
            *   *Example:* Using SPT N-values to estimate bearing capacity for a raft foundation.
        *   **Dynamic Probing (DPH, DPSH):** Similar to SPT but uses different equipment; useful for a quick assessment of soil resistance.
        *   **Cone Penetration Test (CPT) & Piezocone Penetration Test (CPTU):**
            *   **Procedure:** Pushing a cone-shaped probe into the ground at a constant rate.
            *   **Measurements:** Cone resistance (qc), sleeve friction (fs), pore water pressure (u) (in CPTU).
            *   **Purpose:** Provides continuous data on soil stratigraphy, strength, and stiffness. CPTU also provides information on groundwater conditions. Highly effective for soft to medium dense soils.
            *   *Example:* Using CPT data to estimate the settlement of a proposed road embankment on soft clay.
        *   **Borehole Logging:** Detailed description of soil/rock encountered at different depths, including colour, texture, moisture content, and identification of discontinuities.
        *   **Sampling:**
            *   **Disturbed Samples:** (e.g., SPT split-spoon sampler, bulk samples) Used for classification and some laboratory tests but not for strength or consolidation tests.
            *   **Undisturbed Samples:** (e.g., Shelby tubes, piston samplers, block samples) Essential for laboratory testing of strength, compressibility, and permeability.

*   **In-Situ Testing (Performed within boreholes or on the ground surface):**
    *   **Purpose:** To measure soil properties directly in their natural environment.
    *   **Methods:**
        *   **Field Vane Shear Test (FVST):** Measures the undrained shear strength of soft to firm cohesive soils.
        *   **Plate Load Test (PLT):** Determines the bearing capacity and settlement characteristics of the soil under a loaded plate. Useful for shallow foundation design.
        *   **Pressuremeter Test (PMT):** Measures the in-situ stress-strain characteristics of soil and rock.
        *   **Dilatometer Test (DMT):** Provides readings of horizontal stress index, material index, and dilatometer modulus, from which strength and compressibility parameters can be inferred.
        *   **Packer Test (Permeability Test):** Measures the permeability of rock formations within a borehole.
        *   **Pumping Tests:** Assess aquifer properties (transmissivity, storativity) and potential drawdown.

*   **Laboratory Testing:**
    *   **Purpose:** To obtain detailed information on soil and rock properties from samples collected during field investigations.
    *   **Key Tests:**
        *   **Classification Tests:**
            *   **Moisture Content:** Fundamental property.
            *   **Atterberg Limits (Liquid Limit, Plastic Limit, Plasticity Index):** Classify fine-grained soils.
            *   **Sieve Analysis:** Classify coarse-grained soils.
            *   **Specific Gravity:** Density of soil solids.
        *   **Strength Tests:**
            *   **Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS):** Strength of cohesive soils.
            *   **Triaxial Compression Test (UU, CU, CD):** Measures shear strength parameters (cohesion 'c' and friction angle '$\phi$') under various drainage conditions. Crucial for accurate design.
            *   **Direct Shear Test:** Measures shear strength parameters, usually for granular soils or softer clays.
        *   **Compressibility Tests:**
            *   **Consolidation Test (Oedometer Test):** Determines compressibility, consolidation settlement, and coefficient of consolidation ($c_v$) for cohesive soils.
        *   **Permeability Tests:**
            *   **Constant Head Permeability Test:** For granular soils.
            *   **Falling Head Permeability Test:** For fine-grained soils.
        *   **Other Tests:** Specific gravity, unit weight, swell potential, chemical analysis (e.g., for aggressive soils).

### 4.3. Developing the Investigation Plan Document

*   **Purpose:** A formal document outlining the proposed investigation program.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **Introduction:** Project background, location, and objectives.
    *   **Review of Existing Information:** Summary of findings from desk study.
    *   **Site Description:** Geology, topography, groundwater conditions (as known).
    *   **Geotechnical Objectives:** Specific questions to be answered.
    *   **Proposed Investigation Methods:**
        *   **Grid/Layout:** Location and number of boreholes, trial pits, CPT soundings.
        *   **Depth of Investigation:** Justification for depth based on project requirements.
        *   **Testing Regime:** In-situ and laboratory tests to be performed at each location.
        *   **Sampling Strategy:** Type and frequency of sampling.
        *   **Geophysical Methods (if applicable):** Location and type.
    *   **Schedule:** Timeline for fieldwork and laboratory testing.
    *   **Reporting:** Format and content of the final report.
    *   **Health and Safety:** Site-specific safety plan.
    *   **Budget:** Estimated costs.

## 5. Stage 4: Detailed Planning & Design of Investigation Tasks

### 5.1. Spacing and Depth of Boreholes/Investigative Points

*   **Spacing:** Determined by the variability of ground conditions and the project's sensitivity to variations.
    *   **General Principle:** Closer spacing for more critical structures or highly variable ground.
    *   *Rule of Thumb (often modified):*
        *   Buildings: 15-30m spacing for preliminary; closer for critical areas.
        *   Embankments/Roads: Along centerlines and edges, at intervals of 50-100m.
        *   Slopes: At crest, toe, and potentially mid-slope; closer spacing at critical areas.
*   **Depth:** Determined by the influence zone of the proposed structure's loads.
    *   **Influence Zone:** The depth within which stresses from the structure are significant enough to cause settlement or affect stability.
    *   *General Guidance:*
        *   Shallow Foundations: Investigate to a depth of 1.5 to 2 times the foundation width, or until a firm stratum is encountered.
        *   Deep Foundations (Piles): Investigate to a depth of at least 10-15 pile diameters below the pile toe, or to a satisfactory bearing stratum.
        *   Slope Stability: Investigate to below the potential slip surface, often to a depth equivalent to the slope height.
        *   Embankments: Investigate to a depth where the stresses from the embankment are negligible, typically 1.5 to 2 times the embankment height.

### 5.2. Selecting Specific In-situ and Laboratory Tests

*   **Match tests to objectives:**
    *   *Bearing Capacity & Settlement (Shallow Foundations):* SPT, CPT, Plate Load Test, Triaxial Tests (UU/CU), Consolidation Tests.
    *   *Settlement of Soft Clays:* CPT/CPTU, FVST, Triaxial Tests (CU/CD), Consolidation Tests.
    *   *Slope Stability:* FVST, Triaxial Tests (UU/CU/CD), Direct Shear Tests, Borehole Logging (discontinuities).
    *   *Liquefaction Potential:* SPT, CPT/CPTU, Cyclic Triaxial Tests.
    *   *Dewatering Assessment:* Pumping Tests, Permeability Tests (packer, lab).
*   **Consider soil types:**
    *   Granular Soils: SPT, CPT, Direct Shear, Triaxial (CU/CD), Permeability (Constant Head).
    *   Cohesive Soils: CPTU, FVST, Triaxial (UU/CU/CD), Consolidation, Atterberg Limits, Moisture Content.
    *   Rock: Borehole Logging, UCS, Triaxial Tests, Point Load Test, Slake Durability, Petrographic Analysis.

### 5.3. Developing a Sampling and Testing Schedule

*   **Create a matrix linking borehole/investigation location to the required tests and samples.**
    *   *Example:*
        *   Borehole BH-1: SPT at every 1.5m, Undisturbed samples at 3m, 5m, 7m. Lab tests: Moisture content, Atterberg Limits, Triaxial CU.
        *   CPT CPT-1: Continuous CPT & CPTU to 15m.
*   **Ensure adequate sampling for critical strata.**

## 6. Stage 5: Implementation, Monitoring & Reporting

### 6.1. Executing the Field Investigation

*   **Contracting:** Selecting experienced drilling contractors and laboratory facilities.
*   **Site Control:** Ensuring proper execution of drilling, sampling, and in-situ testing according to standards.
*   **Supervision:** Geotechnical engineer presence on-site to oversee operations, make real-time decisions, and ensure quality.
*   **Health and Safety Management:** Strict adherence to safety protocols.

### 6.2. Laboratory Testing and Data Analysis

*   **Quality Control:** Ensuring lab tests are performed by accredited laboratories according to relevant standards (e.g., BS, ASTM, Eurocode).
*   **Data Interpretation:** Analyzing test results to derive engineering parameters.
*   **Correlation of Results:** Comparing different test results to ensure consistency.

### 6.3. Reporting

*   **Geotechnical Investigation Report:**
    *   **Content:**
        *   Introduction and Project Description
        *   Scope of Investigation
        *   Site Description (Geology, Topography, Hydrogeology)
        *   Fieldwork Records (Borehole logs, CPT logs, test data)
        *   Laboratory Test Results
        *   **Interpretation of Ground Conditions:** Stratigraphy, soil/rock properties.
        *   **Geotechnical Assessment:** Analysis of bearing capacity, settlement, slope stability, liquefaction, etc.
        *   **Recommendations:** Foundation types, allowable bearing pressures, settlement estimates, slope remedial measures, drainage advice, pavement design considerations.
        *   **Limitations:** Any constraints or uncertainties in the investigation.
        *   Appendices: Logs, test data sheets, site plan.
    *   **Audience:** Designed for engineers, architects, and clients. Must be clear, concise, and practical.

## 7. Important Considerations in Planning

*   **Phased Approach:** Start with less expensive methods and progress to more detailed investigations as needed.
*   **Risk Assessment:** Prioritize investigations based on the potential impact of geotechnical hazards.
*   **Project Lifecycle:** Consider the need for future investigations during the design, construction, and operational phases.
*   **Environmental Impact:** Minimize disruption and potential contamination during site investigations.
*   **Sustainability:** Consider the environmental impact of chosen investigation methods and materials.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Balance the cost of investigation against the potential benefits of reduced risk and optimized design.
*   **Communication:** Maintain open communication between the client, designers, and investigation team.

## 8. Practice Questions

**Question 1:**
A developer is planning a new residential housing estate on a previously undeveloped site. The site is relatively flat but has evidence of a former stream bed running through it. What are the primary geotechnical objectives for the initial phase of investigation?

**Question 2:**
For a proposed five-storey building on a site with suspected soft clay deposits, which of the following investigation methods would be most crucial for assessing settlement potential?
a) Trial Pits
b) Dynamic Probing
c) Cone Penetration Test (CPT) with pore pressure measurement (CPTU)
d) Geophysical Survey (Seismic Refraction)

**Question 3:**
Describe the purpose and key information obtained from a Standard Penetration Test (SPT) and how it's used in geotechnical engineering.

**Question 4:**
Outline the typical contents of a Geotechnical Investigation Report.

---

## 9. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
The primary geotechnical objectives for the initial phase of investigation would likely include:
*   **Characterizing the subsurface stratigraphy:** Identifying the different soil layers and their approximate depths.
*   **Assessing the general strength and stiffness of the soils:** This would inform preliminary foundation design options.
*   **Evaluating the groundwater conditions:** Determining the water table depth and potential for groundwater seepage.
*   **Identifying potential geotechnical hazards:** Such as the soft soils associated with the former stream bed, which might have low bearing capacity or significant compressibility.
*   **Determining the suitability of soil for earthworks:** For landscaping and site grading.

**Answer 2:**
The most crucial method would be **c) Cone Penetration Test (CPT) with pore pressure measurement (CPTU)**.
*   **Explanation:** Soft clay deposits are prone to significant consolidation settlement. CPTU provides continuous data on tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore water pressure, which are directly used to estimate the stiffness, strength, and compressibility of clays, allowing for accurate settlement predictions. Trial pits are too shallow. Dynamic probing is less effective in soft clays. Geophysical surveys provide a general overview but not detailed quantitative data required for settlement analysis.

**Answer 3:**
**Purpose of SPT:** The Standard Penetration Test (SPT) is a widely used in-situ test to assess the relative density of granular soils and the consistency of cohesive soils. It also provides a disturbed soil sample from the split-spoon sampler for visual classification and basic laboratory testing.

**Key Information Obtained:**
*   **SPT N-value:** The number of blows of a standard hammer (63.5 kg falling 760 mm) required to drive a Standard Penetration Test split-spoon sampler 300 mm into the soil (after an initial seating drive).
*   **Soil Classification:** Visual description of the soil sample obtained (e.g., sand, clay, gravel, presence of cobbles, moisture content, colour).

**Usage in Geotechnical Engineering:**
*   **Estimating Bearing Capacity:** The N-value is correlated with the allowable bearing pressure for shallow foundations.
*   **Estimating Settlement:** N-values can be used to estimate the modulus of elasticity or constrained modulus for settlement calculations.
*   **Assessing Soil Density:** Higher N-values generally indicate denser sand or stiffer clay.
*   **Identifying Stratigraphy:** Changes in N-values can indicate changes in soil layers.
*   **Liquefaction Potential:** N-values, along with other factors, are used in the assessment of liquefaction susceptibility in seismic areas.

**Answer 4:**
A typical Geotechnical Investigation Report includes the following sections:
1.  **Introduction:** Project name, location, brief description of the proposed works, and the scope of the investigation.
2.  **Site Description:** Details of the site's physical characteristics, including topography, existing structures, vegetation, and general geological setting.
3.  **Information Review:** Summary of findings from the desk study and reconnaissance visit, including any previous investigations.
4.  **Geological and Hydrogeological Conditions:** Description of the subsurface strata encountered, including their lithology, thickness, and continuity. Information on groundwater levels, flow patterns, and any artesian conditions.
5.  **Fieldwork Executed:** Details of the investigative methods used (boreholes, CPTs, trial pits, etc.), including locations, depths, and dates.
6.  **Field and Laboratory Testing:** Summary of the in-situ and laboratory tests performed.
7.  **Field and Laboratory Test Results:** Presentation of raw data, including borehole logs, CPT logs, and laboratory test results (often in tabular form or appendices).
8.  **Interpretation of Ground Conditions:** Detailed description of each soil/rock stratum, including its engineering properties (e.g., shear strength parameters, compressibility characteristics, permeability).
9.  **Geotechnical Analysis and Assessment:** Evaluation of specific design aspects such as bearing capacity, settlement potential, slope stability, liquefaction risk, seismic site classification, etc., based on the interpreted ground conditions.
10. **Recommendations:** Practical advice and design parameters for the proposed project. This may include foundation types and depths, allowable bearing pressures, estimated settlements, recommendations for earthworks, retaining structures, pavement design, groundwater control measures, and any necessary remedial works.
11. **Limitations:** Discussion of any constraints encountered during the investigation or limitations of the data.
12. **Appendices:** Detailed borehole logs, CPT logs, laboratory test certificates, site plans, and photographs.
