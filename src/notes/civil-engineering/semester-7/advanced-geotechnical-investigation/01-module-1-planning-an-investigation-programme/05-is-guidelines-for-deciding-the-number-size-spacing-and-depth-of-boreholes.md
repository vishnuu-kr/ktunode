# Advanced Geotechnical Investigation: Module 1 - Planning an Investigation Programme

## Topic: I.S. Guidelines for Deciding the Number, Size, Spacing, and Depth of Boreholes

This module focuses on the crucial first step in any geotechnical investigation: planning the program. A well-planned investigation ensures that sufficient and appropriate data is collected to address the project's specific needs, minimizing risks and optimizing design. This topic specifically delves into the Indian Standards (I.S.) guidelines for determining the fundamental parameters of a borehole investigation: how many, how big, how far apart, and how deep they should be.

---

### Learning Outcomes:

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

1.  **Understand the fundamental principles** guiding the determination of the number, size, spacing, and depth of boreholes based on I.S. guidelines.
2.  **Identify the factors** that influence the selection of these parameters for a geotechnical investigation.
3.  **Apply I.S. codes and recommendations** to propose an appropriate borehole investigation plan for various project types.
4.  **Recognize the importance of site variability** and its impact on the investigation strategy.
5.  **Differentiate between different types of structures** and their respective borehole investigation requirements.

---

### I. Introduction to Geotechnical Investigation Planning

*   **Purpose:** To gather sufficient and reliable information about subsurface soil and rock conditions to:
    *   Assess the suitability of the site for the proposed construction.
    *   Determine foundation types and design parameters.
    *   Identify potential geotechnical hazards.
    *   Estimate construction costs and timelines.
*   **Boreholes as Primary Data Collection Tools:** Boreholes are essential for direct observation, sampling, and in-situ testing of subsurface materials.
*   **Importance of I.S. Standards:** Indian Standards provide a framework and recommendations to ensure consistency, quality, and adequacy of geotechnical investigations, aligning with best practices.

---

### II. Key Factors Influencing Borehole Parameters (Based on I.S. Guidelines)

The following factors are paramount when deciding on the number, size, spacing, and depth of boreholes, as per various Indian Standards (primarily IS 1892:1979 - Code of Practice for Subsurface Investigation and Geotechnical Investigations, and other relevant codes like IS 2911 for foundations).

#### A. Nature and Importance of the Structure

*   **Heavy and Critical Structures (e.g., high-rise buildings, bridges, dams, nuclear power plants):**
    *   Require more extensive and detailed investigations.
    *   Higher number of boreholes, closer spacing, and greater depths are generally needed.
    *   Emphasis on identifying any anomalies or weak zones.
*   **Light Structures (e.g., single-story residential buildings, sheds):**
    *   May require less intensive investigations.
    *   Fewer boreholes, wider spacing, and shallower depths might be acceptable.
*   **Linear Structures (e.g., roads, pipelines, railways):**
    *   Boreholes are typically located along the alignment.
    *   Spacing is critical to capture variations along the route.
*   **Area Structures (e.g., industrial complexes, housing colonies):**
    *   Boreholes are distributed over the entire area.

#### B. Type and Variability of Soil/Rock Strata

*   **Homogeneous Strata:**
    *   Fewer boreholes might suffice as conditions are expected to be uniform.
*   **Variable Strata (e.g., presence of lenses, pockets of different soil, undulating bedrock):**
    *   Increased number of boreholes and closer spacing are necessary to map these variations.
*   **Presence of Anomalies:**
    *   If potential problems are suspected (e.g., sinkholes, old foundations, buried channels), additional boreholes should be strategically placed to investigate these areas.
*   **Rock vs. Soil:**
    *   Investigations in rock may require different drilling techniques and sampling methods. The quality of rock (e.g., RQD, weathering) is crucial.

#### C. Groundwater Conditions

*   **Depth of Water Table:**
    *   Influences excavation stability and foundation design.
    *   Boreholes need to be deep enough to determine the water table accurately.
    *   Consideration for perched water tables.
*   **Permeability of Strata:**
    *   Affects drainage and consolidation behavior.
    *   In-situ permeability tests are often conducted within boreholes.
*   **Seepage and Pore Water Pressure:**
    *   Critical for slopes, dams, and retaining structures.

#### D. Topography and Site Conditions

*   **Sloping Sites:**
    *   May require boreholes at different elevations to understand the soil profile.
    *   Slope stability analysis requires careful placement of boreholes.
*   **Existing Structures:**
    *   Investigations around existing structures need to consider potential impacts on them.
*   **Accessibility:**
    *   Practical considerations for placing boreholes can influence spacing.

#### E. Project Requirements and Budget

*   **Level of Accuracy Required:**
    *   Higher accuracy demands more data, hence more boreholes.
*   **Economic Constraints:**
    *   The budget often dictates the extent of the investigation. A balance between thoroughness and cost-effectiveness is essential.
*   **Time Constraints:**
    *   The timeline for the project will influence the speed and extent of the investigation.

### III. I.S. Guidelines for Borehole Parameters

The specific numbers and guidelines often come from a combination of general recommendations and project-specific requirements. IS 1892:1979 provides a foundational framework.

#### A. Number of Boreholes

*   **General Principle:** The number of boreholes should be sufficient to obtain a reliable picture of the subsurface conditions over the entire area of the structure.
*   **Recommendations (as per IS 1892:1979 and common practice):**

    | Structure Type                                   | Number of Boreholes (General Guideline)                                                                                                                                                            |
    | :----------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | **Individual Buildings:**                        |                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
    | Small buildings (e.g., single-story houses)      | Minimum of 2 boreholes, typically at diagonally opposite corners.                                                                                                                                    |
    | Medium-sized buildings                           | Minimum of 3 to 4 boreholes, at corners or key load-bearing points.                                                                                                                                |
    | Large buildings (e.g., multi-story, industrial)  | 4 or more boreholes, distributed to cover the entire footprint. Additional boreholes may be required in areas of significant load concentration or suspected variability.                               |
    | **Bridges:**                                     |                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
    | Abutments and piers                               | Minimum 2 boreholes per abutment and 2 boreholes per pier.                                                                                                                                         |
    | **Roads/Railways:**                              |                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
    | Per km of alignment                              | Typically 1 borehole per 100-300 meters. Spacing can be reduced in areas of high variability or critical structures (e.g., embankments, cuttings, bridges).                                           |
    | **Dams/Embankments:**                            |                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
    | Along the centerline                               | Boreholes at closer intervals, e.g., 50-100 meters, to understand foundation uniformity and potential seepage paths.                                                                                   |
    | **Special Structures (e.g., silos, towers):**    | Minimum 2 boreholes under the foundation area, possibly more depending on the load distribution.                                                                                                     |
    | **Area Foundations (e.g., industrial yards):**   | Boreholes distributed in a grid pattern, with spacing dependent on the variability of the site.                                                                                                    |

*   **Important Note:** These are *minimum* guidelines. The actual number should be based on a thorough assessment of the factors listed above.

#### B. Size of Boreholes

*   **Purpose of Size:**
    *   To allow for the insertion of sampling equipment (e.g., split-spoon sampler, Shelby tube).
    *   To accommodate in-situ testing devices (e.g., SPT, CPT, pressuremeter).
    *   To allow for visual inspection of the borehole walls and extracted samples.
    *   To facilitate the installation of instruments for monitoring (e.g., piezometers).
*   **Standard Diameters (as per IS 1892:1979):**
    *   **Minimum diameter:** Generally 100 mm.
    *   **Common diameters for SPT:** 100 mm or 150 mm.
    *   **Larger diameters (e.g., 150 mm, 200 mm or more):** Recommended for:
        *   Obtaining undisturbed samples (e.g., using larger diameter samplers).
        *   Conducting more extensive in-situ testing (e.g., pressuremeter tests).
        *   Investigating cohesive soils or soft rocks where core recovery is important.
        *   When installing larger diameter casings or monitoring instruments.
*   **Considerations:**
    *   The size of sampling equipment to be used.
    *   The type of in-situ tests planned.
    *   The need for casing to support borehole walls.

#### C. Spacing of Boreholes

*   **General Principle:** Spacing should be such that it captures the significant variations in subsurface conditions.
*   **Recommendations (as per IS 1892:1979 and common practice):**

    | Structure Type                                                                 | Spacing (General Guideline)                                                                                                                                                                  |
    | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | **Individual Buildings:**                                                      |                                                                                                                                                                                              |
    | Small to Medium Buildings                                                      | Typically 5 to 15 meters, especially at corners or under main load-bearing elements.                                                                                                         |
    | Large Buildings (e.g., multi-story, industrial)                                | 10 to 20 meters, arranged in a grid pattern. Closer spacing (e.g., 5-10 meters) might be needed under heavily loaded columns or in areas of suspected variability.                                |
    | **Bridges:**                                                                   |                                                                                                                                                                                              |
    | Abutments and Piers                                                            | Boreholes should be located close to the footing's footprint, often within or immediately adjacent to it. Spacing is dictated by the dimensions of the foundation element.                      |
    | **Roads/Railways:**                                                            |                                                                                                                                                                                              |
    | Per km of alignment                                                            | 100 to 300 meters. Reduced to 50-100 meters in critical sections (e.g., bridges, culverts, steep slopes, areas of known ground issues).                                                      |
    | **Dams/Embankments:**                                                          |                                                                                                                                                                                              |
    | Along the centerline                                                           | 50 to 100 meters. Closer spacing might be required across the dam's width at strategic locations to check for seepage potential.                                                              |
    | **Area Foundations (e.g., industrial yards):**                                 | A grid pattern is common. Spacing can range from 15 to 30 meters, depending on the uniformity of the site and the nature of the structures within the area.                                 |

*   **Key Considerations for Spacing:**
    *   **Variability:** The more variable the ground, the closer the spacing.
    *   **Critical Load Points:** Boreholes should be concentrated under heavy loads or critical structural elements.
    *   **Extent of Investigation:** If the project is large, a broader spacing might be used initially, with denser spacing in areas of concern.
    *   **Geological Features:** Spacing should be adjusted to investigate geological boundaries, fault lines, or other features.

#### D. Depth of Boreholes

*   **General Principle:** Boreholes must extend to a depth sufficient to encounter strata that will not be adversely affected by the proposed structure and to establish the regional geological and hydrogeological conditions.
*   **Key Depths to Reach:**
    *   **Depth of influence of the structure:** This is the depth to which the stresses from the foundation are significant.
    *   **Depth to competent strata:** A layer of soil or rock that can adequately support the foundation loads.
    *   **Depth to bedrock:** If bedrock is intended as the bearing stratum.
    *   **Depth where stresses from the structure are negligible:** Typically, where the vertical stress increase due to the foundation is less than 10% of the effective overburden pressure.
    *   **Depth to the water table:** Essential for assessing buoyancy, pore pressures, and potential liquefaction.
    *   **Depth to a layer that will not undergo significant consolidation settlement.**
*   **I.S. Recommendations for Depth:**

    *   **General Rule of Thumb (IS 1892:1979):** Boreholes should extend to a depth of at least 1.5 times the width of the foundation for shallow foundations, or to a depth where the increase in stress due to the foundation is negligible. For deep foundations, they should extend well below the founding stratum of the pile.
    *   **Specific Depth Considerations:**
        *   **Shallow Foundations:**
            *   For isolated footings: Depth should be at least 2B to 3B below the footing base, where B is the width of the footing.
            *   For raft foundations: Depth should be at least 1.5 to 2 times the width of the raft, or to a depth where stress increase is insignificant.
        *   **Deep Foundations (Piles):**
            *   Boreholes should extend to a depth sufficient to confirm the bearing capacity of the soil/rock at the pile base and along the pile shaft, and to identify any problematic layers. This often means going significantly below the expected pile tip, sometimes to bedrock or a competent stratum.
        *   **Embankments/Retaining Walls:**
            *   Boreholes should extend deep enough to investigate the stability of the slope and potential for bearing capacity failure or excessive settlement. This can be several meters below the toe of the proposed structure.
        *   **Dams:**
            *   Boreholes are drilled to bedrock or a competent, impermeable stratum beneath the dam to ensure proper foundation support and prevent excessive seepage.
        *   **Roads/Railways:**
            *   For flexible pavements, boreholes should extend through the subgrade to a depth where the subgrade's influence is negligible (typically 1.5 to 2 times the expected rut depth). For rigid pavements, investigations should extend to a depth that adequately characterizes the foundation's load-bearing capacity.

*   **Determining the "Negligible Stress Increase" Depth:**
    *   **Formula:** For a square/rectangular footing, the increase in vertical stress ($\Delta \sigma_z$) at a depth 'z' can be estimated using Boussinesq's or Westergaard's equations.
    *   **Guideline:** Continue drilling until $\Delta \sigma_z \leq 0.1 \sigma_{vo}'$, where $\sigma_{vo}'$ is the effective overburden pressure at that depth.

### IV. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
A four-story residential building is planned on a site where preliminary geological surveys suggest the possibility of varying soil conditions, including pockets of soft clay. The building footprint is approximately 20m x 15m.
Based on I.S. guidelines, propose the number, size, spacing, and approximate depth of boreholes required for the geotechnical investigation. Justify your choices.

**Answer 1:**

*   **Number of Boreholes:** Minimum of 4 boreholes are recommended for a building of this size, located at the corners of the footprint. Given the potential for varying conditions, a fifth borehole placed in the center or a location of anticipated higher load concentration would be prudent.
*   **Size of Boreholes:** A standard diameter of 100 mm or 150 mm would be appropriate. 150 mm is preferable to allow for better sampling of cohesive soils and potential for in-situ testing if required.
*   **Spacing of Boreholes:** Boreholes should be placed at approximately 15m and 20m intervals along the building's perimeter, effectively at the corners. The central borehole would be roughly 10m from the nearest perimeter boreholes.
*   **Depth of Boreholes:** The building width is approximately 15m. Following the guideline of 2B to 3B, the boreholes should extend to a depth of at least (2 * 15m) = 30m to (3 * 15m) = 45m below the proposed foundation level. Additionally, the depth should ensure the stress increase is less than 10% of the effective overburden pressure. The water table depth should also be determined, and if it is shallow, additional depth might be needed to understand its influence.

**Question 2:**
For the construction of a highway embankment reaching a height of 10 meters, a geotechnical investigation is to be conducted along a 1 km stretch. The terrain is generally undulating with a few small streams crossing the alignment.
Outline the I.S. recommended approach for determining the number, size, spacing, and depth of boreholes.

**Answer 2:**

*   **Number of Boreholes:** For a 1 km stretch, a baseline of 3 to 4 boreholes per kilometer (e.g., 1 every 250-333m) might be considered. However, given the undulating terrain and potential for variations, especially near streams, a denser spacing (e.g., 1 per 100-150m) would be more appropriate, particularly at locations where embankment height is significant or where geological discontinuities are suspected.
*   **Size of Boreholes:** 100 mm or 150 mm diameter boreholes are typical. 150 mm is beneficial for obtaining larger samples and for potential in-situ testing like Standard Penetration Tests (SPT).
*   **Spacing of Boreholes:** Spacing should ideally be around 100-200 meters along the alignment. Closer spacing (50-100 meters) should be adopted at critical locations such as the crest and toe of the embankment, near stream crossings, and in areas identified as potentially problematic from preliminary surveys.
*   **Depth of Boreholes:** Boreholes should extend to a depth sufficient to investigate the stability of the foundation soil under the embankment load. This typically means extending to a depth of 1.5 to 2 times the height of the embankment, or at least to a layer of firm material, or where the stress increase is negligible. So, for a 10m embankment, boreholes should extend to at least 15-20 meters below the natural ground level, and potentially deeper if weak layers are encountered or if the groundwater table is significant.

### V. Important Points to Remember

*   **No One-Size-Fits-All:** I.S. guidelines provide a framework, but the final plan must be tailored to the specific project and site conditions.
*   **Iterative Process:** Planning an investigation can be iterative. Initial findings might necessitate additional boreholes or adjustments to the program.
*   **Site Variability is Key:** The more variable the subsurface conditions, the more intensive the investigation needs to be.
*   **Critical Structures, Critical Investigation:** Higher importance or risk associated with a structure demands a more thorough and comprehensive investigation.
*   **Document Everything:** Thorough record-keeping of borehole locations, depths, soil descriptions, sampling methods, and test results is crucial.
*   **Consult Relevant Standards:** Always refer to the latest versions of relevant Indian Standards (IS 1892, IS 2911, etc.) for the most up-to-date and specific recommendations.
