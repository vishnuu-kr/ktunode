# ADVANCED GEOTECHNICAL INVESTIGATION

## Module 1: Planning an Investigation Programme

### Topic: Reconnaissance, Preliminary, and Detailed Investigation

---

## 1. Introduction to Geotechnical Investigation Planning

The successful execution of any geotechnical engineering project hinges on a thorough and appropriate geotechnical investigation. This process involves systematically gathering information about the subsurface conditions to assess their suitability for the proposed construction and to design appropriate foundations and earthworks. Planning is paramount to ensure efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and accuracy. This module introduces the phased approach to geotechnical investigations: reconnaissance, preliminary, and detailed investigation.

---

## 2. Reconnaissance Investigation (Desk Study)

**Definition:** The reconnaissance investigation, often referred to as a desk study, is the initial phase of a geotechnical investigation. It involves gathering all available existing information about the site and its surroundings without any site visits or intrusive testing.

**Purpose:**
*   To gain a general understanding of the site's geological, hydrological, and topographical characteristics.
*   To identify potential geotechnical hazards and constraints.
*   To inform the planning of subsequent investigation phases.
*   To estimate the likely geological conditions and potential soil/rock types.
*   To identify areas requiring further investigation.

**Key Activities and Information Sources:**

*   **Review of Existing Geological Maps and Reports:**
    *   **Source:** Geological surveys, national geological institutes.
    *   **Information:** Bedrock geology, surficial deposits (alluvium, glacial till, etc.), geological structures (faults, folds), historical seismicity.
    *   **Example:** Identifying a site located on a known active fault zone or in an area prone to landslides based on geological maps.
*   **Review of Topographical Maps and Aerial Photographs:**
    *   **Source:** Ordnance surveys, national mapping agencies, satellite imagery.
    *   **Information:** Ground surface elevation, slopes, drainage patterns, existing infrastructure, vegetation cover, signs of past earthworks or instability.
    *   **Example:** Observing steep natural slopes or evidence of old quarries on aerial photographs indicating potential stability issues.
*   **Review of Existing Ground Investigation Reports:**
    *   **Source:** Previous geotechnical reports for nearby sites, local authority records, previous owners.
    *   **Information:** Soil types encountered, groundwater levels, foundation designs used, any encountered difficulties.
    *   **Example:** Finding reports from adjacent construction projects that encountered problematic soft clay, suggesting similar conditions might exist on the current site.
*   **Review of Hydrological and Hydrogeological Data:**
    *   **Source:** Water authorities, environmental agencies, flood plain maps.
    *   **Information:** Groundwater levels and fluctuations, surface water bodies, drainage systems, potential for flooding, soil permeability.
    *   **Example:** Identifying a site in a known floodplain or with a shallow water table that could impact excavation and foundation design.
*   **Review of Local Building Records and Anecdotal Evidence:**
    *   **Source:** Local council records, long-term residents, local construction professionals.
    *   **Information:** Historical settlement issues, common foundation types, local knowledge of ground conditions.
    *   **Example:** Hearing from a local builder that many previous structures in the area have experienced significant differential settlement due to compressible fill.
*   **Review of Environmental Data:**
    *   **Source:** Environmental protection agencies, industrial registers.
    *   **Information:** Potential for contamination, previous land use (e.g., industrial sites, landfill).
    *   **Example:** Identifying a site that was formerly a gasworks, raising concerns about soil contamination.

**Outcome:** A reconnaissance report that summarizes findings, highlights potential geotechnical concerns, and provides recommendations for the scope and type of further investigation required.

**Important Point to Remember:** The reconnaissance phase is crucial for minimizing unnecessary field testing and focusing resources on the most critical areas. It's about gathering information without disturbing the ground.

## 3. Preliminary Investigation

**Definition:** The preliminary investigation is the first phase of *in-situ* (on-site) investigation. It aims to confirm the findings of the reconnaissance study and to gather basic geotechnical data to support initial design decisions and plan the detailed investigation.

**Purpose:**
*   To verify or refute the assumptions made during the reconnaissance.
*   To establish a general understanding of the soil and rock strata.
*   To identify the need for more specialized testing.
*   To determine the approximate depth of bearing strata.
*   To obtain preliminary groundwater information.
*   To provide data for initial foundation type selection.

**Key Activities and Techniques:**

*   **Site Walkover and Visual Inspection:**
    *   **Description:** A detailed visual examination of the site and its surroundings.
    *   **Information:** Surface features, existing structures, visible ground conditions, evidence of erosion, vegetation type, signs of past activity.
    *   **Example:** Observing cracks in existing pavements or building walls, noting unusual surface depressions.
*   **Trial Pits (Manually Excavated or Mechanically Excavated):**
    *   **Description:** Excavations made into the ground to expose soil strata.
    *   **Information:** Visual identification of soil types, stratification, presence of groundwater, sampling of disturbed and undisturbed soil.
    *   **Advantages:** Relatively inexpensive, allows for direct observation and sampling of a large volume of soil, can investigate shallow depths effectively.
    *   **Limitations:** Limited to shallow depths (typically up to 4-5m), can be influenced by groundwater ingress, might not represent the average soil conditions if localized variations are significant.
    *   **Example:** Digging a trial pit to observe the transition from fill material to natural clay, and taking samples for classification.
*   **Hand Auger Borings:**
    *   **Description:** Shallow boreholes made with a hand-operated auger.
    *   **Information:** Identification of soil types, approximate groundwater levels, limited sampling.
    *   **Advantages:** Quick, inexpensive, useful for very shallow investigations or initial ground profiling.
    *   **Limitations:** Limited depth, often results in disturbed samples, difficult in very dense soils or cobbles.
*   **Geophysical Methods (Shallow Applications):**
    *   **Description:** Non-intrusive techniques that use physical principles to infer subsurface properties.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR):** Detects buried utilities, shallow voids, and variations in subsurface layers.
        *   **Electrical Resistivity (ER):** Identifies variations in soil moisture content, soil type, and potential contamination plumes.
        *   **Seismic Refraction/Reflection:** Determines the depth to bedrock and seismic velocities of subsurface layers.
    *   **Purpose:** To provide a broader spatial coverage of subsurface conditions and to identify anomalies that warrant more focused investigation.
    *   **Example:** Using GPR to identify the extent of buried foundations from a previous structure on the site.
*   **Limited In-Situ Testing:**
    *   **Description:** Basic tests performed on-site to assess soil properties.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Dynamic Cone Penetration Test (DCPT):** Provides an indication of soil strength and density.
        *   **Portable Dynamic Probing (e.g., Scala Penetrometer):** Similar to DCPT.
        *   **Pocket Penetrometer:** Measures the unconfined compressive strength of cohesive soils.
        *   **Torvane Shear Test:** Measures the undrained shear strength of soft to medium stiff clays.
    *   **Purpose:** To obtain preliminary strength and stiffness parameters.
    *   **Example:** Using a Pocket Penetrometer on samples from trial pits to get a quick estimate of clay strength.

**Outcome:** A preliminary investigation report summarizing the findings, identifying general stratigraphy, providing preliminary geotechnical parameters, and outlining recommendations for the detailed investigation, including the number, type, and location of boreholes and specific tests.

**Important Point to Remember:** The preliminary investigation bridges the gap between desk study and intensive fieldwork. It's about getting on the ground to verify assumptions and gather essential data efficiently.

## 4. Detailed Investigation

**Definition:** The detailed investigation is the most comprehensive phase of the geotechnical investigation. It involves extensive fieldwork and laboratory testing to obtain precise and reliable data required for detailed design.

**Purpose:**
*   To accurately define the stratigraphy, thickness, and extent of soil and rock layers.
*   To determine the engineering properties of the soil and rock for design calculations (strength, compressibility, permeability).
*   To establish the groundwater regime, including levels, fluctuations, and potential inflows.
*   To assess potential geotechnical hazards in detail (e.g., liquefaction potential, slope stability, settlement characteristics).
*   To provide data for the selection and design of foundations, earthworks, and retaining structures.
*   To inform construction methodologies.

**Key Activities and Techniques:**

*   **Boreholes:**
    *   **Description:** Deep holes drilled into the ground.
    *   **Information:** Continuous logging of soil and rock strata, undisturbed sampling, in-situ testing, installation of monitoring instruments.
    *   **Methods:** Rotary drilling, shell and auger, cable percussive.
    *   **Sampling:**
        *   **Disturbed Samples:** For classification and some basic index property tests (e.g., from SPT, augers).
        *   **Undisturbed Samples:** For strength and compressibility tests (e.g., Shelby tubes, piston samplers, block samples).
    *   **Example:** Drilling boreholes to a significant depth below the proposed foundation level to identify the bearing stratum and assess its properties.
*   **In-Situ Testing (Advanced):**
    *   **Standard Penetration Test (SPT):**
        *   **Description:** A dynamic penetration test performed in boreholes using a split-spoon sampler driven by a standard hammer.
        *   **Information:** Provides a measure of soil density and strength (N-value), allows for disturbed sampling.
        *   **Importance:** Widely used for granular soils and stiff clays.
        *   **Example:** Obtaining SPT N-values to estimate the bearing capacity of a sand layer.
    *   **Cone Penetration Test (CPT) / Cone Penetration Test with Pore Pressure Measurement (CPTU):**
        *   **Description:** A continuous penetration test using a cone-shaped probe. CPTU also measures pore water pressure.
        *   **Information:** Provides continuous data on tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore pressure, which can be correlated to soil type, density, and strength.
        *   **Advantages:** Fast, provides continuous data, can detect thin layers, less dependent on operator skill than SPT.
        *   **Example:** Using CPTU to identify soft clay layers and their undrained shear strength.
    *   **Pressuremeter Test (PMT):**
        *   **Description:** An in-situ test where a probe is expanded against the borehole wall to measure stress-strain behaviour.
        *   **Information:** Provides values for the horizontal stress, limit pressure, and deformation modulus.
        *   **Purpose:** To determine the in-situ stress-strain characteristics of soil and rock, particularly useful for laterally loaded structures.
    *   **Dilatometer Test (DMT):**
        *   **Description:** A flat blade with a flexible membrane is pushed into the ground, and the membrane is inflated.
        *   **Information:** Provides indices related to soil stiffness, strength, and stress history.
        *   **Purpose:** Useful for characterizing clays and silts, estimating settlement.
    *   **Plate Load Test (PLT):**
        *   **Description:** A rigid plate is loaded incrementally at the base of an excavation or borehole.
        *   **Information:** Measures the settlement under load, providing a direct assessment of the bearing capacity and settlement characteristics of the soil at foundation level.
        *   **Limitations:** Only tests a limited zone, can be affected by edge effects.
    *   **Geophysical Methods (Deeper Applications):**
        *   **Description:** Techniques used to investigate deeper stratigraphy, bedrock depth, and geological structures.
        *   **Examples:** Seismic refraction/reflection, electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) for deeper profiles.
*   **Groundwater Monitoring:**
    *   **Description:** Installation of piezometers or standpipes in boreholes to monitor groundwater levels and fluctuations over time.
    *   **Purpose:** To accurately determine the groundwater table, its seasonal variations, and potential pressure build-up.
    *   **Example:** Installing piezometers to monitor pore water pressure in an embankment to assess stability during construction.
*   **Laboratory Testing:**
    *   **Description:** Testing of collected soil and rock samples to determine their physical and engineering properties.
    *   **Types of Tests:**
        *   **Classification Tests:** Particle size distribution (sieving, hydrometer), Atterberg limits (liquid limit, plastic limit, plasticity index), moisture content, specific gravity.
        *   **Strength Tests:** Unconfined compressive strength (UCS) for cohesive soils, triaxial compression tests (UU, CU, CD), direct shear tests, consolidated undrained shear strength (CU SS).
        *   **Consolidation/Compressibility Tests:** Oedometer tests (one-dimensional consolidation) to determine compressibility and preconsolidation pressure.
        *   **Permeability Tests:** Constant head and falling head permeability tests.
        *   **Other Tests:** Compaction tests (Proctor tests), durability tests for rocks, chemical tests (e.g., for sulfates, chlorides).
    *   **Example:** Performing triaxial compression tests on undisturbed clay samples to determine the effective stress shear strength parameters ($\text{c'}$ and $\phi'$).

**Outcome:** A detailed geotechnical investigation report containing comprehensive data on soil and rock strata, engineering properties, groundwater conditions, and a detailed assessment of geotechnical risks. This report forms the basis for foundation design, earthwork specifications, and construction planning.

**Important Point to Remember:** The detailed investigation is the most data-intensive phase. Accuracy and representativeness of samples and tests are paramount for safe and economical design.

## 5. Phased Approach and Iterative Nature

It's important to understand that these phases are not always strictly sequential and can be iterative.
*   Findings from the preliminary investigation might necessitate a revision of the scope of the detailed investigation.
*   During detailed investigation, new issues may arise that require further targeted testing.
*   The entire investigation process is informed by the project's scope, budget, and risk tolerance.

**Example of Iteration:** If the preliminary investigation reveals unexpectedly soft ground conditions, the detailed investigation might be expanded to include more CPTUs and laboratory consolidation tests to accurately assess settlement potential, rather than proceeding with initial assumptions for a shallow foundation.

## 6. Practice Questions

**Question 1:** Which phase of a geotechnical investigation is primarily focused on gathering information from existing sources without site visits?
a) Preliminary Investigation
b) Detailed Investigation
c) Reconnaissance Investigation
d) Site Walkover

**Question 2:** Which of the following techniques is most likely to be used during a *preliminary* investigation to get a general idea of soil types at shallow depths?
a) Cone Penetration Test (CPT)
b) Triaxial Compression Test
c) Trial Pits
d) Plate Load Test

**Question 3:** The Standard Penetration Test (SPT) is a key in-situ test commonly performed during which phase of investigation?
a) Reconnaissance Investigation
b) Preliminary Investigation
c) Detailed Investigation
d) Site Walkover

**Question 4:** What is a key purpose of the reconnaissance investigation?
a) To determine precise settlement characteristics of soil.
b) To identify potential geotechnical hazards and constraints from existing data.
c) To measure the undrained shear strength of cohesive soils.
d) To install piezometers for groundwater monitoring.

**Question 5:** Which laboratory test would be most appropriate to determine the compressibility of a clay layer?
a) Particle Size Distribution
b) Atterberg Limits
c) Oedometer Test
d) Unconfined Compressive Strength Test

---

## 7. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:** c) Reconnaissance Investigation
**Explanation:** The reconnaissance investigation, or desk study, relies entirely on existing data and does not involve site visits or intrusive testing.

**Answer 2:** c) Trial Pits
**Explanation:** Trial pits are relatively inexpensive and allow for direct observation and sampling of shallow soil strata, making them suitable for preliminary investigations. CPT is more for detailed profiling, triaxial tests are lab-based for detailed strength, and plate load tests are for foundation level capacity assessment.

**Answer 3:** c) Detailed Investigation
**Explanation:** While SPT can be used in preliminary investigations, it is a cornerstone of detailed investigations for gathering both strength data (N-values) and allowing for disturbed sampling.

**Answer 4:** b) To identify potential geotechnical hazards and constraints from existing data.
**Explanation:** The reconnaissance phase is about understanding the context of the site through available information before any physical work begins.

**Answer 5:** c) Oedometer Test
**Explanation:** The oedometer test (one-dimensional consolidation test) directly measures how a soil sample compresses under applied load, providing data on compressibility and preconsolidation pressure.

## 8. Summary of Key Concepts and Learning Outcomes

*   **Phased Approach:** Geotechnical investigations are typically carried out in phases: Reconnaissance, Preliminary, and Detailed.
*   **Reconnaissance:** Desk study; gather existing information; identify potential hazards; inform subsequent phases.
*   **Preliminary:** Initial site work; verify desk study findings; basic profiling and sampling; inform detailed investigation scope.
*   **Detailed:** Extensive fieldwork and lab testing; precise data for design; define stratigraphy, engineering properties, and groundwater.
*   **Iterative Nature:** The phases can overlap and inform each other, allowing for adjustments to the investigation plan.
*   **Key Techniques:** Understanding the purpose and application of trial pits, boreholes, SPT, CPT, and various laboratory tests is essential.
*   **Importance of Planning:** Proper planning ensures efficient use of resources, cost-effectiveness, and acquisition of necessary data for safe and economical design.
