# Module 3: Control Principles - Wet Collectors

## 1. Introduction to Wet Collectors

Wet collectors, also known as scrubbers, are air pollution control devices that use a liquid (usually water) to remove particulate matter and sometimes gaseous pollutants from an airstream. They are versatile and effective for a wide range of applications.

### Key Concepts:

*   **Scrubbing:** The process of removing pollutants from a gas stream by bringing it into contact with a liquid.
*   **Capture Mechanism:** How the pollutant is removed from the gas stream by the scrubbing liquid.
*   **Mass Transfer:** The movement of a pollutant from the gas phase to the liquid phase.
*   **Momentum Transfer:** The transfer of kinetic energy between the gas and liquid phases, which aids in particle capture and droplet formation.

### Learning Outcome 1: Explain the basic principles of wet collectors.

Wet collectors operate on the principle of bringing a contaminated gas stream into intimate contact with a scrubbing liquid. This contact facilitates the transfer of pollutants from the gas phase to the liquid phase. The primary mechanisms for pollutant removal are:

*   **Impaction:** Larger particles collide directly with liquid droplets or wetted surfaces.
*   **Interception:** Particles following the gas streamlines come into contact with liquid droplets or wetted surfaces and adhere due to van der Waals forces.
*   **Diffusion (Brownian Motion):** Very small particles (sub-micron) move randomly due to collisions with gas molecules. This random motion increases their probability of colliding with and being captured by liquid droplets.
*   **Inertial Impaction:** Gas streamlines bend around obstacles (like droplets), but particles with sufficient inertia continue in their original path, impacting the obstacle. This is more effective for larger particles.
*   **Absorption:** For gaseous pollutants, the process involves dissolving the gas into the liquid. This depends on the solubility of the gas in the scrubbing liquid.
*   **Chemical Reaction:** Some gaseous pollutants can be removed by reacting with a chemical added to the scrubbing liquid.

## 2. Types of Wet Collectors

Wet collectors are broadly classified based on the method of contact between the gas and liquid.

### Learning Outcome 2: Classify different types of wet collectors.

Here's a classification of common wet collector types:

#### 2.1. Spray Towers (Spray Scrubbers)

*   **Description:** Gas flows upwards through a chamber while liquid is sprayed downwards from nozzles.
*   **Mechanisms:** Primarily impaction and interception.
*   **Efficiency:** Relatively low for fine particles. Efficiency can be increased by increasing liquid-to-gas ratio and spray density.
*   **Applications:** Primarily for removing larger particles and some gaseous pollutants.
*   **Example:** Cooling towers, humidifiers.

#### 2.2. Packed Bed Scrubbers

*   **Description:** Gas flows upwards through a bed of packing material (e.g., rings, saddles, balls). Liquid flows downwards over the packing, creating a thin film.
*   **Mechanisms:** Impaction, interception, diffusion, and absorption (as gas passes through the liquid film).
*   **Efficiency:** Higher than spray towers for fine particles and gases due to large surface area provided by packing.
*   **Applications:** Removing fine particulates, acidic gases (e.g., SO2, HCl), and soluble organic vapors.
*   **Example:** Removing SO2 from flue gas in power plants.

#### 2.3. Tray Scrubbers

*   **Description:** Gas flows upwards through a series of trays. Liquid flows downwards, cascading from tray to tray. Gas bubbles through the liquid on each tray.
*   **Mechanisms:** Impaction, interception, diffusion, and absorption.
*   **Efficiency:** High efficiency for both particulate and gaseous pollutants.
*   **Applications:** Similar to packed bed scrubbers, widely used for flue gas desulfurization.
*   **Example:** Sieve tray scrubbers, bubble cap tray scrubbers.

#### 2.4. Venturi Scrubbers

*   **Description:** Gas is accelerated through a converging-diverging throat. Liquid is injected at or near the throat. The high velocity creates fine droplets and intense turbulence, enhancing particle capture.
*   **Mechanisms:** Primarily inertial impaction due to high gas velocity and droplet atomization.
*   **Efficiency:** Very high efficiency for fine and sub-micron particles.
*   **Applications:** High-efficiency removal of fine dust and fumes.
*   **Example:** Removing fine metal fumes from industrial processes.

#### 2.5. Cyclone Wet Scrubber

*   **Description:** Combines principles of a cyclone separator with wet scrubbing. Gas enters tangentially, creating a vortex. Water is introduced to enhance particle removal.
*   **Mechanisms:** Centrifugal force for particle separation, impaction, and absorption.
*   **Efficiency:** Moderate to high efficiency, particularly for larger particles.
*   **Applications:** Removing dust and fumes in various industrial applications.

## 3. Design Considerations for Wet Collectors

Effective design is crucial for optimal performance of wet collectors.

### Learning Outcome 3: Discuss the design and operating parameters affecting the performance of wet collectors.

Several factors influence the efficiency and operational characteristics of wet collectors:

#### 3.1. Liquid-to-Gas Ratio (L/G)

*   **Definition:** The ratio of the volume of scrubbing liquid used to the volume of gas treated, typically expressed in L/m³ or gal/acf.
*   **Effect:** Higher L/G ratios generally increase removal efficiency by providing more scrubbing liquid for pollutant capture and by improving droplet formation. However, excessively high L/G ratios increase operating costs (pumping, liquid treatment) and can lead to increased pressure drop.
*   **Optimization:** Finding an optimal L/G ratio that balances efficiency and cost is essential.

#### 3.2. Pressure Drop ($\Delta P$)

*   **Definition:** The resistance to gas flow through the collector, measured in Pascal (Pa) or inches of water gauge (in. w.g.).
*   **Effect:** Higher pressure drop often indicates more vigorous gas-liquid contact, which generally leads to higher collection efficiency, especially for fine particles. However, high pressure drop increases fan power requirements and operating costs.
*   **Factors:** Related to gas velocity, L/G ratio, packing density (in packed beds), and throat design (in Venturi scrubbers).

#### 3.3. Droplet Size Distribution (for atomized scrubbers like Venturi)

*   **Definition:** The range of droplet sizes produced by the scrubbing liquid.
*   **Effect:** Smaller droplet sizes increase the surface area available for mass transfer, leading to higher collection efficiency, especially for fine particles via diffusion and interception.
*   **Factors:** Liquid pressure, nozzle design, gas velocity, and physical properties of the liquid.

#### 3.4. Gas Velocity

*   **Definition:** The speed at which the gas flows through the collector.
*   **Effect:** Higher gas velocities can increase impaction and turbulence, leading to better particle capture. However, excessively high velocities can reduce residence time, cause re-entrainment of collected particles, and increase pressure drop. For Venturi scrubbers, gas velocity in the throat is critical for atomization.

#### 3.5. Gas-Liquid Contact Time (Residence Time)

*   **Definition:** The average time the gas stream spends in contact with the scrubbing liquid.
*   **Effect:** Longer contact times generally allow for more efficient mass transfer and pollutant removal. This is influenced by the scrubber design, gas flow rate, and liquid flow rate.

#### 3.6. Scrubbing Liquid Properties

*   **Effect:**
    *   **Viscosity:** Affects droplet formation and flow behavior.
    *   **Surface Tension:** Influences droplet breakup and stability.
    *   **Chemical Reactivity:** Crucial for gaseous pollutant removal through absorption or chemical reaction.
    *   **Solubility:** Determines the capacity of the liquid to absorb gaseous pollutants.

#### 3.7. Inlet Particle Loading and Size Distribution

*   **Effect:** The concentration and size of pollutants entering the scrubber significantly impact the required L/G ratio, pressure drop, and overall design for achieving target removal efficiency.

## 4. Applications of Wet Collectors

Wet collectors are widely used across various industries.

### Learning Outcome 4: Identify common applications of wet collectors in industry.

Wet collectors are employed in numerous industrial processes where both particulate and gaseous pollutants are present:

*   **Power Plants:** Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD) to remove sulfur dioxide (SO2) using lime or limestone slurries.
*   **Chemical Industry:** Removal of acidic gases (HCl, HF, SOx), ammonia, and organic vapors from process streams.
*   **Metal Processing:** Fume collection from smelting, refining, and galvanizing operations.
*   **Cement Plants:** Dust collection from kilns and material handling.
*   **Food Processing:** Odor and dust control from drying, baking, and processing operations.
*   **Pulp and Paper Mills:** Removal of particulate matter and sulfur compounds.
*   **Pharmaceutical Manufacturing:** Control of dust and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) from drying and mixing processes.
*   **Foundries:** Removal of dust and fumes from melting and casting operations.

## 5. Advantages and Disadvantages of Wet Collectors

Like any pollution control technology, wet collectors have their pros and cons.

### Learning Outcome 5: Analyze the advantages and disadvantages of wet collectors.

#### 5.1. Advantages:

*   **Simultaneous Removal of Particulates and Gases:** Many wet collectors can effectively remove both particulate matter and gaseous pollutants in a single unit.
*   **Ability to Handle High Temperatures and High Humidity:** Wet collectors can tolerate high gas temperatures and are not significantly affected by high moisture content in the gas stream.
*   **Explosion and Fire Hazard Reduction:** The presence of water can mitigate the risks associated with flammable or explosive dusts.
*   **Removal of Fine Particles:** Venturi scrubbers, in particular, are highly effective for sub-micron particles.
*   **Cooling of Gas Stream:** The evaporation of scrubbing liquid can cool the gas stream, which can be beneficial for downstream processes.
*   **Relatively Simple Design and Operation:** Some types, like spray towers, are relatively simple to operate.
*   **Can Handle Corrosive Gases:** By selecting appropriate scrubbing liquids and materials of construction.

#### 5.2. Disadvantages:

*   **Wastewater Treatment:** The scrubbing liquid becomes contaminated and requires treatment or disposal, which can be costly and environmentally challenging.
*   **Re-entrainment of Droplets:** Fine liquid droplets can be carried out of the scrubber with the clean gas, requiring mist eliminators.
*   **Corrosion:** The presence of water and dissolved pollutants can lead to corrosion of equipment.
*   **Plume:** The saturated and often visible plume from the stack can be aesthetically undesirable, even if it contains minimal pollutants.
*   **Potential for Fouling and Plugging:** In packed beds or on trays, if the scrubbing liquid contains suspended solids or dissolved salts, it can lead to fouling and plugging.
*   **Higher Operating Costs:** Compared to some dry collectors, due to pumping energy for the liquid and wastewater treatment.
*   **Limited Efficiency for Very Fine Particles in Some Designs:** Spray towers and simpler packed beds may not be as effective for sub-micron particles compared to Venturi scrubbers or fabric filters.

## 6. Mist Eliminators

Mist eliminators are essential components of wet collectors.

### Learning Outcome 6: Describe the function and types of mist eliminators used in wet collectors.

**Function:** Mist eliminators are used to remove entrained liquid droplets from the outgoing gas stream, preventing them from being discharged into the atmosphere. These droplets can carry collected pollutants.

#### 6.1. Types of Mist Eliminators:

*   **Chevron-Type Mist Eliminators:**
    *   **Description:** Series of corrugated blades arranged to create a tortuous path for the gas. Droplets impact the blades, coalesce, and drain.
    *   **Mechanism:** Inertial impaction.
    *   **Applications:** Widely used in various scrubbers.

*   **Knitted Mesh Mist Eliminators:**
    *   **Description:** Made of knitted wire or plastic fibers, forming a fibrous bed.
    *   **Mechanism:** Impaction, interception, and diffusion as gas passes through the mesh.
    *   **Applications:** Effective for fine droplets.

*   **Vane-Type Mist Eliminators:**
    *   **Description:** Similar to chevron but with simpler vane configurations.
    *   **Mechanism:** Inertial impaction.

*   **Cyclonic Mist Eliminators (Internal):**
    *   **Description:** Built into the scrubber design to impart a centrifugal force to the gas, separating larger droplets.

## Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
What are the primary mechanisms by which wet collectors remove particulate matter from a gas stream?

**Answer:**
The primary mechanisms are:
*   Impaction
*   Interception
*   Diffusion (Brownian Motion)
*   Inertial Impaction

**Question 2:**
Compare and contrast a Venturi scrubber with a packed bed scrubber in terms of their operating principles and suitability for different applications.

**Answer:**
*   **Venturi Scrubber:**
    *   **Operating Principle:** Gas is accelerated through a throat, and liquid is injected. High velocity creates fine droplets and turbulence for efficient particle capture via inertial impaction.
    *   **Suitability:** Excellent for fine and sub-micron particles, high dust loads, and situations requiring high efficiency. Can handle abrasive dusts.

*   **Packed Bed Scrubber:**
    *   **Operating Principle:** Gas flows through a bed of packing material, coming into contact with a liquid film. Removal mechanisms include impaction, interception, diffusion, and absorption.
    *   **Suitability:** Effective for both fine particles and gaseous pollutants, particularly soluble gases. Less effective than Venturi for very fine particles at similar pressure drops. Prone to fouling with abrasive or sticky materials.

**Question 3:**
Explain the significance of the Liquid-to-Gas (L/G) ratio and pressure drop in the design and operation of wet collectors.

**Answer:**
*   **L/G Ratio:** A higher L/G ratio generally increases removal efficiency by providing more liquid for contact. However, it also increases operating costs (pumping, water treatment) and can lead to higher pressure drop. An optimal L/G is sought to balance efficiency and cost.
*   **Pressure Drop:** Higher pressure drop often correlates with more vigorous gas-liquid contact and higher collection efficiency, especially for fine particles. However, it translates to increased fan power consumption and operating costs. Designs aim to achieve the desired efficiency at an acceptable pressure drop.

**Question 4:**
List three advantages and three disadvantages of using wet collectors for air pollution control.

**Answer:**
*   **Advantages:**
    1.  Simultaneous removal of particulates and gases.
    2.  Ability to handle high temperatures and moisture.
    3.  Reduction of explosion/fire hazards with combustible dusts.

*   **Disadvantages:**
    1.  Generation of wastewater requiring treatment.
    2.  Potential for corrosion.
    3.  Formation of visible plumes from saturated gas.

**Question 5:**
What is the purpose of a mist eliminator, and name two common types.

**Answer:**
*   **Purpose:** To remove entrained liquid droplets from the outgoing gas stream, preventing their discharge into the atmosphere.
*   **Common Types:**
    1.  Chevron-type mist eliminators
    2.  Knitted mesh mist eliminators

## Important Points to Remember

*   Wet collectors are versatile devices capable of removing both **particulate matter** and **gaseous pollutants**.
*   The choice of wet collector type depends heavily on the **pollutant characteristics** (size, concentration, solubility) and the desired **collection efficiency**.
*   **Venturi scrubbers** are particularly effective for **fine and sub-micron particles** due to their high-velocity operation.
*   **Packed bed and tray scrubbers** are good for both particulate and **gaseous pollutant removal**, especially soluble gases like SO2.
*   Key design parameters like **L/G ratio** and **pressure drop** must be carefully optimized to balance efficiency and operating costs.
*   **Wastewater management** is a critical consideration for wet collectors.
*   **Mist eliminators** are essential components to prevent liquid droplet carryover.
*   Consider **corrosion resistance** when selecting materials of construction.
