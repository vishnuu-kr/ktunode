### Topic: Absorption

### 1. Introduction to Absorption

Absorption is a gas cleaning process where pollutants in a gas stream are transferred from the gas phase into a liquid phase. This liquid is typically water or an aqueous solution containing specific chemical absorbents. The process relies on the differential solubility or reactivity of the pollutant in the liquid.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Mass Transfer:** The fundamental principle behind absorption, involving the movement of a solute (pollutant) from a gas phase to a liquid phase across an interface.
*   **Solubility:** The ability of a gas to dissolve in a liquid. This is influenced by temperature, pressure, and the chemical nature of both the gas and the liquid.
*   **Reactivity:** Some pollutants react chemically with the absorbent liquid, forming new compounds that are more soluble or less volatile.
*   **Absorption Efficiency:** The percentage of the pollutant removed from the gas stream.
*   **Absorbent:** The liquid used to remove the pollutant from the gas stream.

**Learning Outcome 1: Understand the fundamental principles of absorption as a pollution control technique.**

*   **Mechanism:** Absorption involves the diffusion of pollutant molecules from the bulk gas phase to the gas-liquid interface, through the gas film, across the interface, and then through the liquid film to the bulk liquid phase. The slowest step in this process usually determines the overall rate.
*   **Driving Force:** The difference in the partial pressure of the pollutant in the gas phase and its equilibrium partial pressure in the liquid phase.
*   **Mass Transfer Coefficients:**
    *   **Gas-side mass transfer coefficient ($k_g$):** Relates the rate of mass transfer to the difference in concentration (or partial pressure) in the gas phase.
    *   **Liquid-side mass transfer coefficient ($k_l$):** Relates the rate of mass transfer to the difference in concentration in the liquid phase.
*   **Overall Mass Transfer Coefficient ($K_g$ or $K_l$):** Combines the individual mass transfer coefficients, accounting for resistance in both phases.

### 2. Factors Affecting Absorption Efficiency

Several factors influence how effectively absorption removes pollutants from a gas stream.

**Learning Outcome 2: Identify and explain the key factors that influence the efficiency of absorption.**

*   **Nature of the Pollutant:**
    *   **Solubility:** Highly soluble gases (e.g., NH₃, HCl, SO₂) are readily absorbed by water.
    *   **Reactivity:** Gases that react chemically with the absorbent (e.g., SO₂ with alkaline solutions) are more efficiently removed.
*   **Nature of the Absorbent:**
    *   **Solubility/Reactivity:** The chemical properties of the liquid determine its affinity for the pollutant.
    *   **Viscosity:** Higher viscosity can hinder mass transfer, reducing efficiency.
    *   **Vapor Pressure:** Absorbents with low vapor pressure are preferred to minimize their loss to the gas stream.
*   **Temperature:**
    *   **Solubility:** Generally, the solubility of gases in liquids decreases with increasing temperature (especially for non-reacting systems).
    *   **Reaction Rate:** For reactive absorption, temperature can increase the reaction rate, potentially improving removal, but may also increase absorbent vapor pressure.
*   **Pressure:**
    *   **Henry's Law:** For physical absorption, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to its partial pressure in the gas phase. Higher operating pressure generally leads to higher absorption efficiency.
*   **Contact Time/Area:**
    *   **Residence Time:** The longer the gas remains in contact with the liquid, the greater the potential for mass transfer.
    *   **Interfacial Area:** A larger interfacial area between the gas and liquid phases enhances the rate of mass transfer. This is achieved through various designs of absorption equipment.
*   **Concentration of Pollutant:** Higher pollutant concentrations can sometimes lead to saturation of the absorbent or reduced driving force if the absorbent capacity is limited.

### 3. Types of Absorption

Absorption can be classified based on the mechanism of mass transfer and the type of equipment used.

**Learning Outcome 3: Differentiate between physical and chemical absorption, and describe various absorption equipment.**

#### 3.1. Physical Absorption

*   **Definition:** The pollutant dissolves in the liquid absorbent based on its physical solubility, obeying Henry's Law. No chemical reaction occurs between the pollutant and the absorbent.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Reversible process.
    *   Efficiency is limited by the solubility of the gas at operating conditions.
    *   Favored by low temperatures and high pressures.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Absorption of ammonia (NH₃) in water.
    *   Absorption of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) in water (though it also undergoes some chemical reactions).
    *   Removal of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) using organic solvents.

#### 3.2. Chemical Absorption (Reactive Absorption)

*   **Definition:** The pollutant reacts chemically with the absorbent to form a new compound that is more soluble or less volatile, thus removing it from the gas stream.
*   **Characteristics:**
    *   Often irreversible or only partially reversible.
    *   Can achieve higher removal efficiencies than physical absorption, especially for poorly soluble gases.
    *   The rate of absorption is influenced by both physical mass transfer and chemical reaction kinetics.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Absorption of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) in alkaline solutions (e.g., sodium hydroxide, calcium carbonate slurry) to form sulfites/sulfates.
    *   Absorption of hydrogen chloride (HCl) in water.
    *   Absorption of acidic gases (like NOₓ) in basic solutions.

#### 3.3. Absorption Equipment (Gas Scrubbers)

Absorption is carried out in various types of equipment designed to maximize gas-liquid contact. These are commonly referred to as **scrubbers**.

**Learning Outcome 4: Explain the design and operation of common absorption equipment used for air pollution control.**

*   **Packed Towers:**
    *   **Description:** Vertical cylindrical columns filled with packing material (e.g., rings, saddles, grids). The packing provides a large surface area for gas-liquid contact.
    *   **Operation:** Liquid flows downwards over the packing, while the gas flows upwards (counter-current) or downwards (co-current).
    *   **Advantages:** High efficiency, low pressure drop for a given contact area, can handle corrosive gases.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Susceptible to fouling, limited turndown ratio, packing can be expensive.
    *   **Examples:** Removal of SO₂, HCl, NH₃.

*   **Tray Towers (Plate Towers):**
    *   **Description:** Vertical columns with a series of trays (e.g., sieve trays, bubble cap trays, valve trays) at different levels. Each tray provides a stage for gas-liquid contact.
    *   **Operation:** Gas flows upwards through perforations or openings in the trays, bubbling through the liquid layer on each tray. Liquid flows downwards from tray to tray.
    *   **Advantages:** Good for handling variable gas flows, predictable performance, less prone to fouling than packed towers.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Higher pressure drop than packed towers, lower gas-liquid contact efficiency per stage compared to well-designed packing.
    *   **Examples:** Used for a wide range of gas cleaning applications.

*   **Spray Towers (Spray Scrubbers):**
    *   **Description:** Vertical cylindrical chambers where liquid is atomized into fine droplets by spray nozzles. The gas flows through the chamber, and pollutants are absorbed by the droplets.
    *   **Operation:** Typically co-current flow (gas and liquid flow in the same direction) or counter-current.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple design, low pressure drop, can handle high gas flow rates and particulate matter.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Lower absorption efficiency compared to packed or tray towers due to shorter contact time and smaller effective interfacial area per unit volume.
    *   **Examples:** Removal of coarse particulate matter along with soluble gases.

*   **Venturi Scrubbers:**
    *   **Description:** A venturi tube with a converging section, a throat, and a diverging section. Liquid is injected at or near the throat. The high gas velocity in the throat causes intense atomization of the liquid and promotes excellent gas-liquid contact.
    *   **Operation:** Primarily used for simultaneous removal of particulate matter and soluble gases. The high turbulence and shear forces enhance mass transfer.
    *   **Advantages:** Highly effective for both particulate and gas removal, simple and robust design.
    *   **Disadvantages:** High pressure drop, requires significant energy input.
    *   **Examples:** Used in power plants, steel mills, chemical plants.

### 4. Design Considerations and Mass Transfer Calculations

Designing an effective absorption system requires understanding mass transfer principles and applying them to equipment sizing.

**Learning Outcome 5: Understand the basic concepts related to mass transfer calculations in absorption.**

*   **Driving Force:** The difference between the actual partial pressure of the solute in the gas phase and the equilibrium partial pressure of the solute in the gas phase that would be in equilibrium with the liquid composition.
*   **Equilibrium Relationship (Henry's Law):**
    *   For physical absorption: $p_A = H_A x_A$
        *   $p_A$: Partial pressure of solute A in the gas phase.
        *   $x_A$: Mole fraction of solute A in the liquid phase.
        *   $H_A$: Henry's Law constant for solute A in the absorbent.
    *   For chemical absorption, the equilibrium is more complex and depends on the specific chemical reaction.
*   **Mass Transfer Rate:**
    *   $N_A = K_G a (p_A - p_A^*) = K_L a (x_A^* - x_A)$
        *   $N_A$: Molar rate of mass transfer of solute A.
        *   $K_G$: Overall gas-side mass transfer coefficient.
        *   $K_L$: Overall liquid-side mass transfer coefficient.
        *   $a$: Interfacial area per unit volume of equipment.
        *   $p_A^*$: Partial pressure of A in equilibrium with the liquid.
        *   $x_A^*$: Mole fraction of A in the liquid in equilibrium with the gas.
*   **Number of Transfer Units (NTU):** A dimensionless parameter representing the difficulty of the absorption process. It is defined as:
    *   $N_t = \int_{y_1}^{y_2} \frac{dy}{y - y^*}$ (for gas-phase controlled)
    *   $N_t = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} \frac{dx}{x^* - x}$ (for liquid-phase controlled)
    *   $y$: Mole ratio of solute in gas.
    *   $x$: Mole ratio of solute in liquid.
    *   $y^*, x^*$: Equilibrium mole ratios.
*   **Height of Transfer Unit (HTU):** The physical height of equipment required to achieve one transfer unit.
    *   $H_t = H / N_t$
        *   $H$: Height of the absorption equipment.
*   **Equipment Sizing:** The required height of the absorber can be estimated using NTU and HTU values, which are typically determined experimentally or from empirical correlations.

### 5. Applications of Absorption in Air Pollution Control

Absorption is a widely used technique for controlling various air pollutants.

**Learning Outcome 6: Discuss specific applications of absorption in controlling various air pollutants.**

*   **Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂) Control (Flue Gas Desulfurization - FGD):**
    *   **Pollutant:** SO₂ from combustion of sulfur-containing fuels.
    *   **Absorbent:** Lime (CaCO₃) or Limestone (CaCO₃) slurry, or Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) solution.
    *   **Equipment:** Packed towers, spray towers, or venturi scrubbers.
    *   **Process:** SO₂ reacts with the alkaline absorbent to form calcium sulfite (CaSO₃) or sodium sulfite (Na₂SO₃), which can be further oxidized to sulfates.
*   **Acid Gas Removal:**
    *   **Pollutants:** HCl, HF, SO₂, NOₓ.
    *   **Absorbent:** Water, alkaline solutions (NaOH, Na₂CO₃).
    *   **Equipment:** Packed towers, tray towers.
    *   **Process:** Acidic gases dissolve in and react with the alkaline absorbent.
*   **Ammonia (NH₃) Recovery and Control:**
    *   **Pollutant:** NH₃ from industrial processes (e.g., fertilizer production).
    *   **Absorbent:** Water.
    *   **Equipment:** Packed towers.
    *   **Process:** NH₃ is highly soluble in water, forming ammonium hydroxide.
*   **Volatile Organic Compound (VOC) Control:**
    *   **Pollutants:** VOCs from various industries (e.g., printing, chemical manufacturing).
    *   **Absorbent:** Organic solvents (e.g., mineral oil, specialized solvents) or water (for highly soluble VOCs).
    *   **Equipment:** Packed towers.
    *   **Process:** VOCs dissolve in the solvent (physical absorption). The spent solvent may require regeneration or disposal.
*   **Odor Control:**
    *   **Pollutants:** Malodorous gases (e.g., H₂S, mercaptans).
    *   **Absorbent:** Oxidizing solutions (e.g., hypochlorite) or alkaline solutions.
    *   **Equipment:** Packed towers.
    *   **Process:** Chemical reaction to convert odoriferous compounds into less offensive ones.

### 6. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
What is the primary difference between physical absorption and chemical absorption? Give an example of each in air pollution control.

**Answer:**
Physical absorption involves the dissolution of a pollutant into a liquid based on its solubility, without a chemical reaction. An example is the removal of ammonia (NH₃) from gas streams using water. Chemical absorption involves a chemical reaction between the pollutant and the absorbent, leading to a more efficient removal. An example is the removal of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) from flue gases using a sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution.

**Question 2:**
List three factors that significantly influence the efficiency of an absorption process and briefly explain their impact.

**Answer:**
1.  **Temperature:** Generally, higher temperatures decrease the solubility of gases in liquids (especially for physical absorption), thus reducing efficiency.
2.  **Pressure:** Higher operating pressure increases the partial pressure of the pollutant, leading to higher solubility and thus improved absorption efficiency, according to Henry's Law.
3.  **Interfacial Area:** A larger interfacial area between the gas and liquid phases increases the rate of mass transfer, leading to higher absorption efficiency. This is achieved through specialized equipment design.

**Question 3:**
Describe the operating principle of a Venturi scrubber. What are its main advantages and disadvantages for gas absorption?

**Answer:**
A Venturi scrubber operates by injecting liquid into a high-velocity gas stream within a Venturi tube. The high gas velocity in the throat of the Venturi atomizes the liquid into fine droplets, creating a large interfacial area for mass transfer.
*   **Advantages:** Highly effective for simultaneous removal of fine particulate matter and soluble gases, simple and robust design.
*   **Disadvantages:** High pressure drop, requiring significant energy input, and generally lower absorption efficiency for gases compared to packed or tray towers due to shorter contact time.

**Question 4:**
For the absorption of a gas (A) in a liquid, Henry's Law states $p_A = H_A x_A$. If the Henry's Law constant ($H_A$) for a specific pollutant in water is very low, what does this imply about the pollutant's solubility in water and the potential efficiency of absorption?

**Answer:**
A very low Henry's Law constant ($H_A$) indicates that the pollutant (A) has a high solubility in water. This means that even at low partial pressures in the gas phase, a significant amount of the pollutant will dissolve in the water. Consequently, absorption using water as the absorbent is likely to be a highly efficient method for removing this pollutant.

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Absorption is a mass transfer process** where a pollutant moves from a gas phase to a liquid phase.
*   **Solubility and reactivity** are key properties determining the choice of absorbent.
*   **Temperature and pressure** significantly impact absorption efficiency, especially for physical absorption (Henry's Law).
*   **Equipment design** is crucial for maximizing gas-liquid contact area and residence time.
*   **Packed towers and tray towers** offer higher efficiencies for gas absorption compared to spray towers due to better gas-liquid contact.
*   **Venturi scrubbers** are excellent for combined particulate and gas removal but have higher energy costs.
*   **Chemical absorption** can achieve higher removal rates for poorly soluble or reactive gases.
*   **Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD)** is a major application of absorption for controlling SO₂ emissions.
*   Understanding **mass transfer coefficients and NTU concepts** is essential for designing and analyzing absorption systems.
