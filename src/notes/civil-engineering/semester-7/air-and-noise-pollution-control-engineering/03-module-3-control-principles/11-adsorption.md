# AIR AND NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL ENGINEERING

### Topic: Adsorption

---

### 1. Introduction to Adsorption

**Definition:**
Adsorption is a surface phenomenon where molecules of a substance (adsorbate) accumulate on the surface of another substance (adsorbent). In the context of air pollution control, adsorption is used to remove gaseous pollutants from air streams by binding them to the surface of a solid material.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Adsorbent:** The solid material with a large surface area that attracts and holds the adsorbate molecules. Examples include activated carbon, zeolites, silica gel, and alumina.
*   **Adsorbate:** The substance being removed from the air stream, typically a gaseous pollutant. Examples include volatile organic compounds (VOCs), sulfur dioxide (SO2), hydrogen sulfide (H2S), mercury (Hg), and odors.
*   **Surface Area:** A critical property of the adsorbent. A larger surface area generally leads to a higher adsorption capacity. This is often achieved through porous structures.
*   **Pore Structure:** The network of pores within the adsorbent material. The size and distribution of these pores influence the accessibility of the surface to adsorbate molecules.
*   **Adsorption Isotherms:** Graphical representations of the relationship between the amount of adsorbate adsorbed and its partial pressure or concentration at a constant temperature. Common isotherms include Langmuir and Freundlich.

**Types of Adsorption:**

*   **Physisorption (Physical Adsorption):**
    *   Weak van der Waals forces between adsorbate and adsorbent.
    *   Reversible and exothermic.
    *   Low activation energy.
    *   Occurs at low temperatures.
    *   Multilayer adsorption is possible.
    *   Generally not specific to the chemical nature of the adsorbate.
*   **Chemisorption (Chemical Adsorption):**
    *   Formation of chemical bonds between adsorbate and adsorbent.
    *   Stronger forces.
    *   Often irreversible or requires significant energy to reverse.
    *   Higher activation energy.
    *   Can occur at higher temperatures.
    *   Monolayer adsorption is typical.
    *   Highly specific to the chemical nature of the adsorbate.

**Importance in Air Pollution Control:**
Adsorption is a highly effective method for removing a wide range of gaseous pollutants, especially at low concentrations. It is often used for:

*   VOC control from industrial processes.
*   Odor control from municipal waste treatment plants.
*   Removal of hazardous air pollutants (HAPs).
*   Air purification in indoor environments.
*   Gas separation and purification.

### 2. Adsorption Mechanisms and Theories

**Theories of Adsorption:**

*   **Monolayer Adsorption (Langmuir Theory):**
    *   Assumes adsorption occurs as a single layer of adsorbate molecules on the adsorbent surface.
    *   Assumes homogeneous surface sites.
    *   Assumes no interaction between adsorbed molecules.
    *   Leads to the Langmuir Isotherm: $\frac{x}{m} = \frac{K L C}{1 + K C}$, where:
        *   $\frac{x}{m}$ is the amount of adsorbate adsorbed per unit mass of adsorbent.
        *   $C$ is the equilibrium concentration of adsorbate.
        *   $K$ is a constant related to the adsorption strength.
        *   $L$ is the maximum adsorption capacity (monolayer capacity).
*   **Multilayer Adsorption (BET Theory - Brunauer, Emmett, and Teller):**
    *   Extends Langmuir theory to allow for the formation of multiple layers of adsorbate molecules on the surface.
    *   Assumes adsorption on the first layer is different from subsequent layers.
    *   More applicable to physisorption at higher pressures and lower temperatures.
    *   Leads to the BET Isotherm, which is more complex and used to determine surface area and pore size distribution.

*   **Non-Ideal Adsorption (Freundlich Isotherm):**
    *   An empirical model that describes adsorption on heterogeneous surfaces.
    *   $\frac{x}{m} = K_F C^{1/n}$, where:
        *   $K_F$ and $n$ are empirical constants.
        *   $n > 1$ indicates that the adsorption capacity increases with concentration.
    *   This isotherm is often observed in practical applications, especially for physisorption.

**Mechanism of Adsorption:**

1.  **Transport of Adsorbate to the Surface:** Pollutant molecules must first reach the surface of the adsorbent. This can involve bulk diffusion, film diffusion, and pore diffusion.
2.  **Adsorption:** The adsorbate molecules attach to the active sites on the adsorbent surface via physisorption or chemisorption.
3.  **Surface Migration:** Adsorbed molecules may move along the surface to more stable locations.
4.  **Desorption (Reverse of Adsorption):** Adsorbate molecules detach from the surface. This is crucial for adsorbent regeneration.

### 4. Adsorption Equipment and Process Design

**Types of Adsorption Equipment:**

*   **Fixed-Bed Adsorbers:**
    *   The most common type.
    *   Adsorbent is packed into a vessel, and the polluted air flows through it.
    *   Can be operated in single-bed or multiple-bed configurations.
    *   **Single-Bed:** Operated until the adsorbent is saturated, then taken offline for regeneration.
    *   **Multiple-Bed:** Usually two or more beds are used in parallel. While one bed is adsorbing, another is regenerating, ensuring continuous operation.
*   **Moving-Bed Adsorbers:**
    *   Adsorbent moves continuously through the adsorber, coming into contact with the gas stream.
    *   Offers more uniform adsorbent utilization and continuous regeneration.
    *   More complex mechanically.
*   **Fluidized-Bed Adsorbers:**
    *   Adsorbent particles are suspended in the gas stream by the upward flow of air.
    *   Good contact between gas and adsorbent, but can lead to attrition (wear) of adsorbent particles.

**Process Design Considerations:**

*   **Adsorbent Selection:** Based on the pollutant to be removed, concentration, flow rate, temperature, humidity, and regeneration requirements.
*   **Bed Depth and Diameter:** Influences contact time and pressure drop.
*   **Flow Rate:** Affects contact time and adsorption efficiency.
*   **Pressure Drop:** The resistance to airflow through the adsorbent bed. Needs to be minimized to reduce fan power.
*   **Breakthrough Time:** The time it takes for the adsorbate concentration in the outlet gas to reach a predetermined allowable level. This is a critical parameter for determining regeneration cycles.
*   **Adsorbent Capacity:** The amount of adsorbate the adsorbent can hold at equilibrium or under dynamic conditions.
*   **Regeneration:** The process of removing the adsorbed pollutant from the adsorbent to restore its capacity. Common regeneration methods include:
    *   **Thermal Swing Adsorption (TSA):** Heating the adsorbent to desorb the pollutant.
    *   **Pressure Swing Adsorption (PSA):** Reducing the pressure to desorb the pollutant.
    *   **Steam Stripping:** Using steam to desorb the pollutant, often for volatile organic compounds.
    *   **Inert Gas Purge:** Using a dry, inert gas to sweep away the desorbed pollutant.

**Design Example (Simplified Fixed-Bed):**
Consider removing VOCs from an air stream using a fixed-bed adsorber filled with activated carbon. The air flows through the bed. As the bed becomes saturated with VOCs, breakthrough occurs. Once breakthrough is detected, the bed is isolated, and regeneration is initiated (e.g., by passing hot steam through it). The regenerated bed is then returned to service.

### 5. Breakthrough and Regeneration

**Breakthrough:**

*   **Definition:** The point at which the concentration of the adsorbate in the outlet gas from the adsorber begins to increase significantly, indicating that the adsorbent's capacity is exhausted.
*   **Breakthrough Curve:** A plot of outlet adsorbate concentration versus time (or volume of air treated). It typically starts at zero concentration and rises gradually, eventually reaching the influent concentration.
*   **Factors Affecting Breakthrough Time:**
    *   Adsorbent type and capacity.
    *   Adsorbate concentration in the influent.
    *   Flow rate.
    *   Bed dimensions.
    *   Temperature and humidity.
    *   Presence of competing adsorbates.

**Regeneration:**

*   **Purpose:** To restore the adsorptive capacity of the adsorbent by removing the adsorbed species.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Temperature Swing Adsorption (TSA):**
        *   **Mechanism:** Heating the adsorbent increases the vapor pressure of the adsorbed substance, causing it to desorb.
        *   **Application:** Effective for compounds that are volatile at moderate temperatures.
        *   **Example:** Using hot air or steam.
    *   **Pressure Swing Adsorption (PSA):**
        *   **Mechanism:** Reducing the pressure lowers the equilibrium concentration of the adsorbate on the adsorbent, causing desorption.
        *   **Application:** Effective for compounds that are not strongly bound to the adsorbent.
        *   **Example:** Vacuum regeneration or purging with an inert gas at reduced pressure.
    *   **Steam Regeneration:**
        *   **Mechanism:** Steam can also act as a carrier gas for desorption, especially for VOCs. It can displace the adsorbed VOCs.
        *   **Application:** Common for activated carbon and many organic pollutants. The desorbed VOCs can be condensed or treated further.
    *   **Stripping Gas Regeneration:**
        *   **Mechanism:** Using a purge gas (e.g., nitrogen) to sweep the desorbed molecules away.
        *   **Application:** Used when the desorbed species are not easily recovered.

*   **Regeneration Efficiency:** The extent to which the adsorbent's original capacity is restored. Incomplete regeneration can lead to a gradual decrease in performance over multiple cycles.
*   **Cost of Regeneration:** Energy consumption (heating, vacuum pumps) and potential loss of adsorbent material.

**Important Point:** The choice of regeneration method depends on the nature of the adsorbate, the adsorbent material, and economic considerations.

### 6. Advantages and Disadvantages of Adsorption

**Advantages:**

*   **High Removal Efficiency:** Capable of removing pollutants to very low concentrations.
*   **Versatility:** Effective for a wide range of gaseous pollutants, including VOCs, odors, and acid gases.
*   **Selectivity:** Certain adsorbents (like zeolites) can be highly selective for specific pollutants.
*   **Potential for Recovery:** Adsorbed substances can sometimes be recovered and reused (e.g., solvent recovery).
*   **Compact Design:** Adsorption units can be relatively compact, especially for smaller flows.
*   **Ambient Temperature Operation:** Many adsorption processes can operate effectively at ambient temperatures.

**Disadvantages:**

*   **Adsorbent Cost:** High-quality adsorbents can be expensive.
*   **Regeneration Requirements:** Regeneration is necessary, which adds complexity and energy costs. Incomplete regeneration can reduce performance.
*   **Adsorbent Deactivation:** Adsorbents can be fouled or deactivated by moisture, high concentrations of certain compounds, or thermal degradation.
*   **Pressure Drop:** Adsorbent beds create a pressure drop, requiring fans or blowers, which consume energy.
*   **Sensitivity to Humidity:** High humidity can compete with pollutants for adsorption sites, reducing capacity.
*   **Disposal of Spent Adsorbent:** If regeneration is not feasible or effective, spent adsorbent may require disposal as hazardous waste.

### 7. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
Define adsorption and differentiate between physisorption and chemisorption, highlighting at least two key differences.

**Answer 1:**
Adsorption is a surface phenomenon where molecules of a substance (adsorbate) accumulate on the surface of another substance (adsorbent).

*   **Physisorption:**
    *   Involves weak van der Waals forces.
    *   Reversible and exothermic.
    *   Low activation energy.
    *   Can form multilayer.
*   **Chemisorption:**
    *   Involves formation of chemical bonds.
    *   Often irreversible or requires high energy to reverse.
    *   Higher activation energy.
    *   Forms monolayer.

**Question 2:**
List three common adsorbents used in air pollution control and provide one specific application for each.

**Answer 2:**

*   **Activated Carbon:** Used for VOC removal from industrial exhaust streams.
*   **Zeolites:** Used for the removal of SO2 and NOx from flue gases.
*   **Silica Gel:** Used as a desiccant for drying air and gases.

**Question 3:**
Explain the concept of "breakthrough" in a fixed-bed adsorber. What factors influence the breakthrough time?

**Answer 3:**
Breakthrough occurs when the adsorbate concentration in the outlet gas from an adsorber starts to increase significantly, indicating that the adsorbent's capacity is saturated. The adsorbent is no longer effectively removing the pollutant. Factors influencing breakthrough time include: adsorbent type and capacity, adsorbate concentration, flow rate, bed dimensions, temperature, humidity, and presence of competing adsorbates.

**Question 4:**
Describe the principles behind Thermal Swing Adsorption (TSA) and Pressure Swing Adsorption (PSA) as regeneration methods.

**Answer 4:**

*   **TSA:** Involves heating the adsorbent to increase the vapor pressure of the adsorbed substance, causing it to desorb. This is effective for compounds that are volatile at moderate temperatures.
*   **PSA:** Involves reducing the pressure to lower the equilibrium concentration of the adsorbate on the adsorbent, leading to desorption. This is useful for less strongly bound compounds.

**Question 5 (Conceptual Exercise):**
An industrial facility is emitting a significant amount of benzene (a VOC) into the atmosphere. The concentration of benzene in the exhaust stream is 200 ppm. The facility wants to reduce benzene emissions by 95%. Would adsorption be a suitable control technology? Explain why or why not, considering the typical characteristics of adsorption.

**Answer 5:**
Yes, adsorption would likely be a suitable control technology. Benzene is a VOC, and activated carbon (a common adsorbent) is highly effective at removing VOCs, even at low concentrations (like 200 ppm). Adsorption is known for its high removal efficiency, making it capable of achieving the desired 95% reduction. The process can be implemented using fixed-bed adsorbers, and the adsorbed benzene could potentially be recovered if economically viable, or the carbon can be regenerated.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   Adsorption is a **surface phenomenon**.
*   **Surface area** and **pore structure** are critical properties of adsorbents.
*   **Activated carbon** is the most versatile and widely used adsorbent for organic pollutants.
*   **Breakthrough** is a key operational parameter defining when regeneration is needed.
*   **Regeneration** is essential to restore adsorbent capacity and can be achieved through **TSA** or **PSA**.
*   The choice of adsorbent and regeneration method depends on the **specific pollutant** and process conditions.
*   Adsorption offers **high removal efficiency** but requires **careful design and operation** to manage costs and performance.
*   **Humidity** can significantly impact adsorption capacity.
