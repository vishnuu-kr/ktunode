# AIR AND NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL ENGINEERING
## Module 2: Air Sampling and Pollution Measurement Methods
### Topic: Principles and Instruments

---

This module focuses on understanding the fundamental principles and practical instruments used for sampling and measuring air pollution. Accurate measurement is crucial for assessing pollution levels, identifying sources, evaluating control strategies, and ensuring compliance with environmental regulations.

---

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   **Understand the fundamental principles of air sampling and measurement.**
*   **Differentiate between various air sampling techniques.**
*   **Identify and describe the working principles of common air pollution measurement instruments.**
*   **Select appropriate sampling and measurement methods based on the pollutant, source, and objective.**
*   **Understand the concepts of accuracy, precision, sensitivity, and selectivity in measurement.**

### 1. Fundamentals of Air Sampling and Measurement

#### 1.1 Why Sample and Measure Air Pollution?

*   **Assessment of Air Quality:** To determine the concentration of pollutants in ambient air and compare it with regulatory standards.
*   **Source Identification:** To pinpoint the origin of pollution.
*   **Exposure Assessment:** To evaluate the level of pollutant exposure to humans and ecosystems.
*   **Evaluation of Control Technologies:** To assess the effectiveness of pollution control devices.
*   **Compliance Monitoring:** To ensure adherence to legal limits and regulations.
*   **Research and Development:** To study the behavior and impact of pollutants.

#### 1.2 Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Pollutant:** A substance that, when introduced into the environment, causes adverse effects.
*   **Concentration:** The amount of a substance present in a given volume or mass of air. Common units include:
    *   **ppm (parts per million):** Moles of pollutant per million moles of air.
    *   **ppb (parts per billion):** Moles of pollutant per billion moles of air.
    *   **µg/m³ (micrograms per cubic meter):** Mass of pollutant per unit volume of air.
    *   **mg/m³ (milligrams per cubic meter):** Mass of pollutant per unit volume of air.
*   **Sampling:** The process of collecting a representative portion of the air to be analyzed.
*   **Measurement:** The process of determining the quantity or concentration of a pollutant in the collected sample or directly in the air.
*   **Ambient Air:** The air surrounding us, as opposed to air within a building or a stack.
*   **Source Emission:** Pollutants released directly from a specific source (e.g., factory stack, vehicle exhaust).
*   **Detector/Sensor:** The component of an instrument that responds to the presence of a pollutant.
*   **Calibration:** The process of adjusting an instrument to ensure its readings are accurate by comparing them against a known standard.
*   **Accuracy:** The closeness of a measurement to the true value.
*   **Precision:** The degree of reproducibility of a measurement (how close successive measurements are to each other).
*   **Sensitivity:** The smallest concentration of a pollutant that an instrument can reliably detect.
*   **Selectivity:** The ability of an instrument to measure a specific pollutant without interference from other substances.
*   **Detection Limit (DL):** The lowest concentration of an analyte that can be reliably distinguished from a blank sample.
*   **Quantification Limit (QL):** The lowest concentration of an analyte that can be reliably measured with acceptable accuracy and precision.

### 2. Air Sampling Techniques

The choice of sampling technique depends on the pollutant, its concentration, the matrix (e.g., ambient air, stack gas), and the analytical method to be used.

#### 2.1 Grab Sampling

*   **Principle:** Collects a single sample of air at a specific time and location.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Direct Injection into Instrument:** The sampled air is directly fed into a continuous monitoring instrument.
    *   **Collection in a Container:** Air is collected in a sealed container (bag, cylinder, syringe) for later analysis.
*   **Instruments:**
    *   **Gas Bags:** Tedlar bags, aluminized bags.
    *   **Syringes:** Glass or stainless steel syringes.
    *   **Canisters:** Stainless steel canisters (e.g., Summa canisters for VOCs).
*   **Advantages:** Simple, quick, cost-effective for high concentrations.
*   **Disadvantages:** Not representative of long-term average concentrations, may miss short-term fluctuations.
*   **Example:** Collecting a sample of car exhaust for immediate analysis using a portable gas analyzer.

#### 2.2 Integrated Sampling (Time-Averaged Sampling)

*   **Principle:** Collects air over an extended period (e.g., hours, days) to obtain an average concentration. This is crucial for assessing exposure to pollutants whose effects are cumulative or for comparing with ambient air quality standards that are often time-averaged.
*   **Methods:**
    *   **Volume Sampling:** A known volume of air is drawn through a collection medium at a controlled rate.
    *   **Mass Sampling:** Measures the mass of pollutant collected on a medium.
*   **Instruments/Media:**
    *   **Sorbent Tubes:** Tubes packed with materials that adsorb specific gaseous pollutants. Air is drawn through them using a **sampling pump**.
        *   *Example:* Activated charcoal tubes for VOCs, Tenax tubes for specific organic compounds.
    *   **Filters:** Used to collect particulate matter. Air is drawn through a filter paper.
        *   *Example:* Glass fiber filters (GFF), PTFE filters, polycarbonate filters.
    *   **Impinger/Bubbler Systems:** Air is bubbled through a liquid absorbent medium.
        *   *Example:* Absorbing SO₂ in a dilute solution of sodium hydroxide.
    *   **High-Volume Air Samplers (Hi-Vol Samplers):** Draw large volumes of ambient air through a filter medium at a high flow rate, typically for 24 hours, to collect particulate matter.
    *   **Cascade Impactors:** Separate particles into different size ranges by inertia. Air is drawn through a series of nozzles, and particles are deposited on collection plates based on their aerodynamic diameter.
*   **Advantages:** Provides time-averaged concentrations, representative of chronic exposure, good for low concentrations.
*   **Disadvantages:** Requires longer sampling times, analysis can be more complex, requires specialized equipment.
*   **Example:** Collecting ambient PM2.5 for 24 hours using a Hi-Vol sampler with a PM2.5 size-selective inlet.

#### 2.3 Continuous Monitoring

*   **Principle:** Measures pollutant concentrations in real-time as the air passes through the instrument.
*   **Methods:** Direct measurement of pollutant concentration in the air stream.
*   **Instruments:**
    *   **Chemiluminescence Analyzers:** Used for NOx, O₃. Based on light emitted from chemical reactions.
    *   **Nondispersive Infrared (NDIR) Analyzers:** Used for CO, CO₂, hydrocarbons. Based on the absorption of IR radiation at specific wavelengths.
    *   **Flame Ionization Detectors (FID):** Used for hydrocarbons. Based on the ionization of organic molecules in a flame.
    *   **Electrochemical Sensors:** Used for O₂, CO, SO₂, NO₂, H₂S. Based on redox reactions on electrodes.
    *   **Photoionization Detectors (PID):** Used for VOCs. Based on ionization by UV light.
*   **Advantages:** Real-time data, continuous tracking of concentration, useful for identifying transient events.
*   **Disadvantages:** High cost, requires regular maintenance and calibration, may not be suitable for all pollutants or low concentrations.
*   **Example:** A network of continuous monitors measuring O₃, SO₂, NO₂, and CO in an urban area.

### 3. Principles and Instruments for Specific Pollutants

#### 3.1 Particulate Matter (PM)

*   **Principle:** Collection of solid or liquid particles suspended in the air.
*   **Instruments:**
    *   **High-Volume Air Samplers (Hi-Vol):**
        *   **Working Principle:** A motor-driven fan draws a large volume of ambient air through a pre-weighed filter. Particles are deposited on the filter. The filter is weighed again after sampling to determine the mass of collected PM. Flow rate is measured to calculate the volume of air sampled.
        *   **Considerations:** Often use PM10 or PM2.5 size-selective inlets to focus on respirable particles.
    *   **Low-Volume Air Samplers (Lo-Vol):** Similar to Hi-Vols but sample smaller volumes, often for shorter durations or for specific receptor monitoring.
    *   **Cascade Impactors:**
        *   **Working Principle:** Air is accelerated through a series of progressively smaller jets. Particles are impacted onto collection stages with decreasing cut-off diameters due to their inertia. Stages are designed to collect particles in specific size ranges.
    *   **Gravimetric Analysis:** The process of weighing filters to determine particulate mass. Requires controlled humidity and temperature for accurate results.
    *   **Optical/Beta Attenuation Monitors:** Real-time monitors that measure PM by light scattering or by the attenuation of beta radiation as particles pass through a filter tape.
        *   **Beta Attenuation:** A radioactive source emits beta particles. As particles accumulate on a filter tape, they absorb some of these beta particles, reducing the amount reaching a detector. This reduction is proportional to the mass of particles.

#### 3.2 Gaseous Pollutants

*   **3.2.1 Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)**
    *   **Sampling Methods:**
        *   **Impinger Method (West-Gaeke Method):** Air is bubbled through a solution of sodium tetrachloromercurate (II). SO₂ forms a stable complex. This complex is then reacted with formaldehyde and sodium parosaniline to form a colored compound that is measured spectrophotometrically.
        *   **Sorbent Tubes:** SO₂ can be adsorbed onto specific sorbent materials.
    *   **Continuous Monitoring:**
        *   **Nondispersive Ultraviolet (NDU) Analyzers:** SO₂ absorbs UV light at a specific wavelength. The amount of UV light absorbed is proportional to the SO₂ concentration.
        *   **Flame Photometric Detectors (FPD):** SO₂ reacts in a hydrogen-rich flame to produce sulfur-containing radicals, which emit light at a specific wavelength when they relax back to their ground state.

*   **3.2.2 Nitrogen Oxides (NOx)**
    *   **Sampling Methods:**
        *   **Impinger Method (Saltzman Method):** NO is oxidized to NO₂. Both NO and NO₂ are then absorbed in a solution containing sulfanilamide and N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride, forming a pink colored complex measured spectrophotometrically.
        *   **Sorbent Tubes:** Specific sorbent tubes can be used.
    *   **Continuous Monitoring:**
        *   **Chemiluminescence Analyzers:** The most common method. NO reacts with ozone (O₃) to produce electronically excited NO₂. As NO₂ returns to its ground state, it emits photons (light). The intensity of this light is proportional to the NO concentration. To measure NO₂, the sample is passed through a converter that reduces NO₂ to NO before the chemiluminescent reaction.

*   **3.2.3 Carbon Monoxide (CO)**
    *   **Sampling Methods:**
        *   **Gas Bags/Canisters:** For grab sampling.
    *   **Continuous Monitoring:**
        *   **Non-Dispersive Infrared (NDIR) Analyzers:** CO absorbs infrared radiation at a specific wavelength. The amount of IR radiation absorbed by the sample is measured and compared to a reference, giving the CO concentration.

*   **3.2.4 Ozone (O₃)**
    *   **Sampling Methods:**
        *   **Impinger Method:** Ozone can be sampled using specific chemical solutions.
    *   **Continuous Monitoring:**
        *   **UV Photometric Analyzers:** Ozone absorbs UV light at a specific wavelength (254 nm). The absorption is proportional to the ozone concentration.
        *   **Chemiluminescence Analyzers:** Ozone reacts with ethylene to produce light.

*   **3.2.5 Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)**
    *   **Sampling Methods:**
        *   **Sorbent Tubes:** Activated charcoal, Tenax GC, or other sorbent materials are used to trap VOCs. The trapped VOCs are then desorbed thermally or solvent-extracted for analysis by Gas Chromatography (GC).
        *   **Canisters (Summa Canisters):** Evacuated stainless steel canisters treated with an inert coating are used for sampling ambient air. VOCs are then desorbed and analyzed by GC.
        *   **Midget Impingers:** Using organic solvents as absorbents.
    *   **Continuous Monitoring:**
        *   **Photoionization Detectors (PID):** Use UV light to ionize VOC molecules. The resulting ions create an electrical current that is proportional to the concentration. PIDs are generally sensitive to a wide range of VOCs.
        *   **Flame Ionization Detectors (FID):** Used in portable analyzers and GC systems, good for hydrocarbons.
        *   **Gas Chromatography (GC):** A separation technique followed by a detector (e.g., FID, MS) for identifying and quantifying individual VOCs.

### 4. Important Considerations in Air Sampling and Measurement

#### 4.1 Representativeness

*   **Site Selection:** The sampling location must be representative of the area of interest (e.g., near pollution sources, in populated areas).
*   **Sampling Duration:** The sampling time should be long enough to capture the average concentration relevant to the study objective.
*   **Sampling Frequency:** How often samples are collected.

#### 4.2 Pollutant Characteristics

*   **Phase:** Is it a gas, vapor, or particulate?
*   **Concentration Range:** High or low concentration will dictate the sensitivity required from the instrument.
*   **Reactivity:** Some pollutants are reactive and may degrade or change during sampling or storage.
*   **Interferences:** Other substances in the air that might affect the measurement of the target pollutant.

#### 4.3 Analytical Method Requirements

*   The chosen sampling method must be compatible with the subsequent analytical technique (e.g., GC, spectrophotometry, Atomic Absorption Spectrometry).

#### 4.4 Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC)

*   **Calibration:** Regular calibration of instruments using certified calibration gases or standards is essential.
*   **Blanks:** Field blanks and laboratory blanks are used to check for contamination.
*   **Replicates:** Duplicate samples are collected to assess precision.
*   **Spikes:** Known amounts of pollutant are added to samples to check for recovery.
*   **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs):** Following documented procedures ensures consistency.

### 6. Answers to Practice Questions

**Answer 1:**
b) To provide time-averaged concentrations representative of longer exposure periods.
*Explanation: Integrated sampling collects air over time, averaging out fluctuations and providing a representative value for longer exposure periods.*

**Answer 2:**
c) Grab sampling
*Explanation: Tedlar bags are used to collect a single, instantaneous sample of air for later analysis.*

**Answer 3:**
c) Absorption of infrared radiation
*Explanation: NDIR analyzers work by measuring how much infrared light of a specific wavelength is absorbed by the target gas, which in this case is CO.*

**Answer 4:**
c) UV Photometric Analyzer or Chemiluminescence Analyzer
*Explanation: These are continuous monitoring instruments that provide real-time data, suitable for tracking diurnal variations.*

**Answer 5:**
b) To ensure its readings accurately reflect the true concentration of the pollutant.
*Explanation: Calibration matches the instrument's output to known standards, ensuring accuracy.*

**Answer 6 (Application):**
To sample and measure ambient PM2.5 concentrations over a 24-hour period:
1.  **Instrument:** A **High-Volume Air Sampler (Hi-Vol)** equipped with a **PM2.5 size-selective inlet**.
2.  **Collection Medium:** A pre-weighed **filter** (e.g., a glass fiber filter or quartz fiber filter).
3.  **Sampling Procedure:**
    *   Weigh the filter accurately in a controlled environment (temperature and humidity).
    *   Install the filter into the Hi-Vol sampler with the PM2.5 inlet.
    *   Connect a **flow meter** to measure the initial and final flow rates.
    *   Start the sampler and run it for **24 hours**, ensuring a constant flow rate (or record flow rate variations).
    *   After 24 hours, turn off the sampler and remove the exposed filter.
    *   Weigh the exposed filter accurately in the same controlled environment.
4.  **Measurement Principle:** The measurement relies on **gravimetric analysis**. The difference in mass between the exposed filter and the pre-weighed filter represents the mass of PM2.5 collected. The volume of air sampled is calculated from the measured flow rate and the sampling time. The PM2.5 concentration is then determined as mass of PM2.5 per unit volume of air (e.g., µg/m³).

### 7. Important Points to Remember

*   **Representativeness is key:** Ensure your sampling location and duration accurately reflect the conditions you are trying to measure.
*   **Know your pollutant:** Its physical and chemical properties will dictate the most appropriate sampling and measurement method.
*   **Accuracy and Precision matter:** Always calibrate instruments and follow QA/QC procedures.
*   **Time-averaging vs. Real-time:** Understand when you need instantaneous data versus an average over time.
*   **Interferences can be a problem:** Be aware of other substances that might affect your measurement and choose selective methods or implement corrective measures.
*   **Safety first:** Always follow safety protocols when operating equipment and handling potentially hazardous samples or chemicals.
