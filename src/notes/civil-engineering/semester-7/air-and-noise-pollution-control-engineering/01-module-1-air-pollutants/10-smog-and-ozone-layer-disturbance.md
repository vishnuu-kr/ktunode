# AIR AND NOISE POLLUTION CONTROL ENGINEERING

## Module 1: Air Pollutants

### Topic: Smog and Ozone Layer Disturbance

---

### **Introduction**

This module delves into the critical issue of air pollutants, focusing specifically on **smog** and **ozone layer disturbance**. Understanding these phenomena is fundamental to the field of Air and Noise Pollution Control Engineering, as they represent significant threats to human health, ecosystems, and the overall global environment. We will explore the formation, types, impacts, and control measures associated with these two distinct but interconnected air pollution issues.

---

### **1. Smog**

Smog is a type of air pollution that reduces visibility. The term "smog" was coined in the early 20th century from the words "smoke" and "fog." It is primarily a mixture of smoke, fog, and other pollutants, often originating from industrial emissions and vehicle exhaust.

#### **1.1 Types of Smog**

There are two main types of smog:

*   **London Smog (Sulfurous Smog or Gray-Brown Smog):**
    *   **Formation:** Typically occurs in cold, damp climates. It is a result of burning fossil fuels (especially coal) that release sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$) and particulate matter.
        *   $\text{SO}_2$ reacts with water vapor in the atmosphere to form sulfuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$).
        *   Particulate matter acts as condensation nuclei for fog droplets, trapping pollutants.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Greyish-brown or black in color.
        *   Characterized by a strong, unpleasant odor.
        *   High concentration of sulfur dioxide and particulate matter.
        *   Reduced visibility due to fog and suspended particles.
    *   **Historical Context:** Famously associated with the "Great Smog of London" in 1952, which caused thousands of deaths.
    *   **Key Pollutants:** Sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), particulate matter (soot, ash).

*   **Los Angeles Smog (Photochemical Smog or Brown-Air Smog):**
    *   **Formation:** Occurs in warm, sunny climates with high levels of vehicular emissions. It is a secondary pollutant, meaning it forms from reactions in the atmosphere.
        *   Primary pollutants from vehicle exhaust (nitrogen oxides - $\text{NO}_\text{x}$, volatile organic compounds - VOCs) react in the presence of sunlight.
        *   Sunlight provides the energy for photochemical reactions.
        *   Key reactions involve the formation of ozone ($\text{O}_3$), peroxyacetyl nitrates (PANs), and other oxidants.
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Brownish or yellowish haze.
        *   Irritating to eyes and lungs.
        *   Reduced visibility.
        *   Presence of ozone, PANs, and other reactive oxygen species.
    *   **Key Pollutants:** Nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_\text{x}$), volatile organic compounds (VOCs), ozone ($\text{O}_3$), PANs.

#### **1.2 Formation Mechanism of Photochemical Smog (Detailed)**

1.  **Emission of Precursors:**
    *   **Nitrogen Oxides ($\text{NO}_\text{x}$):** Primarily from high-temperature combustion in internal combustion engines (vehicles) and industrial processes. $\text{NO}_\text{x}$ includes nitric oxide (NO) and nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$).
    *   **Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs):** Released from vehicle exhaust, industrial solvents, paints, gasoline evaporation, and vegetation.

2.  **Primary Reactions in Atmosphere:**
    *   **Formation of NO:** At high temperatures, nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) and oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) in the air react to form nitric oxide (NO).
        $\text{N}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{NO}$
    *   **Oxidation of NO to $\text{NO}_2$:** NO is rapidly oxidized by oxygen in the air to nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$).
        $2\text{NO} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{NO}_2$

3.  **Photolysis of $\text{NO}_2$ (Key Step):**
    *   In the presence of sunlight, $\text{NO}_2$ absorbs solar radiation and breaks down into nitric oxide (NO) and a highly reactive oxygen atom (O).
        $\text{NO}_2 + \text{UV light} \rightarrow \text{NO} + \text{O}$

4.  **Ozone Formation:**
    *   The highly reactive oxygen atom (O) then reacts with molecular oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) to form ozone ($\text{O}_3$).
        $\text{O} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{O}_3$
    *   This ozone is a primary component of photochemical smog and is responsible for many of its harmful effects.

5.  **Chain Reactions and Other Pollutants:**
    *   Ozone, being a strong oxidant, reacts with NO, converting it back to $\text{NO}_2$, which can then be photolyzed again, creating a catalytic cycle that perpetuates ozone formation.
        $\text{O}_3 + \text{NO} \rightarrow \text{NO}_2 + \text{O}_2$
    *   VOCs play a crucial role by reacting with OH radicals (formed from ozone photolysis) to produce peroxy radicals. These radicals then react with NO, leading to the formation of $\text{NO}_2$ more efficiently and contributing to a build-up of ozone.
    *   VOCs and $\text{NO}_\text{x}$ also react to form secondary pollutants like PANs, aldehydes, and organic nitrates, which are also harmful.
        *   **Peroxyacetyl Nitrates (PANs):** Formed from the reaction of nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$) with acetyl peroxy radicals (derived from VOCs). PANs are also eye irritants and can damage vegetation.

#### **1.3 Impacts of Smog**

*   **Human Health:**
    *   **Respiratory Problems:** Irritation of the respiratory tract, coughing, wheezing, shortness of breath. Exacerbation of asthma, bronchitis, and emphysema.
    *   **Cardiovascular Effects:** Increased risk of heart attacks and strokes, especially in vulnerable populations.
    *   **Eye Irritation:** Burning, stinging, and tearing of the eyes (especially from PANs and ozone).
    *   **Reduced Lung Function:** Long-term exposure can lead to decreased lung capacity.
*   **Environment:**
    *   **Damage to Vegetation:** Ozone and PANs can damage plant tissues, reduce crop yields, and harm forests.
    *   **Reduced Visibility:** Affects transportation safety (airports, roads) and aesthetic value.
    *   **Material Degradation:** Acidic components can corrode buildings and monuments.

#### **1.4 Control Measures for Smog**

*   **Reduction of Precursor Emissions:**
    *   **Vehicular Emission Controls:**
        *   Catalytic converters in vehicles to reduce $\text{NO}_\text{x}$ and VOCs.
        *   Improved fuel efficiency and alternative fuels (e.g., electric vehicles, hydrogen).
        *   Stricter emission standards for vehicles.
        *   Promoting public transportation, cycling, and walking.
    *   **Industrial Emission Controls:**
        *   Scrubbers to remove $\text{SO}_2$ from industrial flue gases.
        *   Low-NO$\text{x}$ burners in power plants and industrial furnaces.
        *   Control of VOC emissions from industrial processes and storage tanks.
*   **Policy and Regulation:**
    *   Air quality standards and monitoring.
    *   Incentives for adopting cleaner technologies.
    *   Urban planning to reduce traffic congestion.
*   **Public Awareness and Behavior Change:**
    *   Educating the public about the causes and effects of smog.
    *   Encouraging voluntary actions like reducing car use on high-smog days.

### **2. Ozone Layer Disturbance**

The ozone layer is a region of Earth's stratosphere that absorbs most of the Sun's ultraviolet (UV) radiation. It is primarily composed of ozone ($\text{O}_3$) molecules. This layer is crucial for life on Earth because it protects us from harmful UV-B radiation, which can cause skin cancer, cataracts, and damage to DNA.

#### **2.1 Ozone Formation and Destruction in the Stratosphere**

Ozone is continuously formed and destroyed in the stratosphere through a natural cyclical process involving oxygen molecules and UV radiation.

*   **Ozone Formation (Chapman Cycle - Simplified):**
    1.  **Photodissociation of Oxygen:** High-energy UV radiation (UV-C) splits an oxygen molecule ($\text{O}_2$) into two highly reactive oxygen atoms (O).
        $\text{O}_2 + \text{UV-C} \rightarrow \text{O} + \text{O}$
    2.  **Ozone Formation:** A free oxygen atom (O) collides with an oxygen molecule ($\text{O}_2$) and combines to form ozone ($\text{O}_3$). This reaction requires a third molecule (like $\text{N}_2$ or $\text{O}_2$) to absorb excess energy and stabilize the ozone molecule.
        $\text{O} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{O}_3$

*   **Ozone Destruction (Chapman Cycle - Simplified):**
    1.  **Ozone Absorption of UV:** Ozone molecules absorb UV radiation (UV-B and some UV-C), which breaks them down into an oxygen molecule ($\text{O}_2$) and an oxygen atom (O).
        $\text{O}_3 + \text{UV-B} \rightarrow \text{O}_2 + \text{O}$
    2.  **Ozone-Oxygen Reaction:** A free oxygen atom (O) collides with an ozone molecule ($\text{O}_3$) and they react to form two oxygen molecules ($\text{O}_2$).
        $\text{O} + \text{O}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{O}_2$

Under natural conditions, the rates of ozone formation and destruction are balanced, maintaining a relatively stable concentration of ozone in the stratosphere.

#### **2.2 Depletion of the Ozone Layer**

Ozone layer depletion refers to the thinning of the ozone layer in the stratosphere, primarily caused by human-made chemicals known as **Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS)**.

#### **2.3 Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS)**

*   **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs):**
    *   **Characteristics:** Inert, non-toxic, non-flammable, and stable compounds. Widely used in refrigeration, air conditioning, aerosol propellants, and as solvents.
    *   **Examples:** Freon-11 ($\text{CCl}_3\text{F}$), Freon-12 ($\text{CCl}_2\text{F}_2$).

*   **Halons:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Similar to CFCs but contain bromine. Excellent fire suppressants.
    *   **Examples:** Halon 1211, Halon 1301.

*   **Carbon Tetrachloride ($\text{CCl}_4$)**: Used as a solvent and in fire extinguishers.

*   **Methyl Chloroform (1,1,1-Trichloroethane)**: Used as a solvent.

*   **Hydrochlorofluorocarbons (HCFCs):**
    *   **Characteristics:** Introduced as temporary replacements for CFCs. Less potent ODS than CFCs but still contribute to depletion. They are also potent greenhouse gases.

*   **Methyl Bromide:** Used as a fumigant for agricultural purposes.

#### **2.4 Mechanism of Ozone Depletion by ODS**

The ODS themselves do not react directly with ozone in the lower atmosphere. They are so stable that they drift up to the stratosphere over decades. Once in the stratosphere, they are broken down by intense UV radiation, releasing reactive halogen atoms (chlorine and bromine).

*   **Release of Halogen Atoms:**
    *   CFCs, for example, release chlorine atoms.
        $\text{CFCl}_3 + \text{UV light} \rightarrow \text{CFCl}_2 + \text{Cl}$
        $\text{CFCl}_2 + \text{UV light} \rightarrow \text{CFCl} + \text{Cl}$
        $\text{CFCl} + \text{UV light} \rightarrow \text{CF} + \text{Cl}$

*   **Catalytic Destruction of Ozone:** The released chlorine (Cl) and bromine (Br) atoms act as catalysts in a chain reaction that destroys ozone molecules.

    *   **Chlorine Catalytic Cycle:**
        1.  $\text{Cl} + \text{O}_3 \rightarrow \text{ClO} + \text{O}_2$ (Chlorine monoxide)
        2.  $\text{ClO} + \text{O} \rightarrow \text{Cl} + \text{O}_2$ (Regeneration of chlorine atom)
        **Net reaction:** $\text{O}_3 + \text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{O}_2$
        *   A single chlorine atom can destroy thousands of ozone molecules before being removed from the stratosphere.

    *   **Bromine Catalytic Cycle:** Bromine is even more effective than chlorine at destroying ozone.
        1.  $\text{Br} + \text{O}_3 \rightarrow \text{BrO} + \text{O}_2$
        2.  $\text{BrO} + \text{O} \rightarrow \text{Br} + \text{O}_2$
        **Net reaction:** $\text{O}_3 + \text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{O}_2$

#### **2.5 The Ozone Hole**

*   **Discovery:** The "ozone hole" is a region of significant thinning of the ozone layer, most notably over Antarctica during the Antarctic spring (September-October). It was first observed by scientists in the mid-1980s.
*   **Formation in Polar Regions:** The formation of the ozone hole is particularly severe in polar regions due to unique meteorological conditions:
    *   **Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs):** Extremely cold temperatures in the Antarctic stratosphere (below -78°C) allow the formation of PSCs. These clouds are composed of ice crystals and nitric acid.
    *   **Surface Reactions:** PSCs provide surfaces on which chemical reactions occur that convert inactive chlorine and bromine compounds (e.g., $\text{HCl}$, $\text{ClONO}_2$) into more reactive forms (e.g., $\text{Cl}_2$).
    *   **Sunlight Activation:** When sunlight returns to the Antarctic in spring, UV radiation breaks down these reactive forms, releasing large quantities of chlorine and bromine atoms, which then rapidly destroy ozone.
    *   $\text{Cl}_2 + \text{UV light} \rightarrow \text{Cl} + \text{Cl}$
    *   $\text{BrCl} + \text{UV light} \rightarrow \text{Br} + \text{Cl}$

#### **2.6 Impacts of Ozone Layer Depletion**

*   **Increased UV Radiation at the Surface:** The thinning of the ozone layer allows more harmful UV-B radiation to reach the Earth's surface.
*   **Human Health:**
    *   **Skin Cancer:** Increased incidence of melanoma and non-melanoma skin cancers.
    *   **Cataracts:** Higher risk of developing cataracts, leading to vision impairment.
    *   **Immune System Suppression:** Weakening of the immune system, making individuals more susceptible to infections.
*   **Environment:**
    *   **Damage to Plants:** Reduced photosynthesis, growth, and reproduction in plants, affecting crop yields and terrestrial ecosystems.
    *   **Marine Life:** Harm to phytoplankton, zooplankton, and fish larvae, which form the base of the marine food web.
    *   **Material Degradation:** UV radiation can degrade plastics and other materials.
*   **Climate Change:** While ODS primarily deplete ozone, many are also potent greenhouse gases. Their reduction has a beneficial effect on mitigating climate change.

#### **2.7 Control Measures and International Agreements**

*   **The Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer (1987):**
    *   **Purpose:** An international treaty designed to protect the ozone layer by phasing out the production and consumption of ODS.
    *   **Success:** It has been highly successful, leading to a significant reduction in the emissions of CFCs and other ODS. The ozone layer is showing signs of recovery, although full recovery is expected to take several decades.
    *   **Amendments:** The protocol has been amended several times to accelerate the phase-out and include new substances. The Kigali Amendment (2016) specifically addresses the phase-down of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), which are used as substitutes for ODS but are potent greenhouse gases.

*   **Development of Alternatives:**
    *   **Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs):** Developed as replacements for CFCs and HCFCs in refrigeration and air conditioning. While they do not deplete ozone, they are powerful greenhouse gases.
    *   **Hydrofluoroolefins (HFOs):** A newer generation of refrigerants with very low global warming potential.

### **3. Interconnections and Key Differences**

*   **Smog** is a ground-level phenomenon, primarily an air quality issue affecting human health and visibility. It is formed by chemical reactions in the lower atmosphere (troposphere).
*   **Ozone Layer Disturbance** is an issue concerning the depletion of ozone in the upper atmosphere (stratosphere), which has implications for the amount of harmful UV radiation reaching the Earth's surface.

While distinct, both are critical air pollution challenges requiring international cooperation and technological solutions.

### **5. Answers to Practice Questions**

**Answer 1:**
*   **London Smog:**
    *   **Formation:** Burning of fossil fuels (coal), especially in cold, damp weather.
    *   **Key Pollutants:** Sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), particulate matter.
    *   **Appearance:** Greyish-brown or black, associated with fog.
*   **Los Angeles Smog (Photochemical Smog):**
    *   **Formation:** Photochemical reactions between $\text{NO}_\text{x}$ and VOCs in the presence of sunlight.
    *   **Key Pollutants:** Ozone ($\text{O}_3$), PANs, $\text{NO}_\text{x}$, VOCs.
    *   **Appearance:** Brownish or yellowish haze.

**Answer 2:**
Photochemical smog formation involves:
1.  **Emission:** $\text{NO}_\text{x}$ (from combustion) and VOCs (from various sources) are released.
2.  **Initial Reactions:** $\text{NO}_\text{x}$ in exhaust is oxidized to $\text{NO}_2$.
3.  **Photolysis:** Sunlight splits $\text{NO}_2$ into NO and atomic oxygen (O).
    $\text{NO}_2 + \text{UV light} \rightarrow \text{NO} + \text{O}$
4.  **Ozone Formation:** Atomic oxygen reacts with $\text{O}_2$ to form ozone ($\text{O}_3$).
    $\text{O} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{O}_3$
5.  **Chain Reactions:** Ozone reacts with NO, regenerating $\text{NO}_2$, which sustains ozone formation. VOCs participate in reactions that accelerate $\text{NO}$ to $\text{NO}_2$ conversion, leading to ozone build-up and the formation of other harmful secondary pollutants like PANs.

**Answer 3:**
Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS) are chemicals that contribute to the thinning of the stratospheric ozone layer.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs):** Used in refrigerants, aerosols, solvents.
    *   **Halons:** Used as fire retardants.
    *   **Carbon Tetrachloride ($\text{CCl}_4$):** Solvent.
*   **Mechanism:** ODS are stable in the troposphere but rise to the stratosphere, where UV radiation breaks them down, releasing chlorine and bromine atoms. These atoms act as catalysts in chain reactions that destroy ozone molecules ($\text{O}_3$). For instance, chlorine reacts with ozone to form chlorine monoxide (ClO) and oxygen ($\text{O}_2$), and then ClO reacts with atomic oxygen to regenerate chlorine, continuing the cycle.

**Answer 4:**
The "ozone hole" is a significant thinning of the ozone layer, most pronounced over Antarctica during spring. Its severity in polar regions is due to:
1.  **Extreme Cold:** Very low stratospheric temperatures allow the formation of Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs).
2.  **PSCs as Reaction Surfaces:** PSCs provide surfaces for chemical reactions that convert inactive chlorine and bromine compounds into more reactive forms.
3.  **Sunlight Activation:** When sunlight returns in spring, UV radiation breaks down these reactive forms, releasing a large amount of ozone-destroying chlorine and bromine atoms, which then rapidly deplete ozone.

**Answer 5:**
Increased UV-B radiation due to ozone layer depletion has significant impacts:
*   **Human Health:** Increased rates of skin cancer (melanoma, basal cell carcinoma), cataracts, and immune system suppression.
*   **Terrestrial Ecosystems:** Damage to plants, leading to reduced photosynthesis, growth, crop yields, and impacts on forests.
*   **Aquatic Ecosystems:** Harm to phytoplankton and marine life, affecting food webs.
*   **Materials:** Degradation of plastics and other materials.

**Answer 6:**
The symptoms described (respiratory illnesses, high PM and ozone levels) strongly suggest the prevalence of **photochemical smog (Los Angeles type)**.
*   **Probable Sources:**
    *   **Particulate Matter (PM):** Likely from vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, and possibly construction or other particulate-generating activities.
    *   **Ozone ($\text{O}_3$):** Formed from reactions involving precursor pollutants. The presence of ozone points to **vehicle exhaust ($\text{NO}_\text{x}$) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs)** from vehicles, industrial processes, and solvent use as the primary sources. The city's topography and meteorology might also be contributing factors (e.g., inversions trapping pollutants).

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   **Smog Types:** Know the difference between sulfurous (London) smog and photochemical (Los Angeles) smog.
*   **Photochemical Smog Ingredients:** Sunlight, $\text{NO}_\text{x}$, and VOCs are essential for its formation.
*   **Stratospheric Ozone:** Protects Earth from harmful UV-B radiation.
*   **ODS Mechanism:** Chlorine and bromine atoms are the primary agents of stratospheric ozone destruction, acting as catalysts.
*   **Montreal Protocol:** A landmark international agreement for ozone protection, demonstrating the effectiveness of global cooperation.
*   **Health Impacts:** Both smog and ozone depletion have serious consequences for human health.
*   **Control Strategies:** Focus on reducing precursor emissions for smog and phasing out ODS for ozone layer protection.
