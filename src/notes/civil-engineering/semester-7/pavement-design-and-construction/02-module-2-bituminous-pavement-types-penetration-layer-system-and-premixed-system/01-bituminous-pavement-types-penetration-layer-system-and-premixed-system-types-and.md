# PAVEMENT DESIGN AND CONSTRUCTION - Module 2: Bituminous Pavement Types

## Module 2: Bituminous Pavement Types: Penetration Layer System and Premixed System

This module delves into the fundamental types of bituminous pavements, focusing on two distinct construction methodologies: the **Penetration Layer System** and the **Premixed System**. We will explore the materials used in each, their specifications, and then examine specialized bituminous layers like Stone Mastic Asphalt (SMA) and Mastic Asphalt.

---

### **1. Bituminous Pavement Types: Penetration Layer System vs. Premixed System**

Bituminous pavements utilize asphaltic materials (bitumen or asphalt cement) as a binder to hold aggregates together, forming a durable and flexible pavement surface. The primary distinction lies in how the binder and aggregate are combined.

---

#### **1.1 Penetration Layer System (Road Mix)**

This method involves applying the bituminous binder (usually asphalt binder or cutback asphalt) directly onto a prepared aggregate base course, and then mixing the binder and aggregate in situ (on the road).

**Key Concepts:**

*   **In-situ Mixing:** The mixing of binder and aggregate occurs directly on the roadbed.
*   **Layered Application:** Binder is applied to layers of aggregate, followed by mixing and compaction.
*   **Less Control:** Generally offers less control over aggregate-binder uniformity compared to premixed systems.

**Construction Process (Simplified):**

1.  **Preparation of Base Course:** A properly prepared and compacted base or sub-base course is essential.
2.  **Aggregate Spreading:** Aggregate of specific grading is spread evenly over the base course to the required thickness.
3.  **Binder Application:** Bituminous binder is applied using a pressure distributor (asphalt distributor) in uniform rates.
4.  **Mixing:** The binder and aggregate are mixed using specialized equipment like road mixers, graders, or even by dragging and blading with motor graders.
5.  **Spreading and Compaction:** The mixed material is spread and compacted in layers to achieve the desired density and smoothness.

**Types of Penetration Layer Systems:**

*   **Road Mix (Single Surface Treatment):** The simplest form, where a single layer of aggregate is coated with binder. Typically used for low-traffic volume roads.
*   **Road Mix (Multiple Surface Treatment):** Involves applying multiple layers of aggregate and binder, with each layer being mixed and compacted. Provides increased thickness and durability.
*   **Plant Mix (Road Laying):** While the mixing happens at a plant, the laying and compaction are similar to in-situ methods, but with pre-mixed materials. This blurs the line slightly, but the *application* on the road is still a key consideration. For the purpose of this module, we focus on *in-situ* mixing in the penetration system.

---

#### **1.2 Premixed System (Plant Mix)**

In this system, the aggregate and bituminous binder are mixed at a central plant (asphalt mixing plant) before being transported to the construction site for spreading and compaction.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Centralized Mixing:** Binder and aggregate are mixed in a controlled environment at a plant.
*   **Uniformity:** Offers much greater control over aggregate gradation, binder content, and mixing temperature, leading to a more uniform product.
*   **Higher Quality:** Generally produces a higher quality and more durable pavement layer.

**Construction Process (Simplified):**

1.  **Aggregate Stockpiling and Screening:** Aggregates are stored and screened to achieve desired gradations.
2.  **Heating and Drying:** Aggregates are heated and dried in a rotary dryer.
3.  **Binder Heating:** Bituminous binder is heated to the required temperature.
4.  **Batching and Mixing:** Measured quantities of hot aggregates and binder are accurately batched and mixed in an asphalt mixing plant.
5.  **Transportation:** The hot mix asphalt (HMA) is transported to the site in insulated trucks.
6.  **Spreading:** The HMA is spread using asphalt pavers in uniform layers.
7.  **Compaction:** The spread material is compacted using rollers (pneumatic, static steel-wheel, vibratory) to achieve the required density.

**Types of Premixed Systems:**

*   **Asphalt Concrete (AC):** A widely used pavement layer consisting of well-graded aggregate thoroughly mixed with hot asphalt cement. Typically used for surface courses and binder courses.
*   **Asphalt Treated Base (ATB):** Similar to AC but with a lower binder content, used as a base layer.
*   **Plant Mix Bituminous Macadam (PMBM):** Aggregates are premixed with binder and then laid and rolled.
*   **Open Graded Friction Course (OGFC):** A highly permeable surface layer with a larger aggregate structure and higher binder content, designed to improve skid resistance and reduce splash and spray.

### **2. Types and Specifications of Materials Used**

The performance of bituminous pavements is highly dependent on the quality and properties of the materials used.

---

#### **2.1 Bituminous Binders**

The binder is the glue that holds the aggregate together.

**Types:**

*   **Asphalt Cement (AC) / Bitumen:**
    *   **Penetration Grade Bitumen:** Classified by its penetration value (e.g., 60/70, 80/100). A lower penetration value indicates a harder bitumen. Used in hot mix asphalt.
    *   **Viscosity Grade Bitumen:** Classified by its viscosity at specific temperatures (e.g., VG-30, VG-40). Provides better performance prediction over a wider temperature range.
    *   **Performance Graded (PG) Bitumen:** The most advanced classification, based on the expected climate and traffic conditions (e.g., PG 64-22). It specifies the binder's performance at high and low temperatures.
*   **Cutback Asphalt:** Asphalt cement dissolved in a volatile solvent (like naphtha or kerosene). The solvent evaporates after application, leaving the asphalt cement to bind the aggregate.
    *   **Rapid Curing (RC):** Uses a highly volatile solvent.
    *   **Medium Curing (MC):** Uses a medium volatile solvent.
    *   **Slow Curing (SC):** Uses a less volatile solvent, often referred to as asphalt emulsion rejuvenators or binders for dust control.
*   **Asphalt Emulsion:** Asphalt cement dispersed in water with an emulsifying agent. Can be cationic (positively charged) or anionic (negatively charged).
    *   **Rapid Setting (RS):** Emulsion breaks quickly, allowing rapid mixing.
    *   **Medium Setting (MS):** Emulsion breaks slower than RS.
    *   **Slow Setting (SS):** Emulsion breaks slowly, suitable for soil stabilization or fog seals.

**Specifications (General):**

*   **Penetration Grade:** Penetration at 25°C (77°F), softening point, ductility, flash point.
*   **Viscosity Grade:** Kinematic viscosity at 60°C (140°F) and 135°C (275°F), penetration, ductility, flash point.
*   **PG Grade:** Performance grading based on stiffness at high temperatures and ductility at low temperatures.
*   **Cutback Asphalt:** Viscosity, solvent content, distillation properties, penetration of residue, ductility of residue.
*   **Emulsified Asphalt:** Viscosity, residue content, demulsification, miscibility, sieve test, float test, penetration of residue, ductility of residue.

**Example:** For a surface course in a hot climate, a harder penetration grade bitumen (e.g., 60/70) or a higher PG grade (e.g., PG 76-22) might be specified.

---

#### **2.2 Aggregates**

Aggregates provide the structural skeleton of the pavement and influence its strength, durability, and skid resistance.

**Types:**

*   **Coarse Aggregate:** Retained on the 4.75 mm (No. 4) sieve. Typically crushed stone or crushed gravel.
*   **Fine Aggregate:** Passing the 4.75 mm (No. 4) sieve. Typically natural sand or manufactured sand.
*   **Mineral Filler:** Very fine material passing the 0.075 mm (No. 200) sieve, usually rock dust or portland cement.

**Specifications (General):**

*   **Aggregate Gradation:** The distribution of aggregate particle sizes, determined by sieve analysis. This is critical for achieving dense packing and good interlocking.
    *   **Dense-Graded Mixes:** Have a continuous gradation with a low void content. Used for most surface and binder courses.
    *   **Open-Graded Mixes:** Have a coarser gradation with high void content. Used for friction courses.
    *   **Gap-Graded Mixes:** Have a limited range of aggregate sizes, with one or more intermediate sizes intentionally omitted. Used in some specialized mixes like SMA.
*   **Aggregate Properties:**
    *   **Hardness and Durability:** Resistance to crushing, abrasion, and impact (e.g., Los Angeles Abrasion test, Aggregate Impact Value).
    *   **Soundness:** Resistance to disintegration from weathering (e.g., Sodium/Magnesium Sulfate Soundness test).
    *   **Particle Shape and Texture:** Flakiness and elongation indices, angularity, and surface texture. Angular aggregates interlock better, providing higher stability.
    *   **Adhesion (Affinity):** Ability of the aggregate surface to bond with the asphalt binder, especially in the presence of moisture (e.g., Coating and Moisture Sensitivity tests).
    *   **Cleanliness:** Absence of deleterious materials like clay lumps, organic matter, and fines (e.g., Clay Content test).

**Example:** For a high-traffic surface course, aggregates with high crushing strength, good particle shape (angularity), and good adhesion properties would be specified.

---

#### **2.3 Additives**

Additives are used to enhance specific properties of the asphalt mix.

**Types:**

*   **Anti-stripping Agents (Adhesion Promoters):** Improve the bond between the asphalt binder and the aggregate, especially in the presence of moisture. Examples include hydrated lime, liquid amines, and Portland cement.
*   **Polymers:** Improve the viscoelastic properties of the binder, enhancing resistance to rutting at high temperatures and cracking at low temperatures. Examples include Styrene-Butadiene-Styrene (SBS) and Ethylene Vinyl Acetate (EVA).
*   **Fibers:** Added to SMA to prevent binder drain-down and improve mixture cohesion. Examples include cellulose fibers and mineral fibers.
*   **Rejuvenators:** Used in asphalt recycling to restore aged asphalt binder properties.

**Specifications:** Varies greatly depending on the additive type. Performance-based specifications are often used.

**Example:** Hydrated lime is commonly added to asphalt mixes to prevent stripping, especially when using aggregates with a tendency to lose adhesion.

### **3. Special Types of Bituminous Layers**

Beyond standard asphalt concrete, certain specialized mixes offer enhanced performance characteristics for specific applications.

---

#### **3.1 Stone Mastic Asphalt (SMA)**

SMA is a gap-graded asphalt mixture designed for high-traffic flexibility and durability, particularly resistant to rutting and fatigue cracking.

**Key Features:**

*   **Gap-Graded Aggregate Structure:** Contains a large proportion of coarse aggregate (stone skeleton) and a reduced amount of fine aggregate, creating significant interconnected voids.
*   **High Binder Content:** Achieved by using a stiff, polymer-modified asphalt binder and stabilizing it with fibers to prevent drain-down.
*   **Excellent Durability:** The robust stone skeleton provides high shear strength and resistance to deformation.
*   **High Rutting Resistance:** Due to the stiff binder and stone-on-stone contact.
*   **Good Fatigue Resistance:** The flexibility provided by the polymer-modified binder.
*   **Reduced Noise:** Often has a coarser texture than conventional asphalt, which can lead to lower tire-pavement noise.

**Materials Specifications:**

*   **Aggregate:** High proportion of high-quality crushed coarse aggregate (stone-on-stone contact is crucial). Fine aggregate is used in limited quantities.
*   **Filler:** Mineral filler (e.g., hydrated lime, Portland cement) is used to increase the stiffness of the mastic binder and improve adhesion.
*   **Binder:** Typically a polymer-modified bitumen (e.g., SBS modified) or a high-viscosity bitumen is used to achieve the required stability and resistance to drain-down.
*   **Fibers:** Cellulose fibers or mineral fibers are essential to stabilize the high binder content and prevent it from draining out of the mix during transport and placement.

**Typical Applications:** High-traffic roads, highways, bridge decks, bus lanes, intersections, and areas prone to heavy loading and rutting.

**Example Specification:** A typical SMA mix might contain 70-80% coarse aggregate, 8-12% fine aggregate, 10-15% mineral filler, 6-8% binder, and 0.3-0.5% fibers.

---

#### **3.2 Mastic Asphalt (Sheet Asphalt / Asphalt Mastic)**

Mastic asphalt is a flowable, cohesive, and voidless mixture of bitumen, fine aggregate, and filler. It is essentially a very rich asphalt binder with finely graded filler and sand.

**Key Features:**

*   **Voidless and Impermeable:** The dense nature of the mix makes it impermeable, protecting underlying layers from water infiltration.
*   **Flowable:** Can be spread and worked with shovels and smoothing tools, allowing for the creation of smooth surfaces and filling of irregularities.
*   **Cohesive:** The high binder content makes it very cohesive.
*   **Excellent Adhesion:** Bonds well to underlying surfaces.
*   **Low Strength:** Typically has lower structural strength compared to asphalt concrete due to the absence of a coarse aggregate skeleton.
*   **Good Durability and Weather Resistance:** Its impermeability and binder-rich nature contribute to good weather resistance.

**Materials Specifications:**

*   **Binder:** High binder content, often a hard bitumen or polymer-modified binder.
*   **Aggregate:** Primarily fine aggregate (sand) and a significant amount of mineral filler. There is a general absence of coarse aggregate.
*   **Filler:** Critical for stiffening the binder and creating the mastic effect. Often Portland cement or hydrated lime is used.

**Construction Process:**

Mastic asphalt is mixed at a plant and transported to the site. It is then spread by hand or specialized equipment and smoothed to a dense, even surface. It is often laid in thin layers.

**Typical Applications:**

*   Waterproofing layers on bridge decks and tunnels.
*   Wearing courses on pedestrian areas and industrial floors.
*   Patching and repair of existing asphalt surfaces.
*   As a bedding layer for paving stones.
*   Overlay on existing pavements to provide a smooth riding surface and waterproofing.

**Example Specification:** A mastic asphalt mix might contain around 20-25% binder, with the remaining proportion being a blend of sand and filler.

### **Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** Differentiate between the penetration layer system and the premixed system of bituminous pavement construction, highlighting the key advantages of each.

**Answer:**

*   **Penetration Layer System (Road Mix):**
    *   **Mixing:** In-situ, on the roadbed.
    *   **Control:** Less control over uniformity of binder-aggregate mix.
    *   **Equipment:** Simpler equipment (graders, distributors).
    *   **Advantages:** Lower initial equipment cost, suitable for low-volume roads, can be adapted to local materials more readily.
*   **Premixed System (Plant Mix):**
    *   **Mixing:** Centralized at an asphalt mixing plant.
    *   **Control:** High control over aggregate gradation, binder content, temperature, and mixing, leading to uniform quality.
    *   **Equipment:** Requires an asphalt mixing plant, pavers, and specialized rollers.
    *   **Advantages:** Produces higher quality and more durable pavement, suitable for high-volume roads, better performance prediction.

**Question 2:** What are the essential components of a bituminous pavement mix, and what are the key properties that are specified for each?

**Answer:**

The essential components are:

*   **Bituminous Binder:**
    *   **Key Properties:** Viscosity (for workability and durability), penetration (for hardness), ductility (for flexibility), softening point (resistance to deformation), flash point (safety), adhesion with aggregates, and performance grade (for temperature susceptibility).
*   **Aggregates:**
    *   **Key Properties:** Gradation (for packing and void content), hardness and durability (resistance to crushing/abrasion), soundness (resistance to weathering), particle shape and texture (for interlocking and stability), adhesion with binder, and cleanliness.
*   **Additives (Optional):**
    *   **Key Properties:** Depends on the additive – e.g., for anti-stripping agents, improved adhesion; for polymers, enhanced elasticity and temperature resistance.

**Question 3:** Explain the primary characteristics and typical applications of Stone Mastic Asphalt (SMA).

**Answer:**

*   **Primary Characteristics:**
    *   Gap-graded aggregate structure with a stone skeleton.
    *   High binder content stabilized with fibers.
    *   Excellent rutting resistance.
    *   Good fatigue resistance.
    *   High durability.
    *   Often provides reduced tire-pavement noise.
*   **Typical Applications:**
    *   High-traffic highways and arterial roads.
    *   Bridge decks.
    *   Bus lanes and heavy-duty areas.
    *   Intersections.

**Question 4:** Why are fibers essential in Stone Mastic Asphalt (SMA), and what is the function of mineral filler in both SMA and Mastic Asphalt?

**Answer:**

*   **Fibers in SMA:** Fibers (cellulose or mineral) are crucial in SMA to **prevent asphalt binder drain-down** during mixing, transportation, and placement. The high binder content in SMA would otherwise separate from the aggregates due to gravity, leading to a non-uniform mix and poor performance. The fibers act as a stabilizer, holding the binder within the mix.
*   **Mineral Filler in SMA and Mastic Asphalt:** In both SMA and mastic asphalt, mineral filler (like hydrated lime or Portland cement) plays a critical role in **stiffening the bitumen binder**. This stiffening effect increases the viscosity of the binder film, contributing to the high shear resistance and rutting resistance in SMA. In mastic asphalt, the high proportion of filler, combined with fine aggregate and bitumen, creates the characteristic voidless and cohesive mastic mixture. It also improves the adhesion of the binder to the aggregate.

### **Important Points to Remember**

*   The choice between penetration and premixed systems depends on traffic volume, available resources, and desired pavement quality.
*   Premixed systems generally offer superior quality and durability due to controlled mixing.
*   Aggregate gradation is a fundamental parameter that dictates the mix design and performance of bituminous pavements.
*   Bitumen properties are crucial for flexibility and resistance to temperature fluctuations; Performance Graded (PG) binders are the modern standard.
*   Additives are used strategically to overcome specific material deficiencies or enhance performance.
*   SMA is designed for high-stress conditions, emphasizing rutting resistance and durability through its unique aggregate structure and binder system.
*   Mastic Asphalt is a niche product known for its impermeability and cohesive, voidless nature, making it suitable for waterproofing and specialized surface applications.
*   Understanding the specifications for each material is vital for ensuring the successful design and construction of bituminous pavements.
