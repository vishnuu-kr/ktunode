# PAVEMENT DESIGN AND CONSTRUCTION - Module 3: Construction of Flexible Pavement

## Topic: Construction of Flexible Pavement - Construction Steps, Equipment, and Quality Control

This module focuses on the practical aspects of constructing a flexible pavement, detailing the sequence of operations, the specialized equipment involved, and the crucial quality control measures at each layer.

---

### **1. Introduction to Flexible Pavement Construction**

Flexible pavements derive their structural integrity from the collective action of their constituent layers, distributing wheel loads to the subgrade. Their construction involves a series of carefully sequenced steps to ensure a durable and safe road surface.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Flexible Pavement:** A pavement structure that depends on the aggregate interlock and cohesion of its layers to distribute loads. It deforms under load and returns to its original shape.
*   **Layers:** Flexible pavements typically consist of the following layers, from bottom to top:
    *   Subgrade
    *   Sub-base (optional, but common)
    *   Base Course
    *   Binder Course (optional)
    *   Wearing Course (Surface Course)
*   **Construction Sequence:** The order in which layers are placed and compacted is critical for achieving the desired structural performance.

---

### **2. Construction of the Subgrade Layer**

The subgrade is the natural ground surface upon which the pavement structure is built. Its stability and uniformity are paramount.

**2.1. Construction Steps:**

*   **Site Preparation:**
    *   **Clearing:** Removal of vegetation, topsoil, debris, and any unsuitable material.
    *   **Grubbing:** Removal of roots and stumps.
    *   **Dewatering:** If the area is waterlogged, appropriate measures are taken to remove excess water.
*   **Earthwork Operations:**
    *   **Excavation:** Cutting down high areas to the required formation level.
    *   **Embankment/Filling:** Placing and compacting selected soil in low-lying areas to achieve the required formation level.
    *   **Compaction:** Essential for increasing the density and strength of the subgrade, reducing future settlement.
    *   **Grading/Leveling:** Ensuring a uniform and smooth surface for the subsequent layer.

**2.2. Equipment Used:**

*   **Bulldozers/Angledozers:** For clearing, excavation, and spreading earth.
*   **Motor Graders:** For fine grading and leveling the subgrade.
*   **Excavators/Backhoe Loaders:** For excavation and loading of unsuitable material.
*   **Dump Trucks/Tippers:** For transporting excavated material and fill material.
*   **Sheepsfoot Rollers:** Effective for compacting cohesive soils.
*   **Vibratory Rollers (Smooth Drum):** For compacting granular and mixed soils.
*   **Pneumatic Tire Rollers:** For final surface compaction and sealing.
*   **Water Tankers:** For moistening the soil to achieve optimum moisture content for compaction.

**2.3. Quality Control Checks:**

*   **Material Testing:**
    *   **Gradation:** Ensuring the soil falls within acceptable particle size distribution.
    *   **Atterberg Limits (Liquid Limit, Plastic Limit, Plasticity Index):** To assess the plasticity and suitability of the soil for subgrade. PI generally should be < 10 for subgrade.
    *   **Moisture Content:** Ensuring it is close to the Optimum Moisture Content (OMC) for maximum dry density.
*   **Compaction Control:**
    *   **Field Density Tests (e.g., Sand Cone Method, Nuclear Gauge):** To verify the degree of compaction achieved. Minimum 97% of Maximum Dry Density (MDD) is usually required.
    *   **Field Moisture Content Tests:** To check if the moisture content is within acceptable limits of OMC.
*   **Leveling and Smoothness:**
    *   **Leveling Instruments (e.g., Dumpy Level, Laser Level):** To ensure the formation level is as per design.
    *   **Straightedge/Profile Check:** To verify the smoothness of the prepared subgrade surface.

**Important Point to Remember:** The subgrade is the foundation of the entire pavement structure. Any deficiencies here will significantly impact the performance and lifespan of the pavement.

### **3. Construction of Granular Sub-base (GSB) Layer**

The GSB layer provides a uniform and stable platform for the base course, distributes loads, and helps in improving drainage. It is typically composed of crushed stone or gravel.

**3.1. Construction Steps:**

*   **Preparation of Subgrade:** Ensure the subgrade is clean, well-compacted, and at the correct level.
*   **Material Spreading:**
    *   GSB material is brought to the site in trucks and spread evenly over the prepared subgrade using motor graders or front-end loaders.
    *   The material is spread in layers, usually not exceeding the vibratory roller's effective compaction depth (typically 150-200 mm loose thickness).
*   **Moistening:** Water is applied uniformly to achieve the optimum moisture content (OMC) for compaction.
*   **Compaction:**
    *   The spread material is compacted using a combination of rollers.
    *   Initial breakdown rolling is done with a heavy vibratory roller or pneumatic tire roller.
    *   Intermediate rolling is done with a smooth drum vibratory roller.
    *   Finish rolling ensures a smooth and uniform surface.
*   **Leveling and Shaping:** Motor graders are used to achieve the correct profile and cross-slope.

**3.2. Equipment Used:**

*   **Dump Trucks/Tippers:** For transporting GSB material.
*   **Front-end Loaders:** For loading and unloading GSB material.
*   **Motor Graders:** For spreading, leveling, and shaping the GSB layer.
*   **Water Tankers:** For moistening the material.
*   **Vibratory Rollers (Smooth Drum):** Primary equipment for compaction.
*   **Pneumatic Tire Rollers:** Can be used for initial breakdown or final finishing.

**3.3. Quality Control Checks:**

*   **Material Testing:**
    *   **Gradation:** Crucial for achieving good mechanical interlocking. Tests according to specifications (e.g., IS or AASHTO standards).
    *   **Plasticity Index (PI):** GSB material should be non-plastic (PI = 0).
    *   **California Bearing Ratio (CBR):** To assess the strength of the GSB material. Minimum 30% is typically required.
    *   **Water Absorption:**
*   **Compaction Control:**
    *   **Field Density Tests:** To ensure the required degree of compaction (typically 97-98% of MDD).
    *   **Field Moisture Content Tests:** To confirm moisture content is within ±1% of OMC.
*   **Thickness Measurement:** Checking the actual compacted thickness against the design.
*   **Leveling and Smoothness:**
    *   **Leveling Instruments:** For verifying the finished level.
    *   **Straightedge/Profile Check:** To ensure the surface is smooth and conforms to the required profile and cross-slope.

**Important Point to Remember:** Proper compaction of the GSB layer is vital to prevent future settlement and ensure uniform load distribution.

### **4. Construction of Water Bound Macadam (WBM) Layer**

WBM is a base course layer constructed by spreading, rolling, and binding crushed aggregate with stone screenings and water. It's a common and economical base course material.

**4.1. Construction Steps:**

*   **Preparation of Sub-base/Subgrade:** Ensure the underlying layer is clean, compacted, and at the correct level.
*   **Spreading of Coarse Aggregate:**
    *   Crushed stone aggregate of specified size is spread uniformly over the prepared surface in layers.
    *   Each layer's loose thickness is typically around 75-100 mm.
*   **Rolling (Initial):**
    *   The spread aggregate is rolled using a three-wheeler roller or a vibratory roller to achieve initial compaction and interlock the stones.
*   **Spreading of Stone Screenings (Binding Material):**
    *   Fine aggregate (stone screenings) is spread over the coarse aggregate.
    *   This material fills the voids between the coarse aggregates.
*   **Moistening:** Water is sprinkled uniformly to facilitate the binding action of screenings and create a slurry.
*   **Rolling (Intermediate & Final):**
    *   Rolling with a three-wheeler roller or vibratory roller is continued while sprinkling water.
    *   This process forces the stone screenings down into the voids, forming a stable, interlocking mass.
    *   The surface is rolled until the aggregate is firmly keyed and no displacement occurs.
*   **Dressing:** Any high spots are dressed down, and low spots are filled with screenings.

**4.2. Equipment Used:**

*   **Dump Trucks/Tippers:** For transporting coarse aggregate and screenings.
*   **Motor Graders/Front-end Loaders:** For spreading aggregate and screenings.
*   **Water Tankers:** For sprinkling water.
*   **Three-Wheeler Rollers (Static):** Traditional roller for WBM.
*   **Vibratory Rollers (Smooth Drum):** Increasingly used for better compaction.
*   **Sprinklers:** For uniform water application.

**4.3. Quality Control Checks:**

*   **Material Testing:**
    *   **Gradation of Coarse Aggregate:** Specific size ranges for different layers (e.g., WBM I, II, III).
    *   **Aggregate Impact Value (AIV):** To check for toughness.
    *   **Flakiness Index & Elongation Index:** To check the shape of aggregate.
    *   **Gradation of Stone Screenings:** As per specifications.
*   **Compaction Control:**
    *   **Field Density Tests:** To ensure adequate compaction.
    *   **Number of Passes:** Specified number of roller passes is usually recorded.
*   **Thickness Measurement:** Verifying the compacted thickness.
*   **Leveling and Smoothness:**
    *   **Leveling Instruments:** For checking the finished surface level.
    *   **Straightedge:** For checking smoothness and profile.
*   **Binding Material Distribution:** Visual inspection to ensure adequate filling of voids.

**Important Point to Remember:** The success of WBM lies in the proper filling of voids with screenings and achieving a dense, interlocking matrix through effective rolling and watering.

### **5. Construction of Wet Mix Macadam (WMM) Layer**

WMM is a superior base or sub-base layer compared to WBM. It's a scientifically graded mixture of coarse aggregate, fine aggregate, and water, laid and compacted in layers.

**5.1. Construction Steps:**

*   **Preparation of Underlying Layer:** Ensure the sub-base/subgrade is clean, compacted, and at the correct level.
*   **Material Mixing:**
    *   Coarse aggregate and fine aggregate are mixed in specified proportions at a plant (batching plant or continuous mixing plant).
    *   Water is added to achieve the desired moisture content during mixing.
*   **Transportation:** The mixed material is transported to the site in trucks.
*   **Spreading:**
    *   The WMM material is spread evenly over the prepared surface using motor graders or specialized pavers.
    *   Spreading is done in layers, with a loose thickness typically around 100-150 mm.
*   **Compaction:**
    *   Rolling starts immediately after spreading.
    *   Initial breakdown rolling is done by a heavy vibratory roller.
    *   This is followed by intermediate rolling with pneumatic tire rollers to knit the layer together and achieve density.
    *   Final rolling is done with a smooth drum vibratory roller to create a dense, smooth, and uniform surface.
*   **Leveling and Shaping:** Motor graders ensure the correct profile and cross-slope.

**5.2. Equipment Used:**

*   **Aggregate Crushing Plants:** For producing graded aggregates.
*   **Batching Plants/Continuous Mixing Plants:** For accurately proportioning and mixing aggregates with water.
*   **Dump Trucks/Tippers:** For transporting the mixed WMM.
*   **Motor Graders:** For spreading and initial leveling.
*   **Pavers (Optional but preferred):** For more uniform spreading and initial leveling.
*   **Vibratory Rollers (Smooth Drum):** For initial breakdown and final finishing.
*   **Pneumatic Tire Rollers:** Crucial for intermediate compaction and sealing the surface.
*   **Water Tankers:** For any necessary additional moistening (though usually mixed at plant).

**5.3. Quality Control Checks:**

*   **Material Testing:**
    *   **Gradation of Coarse Aggregate:** As per specifications.
    *   **Gradation of Fine Aggregate:** As per specifications.
    *   **Gradation of Mix:** The combined aggregate gradation must meet specific requirements for WMM.
    *   **Aggregate Impact Value (AIV), Flakiness & Elongation Index:** As per specifications.
    *   **Optimum Moisture Content (OMC) and Maximum Dry Density (MDD):** Determined from laboratory tests.
*   **Compaction Control:**
    *   **Field Density Tests:** To ensure the required degree of compaction (typically 97-98% of MDD).
    *   **Field Moisture Content Tests:** To verify moisture content is within ±1% of OMC.
*   **Thickness Measurement:** Ensuring the compacted thickness meets design requirements.
*   **Leveling and Smoothness:**
    *   **Leveling Instruments:** For checking the finished surface level.
    *   **Straightedge/Profile Check:** To verify smoothness and profile.
*   **Plant Mix Verification:** Regular checks of the mix proportions and water content at the mixing plant.

**Important Point to Remember:** WMM offers better strength and durability due to controlled mixing and compaction. Proper control over mix design and moisture content is critical.

### **6. Construction of Bituminous Macadam (BM) Layer**

Bituminous Macadam is a type of open-graded premix asphaltic material laid and rolled in courses. It typically serves as a binder or base course in some pavement designs.

**6.1. Construction Steps:**

*   **Preparation of Underlying Layer:** Ensure the base course is clean, dry, and at the correct level.
*   **Priming (if required):** Application of a prime coat (a light application of bituminous material) on the granular base to promote adhesion and prevent loss of fines.
*   **Premixing:** Aggregates are heated and mixed with a specified grade of bitumen at a hot mix plant to produce an open-graded premix.
*   **Transportation:** The hot premix is transported to the site in insulated trucks.
*   **Spreading:**
    *   The hot premix is spread evenly over the primed surface using a paver or motor grader.
    *   It's laid in courses, typically with a loose thickness of around 70-100 mm.
*   **Rolling:**
    *   Rolling starts immediately after spreading while the material is hot.
    *   Initial breakdown rolling with a heavier roller (e.g., 8-10 tonne static or vibratory).
    *   Intermediate rolling with a pneumatic tire roller (to seal the surface and knit the layer).
    *   Final rolling with a lighter static roller (e.g., 6-8 tonne) to achieve a smooth and dense surface.
*   **Leveling and Shaping:** Motor graders are used for initial leveling, followed by pavers for precision.

**6.2. Equipment Used:**

*   **Hot Mix Plant (HMP):** For heating and mixing aggregates with bitumen.
*   **Dump Trucks/Tippers (Insulated):** For transporting hot premix.
*   **Bitumen Sprayer:** For applying the prime coat.
*   **Pavers (Asphalt Paver Finisher):** Ideal for uniform spreading and leveling.
*   **Motor Graders:** Can be used for spreading and leveling in simpler applications.
*   **Rollers:**
    *   **Static Rollers (3-wheel and Tandem):** For breakdown and finishing.
    *   **Vibratory Rollers:** For breakdown and compaction.
    *   **Pneumatic Tire Rollers:** For intermediate rolling and sealing.

**6.3. Quality Control Checks:**

*   **Material Testing:**
    *   **Aggregate Gradation:** As per specifications for open-graded premix.
    *   **Aggregate Properties (AIV, FI, EI):** As specified.
    *   **Bitumen Grade and Penetration:** To ensure the correct binder is used.
    *   **Mix Properties (Stability, Flow, Void Content):** Tested from samples taken from the plant.
    *   **Temperature of Mix:** Crucial for workability and compaction. Hot mix should be laid at temperatures between 140-170°C.
*   **Compaction Control:**
    *   **Field Density Tests:** To ensure the required degree of compaction (usually expressed as a percentage of laboratory density).
    *   **Number of Roller Passes:** Specified number of passes is usually recorded.
*   **Thickness Measurement:** Verifying the compacted thickness.
*   **Leveling and Smoothness:**
    *   **Straightedge/Profile Check:** To ensure a smooth and even surface.
    *   **Surface Irregularities:** Maximum allowable deviation is specified.

**Important Point to Remember:** Temperature control during mixing and laying is critical for Bituminous Macadam. It must be laid and compacted while hot.

### **7. Construction of Bituminous Concrete (BC) / Wearing Course**

Bituminous Concrete (BC) is the top layer of the flexible pavement, providing a smooth, durable, and skid-resistant riding surface. It's a dense-graded hot mix asphalt (HMA) laid and compacted at high temperatures.

**7.1. Construction Steps:**

*   **Preparation of Underlying Layer:** Ensure the binder course (or base course) is clean, dry, and at the correct level.
*   **Tack Coat Application:** A light application of a suitable bituminous binder (tack coat) is applied to the underlying layer to ensure good adhesion between layers.
*   **Premixing:** Aggregates and filler are heated and mixed with bitumen at a hot mix plant to produce a dense-graded hot mix asphalt (BC mix).
*   **Transportation:** The hot BC mix is transported to the site in insulated trucks.
*   **Spreading:**
    *   The hot BC mix is spread evenly over the tack-coated surface using an asphalt paver finisher.
    *   Pavers ensure uniform thickness, width, and initial leveling.
    *   The layer thickness is typically between 25 mm to 50 mm, depending on the pavement design.
*   **Rolling:**
    *   Rolling is the most critical stage after spreading. It must be performed immediately and in a systematic manner while the mix is still hot.
    *   **Breakdown Rolling:** Done by a heavy roller (8-10 tonne static or vibratory) to achieve initial compaction and remove large air voids.
    *   **Intermediate Rolling:** Performed with pneumatic tire rollers to achieve higher density and knit the mix together.
    *   **Finish Rolling:** Done by a lighter static roller (6-8 tonne) to achieve the final smooth and dense surface texture.
*   **Joint Construction:** Transverse and longitudinal joints must be constructed properly to ensure a continuous and sealed surface.

**7.2. Equipment Used:**

*   **Hot Mix Plant (HMP):** Essential for producing the dense-graded BC mix.
*   **Dump Trucks/Tippers (Insulated):** For transporting hot mix.
*   **Bitumen Sprayer:** For applying the tack coat.
*   **Asphalt Paver Finisher:** The primary equipment for spreading and leveling the BC layer.
*   **Rollers:**
    *   **Vibratory Rollers (Static/Dynamic):** For breakdown and compaction.
    *   **Pneumatic Tire Rollers:** For intermediate compaction and sealing.
    *   **Tandem Rollers (Static):** For final finishing.
*   **Hand Tools:** Lutes, rakes, shovels for working around paver and joint finishing.

**7.3. Quality Control Checks:**

*   **Material Testing:**
    *   **Aggregate Gradation:** For dense-graded mix (as per IRC/AASHTO specifications).
    *   **Aggregate Properties (AIV, FI, EI, Water Absorption):** Critical for durability and skid resistance.
    *   **Bitumen Grade and Softening Point:** Ensuring correct binder properties.
    *   **Filler Properties:**
    *   **Mix Properties (Stability, Flow, Void Content, Asphalt Content):** Laboratory tests on samples from the plant to verify the mix design.
    *   **Temperature of Mix:** Critical for workability and compaction. Should be within specified range (e.g., 150-170°C).
*   **Compaction Control:**
    *   **Field Density Tests:** To ensure the required degree of compaction (usually 95-97% of laboratory mix density).
    *   **Surface Smoothness:** Checked with a straightedge (maximum allowable deviation is usually 5 mm over 3 meters).
    *   **Surface Texture:** Visual inspection and sometimes specialized texture meters for skid resistance.
    *   **Temperature of Surface During Rolling:** Must remain within a workable range for effective compaction.
*   **Thickness Measurement:** Verifying the compacted thickness against design.
*   **Joint Quality:** Ensuring clean, square, and properly sealed joints.

**Important Point to Remember:** Bituminous Concrete is the final wearing surface. Its quality directly impacts ride comfort, safety, and the overall lifespan of the pavement. Attention to temperature, compaction, and joint construction is paramount.

### **Practice Questions and Answers**

**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of a Granular Sub-base (GSB) layer in a flexible pavement?
**Answer:** The primary purpose of a GSB layer is to provide a uniform and stable platform for the overlying base course, distribute wheel loads to the subgrade, and prevent pumping of finer materials from the subgrade.

**Question 2:** Which type of roller is most effective for compacting cohesive soils in the subgrade layer?
**Answer:** Sheepsfoot rollers are most effective for compacting cohesive soils in the subgrade layer.

**Question 3:** What is the critical parameter to control during the construction of Bituminous Macadam (BM) and Bituminous Concrete (BC)?
**Answer:** The critical parameter to control during the construction of BM and BC is the **temperature** of the mix during laying and rolling, as well as ensuring proper **compaction**.

**Question 4:** Why is a tack coat applied before laying the Bituminous Concrete (BC) layer?
**Answer:** A tack coat is applied to ensure good adhesion between the underlying layer (binder course or base course) and the new BC layer, preventing slippage and delamination.

**Question 5:** A field density test on a WMM layer shows a dry density of 2100 kg/m³ and the laboratory maximum dry density is 2250 kg/m³. What is the degree of compaction?
**Answer:**
Degree of Compaction = (Field Dry Density / Laboratory Maximum Dry Density) * 100
Degree of Compaction = (2100 kg/m³ / 2250 kg/m³) * 100 = 93.33%

*(Note: For WMM, typically a minimum of 97% is required, so this result would be unacceptable)*

### **Summary of Key Points to Remember:**

*   **Subgrade is the Foundation:** Any compromise in subgrade quality or compaction will affect the entire pavement.
*   **Layer Thickness:** Adherence to specified layer thicknesses during construction is crucial for load distribution.
*   **Compaction is Key:** Proper compaction of each layer is essential to achieve the desired strength, density, and to minimize future settlement.
*   **Moisture Control:** Maintaining the correct moisture content (near OMC) during the compaction of unbound layers (Subgrade, GSB, WBM, WMM) is vital.
*   **Temperature Control:** For bituminous layers (BM, BC), temperature control during mixing, transportation, laying, and rolling is critical.
*   **Cleanliness:** Each layer must be free from foreign materials before the next layer is placed.
*   **Adhesion:** Tack coats are essential for bonding bituminous layers.
*   **Smoothness and Profile:** The finished surface of each layer must be smooth and conform to the designed profile and cross-slope.
*   **Quality Control:** Rigorous and continuous quality control checks at every stage are non-negotiable for constructing a durable and safe flexible pavement.
