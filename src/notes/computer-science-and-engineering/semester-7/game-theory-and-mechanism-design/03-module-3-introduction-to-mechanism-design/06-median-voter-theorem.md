## 1. Core Concept of the Median Voter Theorem

The Median Voter Theorem is a fundamental result in social choice theory and political science that predicts the outcome of a majority-rule election where voters have single-peaked preferences on a one-dimensional policy space.

**Key Idea:** In a two-candidate election where voters vote sincerely for the candidate closest to their ideal policy position, the candidate who proposes the **median voter's ideal policy** will win a majority of the votes, regardless of the other candidate's proposal.

---

## 2. Conditions Under Which the MVT Holds

The MVT relies on a specific set of assumptions. If these assumptions are violated, the theorem's predictive power diminishes significantly.

### 2.1. One-Dimensional Policy Space

*   **Definition:** The set of possible policies or outcomes can be represented by a single continuous variable (e.g., the level of government spending, the tax rate, the position of a public good).
*   **Example:** If the issue is "how much should the government spend on education?", the policy space is a single dimension from low spending to high spending.
