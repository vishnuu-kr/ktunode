# BIOINFORMATICS: Module 2 - Biological Databases and Data Formats

## Topic: EMBL-Bank

### 1. Introduction to EMBL-Bank

*   **What is EMBL-Bank?**
    *   EMBL-Bank is the European Molecular Biology Laboratory's Nucleotide Sequence Database.
    *   It is one of the three major international nucleotide sequence databases, alongside GenBank (NCBI, USA) and DDBJ (DNA Data Bank of Japan).
    *   These three databases participate in the International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration (INSDC), which ensures that sequence data is freely and publicly available.

*   **Purpose:**
    *   To archive and make publicly accessible the vast amount of nucleotide sequence data generated by research worldwide.
    *   To provide a comprehensive and up-to-date repository of DNA and RNA sequences.

*   **Key Features:**
    *   **Global Collaboration:** Works with GenBank and DDBJ to share data daily, ensuring comprehensive coverage.
    *   **Primary Data:** Primarily contains primary sequence data submissions from researchers.
    *   **Annotation:** Data is annotated with information about the sequence's origin, function, experimental details, and related literature.
    *   **Diverse Data Types:** Includes genomic DNA, cDNA, mRNA, ESTs (Expressed Sequence Tags), GSSs (Genome Survey Sequences), and more.
    *   **International Focus:** While having a European origin, it serves a global scientific community.

### 2. Data Content and Structure

*   **Types of Sequences Stored:**
    *   **Genomic DNA:** Complete genomes, chromosomes, plasmids, organelle DNA.
    *   **cDNA (complementary DNA):** Derived from mRNA, representing transcribed regions (genes).
    *   **mRNA (messenger RNA):** Direct RNA sequences.
    *   **ESTs (Expressed Sequence Tags):** Short, single-pass sequences from cDNA libraries, used for gene discovery and expression profiling.
    *   **GSSs (Genome Survey Sequences):** Similar to ESTs but derived from genomic DNA, often used for genome mapping.
    *   **tRNA, rRNA, and other functional RNA sequences.**

*   **Submission Process:**
    *   Researchers submit their sequence data to EMBL-Bank (or GenBank/DDBJ).
    *   Submissions undergo a review process to ensure data quality and adherence to standards.
    *   Upon acceptance, sequences are assigned unique accession numbers.

*   **Accession Numbers:**
    *   Unique identifiers for each sequence record.
    *   Format: Typically a combination of letters and numbers (e.g., X12345, AM491002.1).
    *   The accession number is stable and is used to retrieve the sequence and its associated annotation.
    *   The `.1` often indicates the version number of the entry.

*   **Sequence Record Structure (EMBL Format):**
    *   While GenBank is often used as a common format, understanding the underlying EMBL structure is important. EMBL and GenBank formats are very similar due to INSDC collaboration.
    *   **Definition Line (DE):** A brief description of the sequence.
    *   **Accession Line (AC):** The unique accession number.
    *   **Version Line (VN):** The version number of the accession.
    *   **Keywords Line (KW):** Relevant keywords for searching.
    *   **Source Line (OS):** The biological source of the sequence (organism, tissue, etc.).
    *   **Organism Line (OG):** Taxonomic classification of the organism.
    *   **Reference Lines (RN, RA, RL, RT, RX):** Information about publications associated with the sequence.
    *   **Features Line (FH, FT, FF):** Describes the biological features within the sequence (genes, exons, introns, coding regions, etc.). This is a crucial part of the annotation.
        *   `CDS` (Coding Sequence): Region that codes for a protein.
        *   `gene`: The entire gene locus.
        *   `mRNA`: Mature messenger RNA transcript.
        *   `exon`, `intron`: Parts of a gene.
        *   `rRNA`, `tRNA`: Ribosomal and transfer RNA.
        *   `repeat_region`: Repetitive DNA sequences.
    *   **Sequence Data Line (SQ):** The actual nucleotide sequence itself.

### 3. Accessing and Retrieving Data

*   **EMBL-EBI Website:**
    *   The primary portal for accessing EMBL-Bank and other EMBL-EBI resources.
    *   Provides powerful search interfaces.

*   **Search Tools:**
    *   **Sequence Search:** Allows searching by accession number, gene name, organism, keywords, etc.
    *   **BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool):** A fundamental tool for comparing a query sequence against databases to find similar sequences. EMBL-EBI hosts BLAST services.
    *   **Genome Browsers:** Tools like Ensembl provide visual navigation and exploration of genomic data.

*   **Data Retrieval Formats:**
    *   **EMBL Format:** The native format, containing detailed annotation.
    *   **FASTA Format:** A simple and widely used format for sequences, containing only the sequence ID and the nucleotide sequence.
    *   **GenBank Format:** Very similar to EMBL format, often interchangeable.
    *   **XML Format:** Structured data for programmatic access.

### 4. Role in the Bioinformatics Workflow

*   **Foundation for Analysis:** EMBL-Bank provides the essential raw sequence data for many bioinformatics tasks.
*   **Gene Annotation:** Used to identify genes, predict protein-coding regions, and assign functions.
*   **Comparative Genomics:** Comparing sequences from different organisms to understand evolutionary relationships and identify conserved regions.
*   **Evolutionary Studies:** Tracing evolutionary history through sequence comparisons.
*   **Disease Research:** Identifying genes associated with diseases by comparing sequences from healthy and affected individuals.
*   **Drug Discovery:** Identifying potential drug targets based on gene and protein sequences.

### 6. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **EMBL-Bank:** European Molecular Biology Laboratory Nucleotide Sequence Database.
*   **INSDC (International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration):** Partnership between EMBL-Bank, GenBank, and DDBJ for data sharing.
*   **Accession Number:** Unique identifier for a sequence record.
*   **Annotation:** Descriptive information about a sequence, including its biological features, origin, and context.
*   **EST (Expressed Sequence Tag):** Short sequence from cDNA, representing gene expression.
*   **GSS (Genome Survey Sequence):** Short sequence from genomic DNA, used for mapping.
*   **FASTA Format:** Simple text-based format for sequences.
*   **EMBL Format:** Detailed annotation format for nucleotide sequences.
*   **BLAST:** Algorithm for sequence similarity searching.

### 7. Examples

*   **Accession Number Example:** `AM491002.1` might represent a submission of genomic DNA from *Homo sapiens*.
*   **Feature Example (in EMBL format snippet):**
    ```
    FT   gene            100..1500
    FT                   /gene="ABC1"
    FT   CDS             100..1200
    FT                   /codon_start=1
    FT                   /product="Hypothetical protein ABC1"
    ```
    This shows a gene named "ABC1" spanning bases 100 to 1500, with a coding sequence (CDS) from 100 to 1200, which starts at the first codon and codes for a hypothetical protein.

### 8. Practice Questions and Exercises

**Question 1:**
What are the three major international nucleotide sequence databases that collaborate under the INSDC?
a) EMBL-Bank, RefSeq, UniProt
b) GenBank, DDBJ, UniProt
c) EMBL-Bank, GenBank, DDBJ
d) RefSeq, DDBJ, UniProt

**Answer 1:**
c) EMBL-Bank, GenBank, DDBJ

**Question 2:**
Which of the following is NOT a type of data commonly found in EMBL-Bank?
a) Genomic DNA
b) Protein sequences
c) Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs)
d) Messenger RNA (mRNA)

**Answer 2:**
b) Protein sequences (While some protein-coding regions are described, the primary focus is nucleotide sequences. Protein databases like UniProt are dedicated to protein sequences.)

**Question 3:**
What is the primary purpose of an accession number in EMBL-Bank?
a) To provide a detailed description of the sequence's function.
b) To uniquely identify a sequence record for retrieval and citation.
c) To indicate the species from which the sequence was derived.
d) To specify the experimental method used for sequencing.

**Answer 3:**
b) To uniquely identify a sequence record for retrieval and citation.

**Question 4:**
Describe the significance of the "Features" section in an EMBL format sequence record.

**Answer 4:**
The "Features" section is crucial for understanding the biological significance of the nucleotide sequence. It describes various elements within the sequence, such as genes, coding regions (CDS), exons, introns, regulatory elements, and other functional or structural components. This annotation helps researchers interpret the sequence's role in the organism's biology.

**Question 5 (Exercise):**
Imagine you have a gene of interest and you want to find similar sequences in EMBL-Bank to study its evolutionary conservation. What tool would you most likely use, and what type of search would you perform?

**Answer 5:**
You would most likely use the **BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool)**. You would perform a **nucleotide BLAST (blastn)** search, using your gene sequence as the query against the EMBL-Bank database. This would identify sequences in EMBL-Bank that are similar to your query sequence.

### 9. Important Points to Remember

*   **EMBL-Bank is part of a global data sharing initiative (INSDC).**
*   **Accession numbers are stable identifiers.**
*   **The "Features" section is key to understanding biological context.**
*   **EMBL-Bank primarily stores nucleotide sequences.**
*   **Tools like BLAST are essential for data retrieval and analysis.**
*   **Data in EMBL-Bank is free and publicly available.**
