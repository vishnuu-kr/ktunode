# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - MODULE 2: COST CONCEPTS - OLIGOPOLY

## Topic: Oligopoly (Features and Equilibrium of a Firm)

**Learning Outcomes:**

*   Understand the characteristics of an oligopoly market structure.
*   Identify the different types of oligopoly models.
*   Analyze the factors influencing pricing and output decisions in an oligopoly.
*   Explain the concept of kinked demand curve and its significance in oligopoly.
*   Determine the short-run and long-run equilibrium of a firm in an oligopoly.
*   Discuss the potential benefits and drawbacks of oligopoly.

---

### 1. Introduction to Oligopoly

*   **Definition:** An oligopoly is a market structure in which a few firms dominate the industry. These firms have significant market power and their actions affect each other.
*   **Key Feature:**  Mutual interdependence - Firms must consider the actions and reactions of their rivals when making decisions about pricing, output, and advertising.

### 2. Characteristics of Oligopoly

*   **Few Sellers, Many Buyers:** A small number of firms control a large portion of the market.
    *   Example:  Airline industry, mobile phone service providers, automobile manufacturers.
*   **Homogeneous or Differentiated Products:** Products can be either identical (homogeneous) or differentiated.
    *   *Homogeneous Oligopoly:* Steel, cement (often price competition is intense).
    *   *Differentiated Oligopoly:* Automobiles, soft drinks (competition often focuses on branding and product features).
*   **High Barriers to Entry:** Significant obstacles prevent new firms from entering the market. This can include:
    *   *Economies of Scale:* Existing firms operate at lower costs due to large production volumes.
    *   *High Capital Requirements:*  Significant investment is needed to start a firm.
    *   *Control of Essential Resources:* One or a few firms control a vital resource.
    *   *Patents and Copyrights:*  Legal protection prevents others from copying products or processes.
    *   *Brand Loyalty:*  Established brands have a strong following.
    *   *Government Regulations:* Licenses, permits, or other regulations may limit entry.
*   **Interdependence:**  Firms are highly interdependent and must consider rivals' reactions when making decisions.
*   **Non-Price Competition:**  Firms often engage in advertising, product differentiation, and quality improvements to attract customers, rather than solely relying on price cuts.

### 3. Types of Oligopoly Models

Several models attempt to explain the behavior of firms in an oligopoly. The main difference lies in how firms are assumed to react to each other.

*   **Cournot Model:**
    *   Firms independently choose their output levels, assuming that their rivals' output remains constant.
    *   Results in a stable equilibrium output level, somewhere between perfect competition and monopoly.
    *   Example:  Two mineral water companies deciding how much water to extract from a source.
*   **Bertrand Model:**
    *   Firms independently choose their prices, assuming that their rivals' prices remain constant.
    *   Leads to a price war and prices tend to fall to the level of marginal cost (similar to perfect competition) if products are homogeneous.
    *   Example: Two gas stations on opposite corners deciding their price, in hopes of attracting customers.
*   **Stackelberg Model:**
    *   One firm (the leader) sets its output level first, and the other firm (the follower) reacts to the leader's output.
    *   The leader has an advantage and can achieve a higher profit.
    *   Example: A dominant company in the semi-conductor industry announces new technology, other smaller competitors adjust.
*   **Sweezy Model (Kinked Demand Curve):**
    *   Explains price rigidity in oligopoly markets.  Firms believe that if they raise their price, competitors will not follow, but if they lower their price, competitors will match the price cut.
    *   Results in a kinked demand curve and a discontinuous marginal revenue curve.

### 4. The Kinked Demand Curve Model (Sweezy Model)

*   **Assumptions:**
    *   If a firm raises its price, its rivals will not follow, and the firm will lose a significant portion of its market share (demand is elastic above the current price).
    *   If a firm lowers its price, its rivals will match the price cut to maintain their market share (demand is inelastic below the current price).
*   **The Kink:** The demand curve is kinked at the current market price.
*   **Marginal Revenue:** The marginal revenue curve is discontinuous (has a gap) at the level of output corresponding to the kink.
*   **Price Rigidity:**  Firms are reluctant to change prices because they believe that they will lose profits if they do so.  Cost fluctuations can occur within the discontinuous MR curve without influencing price.

### 5. Factors Influencing Pricing and Output Decisions

*   **Cost Structures:** Firms consider their production costs (fixed and variable) when determining output levels and prices.
*   **Market Demand:** The overall demand for the product influences the price that firms can charge.
*   **Rivals' Behavior:** The most crucial factor.  Firms must anticipate how their rivals will react to their decisions.
*   **Collusion:**  Firms may collude (form a cartel) to restrict output and raise prices. This is illegal in many countries but may still occur tacitly (implied collusion).
    *   Example:  OPEC (Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries) attempts to control oil production and prices.
*   **Government Regulations:**  Antitrust laws and regulations can affect pricing and output decisions.

### 6. Equilibrium of a Firm in Oligopoly

*   **Short-Run Equilibrium:**
    *   A firm in an oligopoly maximizes its profits by producing the output level where marginal cost (MC) equals marginal revenue (MR).
    *   The specific output level and price depend on the particular oligopoly model (Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg, etc.).
    *   Unlike perfect competition, price is not necessarily equal to marginal cost.
    *   The firm can earn positive economic profits, losses, or break even in the short run.
*   **Long-Run Equilibrium:**
    *   There is no single, well-defined long-run equilibrium for an oligopoly.  The outcome depends on:
        *   Barriers to entry: If barriers are high, firms can maintain long-run economic profits.
        *   Potential for collusion: Collusion can lead to higher profits in the long run.
        *   Strategic interaction: Continuous strategic interaction between firms makes the long-run outcome uncertain.
    *   Entry of new firms is possible in the long run if barriers aren't perfectly impenetrable which reduces the profit earning ability of the existing firms.

### 7. Benefits and Drawbacks of Oligopoly

*   **Potential Benefits:**
    *   **Economies of Scale:**  Large firms can achieve lower costs due to economies of scale.
    *   **Innovation:**  Firms may invest in research and development to gain a competitive edge.
    *   **Product Variety:**  Differentiated oligopolies can offer consumers a wider range of product choices.
*   **Potential Drawbacks:**
    *   **Higher Prices and Lower Output:**  Compared to perfect competition, oligopolies may charge higher prices and produce less output.
    *   **Reduced Competition:**  Limited competition can lead to inefficiency and a lack of innovation.
    *   **Collusion:**  Collusion can harm consumers by leading to higher prices and reduced choice.
    *   **Rent-Seeking Behavior:** Firms may spend resources on lobbying and other activities to protect their market power rather than focusing on improving efficiency.

### 8. Important Points to Remember

*   Oligopoly is characterized by mutual interdependence among firms.
*   The Kinked Demand Curve model explains price rigidity in oligopoly.
*   The equilibrium of a firm in an oligopoly depends on the specific model used and the behavior of rival firms.
*   Oligopolies can have both benefits and drawbacks for consumers and society.

### Practice Questions/Exercises

**1. Explain the concept of mutual interdependence in oligopoly. Give an example of how one firm's actions can affect its competitors.**

*   **Answer:** Mutual interdependence in oligopoly means that each firm's decisions significantly impact the other firms in the market.  A firm cannot make pricing or output decisions without considering how its rivals will react. For example, if one airline reduces its fares on a particular route, other airlines will likely follow suit to maintain their market share, resulting in a price war.

**2. What are the main barriers to entry in an oligopoly? Provide specific examples.**

*   **Answer:** Main barriers to entry include:
    *   *Economies of Scale:* Existing firms produce at such a massive scale they have drastically lower per unit costs than a potential entrant.  Example: Automobile manufacturing.
    *   *High Capital Requirements:*  Starting a business requires a huge initial investment. Example:  Telecommunications infrastructure.
    *   *Control of Essential Resources:* A single or small number of firms control essential raw materials. Example: De Beers (historically) controlling diamond supply.
    *   *Patents and Copyrights:* Exclusive rights to technology or products. Example: Pharmaceutical companies with patented drugs.
    *   *Brand Loyalty:* Customers are very loyal to established brands. Example:  Coca-Cola vs. a new soft drink brand.
    *   *Government Regulations:* Licenses and permits required to operate are difficult to obtain. Example:  Broadcasting licenses.

**3. Explain the Kinked Demand Curve model. Why does it predict price rigidity?**

*   **Answer:**  The Kinked Demand Curve model explains price rigidity in oligopoly. It assumes that if a firm raises its price, its rivals will not follow (demand is elastic above the current price), but if a firm lowers its price, its rivals will match the cut (demand is inelastic below the current price).  This creates a kink in the demand curve at the current price and a discontinuity in the marginal revenue curve.  Because of this discontinuity, changes in marginal cost within a certain range will not lead to changes in the profit-maximizing price and output.

**4. What is collusion? What are the potential consequences of collusion for consumers?**

*   **Answer:** Collusion is an agreement between firms to restrict output, raise prices, and/or divide the market. It is essentially firms acting as a monopoly. The potential consequences of collusion for consumers are:
    *   Higher prices
    *   Reduced output
    *   Less choice
    *   Reduced innovation (lack of competition)

**5. Compare and contrast the Cournot and Bertrand models of oligopoly.**

*   **Answer:**
    *   **Cournot:** Firms compete by choosing output levels independently, assuming their rivals' output remains constant. Leads to a stable equilibrium output level between perfect competition and monopoly.
    *   **Bertrand:** Firms compete by choosing prices independently, assuming their rivals' prices remain constant. Leads to prices falling to the level of marginal cost (similar to perfect competition) if products are homogeneous.

    *   **Key Difference:** Cournot focuses on quantity competition, while Bertrand focuses on price competition.

**6.  A small town has only two internet service providers.  What type of market structure is this?  What factors might determine the price they charge for internet service?**

*   **Answer:**  This is an oligopoly market structure, since there are a small number of firms (two) dominating the market.  Factors that might determine the price they charge:
    *   Their individual costs of providing the service (infrastructure, customer support, etc.)
    *   The perceived quality and reliability of their service
    *   Whether they engage in any form of implicit or explicit collusion (avoiding price wars)
    *   Government regulation (if any)
    *   The availability and price of alternative technologies (e.g., satellite internet) - even if less desirable, it provides *some* constraint on their pricing.
