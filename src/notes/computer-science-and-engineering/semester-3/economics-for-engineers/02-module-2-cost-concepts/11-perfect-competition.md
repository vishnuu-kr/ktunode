# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 2: Cost Concepts - Perfect Competition

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Define Perfect Competition and its characteristics.
*   Explain the implications of perfect competition for individual firms.
*   Describe how price and output are determined under perfect competition in the short run.
*   Describe how price and output are determined under perfect competition in the long run.
*   Understand the concept of economic efficiency in perfect competition.

## 1. Introduction to Perfect Competition

*   **Definition:** Perfect competition is a market structure characterized by a large number of buyers and sellers, homogeneous (identical) products, free entry and exit, and perfect information.  No single buyer or seller can influence the market price.

## 2. Characteristics of Perfect Competition

*   **Large Number of Buyers and Sellers:**
    *   No single buyer or seller is large enough to influence the market price.
    *   Each participant is a "price taker," meaning they must accept the prevailing market price.
*   **Homogeneous Product:**
    *   All firms sell identical products.  There is no product differentiation.
    *   Consumers perceive no difference between the goods or services offered by different firms.
    *   Examples (in theory): Basic agricultural commodities like wheat or corn (though even these have branding and quality differences in reality).
*   **Free Entry and Exit:**
    *   Firms can freely enter or exit the market without significant barriers (e.g., high start-up costs, government regulations, patents).
    *   This ensures that profits and losses are temporary, and resources are allocated efficiently.
*   **Perfect Information:**
    *   All buyers and sellers have complete and accurate information about prices, costs, and product quality.
    *   This ensures that resources are allocated efficiently.
    *   Consumers know where to find the lowest prices.
    *   Firms know the latest production techniques and technologies.
*   **No Government Intervention:**  (Often included as an assumption)
    *   The government does not intervene with price controls, subsidies, or other regulations that would distort the market.
*   **No Collusion:**
    *   Firms act independently and do not collude to fix prices or restrict output.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Price Taker:** A firm in a perfectly competitive market has no power to influence the market price; it must accept the prevailing market price.
*   **Homogeneous Product:** Identical goods or services offered by different firms.
*   **Free Entry and Exit:**  The ability of firms to easily enter or leave a market without significant barriers.

## 3. Implications of Perfect Competition for Individual Firms

*   **Demand Curve for an Individual Firm:** Because the firm is a price taker, its demand curve is perfectly elastic (horizontal) at the market price. This means the firm can sell as much as it wants at the market price but will sell nothing if it tries to charge even slightly more.
*   **Marginal Revenue (MR):**  For a perfectly competitive firm, marginal revenue is equal to the market price (MR = P). This is because each additional unit sold generates revenue equal to the market price.
*   **Average Revenue (AR):** Average revenue is also equal to the market price (AR = P).
*   **Profit Maximization:** Firms maximize profit by producing the quantity where marginal cost (MC) equals marginal revenue (MR). Since MR = P, the profit-maximizing condition is MC = P.

**Example:**

Suppose the market price of wheat is $5 per bushel. A wheat farmer under perfect competition can sell any quantity of wheat at $5 per bushel. Therefore, the farmer's marginal revenue (MR) is $5 per bushel. The farmer will produce the quantity of wheat where their marginal cost (MC) of producing an additional bushel equals $5.

## 4. Short-Run Equilibrium in Perfect Competition

*   **Firm's Supply Curve:** The firm's short-run supply curve is its marginal cost (MC) curve above the average variable cost (AVC) curve. This is because the firm will only produce if the price is high enough to cover its variable costs.
*   **Market Supply Curve:** The market supply curve is the horizontal summation of all individual firms' supply curves.
*   **Market Equilibrium:**  The market equilibrium price and quantity are determined by the intersection of the market supply and market demand curves.
*   **Possible Outcomes for Firms in the Short-Run:**
    *   **Economic Profit:** If the market price is above the firm's average total cost (ATC), the firm earns an economic profit.
    *   **Economic Loss:** If the market price is below the firm's average total cost (ATC) but above the average variable cost (AVC), the firm incurs an economic loss but continues to operate in the short run to minimize losses.
    *   **Shutdown:** If the market price is below the firm's average variable cost (AVC), the firm will shut down production in the short run because it cannot even cover its variable costs.

## 5. Long-Run Equilibrium in Perfect Competition

*   **Entry and Exit:** The presence of economic profits in the short run attracts new firms to enter the market. The presence of economic losses causes existing firms to exit the market.
*   **Effect on Market Supply:** Entry of new firms increases market supply, which drives down the market price. Exit of firms decreases market supply, which drives up the market price.
*   **Long-Run Equilibrium Condition:** In the long run, the market price will adjust until firms earn zero economic profit. This occurs when the market price is equal to the minimum point of the firm's average total cost (ATC) curve.
*   **Long-Run Supply Curve:** In a constant-cost industry (where input prices do not change as the industry expands or contracts), the long-run supply curve is perfectly elastic (horizontal) at the minimum point of the ATC curve.

**Key Concept:**

*   **Zero Economic Profit:** A situation where a firm earns a normal rate of return on its investment but no additional profit.  This is sufficient to keep the firm in the industry.

**Example:**

If wheat farmers are making economic profits, new farmers will enter the market. This will increase the supply of wheat, driving down the price. This continues until the price is just high enough to cover the average total cost of production, leading to zero economic profit.

## 6. Economic Efficiency in Perfect Competition

*   **Allocative Efficiency:** Perfect competition achieves allocative efficiency because the market price reflects the marginal cost of production (P = MC). This means that resources are allocated to their most valued uses, and society's welfare is maximized.  Consumers pay a price that accurately reflects the cost of producing the good.
*   **Productive Efficiency:** In the long run, firms produce at the minimum point of their average total cost (ATC) curve. This means that they are producing goods at the lowest possible cost.
*   **Consumer Surplus and Producer Surplus:**  Perfect competition maximizes the sum of consumer surplus (the difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay) and producer surplus (the difference between what producers receive and their minimum supply price).

**Key Concept:**

*   **Allocative Efficiency:**  A situation where resources are allocated to their most valued uses from society's perspective.
*   **Productive Efficiency:** A situation where goods and services are produced at the lowest possible cost.

## 7. Important Points to Remember

*   Perfect competition is a theoretical ideal that is rarely observed in its pure form in the real world.
*   It serves as a benchmark for evaluating the efficiency of other market structures.
*   The assumption of perfect information is often violated in practice.
*   Despite its limitations, the model of perfect competition provides valuable insights into how markets work and how prices are determined.

## 8. Practice Questions & Exercises

1.  **Describe the four main characteristics of perfect competition. Give a brief explanation of each.**

    *   **Answer:**
        *   *Large Number of Buyers and Sellers:* No individual buyer or seller has significant influence on the market price.
        *   *Homogeneous Product:* All firms sell identical products, and consumers perceive no difference.
        *   *Free Entry and Exit:* Firms can easily enter or leave the market without significant barriers.
        *   *Perfect Information:* All buyers and sellers have complete and accurate information.

2.  **Explain why a firm in a perfectly competitive market is a "price taker."**

    *   **Answer:** A firm is a price taker because it is a small part of the overall market. Its output is insignificant relative to the total market supply, so it cannot influence the market price by changing its output. If it tries to charge more than the market price, it will sell nothing because consumers can buy the same product from other firms at the market price.

3.  **Why is the demand curve faced by an individual firm under perfect competition perfectly elastic? What are the implications of this?**

    *   **Answer:** The demand curve is perfectly elastic because the firm can sell as much as it wants at the market price.  The implication is that the firm's marginal revenue (MR) is equal to the market price (P), and the firm maximizes profits by producing where MC = P.

4.  **Explain how the entry and exit of firms ensure that economic profits are eliminated in the long run in a perfectly competitive market.**

    *   **Answer:** If firms are earning economic profits, new firms will be attracted to enter the market. This increases the market supply, which drives down the market price. This process continues until the price is just high enough to cover the average total cost of production, leading to zero economic profit. Conversely, if firms are experiencing economic losses, some firms will exit the market. This decreases the market supply, which drives up the market price. This process continues until the price is just high enough to cover the average total cost of production, leading to zero economic profit.

5.  **A perfectly competitive firm has the following cost information: Fixed Costs = $50, Variable Costs = $100 when producing 10 units, $160 when producing 12 units, and $230 when producing 14 units. If the market price is $15 per unit, what output level will the firm choose to maximize profits, and what will its profits be?**

    *   **Answer:**  First, calculate the Marginal Cost (MC) for each level of output.
        *   MC (11th & 12th unit) = ($160 - $100) / (12-10) = $60/2 = $30
        *   MC (13th & 14th unit) = ($230 - $160) / (14-12) = $70/2 = $35
        *   We want to find when the Marginal Cost is closest to the Price ($15). Since the Marginal cost increases continuously as output increase, let's calculate the marginal cost for previous unit and see if it gets closer to the price.
        *   VC (8 units) = We don't know. However, we know VC(10) = 100. So lets *approximate* VC(8) = $40 (This value is such that the MC is below $15 and is closest to it). This will yield MC(9th and 10th Unit) = (100-40)/(10-8) = $30
        *   Since the price ($15) will be close to an output level somewhere between 8 and 10, we will assume that the firm doesn't produce since MC is greater than P.  **The best choice is to produce zero output.**

        *   If the firm produces zero output, its Profit = TR - TC = 0 - 50 = -$50 (a loss).  Any other production quantity would yield higher loss.

6.  **Explain allocative and productive efficiency and how these are achieved in a perfectly competitive market.**

    *   **Answer:**
        *   *Allocative Efficiency:* Resources are allocated to their most valued uses from society's perspective.  Perfect competition achieves this because P = MC. This means consumers are paying a price that reflects the cost of producing the last unit, and no resources are wasted on producing goods that are not valued by consumers.
        *   *Productive Efficiency:* Goods and services are produced at the lowest possible cost. In the long run, firms in a perfectly competitive market produce at the minimum point of their ATC curve, ensuring productive efficiency. They are forced to minimize costs to survive the intense competition.
