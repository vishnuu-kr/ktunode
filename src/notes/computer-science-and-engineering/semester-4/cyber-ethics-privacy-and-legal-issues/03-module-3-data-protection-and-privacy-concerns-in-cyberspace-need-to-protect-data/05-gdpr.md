## CYBER ETHICS, PRIVACY AND LEGAL ISSUES: Module 3 - GDPR

**Module:** Module 3: Data Protection and Privacy Concerns in Cyberspace : Need to protect data in cyberspace
**Topic:** GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

**Introduction:**

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is a landmark piece of legislation designed to protect the data privacy of individuals within the European Union (EU) and the European Economic Area (EEA).  Even businesses outside the EU/EEA must comply if they process the data of individuals residing in those regions.  Understanding GDPR is crucial for anyone involved in processing personal data, as non-compliance can lead to significant penalties. This module delves into the core principles, key articles, and implications of GDPR.

**Learning Outcomes:**

*   Understand the core principles of GDPR and its scope.
*   Identify the key roles and responsibilities under GDPR (Data Controller, Data Processor, Data Protection Officer).
*   Explain the rights of data subjects under GDPR.
*   Understand the requirements for lawful processing of personal data.
*   Recognize the implications of GDPR for organizations and individuals.
*   Describe the penalties for non-compliance with GDPR.
*   Understand the importance of data security measures under GDPR.

**1. Core Principles of GDPR and Scope:**

*   **Key Concept: Personal Data:**  Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural or social identity of that natural person.
    *   **Examples:** Name, email address, IP address, photographs, bank details, medical information, social media posts.
*   **Key Concept: Processing:** Any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organisation, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.
*   **Scope of GDPR:**  Applies to:
    *   Organizations established in the EU, regardless of where the data processing takes place.
    *   Organizations *not* established in the EU, if they process personal data of EU residents in relation to:
        *   Offering goods or services to EU residents (even if free).
        *   Monitoring the behavior of EU residents (e.g., tracking their online activity).

*   **Core Principles (Article 5):**
    *   **Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency:** Data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject.
    *   **Purpose Limitation:** Data must be collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes.
        *   **Example:** Collecting email addresses for newsletter subscriptions only, not for selling to third parties.
    *   **Data Minimization:** Data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.
        *   **Example:** Not requiring someone's marital status for a simple product order.
    *   **Accuracy:** Data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate, having regard to the purposes for which they are processed, are erased or rectified without delay.
    *   **Storage Limitation:** Data must be kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed.
    *   **Integrity and Confidentiality (Security):** Data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures.
    *   **Accountability:** The controller shall be responsible for, and be able to demonstrate compliance with, the principles relating to processing of personal data.  This is a key principle requiring documentation and processes to prove adherence to GDPR.

**2. Key Roles and Responsibilities Under GDPR:**

*   **Data Controller:** The entity that determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data.  Essentially, they decide *why* and *how* personal data is processed.
    *   **Example:** A company deciding to collect customer data for marketing purposes.
*   **Data Processor:** An entity that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.  They act on the controller's instructions.
    *   **Example:** A cloud storage provider storing customer data for a company.
*   **Data Protection Officer (DPO):**  An expert in data protection law and practices.  DPOs are mandatory for certain organizations, including:
    *   Public authorities (except for courts acting in their judicial capacity).
    *   Organizations whose core activities consist of processing operations that require regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale.
    *   Organizations whose core activities consist of processing on a large scale of special categories of data (e.g., health data) or data relating to criminal convictions and offences.

    *   **Responsibilities of a DPO:**
        *   Advising the organization and its employees about their obligations under data protection law.
        *   Monitoring compliance with data protection law.
        *   Providing advice regarding data protection impact assessments (DPIAs).
        *   Acting as a contact point for the supervisory authority.
        *   Cooperating with the supervisory authority.

**3. Rights of Data Subjects Under GDPR:**

*   **Right to be Informed (Articles 13 & 14):**  Individuals have the right to be informed about the collection and use of their personal data.  This information must be provided in a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language.  This includes things like the purpose of processing, data retention periods, and who the data will be shared with.
*   **Right of Access (Article 15):** Individuals have the right to obtain confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed, and, where that is the case, access to the personal data and certain information about the processing.
*   **Right to Rectification (Article 16):** Individuals have the right to have inaccurate personal data concerning them rectified, and to have incomplete personal data completed.
*   **Right to Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten") (Article 17):** Individuals have the right to have their personal data erased in certain circumstances, such as when the data is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, or when they withdraw their consent.  However, this right is not absolute and there are exceptions (e.g., data is needed for compliance with legal obligations).
*   **Right to Restriction of Processing (Article 18):** Individuals have the right to restrict the processing of their personal data in certain circumstances, such as when they contest the accuracy of the data, or when the processing is unlawful but they oppose erasure.
*   **Right to Data Portability (Article 20):** Individuals have the right to receive their personal data in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and have the right to transmit those data to another controller without hindrance from the controller to which the personal data have been provided.
*   **Right to Object (Article 21):** Individuals have the right to object to the processing of their personal data in certain circumstances, including processing for direct marketing purposes.
*   **Rights in relation to automated decision making and profiling (Article 22):** Individuals have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects concerning them or significantly affects them.  There are exceptions (e.g., the decision is necessary for entering into, or performance of, a contract), but safeguards must be in place.

**4. Requirements for Lawful Processing of Personal Data (Article 6):**

Personal data can only be processed if at least one of the following conditions is met:

*   **Consent:** The data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes.  Consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous.  It must also be easily withdrawn.
    *   **Example:** Ticking a box to agree to receive marketing emails.
*   **Contract:** Processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract.
    *   **Example:** Processing payment details to fulfill an order.
*   **Legal Obligation:** Processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject.
    *   **Example:** Providing tax information to the government.
*   **Vital Interests:** Processing is necessary in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person.
    *   **Example:** Sharing medical information in an emergency situation.
*   **Public Interest:** Processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller.
*   **Legitimate Interests:** Processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular where the data subject is a child.  This is a balancing test.

**5. Implications of GDPR for Organizations and Individuals:**

*   **For Organizations:**
    *   Increased compliance costs (legal, technical, operational).
    *   Need for robust data protection policies and procedures.
    *   Requirement for data protection impact assessments (DPIAs) for high-risk processing activities.
    *   Potential for significant fines for non-compliance.
    *   Increased accountability and transparency.
    *   Improved data governance and security.
    *   Potential reputational damage from data breaches.
*   **For Individuals:**
    *   Greater control over their personal data.
    *   Increased transparency about how their data is being used.
    *   Improved protection against misuse of their data.
    *   Ability to exercise their data rights.
    *   Increased awareness of data privacy issues.

**6. Penalties for Non-Compliance with GDPR (Article 83):**

*   GDPR imposes substantial fines for violations, up to:
    *   €20 million, or 4% of the organization's global annual turnover of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher.
    *   Lower tiers of fines exist for less severe infringements.

*   Factors considered when determining the amount of the fine:
    *   Nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement.
    *   Intentional or negligent character of the infringement.
    *   Actions taken to mitigate the damage suffered by data subjects.
    *   Degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority.
    *   Categories of personal data affected by the infringement.

**7. Importance of Data Security Measures Under GDPR (Article 32):**

*   Data controllers and processors must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk.  This includes:
    *   **Pseudonymisation and encryption of personal data.**
    *   **The ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience of processing systems and services.**
    *   **The ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident.**
    *   **A process for regularly testing, assessing and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures for ensuring the security of the processing.**
*   Data breach notification requirements:  Controllers must notify the supervisory authority of a data breach without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.  Data subjects must also be notified if the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms.

**Important Points to Remember:**

*   GDPR applies broadly to any organization processing the personal data of EU residents.
*   Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous.
*   Organizations must be transparent about how they use personal data.
*   Individuals have significant rights regarding their personal data.
*   Data security is paramount.
*   Non-compliance can result in substantial penalties.
*   Documentation and accountability are crucial for demonstrating compliance.

**Practice Questions/Exercises:**

1.  **Scenario:** A US-based company sells products online.  They ship products to EU residents.  Do they need to comply with GDPR? Why or why not?
    *   **Answer:** Yes, they need to comply with GDPR.  The company is offering goods to EU residents, which triggers the GDPR's extraterritorial scope.

2.  **Define the roles of Data Controller and Data Processor. Give an example of each in the context of a social media platform.**
    *   **Answer:**
        *   **Data Controller:**  The entity that determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data.  On a social media platform, the social media company itself is the data controller. They decide why and how user data is collected and used (e.g., for targeted advertising, content personalization).
        *   **Data Processor:**  An entity that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.  A data processor for a social media platform could be a cloud storage provider where user data is stored, or an analytics company that processes user data to provide insights to the platform.

3.  **List three rights that data subjects have under GDPR and explain briefly what they mean.**
    *   **Answer:**
        *   **Right to Access:** Individuals have the right to know what personal data an organization holds about them and to receive a copy of that data.
        *   **Right to Erasure (Right to be Forgotten):** Individuals have the right to have their personal data erased in certain circumstances.
        *   **Right to Rectification:** Individuals have the right to have inaccurate personal data corrected.

4.  **An organization wants to process personal data based on "legitimate interests." What do they need to consider before doing so?**
    *   **Answer:**  They need to conduct a balancing test.  They must assess their legitimate interests against the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  They need to consider whether their interests are overridden by the data subjects' rights, particularly if the data subjects are children.  Documentation of this assessment is critical.

5.  **What is a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) and when is it required?**
    *   **Answer:**  A DPIA is a process to systematically analyze and evaluate the potential privacy risks associated with a proposed data processing activity. It is required when the processing is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. Examples include large-scale processing of sensitive data or systematic monitoring of individuals.

6. **What are the possible penalities for data breaches?**
     * **Answer:** Penalties can be up to €20 million, or 4% of the organization's global annual turnover of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher.  Lower tiers of fines exist for less severe infringements.
