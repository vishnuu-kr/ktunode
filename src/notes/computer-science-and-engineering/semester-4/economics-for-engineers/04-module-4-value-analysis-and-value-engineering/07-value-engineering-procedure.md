# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - MODULE 4: VALUE ANALYSIS & VALUE ENGINEERING - VALUE ENGINEERING PROCEDURE

## Learning Outcomes:

Upon completion of this topic, you will be able to:

*   Understand the systematic Value Engineering (VE) procedure.
*   Identify the different phases within the VE procedure.
*   Describe the purpose and activities involved in each phase.
*   Apply the VE procedure to solve engineering problems.
*   Appreciate the benefits of utilizing the VE procedure.

## 1. Introduction to the Value Engineering Procedure

Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic, function-oriented approach used to improve the value of a product, project, system, or service. The Value Engineering procedure is a structured process designed to identify and eliminate unnecessary costs while maintaining or improving performance, reliability, quality, safety, and customer satisfaction.  The ultimate goal is to achieve the required functions at the lowest total cost of ownership.

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Value:** The ratio of function to cost (Value = Function/Cost). Improving value can be achieved by improving function, reducing cost, or both.
*   **Function:** The primary purpose or requirement of a product, project, or service. It's what the item *does*, not what it *is*.
*   **Cost:** The total cost associated with an item, including design, manufacturing, installation, operation, and maintenance.
*   **Unnecessary Cost:** Costs that do not contribute to the required function or performance.

## 2. The Value Engineering Procedure - Phases

The VE procedure typically follows a structured sequence of phases. While the specific number and names of phases may vary slightly depending on the industry or organization, the core principles remain consistent. A common model involves the following phases:

**2.1. Information Phase:**

*   **Purpose:** To gather and analyze all relevant information about the project, product, or service being studied.
*   **Activities:**
    *   **Gather data:** Collect drawings, specifications, cost data, production information, sales figures, maintenance records, and customer feedback.
    *   **Understand requirements:** Clearly define the performance, reliability, and quality requirements.
    *   **Identify functions:** Determine the essential functions of the product/project using a function analysis system technique (FAST) diagram (more on this later if that's covered elsewhere in the module).  Use verbs and nouns to describe functions (e.g., "Support Load," "Maintain Pressure").
    *   **Determine costs:** Identify the cost associated with each function or component.
    *   **Identify areas of high cost:** Pinpoint areas where the cost is disproportionately high compared to the function provided.  These are potential targets for improvement.
*   **Example:** In designing a bridge, this phase would involve collecting data on traffic volume, soil conditions, environmental constraints, construction costs, maintenance costs, and required load-bearing capacity. Functions identified might include "Support Weight," "Resist Wind," and "Enable Passage."

**2.2. Function Analysis Phase:**

*   **Purpose:** To rigorously analyze the functions identified in the previous phase and determine their importance.
*   **Activities:**
    *   **Develop a Function Analysis System Technique (FAST) diagram (if covered elsewhere):**  A visual representation of the relationships between functions. FAST helps to identify the basic function (the essential reason the item exists) and secondary functions.
    *   **Classify functions:** Categorize functions as basic, secondary, or support functions.
    *   **Evaluate function worth:**  Determine the value (importance) of each function.
    *   **Compare cost and worth:**  Identify functions that have a high cost relative to their worth.  These are potential candidates for value improvement.
*   **Example:** Using the bridge example, this phase might identify "Support Weight" as the basic function and "Prevent Corrosion" as a secondary function. Analyzing costs might reveal that the corrosion prevention system constitutes a significant portion of the overall cost.

**2.3. Creative Phase (Speculation/Idea Generation):**

*   **Purpose:** To generate a wide range of potential alternative solutions that can perform the required functions.
*   **Activities:**
    *   **Brainstorming:** Generate as many ideas as possible, without judging or criticizing them.  Encourage creative thinking and out-of-the-box solutions.
    *   **Checklist application:** Use checklists of common value engineering ideas (e.g., simplification, standardization, material substitution).
    *   **Lateral thinking:** Explore unconventional approaches and consider different perspectives.
*   **Example:** For the bridge corrosion prevention system, brainstorming might generate ideas such as alternative coating materials, cathodic protection systems, or different structural designs that minimize exposure to corrosive elements.

**2.4. Evaluation Phase (Analysis/Judgment):**

*   **Purpose:** To evaluate the feasibility, cost-effectiveness, and potential benefits of the alternative solutions generated in the creative phase.
*   **Activities:**
    *   **Refine and combine ideas:** Group similar ideas and refine promising concepts.
    *   **Technical evaluation:** Assess the technical feasibility of each alternative.
    *   **Cost estimation:** Estimate the cost of implementing each alternative.
    *   **Risk assessment:** Identify potential risks associated with each alternative.
    *   **Prioritization:** Rank the alternatives based on their potential for value improvement.
*   **Example:** The alternative corrosion prevention methods are evaluated based on their effectiveness, cost, ease of installation, and environmental impact.  The most promising options are selected for further development.

**2.5. Development Phase (Recommendation):**

*   **Purpose:** To develop the selected alternatives into detailed proposals with supporting documentation.
*   **Activities:**
    *   **Develop detailed designs:** Create detailed drawings, specifications, and cost estimates for the recommended alternatives.
    *   **Prepare a proposal:** Document the proposed changes, including their cost savings, performance improvements, and potential risks.
    *   **Justify the proposal:** Provide a clear and convincing rationale for why the proposed changes should be implemented.
*   **Example:** A detailed design and cost estimate are prepared for the chosen corrosion prevention system, along with a report outlining the potential cost savings and improved longevity of the bridge.

**2.6. Implementation Phase:**

*   **Purpose:** To implement the recommended changes and monitor their performance.
*   **Activities:**
    *   **Obtain approval:** Secure approval from relevant stakeholders (e.g., management, clients).
    *   **Implement the changes:** Put the changes into effect.
    *   **Monitor performance:** Track the cost savings and performance improvements achieved.
    *   **Document the results:** Record the outcomes of the VE study and implementation process.
*   **Example:** The new corrosion prevention system is installed during the bridge construction or renovation process. The performance of the system is monitored over time to ensure its effectiveness.

**2.7. Reporting Phase:**

*   **Purpose:** Document the entire VE process, including the findings, recommendations, and results. This is essential for knowledge sharing and future VE studies.
*   **Activities:**
    *   Compile a comprehensive report covering all phases of the VE study.
    *   Share the report with stakeholders.
    *   Maintain records for future reference.

## 3. Benefits of Utilizing the VE Procedure

*   **Cost Reduction:** Eliminates unnecessary costs without sacrificing performance.
*   **Improved Performance:** Enhances the functionality, reliability, and quality of products and services.
*   **Increased Value:** Maximizes the value delivered to customers.
*   **Enhanced Innovation:** Encourages creative thinking and problem-solving.
*   **Better Decision-Making:** Provides a structured framework for evaluating alternative solutions.
*   **Improved Communication:** Fosters collaboration and communication among team members.

## 4. Examples of Value Engineering Application

*   **Product Design:** Simplifying the design of a product to reduce manufacturing costs.
*   **Construction Projects:** Substituting materials to reduce construction costs without compromising structural integrity.
*   **Service Delivery:** Streamlining processes to improve efficiency and reduce delivery time.

## 5. Important Points to Remember

*   Value Engineering is a *systematic* and *function-oriented* approach.
*   The *function* of an item is its primary purpose.
*   *Cost* includes all expenses associated with an item throughout its lifecycle.
*   The VE procedure involves a series of distinct *phases*.
*   *Teamwork* is essential for successful Value Engineering.
*   Continuous improvement is a key principle of Value Engineering.

## 6. Practice Questions/Exercises

**Question 1:** Define Value Engineering and explain its purpose.

**Answer:** Value Engineering is a systematic, function-oriented approach used to improve the value of a product, project, system, or service. Its purpose is to identify and eliminate unnecessary costs while maintaining or improving performance, reliability, quality, safety, and customer satisfaction.

**Question 2:** List the phases of the Value Engineering procedure in the correct order.

**Answer:** The phases are: 1. Information Phase, 2. Function Analysis Phase, 3. Creative Phase, 4. Evaluation Phase, 5. Development Phase, 6. Implementation Phase, 7. Reporting Phase.

**Question 3:** Explain the purpose of the Function Analysis Phase and describe its activities.

**Answer:** The purpose of the Function Analysis Phase is to rigorously analyze the functions identified in the Information Phase and determine their importance.  Activities include: developing a FAST diagram (if covered), classifying functions as basic, secondary, or support functions; evaluating function worth; and comparing cost and worth to identify functions that have a high cost relative to their worth.

**Question 4:** In the context of Value Engineering, what is the formula for calculating "Value"? Explain how you could increase the "Value" of a component.

**Answer:** Value = Function/Cost.  You could increase the Value of a component by:
1.  Increasing the Function (performance, features, etc.) while keeping the Cost constant.
2.  Decreasing the Cost while keeping the Function constant.
3.  Increasing the Function and decreasing the Cost simultaneously.

**Question 5:** Give a real-world example where Value Engineering could be applied and describe how the process could benefit the project.

**Answer:** Consider the design of a public transportation bus stop.  Using Value Engineering, the project team can analyze the functions of the bus stop (e.g., provide shelter, display information, offer seating). The information phase would gather data on usage, demographics, weather conditions, maintenance, and costs. The function analysis would determine the importance of each function. The creative phase would generate alternative designs using lower-cost materials, modular construction, or simplified layouts. The evaluation phase would assess the feasibility and cost-effectiveness of each alternative. The development phase would create detailed plans for the most promising designs. The implementation phase would involve constructing the new bus stop design. This VE process could result in a more functional, cost-effective, and user-friendly bus stop.

## 7. Conclusion

The Value Engineering procedure provides a powerful framework for improving the value of engineering projects, products, and services. By systematically analyzing functions and costs, engineers can identify opportunities to reduce unnecessary expenses while maintaining or improving performance. Mastering the VE procedure is a valuable skill for engineers seeking to deliver optimal value in their work.
