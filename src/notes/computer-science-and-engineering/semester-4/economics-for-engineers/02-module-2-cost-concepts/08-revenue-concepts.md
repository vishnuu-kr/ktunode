# ECONOMICS FOR ENGINEERS - Module 2: Cost Concepts - Revenue Concepts

## Learning Outcomes:

Upon completion of this topic, you should be able to:

*   Define and differentiate between Total Revenue (TR), Average Revenue (AR), and Marginal Revenue (MR).
*   Explain the relationship between revenue curves and the price elasticity of demand.
*   Calculate TR, AR, and MR from given data.
*   Understand the influence of market structure on revenue curves.
*   Apply revenue concepts to make informed economic decisions.

## 1. Key Concepts and Definitions:

*   **Revenue:** The total income generated by a firm from the sale of its goods or services.

*   **Total Revenue (TR):** The total amount of money received by a firm from selling a certain quantity of output.

    *   **Formula:** TR = Price (P) x Quantity (Q)

*   **Average Revenue (AR):** The revenue earned per unit of output sold.  It is essentially the price per unit.

    *   **Formula:** AR = Total Revenue (TR) / Quantity (Q)  or  AR = P

*   **Marginal Revenue (MR):** The additional revenue generated from selling one more unit of output.

    *   **Formula:** MR = Change in Total Revenue (ΔTR) / Change in Quantity (ΔQ)

    *   Mathematically, MR is the first derivative of TR with respect to Q.

## 2. Revenue Curves and the Price Elasticity of Demand:

*   **Relationship between AR and Demand Curve:** The Average Revenue (AR) curve is essentially the firm's demand curve.  It shows the relationship between the price of a product and the quantity demanded.

*   **Price Elasticity of Demand:** Measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.

    *   **Elastic Demand ( |Ed| > 1 ):**  A percentage change in price leads to a larger percentage change in quantity demanded.  Lowering the price increases total revenue.

    *   **Inelastic Demand ( |Ed| < 1 ):** A percentage change in price leads to a smaller percentage change in quantity demanded. Lowering the price decreases total revenue.

    *   **Unitary Elastic Demand ( |Ed| = 1 ):** A percentage change in price leads to an equal percentage change in quantity demanded. Total revenue is maximized at this point.

*   **Relationship between TR, MR and Elasticity:**

    *   **Elastic Region:** As price decreases and quantity increases, TR increases, and MR is positive.
    *   **Unitary Elastic Region:** At the quantity where demand is unitary elastic, TR is maximized, and MR = 0.
    *   **Inelastic Region:** As price decreases and quantity increases, TR decreases, and MR is negative.

*   **Visual Representation:**

    *   Imagine a TR curve that starts at zero, rises to a maximum point, and then falls back to zero. The point where it reaches maximum corresponds to unitary elastic demand (MR=0).
    *   The MR curve will lie below the AR curve (except in perfect competition). The MR curve will cross the x-axis at the quantity where TR is maximized.

## 3. Calculation of TR, AR, and MR:

**Example 1:**

| Quantity (Q) | Price (P) | Total Revenue (TR) | Average Revenue (AR) | Marginal Revenue (MR) |
|--------------|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|-----------------------|
| 0            | 10        | 0                 | -                    | -                     |
| 1            | 9         | 9                 | 9                    | 9                     |
| 2            | 8         | 16                | 8                    | 7                     |
| 3            | 7         | 21                | 7                    | 5                     |
| 4            | 6         | 24                | 6                    | 3                     |
| 5            | 5         | 25                | 5                    | 1                     |
| 6            | 4         | 24                | 4                    | -1                    |
| 7            | 3         | 21                | 3                    | -3                    |

**Calculations:**

*   **TR:** P x Q (e.g., for Q = 2, TR = 8 x 2 = 16)
*   **AR:** TR / Q (e.g., for Q = 2, AR = 16 / 2 = 8)
*   **MR:** ΔTR / ΔQ  (e.g., MR when moving from Q=1 to Q=2 is (16-9)/(2-1) = 7)

**Example 2:**

A firm sells 100 units of a product at a price of $5 each. When it reduces the price to $4.50, the quantity demanded increases to 120 units. Calculate the TR, AR, and MR.

*   **Initial Situation:**
    *   Q1 = 100
    *   P1 = $5
    *   TR1 = 100 * $5 = $500
    *   AR1 = $500 / 100 = $5
*   **New Situation:**
    *   Q2 = 120
    *   P2 = $4.50
    *   TR2 = 120 * $4.50 = $540
    *   AR2 = $540 / 120 = $4.50
*   **Marginal Revenue:**
    *   ΔTR = $540 - $500 = $40
    *   ΔQ = 120 - 100 = 20
    *   MR = $40 / 20 = $2

## 4. Influence of Market Structure on Revenue Curves:

*   **Perfect Competition:**
    *   Many firms selling identical products.
    *   Firms are price takers (they cannot influence the market price).
    *   Demand curve is perfectly elastic (horizontal line).
    *   AR = MR = Price (P)
    *   TR curve is a straight line upward sloping from the origin.

*   **Monopoly:**
    *   Single firm controls the entire market.
    *   Firm has significant control over price.
    *   Demand curve is downward sloping.
    *   MR curve lies below the AR curve.
    *   To sell more, the monopolist must lower the price of all units, not just the additional unit. This is why MR < P.

*   **Monopolistic Competition:**
    *   Many firms selling differentiated products.
    *   Firms have some control over price.
    *   Demand curve is downward sloping, but more elastic than in a monopoly.
    *   MR curve lies below the AR curve.

*   **Oligopoly:**
    *   Few firms dominate the market.
    *   Firms are interdependent (one firm's actions affect the others).
    *   Demand curve can be kinked (due to strategic interactions between firms).
    *   MR curve can have a discontinuous segment.

## 5. Application of Revenue Concepts to Economic Decisions:

*   **Production Decisions:** Firms use revenue and cost data to determine the optimal level of production.  The profit-maximizing level of output occurs where Marginal Revenue (MR) equals Marginal Cost (MC).

*   **Pricing Decisions:**  Understanding the price elasticity of demand is crucial for setting prices.  If demand is elastic, a firm can increase total revenue by lowering the price.  If demand is inelastic, a firm can increase total revenue by raising the price.

*   **Investment Decisions:**  Revenue projections are critical for evaluating the potential profitability of new investments.

*   **Market Entry/Exit Decisions:**  Firms consider potential revenues and costs before entering or exiting a market.

## 6. Important Points to Remember:

*   AR is always equal to the price (AR = P).
*   In perfect competition, AR = MR = P.
*   MR is the change in TR from selling one more unit.
*   The relationship between TR, MR, and elasticity of demand is critical for pricing decisions.
*   Profit maximization occurs where MR = MC.

## 7. Practice Questions and Exercises:

**Question 1:**

A company sells widgets. The following table shows the quantity demanded at different prices:

| Price ($) | Quantity Demanded |
|-----------|-------------------|
| 10        | 100               |
| 9         | 120               |
| 8         | 150               |

a) Calculate Total Revenue (TR), Average Revenue (AR), and Marginal Revenue (MR) for each price level.
b) What is the price elasticity of demand between a price of $10 and $9? Is it elastic or inelastic?
c) At what price does total revenue appear to be maximized?

**Answer 1:**

a)

| Price ($) | Quantity Demanded | Total Revenue (TR) | Average Revenue (AR) | Marginal Revenue (MR) |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|----------------------|-----------------------|
| 10        | 100               | 1000              | 10                   | -                     |
| 9         | 120               | 1080              | 9                    | 4                     |
| 8         | 150               | 1200              | 8                    | 4                     |

*MR is calculated as change in TR / change in Q.*

b)  Price elasticity of demand between $10 and $9:

*   Percentage change in price = (($9 - $10) / $10) * 100 = -10%
*   Percentage change in quantity = ((120 - 100) / 100) * 100 = 20%
*   Price elasticity of demand = 20% / -10% = -2
*   Absolute value = 2
*   |Ed| > 1, therefore, the demand is elastic.

c) Total revenue appears to be maximized at a price of $8 (TR = $1200).

**Question 2:**

Explain how the revenue curves (AR and MR) differ between a perfectly competitive firm and a monopoly.

**Answer 2:**

*   **Perfectly Competitive Firm:** The AR and MR curves are the same and are a horizontal line at the market price.  This is because the firm is a price taker and can sell as much as it wants at the prevailing market price.

*   **Monopoly:** The AR curve is downward sloping, reflecting the market demand curve. The MR curve is also downward sloping, but it lies below the AR curve. This is because the monopolist must lower the price on all units to sell an additional unit.

**Question 3:**

A firm is considering whether to increase its advertising expenditure. If the firm increases its advertising, it expects to sell 10% more units. The current price elasticity of demand for the firm's product is -1.5. Should the firm increase its advertising expenditure?  Explain.

**Answer 3:**

First, consider the impact of advertising on the demand. If advertising is effective, it will shift the demand curve outwards (to the right). This means that at any given price, the quantity demanded will increase. The firm believes this increase in advertising will boost sales by 10% and we know demand is price elastic (|Ed| = 1.5).

Because the demand is price elastic (-1.5), decreasing the price by a small percentage will increase quantity demanded by a larger percentage.  This means TR increases when price is decreased.  Thus, the firm *should* increase advertising.  While an increase in costs will come with advertising, the increase in sales resulting from increased demand will likely result in a greater profit. To ensure it's a beneficial decision, the firm should calculate the projected increase in TR and ensure it exceeds the increased costs of advertising.
