## Economics for Engineers - Module 2: Cost Concepts - Explicit and Implicit Costs

**Learning Outcomes:**

*   Understand the difference between explicit and implicit costs.
*   Be able to identify explicit and implicit costs in engineering projects and business scenarios.
*   Calculate economic profit and accounting profit.
*   Appreciate the importance of considering both explicit and implicit costs in decision-making.

---

### 1. Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Cost:** In economics, cost represents the value of resources sacrificed to obtain a benefit.  It's not just about money changing hands; it encompasses the opportunity cost as well.

*   **Explicit Costs:**
    *   **Definition:** These are direct, out-of-pocket expenses involving an actual outlay of money.  They are easily identifiable and are recorded in a company's accounting records.
    *   **Also known as:** Accounting costs
    *   **Characteristics:**  Tangible, measurable, and documented.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   Salaries and wages paid to employees
        *   Raw material purchases
        *   Rent payments for office or factory space
        *   Utility bills (electricity, water, gas)
        *   Depreciation of machinery (calculated using accounting methods)
        *   Interest payments on loans
        *   Marketing and advertising expenses
        *   Transportation costs

*   **Implicit Costs:**
    *   **Definition:** These are the opportunity costs of using resources that the firm already owns. They do *not* involve a direct cash outlay. They represent the value of the next best alternative use of those resources.
    *   **Also known as:** Imputed costs, opportunity costs
    *   **Characteristics:** Intangible, not easily measurable (requires estimation), and not usually recorded in accounting records.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   **Foregone salary:**  The salary an engineer gives up to start their own consulting firm.
        *   **Foregone rent:** The rental income a company forgoes by using its own building instead of renting it out.
        *   **Depreciation (Economic):** The true decline in the market value of an asset over time, rather than the accounting depreciation.
        *   **Normal profit:**  The minimum return required to keep an entrepreneur in their current business (opportunity cost of their time and capital). This is the return the entrepreneur could earn in the next best alternative.  This is often approximated by the market rate of return.
        *   **Inventory usage:** If you use goods from inventory for your project, the cost is the price you could have sold those goods for.

*   **Economic Profit vs. Accounting Profit:**
    *   **Accounting Profit:** Total Revenue - Explicit Costs
    *   **Economic Profit:** Total Revenue - (Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs)  OR  Accounting Profit - Implicit Costs

    *   **Important Note:** Economic profit is ALWAYS less than or equal to accounting profit.  Economic profit takes into account all costs (both explicit and implicit), providing a more accurate picture of profitability.  A business may show an accounting profit but have an economic loss if the implicit costs are greater than the accounting profit.

*   **Normal Profit (as it relates to Economic Profit):** A firm earns normal profit when economic profit is equal to zero.  This means the firm is earning just enough to cover all its costs, including the opportunity cost of its resources. It is the minimum level of profit required to keep the resources employed in their current use.

### 2. Identifying Explicit and Implicit Costs in Engineering Projects and Business Scenarios

Let's look at some engineering-related scenarios:

*   **Scenario 1: Designing a New Bridge**

    *   **Explicit Costs:**
        *   Cost of materials (steel, concrete, etc.)
        *   Labor costs for construction workers
        *   Consulting fees for structural engineers
        *   Permit fees
        *   Equipment rental (cranes, bulldozers)

    *   **Implicit Costs:**
        *   The time spent by the lead engineer on the project. They could have been working on other billable projects.  Calculate the value of their time based on their hourly rate or potential project earnings.
        *   The opportunity cost of using company-owned equipment on the bridge project instead of renting it out.
        *   Potential delays in other ongoing projects due to the allocation of resources to the bridge construction. The cost of these delays (missed deadlines, potential penalties) is an implicit cost.

*   **Scenario 2: Implementing a New Software System in a Manufacturing Plant**

    *   **Explicit Costs:**
        *   Purchase price of the software
        *   Installation fees
        *   Training costs for employees
        *   Annual maintenance fees
        *   Cost of new hardware to run the software (if required)

    *   **Implicit Costs:**
        *   Lost productivity during the implementation and training period.
        *   The value of the time spent by IT staff managing the implementation, which could have been used for other projects.
        *   Potential disruption to production processes during the transition.
        *   The value of the old software system if it could have been sold or used for other purposes.

*   **Scenario 3: An Engineer Starting a Freelance Consulting Business**

    *   **Explicit Costs:**
        *   Software licenses (CAD, simulation software)
        *   Office supplies
        *   Marketing and advertising expenses
        *   Travel expenses for client meetings
        *   Internet and phone bills

    *   **Implicit Costs:**
        *   The salary the engineer gave up from their previous full-time job.
        *   The interest earned on savings used to fund the business (instead of investing that money).
        *   The value of the engineer's time spent on administrative tasks (billing, marketing) that could have been used for billable client work.

### 3. Calculating Economic Profit and Accounting Profit

**Example:**

An engineer starts a consulting business.  In the first year, they generate $200,000 in revenue.

*   **Explicit Costs:**
    *   Rent: $20,000
    *   Utilities: $5,000
    *   Software: $10,000
    *   Marketing: $15,000
    *   Total Explicit Costs = $50,000

*   **Implicit Costs:**
    *   Foregone Salary (from previous job): $80,000
    *   Foregone Interest on Savings (used to start the business): $5,000
    *   Total Implicit Costs = $85,000

**Calculations:**

*   **Accounting Profit:** $200,000 (Revenue) - $50,000 (Explicit Costs) = $150,000
*   **Economic Profit:** $200,000 (Revenue) - ($50,000 (Explicit Costs) + $85,000 (Implicit Costs)) = $65,000

**Interpretation:**

While the engineer made an accounting profit of $150,000, their economic profit is only $65,000. This means that after considering all costs (including the opportunity cost of their time and money), they only earned $65,000 more than they could have earned in their next best alternative.

**What if the foregone salary was $160,000 instead of $80,000?**

*   **Implicit Costs:** $160,000 (Foregone Salary) + $5,000 (Foregone Interest) = $165,000
*   **Economic Profit:** $200,000 - ($50,000 + $165,000) = -$15,000

In this case, the engineer has an economic loss of $15,000.  Even though they have an accounting profit, they would have been better off financially by keeping their old job.

### 4. Importance of Considering Both Explicit and Implicit Costs in Decision-Making

*   **Accurate Profitability Assessment:**  Ignoring implicit costs leads to an overestimation of profitability. Economic profit provides a more realistic assessment of the true profitability of a project or business.

*   **Informed Resource Allocation:** By recognizing the opportunity cost of resources, businesses can make better decisions about how to allocate those resources. For example, deciding whether to use existing equipment for a project or to rent it out and lease new equipment.

*   **Better Investment Decisions:**  Economic profit helps in evaluating investment opportunities.  If a project's economic profit is negative, it means the resources could be used more productively elsewhere.

*   **Long-Term Sustainability:** Considering both types of costs ensures that decisions are not based solely on short-term gains, but rather on long-term economic viability.

*   **Strategic Planning:** Understanding implicit costs is crucial for developing effective business strategies. For example, understanding the opportunity cost of expanding into a new market can help companies make more informed decisions about growth.

### 5. Practice Questions/Exercises

**Question 1:** A civil engineer is considering starting their own bridge inspection company. They have the following information:

*   Potential Revenue: $300,000 per year
*   Office Rent: $30,000 per year
*   Equipment Rental: $50,000 per year
*   Salary for an assistant: $40,000 per year
*   Foregone salary from their previous job: $100,000 per year
*   Foregone interest on savings used to start the business: $10,000 per year

Calculate the accounting profit and economic profit.  Should the engineer start the company?

**Answer:**

*   **Explicit Costs:** $30,000 + $50,000 + $40,000 = $120,000
*   **Accounting Profit:** $300,000 - $120,000 = $180,000
*   **Implicit Costs:** $100,000 + $10,000 = $110,000
*   **Economic Profit:** $300,000 - ($120,000 + $110,000) = $70,000

**Decision:** The engineer should start the company.  While the economic profit is less than the accounting profit, it is still positive, meaning the engineer is earning more than they could have in their next best alternative.

**Question 2:** A manufacturing company is deciding whether to upgrade its existing machinery. The upgrade will cost $50,000 in explicit costs. It is estimated to increase revenue by $80,000 per year.  However, the upgrade will require the company's lead engineer to dedicate 200 hours to the project. The engineer's hourly rate is $100.  Calculate the economic profit of the upgrade.

**Answer:**

*   **Explicit Costs:** $50,000
*   **Increased Revenue:** $80,000
*   **Implicit Costs:** 200 hours * $100/hour = $20,000
*   **Economic Profit:** $80,000 - $50,000 - $20,000 = $10,000

**Question 3:** Explain the difference between accounting profit and economic profit and why is considering economic profit important in decision-making?

**Answer:**
Accounting profit only considers the explicit costs which are the direct, out-of-pocket expenses involving an actual outlay of money. Economic profit, on the other hand, considers both explicit and implicit costs. Implicit costs are the opportunity costs of using resources that the firm already owns, and they do not involve a direct cash outlay. Economic profit is important in decision-making because it provides a more realistic assessment of the profitability by considering the opportunity costs of all resources used in a project or business. Decisions based on economic profit help ensure that resources are allocated efficiently and that the project generates a return greater than the best alternative use of those resources.

**Question 4:** Give an example of an explicit and implicit cost for starting a business.

**Answer:**
Example:
*   **Explicit Cost:** Rent for office space.
*   **Implicit Cost:** Foregone salary from your previous employment.

### 6. Important Points to Remember

*   Explicit costs are easily identifiable and involve actual cash outlays.
*   Implicit costs are opportunity costs and represent the value of forgone alternatives.
*   Economic profit is a more comprehensive measure of profitability than accounting profit.
*   Always consider both explicit and implicit costs when making engineering and business decisions.
*   A positive economic profit indicates that the current use of resources is the most efficient.
*   Normal profit is the level of profit where economic profit is zero; it represents the minimum return required to keep resources employed in their current use.
