# NEXT GENERATION INTERACTION DESIGN - Module 2: User

## Topic: Immersion and Presence in AR/VR

---

### 1. Introduction to Immersion and Presence

**Key Concepts:**

*   **Immersion:** The degree to which a system can deliver a convincing sensory experience to the user. It's about blocking out the real world and drawing the user into the virtual or augmented environment.
    *   Think of it as the *quality of the experience* provided by the technology.
*   **Presence:** The subjective feeling of "being there" in a virtual or augmented environment, rather than simply observing it. It's the user's psychological connection to the simulated world.
    *   Think of it as the *user's psychological state*.

**Relationship between Immersion and Presence:**

*   **Immersion is a prerequisite for presence:** Higher levels of immersion generally lead to a stronger sense of presence. If the sensory experience isn't convincing, it's difficult to feel "present."
*   **Presence is the goal:** While immersion is the technological aspect, presence is the desired outcome for effective AR/VR experiences.

**Analogy:**

Imagine watching a movie on a small TV versus watching it in a high-quality IMAX theater with surround sound.

*   **Small TV:** Low immersion (limited visual field, less impactful audio). Likely low presence.
*   **IMAX Theater:** High immersion (large screen, enveloping audio, dark room). High potential for presence.

---

### 2. Dimensions of Immersion

**Key Concepts:**

Immersion can be broken down into several key dimensions, often influenced by technological capabilities and design choices.

*   **Sensory Fidelity:**
    *   **Definition:** The realism and detail of the sensory information presented to the user (visual, auditory, haptic, etc.).
    *   **Visual Fidelity:** Resolution, frame rate, field of view (FOV), color accuracy, realistic lighting, textures, and object detail.
    *   **Auditory Fidelity:** Spatial audio (3D sound), realism of sound effects, voice quality, absence of distracting background noise.
    *   **Haptic Fidelity:** Tactile feedback (vibrations, textures, force feedback) that corresponds to interactions in the virtual or augmented world.
    *   **Other Senses:** While less common, some research explores olfactory (smell) and gustatory (taste) feedback.

*   **Interactivity:**
    *   **Definition:** The degree to which users can meaningfully interact with and manipulate the virtual or augmented environment.
    *   **Responsiveness:** How quickly and accurately the system reacts to user input.
    *   **Agency:** The feeling that the user's actions have consequences and can affect the environment.
    *   **Control:** The extent to which users feel in control of their actions and the environment.

*   **Content & Narrative:**
    *   **Definition:** The quality and engagement of the virtual or augmented world itself, including its story, characters, and themes.
    *   **Believability:** A cohesive and internally consistent world that doesn't break the user's immersion.
    *   **Engagement:** Compelling narratives, interesting characters, and meaningful goals that keep the user invested.
    *   **Emotional Resonance:** Experiences that evoke emotions in the user, strengthening their connection to the virtual world.

**Examples:**

*   **Sensory Fidelity:**
    *   *High:* A VR game with photorealistic graphics, precise positional tracking, and realistic spatial audio that makes you duck when a virtual object flies past your head.
    *   *Low:* A VR experience with pixelated graphics, laggy tracking, and mono audio that makes the environment feel artificial and unconvincing.
*   **Interactivity:**
    *   *High:* In VR, being able to pick up objects, throw them, and see them realistically interact with the physics of the world.
    *   *Low:* In AR, having a virtual object that you can't move or interact with in any meaningful way, just a static overlay.
*   **Content & Narrative:**
    *   *High:* A VR story-driven adventure where the characters' dialogue and actions feel natural, and the plot pulls you in.
    *   *Low:* A VR experience with robotic dialogue, plot holes, and an uninteresting environment that feels like a tech demo.

### 3. Factors Affecting Presence

**Key Concepts:**

Presence is a subjective experience influenced by a combination of technological and psychological factors.

*   **Technological Factors (related to Immersion):**
    *   **Display Quality:** Resolution, refresh rate, FOV, contrast ratio, absence of screen-door effect (visible gaps between pixels).
    *   **Tracking Accuracy & Latency:** How precisely and quickly the system tracks head, hand, and body movements. High latency (delay) is a major detractor from presence.
    *   **Audio Quality:** Spatial audio, clarity, and absence of clipping or distortion.
    *   **Haptic Feedback:** The quality and relevance of tactile feedback.
    *   **Field of View (FOV):** A wider FOV can increase the feeling of enclosure and being "in" the environment.
    *   **Tracking Limitations:** Incomplete tracking (e.g., only head tracking) can break presence as the user might notice their virtual body doesn't match their real one.

*   **Psychological Factors:**
    *   **Expectations:** What the user expects from the experience. Preconceived notions can influence their perception.
    *   **Engagement & Attention:** The user's level of focus and involvement in the experience.
    *   **Motivation:** Why the user is engaging with the AR/VR content.
    *   **Prior Experience:** Previous experiences with AR/VR can shape expectations and understanding.
    *   **User's State:** Fatigue, motion sickness, or cognitive load can negatively impact presence.
    *   **Social Presence:** The feeling of being with other people (real or virtual) in the AR/VR environment.

*   **Environmental Factors:**
    *   **Real-world distractions:** Noise, interruptions, or visual clutter in the user's actual environment can break presence.
    *   **Physical comfort:** Ill-fitting headsets, uncomfortable seating, or motion sickness can detract from the experience.

**Important Point to Remember:**

Presence is not solely a technical achievement; it is a **psychological construct** that is *facilitated* by technology but ultimately experienced by the user.

### 4. Types of Presence

**Key Concepts:**

While often used interchangeably, researchers distinguish different facets of presence.

*   **Spatial Presence:**
    *   **Definition:** The feeling of being physically located in the non-physical, mediated environment. It's about feeling "in" the virtual space.
    *   **Key Aspect:** Embodiment – feeling like you have a body within the virtual world.
    *   **Example:** Feeling like you are actually standing on a virtual cliff edge, rather than just looking at a screen.

*   **Social Presence:**
    *   **Definition:** The feeling of being with and interacting with other people (real or virtual agents) in the mediated environment.
    *   **Key Aspect:** Connection and communication with others.
    *   **Example:** Engaging in a conversation with an avatar of another user in a VR meeting space, feeling their presence and responsiveness.

*   **Self-Presence (or Embodiment):**
    *   **Definition:** The feeling of being present as a self within the mediated environment. This often relates to having an avatar that accurately represents the user's movements and appearance.
    *   **Key Aspect:** Sense of agency and embodiment of one's own self in the virtual space.
    *   **Example:** Seeing your virtual hands mirror your real hand movements accurately, allowing you to manipulate virtual objects convincingly.

### 5. Measuring Immersion and Presence

**Key Concepts:**

Quantifying these subjective experiences is challenging but crucial for design evaluation and research.

*   **Subjective Measures:**
    *   **Questionnaires/Surveys:** The most common method. Users rate their experience on Likert scales for various aspects of immersion and presence.
        *   **Examples:**
            *   *Presence Questionnaire (PQ)* by Witmer & Singer.
            *   *Igroup Presence Questionnaire (IPQ)* by Schubert, Friedmann, & Regenbrecht.
            *   *Slater-Usoh-Steed (SUS) Presence Questionnaire*.
    *   **Interviews & Think-Aloud Protocols:** Qualitative data collection to understand user perceptions.

*   **Objective Measures:**
    *   **Behavioral Observations:** Analyzing user actions within the virtual environment.
        *   **Examples:**
            *   **Reaction Times:** How quickly users react to stimuli.
            *   **Task Performance:** Efficiency and accuracy in completing tasks.
            *   **Navigation Patterns:** How users explore the virtual space.
            *   **Startle Reflex:** Measuring physiological responses to unexpected events.
    *   **Physiological Measures:** Recording biological responses that may correlate with presence and immersion.
        *   **Examples:**
            *   **Heart Rate (HR) & Heart Rate Variability (HRV):** Can indicate arousal and stress.
            *   **Electroencephalography (EEG):** Measuring brainwave activity.
            *   **Electromyography (EMG):** Measuring muscle activity.
            *   **Skin Conductance (Electrodermal Activity - EDA):** Measuring emotional arousal.
            *   **Eye Tracking:** Analyzing gaze patterns and pupil dilation.

**Challenges:**

*   **Subjectivity:** Presence is an individual experience.
*   **Correlation vs. Causation:** Physiological responses don't always directly map to presence.
*   **Observer Effect:** The act of measuring can sometimes alter the experience.

### 6. Design Implications for Enhancing Immersion and Presence

**Key Concepts:**

Understanding immersion and presence directly informs how AR/VR experiences should be designed.

*   **Prioritize High-Fidelity Sensory Input:**
    *   **Visuals:** High resolution, wide FOV, high refresh rates, accurate color, realistic lighting and textures.
    *   **Audio:** Implement spatial audio so sound sources are perceived as coming from specific directions and distances.
    *   **Haptics:** Integrate haptic feedback that is meaningful and congruent with visual/auditory cues.

*   **Minimize Latency and Jitter:**
    *   **Definition:** Ensure the system responds immediately and smoothly to user movements.
    *   **Why it matters:** High latency is a primary cause of motion sickness and breaks presence.

*   **Provide Intuitive and Responsive Interaction:**
    *   **Natural Mapping:** Ensure controls and interactions feel natural and intuitive (e.g., reaching out to grab an object).
    *   **Direct Manipulation:** Allow users to directly interact with virtual objects.
    *   **Clear Feedback:** Provide immediate visual, auditory, or haptic feedback for all user actions.

*   **Design for Embodiment:**
    *   **Accurate Avatars:** If using avatars, ensure they track user movements realistically and can be customized.
    *   **Body Tracking:** Utilize full-body tracking where appropriate to enhance the sense of self-presence.

*   **Create Believable and Engaging Content:**
    *   **Consistent World-Building:** Maintain a consistent visual style and internal logic for the virtual environment.
    *   **Meaningful Narratives:** Develop compelling stories and goals that motivate user engagement.
    *   **Social Integration:** Design for social interaction if relevant, focusing on clear communication and presence of other users.

*   **Manage User Comfort and Prevent Motion Sickness:**
    *   **Comfort Settings:** Offer options for locomotion (e.g., teleportation vs. smooth movement), turning (snap vs. smooth).
    *   **Frame Rate Stability:** Maintain a consistent and high frame rate.
    *   **Field of View:** While wide FOV is good, ensure the display doesn't cause visual discomfort or distortion at the periphery.
    *   **Avoid Discrepancies:** Minimize mismatches between visual motion and vestibular input.

*   **Consider the Real-World Context (for AR):**
    *   **Seamless Blending:** Design augmented elements that feel integrated into the real world.
    *   **Environmental Awareness:** Utilize sensors to understand and react to the user's real environment.

**Important Point to Remember:**

Designers must strike a balance between technological capabilities and user experience. Over-reliance on one aspect (e.g., pure visual fidelity) without considering interactivity or content can still lead to a lack of presence.

### Answers

**Answer 1:**
*   **Immersion:** The degree to which a system can deliver a convincing sensory experience to the user, blocking out the real world and drawing the user into the virtual or augmented environment.
*   **Presence:** The subjective feeling of "being there" in a virtual or augmented environment, rather than simply observing it; the user's psychological connection to the simulated world.
*   **Relationship:** Immersion is a key enabler of presence. Higher levels of immersion (better technology, more convincing sensory input) generally lead to a stronger sense of presence, but presence is the ultimate psychological goal.

**Answer 2:**
1.  **Sensory Fidelity:** The realism and detail of sensory information (visual, auditory, haptic).
    *   *Example:* High-resolution graphics and realistic sound effects in a VR game.
2.  **Interactivity:** The degree to which users can meaningfully interact with and manipulate the environment.
    *   *Example:* Being able to pick up, examine, and throw virtual objects with realistic physics.
3.  **Content & Narrative:** The quality, believability, and engagement of the virtual world itself, including its story and themes.
    *   *Example:* A compelling storyline and well-developed characters in a VR narrative experience.

**Answer 3:**
*   **Technological Factors:**
    *   Display Quality (e.g., resolution, FOV)
    *   Tracking Accuracy & Latency
    *   Audio Quality (e.g., spatial audio)
    *   Haptic Feedback
*   **Psychological Factors:**
    *   User Expectations
    *   Engagement & Attention
    *   Motivation
    *   Prior Experience

**Answer 4:**
*   **Spatial Presence:** The feeling of being physically located *in* the mediated environment. It's about feeling "present" in the virtual space itself.
*   **Social Presence:** The feeling of being with and interacting *with other people* (real or virtual agents) within the mediated environment. It's about connection and communication.

**Answer 5:**
1.  **Implement high-fidelity spatial audio:** This would make sounds (e.g., ambient city noise, voices of historical figures) appear to come from specific directions and distances, making the environment feel more real and enclosed, thus enhancing spatial presence.
2.  **Provide interactive elements:** Allow users to pick up and examine virtual artifacts (e.g., pottery shards, tools) with realistic weight and visual detail. This enhances interactivity, giving users a sense of agency and direct manipulation, which boosts presence.

**Answer 6:**
Minimizing latency is crucial because high latency (a noticeable delay between user movement and the system's response) directly disconnects the user's senses from their actions. This mismatch causes disorientation, can lead to motion sickness, and breaks the illusion of being in the virtual world, severely hindering the sense of presence.

### Important Points to Remember

*   **Immersion is the "how," Presence is the "what it feels like."**
*   **High immersion doesn't guarantee high presence, but it's usually necessary.**
*   **Presence is a subjective, psychological state.**
*   **Latency is the enemy of presence.**
*   **Designing for presence requires considering technology, user psychology, and content.**
*   **Both objective and subjective measures are used to evaluate presence, but challenges remain.**
*   **Social interaction can significantly enhance presence.**
*   **In AR, the seamless blending of virtual and real is key to presence.**
